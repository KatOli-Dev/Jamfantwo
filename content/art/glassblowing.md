## The Transformation of Sand

Glassblowing is the art of shaping molten glass into objects of use and beauty by means of a hollow pipe through which the glassmaker blows air into the gathering of molten material. The art is among the more recent of the crafts, for the technique of glassblowing was developed within the memory of the present age and spread across the known world through the agency of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the merchants of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). Before the development of glassblowing, glass was shaped by casting and by the cutting of blocks of cooled glass, techniques that limited the forms that could be produced and the purposes that glass could serve.

The raw material of glass is silica, the most abundant mineral in the world, found in the sands of the rivers and the shores of every continent. The silica is mixed with a flux, typically soda or potash, that lowers the melting point of the silica to a temperature that can be achieved in the furnaces of the glassmakers. Lime is added to stabilise the glass and to prevent it from dissolving in water. The mixture is heated in a furnace of refractory clay that can withstand the temperatures required, and the molten glass is gathered on the end of the blowpipe for shaping.

## The Origins and Spread of Glassblowing

The technique of glassblowing was developed in the workshops of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) in the generation preceding the present one, and it was from Amurra that the technique spread to the glassmaking centres of the other continents. The Amurran glassmakers discovered that a gathering of molten glass on the end of a hollow pipe could be inflated with the breath of the glassmaker into a bubble that could be shaped by the manipulation of the pipe and the use of simple tools.

The technique was a revelation to the glassmakers of the old school, who had been limited to the casting of glass in moulds and the cutting of glass from blocks. The blown glass was thinner and lighter than the cast glass, and it could be shaped into forms that the old techniques could not achieve. The demand for blown glass grew rapidly, and the glassmakers of Amurra could not produce enough to satisfy the merchants who came to the city seeking the new ware.

The glassmakers of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) learned the technique from the Amurran glassmakers and adapted it to the materials and the tastes of the south. The Khalisar glassmakers produce glass of a distinctive greenish tint, caused by the iron in the local sands, and they have developed decorative techniques that suit the colour of the glass. The Khalisar glassmakers also produce glass coloured with the metal oxides that are available in the Sahrani deserts, including the deep blue of cobalt and the turquoise of copper.

## The Furnace and the Tools

The glassblower's furnace is the heart of the workshop, a structure of refractory clay that must maintain a temperature high enough to keep the glass molten throughout the working day. The furnace is built with a central chamber in which the glass is melted, a working hole through which the glassmaker gathers the glass, and a flue that draws the smoke and the hot gases away from the workspace. The furnace is fuelled by wood or charcoal, and the glassmaker must maintain the fire throughout the day, adjusting the airflow to keep the temperature steady.

The principal tool of the glassblower is the blowpipe, a hollow iron rod of four to six feet in length with a mouthpiece at one end and a gathering bulb at the other. The glassmaker gathers a mass of molten glass on the end of the blowpipe, rolls it on a flat iron plate called the marver to shape it and to cool it slightly, and then blows through the mouthpiece to inflate the glass into a bubble. The bubble is shaped by the manipulation of the pipe, by the use of wooden paddles and metal tools, and by the application of heat from the furnace.

The glassmaker also uses a second tool called the pontil, a solid iron rod that is attached to the base of the finished object to hold it while the blowpipe is removed and the rim of the object is shaped. The pontil is attached by a small gathering of glass, and the mark that the pontil leaves on the base of the object is the characteristic sign of the blown glass.

## The Colours of Glass

The natural colour of glass is greenish, caused by the iron that is present in the sands from which the glass is made. The glassmakers have learned to control this colour and to produce glass of many different colours by the addition of metal oxides to the molten batch. The addition of cobalt oxide produces a deep blue, the addition of copper oxide produces a turquoise or a green, the addition of manganese oxide produces a purple, and the addition of iron oxide in controlled amounts produces a yellow or a brown.

The glassmakers of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) have developed a distinctive technique for the production of coloured glass that is unlike the techniques of the south. The dwarven glassmakers add powdered metals to the glass batch, producing colours that are more intense and more varied than the colours of the human glassmakers. The dwarven glass also contains bubbles of gas that catch the light and produce a sparkling effect that is characteristic of the dwarven style.

The glassmakers of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) produce glass of a purity that is unmatched in the known world. The Thessaran glass is made from the white sands of the [Thessaran Basin](/content/location/settlement/region/thessaran-basin), sands that are almost free of the iron that colours the glass of other regions. The Thessaran glass is clear and colourless, and it is valued for the windows and the vessels that require the transparency that only the purest glass can provide.

## The Forms of Blown Glass

The glassblower produces vessels of many forms, each suited to a particular purpose. The bottle is the most common form, produced in a range of sizes and shapes for the storage of liquids. The bottle is shaped by blowing the glass into a mould or by the free-blowing of the bubble and the shaping of the neck with the tools.

The bowl is another common form, produced by blowing a bubble and then opening it and shaping the rim with the tools. The bowl is used for the serving of food and for the decoration of the table. The bowl is also produced in the form of the drinking vessel, the cup and the goblet, which are shaped by the manipulation of the bubble and the addition of a stem and a foot.

The window pane is a form of blown glass that serves a different purpose. The window pane is produced by blowing a large bubble and then spinning it on the pontil until the centrifugal force flattens it into a disc. The disc is cut into panes of the required size and shape. The window panes of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) are the finest in the known world, and the glass of the Vashari sea-temples is admired for its clarity and its colour.

## The Decoration of Glass

The decoration of glass is accomplished by a range of techniques that are applied to the surface of the finished object or incorporated into the glass during the blowing. The simplest decoration is the cutting of the surface with a wheel of copper or stone, a technique that produces the patterns and the figures that are characteristic of the cut glass of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic).

The technique of enamelling is applied to glass as it is to metal, with the difference that the enamel must be fired at a lower temperature to avoid melting the glass itself. The enamelled glass of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) is among the most valued of the Sahrani exports, and the patterns of the Khalisar enamels are the standard by which the enamellers of other cities measure their work.

The technique of gilding is applied to glass by the application of gold leaf to the surface of the finished object, a technique that is practised by the glassmakers of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) and the other cities of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth). The gilded glass is used for the vessels of the temples and the palaces, and the gold leaf is protected by a layer of clear glass that is applied to the surface.

## The Glassmakers and Their Guilds

The glassmakers are organised into [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) in the cities where the craft is practised. The guild of the glassmakers of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) is the oldest and the most powerful, and the Amurran glassmakers are the standard-setters for the craft across the known world. The guild maintains the standards of the craft, regulates the training of the apprentices, and settles disputes between the workshops.

The apprenticeship for a glassmaker is no less than ten years, and the apprentice must master the techniques of the gathering, the blowing, and the shaping before being admitted to the rank of journeyman. The journeyman must serve a further period before being admitted to the rank of master, and the master must demonstrate the ability to produce the full range of forms and decorations that the guild requires. The guild also maintains the formulas for the glass batches and the colours, preserving the knowledge of the craft for the generations that follow.
