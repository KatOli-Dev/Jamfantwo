## The Art of the Forest

Elven Woodland Art is the body of artistic practice that has developed among the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the other forest regions of the known world. The art is shaped by the elven relationship with the forest, the source of the materials that the elven artists work and the context within which the art is created. The elven approach to art is distinct from the approach of the human and the [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) peoples, for the elves do not regard the art object as a separate thing imposed upon the world but as a continuation of the natural processes that the forest expresses.

The philosophy of elven art is founded on the principle of cooperation with the natural world. The elven artist does not seek to dominate the material or to force it into a shape that is foreign to its nature. The elven artist seeks instead to reveal the form that is already present in the material, to assist the wood or the stone or the fibre in becoming what it already has the potential to be. The elven artist is not a creator in the manner of the human or the dwarven artist but a midwife, bringing forth the beauty that the natural world contains.

## Working with the Living Wood

The most distinctive form of elven art is the working of the living wood, a technique in which the elven artist shapes the growth of the tree itself rather than cutting the timber and carving it after it is dead. The elven artist selects a young tree, a sapling of the oak or the elm or the beech, and begins to guide its growth by the careful tying and weaving of the branches. The branches are bent into the forms that the artist intends, held in place by ties of bark or fibre, and allowed to grow in the shape that the artist has set.

The process is slow, for the growth of the tree cannot be hurried. The elven artist works with the tree over a period of years, returning to it season after season to adjust the ties and to guide the growth. The tree becomes a part of the artist's life, a companion in the work, and the elven artists speak of the trees they have shaped with the affection that they would give to a friend.

The living wood is used for the construction of the elven dwellings, which are not built from cut timber but are grown from the trees of the forest. The elven dwelling is a structure of living branches and trunks, shaped into rooms and passages that are continuous with the forest itself. The elven dwelling is not a separate thing that is placed in the forest, but a part of the forest that has been shaped to the purposes of the elves.

## The Carving of Fallen Timber

The elves also work in timber that has fallen naturally in the forest, for the fallen tree is regarded as a gift from the forest that the elves may use with gratitude. The fallen timber is not cut from the living tree, for the elves do not cut the trees of the forest except in the case of dire necessity. The fallen tree is allowed to season in the forest before it is taken, and the wood of the fallen tree is worked with the same care that the elves give to the living wood.

The elven carvers work with chisels and knives of stone and metal, but they also work with tools of shell and bone that produce surfaces of a different character. The elven carver does not force the tool into the wood but allows the tool to follow the grain, cutting with the direction of the fibres rather than against it. The surface of the elven carving is smooth to the touch, with a softness that the human carver cannot achieve.

The forms of elven carving are the forms of the forest, the leaves and the flowers, the branches and the roots, the creatures that share the forest with the elves. The elven carving is not representational in the manner of human art, for the elves do not seek to imitate the forms of the forest but to express them. The leaf that is carved in wood is not a copy of a leaf but the expression of the leaf-ness that the elven carver has observed in the leaves of the forest.

## The Elven Use of Colour

The elves of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) use colour in a manner that is distinct from the human and the dwarven traditions. The elven palette is the palette of the forest, the greens and the browns, the golds and the russets, the blues of the sky that shows through the canopy and the whites of the clouds that pass above the trees. The elven artists do not use the bright colours that the human painters favour, for the forest does not produce those colours in abundance and the elven aesthetic does not demand them.

The elven dyes are derived from the plants of the forest, the barks and the leaves, the roots and the berries. The elven dyers have developed techniques for the extraction and the fixing of the forest colours that are unknown to the dyers of the human cities. The elven colours are softer and more subtle than the human colours, and they do not fade in the light of the forest or in the changing of the seasons.

The elven textiles are dyed in the colours of the forest, and the cloth that the elven weavers produce is the most subtle and the most beautiful in the known world. The elven cloth is not exported in any quantity, for the elves do not produce more than they need and the cloth that leaves the forest is a gift rather than a trade good.

## The Elven Relationship with Stone

The elves do not work in stone to the extent that the dwarves and the humans do, for the stone of the earth is not the material of the forest and the elven aesthetic does not require the permanence that stone provides. The elves use stone for the tools and the weapons that require a hard and durable edge, but they do not carve stone for the purpose of decoration.

The elven relationship with stone is different from the dwarven relationship. The elves do not mine the stone of the mountains, for the elves do not enter the deep places of the earth where the dwarves work. The elves use the stones that are found in the beds of the forest streams and the stones that are exposed by the erosion of the hillsides. The stones are used in the form in which they are found, with no more shaping than the grinding of the edge that the tool requires.

## The Music and the Poetry of the Elves

The Elven Woodland Art extends beyond the visual arts to include the music and the poetry of the elves, which are as distinctive as the visual forms. The music of the elves is played on instruments that are made from the materials of the forest, the flutes of hollowed branch and the strings of twisted fibre. The music of the elves follows the rhythms of the forest, the wind and the water and the growth of the trees, and the human ear that hears elven music for the first time often finds it strange and difficult to understand.

The poetry of the elves is composed in the elven language, a language of many vowels and subtle tones that the human voice cannot reproduce. The elven poetry is not written down, for the elves do not use writing in the manner of the human peoples. The poems are preserved in the memory of the poets, passed from one generation to the next through the recitation. The poems of the elves are long and complex, with layers of meaning that are revealed only to those who have studied the elven tradition.

## The Preservation of the Elven Tradition

The Elven Woodland Art is preserved through the teaching of the young by the old, in the manner of the elven tradition. The elven artists do not write down their techniques or their designs, for the written word cannot convey the knowledge that the elven artist requires. The apprentice elven artist learns through the observation and the imitation of the master, and through the performance of the work that the master assigns.

The elven tradition is also preserved through the works that are produced under it, the dwellings and the carvings and the textiles that are the expression of the elven relationship with the forest. The works are not collected in the manner of human art, for the elven works are part of the forest and they return to the forest when their purpose is fulfilled. The elven tradition is a living tradition, changing and adapting as the forest changes, and the elven artists of the present generation are the heirs of a tradition that has continued since the first elves walked the forests of Aethelar.
