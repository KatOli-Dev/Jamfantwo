## The Art of the Assembled Surface

Sahrani Tile and Mosaic is the art of assembling small pieces of coloured material into patterns and images that decorate the surfaces of buildings and objects. The art is practised chiefly in the desert regions of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where the tradition has developed over many centuries to a high degree of sophistication. The tiles and the mosaics of the Sahrani cities are among the most distinctive features of the Sahrani decorative tradition, and the works that the Sahrani tile-makers produce are admired across the known world.

The materials of the tile and mosaic art are the clays of the desert, fired to produce the ceramic tiles that form the basis of the tradition. The tiles are glazed in the colours that the Sahrani palette provides, the blues and the turquoises, the greens and the whites, the browns and the blacks. The tiles are cut to the shapes that the pattern requires, and the tiles are assembled on the surface of the wall or the floor to produce the design that the tile-maker has planned.

## The Origins and the Centres of the Tradition

The tradition of tile and mosaic work in Sahrana is ancient, predating the arrival of the human peoples in the region. The oldest examples of Sahrani tile work are found in the ruins of the cities that were built by peoples whose names are forgotten, structures that survive in the deserts of the south as evidence of the former inhabitants who worked in the same tradition. The scholars of the [Chroniclers College](/content/government/local/chroniclers-college) have studied these ruins and have noted the similarities between the ancient tile work and the tile work of the present age.

The principal centres of the tile and mosaic tradition in the present age are the cities of [Sahran](/content/location/settlement/city/sahran), [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), and [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir). The tile-makers of these cities are organised into [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) that maintain the standards of the craft and regulate the training of the apprentices. The tile-makers of Khalisar are the most numerous and the most productive, but the tile-makers of Sahran are regarded as the most accomplished in the technique of the figured mosaic, in which the tiles are arranged to produce images of the human figure and the natural world.

## The Production of the Tiles

The production of the tiles that are used in the Sahrani tile and mosaic tradition is a craft that requires skills that are distinct from the skills of the potter. The tile-maker must produce tiles that are flat and of uniform thickness, for the tiles must fit together without gaps when they are set into the wall. The tile-maker must also control the shrinkage of the clay during the firing, for an error in the estimation of the shrinkage can produce tiles that are the wrong size for the pattern they are intended to fill.

The clay that is used for the tiles is prepared with care, mixed with the fine sand of the desert that gives the fired tile its distinctive texture. The clay is pressed into wooden moulds that are shaped to the dimensions that the pattern requires, and the tiles are dried in the sun before they are fired. The firing is accomplished in kilns that are heated to a temperature that produces a hard, durable body, and the tiles are glazed after the first firing.

The glazing of the tiles is the most critical stage of the production, for the colour and the finish of the glaze determine the appearance of the finished work. The glazes of the Sahrani tradition are made from the same materials that the [Pottery and Ceramics](/content/art/pottery-and-ceramics) tradition uses, with the addition of the metal oxides that produce the distinctive colours of the Sahrani palette. The blue of the Sahrani tile is produced from cobalt, the turquoise from copper, the green from a mixture of copper and iron, and the white from a tin oxide that produces a surface of exceptional brightness.

## The Geometric Patterns of the Sahrani Tradition

The Sahrani tile and mosaic tradition is characterised by the use of geometric patterns, the interlocking stars and the polygons, the bands and the borders, that cover the surfaces of the walls and the floors. The geometric patterns are not arbitrary but are based on the mathematical principles that the Sahrani scholars have developed over the centuries. The patterns are constructed from the repetition of a small number of basic shapes, the square and the triangle, the hexagon and the star, arranged in combinations that produce an effect of infinite variety.

The construction of the geometric patterns requires a precise understanding of the relationship between the shapes and the spaces that they occupy. The tile-maker must be able to calculate the dimensions of the tiles that are required to produce the pattern and to arrange the tiles in the order that the pattern demands. The tile-maker who has mastered the geometric tradition can produce patterns of great complexity without the need for a prepared design, for the pattern is generated by the application of the rules that the tradition has established.

The geometric patterns of the Sahrani tradition are not confined to the decoration of buildings. The patterns are also used in the decoration of the objects that the Sahrani craftsmen produce, the vessels and the textiles, the metalwork and the woodwork. The geometric pattern is the unifying element of the Sahrani decorative tradition, and the presence of the Sahrani geometric pattern on an object is the mark of the Sahrani style.

## The Figured Mosaics of the Sahrani Courts

The figured mosaic is a development of the geometric tradition, in which the tiles are arranged to produce images of the human figure, the animal form, and the natural world. The figured mosaic is the most demanding form of the tile and mosaic art, for it requires the tile-maker to be able to translate the forms of the natural world into the language of the tile, a language of coloured squares and rectangles that must be arranged in a specific order to produce the image.

The figured mosaics of the Sahrani courts are found in the palaces and the gardens of the nobility, where they decorate the walls and the floors of the principal rooms. The mosaics depict the activities of the court, the hunts and the feasts, the music and the dance, the ceremonies of the [Sands Assembly](/content/culture/sands-assembly) and the [Sahrani Caravan Feast](/content/culture/sahrani-caravan-feast). The figures are stylised in the manner of the Sahrani tradition, with an emphasis on the pattern and the rhythm of the composition rather than on the naturalism of the representation.

The figured mosaics of [Sahran](/content/location/settlement/city/sahran) are the most accomplished in the Sahrani tradition. The tile-makers of Sahran have developed a technique for the cutting of the tiles that produces a surface of exceptional smoothness, and the mosaics of Sahran are as smooth to the touch as they are pleasing to the eye. The mosaics of Sahran are regarded as the highest achievement of the Sahrani tile and mosaic tradition, and the tile-makers of the other Sahrani cities measure their work against the standard of Sahran.

## The Tile and Mosaic in the Architecture of Sahrana

The tile and mosaic tradition is integral to the architecture of the Sahrani cities, for the buildings of the Sahrani tradition are decorated with tiles and mosaics from the foundation to the roof. The walls of the Sahrani houses are covered with tiles from the floor to the height of a man, for the tiles are easy to clean and they reflect the heat of the desert sun. The floors of the Sahrani houses are covered with mosaics that are designed to be viewed from above, with the patterns that are arranged to produce the effect of a carpet in stone.

The mosques and the temples of the Sahrani faiths are decorated with tiles and mosaics of the highest quality, for the decoration of the sacred building is regarded as an act of devotion. The mosques of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir) are covered with tiles of blue and turquoise, with the patterns of the geometric tradition that create a surface of dazzling beauty. The mosques of the Sahrani cities are among the most beautiful buildings in the known world, and the tile and mosaic work that decorates them is a measure of the devotion of the people who built them.

## The Export and the Influence of the Sahrani Tradition

The tiles and the mosaics of the Sahrani tradition are exported to every region of the known world, for the beauty and the durability of the Sahrani tile work are admired by every people. The tiles of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) are found in the palaces of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) and [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), in the sea-temples of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), and in the houses of the merchants of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic). The Sahrani tile tradition has influenced the decorative arts of every region with which Sahrana has trade relations.
