## The Spoken Word as Art

Epic poetry and storytelling are the arts of the spoken word, the telling of stories in language that is shaped and patterned to move the hearts and the minds of the hearers. The art is among the most ancient of human crafts, predating the written word by many centuries. The epic poets and the storytellers of the known world are the keepers of the history, the mythology, and the values of their peoples, and their art is the foundation upon which the written literature of the present age is built.

The epic poem is a long narrative poem that tells the story of a hero or a people. The epic is composed in verse that is designed to be recited aloud, with a rhythm and a structure that aids the memory and that suits the voice of the reciter. The epic poems of the known world are among the longest and the most complex works of literature that have been produced by any people, and they are the measure of the literary achievement of the cultures that produced them.

## The Epic Tradition of the North

The northern peoples of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) have the most developed epic tradition in the known world. The northern epics are composed in a verse form that is based on alliteration and stress, with each line containing a fixed number of stressed syllables and a pattern of initial sounds that link the halves of the line. The verse form is suited to the northern languages, which are rich in consonants and in stressed syllables, and the alliterative patterns of the verse create a sound that is distinctive and memorable.

The northern epics are the sagas, the long narratives that tell the history of the clans and the deeds of the heroes. The sagas were composed over many centuries, with each generation of poets adding to the stories that were passed down from the generations before. The sagas are not the work of a single poet but the accumulated achievement of a tradition, and the poets who composed them are often unknown, their names lost to the history that they preserved.

The recitation of the sagas is a performance that requires a skilled voice and a commanding presence. The saga-performer does not merely read the words but acts them, using the voice to create the characters and the scenes, the battles and the journeys, the loves and the deaths that the saga describes. The saga-performer of the north is among the most respected of the northern artists, and the performance of the saga in the hall of the chieftain is an occasion that draws the people from the surrounding countryside.

## The Bardic Tradition of Aethelar

The bardic tradition of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) is distinct from the epic tradition of the north. The bards of Aethelar are the poets who serve the courts of the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) realm and the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) realm, composing poems that celebrate the deeds of the nobility and the events of the court. The bards are trained in the academies of the [Chroniclers College](/content/government/local/chroniclers-college), where they study the forms and the techniques of the bardic tradition.

The bardic poem is composed in a verse form that is based on rhyme and syllable count, with a regular rhythm that is suited to the accompaniment of the harp. The bardic poem is shorter than the northern saga, typically lasting no more than an hour in performance, and it focusses on a single event or a single character rather than on the extended narrative of the saga.

The bards of Aethelar are also the keepers of the genealogies of the nobility, the lists of ancestors that establish the claims of the noble houses to their lands and their titles. The genealogies are composed in verse that is designed to be memorised, and the bard who can recite the genealogy of a noble house from its foundation to the present generation is valued above all others.

## The Oral Histories of the Sahrani Tribes

The [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the other peoples of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) have a tradition of oral history that is distinct from the epic and bardic traditions of the north. The Sahrani storytellers are the keepers of the history of the tribes, the tales of the ancestors, the wars, and the migrations that have shaped the Sahrani people.

The Sahrani storytellers perform in the markets and the camps, gathering the people around them with the power of their voices and the skill of their telling. The stories are told in prose rather than verse, but the language is shaped and patterned in a manner that distinguishes the storytelling from ordinary speech. The storyteller uses the rhythms of the Sahrani language, the repetitions and the parallel structures, to create a narrative that moves the hearers and that fixes the story in the memory.

The Sahrani storytellers also perform the tales of the [Unwritten Name](/content/religion/unwritten-name) faith, the stories of the creation of the world and the nature of the divine. The tales are not written down, for the faith of the Unwritten Name holds that the written word cannot contain the truth that the stories convey. The storytellers are the priests of the faith, and their performances are the ceremonies of the Unwritten Name.

## The Storytelling of the Plains Confederation

The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) has a tradition of storytelling that is practised by the elders of the clans, who gather the young people around the fire in the evenings to tell the stories of the plains. The stories are the history of the plains people, the tales of the great hunts and the great journeys, the stories of the heroes and the heroines of the plains.

The storytelling of the plains is also the means by which the laws and the customs of the plains people are transmitted from one generation to the next. The stories contain the examples of right conduct and wrong conduct, the consequences of honour and dishonour, the rewards of courage and the penalties of cowardice. The young people who hear the stories learn not only the history of their people but the values that guide the conduct of the plains people.

The storytellers of the plains are also the composers of the praise-songs, the poems that celebrate the deeds of the living and the dead. The praise-song is composed for a specific occasion, the celebration of a victory or the mourning of a death, and it is performed before the assembled clan. The praise-singer of the plains must be able to compose the song on the occasion, for the performance cannot be prepared in advance, and the skill of the praise-singer is measured by the ability to find the right words at the right moment.

## The Elven Traditions of Recitation

The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) have a tradition of recitation that is unlike the human traditions. The elven recitation is performed in the language of the elves, a language that is rich in vowels and in subtle variations of tone. The elven poems are composed in a verse form that is based on the patterns of the natural world, the rhythm of the wind and the water and the growth of the trees.

The elven recitation is not performed before a large audience, as the human recitation is. The elven poet recites before a small company of hearers, in a clearing of the forest or in the hall of the elven dwelling. The recitation is intimate and personal, and the hearers are expected to respond to the poem with their own thoughts and their own feelings.

The elven poems are not written down, for the elves do not use writing in the manner of the human peoples. The poems are preserved in the memory of the poets, passed from one generation to the next through the recitation. The elves say that the written word is a dead thing, and that the poem lives only in the voice and the breath of the poet.

## The Guilds and the Training of the Poets

The poets and the storytellers of the human realms are organised into [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) that maintain the standards of the craft and regulate the training of the apprentices. The guild of the bards of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) is the oldest and the most influential of the poetic guilds, and the bards of Eldreth are the standard-setters for the craft across the known world.

The training of the poet is a long process, requiring the mastery of the forms and the techniques of the tradition, the memorisation of the established works, and the development of the voice and the presence that the performance requires. The apprentice poet serves a period of study under a master poet, learning the craft through the imitation of the master and the composition of original works under the master's guidance.
