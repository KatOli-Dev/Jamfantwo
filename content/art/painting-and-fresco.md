## The Art of Colour on Surface

Painting is the application of pigment to a surface for the purpose of representation or decoration, and it is among the most widespread of the visual arts. Every thinking people has developed some form of painting, from the simplest marks made with charcoal on bark to the elaborate frescoes that cover the walls of temples and palaces. The materials and techniques differ across regions, but the impulse to make a mark that endures beyond the moment of its making appears to be universal.

The practice of painting depends upon the availability of suitable pigments and binders. Pigments are derived from minerals, plants, and animals, ground to a fine powder and mixed with a binder that causes the pigment to adhere to the surface. The choice of binder determines the character of the paint and the surface to which it can be applied. Egg yolk, plant gums, animal glues, and drying oils are the principal binders used in the known world, and each produces a paint with distinct properties of opacity, gloss, and durability.

## Fresco and the Art of the Wet Plaster

Fresco is the technique of painting upon wet lime plaster, a method that produces a particularly durable image because the pigment becomes chemically bonded to the plaster as it dries. The technique is practised chiefly in the regions where lime for plaster is readily available, particularly in the limestone-rich territories of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league).

A fresco is created by applying a layer of wet plaster to the wall and painting upon it before the plaster dries. The painter works quickly, for the plaster remains workable for only a few hours, and the painting must be completed in sections that correspond to the amount of plaster that can be applied in a single session. The joins between sections are visible on close inspection and are called the seams of the fresco. A skilled fresco painter plans the work so that the seams fall along the lines of the composition, where they are least noticeable.

The frescoes of the temples of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) in [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) and [Caerwyn](/content/location/settlement/city/caerwyn) are among the finest examples of the technique in the known world. The walls of the great temple in [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) are covered with frescoes depicting the history of the faith, the lives of the saints, and the visions of the prophets. The colours remain as bright as the day they were painted, preserved by the chemical bond between pigment and plaster. The painters of the Still Flame are organised into a guild that maintains the traditions of the craft, and the apprentices of the guild learn the fresco technique as the foundation of all painting.

The [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) has a tradition of fresco in the sea-temples of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast), but the Vashari frescoes are smaller in scale and more intimate in character than the great works of the Aldreth temples. The Vashari painters work in the colours of the sea and the shore, using blues, greens, and greys that echo the waters of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea). The frescoes depict the creatures of the deep, the ships of the Pact, and the faces of the sea mothers, and they serve as a focus for the devotion of the Vashari faithful.

## Panel Painting and the Portable Image

Panel painting is the technique of painting on a prepared wooden panel, a method that produces a portable image that can be moved from place to place. The panels are made from seasoned timber, selected for its freedom from knots and its resistance to warping. The panels of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) are the most valued for panel painting, for the oak and the elm of the heartwood are stable and durable when properly prepared.

The panel is prepared by coating it with a ground of gesso, a mixture of powdered chalk or gypsum with animal glue, applied in multiple thin layers and sanded smooth. The ground provides a white, absorbent surface that accepts the paint and holds the colours bright. The painter draws the design on the ground with charcoal or ink and then applies the paint in thin layers, building up the image from the background to the foreground.

The panel paintings of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) are known for their naturalism and their attention to the effects of light. The Amurran painters have developed a technique of layering translucent glazes of colour over a darker under-painting, producing a depth and richness of tone that is not achievable with opaque paint alone. The technique requires patience and a precise understanding of the behaviour of the pigments, and the Amurran painters guard their formulas for glazes as closely as the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) guard their formulas for metal alloys.

## The Painting of the Sahrani Courts

The courts of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) have developed a tradition of painting on prepared leather and parchment that is distinct from the panel and fresco traditions of the north. The Sahrani painters work with pigments ground from the minerals of the desert, including the brilliant blues derived from azurite and lapis lazuli that are among the most expensive colours in the known world. The Sahrani palette is dominated by warm colours, reds, oranges, and golds, that reflect the light of the desert sun.

The paintings of the Sahrani courts are decorative in character, adorning the walls of palaces and the pages of manuscripts with geometric patterns, floral motifs, and scenes of courtly life. The human figure appears in Sahrani painting but is stylised according to conventions that emphasise grace and elegance rather than naturalism. The painters of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir) are the most accomplished in the Sahrani tradition, and their works are collected by the nobility of every continent.

The relationship between Sahrani painting and the [Sahrani Tile and Mosaic](/content/art/sahrani-tile-and-mosaic) tradition is close, for the same geometric patterns and colour schemes appear in both media. A Sahrani painter who has mastered the tile tradition can work as a painter in the court style, and the two crafts are often taught together in the workshops of the Sahrani cities.

## The Painting of the Elven Groves

The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) practise a form of painting that is unlike the human traditions. Elven painting is applied to the living bark of certain trees, using pigments that are absorbed into the outer layers of the bark and become part of the tree itself. The image grows with the tree, changing as the bark expands and thickens over the years, so that a painting that is fresh and sharp becomes softer and more diffuse as the decades pass.

The elven painters work with brushes made from the tail hairs of forest creatures and with pigments derived from the plants and minerals of the woodland. The colours are the colours of the forest, greens and browns and golds, and the images are of the forest itself, the patterns of light and shadow, the forms of leaves and branches, the faces of the creatures that share the woodland with the elves. The paintings are not intended to be permanent in the manner of fresco or panel painting. The elves regard the gradual fading and transformation of the image as part of its beauty, and a painting that has become almost invisible with age is considered to have reached its state of perfection.

## The Materials of the Painter

The painter's craft depends upon the quality of the materials, and the production of pigments is an industry that spans the known world. The finest red pigments come from the cochineal insects of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), while the finest blues come from the lapis lazuli of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range). The whites are produced from the chalk of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the lead of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds). The [spices and dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) trade is closely linked to the supply of pigments, and the merchants who deal in both are among the wealthiest in the known world.

The guilds of the painters regulate the quality of the materials and the training of the apprentices. A painter who uses inferior pigments or who fails to prepare the ground correctly is subject to the discipline of the guild. The standards of the guilds ensure that the paintings of the present age will endure for the generations that follow, and the scholars of the [Chroniclers College](/content/government/local/chroniclers-college) have noted that the paintings of the present century are of a higher quality than those of the preceding century, a measure of the effectiveness of the guild system.
