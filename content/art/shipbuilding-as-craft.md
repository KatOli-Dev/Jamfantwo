## The Art of the Shipwright

The craft of shipbuilding is the art of designing and constructing vessels that travel upon the waters of the known world. The art is distinguished from the industry of shipbuilding, which is concerned with the production of vessels for the purposes of trade and war, by its focus upon the skill and the knowledge of the individual shipwright and upon the traditions of design and construction that have developed in the shipyards of the different regions. The craft of the shipwright is among the most demanding of the crafts, for the vessel that the shipwright builds must withstand the forces of the sea and must serve the purposes of the people who sail it.

The shipwright must understand the properties of the materials that are used in the construction of the vessel, the timber and the metal and the cordage that are the elements of the shipwright's craft. The shipwright must also understand the behaviour of the vessel in the water, the way that the hull moves through the waves and the way that the rigging responds to the wind. The knowledge of the shipwright is the accumulation of centuries of experience, and the traditions of the shipwright's craft are the foundation upon which the vessels of the present age are built.

## The Shipwrights of the Great Ports

The shipwrights of the great ports of the known world are the most accomplished in the craft, for they have access to the finest materials and they work in the yards that have produced vessels for generations. The shipwrights of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) in the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) realm are the most numerous and the most experienced, for the yards of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) have been producing vessels since the founding of the city and the traditions of the Eldreth shipwrights are the standard by which the shipwrights of the other ports measure their work.

The shipwrights of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) are the second most accomplished in the known world. The [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) shipwrights work in the traditions of the south, building vessels that are suited to the warm waters of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the trade routes that connect the continents. The vessels of Khalisar are built from the timber that is imported from the forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and the Khalisar shipwrights have developed techniques for the working of the imported timber that allow them to produce vessels of a quality that rivals the vessels of Eldreth.

The shipwrights of [Nordhaven](/content/location/settlement/city/nordhaven) in the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) are the masters of the northern tradition, building vessels that are designed for the cold waters of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) and the seas of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The [Nordhaven](/content/location/settlement/city/nordhaven) shipwrights use the timber of the northern forests, the pine and the spruce that are available in the region, and they have developed techniques for the construction of vessels that can withstand the storms and the ice of the northern waters.

## The Design of the Vessel

The design of the vessel is the first and the most important stage of the shipwright's work. The design must balance the competing demands of speed and capacity, of strength and lightness, of stability and manoeuvrability. The shipwright who is designing a vessel must consider the purpose that the vessel will serve, the waters that it will navigate, and the cargo that it will carry.

The shipwright begins the design with the drawing of the lines of the hull, the shape of the vessel as it will appear from the side, the front, and the top. The lines are drawn on a flat surface, the mould loft floor, and they are the plan from which the vessel will be built. The shipwright who draws the lines must have a precise understanding of the relationship between the shape of the hull and the performance of the vessel in the water, and the best of the shipwrights have developed an eye for the lines that produce the sweetest sailing.

The shipwright also designs the rigging of the vessel, the arrangement of the masts and the sails that will propel the vessel through the water. The rigging must be suited to the conditions of the waters that the vessel will navigate, with the masts that are tall enough to catch the wind and the sails that are strong enough to withstand the gusts. The shipwright must also consider the crew that will sail the vessel, for the rigging must be manageable by the number of sailors that the vessel will carry.

## The Selection and the Preparation of the Timber

The timber that the shipwright uses is the most critical material in the construction of the vessel, for the quality of the timber determines the strength and the durability of the hull. The shipwright selects the timber from the forests that provide the best material for the purpose, the oak of [Brackenshield Wood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/brackenshield-wood) for the frames and the keels, the elm of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) for the planking, and the pine of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) for the masts and the spars.

The timber must be seasoned before it can be used, dried slowly over a period of years to prevent the warping and the cracking that would ruin the hull. The shipwrights of the great ports maintain timber yards where the logs are stacked to season, and the inventory of the seasoned timber is the most valuable asset of the shipyard.

The shipwright also uses the compass timber, the curved pieces of wood that are used for the frames and the knees that give the hull its strength. The compass timber is cut from the branches and the roots of the trees, where the natural curve of the wood matches the curve that the hull requires. The shipwright who selects the compass timber must have an eye for the shape that the wood will take when it is cut, and the best of the shipwrights are known for their ability to find the perfect piece of timber for every part of the hull.

## The Construction of the Hull

The construction of the hull is the principal work of the shipyard, the process by which the design of the vessel is translated into the reality of the finished ship. The construction begins with the laying of the keel, the backbone of the vessel, a heavy timber that runs from the bow to the stern and that supports the entire structure of the hull.

The frames are then attached to the keel, rising from the keel to the height of the sides. The frames are the ribs of the vessel, providing the shape and the strength of the hull. The frames are cut from the compass timber and are shaped to the lines that the shipwright has drawn on the mould loft floor.

The planking is then applied to the frames, covering the skeleton of the vessel with the skin that will keep the water out. The planks are fitted edge to edge, with the seams that are caulked with oakum and sealed with pitch to make the hull watertight. The shipwright who is fitting the planking must work with precision, for the gaps between the planks must be no larger than the thickness of a knife blade.

## The Fitting of the Rigging and the Equipment

The rigging of the vessel is the system of ropes and pulleys that controls the masts and the sails. The rigging is fitted after the hull is complete, and the work requires skills that are distinct from the skills of the hull builder. The rigger must understand the forces that the rigging will bear and must know how to splice the ropes and to set the blocks and the tackles that control the movement of the sails.

The equipment of the vessel includes the anchors and the chains, the pumps and the boats, the compass and the charts that the navigator will use. The shipwright oversees the fitting of the equipment, ensuring that everything is in its place and that the vessel is ready for the sea.

## The Guilds of the Shipwrights

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the shipwrights are among the oldest and the most respected of the trade organisations in the known world. The guild of the shipwrights of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) is the largest and the most influential, and the standards that the Eldreth guild sets are the standards that the shipwrights of the other ports follow.

The training of the shipwright is a long process, beginning with the apprenticeship that lasts for no less than seven years. The apprentice learns the selection and the preparation of the timber, the cutting and the fitting of the frames and the planking, and the techniques of the caulking and the sealing of the hull. The apprentice must produce a masterpiece, a scale model of a vessel that demonstrates the apprentice's mastery of the craft, before being admitted to the rank of journeyman.

The shipwrights of the known world are the heirs of a tradition that has been passed from one generation to the next for centuries. The vessels that the shipwrights build are the foundation of the trade and the travel that connect the continents, and the craft of the shipwright is among the most essential of the crafts of the present age.
