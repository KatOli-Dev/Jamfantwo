## The Place of Music in the Known World

Music is among the most widespread of the arts, practised in some form by every thinking people across the three continents. The forms and instruments differ greatly from region to region, shaped by the materials available, the character of the people who produce them, and the purposes for which music is made. A work song sung in the fields of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) bears little resemblance to a courtly air played in the halls of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth), yet both are recognised as music by those who hear them, and both serve the human need for rhythm and melody.

The study of music is not confined to performers. Scholars of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) have written extensively on the mathematical properties of musical intervals and the ratios that govern harmonious sounds. The treatises of the Amurran academies on the relationship between string length and pitch are consulted by instrument-makers across the known world. In the [Chroniclers College](/content/government/local/chroniclers-college), music is studied as a historical record, for the songs of a people preserve details of their past that written chronicles may omit.

## The String Instruments of the North

The northern peoples of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) favour stringed instruments built for the accompaniment of epic poetry and saga. The principal instrument is the harp, a frame of bent wood holding strings of gut or wire that are plucked with the fingers or a plectrum. The harps of the north are smaller than the great harps of the southern courts, built to be carried from hall to hall by the travelling skalds who serve as the keepers of northern tradition. The strings are tuned to a pentatonic scale that suits the modal melodies of northern music, and the harpist accompanies the voice in a style that blends chord and melody into a single line.

The bowed lyre is another northern instrument, held between the knees and played with a horsehair bow. The sound is softer and more reedy than that of the harp, suited to indoor performance in the long halls of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) and the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm). The bowed lyre is used chiefly for instrumental pieces that tell a story without words, a form the northern peoples call a song without a tongue. Musicians who master the bowed lyre are held in high regard, for the instrument requires years of practice to produce a clear and steady tone.

The [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) peoples of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) have developed a distinctive string instrument called the hammered dulcimer, a trapezoidal box of wood across which strings are stretched and struck with small hammers held in the hand. The sound is bright and percussive, carrying well through the stone halls of the dwarven holds. The hammered dulcimer is played both as a solo instrument and in ensemble with other dwarven instruments, and the repertoire includes both dance tunes and meditative pieces that the dwarves regard as an expression of the order of the mountain.

## Wind Instruments and the Breath of the People

Wind instruments are found in every region, made from whatever materials are to hand. The simplest are the pipes and flutes cut from reed or wood, found among the shepherds of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) and the goatherds of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league). These instruments have no finger holes and produce but a single note, used for signalling across distance or for the simple melodies that herders play to pass the time. More complex flutes with finger holes are made from bone, wood, and metal, and the best of these are produced in the workshops of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth), where the instrument-makers have refined the proportions of the bore and the placement of the holes to a high precision.

The bagpipe is the principal wind instrument of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and the highlands of [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark). A bagpipe consists of a leather bag that serves as a reservoir for air, a blowpipe through which the player fills the bag, a melody pipe with finger holes, and one or more drone pipes that sound a continuous note. The player keeps the bag inflated by breath and arm pressure, producing an uninterrupted sound that can be sustained for as long as the player has air. The music of the bagpipe is loud and penetrating, suited to outdoor performance and to the accompaniment of marching and dancing. The [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) use the bagpipe in war, and the sound of the pipes is said to carry courage into the hearts of the clansmen and fear into the hearts of their enemies.

Brass instruments are less common than woodwinds, for the working of metal into the curved shapes that brass instruments require is a craft that not every community possesses. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) produce horns of hammered copper and bronze that are used for ceremonial purposes and for signalling across the mountain passes. The horns are straight or slightly curved, lacking the valves and slides of more complex instruments, and they sound only the notes of the natural harmonic series. The [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) uses paired horns in their sea-rites, and the call of the Vashari horn carries across the water from ship to ship.

## Percussion and the Rhythm of the Dance

Percussion instruments are the most ancient and the most universal of musical tools, for the impulse to strike a surface in rhythm is found among every people. Drums are made from hollowed logs, from clay pots, and from wooden hoops covered with stretched hide. The [Sahrani](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) peoples produce drums of fired clay with heads of goatskin, tuned by the application of heat to tighten the skin. The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) uses frame drums of shallow wooden hoops with a single head of horsehide, played with the fingers or a stick.

The [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) of the Aethelaran hill country have perfected the craft of the xylophone, an instrument of graduated wooden bars laid across a frame and struck with mallets. The bars are cut from selected hardwoods, seasoned for years before being shaped and tuned, and the resulting instrument produces a bright, clear sound that carries well through the woodland clearings where gnomish musicians perform. The gnomish xylophone is played in ensembles of two or three instruments of different sizes, producing a layered rhythmic music that the gnomes use for their festivals and dances.

The [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) and the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) have developed percussion instruments suited to their underwater environment. Shells filled with pebbles and sealed with wax produce a rattling sound when shaken. Hollow logs weighted to sink are struck with mallets of bone. The merfolk also use the human and elven instruments they obtain through trade, adapting them to the conditions of the sea with sealskin covers and corrosion-resistant fittings.

## The Voice as Instrument

Singing is the most accessible form of music, requiring no instrument but the voice, and every people has developed traditions of song that suit their language and their temperament. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are renowned for the beauty of their singing voices, which carry a clarity and precision that human singers rarely achieve. Elven song is built upon long, flowing phrases that weave through complex harmonies, often without instrumental accompaniment. The elves sing in parts, with each voice following its own melodic line in a web of interlocking sound that the elves call the voice-weave.

The human peoples of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) and [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) have developed a tradition of choral singing in the great temples and the halls of the nobility. The choirs of [Caerwyn](/content/location/settlement/city/caerwyn) and [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) are among the finest in the known world, their repertoires including both sacred music for the ceremonies of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) and secular pieces composed for courtly entertainment. The choral tradition is supported by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the choir-masters, who train the voices of the singers and maintain the standards of performance.

The [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) have a tradition of call-and-response singing that serves both work and ceremony. A leader sings a line, and the company answers with a repeated refrain. The form is well suited to the communal life of the Kharesh, for it requires no special training and allows every voice to join. The songs of the Kharesh preserve the history of the tribes, and a skilled singer can recite the genealogy of a clan for hours without repetition.

## Music in the Life of the People

Music accompanies every stage of human life, from the lullabies sung to infants to the dirges sung at funerals. The [Deep Mourning](/content/culture/deep-mourning) traditions of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) include a specific repertoire of funeral music performed on horns and drums. The [Crown Harvest](/content/culture/crown-harvest) festival of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) features music and dancing that continue from dusk to dawn.

The relationship between music and the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) is particularly close, for the ceremonies of that faith incorporate singing, instrumental music, and the ringing of bells as elements of worship. The temples of the Still Flame maintain schools of music where young singers and instrumentalists are trained in the repertoires of the faith. The bells of the temple towers are cast with specific tones that combine to form the harmonies the liturgy requires, and the bell-founders who produce them are among the most respected of craftsmen.

Music also serves as a bond between the peoples of the known world. The trade routes that carry goods across the continents also carry songs, and the melodies of one region are adapted to the instruments and tastes of another. The [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) has become a meeting-place of musical traditions, and the musicians of Amurra are known for their willingness to borrow from the styles of other peoples and to combine them into new forms. The music of the present age is richer for this exchange, and the scholars of the republic have documented the spread of musical forms across the continents in treatises that are themselves works of art.
