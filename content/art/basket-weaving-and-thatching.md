## The Fibre Crafts of the Known World

Basket weaving and thatching are the arts of working plant fibres into containers and coverings. The arts are among the most ancient of human crafts, for the materials that they use are available in every region and the techniques that they require are simple enough for any person to learn. The baskets and the thatch that the craftsmen produce are essential to the daily life of every community, and the skills of the basket weaver and the thatcher are among the most widely distributed of the crafts.

The materials of the basket weaver and the thatcher are the stems and the leaves of the plants that grow in the region. The reeds of the marshes, the rushes of the riverbanks, the straw of the grain fields, the bark of the trees, and the vines of the forest are all used in the production of baskets and thatch. The choice of the material determines the character of the finished object, and the craftsmen who work in these materials have learned to select the best of what their region provides.

## The Weaving of Baskets

Basket weaving is the technique of interlacing flexible fibres to produce a container. The basket is woven in the same manner that cloth is woven, with the warp and the weft of the fibres crossing at right angles to produce the structure of the basket. The warp fibres are the stiffer fibres that form the framework of the basket, and the weft fibres are the more flexible fibres that are woven through the warp to fill the spaces between.

The techniques of basket weaving vary from region to region, depending on the materials that are available and the purposes that the basket must serve. The coiled basket is the most common type, made by coiling a bundle of fibres in a spiral and stitching the coils together with a second fibre. The coiled basket is strong and durable, suited to the storage of grain and the transport of goods.

The twined basket is another type, made by twisting the weft fibres around the warp fibres in a pattern that produces a tighter and more flexible structure. The twined basket is suited to the carrying of water and the storage of liquids, for the tightness of the weave prevents the contents from leaking. The twined baskets of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) are among the finest in the known world, woven from the fibres of the desert palm and sealed with the pitch of the desert acacia.

The plaited basket is a third type, made by the interweaving of flat strips of fibre in a pattern that is the same on both sides of the basket. The plaited basket is decorative as well as functional, and the patterns of the plait are used to produce the designs that are characteristic of the basket weaving tradition of the region.

## The Basketry of the Marsh Peoples

The peoples of the marshes and the riverbanks of the known world are the most accomplished basket weavers, for the reeds and the rushes that grow in the wetlands are the finest materials for the craft. The reed marshes of the [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river) produce the reeds that are the standard of quality for the basket weavers of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and the baskets that are woven from the Greywash reeds are exported to every region.

The marsh peoples have developed a range of techniques for the working of the reeds, suited to the different types of reeds that the marshes provide. The bulrush of the shallow water is used for the large storage baskets that are the principal containers of the marsh households. The reed of the deeper water is used for the finer baskets that are used for the serving of food and the decoration of the home.

The marsh peoples also use the reeds for the construction of the mats that cover the floors and the walls of their dwellings. The mats are woven in the same manner as the baskets, but on a larger scale, producing a surface that is smooth and durable and that provides insulation against the damp of the marsh.

## The Basketry of the Forest Peoples

The peoples of the forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the other forested regions of the known world use the bark and the vines of the forest for the weaving of baskets. The bark of the birch and the elm is used for the production of the rigid baskets that are the containers of the forest households. The bark is stripped from the tree in the spring, when the sap is rising and the bark is flexible, and it is shaped into the form of the basket and sewn with the roots of the forest trees.

The vines of the forest are used for the production of the flexible baskets that are used for the carrying of the forest produce. The vines are gathered in the autumn, when they are dry and flexible, and they are woven into baskets that are light and strong and that can be carried on the back of the gatherer.

The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) are the most accomplished of the forest basket weavers, for they have worked with the fibres of the forest for longer than any other people. The elven baskets are woven from the fibres of the forest plants that the elves have cultivated for the purpose, and the elven baskets are among the most beautiful objects that the [Elven Woodland Art](/content/art/elven-woodland-art) produces.

## The Basketry of the Sahrani Deserts

The peoples of the [Sahrani](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) deserts use the fibres of the [desert plants](/content/nature/flora/desert-adapted-plants) for the weaving of their baskets. The leaves of the date palm are the principal material, stripped into thin strips and woven into baskets that are suited to the dry climate of the desert. The palm leaf baskets are light and strong, and they do not rot in the dry air of the desert.

The [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) basket weavers also use the fibres of the desert grasses, the esparto and the halfa that grow in the dry regions of the south. The grasses are gathered in the spring, when they are at their strongest, and they are woven into baskets that are used for the storage of the dates and the grains that are the staples of the Sahrani diet.

## The Thatching of Roofs

Thatching is the technique of covering the roof of a building with a layer of plant material that sheds the rain and provides insulation against the weather. The thatchers of the known world use the materials that their region provides, the reeds of the marshes, the straw of the grain fields, and the grasses of the uplands.

The thatcher begins the work by laying a layer of the thatching material on the roof frame, starting at the lower edge and working toward the ridge. The material is arranged in a thick layer, with the stems running in the direction of the slope and the ends overlapping to create a surface that the water cannot penetrate. The thatch is fixed to the roof frame by the use of the split hazel rods or the wire that the thatcher weaves through the material and secures to the frame.

The thatched roof is the most common type of roof in the rural regions of the known world, for the materials are available in most regions and the technique is simple enough that any competent builder can learn it. The thatched roofs of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) and the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) are the standard of quality for the thatching of the known world, and the thatchers of these regions are the most skilled in the craft.

## The Thatching of the Marsh Regions

The marsh regions of the known world produce the finest thatching material, the reed of the water edge. The reed that grows in the marshes is long and straight and strong, and the thatched roof that is made from the marsh reed is the most durable and the most beautiful of the thatched roofs.

The thatchers of the [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river) marshes are the most accomplished in the craft of reed thatching. The Greywash reed is the finest thatching material in the known world, and the thatched roofs of the Greywash region are the standard by which the thatchers of other regions measure their work. The Greywash thatchers are organised into a guild that maintains the standards of the craft, and the apprenticeship for a Greywash thatcher is no less than five years.

## The Social Role of the Basket Weaver and the Thatcher

The basket weaver and the thatcher occupy a place in every community, for the baskets and the thatch that they produce are essential to the daily life of the people. The basket weaver provides the containers that are used for the storage and the transport of food, the gathering of the harvest, and the carrying of the goods that the household requires. The thatcher provides the roof that protects the household from the weather.

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the basket weavers and the thatchers are among the most widespread of the trade organisations, for these crafts are practised in every region where plant fibres are available. The guilds maintain the standards of the craft, regulate the training of the apprentices, and preserve the knowledge of the techniques that have been developed over the centuries. The basket weaver and the thatcher are the servants of the community, and their skills are among the most valued of the crafts that the community requires.
