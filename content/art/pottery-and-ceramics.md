## The Transformation of Clay

Pottery and ceramics are the arts of shaping and firing clay to produce objects of use and beauty. The art is among the most ancient of human crafts, for clay is abundant in most regions and the transformation of soft earth into hard, waterproof vessels is a process that requires no specialised materials beyond what the earth itself provides. Every thinking people has developed some form of pottery, and the forms and decorations of pottery are among the principal evidences by which scholars trace the movements and interactions of peoples across the centuries.

The raw material of pottery is clay, a fine-grained earth that becomes plastic when wet and hard when dried or fired. The quality of the clay determines the character of the pottery that can be produced from it, and the potters of the known world have learned to identify the clays of their regions and to blend them to achieve the properties they require. The clays of the [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river) are among the finest in the known world, producing a pale, fine-grained body that is well suited to the production of delicate vessels.

## The Wheel and the Hand

The shaping of clay is accomplished by two principal methods: hand-building and wheel-throwing. Hand-building is the older method, practised in every region, in which the clay is shaped by the hands alone or with the assistance of simple tools. The hand-building techniques include coiling, in which the vessel is built up from ropes of clay laid in spiral courses; pinching, in which the vessel is shaped by pressing and pinching the clay between the fingers; and slab-building, in which the vessel is assembled from flat sheets of clay.

The potter's wheel is a later development, a rotating platform that allows the potter to shape the clay by the centrifugal force of the spinning motion. The wheel was first developed in the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) and was spread across the known world by the merchants of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic). The wheel produces vessels of a regularity and a thinness that cannot be achieved by hand-building alone, and the wheel-thrown vessel is the standard of quality in the pottery of the present age.

The potters of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) in the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) realm are the most skilled wheel-throwers in the known world, producing vessels of a thinness and a uniformity that other potters admire but cannot match. The Nessaran potters attribute their success to the quality of the local clay and to the design of their wheels, which are mounted on bearings of polished stone that reduce the friction of the rotation to a minimum.

## Glazing and the Surface of the Vessel

Glazing is the application of a glassy coating to the surface of the fired clay, a technique that serves both to waterproof the vessel and to decorate it. The glaze is made from silica, the same material that forms the basis of glass, mixed with fluxes that lower the melting point and with metal oxides that provide the colour. The glaze is applied to the vessel by dipping, pouring, or brushing, and the vessel is fired a second time to melt the glaze and bond it to the clay body.

The glazes of the known world are of great variety. The potters of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) produce glazes of brilliant turquoise and deep blue, coloured with copper and cobalt oxides that are mined in the deserts of the south. The potters of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) produce glazes of earthy brown and green, coloured with iron and manganese oxides that are extracted from the ores of the mountains. The potters of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) produce glazes of clear and pale tones that allow the natural colour of the clay to show through.

The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) have developed a distinctive form of stoneware that is fired at such high temperatures that the clay body itself becomes vitrified, producing a vessel that is waterproof without the application of a separate glaze. The dwarven stoneware is extremely hard and durable, resistant to thermal shock and to mechanical damage, and it is valued for the storage vessels that must withstand the conditions of the deep mines. The dwarven potters fire their stoneware in kilns that are built into the natural vents of the mountains, using the heat of the earth itself to achieve the temperatures that the vitrification of the clay requires.

## The Pottery of the Plains

The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) has a tradition of pottery that is distinct from the wheel-thrown pottery of the settled regions. The potters of the plains build their vessels by the coiling method, shaping the clay into forms that are practical for the nomadic life, broad and low with wide mouths that are easy to pack and to carry. The decoration of the plains pottery is geometric, painted in brown and red slips on the natural buff of the clay body.

The pottery of the plains is fired in open bonfires rather than in enclosed kilns, a method that produces a softer, more porous body than the kiln-fired pottery of the settled regions. The plains potters accept the limitations of their firing method and have developed shapes and decorations that suit the character of the low-fired clay. The pottery of the plains is less durable than the pottery of the kilns, but it is also lighter and easier to replace, qualities that suit the needs of a people who are frequently on the move.

## The Ceramic Tiles of the Sahrani Tradition

The [Sahrani Tile and Mosaic](/content/art/sahrani-tile-and-mosaic) tradition is closely related to the pottery arts, for the tiles are made from the same clays and fired in the same kilns as the pottery vessels. The tile-makers of [Sahran](/content/location/settlement/city/sahran) and [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir) produce tiles of fired clay that are glazed and decorated with the geometric patterns that are characteristic of the Sahrani decorative tradition.

The production of ceramic tiles requires skills that are distinct from the skills of the potter. The tile-maker must produce tiles that are flat and of uniform thickness, for the tiles must fit together without gaps when they are set into the wall. The tile-maker must also control the shrinkage of the clay during firing, for an error in the estimation of the shrinkage can produce tiles that are the wrong size for the pattern they are intended to fill.

The tile-making workshops of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) are the largest in the known world, employing hundreds of craftsmen who produce tiles for the decoration of palaces, temples, and public buildings across the three continents. The tiles of Khalisar are exported to every region where the Sahrani style is admired, and the patterns of the Khalisar tiles are the standard by which the tile-makers of other cities measure their work.

## The Figurative Ceramics of the Amurran Republic

The [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) has developed a tradition of figurative ceramics that is distinct from the functional pottery of other regions. The Amurran potters produce vessels in the form of human and animal figures, used as containers for oils, perfumes, and other luxury goods. The figures are modelled by hand, with the details of the face and the drapery painted in coloured slips before firing.

The Amurran figurative vessels are among the most valued of the republic's exports, collected by the nobility of every continent. The potters of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) are organised into a guild that maintains the standards of the craft and regulates the training of the apprentices. The apprenticeship for an Amurran figurative potter is no less than twelve years, and the master potters are among the most respected craftsmen in the republic.

## The Social Role of the Potter

The potter occupies a place in every community, for the vessels that the potter produces are necessary to the daily life of the people. The cooking pots, the storage jars, the drinking cups, and the serving dishes that the potter produces are the essential equipment of every household. The potter is also the producer of the vessels that serve the religious and ceremonial life of the community, the offering bowls, the ceremonial urns, and the lamps that light the temples.

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the potters are among the most widespread of the trade organisations, for pottery is practised in every region where clay is available. The guilds maintain the standards of the craft, regulate the training of the apprentices, and settle disputes between the workshops. The guilds also maintain the formulas for the glazes and the clays, preserving the knowledge of the craft for the generations of potters who will follow.
