## The Woven Floor and Wall

Carpet and rug weaving is the art of producing heavy textiles that serve as floor coverings, wall hangings, and furnishings. The art is practised in every region of the known world, but it has reached its highest development in the regions where the nomadic tradition is strongest, among the peoples of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) and the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes). The carpets and the rugs of these peoples are among the most valued of their exports, and the patterns of the plains and the desert carpets are recognised across the known world.

The carpet serves both a practical and a decorative purpose. The carpet provides insulation against the cold of the ground in the winter and a clean surface for the sitting and the sleeping in the summer. The carpet also serves as a decoration of the dwelling, covering the walls and the floors with the patterns and the colours that express the identity of the weaver and the people to whom the weaver belongs.

## The Materials of the Carpet Weaver

The raw materials of the carpet and rug weaving are the fibres of the animals that are kept by the pastoral peoples. The wool of the sheep is the most common fibre, used for the carpets of the plains and the uplands. The wool of the goat is also used, particularly the fine, soft wool of the angora goat that is kept by the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the peoples of the Sahrani uplands. The wool of the camel is used for the carpets of the desert, for the camel wool is strong and durable and resistant to the conditions of the desert climate.

The fibres are prepared by the spinning, the twisting of the fibres into a thread that is strong enough to withstand the tension of the weaving. The spinning is accomplished with the use of the drop spindle, a simple tool that is used by the weavers of every region. The thread is dyed in the colours that the pattern requires, using the dyes that are available in the region of the weaver.

The dyes of the carpet weaver are derived from the plants and the insects of the region. The red of the madder root is the most common colour in the carpets of the plains, and the blue of the indigo plant is the most common colour in the carpets of the desert. The yellow of the safflower and the brown of the walnut husk complete the palette of the carpets of the plains and the desert.

## The Techniques of the Carpet Weaver

The carpet is woven on a loom, in the same manner that the [Textile Arts](/content/art/textile-arts) use for the weaving of cloth. The carpet loom is vertical, with the warp threads stretched from the top beam to the bottom beam, and the weaver works from the bottom to the top, knotting the weft threads around the warp threads and cutting the ends to produce the pile that is characteristic of the carpet.

The knot that is used in the carpet weaving is the distinguishing feature of the tradition. The two principal knots are the symmetrical knot and the asymmetrical knot, each of which produces a different character in the pile. The symmetrical knot is used in the carpets of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), producing a pile that is dense and durable, suited to the conditions of the nomadic life. The asymmetrical knot is used in the carpets of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), producing a pile that is softer and more lustrous, suited to the decoration of the tents of the Sahrani nobility.

The quality of the carpet is determined by the density of the knots, the number of knots per square inch that the weaver has tied. The carpets of the highest quality have a density of several hundred knots per square inch, a density that requires the weaver to work for many months to complete a single carpet. The carpets of the highest quality are the most valued and the most expensive, and they are passed from one generation to the next as heirlooms of the family.

## The Patterns of the Plains Carpets

The carpets of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) are characterised by the geometric patterns that are the tradition of the plains people. The patterns are composed from the shapes that the plains people have used for centuries, the diamond and the triangle, the star and the cross, arranged in repeating sequences that cover the surface of the carpet.

The colours of the plains carpets are the colours of the plains, the red of the earth and the blue of the sky, the green of the grass in the spring and the brown of the grass in the autumn. The patterns of the plains carpets are not arbitrary but carry meanings that the plains people understand. The diamond shape represents the tent of the plains, the triangle represents the mountain that is visible on the horizon, and the star represents the guiding star that the plains people follow in their journeys.

The patterns of the plains carpets are also the records of the history of the weaver's family. The pattern of a plains carpet can be read as a text, with the shapes and the colours telling the story of the family that produced it. The weaver who is reading the pattern of a plains carpet can identify the family of the weaver, the region of the plains from which the weaver comes, and the events that the weaver has recorded in the pattern.

## The Patterns of the Sahrani Carpets

The carpets of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the other peoples of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) are different in character from the carpets of the plains. The Sahrani carpets use the geometric patterns of the [Sahrani Tile and Mosaic](/content/art/sahrani-tile-and-mosaic) tradition, the stars and the polygons that are characteristic of the Sahrani decorative style. The patterns of the Sahrani carpets are more complex than the patterns of the plains carpets, with a greater variety of shapes and a more intricate arrangement of the elements.

The colours of the Sahrani carpets are the colours of the desert, the red and the orange and the yellow of the sand and the sun, the blue and the turquoise of the tiles of the Sahrani cities. The Sahrani carpets use the deep red of the madder root, the brilliant blue of the indigo, and the white of the undyed wool that provides the contrast that the pattern requires.

The Sahrani carpets are produced in the workshops of the cities as well as in the camps of the nomads. The carpets of the city workshops are of a higher quality than the carpets of the camps, for the city weavers have access to the finer materials and the more sophisticated looms. The carpets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir) are the most valued of the Sahrani carpets, and they are exported to every region of the known world.

## The Carpets of the Northern Peoples

The northern peoples of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) produce carpets that are different in character from the carpets of the plains and the desert. The northern carpets are woven from the wool of the northern sheep, a wool that is coarser and more durable than the wool of the southern sheep. The northern carpets use the patterns of the northern tradition, the interlocking geometric forms that are characteristic of the [Dwarven Craft Traditions](/content/art/dwarven-craft-traditions) and the [Sculpture and Stonework](/content/art/sculpture-and-stonework) of the northern halls.

The colours of the northern carpets are the colours of the north, the grey of the stone and the white of the snow, the red of the rowan berry and the green of the northern pine. The northern carpets are woven in long strips that are sewn together to produce the large carpets that cover the floors of the northern halls. The northern carpets are used for the benches and the platforms of the hall as well as for the floor, and the use of the carpet in the northern hall is a sign of the prosperity of the household.

## The Social Role of the Carpet

The carpet plays a central role in the social life of the peoples who produce it. The carpet is the surface upon which the family sits and eats and sleeps, the surface upon which the guests are received and the ceremonies are performed. The carpet is also the surface upon which the weaver has recorded the history and the identity of the family, and the carpet that is passed from the mother to the daughter is a bond that connects the generations.

The carpet is also a form of wealth, for the carpet that is well made is durable enough to last for many generations and valuable enough to be traded for goods of equivalent value. The carpets of the plains and the desert are the principal exports of the regions that produce them, and the trade in carpets is a significant part of the economy of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) and the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes). The carpet trade is regulated by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the carpet merchants, who maintain the standards of the craft and the prices of the carpets that are traded.
