## The Shaping of Shelter and Monument

Architecture is the art of designing and constructing buildings and other structures for human use and for the expression of human values. The architecture of the known world varies greatly across the three continents, shaped by the materials available, the climate of the region, and the cultural traditions of the people who build. A building must serve its purpose, to shelter its inhabitants from the weather and to provide the space that the activities of human life require. But a building also expresses something of the people who built it, their sense of order and beauty, their relationship with the divine, and their place in the world.

The study of architecture is among the disciplines of the [Chroniclers College](/content/government/local/chroniclers-college), where the scholars record the forms and the techniques of the buildings of the known world. The architects of the present age are trained in the methods of their predecessors, but they are also encouraged to innovate, to find new forms that suit the needs of the present generation.

## The Stone Halls of the Dwarven Holds

The [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) peoples of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) are the most accomplished architects in stone in the known world. The dwarven holds are carved into the living rock of the mountains, and the architecture of the holds is the architecture of the mountain itself. The dwarven architects do not build upon the earth but within it, and their constructions are shaped by the nature of the stone in which they are carved.

The great hall of a dwarven hold is a cavern of immense size, with a ceiling that rises to a height of fifty feet or more. The hall is supported by columns of natural stone, left in place by the dwarven excavators and carved with the patterns of the dwarven decorative tradition. The floor is level, cut from the living rock and polished to a smoothness that reflects the light of the torches. The walls are adorned with the relief carvings that tell the history of the hold, and the inlays of metal and stone that are the glory of the dwarven craft.

The dwarven hold is also a fortress, designed to withstand the attacks of enemies and the forces of nature. The entrances to the hold are narrow and defensible, with gates of stone and metal that can be closed against any threat. The passages within the hold are designed to confuse and to delay an invader, with multiple levels and unexpected turns. The dwarven hold is a work of architecture that serves the purposes of defence as well as the purposes of habitation.

The dwarven architects also build above ground, on the slopes of the mountains where the holds are located. The above-ground structures are built from the stone that is excavated from the holds, shaped into blocks and set in courses that follow the tradition of dwarven masonry. The above-ground structures include the watchtowers and the forges, the storehouses and the quarters for the guests who come to the hold for trade.

## The Timber Halls of the Northern Peoples

The northern peoples of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) build in timber, for the forests of the north provide the material in abundance and the techniques of timber construction are well suited to the cold climate of the region. The timber hall is the principal building of the northern settlement, a long, rectangular structure with a high roof and a central hearth that provides the heat and the light for the household.

The timber hall is constructed from the trunks of the pine and the spruce of the northern forests, hewn square and fitted together with joints that are cut into the timber. The walls are formed from horizontal logs, notched at the corners to hold them in place. The roof is supported by a frame of timbers that rise from the walls to the ridge, and the roof is covered with shingles of split wood or with turf that provides insulation against the cold.

The northern hall is also the centre of the social life of the community. The hall is the place where the people gather for the feasts and the ceremonies, the place where the chieftain holds court and the place where the storytellers perform the sagas. The hall is the heart of the northern settlement, and the architecture of the hall reflects the importance of the community that it serves.

The [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) build their halls in the style of the north, but with differences that reflect the conditions of the Frostwall Marches. The Frostwall halls are built lower to the ground than the halls of the south, with walls that are thicker and roofs that are steeper, designed to withstand the heavy snows of the Frostwall winter. The Frostwall halls are also built with the entrances facing away from the prevailing wind, a detail that illustrates the practical knowledge that the northern architects have accumulated over the centuries.

## The Stone and Brick Architecture of the Southern Cities

The cities of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) build in stone and brick, for the timber of the north is less abundant in the south and the techniques of masonry are well developed. The cities of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) and [Caerwyn](/content/location/settlement/city/caerwyn) are built of stone, with streets that are paved and buildings that rise to four storeys or more.

The architecture of the Aldreth cities is characterised by the use of the arch and the vault, techniques that allow the construction of large spans without the use of timber. The arches of the temples and the public buildings of Eldreth are among the finest in the known world, built from blocks of stone that are cut to precise dimensions and fitted without mortar. The vaults of the Aldreth buildings are constructed from the same technique, with the stones of the vault arranged in courses that transfer the weight of the structure to the walls.

The [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) builds in brick, for the clay of the [Thessaran Basin](/content/location/settlement/region/thessaran-basin) is abundant and the firing of brick requires less fuel than the quarrying of stone. The brick architecture of the Thessaran cities is characterised by the use of the dome, a technique that the Thessaran architects have developed to a high degree of sophistication. The domes of the Thessaran temples and public buildings are built from brick, with the courses of the brick arranged in a spiral pattern that allows the dome to rise without the support of a central scaffold.

## The Mud-Brick Architecture of the Sahrani Deserts

The peoples of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) build in mud-brick, for the stone and the timber of the north are not available in the deserts of the south. The mud-brick is made from the clay of the desert wadis, mixed with straw and water, shaped in wooden moulds, and dried in the sun. The mud-brick is a material of limited strength, but the Sahrani architects have developed techniques of construction that produce buildings of great beauty and durability.

The architecture of the Sahrani cities is characterised by the use of the courtyard and the wind-tower. The courtyard is the centre of the Sahrani house, open to the sky and cooled by the evaporation of water from the fountains and the plants that the courtyard contains. The wind-tower is a structure that rises above the roof of the house, catching the breeze and directing it through the rooms below. The wind-tower is a device that the Sahrani architects have developed over the centuries, and it is essential to the comfort of the Sahrani house.

The Sahrani cities of [Sahran](/content/location/settlement/city/sahran) and [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir) are built in the Sahrani style, with narrow streets that provide shade and with buildings that are whitewashed to reflect the heat of the desert sun. The architecture of the Sahrani cities is among the most distinctive in the known world, and the visitors from the north who see it for the first time are struck by the beauty of the forms and the intelligence of the design.

## The Architecture of the Plains

The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) builds in a manner that suits the nomadic life of the plains people. The principal structure of the plains people is the tent, a portable dwelling that can be erected and dismantled in a short time and that can be carried from place to place on the backs of the horses and the camels of the plains.

The tent of the plains is made from the felted wool of the sheep and the goats of the plains, stretched over a frame of willow poles that are tied together with leather thongs. The tent is round in shape, with a central opening at the top that serves as a chimney for the fire and a source of light for the interior. The tent is decorated with the patterns that are characteristic of the plains tradition, woven into the felt or embroidered in coloured threads.

The architecture of the plains is also expressed in the construction of the ceremonial structures that are built for the gatherings of the clans. The meeting tent of the clan council is a structure of great size, capable of accommodating the hundred or more people who attend the council. The meeting tent is built from the same materials as the dwelling tent, but the size and the decoration of the tent reflect the importance of the occasion.
