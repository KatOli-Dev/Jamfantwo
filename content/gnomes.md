## Origins

The origins of the gnomes are among the least understood of any thinking people. Gnomes are a small folk, reported in the woodlands and hills of several continents, and are said to bear some resemblance to [dwarves](/content/dwarves) in miniature, though the nature of their kinship, if any, is a matter of speculation. Some naturalists have proposed that gnomes share a common stock with the dwarves, pointing to the sturdy build, the broad hands, and the affinity for subterranean dwellings that both peoples display. Others reject this view, noting that gnomes are at home in woodland and open hill country as much as beneath the earth, and that their temperament and habits differ markedly from those of dwarves. The question remains unresolved, and the gnomes themselves, when asked, have offered no clear account of their origins.

What is certain is that gnomes are an old people, well established in their homelands long before the earliest chronicles of the common peoples were written. Their oral traditions are extensive, passed down through countless generations, and they preserve accounts of the lands they inhabit that predate the arrival of [humans](/content/humans) or [elves](/content/elves) in those regions.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Gnomes are the smallest of the sapient peoples, standing no taller than two thirds the height of an average [human](/content/humans). Their frames are compact and sturdy, broader in proportion than those of humans but lighter and more flexible than the heavy-set bodies of dwarves. Their bones are dense for their size, though not soremarkably thick as dwarven bone, and their musculature is built for endurance and dexterity rather than raw strength.

Gnomish senses are acute, particularly their hearing and their sense of touch. Their ears are large in proportion to their heads and are mobile, capable of swivelling toward faint sounds that would pass unnoticed by other peoples. Their eyes are well adapted to the dim light of forest undergrowth and shallow caves, though they are less capable in bright sunlight than the eyes of humans. Their sense of touch is remarkably fine, and gnomish craftsmen are able to detect imperfections in their work by feel alone, a quality that contributes to their reputation for precision.

Gnomish skin ranges from warm brown to ruddy in colour, often weathered by outdoor life. Their hair is typically fair or reddish, and both sexes wear it long and unbound. Gnomes of all ages are prone to freckling, particularly on the face and hands, a trait that is considered a mark of character rather than a blemish.

## Life Cycle

Gnomes are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of one hundred to one hundred and fifty years under favourable conditions. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late teens, but they retain their vigour well into their later years and decline only gradually. Gnomish women carry their young for approximately seven months, the shortest gestation among the sapient peoples, and multiple births are common. Infant mortality is somewhat higher than among the common peoples, but the frequency of births ensures that gnomish populations remain stable where conditions are favourable.

The combination of moderate longevity and frequent childbearing gives gnomish communities a character distinct from either the slow and cautious elves or the rapidly proliferating humans. Gnomish populations grow steadily rather than explosively, and they are resilient in the face of hardship, recovering from misfortune with a patience that has sustained their communities through many difficult periods.

## Habitat and Distribution

Gnomes are found in the woodlands and hills of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar) and the temperate uplands of [Morvind](/content/morvind), with smaller populations reported in the rocky uplands of [Sahrana](/content/sahrana). They favour terrain that offers both cover and variety, neither the open plains preferred by humans nor the deep mountains favoured by dwarves, but the transitional country where forest, hill, and shallow cave systems meet. Gnomish settlements are typically small and inconspicuous, built into hillsides or beneath the roots of mature trees, and a traveller may pass within a short distance of a gnomish community without knowing it.

Their dwellings often combine surface structures with shallow underground chambers, making use of natural hollows and extending them with careful excavation. Unlike the great underground halls of the dwarves, gnomish excavations are modest in scale and are always situated close to the surface, where air and light remain accessible.

## Diet

The gnomish diet is varied and reflects the diverse habitats they occupy. Fruits, nuts, roots, and fungi form the staples, supplemented by small game, fish from woodland streams, and cultivated vegetables grown in sheltered garden plots. Gnomes are skilled foragers, and their knowledge of the plants and fungi of their homelands is extensive, encompassing not only what may be eaten but also what may be used for medicine, dye, and material. They practise small-scale cultivation, tending plots of land near their settlements rather than clearing large fields, and their gardens are often so mingled with the surrounding woodland that they are difficult to distinguish from wild growth.

## Ecology

Gnomes occupy a niche as small-bodied foragers and craftsmen in the woodland and hill country they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is subtle, as they alter the landscape far less than humans or dwarves, and their presence often goes unnoticed by other peoples. Gnomish communities maintain a close relationship with the land they inhabit, and their traditions of stewardship are comparable in their effect to those of the elves, though operating on a smaller scale and over a shorter timespan.

Where gnomish ranges overlap with those of other peoples, contact is generally peaceful but limited. Gnomes and [humans](/content/humans) coexist in the wooded margins of human territory, where each people tends to its own affairs. Gnomes and [elves](/content/elves) share the deeper woodlands of Aethelar, and the two peoples are known to cooperate on matters of mutual concern, though their communities remain separate. Gnomes and [dwarves](/content/dwarves) encounter one another where hill country gives way to mountain, and the relationship between the two is one of cautious mutual respect, coloured by the unresolved question of their kinship.
