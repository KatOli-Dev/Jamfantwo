## Human Realm of the Morvind Interior Valley

Valdmark is the human realm of the principal river valley of the interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), encompassing the settled lands that line the banks of the largest river of the interior. The realm is the only significant human political entity in the Morvind interior, and its capital is [Valdheim](/content/location/settlement/city/valdheim), the largest and most prosperous city of the interior. Valdmark is an agricultural and commercial realm, and its prosperity is built upon the fertility of the river valley and the trade flowing through the interior, connecting the coast to the deep interior and the mountain ranges.

## River Lands and the Interior Breadbasket

The river valley that is the heart of Valdmark is the most fertile region in the interior of Morvind, and the alluvial soils lining the riverbanks are among the most productive on the continent. Agriculture is the foundation of the realm's prosperity, and grain, vegetables, and fodder produced in the valley are the primary exports. The river is navigable for much of its length, and barges carrying goods of the valley to the coast and goods of the coast to the interior are a common sight on the waterway.

The climate of the valley is milder than the surrounding highlands, for the river moderates the temperature and the surrounding peaks shelter the valley from the worst of the wind. The growing season is longer than on the higher plateaus, and agriculture is more varied and productive than in the rest of the interior. The valley is the breadbasket of the Morvind interior, and the grain produced here is exported to the coast and other regions, sustaining populations that cannot grow their own food.

## Valdheim and the River Commerce

Valdmark includes the principal settlements of the interior, drawn from the towns and cities that share in the agricultural and commercial economy of the valley. Valdheim is the capital and largest city, and its market is the primary point of exchange for goods of the interior. [Frostbeak](/content/location/settlement/village/frostbeak) is a valued member, commanding the highland pastures that are the source of wool and leather among the realm's exports. [Coldmoor](/content/location/settlement/village/coldmoor) is the most remote member, situated on the cold steppes of the far interior, and its pastoral economy is a valued supplement to the agriculture of the valley.

The trade of Valdmark is dominated by agricultural products of the valley and mineral products of the surrounding mountains. The realm is the primary point of exchange between the interior and the coast. Grain, wool, and leather are traded at Valdheim for metalwork, cloth, and manufactured goods imported from the coast and the [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) cities of the interior. The river is the primary route of trade, and the wharves and quays lining the riverbank at Valdheim are the centre of the realm's commerce.

## Appointed Lord and Valley Council

Valdmark is governed from Valdheim by a lord appointed by the council of the valley settlements. The administration is managed through a network of officials and local councils that oversee collection of taxes, maintenance of the river and roads, and administration of justice. The population is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the people of the valley are among the most prosperous and settled in the Morvind interior. Dwarves of the surrounding mountains are present in the valley, trading their metalwork for agricultural products, and the [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines) of the cold steppes are occasional visitors trading fur and meat for grain and manufactured goods.

The lord of Valdmark is chosen by the council of the valley settlements, a body composed of representatives of the towns and cities that make up the realm. The council meets at Valdheim, and selection of the lord is determined by majority vote. The term of the lord is for life, but the council retains the power to remove a lord judged unfit, a safeguard invoked only rarely in the realm's history. This arrangement places Valdmark between the absolute rule of the dwarven kings and the popular assemblies of the plains: the lord rules with real authority and wide discretion, but the council provides a check that no single ruler can safely ignore. The lord's authority extends to administration of the realm, command of military forces, and representation of Valdmark in relations with other political entities of Morvind.

## The Accord of the River

Valdmark traces its origins to early human settlements of the river valley, established during the first expansion of humans into the interior of Morvind. The fertility of the valley and navigability of the river drew settlers inland, and the towns and cities that grew along the riverbanks became the foundation of the realm. The unification of the settlements under a single governance is recorded in the traditions of the valley as the Accord of the River, an agreement among community leaders to appoint a common lord and co-ordinate the administration of the valley and management of the river.

The history of Valdmark has been marked by steady and peaceful growth. The valley is sheltered by surrounding mountains, and the realm has faced few of the external threats that have troubled coastal regions. The greatest challenges are the periodic harshness of interior winters, which can damage crops and herds, and occasional flooding of the river, which can inundate low-lying fields and riverside settlements. The management of these challenges has been the primary occupation of the lord and council, and investment in flood defences and winter storehouses has been a significant component of governance.

## Trade with Khazgar and Stoneheim

Valdmark maintains a close and mutually beneficial relationship with the dwarven cities of the surrounding mountains. Trade between the valley and the mountains is the foundation of the economy of the interior. The dwarven cities of [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar) and [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) are the primary trading partners, and metalwork and tools produced in dwarven forges are traded to the valley in exchange for grain, wool, and leather from the valley and highland pastures. Trade is conducted through overland routes crossing the mountain passes, and caravans travelling these routes are a common sight in the valley settlements.

The relationship between Valdmark and the dwarven cities is one of mutual respect and commercial co-operation. The two peoples have developed a tradition of trade and exchange that has sustained the economy of the interior for many centuries. The dwarven smiths value the grain and wool of the valley, which are primary supplies for dwarven communities that cannot produce their own food, and human settlers value the metalwork and tools of the dwarven forges, which are the foundation of the agriculture and construction of the valley. The trade between the two peoples is a model of co-operation that has sustained the interior of Morvind.

## Agricultural Centre of the Morvind Interior

Valdmark is the only significant human political entity in the Morvind interior, and its agricultural wealth and commercial role are the foundation of its significance. The realm's trade connects the coast to the deep interior, and its grain sustains populations of the interior that cannot grow their own food. Valdmark's position as the agricultural and commercial centre of the interior makes it a symbol of the human capacity to establish and maintain prosperous settlements in the demanding environment of the Morvind interior.

The river management system developed by the valley communities, using a combination of dykes, sluices, and diversion channels, has turned the seasonal flood from a hazard into a reliable source of irrigation. The winter storehouses at Valdheim, Coldmoor, and Frostbeak are filled each autumn with preserved grain, dried meat, and salted vegetables sufficient to sustain the population through the worst winters, and the council maintains a register of each settlement's stores to ensure equitable distribution. The overland routes to the dwarven cities are marked with stone wayposts that also serve as shelters, and the maintenance of these routes is a standing obligation of the communities through whose territory they pass. The Accord of the River, originally agreed orally, was written down in the third century of the realm's existence and is read aloud at each council meeting as a reminder of the principles upon which Valdmark was founded.
