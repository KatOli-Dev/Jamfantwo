## The Wardens and Councils of the Mountain Kingdom

The Karhold Warden Councils are the bodies of wardens and elders who administer the individual holds and territories of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm), the [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) kingdom of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The warden councils are not sovereign bodies but administrative institutions of the realm, their authority derived from the king and exercised within the boundaries established by royal decree. The warden councils are the primary instruments of local governance in the dwarven communities of the mountain spine, and their functions include the management of the mines and forges, the administration of local justice, and the maintenance of the halls and roads.

The warden council system of the Karhold Realm is one of the oldest continuous administrative structures in Aethelar, its origins predating the unification of the realm under the Deep King. The councils were established in the early centuries of dwarven settlement, when the communities of the mountain spine first organised themselves into bodies for the management of the mines and the resolution of disputes. The council system was retained and formalised when the realm was united under the first Deep King, and the councils have been a feature of the realm's governance ever since.

## Composition and Appointment

Each warden council in the Karhold Realm is composed of a variable number of members, depending on the size and importance of the hold or territory it governs. The councils are composed of the wardens, who are the officials appointed by the Deep King to oversee the administration of the holds, and the elders, who are the most experienced and respected members of the community. The wardens are appointed by the king and serve at his pleasure. The elders are chosen by the community and serve for life, subject to the confirmation of the king.

The warden is the principal official of the hold, responsible for the execution of the king's orders, the management of the hold's resources, and the administration of justice. The warden is appointed by the Deep King from among the most capable members of the dwarven community. The appointment is based on merit and experience, and the warden must have served as an elder or an official in the hold for a minimum period before appointment. The warden holds office for a term of ten years and may be reappointed by the king.

The elders are chosen by the community of the hold from among its most experienced and respected members. The selection is made at a gathering of the adult members of the hold, and the candidates are proposed and discussed before a vote is taken. The elders serve for life, and they are expected to provide the warden with advice and counsel on the management of the hold's affairs. An elder who is judged by the community to have failed in their duties may be removed by a vote of the adult members of the hold.

## Functions and Responsibilities

The primary function of the warden councils is the management of the mines and forges that are the foundation of the realm's economy. The councils oversee the extraction of ore from the mines, the operation of the forges and smelteries, and the distribution of the metalwork and stone that are the products of the realm. The councils allocate the mining rights among the families of the hold, regulate the working conditions and safety practices in the mines, and manage the distribution of the tools and equipment that the miners and smiths require.

The councils also manage the maintenance of the halls and roads that are the infrastructure of the realm. The dwarven halls are carved from the living rock of the mountain, and they require constant maintenance to keep them in good condition. The councils oversee the repair of the halls, the maintenance of the ventilation shafts and water drainage systems, and the upkeep of the roads and bridges that connect the holds. The councils also manage the construction of new halls and the expansion of existing ones when the growth of the population requires it.

The administration of justice is another important function of the warden councils. The warden, acting as the chief magistrate of the hold, hears disputes between members of the community over mining rights, the use of water and other resources, and the boundaries of halls and territories. The warden also deals with offences against the laws of the realm, including theft, damage to property, and violence. The warden's decisions may be appealed to the Deep King, but such appeals are rare and are generally discouraged.

## The Warden Council in Session

The warden council meets at regular intervals in the hall of the hold. The meetings are conducted in a formal manner, with the warden presiding and the elders seated according to their seniority. The meetings are open to the adult members of the hold, who may attend as observers but may not speak unless invited by the warden. The agenda of the meeting is set by the warden, and the elders discuss the matters that are brought before them before the warden announces the decisions.

The decisions of the warden council are made by the warden after consultation with the elders. The warden may accept or reject the advice of the elders, but a warden who consistently ignores the advice of the elders risks losing the confidence of the community and the support of the king. The elders serve as a check on the warden's authority, and the warden who governs without the consent of the elders risks finding the hold ungovernable.

## The Warden Councils and the Deep King

The warden councils exercise their authority under the authority of the Deep King, and they are subject to the supervision of the royal officials at Karhold. The councils are required to submit regular reports to the king on the state of the mines, the condition of the halls and roads, and the administration of justice. The royal officials inspect the holds at regular intervals, and a council that is found to be mismanaging its affairs may be dissolved by the king and replaced with interim administrators.

The relationship between the warden councils and the Deep King is one of mutual dependence. The councils administer the local affairs of the holds on behalf of the king, and they collect the taxes and contributions that fund the royal administration and the defence of the realm. The king, for his part, provides the legal framework within which the councils operate, enforces their decisions when necessary, and provides the protection that is essential for the security of the holds. The arrangement has endured for centuries, and the warden council system of the Karhold Realm is regarded as one of the most effective systems of local administration in the dwarven world.

The Deep King also maintains a corps of royal agents known as the King's Surveyors, who travel through the holds and report directly to the king on the state of the realm. The Surveyors have the authority to inspect any mine, forge, or hall in the realm, and to examine the records and accounts of the warden councils. The Surveyors are a check on the wardens and elders, and their reports provide the king with the information necessary to govern the realm effectively.

## The Warden Councils and the Mining Rights

The allocation of mining rights is one of the most important functions of the warden councils. The veins of ore that run through the mountain spine are the foundation of the realm's wealth, and the rights to exploit them are carefully regulated. The councils maintain a register of mining claims, which records the location, extent, and ownership of each claim. The claims are allocated to families of the hold according to their seniority and their contribution to the maintenance of the hold's forges and halls.

The councils also regulate the working conditions in the mines, setting the standards for safety, ventilation, and the drainage of water. The mines of the Karhold Realm are among the deepest in the known world, and the work is demanding and dangerous. The councils enforce the safety regulations rigorously, for the loss of a skilled miner is a serious blow to the productivity of a hold. The councils also manage the distribution of the tools and equipment that the miners require, and they maintain the stocks of timber and other materials that are necessary for the shoring and ventilation of the mine workings.

## The Warden Councils in the Life of the Realm

The warden councils are the institutions through which the dwarven communities of the Karhold Realm govern themselves and manage their resources. The councils provide a framework for local administration that is both effective and accountable, and they are the institution through which the ordinary dwarves of the realm participate in the management of their communities. The councils have sustained the prosperity of the holds for centuries, and the system of coordinated management of the mines, forges, and halls that they have developed is a model of dwarven governance that has been studied and admired by the other peoples of the known world.

The councils are also the forum in which the traditions and values of the dwarven people are preserved and transmitted. The elders of the council are the keepers of the hold's history and the guardians of its customs. The stories of the founding of the hold, the deeds of its heroes, and the decisions of its past councils are preserved in the memories of the elders and are recited at the gatherings of the community. The warden councils are thus not only instruments of administration but also institutions of cultural continuity, and their role in the preservation of dwarven tradition is as important as their role in the management of the realm's resources.
