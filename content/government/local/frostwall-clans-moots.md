## The Assemblies of the Deep Interior Clans

The Frostwall Clan Moots are the assemblies of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), the dwarven clans of the deep interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The moots are the primary political institutions of the clans, responsible for the coordination of defence, the management of mining operations, the resolution of disputes between clans, and the preservation of the traditions and laws that bind the clans together. The moots are not a permanent governing body but a periodic assembly of the chiefs and elders of the clans, convened at regular intervals and at times of crisis.

The moots are held at [Grimhold](/content/location/settlement/city/grimhold), the principal fortress-city of the clans, situated at the heart of the deep interior. [Grimhold](/content/location/settlement/city/grimhold) is carved into the rock of a great peak that dominates the surrounding ranges, and its fortress is among the most formidable in the dwarven world. The moot hall is a vast chamber deep within the fortress, its walls lined with the shields and banners of the clans and its ceiling supported by pillars carved from the living rock.

## The Convening of the Moot

The principal moot of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) convenes twice each year: the spring moot, held after the winter snows have receded and the passes have become passable, and the autumn moot, held before the winter snows close the passes and isolate the interior from the coast. The spring moot is the more important of the two, for it is the occasion at which the plans for the coming season are made, the allocation of mining rights and grazing lands is agreed, and the arrangements for collective defence are confirmed.

The moots are convened by the council of clan chiefs, which meets at Grimhold to determine the date and agenda of each moot. The chiefs are the hereditary leaders of the individual clans, and the council is the body that coordinates the affairs of the clans between the moots. When the council determines that a moot is necessary, the chiefs send word to their clans by the [Frostwall Fire Signs](/content/language/pseudo/frostwall-fire-signs), a relay system of signal fires that can carry a message from the most remote settlements of the interior to Grimhold in a single night.

The moots are attended by the chiefs and elders of the clans, together with the warriors and craftmasters who have particular expertise in the matters to be discussed. The attendance at a moot can number in the hundreds, for the Frostwall Clans include many families and settlements spread across the deep interior. The journey to Grimhold is long and demanding, and the representatives must travel through the passes that are the only routes through the mountain ranges, guarded by the clan warriors against the threats that lurk in the deeper valleys.

## The Moot in Session

The moot is conducted in a formal manner, with the chiefs seated according to the seniority of their clans and the elders seated behind them. The proceedings are presided over by the eldest chief of the council, known as the First Speaker, who guides the discussion and announces the decisions. The First Speaker is chosen by the council of chiefs and serves for a term of five years, and the office rotates among the clans to ensure that no single clan dominates the proceedings.

The moot begins with the recitation of the histories and traditions of the clans. The elders recite the stories of the founding of the clans, the deeds of their heroes, and the decisions of the past moots. The recitation is a reminder of the shared heritage of the clans and the obligations that bind them together, and it is followed by the formal business of the moot.

The business of the moot includes the discussion of defence, the allocation of mining rights and grazing lands, the resolution of disputes between clans, and the planning of collective action against the threats that face the deep interior. The discussions are conducted in an open manner, with any chief or elder who wishes to speak being heard. The decisions are made by consensus, and a decision that does not command the consent of all the clans represented is not regarded as binding.

## Defence and the Coordination of the Forces

The coordination of the defence of the deep interior is the most important function of the moots. The Frostwall Clans are the guardians of the most remote and demanding region of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and the defence of the interior against the threats that emerge from the deeper mountains and the cold steppes is a constant concern. The moots allocate the responsibility for the defence of the passes and the patrol of the high valleys among the clans, and they coordinate the deployment of the clan forces in response to threats.

The moot also reviews the state of the fortress of Grimhold and the other fortifications of the interior. The fortifications are maintained by the labour of the clans, and the moot allocates the contributions of each clan to the maintenance and improvement of the defences. The moot also reviews the training and equipment of the clan warriors and ensures that the standards of readiness are maintained.

The Frostwall Clans maintain a system of [Frostwall Fire Signs](/content/language/pseudo/frostwall-fire-signs) that can carry warnings and messages across the interior in a matter of hours. The moot reviews the operation of this system and ensures that the signal stations are maintained and the codes are kept current. The moot also coordinates the patrols of the high passes and the remote valleys, ensuring that the clans are aware of any movement of the wild peoples of the mountains or the [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines) of the cold steppes.

## Mining Rights and Resource Management

The allocation of mining rights is another important function of the moots. The deep interior of Morvind is rich in minerals, and the mining operations are the foundation of the economy of the clans. The moots allocate the rights to the veins of ore that run through the interior ranges among the clans, and they regulate the methods and intensity of the mining operations to ensure the sustainability of the resource.

The moots also coordinate the management of the grazing lands that sustain the herds of the clans. The interior ranges are too high and cold for agriculture, and the clans depend upon the herds of mountain goats and other animals for their meat, wool, and leather. The moots allocate the grazing lands among the clans and regulate the movement of the herds to prevent overgrazing and to ensure that the pastures have time to recover between the grazing seasons.

## The Resolution of Disputes

The resolution of disputes between the clans is a further function of the moots. Disputes over mining rights, grazing lands, water sources, and the boundaries of clan territories are common in the deep interior, and the moots provide a forum in which such disputes can be resolved by negotiation and compromise. The elders of the clans hear the arguments of the parties and propose solutions, and the decision of the moot is binding on all the clans.

The moot also hears appeals from the decisions of the chiefs and the councils of the individual clans. A member of a clan who is dissatisfied with a decision of their chief may appeal to the moot, and the moot may confirm, modify, or reverse the decision. The appeal is a rarely used but important safeguard against injustice, and the knowledge that the moot may review their decisions encourages the chiefs to act fairly and in accordance with the traditions of the clans.

## The Autumn Moot and the Winter Preparations

The autumn moot is the occasion at which the preparations for the winter are coordinated. The winter in the deep interior is long and harsh, and the clans must be prepared to survive for many months in isolation, for the passes close with the first snows and do not open again until the spring. The autumn moot reviews the stores of the clans, ensures that each has sufficient food, fuel, and other supplies to last through the winter, and coordinates the distribution of resources among the clans that have less than they need.

The autumn moot also reviews the condition of the passes and the roads and determines which sections must be cleared before the snows close them for the season. The work of clearing the passes and maintaining the roads is allocated among the clans, and the moot sets the schedule for the completion of the work before the winter sets in.

## The Significance of the Moots

The Frostwall Clan Moots are one of the most distinctive political institutions in the dwarven world. The assemblies combine the traditions of hereditary chieftainship with the principles of consensus and collective action, and they have sustained the dwarven presence in the most remote and demanding region of Morvind for many centuries. The moots are the forum in which the clans negotiate their shared interests and resolve their differences, and they are the foundation of the unity that enables the clans to defend the interior against the threats that surround them.

The moots are also the institution through which the traditions and laws of the Frostwall Clans are preserved and renewed. The recitation of the histories and the discussion of the customs at each moot ensure that the knowledge of the clans is transmitted from one generation to the next. The decisions of the moots are recorded in the oral traditions of the clans and are cited as precedent in future deliberations, and the accumulation of precedent across the centuries has created a body of law and custom that is as detailed and comprehensive as any written code.
