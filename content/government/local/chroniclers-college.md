## The Body of Records

The Chroniclers' College is the body that maintains the archives and the records of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), and the college is the principal historiographical institution of the island communities of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The college is composed of the chroniclers who serve the individual islands of the confederation, and the chroniclers are appointed by the assemblies of the islands for their knowledge of the traditions and their skill in the compilation of the records. The chronicler notes that the college is regarded by the islanders as the keeper of the memory of the confederation, and the college is held in high esteem by every magistrate and every elder of the member islands.

The college is the seat of the high chronicler, the chief magistrate of the college, and the high chronicler is the principal keeper of the central archives and the principal officer of the college in the conduct of the relations with the foreign princes whose representatives visit the isles. The high chronicler is elected by the assembly of the confederation for a term of nine years, and the high chronicler is responsible for the maintenance of the central archives, the publication of the chronicles of the confederation, and the conduct of the relations with the chroniclers of the other nations. The chronicler notes that the present high chronicler is [Marisel](/content/people/notable/marisel), and the high chronicler's tenure of the office has been marked by a substantial extension of the archives and by the publication of a comprehensive chronicle of the isles.

## Origins in the Earlier Tradition

The Chroniclers' College traces its foundation to the earlier tradition of the maritime chroniclers who served the island communities of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) in the generations before the confederation was established. The tradition is associated by the chroniclers of the college with the name of [Vaelen](/content/people/historical/vaelen), whose work in compiling the natural history of the known world established the method of systematic observation that the chroniclers of the isles have adopted for the compilation of their own records. The chronicler notes that Vaelen is remembered by the college as a predecessor rather than as a founder, and the college is content that the connection between the two traditions should be remembered without being claimed.

The first college was a small chamber in which the chroniclers of the principal island met to compare their records and to compile the chronicle of the confederation. The chroniclers' successors added a second chamber and then a third, and the college grew in the measure of the records that the chroniclers brought to it and the number of islands that were admitted to the confederation. The chronicler notes that the college has been enlarged several times in the centuries that have followed its foundation, and the enlargements are recorded in the register of the college as the most significant events in the history of the institution.

## The Central Archives

The archives of the college are housed in a building that adjoins the hall of the assembly on the principal island, and the archives contain the records of the confederation from its foundation to the present day. The records include the treaties that the confederation has negotiated with the foreign princes, the accounts of the voyages that the islanders have conducted upon the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), and the chronicles that the chroniclers have compiled of the events of the confederation. The chronicler notes that the archives are regarded by the college as the heart of its work, and the archives are the place to which every chronicler returns at the close of a voyage or a circuit of the islands.

The high chronicler is responsible for the maintenance of the archives and for the publication of the chronicles that record the events of the confederation, and the work of the high chronicler is the foundation of the historical record of the isles. The chroniclers of the individual islands send their records to the central archives at the end of every season, and the records are entered in the general register of the college by the chroniclers of the staff. The chronicler notes that the general register is among the most complete of the records of any political entity of the known world, and the general register is the measure of the college's reputation for careful administration.

## The Chronicle of the Isles

The most substantial of the publications of the present high chronicler is the comprehensive chronicle of the isles that has been published under her direction. The chronicle is a work of many volumes that sets forth the history of the confederation from its foundation to the present day, and the chronicle has been compiled from the records that are preserved in the archives and from the oral traditions that are maintained by the elders of the islands. The chronicler notes that the chronicle is the standard reference of the students of the maritime history of the known world, and the work has been received with acclaim by the scholars of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the other academies of the continent.

The chronicle has been published in several instalments, and the instalments are distributed to the chroniclers of the individual islands and to the scholars who visit the isles from the foreign academies. The work is being continued under the direction of the high chronicler, and the remaining volumes of the chronicle are expected to be published over the course of the next several years. The chronicler notes that the publication of the chronicle is one of the most significant events in the intellectual life of the isles, and the work is a credit to the college and to the confederation that the college serves.

## Composition and Officers

The college is composed of the chroniclers of the individual islands of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) and the chroniclers of the staff who serve the central archives. The chroniclers of the individual islands are appointed by the assemblies of the islands for terms of seven years, and the chroniclers are eligible for re-appointment without limit. The chroniclers of the staff are appointed by the high chronicler and hold office at the pleasure of the college, and the chroniclers of the staff are responsible for the daily maintenance of the archives, the correspondence of the college, and the assistance of the high chronicler in the conduct of the relations with the foreign princes.

The high chronicler is elected by the assembly of the confederation for a term of nine years, and the high chronicler must be a chronicler of at least fifteen years' standing who has been commended by the chroniclers of the individual islands. The high chronicler is the chief magistrate of the college, and the high chronicler is responsible for the maintenance of the central archives, the publication of the chronicles of the confederation, and the conduct of the relations with the foreign princes whose representatives visit the isles. The chronicler notes that the high chronicler is the principal officer of the assembly in the conduct of the relations with the foreign princes in matters that touch upon the records and the chronicles.

## The Assembly and the Confederation

The college maintains a regular correspondence with the assemblies of the individual islands of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), and the assemblies are the source of the chroniclers who serve the college. The high chronicler reports to the assembly of the confederation at the close of every season upon the state of the archives, upon the publication of the chronicles, and upon the relations of the college with the foreign princes. The chronicler notes that the assembly is regarded by the college as the principal patron of the institution, and the assembly is the source of the revenues that sustain the college's work.

The college is also the principal channel of communication between the assembly of the confederation and the foreign princes in matters that touch upon the records and the chronicles. The ambassadors of the foreign princes are received by the high chronicler upon their arrival at the principal island, and the high chronicler is responsible for the conduct of the negotiations that take place during the visit. The high chronicler is also responsible for the maintenance of the seals of the confederation, and the treaties that are negotiated by the assembly are sealed with the seals that are in the custody of the high chronicler. The chronicler notes that the high chronicler's influence in the conduct of these relations is considerable, and the high chronicler is the measure of the college's reputation among the foreign princes.

## The College and the Foreign Academies

The college maintains a regular correspondence with the chroniclers and the archivists of the foreign princes, and the letters that are exchanged are preserved in the archives as a record of the intellectual relations of the confederation. The scholars of the foreign academies have corresponded with [Marisel](/content/people/notable/marisel) upon the matters that concern the history and the records of the isles, and the high chronicler is the principal channel of communication between the college and the academies of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the other principal cities of the continent. The chronicler notes that the correspondence is the measure of the college's openness to the wider world, and the college regards the correspondence as a privilege rather than a claim.

The college has also exchanged scholars with the academies of the continent, and the exchanged scholars are admitted to the central archives and to the conference of the chroniclers of the staff. The scholars who visit the archives speak well of the welcome that they receive at the hall of the college, and the welcome is the measure of the college's reputation for hospitality. The chronicler notes that the exchanges are recorded in the register of the college, and the register is the source of the college's knowledge of the wider world.

The college is the successor of the earlier tradition of the maritime chroniclers, and the tradition is associated by the chroniclers of the college with the name of [Vaelen](/content/people/historical/vaelen). The chronicler notes that Vaelen is remembered by the college as the scholar who established the method of systematic observation that the chroniclers of the isles have adopted, and the method is the foundation of the college's work. The college maintains a small exhibit of the manuscripts that the predecessor tradition has preserved, and the exhibit is the source of the college's knowledge of its own beginnings.

The chronicler notes that the connection between the college and the predecessor tradition is held in respect without being claimed, and the college is content that the connection should be remembered without being pressed. The predecessor tradition is regarded by the college as the foundation of its method, and the college regards the predecessor tradition as a privilege rather than a claim.
