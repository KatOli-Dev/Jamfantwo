## The Keeper of the Confederation's Memory

The Chroniclers' College maintains the archives and records of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) and is the principal historiographical institution of the island communities of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The college is composed of chroniclers who serve the individual islands of the confederation, appointed by the island assemblies for their knowledge of traditions and skill in compiling records. The college is regarded by the islanders as the keeper of the confederation's memory and is held in high esteem by every magistrate and elder of the member islands.

The college is the seat of the high chronicler, the chief magistrate and principal keeper of the central archives, who also conducts relations with foreign princes whose representatives visit the isles. The high chronicler is elected by the assembly of the confederation for a term of nine years and is responsible for maintaining the central archives, publishing the chronicles of the confederation, and managing relations with chroniclers of other nations. The present high chronicler is [Marisel Auric](/content/people/notable/marisel-auric), whose tenure has been marked by a substantial extension of the archives and the publication of a comprehensive chronicle of the isles.

## The Maritime Chroniclers and Vaelen Eldreth

The Chroniclers' College traces its foundation to the earlier tradition of maritime chroniclers who served the island communities of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) in the generations before the confederation was established. This tradition is associated with [Vaelen Eldreth](/content/people/historical/vaelen-eldreth), whose work compiling the natural history of the known world established the method of systematic observation that the chroniclers of the isles have adopted for their own records. Vaelen Eldreth is remembered by the college as a predecessor rather than a founder, and the college is content that the connection between the two traditions should be remembered without being claimed.

The first college was a small chamber in which the chroniclers of the principal island met to compare records and compile the chronicle of the confederation. Successive generations added a second chamber and then a third, and the college grew in proportion to the records brought to it and the number of islands admitted to the confederation. The college has been enlarged several times in the centuries following its foundation, with each enlargement recorded in the register as among the most significant events in the institution's history.

## The Building Adjoining the Assembly Hall

The archives of the college are housed in a building adjoining the hall of the assembly on the principal island, containing the records of the confederation from its foundation to the present day. These include treaties negotiated with foreign princes, accounts of voyages conducted upon the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), and the chronicles compiled by the chroniclers of the confederation's events. The archives are regarded by the college as the heart of its work, the place to which every chronicler returns at the close of a voyage or a circuit of the islands.

The high chronicler is responsible for maintaining the archives and publishing the chronicles that record the events of the confederation. The chroniclers of the individual islands send their records to the central archives at the end of every season, where they are entered into the general register by the staff chroniclers. The general register is among the most complete records of any political entity in the known world and is the measure of the college's reputation for careful administration.

## The Comprehensive History of the Isles

The most substantial publication of the present high chronicler is the comprehensive chronicle of the isles published under her direction. This work of many volumes sets forth the history of the confederation from its foundation to the present day, compiled from the records preserved in the archives and the oral traditions maintained by the elders of the islands. The chronicle is the standard reference for students of the maritime history of the known world and has been received with acclaim by scholars of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and other academies of the continent.

The chronicle has been published in several instalments, distributed to the chroniclers of the individual islands and to visiting scholars from foreign academies. The work continues under the direction of the high chronicler, with remaining volumes expected to be published over the next several years. The publication of the chronicle is among the most significant events in the intellectual life of the isles and is a credit to the college and the confederation it serves.

## The Island Chroniclers and the High Chronicler

The college is composed of the chroniclers of the individual islands of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) and the staff chroniclers who serve the central archives. The chroniclers of the individual islands are appointed by the island assemblies for terms of seven years and are eligible for re-appointment without limit. The staff chroniclers are appointed by the high chronicler and hold office at the pleasure of the college, responsible for the daily maintenance of the archives, the correspondence of the college, and assisting the high chronicler in relations with foreign princes.

The high chronicler is elected by the assembly of the confederation for a term of nine years and must be a chronicler of at least fifteen years' standing who has been commended by the chroniclers of the individual islands. The high chronicler serves as chief magistrate of the college, responsible for the central archives, the publication of the chronicles, and relations with foreign princes. The high chronicler is also the principal officer of the assembly in matters touching the records and chronicles.

## Correspondence with the Island Assemblies

The college maintains regular correspondence with the assemblies of the individual islands of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), which are the source of the chroniclers who serve the college. The high chronicler reports to the assembly of the confederation at the close of every season on the state of the archives, the publication of the chronicles, and the college's relations with foreign princes. The assembly is regarded by the college as its principal patron and the source of the revenues that sustain its work.

The college is also the principal channel of communication between the assembly of the confederation and foreign princes in matters touching the records and chronicles. Ambassadors of foreign princes are received by the high chronicler upon their arrival at the principal island, and the high chronicler is responsible for conducting negotiations during the visit. The high chronicler also maintains the seals of the confederation, and treaties negotiated by the assembly are sealed with the seals in the high chronicler's custody. The high chronicler's influence in these relations is considerable and is the measure of the college's reputation among foreign princes.

## Exchanges with the Continental Academies

The college maintains regular correspondence with chroniclers and archivists of foreign princes, with the letters preserved in the archives as a record of the confederation's intellectual relations. Scholars of foreign academies have corresponded with [Marisel Auric](/content/people/notable/marisel-auric) on matters concerning the history and records of the isles, and the high chronicler is the principal channel of communication between the college and the academies of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and other principal cities of the continent. This correspondence reflects the college's openness to the wider world, a privilege rather than a claim.

The college has also exchanged scholars with the academies of the continent, who are admitted to the central archives and the conference of the staff chroniclers. Visiting scholars speak well of the welcome they receive at the hall of the college, reflecting its reputation for hospitality. These exchanges are recorded in the register of the college.

## The Exhibit of Earlier Manuscripts

The college is the successor of the earlier tradition of maritime chroniclers associated with [Vaelen Eldreth](/content/people/historical/vaelen-eldreth), who established the method of systematic observation that the chroniclers of the isles have adopted. This method is the foundation of the college's work. The college maintains a small exhibit of manuscripts preserved by the predecessor tradition, which provides knowledge of its own beginnings.

The connection between the college and the predecessor tradition is held in respect without being claimed. The predecessor tradition is regarded as the foundation of the college's method, a privilege rather than a claim. The exhibit of manuscripts is open to the chroniclers of the individual islands and to visiting scholars.

## The Foundation of the Historical Record of the Confederation

The Chroniclers' College maintains the archives and records of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) and is the principal historiographical institution of the island communities of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The college is the seat of the high chronicler, the central archives, the chronicle of the isles, and the conference of the chroniclers of the individual islands. The college has been one of the quiet foundations of the intellectual life of the isles and is held in high esteem by every magistrate and elder of the member islands. The work of the college is the foundation of the historical record of the confederation, and the chronicle compiled under the direction of the high chronicler is the standard reference for students of the maritime history of the known world.
