## The Guild of Wine and Ale Merchants

The Vintners and Cellarers is the standing guild of the merchants and craftsmen who handle the wine of the inland valleys and the ale of the river towns of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands), the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast), and the lower reaches of the Greyring. The Council regulates the trade in wine and ale, keeps the standards of the vintners and brewers, and adjudicates disputes among the merchants, innkeepers, and tavern-keepers of the region. The Council is composed of the masters of the principal vintner-houses and the head brewers of the principal towns, meeting in the hall of the vintners at [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) four times each year.

The Council is one of the older merchant guilds of the cold north, its foundation ascribed to the accord the vintners of the inland valleys made with the brewers of the river towns more than two centuries ago. The accord established the standards of wine and ale, the rules of trade between the vintners and brewers, and the procedures for adjudicating disputes. The accord has been revised from time to time, with revisions entered in the register of the Council and confirmed by the magistrates of the principal towns.

## The Master, Wardens, and Sworn Members

The Council is composed of a master, two wardens, a clerk, and the sworn members who represent the vintner-houses and breweries. The master is elected by the members for a term of two years and must be a vintner or brewer of at least fifteen years' standing. The wardens are elected for the same term and represent the vintners and the brewers respectively. The clerk is appointed by the master and the wardens and holds office at the pleasure of the Council, responsible for the registers, correspondence, and accounts.

The sworn members are the masters of the vintner-houses that hold at least a thousand barrels of capacity, the head brewers of the breweries that produce at least five hundred barrels in a year, and the innkeepers who hold a charter from the Council to keep a public house. A vintner or brewer who falls below the threshold may continue as a sworn member for life, but no new member is admitted who does not meet the threshold. The threshold has been raised from time to time as the trade has grown, a subject of long debate in the Council.

## Colour, Clarity, Fragrance, and the Barrel Seal

The Council maintains the standards of wine and ale sold within its jurisdiction, enforced by the wardens and sworn members. The wine is judged by colour, clarity, fragrance, and taste, the standard set by the tasting conducted each year at the autumn meeting. The ale is judged by colour, fragrance, the head it carries, and taste, the standard set by the tasting conducted each year at the spring meeting. A wine or ale that fails the standard may not be sold under the seal of the Council, and the seller is fined.

The Council also maintains the standards of the vessels in which wine and ale are kept. The barrel must be of oak or beech, and the hoops must be of iron or well-tanned willow. The barrel must be tight, tested by the wardens at the time of sealing. The seal of the Council is set upon the barrel that passes the test, the guarantee that the barrel holds wine or ale of the standard. A barrel bearing the seal of the Council may be sold throughout the region without further inspection, and the seal has been the foundation of the reputation of the vintners and brewers of the cold north.

## The Exchange Between Vintners and Brewers

The Council regulates the trade between vintners and brewers, and the regulations have been the source of much of the prosperity of the inland valleys and river towns. The vintner sells his wine to the brewer, who blends it with his ale to produce the small beer that is the common drink of the inland towns. The brewer pays the vintner in coin and in ale, the proportion set by the Council at the autumn meeting. The proportion has been the subject of long debate, and the Council has revised it from time to time to reflect the harvest and demand.

The Council also regulates the trade between brewers and innkeepers, who are bound by the charter the Council has issued. The charter sets the price at which the innkeeper may sell ale and small beer and the quality that must be served. An innkeeper who violates the charter is brought before the Council, which has the power to revoke the charter. Revocations have been rare, for the innkeepers are jealous of their charters, and each revocation has been the subject of much local controversy.

## The Hearing and the Charter Powers

The Council adjudicates disputes among vintners, brewers, and innkeepers, and the adjudication is final within the trade. The dispute is brought before the master and the wardens, who hear the parties and the witnesses, then retire to consider the matter and return with a judgement entered in the register. The judgement may be appealed to the full Council at the next quarterly meeting, which may confirm, modify, or reverse the judgement.

The Council has the power to compel the attendance of parties and witnesses and to fine any party found to have violated the standards or regulations. The Council does not have the power to imprison; a party who refuses to pay the fine is reported to the magistrate of the town. The magistrates have always supported the Council in the collection of fines, recognising that the Council regulates a trade essential to the prosperity of the region.

## The Hall of the Vintners at Vassholm

The Council meets in the hall of the vintners at Vassholm, a substantial building of stone and timber erected more than a century ago by the subscriptions of the members. The hall contains the council chamber, the offices of the clerk, the cellars in which the standard wines and ales are kept, and the archives in which the records of the Council are preserved. The archives are open to the sworn members and to the magistrates and are among the most complete records of any guild in the known world.

The records of the Council include the register of the sworn members, the register of the judgements, the register of the standards, and the register of the seals. The registers are kept by the clerk, responsible for the accuracy of the entries. The registers have been consulted by magistrates and historians in many a dispute and are the foundation of the Council's reputation for fair dealing and careful administration.

## The Quiet Foundation of the Inland Valleys and River Towns

The Vintners and Cellarers is one of the most useful and least controversial of the local governing bodies of the known world. Its officers and members are drawn from the vintner-houses and breweries of the inland valleys and river towns, and its jurisdiction covers a trade essential to the prosperity and comfort of the people of the cold north. The Council has maintained the standards of wine and ale, regulated the trade between vintners and brewers, and adjudicated the disputes that arise among merchants and innkeepers of the region. The Council has been one of the quiet foundations of the prosperity of the inland valleys and river towns and is held in high esteem by every magistrate and every merchant of the region.
