## The Governing Body of the Sahrani Port Alliance

The [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) Council is the governing body of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league), the alliance of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coastal city-states of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The council is the primary political institution of the league, responsible for the coordination of trade, navigation, and defence among the member city-states. The council is composed of representatives from each member city, and its decisions are binding on all members of the league. The council's governance is built upon the principles of representation, collective action, and the pursuit of the common commercial interest that unites the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast.

The council meets in the city of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), which serves as the de facto capital of the league. The council hall is a substantial building situated near the harbour, its architecture reflecting the wealth and refinement of the Sahrani coast. The council chamber is a spacious room with high ceilings and windows that overlook the harbour and the sea. A great table of polished stone, carved from the same marble that lines the floors of the [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) temples, occupies the centre of the chamber, and the representatives sit according to the seniority of their cities around the table.

## Composition and Representation

The Northern Coast League Council is composed of representatives from each member city-state. The number of representatives from each city is fixed by the league's charter: each member city sends three representatives to the council. The representatives are chosen by each member city according to its own customs, and the methods of selection reflect the diversity of governance among the member cities.

The representatives serve for a term of four years and may be re-appointed without limit. The representatives are expected to attend every council session, and a representative who misses two consecutive sessions without good cause may be replaced by the member city. The council maintains a register of representatives, and the credentials of each representative are verified at the opening of each session.

The methods of selection of representatives vary among the member cities according to their internal governance. In [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), the representatives are chosen by the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council), the governing body of the city, from among the most experienced and respected members of the merchant and civic community. The representatives of Khalisar are typically drawn from the elected officials and appointed elders who have served the city with distinction. In [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), the representatives are appointed by the merchant council that governs the republic. In [Taretha](/content/location/settlement/city/taretha), the representatives are chosen by the elders of the city's scholarly and commercial families. In the smaller member cities, the representatives are elected by popular vote of all free adult citizens.

## The Presiding Officer and Administration

The council elects from among its members a presiding officer, known as the Warden of the Coast, who serves for a term of two years and is responsible for the conduct of the council's business and the carrying out of its decisions. The Warden is chosen from among the representatives by a majority vote, and the office rotates among the member cities to ensure that no single city dominates the governance of the league. The rotation follows a fixed schedule, and each member city holds the office for a single term before passing it to the next city.

The Warden is assisted by a body of administrators who manage the day-to-day affairs of the league. The chief administrator is the Clerk of the League, who maintains the records of the council's proceedings, the registers of members and contributions, and the archives of the league. The Clerk is appointed by the council and serves for a term of five years. The Clerk is assisted by a staff of scribes, accountants, and messengers who manage the correspondence, accounts, and communications of the league.

The administration of the league also includes a number of officials responsible for specific functions. The Master of the Harbours oversees the maintenance of the harbour facilities in the member cities and coordinates the allocation of berths and the provision of services to visiting vessels. The Master of the Pilots manages the pilotage services that guide vessels through the approaches to the Sahrani harbours. The Master of the Fleet oversees the maintenance and deployment of the league's collective fleet. The Master of the Tariffs coordinates the collection of the unified port tariffs that apply in all member harbours.

## Functions and Powers

The council's primary function is the coordination of trade among the member city-states. The council establishes the unified port tariffs that apply in all member harbours, ensuring that no member city can undercut the others by offering lower fees or preferential treatment to certain merchants. The council also establishes the standards for [weights and measures](/content/economy/weights-and-measures), the inspection of cargoes, and the documentation required for the movement of goods through the league's harbours. The standardisation of these practices across the member cities has reduced the administrative burden on merchants and eliminated the advantage that individual cities might otherwise gain by undercutting each other.

The council also coordinates the defence of the Sahrani coast through the maintenance of the collective fleet. The fleet is composed of vessels contributed by the member cities in proportion to their share of the trade, and the fleet is commanded by an admiral appointed by the council. The admiral serves for a term of three years, and the office rotates among the member cities to prevent any single city from dominating the naval command. The fleet patrols the trade routes of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the coast, and its primary function is the suppression of piracy, which has been a recurring problem in the waters off the Sahrani coast.

The council also coordinates the maintenance of the navigation aids that are essential for the safety of the trade routes. The lighthouses, beacons, and buoys that line the Sahrani coast are maintained by the league, and the council allocates the contributions of the member cities for this purpose. The league also maintains a system of coastal watchtowers spaced at intervals along the entire Sahrani shore, each staffed by lookouts who signal the approach of vessels by polished-metal mirrors during the day and oil lamps at night. This signalling network can relay a message from [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir) to [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) in the time it takes a fast galley to sail a fraction of that distance, and it has proven invaluable for both commercial intelligence and early warning of raiding fleets.

The council has the power to levy contributions on the member cities to fund the activities of the league. The contributions are assessed annually on the basis of the volume of trade passing through each member city's harbour, and the council may adjust the assessments if the circumstances require. The council also has the power to negotiate treaties with external powers on behalf of the league, and any treaty negotiated by the council is binding on all member cities.

## Decision-Making and the Council in Session

The council reaches its decisions through a process of deliberation and voting. Each representative has one vote, and decisions are made by a simple majority of those present, except for matters of particular importance, which require a two-thirds majority. The matters that require a two-thirds majority include the admission of new members, the amendment of the league's charter, the declaration of war, and the negotiation of treaties with external powers.

The council meets in regular session three times each year. The spring session is held in Khalisar, the summer session in [Taretha](/content/location/settlement/city/taretha), and the autumn session in [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra). This rotation of the meeting place among the larger member cities is a deliberate feature of the league's governance, designed to ensure that each major city has the opportunity to host the council and that the representatives become familiar with the circumstances of the different member cities.

The sessions are conducted in a formal manner, with the Warden presiding and the Clerk recording the proceedings. The representatives speak in turn, and the discussions are conducted in the common tongue of the Sahrani trade, which all representatives are expected to speak fluently. The sessions are open to the officials of the league and to the representatives of the member cities, and the proceedings are published for the information of the merchants and citizens of the league.

## The Council and the Inland Trade

The council also coordinates the relationship between the league and the inland powers of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The trade between the coast and the interior, managed through the caravan routes that cross the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), is a vital component of the Sahrani economy. The council negotiates with the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) and the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) to ensure the security and efficiency of the caravan routes, and it coordinates the flow of goods between the coastal harbours and the caravans that travel to the interior.

The council also maintains a permanent committee that deals with inland trade, composed of representatives from the member cities that have the most extensive connections with the interior. The committee meets in Khalisar between the regular council sessions and reports its recommendations to the full council. The committee's work has been instrumental in maintaining the flow of trade between the coast and the interior, and in resolving the disputes that occasionally arise between the coastal cities and the inland powers.

## The Council and the Law of the Sea

The council also exercises a judicial function through the league's admiralty court, which sits permanently in Khalisar. The admiralty court hears disputes involving maritime contracts, insurance claims, collisions at sea, and the salvage of vessels and cargoes. The court's decisions are binding on all member cities and are enforced by the collective authority of the league.

The admiralty court of the Northern Coast League has developed a body of [maritime law](/content/law/maritime-law) that is closely aligned with the commercial practices of the Sahrani coast. The court's procedures are efficient and its decisions are predictable, and the court is respected by merchants and mariners throughout the known world. The existence of the court has contributed significantly to the confidence that merchants place in the trade of the Sahrani coast, for they know that their contracts will be enforced and their disputes resolved by a fair and experienced tribunal.

The council has also established a system of arbitration that allows merchants to resolve disputes without recourse to the formal procedures of the admiralty court. The arbitrators are experienced merchants appointed by the council, and their decisions are binding on the parties to the arbitration. The arbitration system is popular among the merchants of the Sahrani coast, for it offers a faster and less expensive means of resolving disputes than the formal court proceedings.
