## The Courts and Councils of the Interior Valley

The Valdmark Holding Courts are the local courts and councils of the river valley settlements of [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark), the human realm of the interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The holding courts are the primary institutions of local governance in the valley communities, responsible for the administration of justice, the management of the river and the irrigation system, the regulation of the agricultural economy, and the maintenance of the roads and bridges that connect the valley settlements.

The holding court system of Valdmark is a product of the agricultural character of the realm. The valley of the principal river of the Morvind interior is the most fertile region of the continent, and the settlements that line its banks are primarily agricultural communities whose prosperity depends upon the management of the river and the irrigation system. The holding courts were established in the early centuries of settlement, when the communities of the valley first organised themselves into bodies for the management of the water resources and the resolution of disputes over land and water rights.

## Composition and Appointment

Each holding court of Valdmark is composed of a variable number of members, depending on the size and importance of the settlement. The courts are composed of the local magistrates, the elders of the community, and the representatives of the freeholders. The magistrates are appointed by the lord of Valdmark and serve at his pleasure. The elders are chosen by the community and serve for life, subject to good behaviour and the confirmation of the lord. The representatives are elected by the freeholders of the settlement and serve for a term of three years.

The magistrate is the principal official of the holding court, responsible for the administration of justice, the management of the court's affairs, and the execution of the lord's orders. The magistrate is appointed by the lord of Valdmark from among the most experienced and respected members of the community. The magistrate must have served as an elder or a representative for a minimum period before appointment, and the appointment is based on merit and experience.

The elders are chosen by the community from among its most experienced and respected members. The selection is made at a gathering of the adult members of the settlement, and the candidates are proposed and discussed before a vote is taken. The elders serve for life, and they are expected to provide the magistrate with advice and counsel on the management of the court's affairs.

The representatives are elected by the freeholders of the settlement. The freeholders are those who hold land or a substantial leasehold within the territory of the settlement. The elections are held at a gathering of the freeholders, and any freeholder may stand for election. The representatives serve for a term of three years and may be re-elected without limit.

## Functions and Responsibilities

The primary function of the Valdmark Holding Courts is the administration of justice. The courts hear disputes between the inhabitants of the valley over land and water rights, the boundaries of fields and pastures, the inheritance of property, and the terms of contracts and agreements. The courts also deal with offences against the laws of the realm, including theft, damage to property, and violence. The decisions of the holding courts may be appealed to the council of the valley settlements at [Valdheim](/content/location/settlement/city/valdheim), but such appeals are rare and are generally discouraged.

The management of the river and the irrigation system is another important function of the holding courts. The river is the lifeblood of the valley, and the management of the irrigation channels that distribute its waters to the fields is essential for the agricultural prosperity of the realm. The holding courts coordinate the opening and closing of the sluices that control the flow of water, the maintenance of the dykes and channels that direct the water to the fields, and the resolution of disputes over the allocation of water among the freeholders.

The regulation of the agricultural economy is a further function of the holding courts. The courts oversee the operation of the markets and the trading establishments of the settlements, enforce the standards of weights and measures, and regulate the prices of essential goods. The courts also manage the winter storehouses that are essential for the survival of the communities through the harsh Morvind winters. The storehouses are filled each autumn with preserved grain, dried meat, and salted vegetables sufficient to sustain the population through the worst winters, and the courts maintain a register of each settlement's stores to ensure equitable distribution.

## The Holding Court in Session

The holding court meets at regular intervals in the court hall of the settlement. The meetings are conducted in a formal manner, with the magistrate presiding and the elders and representatives seated according to their seniority. The meetings are open to the freeholders of the settlement, who may attend as observers and may address the court during the period reserved for public comment.

The business of the court includes the hearing of disputes and offences, the review of the condition of the irrigation system and the river defences, the assessment of the state of the winter storehouses, and the discussion of any matters affecting the welfare of the community. The court also receives the reports of the officials responsible for the management of the river, the roads, and the markets, and it issues the orders necessary for the maintenance of the infrastructure and the enforcement of the regulations.

The decisions of the holding court are made by the magistrate after consultation with the elders and representatives. The magistrate may accept or reject the advice of the elders and representatives, but a magistrate who consistently ignores the advice of the court risks losing the confidence of the community and the support of the lord of Valdmark.

## The Holding Courts and the Lord of Valdmark

The holding courts exercise their authority under the authority of the lord of Valdmark, and they are subject to the supervision of the lord's officials at Valdheim. The courts are required to submit regular reports to the lord on the state of the irrigation system, the condition of the roads and bridges, the administration of justice, and the management of the winter storehouses. The lord's officials inspect the valley settlements at regular intervals, and a court that is found to be failing in its duties may be dissolved by the lord and replaced with interim administrators.

The relationship between the holding courts and the lord of Valdmark is one of mutual dependence. The courts administer the local affairs of the valley settlements on behalf of the lord, and they maintain the infrastructure and the regulations that sustain the agricultural economy of the realm. The lord, for his part, provides the legal framework within which the courts operate, enforces their decisions when necessary, and provides the military support that is essential for the defence of the valley against external threats.

## The Holding Courts and the Water Rights

The allocation of water rights is one of the most important functions of the holding courts. The irrigation system of the valley is the foundation of the agricultural economy, and the rights to draw water from the river and the channels are carefully regulated. The holding courts maintain the register of water rights, which records the allocation of water to each freeholder according to the size and location of the lands.

The holding courts also manage the system of sluices and channels that distribute the water to the fields. The operation of the sluices is coordinated by the courts according to a schedule that takes account of the needs of the crops and the availability of water. The courts also maintain the dykes and channels that protect the settlements and fields from the periodic floods that can threaten the valley. The management of the water rights is a constant source of disputes, and the resolution of these disputes is one of the most time-consuming responsibilities of the holding courts.

## The Holding Courts and the Seasonal Agriculture

The holding courts also coordinate the seasonal activities of the agricultural communities. The courts set the schedule for the planting and harvesting, the opening and closing of the irrigation channels, and the movement of livestock between the valley pastures and the highland grazing lands. The courts also coordinate the storage of the harvest in the winter storehouses and the distribution of the stored supplies during the winter months.

The courts also manage the response to the challenges that periodically threaten the agricultural economy of the valley. The most serious of these challenges are the harsh winters that can damage crops and herds, the floods that can inundate the low-lying fields and settlements, and the droughts that can reduce the flow of the river and the productivity of the irrigation system. The courts coordinate the response to these challenges, mobilising the labour of the freeholders and the resources of the community to protect the fields and maintain the productivity of the valley.

## The Significance of the Holding Courts

The Valdmark Holding Courts are a distinctive and valued feature of the governance of the interior valley. The courts provide a framework for local administration that is both effective and responsive to the needs of the agricultural communities, and they are the institution through which the people of the valley manage their collective affairs and participate in the governance of the realm. The courts have sustained the agricultural prosperity of the valley for many centuries, and the system of coordinated management of the river, the irrigation system, and the winter storehouses is a model of local governance that has been studied and admired by the agricultural communities of other continents.
