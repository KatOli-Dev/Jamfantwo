## Overview

The Salt Court of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) Coast is the standing body of the salt-makers, salt-merchants, and magistrates of the salt pans who regulate the harvest, trade, and export of salt along the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip). The Court is the keeper of the standards of salt, the regulator of the salt pans, and the adjudicator of disputes among salt-makers, merchants, and the captains of vessels that carry salt to the markets of the world. The Court is composed of the master salt-makers, the sworn deputies of the salt pans, and the magistrates of the principal towns of the coast. The Court meets in the hall of the salt merchants at [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) four times each year.

The Court is one of the older local bodies of the warm coast, its foundation ascribed to the accord the salt-makers of the Sahrani coast made with the merchants of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) more than three centuries ago. The accord established the standards of salt, the seasons of the harvest, the rules of trade, and the procedures for adjudicating disputes. The accord has been revised from time to time, with revisions entered in the register of the Court and confirmed by the magistrates of the principal towns.

## Composition and Officers

The Court is composed of a master, two wardens, a clerk, and the sworn members who represent the salt pans and salt merchants. The master is elected by the sworn members for a term of three years and must be a salt-maker or salt-merchant of at least fifteen years' standing. The wardens are elected for the same term and represent the salt-makers and the merchants respectively. The clerk is appointed by the master and the wardens and holds office at the pleasure of the Court, responsible for the registers, correspondence, and accounts.

The sworn members are the masters of the salt pans that produce at least a thousand baskets of salt in a season, the sworn deputies of the smaller pans, and the heads of the salt-merchant houses of Khalisar and the other towns of the coast. A salt pan or merchant house that falls below the threshold may continue as a sworn member for life, but no new member is admitted who does not meet the threshold. The threshold has been raised from time to time as the trade has grown, a subject of long debate in the Court.

## Standards of the Salt

The Court maintains the standards of the salt harvested within its jurisdiction, enforced by the wardens and sworn members. The salt is judged by colour, grain, dryness, and taste, and the standard is set by the testing the Court conducts each year at the autumn meeting. The standard is entered in the register of the Court and is the reference by which every basket of salt is judged. A basket that fails the standard may not be sold under the seal of the Court, and the seller is fined.

The Court also maintains the standards of the baskets in which the salt is packed, which must be of a pattern approved by the wardens. The basket must be tight, tested by the wardens at the time of sealing. The seal of the Court is set upon the basket that passes the test, the guarantee that the basket holds salt of the standard. A basket bearing the seal of the Court may be sold throughout the region without further inspection, and the seal has been the foundation of the reputation of the salt of the Sahrani coast.

## Seasons of the Harvest

The Court regulates the harvest of the salt, and the regulations have been the source of much of the prosperity of the salt pans and towns of the coast. The harvest is opened in the late summer, when the pans have been flooded and the sun has evaporated the first of the water, and closed in the autumn, when the rains begin and the pans must be drained for the winter. The seasons are set by the master after consultation with the sworn members and entered in the register. The seasons are a careful compromise between the abundance of the sun and the need of the people, and they have been the subject of long debate in the Court.

The Court also regulates the labour of the harvest, with labourers licensed by the wardens. A licensed labourer may work in the salt pans during the harvest, bound by the regulations of the Court. The labourer must be paid in coin and in kind, the proportion set by the wardens at the beginning of every harvest. A labourer who violates the regulations is brought before the Court, which has the power to revoke the licence. Revocations have been rare, for the labourers are jealous of their licences, and each revocation has been the subject of much local controversy.

## The Trade in Salt

The Court regulates the trade in salt, and the regulations have been the source of much of the prosperity of the merchants of Khalisar and the other towns of the coast. The salt-maker sells his salt to the merchant, who sells it to the captains of the vessels that carry it to the markets of the world. The merchant pays the salt-maker in coin and in goods, the proportion set by the Court at the autumn meeting. The proportion has been the subject of long debate, and the Court has revised it from time to time to reflect the harvest and demand.

The Court also regulates the export, licensed by the master. A captain who wishes to carry salt from the Sahrani coast must apply to the master for a licence, which may be granted or refused as the Court directs. The captain must enter the cargo in the register of the Court and pay the small due imposed for the maintenance of the pans. The due has been stable for several generations and is one of the most carefully audited of the public revenues of the region.

## Adjudication of Disputes

The Court adjudicates disputes among the salt-makers, merchants, and captains, and the adjudication is final within the trade. The dispute is brought before the master and the wardens, who hear the parties and the witnesses, then retire to consider the matter and return with a judgement entered in the register. The judgement may be appealed to the full Court at the next quarterly meeting, which may confirm, modify, or reverse the judgement.

The Court has the power to compel the attendance of parties and witnesses and to fine any party found to have violated the standards or regulations. The Court does not have the power to imprison; a party who refuses to pay the fine is reported to the magistrate of the nearest town. The magistrates have always supported the Court in the collection of fines, recognising that the Court regulates a trade essential to the prosperity of the region.

## The Hall and the Records

The Court meets in the hall of the salt merchants at Khalisar, a substantial building of stone and timber erected more than a century ago by the subscriptions of the sworn members. The hall contains the council chamber, the offices of the clerk, a small yard in which the standard salts are kept, and the archives in which the records of the Court are preserved. The archives are open to the sworn members and to the magistrates and are among the most complete records of any local body in the known world.

The records of the Court include the register of the sworn members, the register of the judgements, the register of the standards, and the register of the exports. The registers are kept by the clerk, responsible for the accuracy of the entries. The registers have been consulted by magistrates and historians in many a dispute and are the foundation of the Court's reputation for fair dealing and careful administration.

## Significance

The Sahrani Salt Court is one of the most useful and least controversial of the local governing bodies of the known world. Its officers and members are drawn from the salt pans and merchant houses of the coast, and its jurisdiction covers a trade essential to the prosperity and comfort of the people of the warm coast and of every land to which the salt is exported. The Court has maintained the standards of salt, regulated the harvest and export, and adjudicated the disputes that arise among the salt-makers and merchants of the region. The Court has been one of the quiet foundations of the prosperity of the Sahrani coast and is held in high esteem by every magistrate and every merchant of the region.
