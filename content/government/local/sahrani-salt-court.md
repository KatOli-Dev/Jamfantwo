## Overview

The Salt Court of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) Coast is the standing body of the salt-makers, the salt-merchants, and the magistrates of the salt pans who regulate the harvest, the trade, and the export of salt along the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip). The Court is the keeper of the standards of the salt, the regulator of the salt pans, and the adjudicator of the disputes that arise among the salt-makers, the merchants, and the captains of the vessels that carry the salt to the markets of the world. The Court is composed of the master salt-makers, the sworn deputies of the salt pans, and the magistrates of the principal towns of the coast. The Court meets in the hall of the salt merchants at [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) four times in each year.

The Court is one of the older of the local bodies of the warm coast, and its foundation is ascribed to the accord that the salt-makers of the Sahrani coast made with the merchants of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) more than three centuries ago. The accord established the standards of the salt, the seasons of the harvest, the rules of the trade, and the procedures by which the disputes are adjudicated. The accord has been revised from time to time, and the revisions have been entered in the register of the Court and confirmed by the magistrates of the principal towns.

## Composition and Officers

The Court is composed of a master, two wardens, a clerk, and the sworn members who represent the salt pans and the salt merchants. The master is elected by the sworn members for a term of three years, and the master must be a salt-maker or a salt-merchant of at least fifteen years' standing. The wardens are elected for the same term and represent the salt-makers and the merchants respectively. The clerk is appointed by the master and the wardens and holds office at the pleasure of the Court, and the clerk is responsible for the registers, the correspondence, and the accounts of the Court.

The sworn members are the masters of the salt pans that produce at least a thousand baskets of salt in a season, the sworn deputies of the smaller pans, and the heads of the salt-merchant houses of Khalisar and the other towns of the coast. A salt pan or a merchant house that falls below the threshold may continue as a sworn member for life, but no new member is admitted who does not meet the threshold. The chronicler notes that the threshold has been raised from time to time as the trade has grown, and the raising has been the subject of long debate in the Court.

## Standards of the Salt

The Court maintains the standards of the salt that is harvested within its jurisdiction, and the standards are enforced by the wardens and by the sworn members. The salt is judged by colour, by grain, by dryness, and by the taste, and the standard is set by the testing that the Court conducts each year at the autumn meeting. The standard is entered in the register of the Court, and the standard is the reference by which every basket of salt is judged. A basket that fails the standard may not be sold under the seal of the Court, and the seller is fined.

The Court also maintains the standards of the baskets in which the salt is packed, and the baskets must be of a pattern approved by the wardens. The basket must be tight, and the tightness is tested by the wardens at the time of the sealing. The seal of the Court is set upon the basket that passes the test, and the seal is the guarantee that the basket holds the salt of the standard. A basket that bears the seal of the Court may be sold throughout the region without further inspection, and the chronicler notes that the seal has been the foundation of the reputation of the salt of the Sahrani coast.

## Seasons of the Harvest

The Court regulates the harvest of the salt, and the regulations have been the source of much of the prosperity of the salt pans and the towns of the coast. The harvest is opened in the late summer, when the pans have been flooded and the sun has evaporated the first of the water, and the harvest is closed in the autumn, when the rains begin and the pans must be drained for the winter. The seasons are set by the master after consultation with the sworn members, and the seasons are entered in the register of the Court. The chronicler notes that the seasons are a careful compromise between the abundance of the sun and the need of the people, and the seasons have been the subject of long debate in the Court.

The Court also regulates the labour of the harvest, and the labourers are licensed by the wardens. A labourer who holds a licence may work in the salt pans during the harvest, and the labourer is bound by the regulations of the Court. The labourer must be paid in coin and in kind, and the proportion is set by the wardens at the beginning of every harvest. A labourer who violates the regulations is brought before the Court, and the Court has the power to revoke the licence. The chronicler notes that the revocation has been rare, for the labourers are jealous of their licences, and the revocation has been the subject of much local controversy when it has occurred.

## The Trade in Salt

The Court regulates the trade in salt, and the regulations have been the source of much of the prosperity of the merchants of Khalisar and the other towns of the coast. The salt-maker sells his salt to the merchant, and the merchant sells the salt to the captains of the vessels that carry it to the markets of the world. The merchant pays the salt-maker in coin and in goods, and the proportion is set by the Court at the autumn meeting. The proportion has been the subject of long debate, and the Court has revised the proportion from time to time to reflect the harvest and the demand.

The Court also regulates the export, and the export is licensed by the master. A captain who wishes to carry salt from the Sahrani coast must apply to the master for a licence, and the master may grant or refuse the licence as the Court directs. The captain must enter the cargo in the register of the Court, and the captain must pay the small due that the Court has imposed for the maintenance of the pans. The chronicler notes that the due has been stable for several generations, and the due is one of the most carefully audited of the public revenues of the region.

## Adjudication of Disputes

The Court adjudicates the disputes that arise among the salt-makers, the merchants, and the captains, and the adjudication is final within the trade. The dispute is brought before the master and the wardens, and the master and the wardens hear the parties and the witnesses. The master and the wardens then retire to consider the matter, and they return with a judgement that is entered in the register of the Court. The judgement may be appealed to the full Court at the next quarterly meeting, and the full Court may confirm, modify, or reverse the judgement of the master and the wardens.

The Court has the power to compel the attendance of the parties and the witnesses, and the Court has the power to fine any party who is found to have violated the standards or the regulations of the Court. The Court does not have the power to imprison, and a party who refuses to pay the fine is reported to the magistrate of the nearest town. The chronicler notes that the magistrates have always supported the Court in the collection of the fines, for the magistrates recognise that the Court is the regulator of a trade that is essential to the prosperity of the region.

## The Hall and the Records

The Court meets in the hall of the salt merchants at Khalisar, and the hall is a substantial building of stone and timber that was erected more than a century ago by the subscriptions of the sworn members. The hall contains the council chamber, the offices of the clerk, the small yard in which the standard salts are kept, and the archives in which the records of the Court are preserved. The archives are open to the sworn members and to the magistrates, and the chronicler notes that the archives are among the most complete of the records of any local body of the known world.

The records of the Court include the register of the sworn members, the register of the judgements, the register of the standards, and the register of the exports. The registers are kept by the clerk, and the clerk is responsible for the accuracy of the entries. The chronicler notes that the registers have been consulted by the magistrates and by the historians in many a dispute, and the registers are the foundation of the reputation of the Court for fair dealing and careful administration.

## Significance

The Sahrani Salt Court is one of the most useful and least controversial of the local governing bodies of the known world. Its officers and members are drawn from the salt pans and the merchant houses of the coast, and its jurisdiction covers a trade that is essential to the prosperity and the comfort of the people of the warm coast and of every land to which the salt is exported. The Court has maintained the standards of the salt, has regulated the harvest and the export, and has adjudicated the disputes that arise among the salt-makers and the merchants of the region. The chronicler notes that the Court has been one of the quiet foundations of the prosperity of the Sahrani coast, and the Court is held in high esteem by every magistrate and every merchant of the region.
