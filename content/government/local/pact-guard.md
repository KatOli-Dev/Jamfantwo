## Overview

The Pact Guard is the standing military force of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the collective body of soldiers and guards who are contributed by the member cities of the alliance to the protection of the caravan routes of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) and to the defence of the inland settlements at the edge of the great desert. The Guard is composed of the foot soldiers, the mounted scouts, and the watchtower garrisons that the council of the Pact has established along the principal routes, and the Guard is commanded by a captain who is appointed by the council for a customary term of six years. The Guard is the foundation of the security of the caravan trade that connects the coastal cities of the northern [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) to the oases and the upland settlements of the interior, and the Guard is the principal military instrument of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact). The chronicler of the inland cities records that the Guard has been maintained without interruption since the early years of the Pact, and the Guard is the most distinctive of the collective institutions of the alliance.

## Composition and Officers

The Pact Guard is composed of the soldiers and the guards who are contributed by the member cities of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), and the contribution of each member is set by the council in proportion to the city's ability to provide. The foot soldiers form the principal body of the Guard, and the foot soldiers are drawn from the households of the inland cities and from the salawa communities of the desert margin. The mounted scouts form the second body of the Guard, and the mounted scouts are drawn from the households of the cities that maintain the breeding herds. The watchtower garrisons form the third body of the Guard, and the garrisons are drawn from the experienced soldiers of the principal cities. The chronicler records that the composition of the Guard is determined by the council at the spring session of each year, and the determination is the formal beginning of the campaigning season.

The captain of the Guard is the principal military officer of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), and the captain's authority extends over the soldiers of all the member cities who are detached for the service of the collective force. The captain is appointed by the council for a term of six years, and the captain may be reappointed at the discretion of the council. The captain is responsible for the discipline of the force, the conduct of the patrols, and the defence of the member cities in the event of an attack. The chronicler records that the present captain, [Captain Sael of the Pact Guard](/content/people/notable/captain-sael-of-the-pact-guard), is the most distinguished of the recent commanders, and the captain's tenure has been marked by a substantial improvement in the discipline of the force.

The captain is assisted by the lieutenants of the Guard, and the lieutenants are appointed by the captain from among the soldiers who have been recommended by the commanders of the individual cities. The lieutenants are stationed at the principal watchtowers and the shelters that the Guard maintains upon the caravan routes, and the lieutenants are responsible for the conduct of the patrols in the sectors assigned to them. The chronicler records that the lieutenants are the backbone of the daily operation of the Guard, and the lieutenants are the foundation of the reputation of the force for the security of the routes.

The sergeants of the Guard are the senior non-commissioned officers of the force, and the sergeants are drawn from the soldiers who have served the customary term of years. The sergeants are responsible for the daily discipline of the foot soldiers and the mounted scouts, and the sergeants are responsible for the maintenance of the arms and the provisions that the Guard requires. The chronicler notes that the sergeants are the foundation of the continuity of the discipline of the Guard, and the sergeants are the foundation of the tradition of careful preparation that the force has maintained.

## The Caravan Routes

The principal concern of the Guard is the security of the caravan routes that cross the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) and connect the inland cities to the coast, and the routes are the lifeline of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact). The [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) is the network of overland passages that the caravans travel between the coastal cities of the north and the oases and the upland settlements of the interior, and the Guard maintains a continuous patrol along the principal arteries of the network. The chronicler of the inland cities records that the security of the routes is the foundation of the prosperity of the member cities, and the security is the foundation of the reputation of the Guard as the principal military instrument of the Pact.

The disposition of the patrols is the responsibility of the captain, and the captain sets the pattern of the patrols in consultation with the lieutenants of the Guard. The patrols are conducted both by day and by night, and the soldiers of the Guard are accustomed to the demands of the desert service. The patrols are conducted in pairs or in small parties, and the parties are equipped with the arms and the provisions that the desert requires. The chronicler notes that the patrols are the most visible sign of the Guard's presence upon the routes, and the patrols are the foundation of the reputation of the Guard as a deterrent to the raiders who might otherwise prey upon the caravans.

The Guard is supplemented by the salawa guides who accompany the caravans, and the salawa provide the knowledge of the desert that is essential for the safe passage of the routes. The chronicler records that the salawa guides are the most respected members of any caravan, for the guides' decisions can mean the difference between the safe arrival at the next oasis and the slow death that awaits the lost in the deep desert. The salawa guides are not members of the Guard, but the guides are under the protection of the Guard, and the Guard has the power to call upon the guides for assistance in the conduct of the patrols. The cooperation between the Guard and the salawa is the foundation of the security of the routes, and the cooperation is one of the most distinctive features of the inland trade.

## The Watchtowers

The Guard maintains a chain of watchtowers along the principal caravan routes, and the watchtowers are the most visible sign of the Guard's presence upon the desert. The watchtowers are situated at the points where the routes are most exposed to the threat of raiding, and the watchtowers are constructed of the local stone in a form that the desert builders have long employed. The watchtowers are of two stories, with a stout door and a stout roof, and the watchtowers are built upon a rise of ground that commands a wide view of the route in both directions. The chronicler records that the watchtowers have been the foundation of the security of the routes, and the watchtowers have been the refuge of many a traveller who has been caught upon the route in time of storm.

The garrison of a watchtower consists of a sergeant and a squad of soldiers, and the garrison holds the watchtower for a tour of two months. The sergeant is responsible for the maintenance of the watchtower, for the signals that are exchanged with the neighbouring watchtowers, and for the register of the travellers who shelter in the watchtower. The chronicler notes that the sergeant has a duty to keep the register carefully, for the register is the evidence by which the council judges the state of the routes in time of dispute. The register is sent to the captain of the Guard at the end of every month, and the captain enters the entries in the general register of the Guard.

The present captain has directed the construction of two new watchtowers upon the routes that pass through the deep desert, and the construction has been a significant undertaking that has required the labour of the soldiers of the Guard and the craftsmen of the member cities. The watchtowers are now in operation, and the patrols that are conducted from the watchtowers have substantially reduced the incidence of raiding upon the routes that they command. The chronicler records that the construction of the new watchtowers is one of the most distinctive achievements of the present captain's tenure, and the construction is the foundation of the reputation of the captain as a commander of considerable accomplishment.

## The Reserve

The captain of the Guard maintains a reserve at the city of [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) that may be dispatched to the relief of any of the cities or the watchtowers that may be threatened. The reserve is composed of the most experienced soldiers of the Guard, and the reserve is equipped with the arms and the provisions that would be required for a campaign in the deep desert. The chronicler records that the reserve has been called upon on several occasions during the long history of the Guard, and the reserve has been the foundation of the defence of the inland cities in the most serious of the crises that the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) has faced.

The captain is responsible for the coordination of the militias of the individual cities, and the lieutenants of the Guard are in regular communication with the commanders of the militias to ensure that the defences of the cities are in a state of readiness. The chronicler notes that the militias are the foundation of the defence of the member cities, and the militias are the foundation of the tradition of collective security that the Pact has maintained. The captain maintains a careful record of the strength and the disposition of the forces that are available for the defence of the cities, and the reports that the captain submits to the council are among the most important of the documents that come before the sessions.

## The Cycle of the Year

The Guard operates according to a cycle of the year that is determined by the rhythms of the caravan trade and the climate of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). The campaigning season begins with the spring session of the council, and the season is the period during which the caravans are most active and the patrols are most necessary. The summer months are the most demanding of the season, for the heat of the desert places a severe strain upon the soldiers of the Guard, and the soldiers must be provisioned with a care that the captain supervises. The autumn brings a reduction in the traffic, and the autumn is the period during which the Guard conducts the maintenance of the watchtowers and the shelters. The winter is the period of rest, and the winter is the period during which the soldiers return to their home cities and the watchtowers are held by a reduced garrison.

The chronicler notes that the cycle of the year is one of the foundations of the continuity of the Guard, and the cycle is the foundation of the tradition of careful preparation that the force has maintained. The captain sets the pattern of the cycle in consultation with the lieutenants and the sergeants, and the captain's pattern is the formal beginning of the campaigning season. The chronicler records that the cycle has been observed without interruption since the early years of the Pact, and the cycle is one of the most distinctive features of the institution.

## Relations with the Tribes

The Guard maintains a complex relationship with the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), the confederation of nomadic peoples of the desert, and the relationship is the foundation of the security of the caravan routes. The tribes provide the knowledge of the desert that the caravans require, and the Guard provides the protection of the routes that the tribes need for the conduct of their trade. The chronicler records that the cooperation between the Guard and the tribes is the foundation of the security of the inland trade, and the cooperation is one of the most distinctive features of the institution.

The Guard has, on several occasions, been called upon to mediate disputes between the member cities and the tribes, and the mediation has been a significant factor in the maintenance of the peace and the cooperation that sustain the caravan trade. The chronicler notes that the mediation is conducted by the captain in consultation with the elders of the tribes, and the mediation is the foundation of the reputation of the Guard as a force for the maintenance of order in the desert. The captain maintains a careful record of the agreements that have been concluded with the tribes, and the agreements are the formal foundation of the security of the routes.

The scholars of the [Theory of the Ebb and Flow](/content/belief/science/theoretical/theory-of-the-ebb-and-flow) have noted that the tides of the waters and the cycles of the trade are observed with a care that the Guard has long employed, and the scholars have observed that the records of the Guard are among the most reliable of the inland sources for the study of the cycles of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). The chronicler notes that the cooperation between the Guard and the scholars is one of the most distinctive features of the institution, and the cooperation is the foundation of the reputation of the Guard as a force for the maintenance of order in the desert.

## Significance

The Pact Guard is the standing military force of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), and the Guard is the principal military instrument of the alliance. The Guard has been maintained without interruption since the early years of the Pact, and the Guard is the foundation of the security of the caravan trade that connects the coastal cities of the northern Sahrani to the oases and the upland settlements of the interior. The chronicler finds in the Pact Guard one of the most useful and least controversial of the collective institutions of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), maintained not by a ruling house or a priesthood but by the steady cooperation of the member cities and the captain whom the council has appointed. The Guard endures, in the watchtowers and upon the routes, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the caravan trade of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh).
