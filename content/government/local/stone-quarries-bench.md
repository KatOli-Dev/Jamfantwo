## The Standing Body of Master Masons and Quarrymen

The Stone Quarries' Bench is the standing body of the master masons, quarrymen, and stone-cutters who work the quarries of the southern flank of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and the foothills of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands). The Bench regulates the quarries, keeps the standards of the stone, and adjudicates disputes among quarrymen, masons, and the merchants who buy the stone of the region. The Bench is composed of the masters of the principal quarries, the heads of the masonic lodges, and the sworn deputies who represent the quarry villages. The Bench meets in the [Stone Quarries' Hall](/content/government/local/stone-quarries-hall) at the town of [Quarryside](/content/location/settlement/town/quarryside) four times each year.

The Bench is one of the older trade bodies of the cold north, its foundation ascribed to the accord the master masons of the southern quarries made with the lords of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) more than three centuries ago. The accord established the bounds of the principal quarries, the standards of the stone, the rules of apprenticeship, and the procedures for adjudicating disputes. The accord has been revised from time to time, with revisions entered in the register of the Bench and confirmed by the magistrates of the principal towns.

## The Master, the Wardens, and the Sworn Members

The Bench is composed of a master, two wardens, a clerk, and the sworn members who represent the quarries and the masonic lodges. The master is elected by the sworn members for a term of three years and must be a master mason of at least fifteen years' standing. The wardens are elected for the same term and represent the quarrymen and the masons respectively. The clerk is appointed by the master and the wardens and holds office at the pleasure of the Bench, responsible for the registers, correspondence, and accounts.

The sworn members are the masters of the quarries that produce at least five hundred tonnes of dressed stone in a year, the heads of the masonic lodges that employ at least twenty journeymen, and the sworn deputies of the quarry villages. A quarry or lodge that falls below the threshold may continue as a sworn member for life, but no new member is admitted who does not meet the threshold. The threshold has been raised from time to time as the trade has grown, a subject of long debate in the Bench.

## Colour, Grain, Hardness, and the Ring of the Hammer

The Bench maintains the standards of the stone quarried and dressed within its jurisdiction, enforced by the wardens and sworn members. The stone is judged by colour, grain, hardness, and the sound it gives when struck with a hammer. The standard is set by the testing the Bench conducts each year at the autumn meeting and is entered in the register. A stone that fails the standard may not be sold under the seal of the Bench, and the seller is fined.

The Bench also maintains the standards of dressing, judged by the squareness of the face, the straightness of the edge, and the fineness of the joint. The standard is set by the inspection conducted each year at the spring meeting at the principal yards. A stone that fails the standard of dressing must be re-dressed before it may be sold, and the quarryman or mason who offers it is fined. The seal of the Bench has been the foundation of the reputation of the stone of the region, and the stone of the southern quarries is held in high esteem by the builders of the cold north.

## Quarrymen, Masons, and the Apprenticeship System

The Bench regulates the trade in stone, and the regulations have been the source of much of the prosperity of the quarry villages and masonic towns. The quarryman sells his stone to the mason, who dresses it and sells it to the builder. The mason pays the quarryman in coin and in dressed stone, the proportion set by the Bench at the autumn meeting. The proportion has been the subject of long debate, and the Bench has revised it from time to time to reflect the harvest of the quarry and the demand of the builders.

The Bench also regulates the apprenticeship, and the apprentice is bound to a master mason for a term of seven years. The master is responsible for the training of the apprentice in the craft, and the apprentice is examined by the Bench at the end of the term. The examination is conducted by the wardens and two senior sworn members, and the apprentice who passes is admitted to the Bench as a journeyman. The apprenticeship has been the foundation of the skill of the masons of the region, and the Bench maintains the standards of the apprenticeship as one of its most important responsibilities.

## The Hearing and the Appeal Process

The Bench adjudicates disputes among quarrymen, masons, and the merchants who buy the stone of the region, and the adjudication is final within the trade. The dispute is brought before the master and the wardens, who hear the parties and the witnesses, then retire to consider the matter and return with a judgement entered in the register. The judgement may be appealed to the full Bench at the next quarterly meeting, which may confirm, modify, or reverse the judgement.

The Bench has the power to compel the attendance of parties and witnesses and to fine any party found to have violated the standards or regulations of the Bench. The Bench does not have the power to imprison; a party who refuses to pay the fine is reported to the magistrate of the nearest town. The magistrates have always supported the Bench in the collection of fines, recognising that the Bench regulates a trade essential to the prosperity of the region.

## The Hall and the Archives

The Bench meets in the [Stone Quarries' Hall](/content/government/local/stone-quarries-hall) at the town of [Quarryside](/content/location/settlement/town/quarryside), a substantial building of stone and timber erected more than a century ago by the subscriptions of the sworn members. The hall contains the council chamber, the offices of the clerk, a small yard in which the standard stones are kept, and the archives in which the records of the Bench are preserved. The archives are open to the sworn members and to the magistrates and are among the most complete records of any trade body in the known world.

The records of the Bench include the register of the sworn members, the register of the judgements, the register of the standards, and the register of the apprentices. The registers are kept by the clerk, responsible for the accuracy of the entries. The registers have been consulted by magistrates and historians in many a dispute and are the foundation of the Bench's reputation for fair dealing and careful administration.

## The Quiet Foundation of the Quarry Villages

The Stone Quarries' Bench is one of the most useful and least controversial of the local governing bodies of the known world. Its officers and members are drawn from the quarries and masonic lodges of the southern flank of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the foothills of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), and its jurisdiction covers a trade essential to the prosperity and comfort of the people of the cold north. The Bench has maintained the standards of the stone and the dressing, regulated the trade between quarrymen and masons, and adjudicated the disputes that arise among merchants and builders of the region. The Bench has been one of the quiet foundations of the prosperity of the quarry villages and masonic towns and is held in high esteem by every magistrate and every builder of the region.
