## Overview

The Heartwood Fellowship is the standing body of the wardens, beaters, and trappers who tend the [Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) and the surrounding forests of the southern reach of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands). The Fellowship regulates the chase, keeps the bounds of the forest, and adjudicates disputes among the villagers, charcoal-burners, and small lords who hold land within or adjacent to the wood. The Fellowship is composed of the chief warden, two assistants, and the sworn fellows who represent the principal villages of the forest margin. The Fellowship meets in the forest hall at the clearing of Greenwell four times each year, and the meetings are open to the sworn fellows and to the lords who hold an interest in the wood.

The Fellowship is one of the older local bodies of the cold north, its foundation ascribed to the accord the villages of the forest margin made with the lords of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) more than three centuries ago. The accord established the bounds of the forest, the seasons of the chase, the rules of trapping and wood-cutting, and the procedures for adjudicating disputes. The accord has been revised from time to time, with revisions entered in the register of the Fellowship and confirmed by the magistrates of the principal towns.

## Composition and Officers

The Fellowship is composed of a chief warden, two assistants, a clerk, and the sworn fellows who represent the villages of the forest margin. The chief warden is elected by the sworn fellows for a term of five years and must be a warden of at least fifteen years' standing. The assistants are elected for the same term and represent the northern and southern reaches of the forest. The clerk is appointed by the chief warden and the assistants and holds office at the pleasure of the Fellowship, responsible for the registers, correspondence, and accounts.

The sworn fellows are the deputies of the principal villages of the forest margin, each village entitled to one fellow for every hundred households within its bounds. A fellow is elected by the headmen of the village for a term of three years and may be re-elected without limit. The fellows are drawn from the households of foresters, charcoal-burners, and trappers, possessing deep knowledge of the wood and its ways. The Fellowship has been an avenue of advancement for the sons of the forest, and several chief wardens have been born to no greater station than a forester of the southern reach.

## The Bounds of the Forest

The Fellowship maintains the bounds of the forest, marked by the ancient stones set by the founders of the accord. The bounds run from the ford of the Greywash in the north to the head of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) in the south, enclosing the whole of the Heartwood and the fringes reserved to the chase. The chief warden inspects the bounds once each year in the company of the fellows of the villages along the bound, and the inspection is recorded in the register. Any stone that has fallen or any mark obscured is restored.

The Fellowship has the power to fine any person who cuts timber or sets a snare within the bounds without the licence of the chief warden and to seize any timber or game taken in violation of the bounds. The bounds have been the subject of long dispute between the Fellowship and the small lords who hold land within or adjacent to the wood, with disputes adjudicated by the magistrates of the principal towns. The Fellowship has generally prevailed, for the bounds are ancient and the accord clear, but the disputes have been a source of friction for as long as the Fellowship has existed.

## Seasons of the Chase

The Fellowship regulates the chase, and the regulations have been the source of much of the prosperity of the villages of the forest margin. The chase is opened in the autumn and closed in the spring, the seasons set by the chief warden after consultation with the sworn fellows. The chief warden determines the bag each village may take in a season, entered in the register of the Fellowship. The bag is a careful compromise between the abundance of the wood and the needs of the villages, and it has been the subject of long debate in the Fellowship.

The Fellowship also regulates the trapping, with trappers licensed by the chief warden. A licensed trapper may set a certain number of snares within the bounds, of a pattern approved by the Fellowship. The trapper must visit the snares daily and enter the catch in the register maintained by the Fellowship. A trapper who violates the regulations is brought before the Fellowship, which has the power to revoke the licence. Revocations have been rare, for the trappers are jealous of their licences, and each revocation has been the subject of much local controversy.

## Adjudication of Disputes

The Fellowship adjudicates disputes among the villagers, charcoal-burners, trappers, and small lords of the forest margin, and the adjudication is final within the bounds. The dispute is brought before the chief warden and the assistants, who hear the parties and the witnesses, then retire to consider the matter and return with a judgement entered in the register. The judgement may be appealed to the full Fellowship at the next quarterly meeting, which may confirm, modify, or reverse the judgement.

The Fellowship has the power to compel the attendance of parties and witnesses and to fine any party found to have violated the bounds or regulations. The Fellowship does not have the power to imprison; a party who refuses to pay the fine is reported to the magistrate of the nearest town. The magistrates have always supported the Fellowship in the collection of fines, recognising that the Fellowship regulates a resource essential to the prosperity of the region.

## The Hall and the Records

The Fellowship meets in the forest hall at the clearing of Greenwell, a substantial timber building erected more than a century ago by the subscriptions of the sworn fellows. The hall contains the council chamber, the offices of the clerk, a small armory in which the bows and bills of the foresters are kept, and the archives in which the records of the Fellowship are preserved. The archives are open to the sworn fellows and to the magistrates and are among the most complete records of any local body in the known world.

The records of the Fellowship include the register of the sworn fellows, the register of the judgements, the register of the bounds, and the register of the chase. The registers are kept by the clerk, who is responsible for the accuracy of the entries. The registers have been consulted by magistrates and historians in many a dispute and are the foundation of the Fellowship's reputation for fair dealing and careful administration.

## Significance

The Heartwood Fellowship is one of the most useful and least controversial of the local governing bodies of the known world. Its officers and fellows are drawn from the villages of the forest margin, and its jurisdiction covers a wood essential to the prosperity and comfort of the people of the cold north. The Fellowship has maintained the bounds of the forest, regulated the chase and the trapping, and adjudicated the disputes that arise among the villagers and the small lords of the region. The Fellowship has been one of the quiet foundations of the prosperity of the forest margin and is held in high esteem by every magistrate and every forester of the region.
