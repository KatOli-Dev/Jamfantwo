## Overview

The Heartwood Fellowship is the standing body of the wardens, the beaters, and the trappers who tend the [Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) and the surrounding forests of the southern reach of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands). The Fellowship is the regulator of the chase, the keeper of the bounds of the forest, and the adjudicator of the disputes that arise among the villagers, the charcoal-burners, and the small lords who hold land within or adjacent to the wood. The Fellowship is composed of the chief warden, two of his assistants, and the sworn fellows who represent the principal villages of the forest margin. The Fellowship meets in the forest hall at the clearing of Greenwell four times in each year, and the meetings are open to the sworn fellows and to the lords who hold an interest in the wood.

The Fellowship is one of the older of the local bodies of the cold north, and its foundation is ascribed to the accord that the villages of the forest margin made with the lords of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) more than three centuries ago. The accord established the bounds of the forest, the seasons of the chase, the rules of the trapping and the wood-cutting, and the procedures by which the disputes are adjudicated. The accord has been revised from time to time, and the revisions have been entered in the register of the Fellowship and confirmed by the magistrates of the principal towns.

## Composition and Officers

The Fellowship is composed of a chief warden, two assistants, a clerk, and the sworn fellows who represent the villages of the forest margin. The chief warden is elected by the sworn fellows for a term of five years, and the chief warden must be a warden of at least fifteen years' standing. The assistants are elected for the same term and represent the northern and the southern reaches of the forest. The clerk is appointed by the chief warden and the assistants and holds office at the pleasure of the Fellowship, and the clerk is responsible for the registers, the correspondence, and the accounts of the Fellowship.

The sworn fellows are the deputies of the principal villages of the forest margin, and each village is entitled to one fellow for every hundred households within its bounds. A fellow is elected by the headmen of the village for a term of three years, and the fellow may be re-elected without limit. The chronicler notes that the fellows are drawn from the households of the foresters, the charcoal-burners, and the trappers, and the fellows have a deep knowledge of the wood and its ways. The chronicler observes that the Fellowship has been an avenue of advancement for the sons of the forest, and several chief wardens have been born to no greater station than a forester of the southern reach.

## The Bounds of the Forest

The Fellowship maintains the bounds of the forest, and the bounds are marked by the ancient stones that were set by the founders of the accord. The bounds run from the ford of the Greywash in the north to the head of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) in the south, and the bounds enclose the whole of the Heartwood and the fringes that the accord has reserved to the chase. The chief warden inspects the bounds once in each year, and the inspection is conducted in the company of the fellows of the villages along the bound. The inspection is recorded in the register of the Fellowship, and any stone that has fallen or any mark that has been obscured is restored.

The Fellowship has the power to fine any person who cuts timber or sets a snare within the bounds without the licence of the chief warden, and the Fellowship has the power to seize any timber or any game that has been taken in violation of the bounds. The chronicler notes that the bounds have been the subject of long dispute between the Fellowship and the small lords who hold land within or adjacent to the wood, and the disputes have been adjudicated by the magistrates of the principal towns. The Fellowship has generally prevailed in the disputes, for the bounds are ancient and the accord is clear, but the disputes have been a source of friction for as long as the Fellowship has existed.

## Seasons of the Chase

The Fellowship regulates the chase, and the regulations have been the source of much of the prosperity of the villages of the forest margin. The chase is opened in the autumn and is closed in the spring, and the seasons are set by the chief warden after consultation with the sworn fellows. The chief warden determines the bag that each village may take in a season, and the bag is entered in the register of the Fellowship. The chronicler notes that the bag is a careful compromise between the abundance of the wood and the needs of the villages, and the bag has been the subject of long debate in the Fellowship.

The Fellowship also regulates the trapping, and the trappers are licensed by the chief warden. A trapper who holds a licence may set a certain number of snares within the bounds, and the snares must be of a pattern approved by the Fellowship. The trapper must visit the snares daily, and the trapper must enter the catch in the register that the Fellowship maintains. A trapper who violates the regulations is brought before the Fellowship, and the Fellowship has the power to revoke the licence. The chronicler notes that the revocation has been rare, for the trappers are jealous of their licences, and the revocation has been the subject of much local controversy when it has occurred.

## Adjudication of Disputes

The Fellowship adjudicates the disputes that arise among the villagers, the charcoal-burners, the trappers, and the small lords of the forest margin, and the adjudication is final within the bounds. The dispute is brought before the chief warden and the assistants, and the chief warden and the assistants hear the parties and the witnesses. The chief warden and the assistants then retire to consider the matter, and they return with a judgement that is entered in the register of the Fellowship. The judgement may be appealed to the full Fellowship at the next quarterly meeting, and the full Fellowship may confirm, modify, or reverse the judgement of the chief warden and the assistants.

The Fellowship has the power to compel the attendance of the parties and the witnesses, and the Fellowship has the power to fine any party who is found to have violated the bounds or the regulations of the Fellowship. The Fellowship does not have the power to imprison, and a party who refuses to pay the fine is reported to the magistrate of the nearest town. The chronicler notes that the magistrates have always supported the Fellowship in the collection of the fines, for the magistrates recognise that the Fellowship is the regulator of a resource that is essential to the prosperity of the region.

## The Hall and the Records

The Fellowship meets in the forest hall at the clearing of Greenwell, and the hall is a substantial building of timber that was erected more than a century ago by the subscriptions of the sworn fellows. The hall contains the council chamber, the offices of the clerk, the small armory in which the bows and the bills of the foresters are kept, and the archives in which the records of the Fellowship are preserved. The archives are open to the sworn fellows and to the magistrates, and the chronicler notes that the archives are among the most complete of the records of any local body of the known world.

The records of the Fellowship include the register of the sworn fellows, the register of the judgements, the register of the bounds, and the register of the chase. The registers are kept by the clerk, and the clerk is responsible for the accuracy of the entries. The chronicler notes that the registers have been consulted by the magistrates and by the historians in many a dispute, and the registers are the foundation of the reputation of the Fellowship for fair dealing and careful administration.

## Significance

The Heartwood Fellowship is one of the most useful and least controversial of the local governing bodies of the known world. Its officers and fellows are drawn from the villages of the forest margin, and its jurisdiction covers a wood that is essential to the prosperity and the comfort of the people of the cold north. The Fellowship has maintained the bounds of the forest, has regulated the chase and the trapping, and has adjudicated the disputes that arise among the villagers and the small lords of the region. The chronicler notes that the Fellowship has been one of the quiet foundations of the prosperity of the forest margin, and the Fellowship is held in high esteem by every magistrate and every forester of the region.
