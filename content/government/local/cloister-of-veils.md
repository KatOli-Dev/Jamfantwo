## The Quiet Establishment

The Cloister of Veils is the central establishment of the [Path of the Sunless Veils](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/path-of-the-sunless-veils), the quiet house in which the order's retreat is conducted and in which the long discipline of the veils is sustained. The cloister is found in the inland valleys of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the sun's rays reach only briefly in the winter months, and the cloister is a humble building of stone and timber that is curtained against the brightness of the day. The chronicler notes that the cloister is regarded by the path as the chief instrument by which the veils are lifted in their order, and the path holds the cloister in greater esteem than any of the meeting-houses or the chapels that are found in the towns of the hill country.

The cloister is the seat of the senior veilers, the place where the keepers of the veils instruct the novices and oversee the retreats. The cloister is supported by the labour of the community and by the small offerings of the faithful of the surrounding hill towns, who regard the long retreat as a service to the wider faith and who contribute to the cloister's maintenance accordingly. The chronicler notes that the cloister has no endowment of consequence, and the cloister's small revenues are derived from the work of the community's hands and from the customary gifts of the hill towns.

## Origins among the First Veilers

The Cloister of Veils traces its foundation to the community of hermits who withdrew from the older market towns of the central highlands and took up residence in the quiet valleys where the sun's rays reach only briefly in the winter months. The hermits taught that the world of bright surfaces and loud voices was a kind of veil, and the community's founders are remembered only by the title of the First Veilers. The chronicler notes that whether the title belonged to one or to several is left to the chronicler's discretion, and the path is content that the teaching should be remembered whatever the case may be.

The first cloister was a single chamber cut into the side of a hill, and the chamber was curtained against the day. The community's successors added a second chamber and then a third, and the cloister grew in the measure of the number of novices who were admitted to the long retreat. The path spread quietly through the inland valleys, and each new valley that received the teaching required its own cloister, for the distance between the valleys is such that a single cloister could not serve the whole of the faithful. The chronicler notes that the cloisters of the path are therefore many, though each cloister is modelled upon the first, and each cloister is governed by the same discipline that the first community established.

## The Chamber of the Retreat

The central observance of the path is the long retreat, a discipline of withdrawal that is undertaken at the cloister for a season of weeks or months. The retreat is conducted in silence and in the dim light of a small chamber that is curtained against the day's brightness, and the practitioner is permitted the company only of a senior veiler who attends to the discipline without conversation. The chamber is furnished with a low couch, a small table, a basin of water, and a single lamp that is kept burning through the hours of the retreat. The curtains of the chamber are drawn at the beginning of the retreat and are not opened until the close, and the practitioner is held to remain within the chamber for the duration of the season.

The retreat is not a punishment, and the path is careful to mark the difference. The retreat is held to be the chief instrument by which the veils are lifted in their order, and the elders regard the retreat as the most demanding and the most rewarding of the path's disciplines. The practitioner who undertakes the retreat is held to come forth from the chamber quieter than the practitioner who entered, and the change in the practitioner is regarded by the elders as the measure of the retreat's success. The chronicler notes that the chamber of the retreat is the heart of the cloister, and the chamber is the place to which every novice aspires and to which every senior veiler returns.

## The Schedules of Study

The path maintains at the cloister a schedule of study that the novices and the senior veilers observe throughout the year. The schedule is divided into the seasons of withdrawal and the seasons of instruction, and the seasons alternate in a cycle that the path has refined over generations. The seasons of withdrawal are set apart for the long retreat, and the seasons of instruction are set apart for the reading of the older texts and the discussion of the doctrine of the veils. The chronicler notes that the schedule is observed without exception, and the breaking of the schedule is regarded by the elders as a failure of discipline.

The seasons of instruction are conducted by the senior veilers, who lead the novices through the readings that the path has preserved. The readings are drawn from the older writings of the path and from the commentaries that the senior veilers have added over the generations. The readings are conducted in the meeting-hall of the cloister, and the meeting-hall is a plain room that is lit by a single window that is curtained against the brightness of the day. The chronicler notes that the meeting-hall is the second of the principal rooms of the cloister, and the meeting-hall is the place in which the doctrine is taught and the discipline of the veils is explained to those who seek it.

## The Library of the Veils

The cloister maintains a small library of the texts that the path has preserved, and the library is the third of the principal rooms of the cloister. The library is housed in a chamber that is lined with shelves of plain wood, and the shelves hold the copies of the older texts and the commentaries that the path has produced. The library is open to the senior veilers and to the novices who have been admitted to the seasons of instruction, and the library is closed to all others. The chronicler notes that the library is one of the smaller libraries of the inland valleys, for the path values the oral tradition above the written, and the path has been slow to commit its teachings to the page.

The texts that the library holds are few in number, and the texts are regarded by the path as the foundation of the doctrine. The texts include the record of the First Veilers' teachings, the commentaries of the early senior veilers, and the small body of letters that the senior veilers have exchanged with the senior readers of the [Doctrine of the Unwritten Name](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/doctrine-of-the-unwritten-name). The chronicler notes that the letters are among the most valued of the texts in the library, for the letters record the kinship between the two traditions and the courtesy that the two traditions have observed toward one another.

## The Library and the Reading

The reading of the texts is conducted in the library under the direction of the senior veilers, and the reading is one of the principal disciplines of the path. The reading is conducted in silence, and the reader is held to attend to the text without the interruption of commentary. The reader is permitted to mark the passages that are found to be of consequence, and the reader is required to bring the marked passages to the senior veiler at the conclusion of the reading. The senior veiler then discusses the marked passages with the reader, and the discussion is the occasion for the instruction of the novice and the correction of the reader's misunderstanding. The chronicler notes that the reading is regarded by the path as a complement to the long retreat, and the path holds that the reader who has not undertaken the retreat is not fitted to interpret the texts.

## The Wanderers and the Cloister

The cloister is the home of the wanderers of the path, the wandering veilers who travel between the cloisters and the hill towns and who maintain the discipline in the wider community. The wanderers are recognised by the small white staff they carry and by their custom of speaking softly and at length only when the company requires it. The wanderers are recruited from among the senior novices of the cloister, and the wandering office is regarded by the path as a worthy complement to the long retreat. The chronicler notes that the wanderers return to the cloister at the close of their wanderings, and the return is the occasion for a brief silence in which the wanderer's labours are observed without ceremony.

The cloister is also the home of the keepers of the quiet houses that the path maintains in the hill towns. The keepers of the quiet houses are appointed by the senior veilers of the nearest cloister, and the office is regarded by the path as a worthy complement to the long retreat. The quiet houses are humble establishments that lack the chamber and the silence of the cloister but provide a few days of quiet and counsel to those who cannot leave their households for long. The chronicler notes that the quiet houses are the principal point of contact between the cloister and the wider community of the hill towns, and the quiet houses are the source of most of the novices who are admitted to the long retreat.

## The Conference of Senior Veilers

The senior veilers of the several cloisters of the path maintain a conference that is held at the principal cloister once in every year. The conference is conducted with great formality, and the order of speaking is observed without variation. The senior veilers report upon the state of the cloisters, upon the number of novices who have been admitted to the long retreat, and upon the affairs of the quiet houses that are within their charge. The conference closes with a brief silence in which the veils are held in mind without being named, and the silence is regarded by the senior veilers as the most important part of the conference. The chronicler notes that the conference is the only occasion upon which the senior veilers of the several cloisters meet, and the conference is the source of the unity of the path.

The conference is open to visitors of good faith, and the path welcomes those who come from other traditions and wish to attend. A senior reader of the [Doctrine of the Unwritten Name](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/doctrine-of-the-unwritten-name) will sometimes attend the conference of senior veilers, and a senior veiler will sometimes attend the conference of readers, and each is welcomed by the other with the courtesy of kindred traditions. The chronicler notes that the conference is the measure of the path's openness to the wider world, and the path regards the conference as a privilege rather than a claim.

## Significance

The Cloister of Veils is the central establishment of the [Path of the Sunless Veils](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/path-of-the-sunless-veils), and the cloister is the seat of the long retreat, the schedules of study, the library of the veils, and the conference of the senior veilers. The cloister is a quiet establishment in the inland valleys of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and the cloister is supported by the labour of the community and by the small offerings of the faithful of the hill towns. The chronicler notes that the cloister has been the source of the path's slow growth in the hill country, and the cloister is a quiet counterweight to the louder claims of the trading cities. The cloister asks of its practitioners that they attend to the difference between lifting the veils and breaking them, and it offers in return the long retreat by which the lifting is learned and the soul is wrapped, at last, in the corresponding stillness of the [Veiled One](/content/belief/deity/veiled-one)'s own nature.
