## The Body of Frontier Watchmen

The Standing Watch is the body of frontier watchmen who maintain the holding of the beacon and the patrol of the border in the northern reaches of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), regarded by the chroniclers of the marches as the central duty of the Northern Watch Canon. The Watch is not a road patrol in the ordinary sense, nor a garrison kept at the command of a single prince. It is the institution that gives the canon its body, for the canon teaches that the watch is a duty to be held against every interruption, and the Standing Watch is the company of men and women by whom the duty is held. The Watch is most often referred to in prose as simply the Standing Watch, a name the chroniclers of the marches have preferred because it names the duty rather than the route or the garrison that serves it.

The Watch is found throughout the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches), along the chain of towers and beacons that line the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches), and at the principal approaches of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass). The Watch is composed of the watchmen who hold the towers, the scouts who range the frontier, and the officers who command the several districts. The officers are appointed by the council of the marches in consultation with the magistrates of the principal towns and are required to have served a full apprenticeship upon the towers before being entrusted with the command of a district.

## The Old Compact and the Northern Watch Canon

The Standing Watch is held to have arisen from the same older compact from which the Northern Watch Canon descends, and the Watch and the canon are so closely bound that the chroniclers of the marches seldom treat the one without the other. The compact by which the captains of the several garrisons held a common watch and exchanged signals of beacon and horn was the foundation of both the teaching and the institution, and the refinement of the compact into the present discipline of the canon was accompanied by the establishment of the Standing Watch as a recognised body within the frontier. The Watch was not invented by the canon but rather given to it, and the canon has ordered the Watch by its precepts without ever claiming to have created the duty the Watch fulfils.

The teaching of the canon requires that the watch be held without interruption, the signs be read with care, the beacon be lit upon the proper occasions, the post be maintained against every assault, and the watchman endure the solitude of the long winter without the comfort of relief. The Standing Watch gives effect to each of these requirements, and the watchmen are held by the captains to be the living instruments of the canon. The Watch is not a religious order and makes no claim to sanctity, but the watchmen are required by custom to keep the precepts of the canon as a condition of service, and a watchman who violates the canon is dismissed without appeal.

## The Captain, the Watchmen, and the Scouts

The Standing Watch is composed of a captain of the marches, two lieutenants, a clerk, and the sworn watchmen, scouts, and tower garrisons that hold the frontier. The captain of the marches is appointed by the council of the marches for a term of five years and must be a watchman of at least fifteen years' standing who has held the command of at least one tower and has been commended by the magistrates of the principal towns. The lieutenants are appointed by the captain and represent the tower watch and the scout watch. The clerk is appointed by the captain and holds office at the pleasure of the Watch, responsible for the registers, correspondence, and accounts.

The sworn watchmen are the men and women who hold the towers and beacons of the frontier, each bound to the captain by a simple oath renewed at the beginning of every tour. The sworn scouts are the men and women who range the frontier and bring word of the movement of beasts and strangers to the watch-towers, bound by the same oath. The tower garrisons are the small bodies of watchmen who hold the watch-towers and signal the approach of any large body of men, each consisting of a sergeant and four watchmen. The watchmen, scouts, and garrison men are drawn from the households of the marches, and several captains have been born to no greater station than a sworn watchman of a frontier tower.

## The Frontier Towers and the Beacon Stacks

The Standing Watch maintains a chain of watch-towers and beacons along the frontier, placed at intervals of a day's march. The tower is a stone building of two stories, with a stout door and roof, built upon a rise of ground commanding a wide view of the frontier in both directions. The tower contains a small hearth, a small store of provisions, and the arms of the garrison. The beacon is a stack of wood and pitch erected beside the tower, lit upon the occasions the canon prescribes. The towers and beacons have been the foundation of the safety of the frontier and the refuge of many a watchman caught upon the frontier in time of storm.

The garrison of a tower holds the tower for a tour of two months, responsible for its maintenance, the signals exchanged with neighbouring towers, and the register of travellers who shelter in the tower. The signals of the beacons are exchanged by the deep resonant horn the watchmen carry, informing the garrisons of the state of the frontier and the approach of any large body of men. The signals have been the foundation of the safety of the frontier.

## The Rigour of the Canon and the Customs of Solitude

The Standing Watch maintains a strict discipline enforced by the captain and the lieutenants, grounded in the Northern Watch Canon. A watchman found asleep at his post is dismissed; a watchman found guilty of misreading the signs is censured and removed from the solitary towers; a watchman found guilty of failing to light the beacon when the warning was required is dismissed and disgraced. This rigour has been criticised by the philosophers of the southern kingdoms, who hold that the watchman is but a man and subject to ordinary frailties, yet the canon has endured because the frontier has shown that the loss of the watch is the loss of all.

The discipline extends beyond the requirements of the canon to the customs the watchmen have developed among themselves. A watchman relieved of his post is required to give a full account of the state of the tower, the signals exchanged, and any occurrence bearing upon the safety of the frontier. A watchman called to serve a solitary tower is given a graduated introduction to the solitude, so ordered that the watchman is not overwhelmed at the first. These customs have been the salvation of many a watchman who would otherwise have been broken by the solitude and are the foundation of the Watch's reputation for endurance.

## The Mutual Obligation Between Watch and Settlement

The Standing Watch is bound to the settlements of the marches by a customary relation held to be part of the discipline of the canon. The settlement is required to maintain the watch in provisions and reinforcements, and the watch is required to maintain the settlement in warning and defence. This mutual obligation has done much to preserve the harmony of the marches, where the settlement and the watch regard one another as parts of a single order. The breach of the mutual obligation is visited by the censure of the community, delivered by the elders of the settlement in the presence of the captain.

The relation of the watch to the settlement is observed in its most characteristic form in the smaller villages of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches), where the watch-tower and the village are closely bound and the villagers regard the watchmen as their own sons and brothers. The custom requires the village to maintain the watch in provisions and the watch to maintain the village in warning, and the breach of the mutual obligation is visited by the censure of the community. The harmony of the watch and the settlement is itself a discipline, and the frontier which preserves the harmony is the frontier which endures.

## The Stonespire Pass and the Winter Closures

The Standing Watch maintains a particular charge at the approaches of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass), among the most demanding of the several districts of the Watch. The pass is the principal route of the land trade between the cold north and the warm coast, and the watchmen who hold the towers at the approaches are required to give warning of any large body of men moving upon the road. The watchmen of the pass are bound by the same discipline as those of the frontier and are required to read the signs with the same care. The captain of the marches appoints one of the lieutenants to the command of the pass, responsible for the maintenance of the towers, signals, and garrison.

The pass is subject to occasional closure when winter snow blocks the road, a source of much hardship for the watchmen and merchants who must wait for the spring to reopen it. The closures are recorded in the chronicles of the magistrates of the towns of both regions as exceptional events, describing the labour the watchmen have organised to clear the road. The Standing Watch has been the foundation of the careful management of the pass, the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of both regions.

## The Influence Beyond the Northern Marches

The Standing Watch is not a body of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) alone, for the Watch is found wherever the Northern Watch Canon has been adopted as the discipline of the frontier. The [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) maintains a Standing Watch upon its northern frontier, and the watchmen of the kingdom are trained in the watch-towers of the marches before being entrusted with greater commands. The [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) has refined the discipline of the Watch into a comprehensive order of the frontier watch, and its watchmen are bound by the same oath as the watchmen of the marches. The Standing Watch has been adopted in several forms by the greater polities of the north, a measure of the usefulness of the discipline the canon prescribes.

The Standing Watch is not a standing army and does not recruit beyond the customary tours of two months. It is sustained by the contributions of the settlements of the marches and by the small fines the captain imposes upon offenders the watchmen arrest upon the frontier. The fines are entered in the register of the Watch, audited by the clerk at the end of every season. The finances of the Watch have been managed with care for several generations, the foundation of its reputation for fair dealing.

## The Enduring Embodiment of the Northern Watch Canon

The Standing Watch is one of the most useful and least controversial of the local governing bodies of the northern reaches of the known world, the embodiment of the central duty of the Northern Watch Canon. The Watch holds the frontier against every interruption, reads the signs with the care the canon requires, lights the beacon upon the proper occasions, and endures the solitude of the long winter without the comfort of relief. The Watch has been one of the quiet foundations of the safety of the marches and is held in high esteem by every magistrate and every elder of the settlements of the frontier. The standing of the Watch is the standing of the canon, and the canon is held by the watchmen to be the inheritance of the marches that no prince may set aside.
