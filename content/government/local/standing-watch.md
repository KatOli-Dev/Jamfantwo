## The Central Duty

The Standing Watch is the body of frontier watchmen who maintain the holding of the beacon and the patrol of the border in the northern reaches of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and it is regarded by the chroniclers of the marches as the central duty of the [Northern Watch Canon](/content/belief/ideology/northern-watch-canon). The Watch is not a road patrol in the ordinary sense, nor is it a garrison kept at the command of a single prince. The Watch is the institution that gives the canon its body, for the canon teaches that the watch is a duty to be held against every interruption, and the Standing Watch is the company of men and women by whom the duty is held. The chronicler notes that the Watch is most often referred to in prose as simply the Standing Watch, a name that the chroniclers of the marches have preferred because it names the duty rather than the route or the garrison that serves it.

The Watch is found throughout the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches), along the chain of towers and beacons that line the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches), and at the principal approaches of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass). The Watch is composed of the watchmen who hold the towers, the scouts who range the frontier, and the officers who command the several districts of the Watch. The officers are appointed by the council of the marches in consultation with the magistrates of the principal towns, and the officers are required to have served a full apprenticeship upon the towers before they are entrusted with the command of a district.

## Origins in the Canon

The Standing Watch is held to have arisen from the same older compact from which the [Northern Watch Canon](/content/belief/ideology/northern-watch-canon) descends, and the Watch and the canon are so closely bound that the chroniclers of the marches seldom treat the one without the other. The compact by which the captains of the several garrisons held a common watch and exchanged the signals of beacon and horn was the foundation of both the teaching and the institution, and the refinement of the compact into the present discipline of the canon was accompanied by the establishment of the Standing Watch as a recognised body within the frontier. The chronicler notes that the Watch was not invented by the canon but rather given to it, and the canon has ordered the Watch by its precepts without ever claiming to have created the duty that the Watch fulfils.

The teaching of the canon requires that the watch be held without interruption, that the signs be read with care, that the beacon be lit upon the proper occasions, that the post be maintained against every assault, and that the watchman endure the solitude of the long winter without the comfort of relief. The Standing Watch is the body that gives effect to each of these requirements, and the watchmen of the Watch are held by the captains to be the living instruments of the canon. The chronicler notes that the Watch is not a religious order and makes no claim to sanctity, but the watchmen are required by custom to keep the precepts of the canon as a condition of their service, and a watchman who violates the canon is dismissed from the Watch without appeal.

## Composition and Officers

The Standing Watch is composed of a captain of the marches, two lieutenants, a clerk, and the sworn watchmen, scouts, and tower garrisons that hold the frontier. The captain of the marches is appointed by the council of the marches for a term of five years, and the captain must be a watchman of at least fifteen years' standing who has held the command of at least one tower and has been commended by the magistrates of the principal towns. The lieutenants are appointed by the captain and represent the tower watch and the scout watch. The clerk is appointed by the captain and holds office at the pleasure of the Watch, and the clerk is responsible for the registers, the correspondence, and the accounts of the Watch.

The sworn watchmen are the men and the women who hold the towers and the beacons of the frontier, and each watchman is bound to the captain by a simple oath that is renewed at the beginning of every tour. The sworn scouts are the men and the women who range the frontier and bring word of the movement of beasts and of strangers to the watch-towers, and each scout is bound to the captain by the same oath. The tower garrisons are the small bodies of watchmen who hold the watch-towers and signal the approach of any large body of men, and each garrison consists of a sergeant and four watchmen. The chronicler notes that the watchmen, the scouts, and the garrison men are drawn from the households of the marches, and several captains have been born to no greater station than a sworn watchman of a frontier tower.

## The Towers and the Beacons

The Standing Watch maintains a chain of watch-towers and beacons along the frontier, and the towers and beacons are placed at intervals of a day's march. The tower is a stone building of two stories, with a stout door and a stout roof, and the tower is built upon a rise of ground that commands a wide view of the frontier in both directions. The tower contains a small hearth, a small store of provisions, and the arms of the garrison. The beacon is a stack of wood and pitch that is erected beside the tower, and the beacon is lit upon the occasions that the canon prescribes. The chronicler notes that the towers and the beacons have been the foundation of the safety of the frontier, and the towers and the beacons have been the refuge of many a watchman who has been caught upon the frontier in time of storm.

The garrison of a tower holds the tower for a tour of two months, and the garrison is responsible for the maintenance of the tower, for the signals that are exchanged with the neighbouring towers, and for the register of the travellers who shelter in the tower. The signals of the beacons are exchanged with the neighbouring towers by the deep resonant horn that the watchmen carry, and the signals inform the garrisons of the state of the frontier and the approach of any large body of men. The chronicler notes that the signals have been the foundation of the safety of the frontier, and the signals are the measure of the careful management that the Standing Watch maintains.

## The Discipline of the Watch

The Standing Watch maintains a strict discipline that is enforced by the captain and the lieutenants, and the discipline is grounded in the [Northern Watch Canon](/content/belief/ideology/northern-watch-canon). A watchman who is found asleep at his post is dismissed from the service; a watchman who is found guilty of misreading the signs is censured and removed from the solitary towers; a watchman who is found guilty of failing to light the beacon when the warning was required is dismissed and disgraced. The chronicler notes that this rigour has been the subject of much criticism by the philosophers of the southern kingdoms, who hold that the watchman is but a man and is subject to the ordinary frailties, yet the canon has endured because the frontier has shown that the loss of the watch is the loss of all.

The discipline of the Watch extends beyond the requirements of the canon to the customs that the watchmen have developed among themselves, and the customs are the measure of the cohesion of the Watch. A watchman who is relieved of his post by the next watch is required to give a full account of the state of the tower, of the signals that have been exchanged, and of any occurrence that may bear upon the safety of the frontier. A watchman who is called to serve a solitary tower is given a graduated introduction to the solitude, and the introduction is so ordered that the watchman is not overwhelmed at the first. The chronicler notes that these customs have been the salvation of many a watchman who would otherwise have been broken by the solitude, and the customs are the foundation of the reputation of the Watch for endurance.

## The Watch and the Settlement

The Standing Watch is bound to the settlements of the marches by a customary relation that the chroniclers hold to be a part of the discipline of the canon. The settlement is required to maintain the watch in provisions and in reinforcements, and the watch is required to maintain the settlement in warning and in defence. The chronicler notes that this mutual obligation has done much to preserve the harmony of the marches, where the settlement and the watch are accustomed to regard one another as parts of a single order. The breach of the mutual obligation is visited by the censure of the community, and the censure is delivered by the elders of the settlement in the presence of the captain of the Watch.

The relation of the watch to the settlement is observed in its most characteristic form in the smaller villages of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches), where the watch-tower and the village are closely bound and where the villagers regard the watchmen as their own sons and brothers. The chronicler notes that the custom of the marches requires the village to maintain the watch in provisions and the watch to maintain the village in warning, and that the breach of the mutual obligation is visited by the censure of the community. The teaching holds that the harmony of the watch and the settlement is itself a discipline, and that the frontier which preserves the harmony is the frontier which endures.

## The Watch at the Stonespire Pass

The Standing Watch maintains a particular charge at the approaches of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass), and the charge is among the most demanding of the several districts of the Watch. The pass is the principal route of the land trade between the cold north and the warm coast, and the watchmen who hold the towers at the approaches of the pass are required to give warning of any large body of men that may move upon the road. The chronicler notes that the watchmen of the pass are bound by the same discipline as the watchmen of the frontier, and the watchmen of the pass are required to read the signs with the same care that the frontier watchmen observe. The captain of the marches appoints one of the lieutenants to the command of the pass, and the lieutenant is responsible for the maintenance of the towers, the signals, and the garrison.

The pass is subject to the occasional closure when the snow of the winter blocks the road, and the closures have been the source of much of the hardship of the watchmen and the merchants who must wait for the spring to reopen the road. The chronicler notes that the closures are recorded in the chronicles of the magistrates of the towns of both regions as exceptional events, and the chronicles describe the labour that the watchmen have organised to clear the road and to reopen the pass. The Standing Watch has been the foundation of the careful management of the pass, and the careful management is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of both regions.

## The Watch and the Greater Polities

The Standing Watch is not a body of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) alone, for the Watch is found wherever the [Northern Watch Canon](/content/belief/ideology/northern-watch-canon) has been adopted as the discipline of the frontier. The [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) maintains a Standing Watch upon its northern frontier, and the watchmen of the kingdom are trained in the watch-towers of the marches before they are entrusted with the greater commands. The [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) has refined the discipline of the Watch into a comprehensive order of the frontier watch, and the watchmen of the realm are bound by the same oath as the watchmen of the marches. The chronicler notes that the Standing Watch has been adopted in several forms by the greater polities of the north, and the adoption is a measure of the usefulness of the discipline that the canon prescribes.

The Standing Watch is not a standing army and does not recruit beyond the customary tours of two months that the watchmen serve. The Watch is sustained by the contributions of the settlements of the marches and by the small fines that the captain imposes upon the offenders whom the watchmen arrest upon the frontier. The fines are entered in the register of the Watch, and the register is audited by the clerk at the end of every season. The chronicler notes that the finances of the Watch have been managed with care for several generations, and the careful management is the foundation of the reputation of the Watch for fair dealing.

## Significance

The Standing Watch is one of the most useful and least controversial of the local governing bodies of the northern reaches of the known world, and the Watch is the embodiment of the central duty of the [Northern Watch Canon](/content/belief/ideology/northern-watch-canon). The Watch holds the frontier against every interruption, reads the signs with the care that the canon requires, lights the beacon upon the proper occasions, and endures the solitude of the long winter without the comfort of relief. The chronicler notes that the Watch has been one of the quiet foundations of the safety of the marches, and the Watch is held in high esteem by every magistrate and every elder of the settlements of the frontier. The standing of the Watch is the standing of the canon, and the canon is held by the watchmen to be the inheritance of the marches that no prince may set aside.
