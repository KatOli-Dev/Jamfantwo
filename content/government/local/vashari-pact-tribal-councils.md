## The Governing Councils of the Inland Caravan Cities

The Vashari Pact Councils are the governing bodies of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the alliance of the inland caravan cities at the edge of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) in [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The pact is governed by a council of representatives from each member city, and the councils are the primary political institutions of the alliance. The governance of the pact is built upon the principles of popular election, public accountability, and the pursuit of the common commercial interest that unites the inland caravan trade.

The councils meet at [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari), the largest and most influential member city, situated at the terminus of the great caravan routes that cross the Kharesh. The council hall is a substantial building situated in the great market square of Vashari, its architecture reflecting the commercial character of the alliance. The council chamber is a spacious room with high ceilings and windows that overlook the market square, where the caravans assemble before their journeys into the desert.

## Composition and Election

The council of the Vashari Pact is composed of representatives from each member city. The number of representatives from each city is determined by the population of the city: each city sends one representative for every ten thousand inhabitants, with a minimum of two representatives for the smallest member cities. The representatives are chosen by popular vote of all free adults in each member city who have reached the age of majority, without restriction by species or wealth. The franchise is universal among citizens, and any citizen who has reached the age of majority may vote and stand for election.

The elections are held at regular intervals, and any citizen may stand for election. The candidates present themselves before the community at a public gathering held in the market square or the principal meeting place of the city, and the citizens vote by a show of hands or, in the larger cities, by the deposit of marked tokens in containers at designated polling places. The votes are counted by the elders of the city and the result is announced at a public gathering the following day.

The representatives serve for a term of three years and may be re-elected without limit. A representative who is judged by the citizens of the home city to have acted against the interests of the community may be recalled by a petition signed by a majority of the adult citizens, and the recall triggers a new election. The recall is a rarely used but important safeguard against abuse, and the knowledge that they may be recalled concentrates the minds of the representatives on the interests of their constituents.

## The Council in Session

The council meets at Vashari, and the sessions are attended by the representatives of the member cities and by the caravan masters who operate the routes through the desert. The council meets in regular session twice each year, in the spring and in the autumn. The spring session is the more important of the two, for it is the occasion at which the plans for the coming trading season are made and the arrangements for the caravans are agreed.

The sessions are conducted in a formal manner, with a presiding officer chosen from among the representatives at the opening of each session. The presiding officer, known as the Voice of the Council, serves for the duration of the session and is responsible for the conduct of the council's business and the maintenance of order. The Voice of the Council is chosen by a majority vote of the representatives, and the office rotates among the members to ensure that no single city or faction dominates the proceedings.

Before each council session, a public forum is held in the great market square of Vashari where any citizen of the pact may bring a concern, question a representative, or propose a matter for the council's consideration. The council is expected to respond to each submission before proceeding to its regular business. The forum is the principal means by which the ordinary citizens of the pact the merchants, caravan drivers, craftsmen, and innkeepers who serve the caravan trade can make their voices heard in the governance of the alliance. The forum is also the occasion at which the representatives are held to account for their conduct since the previous session.

## Functions and Powers

The primary function of the council is the coordination of the caravan trade that is the foundation of the pact's economy. The council manages the caravan routes, the provisioning of the caravans, and the protection of the routes against raiders and the hazards of the desert. The council maintains a registry of caravan routes and water sources, the official record of the trade and infrastructure that sustains the caravan commerce. The registry includes information on the location and condition of every well and spring along the routes, the distances between waypoints, the availability of forage for animals, and the seasonal variations in temperature and wind.

The council also maintains a system of waybills and seals that authenticate caravans and their cargoes. The waybills record the origin, destination, and contents of each caravan, and the seals certify that the cargo has been inspected and approved by the officials of the pact. The waybills and seals are a critical tool in the regulation and protection of trade, for they provide a verifiable record of ownership and origin that can be presented at toll stations and market towns along the routes. The system of waybills and seals has been adopted by caravan operators across Sahrana, reducing fraud and theft by providing a verifiable record of cargo ownership and origin.

The council also coordinates the defence of the caravan routes through the [Pact Guard](/content/government/local/pact-guard), the collective force of the alliance. The Pact Guard consists of soldiers and guards contributed by the member cities, commanded by a captain appointed by the council. The force is stationed at points along the caravan routes where the threat of raiding is most acute, and the patrols conducted by the guard deter raiders who might otherwise prey on the caravans. The guard is supplemented by [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides who accompany caravans and provide the knowledge of the desert essential for safe passage of the routes.

## The Council and the Kharesh Tribes

The council's relationship with the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) is one of the defining features of the governance of the inland region. The tribes provide the knowledge of the desert that the caravans require, and the pact provides the goods and markets that the tribes need. The council negotiates annually with the elders of the tribes on the terms of the co-operation between the two entities, including the fees to be paid to the tribes for their guidance and protection of the caravans.

The council also mediates disputes that arise between the member cities and the tribes. Such disputes are rare, for both the pact and the tribes recognise that their co-operation is essential for the prosperity of both, but they can arise over matters of access to water sources, the movement of herds across the caravan routes, and the actions of individual members of the tribes or the cities. The council's mediation has been a significant factor in maintaining the peace and co-operation that sustain the caravan trade.

## The Council and the Citizenry

The Vashari Pact Councils are among the most participatory political institutions in the known world. The right of every citizen to vote, the right of every citizen to stand for election, and the right of recall are the foundations of the pact's accountability. Any citizen may petition the council in writing, and the council must provide a formal response to each petition at the next public forum. The right of petition is regarded as the foundation of accountability in the pact, and a representative who ignores the petitions of their constituents may face removal at the next election.

The council also maintains a register of petitions and responses, which is available for inspection by any citizen. The register is a public record of the concerns of the citizens and the actions of the council, and it serves as a check on the representatives and a source of information for the citizens. The council's commitment to transparency is notable even among the participatory polities of the known world, and the citizens of the pact are among the best informed and most engaged in the governance of their communities.

The council's system of popular elections and formal public accountability places it among the democratic polities of the known world, though its reliance on written procedures and fixed electoral terms makes it less continuously participatory than the open assemblies of the Kharesh Tribes or the face-to-face deliberations of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles). The council nonetheless provides a framework for popular participation that is both effective and stable, and it has sustained the prosperity of the inland caravan cities for many centuries.

## The Significance of the Councils

The Vashari Pact Councils are the primary political institutions of the inland Sahrani, and their governance of the caravan trade connecting the coast to the interior is the foundation of the economy of the desert regions. The councils' system of popular election, public accountability, and collective action in the management of the caravan routes has sustained the trade that is the lifeblood of the inland cities. The councils are a model of the capacity of commercial communities to govern themselves effectively, and their institutions have been studied by the merchants and magistrates of every continent.
