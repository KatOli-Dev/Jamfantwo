## The Waters That Connect the North

The [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) is the body of water that lies between [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), a stretch of ocean that is known for its fierce storms, its treacherous currents, and its legends that are among the most dramatic in the known world. The Stormfall Sea is the highway of the northern trade routes, and the ships that cross it carry the goods of the northern continents between the ports of Morvind and the markets of Aethelar. The sea is also the source of some of the most persistent and the most haunting legends that the northern peoples preserve.

The legends of the Stormfall Sea are preserved by the coastal communities of both continents, and they are told by the sailors and the fishermen and the traders who have spent their lives on the northern waters. The legends describe creatures that inhabit the depths, islands that appear and disappear, lights that dance above the waves, and ships that sail with crews of the dead. The Stormfall Sea is a place of mystery and danger, the legends say, and those who sail upon it must be prepared for encounters that defy the ordinary understanding of the world.

## The Serpent of the Deeps

The most famous of the Stormfall Sea legends is the tale of the Serpent of the Deeps, a creature of enormous size that is said to dwell in the deepest waters of the sea and that appears only during the worst of the storms. The Serpent is described as a creature of great length, its body as thick as the mast of a ship and its length extending beyond the range of vision. The Serpent's scales are said to be the colour of the deep water, dark and elusive, and its eyes are said to glow with a pale light that can be seen through the spray of the storm.

The sailors who claim to have seen the Serpent describe the encounter in similar terms across the different versions of the legend. The Serpent rises from the depths when the storm is at its height, and its appearance is a sign that the storm is about to break. The Serpent does not attack the ships that it encounters, the legend says, but the mere presence of the creature is a danger, for the disturbance that its rising causes can capsize the smaller vessels and the fear that it inspires can drive the sailors to panic.

The legend of the Serpent of the Deeps is told as a warning and as a comfort: the warning is that the Stormfall Sea is not a place where the ordinary rules of the world apply, and the comfort is that the Serpent, for all its fearsome appearance, does not harm those who respect the sea and who do not provoke it. The sailors who have seen the Serpent and survived are regarded with respect in the port communities, and their accounts are preserved in the records of the sea that the port councils maintain.

## The Drowned Isles

The legend of the [Drowned Isles](/content/location/natural/feature/drowned-isles) describes a chain of islands that lie beneath the waters of the Stormfall Sea and that rise to the surface only during certain conditions of the tide and the weather. The islands are said to be the remains of a land that sank beneath the sea in the ancient times, and the buildings and the structures of the lost land are visible through the clear water when the conditions are right.

The sailors who claim to have seen the Drowned Isles describe towers and walls and streets that are preserved beneath the water, intact and undisturbed, as though the land had sunk gently rather than violently. The legends say that the Drowned Isles are the home of the spirits of the people who perished when the land sank, and that the spirits can be heard singing on the nights when the moon is full and the water is calm.

The Drowned Isles are the subject of many stories of treasure and loss, and the legends describe the efforts of the treasure-seekers who have attempted to recover the wealth that is said to lie beneath the waters. The treasure-seekers who descend to the Drowned Isles do not return, the legends caution, for the spirits of the lost land do not welcome the intruders who would disturb their rest. The Drowned Isles are a place of beauty and danger, and the wise sailor avoids the area where they are said to lie.

## The Phantom Ship

The legend of the Phantom Ship is the most famous of the Stormfall Sea ghost stories, and versions of it are told in every port of the northern coasts. The Phantom Ship is described as a vessel that appears on the horizon during the fog, a ship of ancient design that sails without crew and that is lit by a pale light that flickers like a candle. The Phantom Ship does not respond to the signals of the living ships, and it vanishes when approached, dissolving into the fog as though it had never been.

The Phantom Ship is said to be the vessel of a crew that died at sea and that continues to sail the Stormfall Sea because they cannot find their way to the shore. The ship is a sign of ill fortune, the sailors say, and those who see the Phantom Ship must prepare for a difficult passage. Some versions of the legend say that the Phantom Ship is looking for a living crew to take the place of the dead, and the sailors who board the phantom are never seen again.

The legend of the Phantom Ship is one of the most persistent of the sea legends, and the scholars of the Cloisters of Reading have collected many accounts of sightings that share a remarkable consistency in their descriptions. The scholars acknowledge that the sightings may have a natural explanation, but they also acknowledge that the consistency of the accounts suggests that the legend has a basis in experiences that the sailors have genuinely had.

## The Stormfall Lights

The lights that appear above the Stormfall Sea during the winter months are one of the most beautiful of the sea's phenomena, and the legends that surround them are among the most poetic of the northern traditions. The Stormfall Lights are bands of colour that dance across the sky, green and purple and red, shifting and flowing like curtains of light in the wind. The lights are visible from both the Aethelaran and the Morvind coasts, and the northern peoples have developed elaborate traditions of interpreting their movements.

The legends of the Stormfall Lights vary among the different coastal communities. The humans of the [Nordhaven](/content/location/settlement/city/nordhaven) coast say that the lights are the spirits of the dead dancing across the sky, celebrating their release from the world of the living. The dwarves of the Morvind coast say that the lights are the reflections of the great forges of the deep halls, shining upward through the stone and the water and the air. The elves of the northern forests say that the lights are the veil between the worlds, the boundary that separates the realm of the living from the realm of the spirits.

The [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) of the far north regard the Stormfall Lights as the most sacred of the natural phenomena, and they conduct ceremonies at the times when the lights are brightest. The Frostwall traditions include the practice of the Light Dance, a movement that the dancers perform to mirror the patterns of the lights, and the dancers believe that the performance of the dance strengthens the connection between the world of the living and the world of the spirits.

## The Wisdom of the Sea

The legends of the Stormfall Sea are not merely stories that the northern peoples tell to entertain themselves during [the long winter](/content/history/the-long-winter) nights. The legends contain the accumulated wisdom of the northern seafarers, the knowledge of the sea and its dangers and its moods that has been preserved through the generations. The legends teach the signs of the approaching storm, the places where the currents are strongest, the times when the sea is safest and the times when it is most dangerous.

The sailors who know the legends are better prepared for the crossings than those who do not, for the legends contain the observations of countless generations of seafarers who have studied the Stormfall Sea and who have passed their knowledge down through the stories. The scholars of the Cloisters of Reading who have studied the legends acknowledge that the knowledge that they contain is genuine, and they have incorporated the observations of the sailors into their own studies of the sea and its behaviour.
