## The Fire That Came From the Stone

The dwarven legend of the First Forge is second only to the Stone Song in the hierarchy of dwarven traditions, and it is the story that the dwarven smiths regard as the foundation of their craft. The First Forge was not a forge in the ordinary sense, the tradition teaches; it was the fire that was awakened from the stone by the song of the mountain, the first heat that the dwarves learned to control and to shape and to use for the creation of the works that would establish the dwarven people as the master crafters of the known world.

The legend is preserved in its fullest form in the great forges of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), where the master smiths recite it to the apprentices as part of their training. The legend tells of the time after the dwarves had been shaped from the stone but before they had learned to work the metals that were present in the mountain around them. The dwarves were aware of the metals and the stones and the crystals, but they did not yet know how to transform them, and they lived in the caverns as the other creatures of the deep places lived, taking what the mountain offered without changing it.

## The Awakening of the Fire

The First Forge was awakened when a dwarf whose name is lost to the ages struck a piece of flint against a vein of iron in the darkness of a deep cavern. The spark that leaped from the strike was the first fire that the dwarves had seen, and it revealed to them the possibility of heat and light in the darkness that had always surrounded them. The tradition does not name the dwarf who made the first spark, for the dwarves hold that the discovery belonged to the people rather than to any individual, and the name has been allowed to fade from memory so that no dwarf may claim the credit that belongs to all.

The spark was small, the legend says, but it was enough. The dwarf who had struck it carried the spark to the community and showed it to the others, and the dwarves gathered around it and watched it flicker and fade and die. The spark was gone, but the memory of the light remained, and the dwarves set themselves to the task of learning how to create and sustain the fire that the spark had revealed.

The first fire that the dwarves learned to sustain was not the fire of the forge but the fire of the hearth, the flame that warmed the caverns and lit the darkness and made the deep places more hospitable to life. The fire of the hearth was the first gift of the First Forge, the dwarves say, and it was only after they had learned to keep the fire that they began to experiment with its power to transform the metals of the mountain.

## The First Smelting

The discovery of smelting was the second great event in the legend of the First Forge, and it is the moment that the dwarven smiths regard as the true beginning of their craft. The legend tells of a dwarf whose name is preserved as Khazad-Vorn, the Stone-Speaker, who noticed that the ore that had fallen into the fire from a crack in the cavern wall had melted and had formed a pool of liquid metal at the base of the flames. Khazad-Vorn was the first to understand that the fire could be used to release the metal from the stone, and his understanding changed the dwarven people forever.

Khazad-Vorn's discovery spread through the dwarven communities with the speed of the fire itself, and within a generation the dwarves had developed the first forges and the first furnaces and the first tools for shaping the molten metal. The dwarves say that the metal itself was eager to be shaped, that it had been waiting within the stone for the fire that would release it, and that the dwarven smiths who worked the metal were not imposing their will upon it but helping it to achieve the form that it had always desired.

## The First Great Works

The first great works of the dwarven smiths were not weapons or tools but objects of beauty and meaning: the first bells, the first mirrors, the first vessels for holding the fire and the water and the light. The dwarves say that the first bell was forged from the metal that was closest to the heart of the mountain, and its tone was the same pitch as the song that the mountain sang, and the dwarves who heard it recognised the sound of their own origin. The first mirror was polished to a finish that had never been seen before, and the dwarves who looked into it saw not only their own faces but the depths of the stone behind them.

The first great work that the dwarves completed was the Forge itself, the permanent structure that housed the fire that the dwarves had awakened from the stone. The Forge was built around the fire, not the fire around the forge, and the structure was designed to channel the heat and the light and the sound of the mountain's song into the work of the smiths. The Forge that stands in the [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) Holds today is said to be built upon the same site as the First Forge, and the fire that burns within it is the direct descendant of the fire that Khazad-Vorn awakened.

## The Forge and the Song

The First Forge is intimately connected to the Stone Song in the dwarven understanding of the world. The fire that was awakened from the stone is the same fire that the song awakened in the beginning, and the work of the smith is a continuation of the work of the song. The dwarven smiths say that the metal has its own voice, as the stone has its own voice, and that the skilled smith is the one who can hear the voice and shape the metal in accordance with its nature.

The smiths who work at the great forges of the dwarven halls undergo a training that is as long and as demanding as the training of the stone-singers. The apprentice smith learns the properties of the different metals and the techniques of the forge and the hammer, but the true training is the training of the ear and the sensibility, the ability to perceive the voice of the metal and the rhythm of the fire. The dwarven smiths say that the forge is a place of listening, and the smith who cannot hear the metal is no true smith.

## The Variations Among the Dwarven Peoples

The legend of the First Forge is preserved in different forms among the dwarven peoples of the different continents. The dwarves of the Stoneheim Holds emphasise the technical aspects of the legend, the details of the smelting and the forging and the composition of the alloys. The dwarves of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) emphasise the spiritual aspects, the connection between the forge and the song and the role of the smith as the mediator between the fire and the metal.

The dwarves of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), who have adapted their craft to the conditions of the desert, preserve a version of the legend that emphasises the cooling and the quenching of the metal in the desert waters. The [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) dwarves say that the First Forge was awakened in the north but that the secrets of the quenching were discovered in the south, and they claim that the waters of the Sahrani oases give their metal a temper that the northern dwarves cannot match. The dispute over the quenching is a friendly one, and the dwarven smiths of the different continents exchange their techniques and their knowledge at the great gatherings of the craft.

## The Continuing Fire

The First Forge is not a legend of the past, the dwarven smiths teach; it is a reality that continues in every forge where the fire burns and the metal is shaped. The fire that the first dwarf awakened from the stone has never been extinguished, and it is passed from forge to forge and from generation to generation through the transfer of the coals and the kindling and the flame itself. The dwarves say that the fire that burns in the forge at Stoneheim today is the same fire that Khazad-Vorn kindled, and that the smith who works at that forge is working in the presence of the first awakening.

The First Forge is the symbol of the dwarven achievement and the dwarven identity, and it is the image that the dwarves hold before themselves when they contemplate their place in the world. The dwarves were shaped from the stone by the song, but they became who they are at the forge, and the fire that they awakened and learned to control is the fire that separates them from the other creatures of the deep places. The First Forge is the beginning of the dwarven story in the world above the stone, and the dwarves honour it still in every spark that leaps from the anvil and every bell that rings from the tower.
