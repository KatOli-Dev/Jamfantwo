## The Tears That Became the Sea

The Sahrani coastal myth of the Sea Mother's gift of salt is among the most ancient and the most cherished of the traditions preserved along the warm shores of Sahrana. The myth is told in the port cities of the northern coast, in the villages of the salt flats, and among the sailors and the fishermen who carry the story with them across the waters of the Auric Ocean. The Sea Mother is the principal deity of the maritime peoples of Sahrana, and her gift of salt is the foundation of the coastal economy and the central image of the coastal faith.

The myth begins with the Sea Mother alone in the void before the world was formed. The Sea Mother was the first being, the tradition teaches, and she was complete in herself and lacked nothing, but she was moved by a compassion that arose from her own nature and that she could not resist. The Sea Mother looked upon the emptiness that surrounded her, and she wept, and her tears fell into the void and became the sea. The sea was salt because the tears of the Sea Mother were salt, and the salt was the substance of her compassion made manifest in the material world.

## The Gift to the People

The Sea Mother did not create the world and then withdraw from it, the tradition teaches. She remained present in the sea and in the salt, and she sought to share her gift with the people who would later come to inhabit the coasts and the islands of the warm seas. The Sea Mother called the first people to the shore and taught them to gather the salt from the flats and the rocks, and she showed them how to use the salt to preserve their food and to heal their wounds and to seal their bonds of hospitality.

The gift of salt was the Sea Mother's first gift to the people, and it was the most precious of all her gifts, for the salt contained the essence of her compassion and the memory of her tears. The people who received the salt were changed by it, the tradition says, and they became the ancestors of the coastal peoples who now inhabit the shores of Sahrana and the islands of the Auric Ocean. The salt entered into their blood and their bones, and they carried the Sea Mother's compassion within them from that time forward.

## The Salt Flats of the Northern Coast

The salt flats of the northern coast of Sahrana are the most visible sign of the Sea Mother's gift, and the tradition teaches that these flats were formed where the Sea Mother's tears first touched the land. The salt flats extend for many miles along the coast, and they are worked by the salt-gatherers who follow the methods that the Sea Mother taught to the first people. The salt-gatherers are regarded with great respect in the coastal communities, for they are the ones who maintain the connection between the people and the Sea Mother's gift.

The work of the salt-gatherers is demanding and requires both skill and patience. The salt is harvested at the end of the dry season, when the sun has evaporated the water from the shallow pans that the gatherers maintain along the coast. The salt crystals that form in the pans are gathered by hand and carried to the settlements, where they are cleaned and sorted and prepared for trade. The gatherers say that the salt from the northern coast is the finest in the known world, and the traders who carry it to the inland cities of the Vashari Pact and the Kharesh Tribes confirm this judgment.

## The Salt Caravans

The salt that is harvested along the northern coast is carried inland by the caravans that follow the ancient trade routes across the Kharesh. The salt caravans are among the most important institutions of the Sahrani economy, and they have been operating for longer than the written records of the region extend. The caravans carry salt from the coast to the inland cities and the desert settlements, and they return with the spices and the dyes and the precious stones that are the wealth of the interior.

The salt caravans are accompanied by the priests of the Sea Mother's Court, who conduct the rites that ensure the safety of the journey and the proper handling of the sacred substance. The priests say that the salt must be treated with respect, for it contains the Sea Mother's compassion, and the caravan masters who treat the salt carelessly are said to suffer bad fortune on their journeys. The salt caravans are the means by which the Sea Mother's gift reaches the peoples of the interior, and the salt that arrives in the desert cities is greeted with ceremonies of thanks and celebration.

## The Healing Properties of Salt

The Sea Mother's gift of salt is not only a substance of economic value; it is also a substance of healing, and the coastal peoples have developed a detailed knowledge of the medicinal uses of salt. The salt is used to treat wounds and infections, to preserve food against spoilage, and to purify water for drinking. The coastal healers say that the salt draws out the corruption from wounds and the bitterness from the spirit, and they prescribe salt baths and salt rubs and salt infusions for a wide range of ailments.

The healing properties of salt are understood by the coastal peoples as a manifestation of the Sea Mother's compassion. The salt that heals is the same substance as the tears that the Sea Mother wept for the world, and the healers who use salt in their work are continuing the Sea Mother's work of compassion. The tradition teaches that the salt must be used with gratitude and reverence, for the misuse of the salt is a misuse of the Sea Mother's gift and brings misfortune upon the healer and the patient alike.

## The Variations Among the Coastal Communities

The myth of the Sea Mother's gift of salt is preserved in different forms among the coastal communities of Sahrana and the islands of the Auric Ocean. The communities of the northern coast, who are closest to the great salt flats, emphasise the economic and practical aspects of the gift: the salt as the foundation of trade and the basis of the coastal prosperity. The communities of the eastern islands, who live further from the salt flats and who must import their salt from the mainland, emphasise the sacred and spiritual aspects of the gift: the salt as the substance of the Sea Mother's compassion and the means of her continuing presence among her people.

The communities of the southern coast, who are closest to the Kharesh and who trade most extensively with the interior, preserve a version of the myth that emphasises the journey of the salt from the coast to the desert. In this version, the salt caravan is itself a sacred journey, and the salt-gatherers and the caravan masters and the desert traders are all participants in a ritual that connects the Sea Mother to the peoples of the interior. The southern version of the myth is the most elaborate and the most detailed of the known versions, and it includes descriptions of the rites that the caravaners perform at each stage of the journey.

## The Sea Mother in the Modern World

The Sea Mother's Court in Khalisar is the principal temple of the Sea Mother in the known world, and the gift of salt is the central teaching of the faith. The priests of the Court maintain the salt flats and conduct the rites that ensure the continued abundance of the harvest, and they teach the tradition of the gift to the novices and to the faithful who come to the temple seeking instruction. The Sea Mother's Court is the most visible institution of the maritime faith, but every coastal community maintains its own smaller temple or shrine where the Sea Mother is honoured and the gift of salt is remembered.

The Sea Mother's gift of salt is not only a myth of the past; it is a living tradition that continues to shape the lives of the coastal peoples. The salt that is harvested from the flats and carried by the caravans and used in the households and the healing houses is the same salt that the Sea Mother gave to the first people, and the compassion that moved the Sea Mother to weep for the world is the same compassion that the coastal peoples extend to one another in their daily lives. The gift of salt is the Sea Mother's continuing presence among her people, and as long as the salt is harvested and traded and used with reverence, the Sea Mother will remain with the world.
