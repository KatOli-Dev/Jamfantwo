## The Guardian Who Never Sleeps

The legend of the Watcher on the Wall is the central myth of the Shield and Song tradition, and it is the story that the order teaches to every new practitioner as the foundation of the calling. The legend tells of the Watcher, the divine being who stands at the boundary between the known world and the darkness that lies beyond it, who guards against the forces that would destroy the order of the world and who never sleeps and never turns away from the watch. The Watcher is the model for every practitioner of the Shield and Song, and the discipline of the watch and the song are the means by which the practitioner participates in the Watcher's keeping of the world.

The legend is preserved in the Memory Houses of the Shield and Song, where the singers maintain the versions that have been handed down through the generations. The legend is not a single narrative but a collection of related stories that together describe the nature and the work of the Watcher. The different stories emphasise different aspects of the Watcher's character, and the practitioners are encouraged to study all of them in order to gain a complete understanding of the figure who stands at the centre of their tradition.

## The Standing at the Boundary

The Watcher stands at the boundary between the known world and the unknown, the legend teaches, and the wall that the Watcher guards is not a wall of stone but a wall of attention, a continuous awareness of the danger that lies beyond the light of the hearth and the song of the community. The Watcher does not prevent the darkness from approaching; the darkness is always present, and it cannot be prevented from existing. The Watcher prevents the darkness from entering the world in a form that would destroy it, and the Watcher does this by the simple act of watching.

The legend describes the Watcher as a figure of great stillness and great patience, standing at the wall with a shield in one hand and a song in the throat. The shield is not a weapon of offence; it is a tool of defence, and the Watcher uses it to turn aside the blows that are aimed at the world. The song is not a song of celebration; it is a song of attention, and the Watcher sings it continuously to maintain the awareness that the watch requires. The shield and the song are the two halves of the Watcher's work, and they correspond to the shield-half and the song-half of the practitioners who follow the Watcher's example.

## The Watcher and the Dark

The darkness that the Watcher guards against is not a physical darkness, the legend teaches, but a condition of the world that would arise if the watch were ever abandoned. The darkness is the absence of attention, the neglect of the boundary, the forgetting of the songs that maintain the community's memory of itself. The darkness is not a being or a force that acts against the world; it is the natural condition of the world when the watch is not kept, and the Watcher is the one who keeps the darkness from becoming the reality in which the world must live.

The legend describes the darkness in terms that are intentionally vague, for the Shield and Song teaches that the darkness cannot be described because it has no form of its own. The darkness takes the form of the attention that is withdrawn from it, and it becomes what the watcher fails to watch against. The darkness is the potential for chaos that lies beneath the order of the world, and the Watcher is the one who maintains the order by the continuous act of attention.

The practitioners of the Shield and Song are taught to understand the darkness not as an enemy to be defeated but as a condition to be managed. The darkness cannot be destroyed, for it is the absence that makes the presence meaningful, and the destruction of the darkness would be the destruction of the world itself. The work of the Watcher and the work of the practitioner is not to eliminate the darkness but to keep it at the boundary, to prevent it from entering the world in a form that would overwhelm the order that the community has established.

## The Watcher and the Community

The legend teaches that the Watcher does not stand alone, and the wall that the Watcher guards is the same wall that every practitioner of the Shield and Song is called to guard. The Watcher is the first and the greatest of the watchers, but every practitioner who undertakes the discipline of the watch is participating in the same work, and the combined attention of all the watchers strengthens the wall and maintains the boundary against the encroaching darkness.

The practitioners of the Shield and Song are taught to see themselves as standing with the Watcher at the wall, and the evening review is the moment when the practitioner renews the awareness of the watch. The review is the act of attention that the practitioner brings to the events of the day, and it is the means by which the practitioner participates in the Watcher's continuous attention. The review is the shield and the song of the individual practitioner, and the sum of all the reviews is the shield and the song of the community.

## The Different Aspects of the Watcher

The different versions of the legend preserved in the Memory Houses describe the Watcher in different terms, and the practitioners are taught to understand that the Watcher is too great to be captured by any single description. The version preserved in the Memory Houses of the Standing Watch emphasises the shield aspect of the Watcher, the stillness and the patience and the endurance of the figure who stands at the boundary without moving. The version preserved in the Memory Houses of the southern communities emphasises the song aspect, the attention and the memory and the continuous awareness that the Watcher maintains.

The version preserved in the oldest Memory Houses of the northern frontier describes the Watcher as a figure of both aspects united, a being who holds the shield and sings the song simultaneously and who never allows either aspect to outweigh the other. This version is regarded as the most complete of the known versions, and it is the version that the practitioners are encouraged to meditate upon when they seek to understand the full nature of the calling.

## The Watcher and the Kindred Traditions

The Shield and Song maintains correspondence with the Still Flame and the Unwritten Name, and the practitioners of the three traditions have exchanged their understandings of the Watcher for many generations. The Still Flame regards the Watcher as an aspect of the Flame, the warmth that maintains the boundary between life and death, the light that keeps the darkness at bay. The Unwritten Name regards the Watcher as a manifestation of the mystery that cannot be named, the presence that stands at the boundary between the known and the unknown and that cannot be described in terms that the mind can grasp.

The practitioners of the Shield and Song do not claim that their understanding of the Watcher is the only true understanding, and they welcome the insights that the kindred traditions bring. The Watcher is too great to be contained by any single tradition, the order teaches, and the different understandings of the Watcher are like different views of the same mountain, each valid from the perspective that it occupies.

## The Watcher's Promise

The legend concludes with the promise that the Watcher will never abandon the wall, that the attention will never falter, that the song will never cease. The promise is not a guarantee of safety or a promise that the darkness will never approach; it is a promise of continuity, a commitment that the watch will be maintained as long as the world endures. The practitioners of the Shield and Song are invited to share in this promise, to stand with the Watcher at the wall, and to add their own attention to the great attention that holds the boundary against the darkness.

The Watcher on the Wall is the image that the practitioners of the Shield and Song carry with them through the days and the nights of their calling, and it is the image that sustains them when the watch is long and the darkness presses close. The Watcher stands, and the practitioner stands with the Watcher, and the wall holds, and the world continues.
