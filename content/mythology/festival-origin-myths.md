## The Beginnings of the Celebrations

The festivals of the known world are the occasions when the communities gather to mark the turning of the seasons, the important passages of life, and the events that the people remember from their history. The origin myths that explain the beginnings of the festivals are among the most varied and the most colourful of the world's folklore traditions, and they reveal the values and the concerns of the cultures that celebrate them. The myths explain not only why the festival is held but how the practices that the festival involves were established, and the myths are retold as part of the festival observance.

The scholars of the Cloisters of Reading have collected the origin myths of the major festivals from every region of the known world, and they have produced a comprehensive catalogue that reveals both the diversity and the common patterns of the festival traditions. The following accounts describe the origin myths of some of the most important festivals, though many more festivals are observed in the different regions and communities.

## The Festival of the First Kindling

The Festival of the First Kindling is the principal winter festival of the Still Flame, and it is observed in the hearth-halls throughout the lands where the faith is practised. The origin myth of the festival tells of the first kindling, the moment when the Still Flame was first awakened in the world. The myth describes a time when the world was cold and dark and the people had no fire, and a woman of the early people discovered that the fire could be kindled by the striking of the flint and the tending of the flame.

The myth tells that the woman who discovered the fire was named Sera Ambervale in the traditions of the southern hearth-halls, though the northern hearth-halls preserve a different name for the discoverer. Sera, or whatever name the discoverer bears, taught the people to kindle the fire and to tend it and to keep it through the winter, and the people gathered on the longest night of the year to celebrate the gift of the fire. The Festival of the First Kindling marks this gathering, and the faithful light the new fire at the hearth-halls and carry the flame to their own hearths.

The festival is observed with the lighting of the great bonfire at the centre of the community, the sharing of the feast that the community has prepared, and the singing of the songs that the Still Flame preserves. The origin myth is recited by the hearth-keeper at the beginning of the festival, and the people who hear the myth are reminded of the gift that the fire represents and of the duty to tend the fire and to share its warmth with those who are in need.

## The Festival of the Long Night

The Festival of the Long Night is the principal winter festival of the northern peoples of Morvind and the Frostwall Clans, and it is observed on the longest night of the year. The origin myth of the festival tells of a time when the darkness threatened to become permanent, when the sun failed to rise and the world was covered by a night that seemed as though it would never end. The people were afraid, and they gathered together and built the great fires that would light the darkness and remind the sun of its duty to return.

The myth says that the fires that the people built were seen by the spirits of the sky, and the spirits were moved by the determination of the people and by the beauty of the flames that the people had kindled. The spirits interceded with the powers that governed the sun, and the sun returned, and the darkness was defeated. The Festival of the Long Night marks the return of the sun, and the people light the great bonfires and keep them burning through the night as the people did in the time of the myth.

The Festival of the Long Night is the most important of the Frostwall observances, and the communities that are separated by the winter weather make every effort to gather at the great halls for the celebration. The festival includes the lighting of the bonfires, the sharing of the winter stores, the telling of the stories of the ancestors, and the singing of the songs that the Frostwall Clans preserve. The origin myth is recited by the elders at the beginning of the festival, and the people who hear the myth are reminded of the determination that the ancestors showed and of the power of the community to overcome the darkness.

## The Festival of the First Grain

The Festival of the First Grain is the principal harvest festival of the halfling communities, and it is observed at the beginning of the harvest season. The origin myth of the festival tells of the first harvest, the time when the hidden folk taught the halflings to cultivate the grain and to prepare the bread that sustains the community. The myth describes the first meeting between the halflings and the hidden folk, the sharing of the seeds, and the first harvest feast that the halflings and the hidden folk shared together.

The Festival of the First Grain is the occasion when the halflings renew their bonds with the hidden folk and with the land that sustains them. The festival includes the baking of the first bread from the new grain, the sharing of the feast with the community, and the leaving of the offerings at the edge of the fields for the hidden folk. The origin myth is recited by the oldest member of the community at the beginning of the festival, and the people who hear the myth are reminded of the gift that the hidden folk gave to the halflings and of the duty to share the bounty of the harvest with those who are in need.

## The Festival of the Salt Caravan

The Festival of the Salt Caravan is the principal festival of the Sahrani coastal communities, and it is observed at the departure of the first salt caravan of the season. The origin myth of the festival tells of the Sea Mother's gift of salt, the moment when the Sea Mother gave the salt to the people of the coast and taught them to harvest it and to carry it to the peoples of the interior. The myth describes the first caravan, the journey that the first salt-carriers made across the desert, and the welcome that they received from the desert peoples.

The Festival of the Salt Caravan is observed with the blessing of the caravan by the priests of the Sea Mother's Court, the loading of the salt onto the pack animals, and the departure of the caravan amidst the cheers of the community. The festival includes the sharing of the salt bread, the singing of the caravan songs, and the telling of the stories of the great caravans of the past. The origin myth is recited by the high priest at the beginning of the festival, and the people who hear the myth are reminded of the gift that the Sea Mother gave and of the importance of the salt trade to the prosperity of the coast.

## The Festival of the Stone Song

The Festival of the Stone Song is the principal festival of the dwarven peoples of the Stoneheim Holds and the Karhold Realm, and it is observed at the winter solstice and the summer solstice. The origin myth of the festival tells of the first sounding of the Stone Song, the moment when the song awoke within the stone and raised the mountains and formed the deep halls. The myth describes the awakening of the first dwarves and the shaping of the first halls, and the festival is the occasion when the dwarves renew their connection to the song.

The Festival of the Stone Song is observed with the singing of the deep songs by the stone-singers, the displaying of the works that the carvers have completed during the year, and the sharing of the feast that the community has prepared. The festival includes the ceremony of the new carving, when the master carvers begin the work on the new pieces that will be completed during the following year. The origin myth is recited by the eldest stone-singer at the beginning of the festival, and the dwarves who hear the myth are reminded of the song that shaped them and of the work that they must continue.
