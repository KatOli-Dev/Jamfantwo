## The Edge of the Known

The peoples of the known world have always wondered about the lands that lie beyond the oceans, and the myths of the world beyond are among the most persistent and the most varied of the folklore traditions. The myths describe lands that lie beyond the reach of the ordinary voyagers, places of wonder and danger and mystery that the sailors have glimpsed from a distance or that the spirits have described to the dreamers. The myths are not maps in the sense that the scholars of the Cloisters of Reading understand maps; they are visions of possibility, accounts of what might exist beyond the edges of the known world.

The scholars who study the myths of the world beyond the ocean have collected accounts from every maritime culture of the known world, and they have catalogued the descriptions of the lands that the myths describe. The accounts are remarkably diverse, but they share a common theme: the lands beyond the ocean are places where the ordinary rules of the world do not apply, where the laws of nature are different, where the beings that inhabit them are unlike any that the known world contains.

## The Islands of the Blessed

The most widespread of the myths of the world beyond the ocean is the story of the Islands of the Blessed, a chain of islands that lies somewhere to the west of the known continents, beyond the reach of the ordinary navigators. The Islands of the Blessed are described as a place of eternal spring, where the climate is mild and the fruits grow without cultivation and the waters are clear and clean. The islands are inhabited by the spirits of the dead who have lived worthy lives, the myths say, and the islands are a reward for the virtue that the dead displayed in the world of the living.

The Islands of the Blessed appear in the traditions of the coastal peoples of every continent, and the descriptions of the islands are remarkably consistent across the different versions. The islands are said to be visible only to those who are ready to see them, and the sailors who have glimpsed the islands from a distance describe a glow on the horizon that is different from the ordinary light of the sun and the sky. The sailors who have attempted to reach the islands say that the islands recede as the ship approaches, and that the islands cannot be reached by those who seek them with the intention of returning.

The myth of the Islands of the Blessed is the foundation of the mariners' belief in the afterlife, and the sailors who die at sea are said to be carried to the islands by the currents that the spirits control. The families of the sailors who are lost at sea take comfort in the belief that the dead have reached the islands and that they dwell there in peace and plenty.

## The Sunken Lands

The myths of the sunken lands describe continents that once rose above the ocean and that have since sunk beneath the waters. The sunken lands are said to have been the homes of great civilisations, peoples who achieved a level of knowledge and power that exceeded anything that the known world has produced, and who were destroyed when their pride or their folly caused the land to sink. The sunken lands are the subject of the most elaborate and the most haunting of the world-beyond-the-ocean myths.

The most famous of the sunken lands is called Aurath in the traditions of the eastern peoples, a continent that is said to have lain beyond the rising sun and that was the home of the first human civilisation. Aurath was a land of great cities and great learning, the myths say, and the people of Aurath mastered the arts of magic and technology long before the peoples of the known world had emerged from their primitive state. The people of Aurath became arrogant, the myths continue, and they attempted to challenge the powers that governed the world, and the powers destroyed Aurath, sinking the entire continent beneath the ocean in a single night.

The myth of Aurath is preserved in the libraries of the Cloisters of Reading, where the scholars have debated whether the myth preserves a memory of an actual historical event. The stone itself does not support the existence of a sunken continent in the eastern ocean, the scholars note, but the consistency of the myth across the different cultures of the eastern lands suggests that the myth has a basis in some actual event that the memory has transformed over time.

## The Land of the Giants

The myths of the land of the giants describe a continent that lies to the far north, beyond the Stormfall Sea and the ice fields that surround the northern pole. The land of the giants is said to be a place of great mountains and deep valleys, of forests that are older than any forests of the known world, of rivers that are wider and deeper than any rivers that the northern peoples have seen. The land is inhabited by giants, the myths say, beings of great size and great strength who live in the mountains and who are the ancestors of the giants that are found in the known world.

The land of the giants is described in the traditions of the Frostwall Clans and the northern peoples of Morvind, and the descriptions are consistent across the different versions. The land is said to be visible on clear days from the northernmost headlands of Morvind, a line of white on the horizon that the sailors call the Wall of the World. The explorers who have attempted to reach the land say that the ice fields are impassable and that the currents that flow from the north are too strong for the ordinary vessels.

The myth of the land of the giants is the foundation of the northern peoples' understanding of the giants that they encounter in their own lands. The giants of the known world are said to be the descendants of the giants who left the northern continent and who settled in the mountains of Morvind and Aethelar, and the giants who remain in the northern continent are said to be the oldest and the most powerful of the giant peoples.

## The Southern Continent

The myths of the southern continent describe a land that lies beyond the southern reaches of Sahrana, a continent of green and fertile lands that is warmed by the sun that passes directly overhead. The southern continent is said to be the home of the spirits of the heat and the light, the powers that govern the sun and the growth of the plants, and the peoples of the southern continent are said to be the most ancient of all the human peoples.

The myth of the southern continent is preserved in the traditions of the Southern Reach and the Vashari Pact, and the descriptions of the continent vary between the two traditions. The Southern Reach traditions describe the southern continent as a place of great cities and great learning, the source of the knowledge that the Southern Reach has preserved. The Vashari traditions describe the southern continent as a place of wild and untamed beauty, a land where the spirits of the desert dwell in their original forms.

The explorers of the Southern Reach have attempted to reach the southern continent on several occasions, and the expeditions have returned with reports of a coastline that appears on the horizon but that cannot be reached, as though the continent recedes before the advancing ships. The myth of the southern continent remains a myth, and the scholars of the Cloisters of Reading continue to debate whether the continent is real or whether it is a projection of the human longing for a land that is beyond the reach of the known world.

## The Meaning of the Myths

The myths of the world beyond the ocean are not merely stories that the maritime peoples tell to pass the time during the long voyages. The myths express the human sense of possibility, the belief that the world is larger than the known world and that there are places beyond the horizon that the human mind cannot imagine. The myths are the expressions of the hope that the world is not fully known, that there are still discoveries to be made, that the edges of the known world are not the edges of the world itself.

The scholars of the Cloisters of Reading who study the myths of the world beyond the ocean observe that the myths have a practical value as well as a spiritual one. The myths encourage the explorers to venture beyond the known shores, and the explorers who have returned from the distant voyages have expanded the knowledge of the world and have brought back the goods and the knowledge that have enriched the known world. The myths are the maps of the possible, and the scholars acknowledge that the myths have been as important as the actual maps in the exploration of the world.
