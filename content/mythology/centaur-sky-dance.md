## The Dance That Formed the Plains

The centaur creation myth, known as the Sky Dance, is the central tradition of the centaur peoples of the Plains Confederation and the open grasslands of Aethelar. The myth tells of a time before the world had shape, when the sky was empty and the land was flat and featureless and nothing moved except the darkness itself. The constellations that now wheel above the world were not yet formed, and the centaurs were not yet present, and there was only the stillness of the beginning.

The Sky Dance is not a story that the centaurs tell in the way that the human peoples tell their creation stories. The Sky Dance is a thing that is performed, a pattern of movement that the centaurs enact at the great festivals of the year, and the performance is the myth. The centaurs do not believe that the world was created in the past and then left to continue on its own; they believe that the world is being created continuously by the movement of the constellations and by the centaurs' own participation in that movement. The Sky Dance is the world's continuing creation, and the centaurs who dance it are the co-creators of the reality they inhabit.

## The First Dancers

In the beginning, the centaur tradition teaches, there were no stars in the sky. The darkness above was empty and silent, and the land below was formless and still, and there was no difference between one place and another and no direction by which to measure movement. The first dancers were not yet present, and the world was not yet born, and the waiting was longer than any measure of time could capture.

The first dancers emerged from the darkness, the tradition says, not as beings that had existed before but as patterns that crystallised from the potential that lay within the emptiness. The constellations were not created by any will or intention; they formed themselves, as crystals form in a cooling solution, and their formation was the first movement in the world. The constellations danced, and their dancing traced the patterns that became the features of the land: the rise and fall of the dancers' hooves became the hills and the valleys, the sweep of their strides became the rivers, the turning of their bodies became the curves of the shoreline.

The centaurs were formed from the same dance, the tradition teaches. The centaurs are not a separate creation but a continuation of the first movement, and the body of the centaur recapitulates the pattern of the constellations: the four legs correspond to the four quarters of the sky, the torso corresponds to the world itself, the head and the arms reach upward toward the stars from which the centaurs were formed. The centaur is a constellation made flesh, and the dance of the centaur is a continuation of the dance that formed the world.

## The Patterns in the Sky

The constellations that the centaurs honour are not the same constellations that the human astronomers study. The centaurs recognise patterns in the night sky that the human scholars do not see, and these patterns are the records of the first dance preserved in the positions of the stars. The centaur constellation known as the Great Herd, for example, is a pattern of stars that the human astronomers regard as six separate constellations but that the centaurs see as a single great formation stretching across a third of the visible sky.

The centaurs divide the year according to the positions of the constellations, and the great festivals of the centaur calendar are timed to the rising and the setting of the principal patterns. The Festival of the Opening Dance, which marks the beginning of the centaur year, occurs when the constellation of the First Dancer rises at sunset, and the centaurs gather from across the plains to perform the dance that renews the world for another cycle. The Festival of the Closing Dance, which marks the end of the year, occurs when the same constellation sets at dawn, and the centaurs perform the dance that brings the year to its close and prepares the world for the new beginning.

## The Dance and the Land

The centaurs believe that the land itself responds to the dance, and that the health of the plains depends upon the proper performance of the patterns. The grass grows where the centaurs dance, the tradition says, and the rivers flow where the centaurs have passed, and the herds of game follow the routes that the centaurs mark with their movement. The centaur migrations across the plains are not merely practical movements in search of pasture and water; they are ritual journeys that maintain the pattern of the world.

The centaur herds move across the plains in patterns that correspond to the movements of the constellations. The great north-south migration of the spring follows the rising of the constellation that the centaurs call the Long March, and the east-west movement of the autumn follows the setting of the constellation that the centaurs call the Wide Turning. The centaurs do not choose their routes arbitrarily; they follow the patterns that were established in the first dance, and their movement is a continuation of the creation.

The centaurs of the Plains Confederation maintain that the dance is the reason their lands have remained fertile and abundant while other regions have declined. The scholars of the human kingdoms regard this claim with scepticism, but even the most sceptical of the human observers acknowledge that the centaur grasslands are among the most productive in the known world and that the centaur herds have maintained their way of life for longer than the human kingdoms have existed.

## The Variations Among the Herds

The Sky Dance is not a single tradition but a family of related practices that vary among the different centaur herds. The herds of the eastern plains emphasise the patterns of the rising stars, and their dances are oriented toward the dawn and the renewal of the day. The herds of the western plains emphasise the patterns of the setting stars, and their dances are oriented toward the dusk and the completion of the cycle. The herds of the northern plains emphasise the patterns of the winter stars, which are visible only during the coldest months, and their dances are the most demanding and the most austere of the centaur traditions.

The variation among the herds is not regarded as a problem by the centaurs, for the Sky Dance teaches that the world is expressed differently in different places and that no single pattern exhausts the possibilities of the dance. The herds exchange dancers at the great festivals of the year, and the visiting dancers perform the patterns of their home herds for the host community, and the diversity of the dance is celebrated as a sign of the richness of the world.

## The Relation to the Other Traditions

The centaurs regard the creation myths of the other peoples with interest and respect, and the centaur scholars who have studied the Stone Song and the First Awakening and the human origin stories have observed that each of these traditions contains elements that are present in the Sky Dance as well. The dwarven Stone Song speaks of the resonance that shapes the stone, and the centaurs recognise in this resonance the same pattern that their dance expresses in movement. The elven First Awakening speaks of the wonder of the first awareness, and the centaurs recognise in this wonder the same quality that they experience in the dance.

The centaurs do not claim that the Sky Dance is the only true account of the world's origins. The centaurs maintain that the world is larger than any single tradition can capture and that the different creation myths express different aspects of a reality that no single story can contain. The Sky Dance is the tradition of the centaurs, and it is true for the centaurs, and the centaurs do not require that it be true for anyone else.

## The Continuing Dance

The Sky Dance is not a myth that belongs to the past. The constellations continue to wheel above the world, and the centaurs continue to dance across the plains, and the creation continues with every performance of the patterns. The centaurs say that if the dance were ever to cease, the world would return to the formless stillness from which it emerged, and the stars would fade from the sky and the grass would wither and the rivers would cease to flow. The dance is the world's life, and the centaurs are the keepers of the dance, and as long as the centaurs dance, the world will continue.
