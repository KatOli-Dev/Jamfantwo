## The Pattern Cast across the World

The moon-weave is the name given by the chroniclers of the Khalisar Council to a pattern of influence that is held, across many traditions, to pass across the world with the moon's motion. The belief is not uniform: different peoples describe the weave in different terms, and some deny its existence altogether. But the observation that the moon is accompanied by a change in the behaviour of resonance, of living things, and of the weather is widespread enough that no account of the known world can omit it.

The weave is not a physical substance, nor is it visible to the eye. It is described as a pattern that moves with the moon's reflected light, a subtle realignment of the resonance of things that follows the moon across the sky and settles into the ground as the moon passes. The Khalisar Council's practitioners claim to detect the weave in the varying ease with which patterns may be read during the moon's different phases. The Sylvan Court has traditionally resisted this explanation, holding that the variations observed are the result of changes in the practitioner's own pattern rather than in the world, but the Court has not produced an alternative account that commands general assent.

## Observations That Precede the Explanation

The observations on which the belief in the moon-weave rests are not in dispute. It is agreed by every tradition that the reading of patterns is easier during the nights when the moon is full and more difficult during the new moon, when the moon is absent from the sky. The difference is subtle and inconstant, and it is not so large that a skilled practitioner would be prevented from working during the dark of the moon, but it is consistent enough across long periods of measurement that the traditions accept it as a real phenomenon.

It is further agreed that the domain of the effect is not limited to the Art. The behaviour of many animals changes with the moon, and the accounts of farmers and shepherds across all three continents describe patterns of activity in their charges that follow the lunar cycle. The growth of some plants is observed to accelerate or slow with the phase of the moon, and the gardeners of the Sahrani city-states have kept records of these correlations for many generations.

The question is whether these observations are evidence of a single pattern that moves with the moon, or whether they are the result of many separate causes that happen to share the moon's cycle. The proponents of the moon-weave argue that the unity of the observations, their consistency across unrelated domains, and the absence of any other explanation that accounts for all of them together require the hypothesis of a single influence. The sceptics argue that the moon is a large and prominent body and that it is not surprising that many things should be affected by it independently, without any underlying unity.

## The Weave as a Rhythmic Grid

The Khalisar Council has produced the most detailed account of the moon-weave, and the account is worth setting down at some length, for it represents the most systematic attempt to describe the phenomenon. The Council holds that the moon-weave is a pattern in the resonance of the world that moves with the moon and that the pattern is not a single field of influence but a rhythmic grid that passes across the surface of the land with the moon's apparent motion.

According to the Council's account, the weave consists of a regular alternation of areas of heightened and lowered resonance, arranged in bands that follow the moon's path across the sky. A practitioner who stands within a band of heightened resonance finds patterns easier to read and to reshape, while a practitioner within a band of lowered resonance finds the same operations more difficult. The bands are not fixed in place but move with the moon, so that a given location passes through a full cycle of heightened and lowered resonance with each lunar day.

The Council's practitioners have published measurements that they claim support this account, but the measurements have been challenged by the Velden Compact, whose own experiments have not reproduced the Council's results. The dispute has not been resolved, and the Council continues to refine its measurements while the Compact continues to question them. The Council's account is presented here as an account of a particular tradition, not as a settled finding.

## The Moon-Weave in Folk Tradition

Beyond the academies and the traditions of the Art, the belief in the moon-weave is expressed in the customs and observances of many peoples. The farmers of the Aethelaran lowlands time their planting by the moon, and the herders of the Confederation Plains move their flocks according to the phase. The Sahrani city-states hold their principal markets in the week following the full moon, when the influence of the weave is said to be most favourable to commerce and negotiation.

The association of the moon-weave with favourable outcomes is not universal. The mariners of the Auric Ocean and the Greyring Ocean have traditionally regarded the full moon as an ill omen for long voyages, and many fishing communities will not put to sea during the three nights of the full moon. The folk explanation varies: some say that the weave confuses the navigator's sense of direction, while others say that it attracts creatures of the deep that are better avoided.

The festivals of the Long Night and the Crown Harvest are timed to the moon, and the association of the lunar cycle with the cycle of the seasons is one of the foundations of the calendar used across much of Aethelar. The weave is not worshipped, for the traditions that describe it do not treat it as a deity or a spirit; it is a property of the order of the world, no more to be worshipped than the turning of the seasons or the falling of the rain.

## Whether the Weave Can Be Measured

The question of whether the moon-weave can be measured by instruments has been pursued by the Khalisar Council and, independently, by the Velden Compact. The Council has constructed devices that it claims detect the passage of the weave through changes in the behaviour of balanced needles and suspended weights, but the Compact has been unable to replicate the results with its own instruments.

The practitioners of the Sylvan Court have declined to participate in the measurement experiments, on the grounds that the weave is of its nature perceptible only through the Art and that attempts to capture it with mechanical devices confuse the nature of the phenomenon. The Court does not deny the existence of the weave; it denies that the weave can be known through any means except the disciplined attention of a practitioner.

The academic consensus, insofar as a consensus can be said to exist, is that the moon-weave has not been conclusively demonstrated by instrumental measurement but that the weight of observational evidence from the Art and from agriculture, herding, and navigation is sufficient to support its reality as a working hypothesis. The question remains open, and the traditions continue to gather observations and to refine their accounts.

## Effects beyond the Art

The influence of the moon-weave extends beyond the Art into areas that the practitioners of the traditions do not always agree upon. The Khalisar Council has published studies suggesting that the weave affects the disposition of living things, making creatures more active and more responsive during the heightened periods and more subdued during the lowered ones. The Council's studies are based on observations of market behaviour, animal activity, and the reported dreams of human and dwarven subjects, and the results have been suggestive but not conclusive.

The Velden Compact has conducted its own studies of the weave's effect on human temperament, using the records of disputes, marriages, and commercial transactions in the city of Velden over a period of several decades. The Compact's analysis found a weak correlation between the phase of the moon and the frequency of disputes and agreements, but the correlation fell below the threshold that the Compact's analysts regard as significant. The Compact has not repeated the study.

The Sylvan Court, characteristically, has taken no position on the effects of the weave beyond the Art, treating the question as one that belongs to natural philosophy rather than to the discipline of the practitioner. The Court maintains that its concern is with what the practitioner can do, not with what the world does in the absence of the practitioner, and that the question of the weave's effects on the non-practitioner world is a matter for scholars of other kinds.

## The Unsettled Question of Origin

The traditions disagree about the origin of the moon-weave, assuming it to be real. The Khalisar Council holds that the weave is a property of the moon itself, carried by the moon's reflected light to the surface of the world. The Velden Compact holds that the weave arises from the interaction of the moon's pattern with the pattern of the world and is no more a property of the moon than the wake of a ship is a property of the ship.

A minority view, preserved in the libraries of Avaros, holds that the moon-weave is not caused by the moon at all but is an independent rhythm in the resonance of the world that happens to coincide with the lunar cycle. The proponents of this view point to the variability of the observed effects and to the difficulty of measuring the weave as evidence that the correlation with the moon may be incidental rather than causal. The view has not gained wide acceptance, but it has not been refuted, and its persistence in the literature of natural philosophy is an indication of how much remains unknown.

For the present, the moon-weave remains a subject of investigation and dispute. It is one of several phenomena that practitioners and natural philosophers have attempted to incorporate into the theory of resonance, and the disputes that surround it are characteristic of a field in which the available evidence is suggestive but incomplete.
