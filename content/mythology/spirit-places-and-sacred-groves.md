## The Spirits of the Land

The belief in the spirits of places is among the most ancient and the most widespread of the traditions of the known world, and it predates the formal religions that have arisen in the later ages. The spirits of places are not gods in the manner of the deities of the temples; they are the presences that inhabit particular locations, the awareness that the people perceive in the groves and the springs and the mountains and the other features of the landscape. The spirits are not worshipped in the manner that the gods are worshipped, but they are acknowledged and honoured, and the people who live near the spirit places observe the customs that maintain the goodwill of the inhabitants.

The tradition of the spirit places is preserved among all the peoples of the world, though the forms that the tradition takes vary from region to region and from species to species. The elves maintain the most elaborate traditions of the spirit places, for the elves have lived in the same forests for longer than any other people and have developed a detailed understanding of the spirits that inhabit the lands they occupy. The dwarves maintain traditions of the spirits of the stone and the deep places, and the centaurs maintain traditions of the spirits of the plains and the open sky. The humans maintain traditions that vary according to the lands they inhabit, and the halflings maintain the most intimate traditions of the spirits of the fields and the gardens.

## The Sacred Groves

The sacred groves are the most widespread of the spirit places, and they are found in every region where trees grow. The groves are not marked by any physical boundary or by any structure that the people have built; they are recognised by the presence of the spirit that inhabits them, a presence that the people who live near the grove can feel when they enter it. The grove is a place of silence and stillness, the people say, and the air within the grove is different from the air outside, and the light that filters through the leaves is different from the light of the open sky.

The sacred groves are protected by the customs of the communities that live near them, and the trees of the grove are never cut and the ground of the grove is never disturbed. The people do not enter the grove except for the purposes of the ceremonies that honour the spirit, and they do not take anything from the grove except the leaves and the branches that have fallen naturally. The grove is a place apart, a remnant of the world as it was before the people began to shape it to their purposes.

The most famous of the sacred groves is the Heartwood itself, the great tree that the elves believe stands at the centre of the world. The Heartwood is the most sacred of all the spirit places, and the elves maintain the most elaborate traditions of honouring the spirit that dwells within it. The groves that the elves maintain in the forests of Aethelar are the most extensive of the sacred groves in the known world, and the elven traditions of the groves have been studied by the scholars of the Cloisters of Reading for many generations.

## The Springs of the Hidden

The springs are the second most common of the spirit places, and they are found in every region where water flows from the ground. The springs are the places where the hidden waters emerge from the earth, and the spirits that inhabit the springs are believed to be the guardians of the waters and the healers of the illnesses that afflict the body and the spirit. The springs are visited by the people who seek healing, and the water of the spring is used in the ceremonies that the communities conduct.

The spirits of the springs are not always benevolent, the people caution, and the spring must be approached with respect. The visitor who comes to the spring with a pure heart and a sincere need will receive the healing that the spirit offers, while the visitor who comes with a false heart or a frivolous purpose will be turned away. The spring must be thanked for the water that it gives, and the offering that the people leave at the spring is a sign of the gratitude that the community feels.

The most famous of the springs in the known world is the Spring of the First Light, which is located on the Cape of the First Light on the eastern coast of Aethelar. The spring is said to have healing properties that exceed those of any other spring, and the people who visit it travel from every continent to seek its waters. The spring is maintained by the community of the First Veilers, who regard the spring as a place where the veil between the worlds is thin.

## The Mountains of the Powers

The mountains are the spirit places that inspire the greatest awe, for the mountains are the largest and the most enduring of the features of the landscape, and the spirits that inhabit them are believed to be the most powerful of the spirits. The mountains are the dwelling places of the spirits that control the weather and the seasons and the movements of the earth, and the people who live near the mountains observe the customs that maintain the goodwill of these powerful spirits.

The offerings that the people leave at the mountain spirit places are the most elaborate of the offerings that the tradition prescribes. The travellers who cross the mountain passes leave stones at the cairns that mark the high points, and the stones are the signs of the traveller's respect for the spirit of the mountain. The communities that live in the shadow of the mountain conduct ceremonies at the times of the solstices and the equinoxes, and they offer the fruits of the harvest and the products of their craft to the spirit that watches over them.

The Crown Range, the great mountain range that runs through the centre of Aethelar, is the site of the most extensive traditions of mountain spirits in the known world. The dwarves of the Stoneheim Holds maintain the most detailed knowledge of the spirits of the Crown Range, and the elven communities of the lower slopes have their own traditions that complement the dwarven knowledge.

## The Spirit Places of the Hearth

The spirit places are not only the natural features of the landscape; they are also the places where the people live and work, and the household itself is the most intimate of the spirit places. The hearth is the centre of the household's spiritual life, and the spirit of the hearth is the most familiar of the spirits, the presence that the family lives with and that shares the family's joys and sorrows.

The spirit of the hearth is honoured by the practices of the Still Flame, the faith that maintains the hearth-halls and the tradition of the tending of the fire. The Still Flame teaches that the spirit of the hearth is the same spirit that inhabits the great fire of the sun and the inner fire of the body, and the tending of the fire is the means by which the household maintains its connection to the spirit of the world.

## The Decline and the Persistence

The tradition of the spirit places has declined in the regions where the great temple religions have become dominant, for the temple religions teach that the spirits of the places are subordinate to the gods that the temples serve and that the honouring of the spirits is a distraction from the worship of the gods. The tradition of the spirit places persists, however, in the rural areas and among the peoples who have maintained the old ways despite the influence of the temple religions.

The scholars of the Cloisters of Reading who have studied the tradition of the spirit places have observed that the tradition is one of the most resilient of the world's spiritual traditions, and that it has survived the rise and fall of the great religions and the changes of the political order. The spirits of the places are patient, the scholars say, and the people who ignore the spirits may find that the spirits have not forgotten the disrespect that they have suffered.
