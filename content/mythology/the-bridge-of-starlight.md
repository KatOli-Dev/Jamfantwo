## The Connection Between the Mountains

The folktale of the Bridge of Starlight is told in the mountain regions of every continent, and it is among the most widespread of the world's myths. The tale tells of a bridge that was built from the light of the stars, a span that connected the highest peaks of the mountain ranges and that allowed the peoples of the different valleys to visit one another and to exchange their knowledge and their goods and their songs. The bridge was a wonder of the ancient world, the tale says, and its destruction was a great loss that the peoples of the mountains still mourn.

The versions of the tale vary among the different mountain regions, but they all share a common structure: a time when the mountains were connected by a bridge of light, a disaster that destroyed the bridge, and the hope that the bridge will one day be rebuilt. The tale is told in the Stoneheim Holds and the Karhold Realm, among the Frostwall Clans and the communities of the Crown Range, and in the high valleys of the Thessaran Basin. Each version reflects the particular conditions of the region where it is told, but the core of the tale is the same everywhere.

## The Building of the Bridge

The Bridge of Starlight was built in the time when the world was young, the tale says, and the peoples of the mountains lived in isolation from one another, separated by the deep valleys and the dangerous passes that the winter made impassable for much of the year. The peoples of the different valleys did not know each other, and they lived in ignorance of the wider world, and their cultures developed in isolation.

The building of the bridge was the work of a great being whose name is forgotten in the southern versions of the tale but remembered in the northern versions as the Star-Weaver. The Star-Weaver was a being of the sky, the northern versions say, a creature of light and air who saw the isolation of the mountain peoples and who pitied them. The Star-Weaver gathered the light of the stars, weaving the rays into a great span that connected the peaks, and the peoples of the mountains saw the bridge and marvelled.

The bridge was not a structure of stone or wood, the tale says, but a structure of pure light, a path that glowed in the darkness and that could be seen from the valleys below as a ribbon of silver stretching across the sky. The bridge was wide enough for a company of travellers to walk side by side, and it was strong enough to bear the weight of laden pack animals and the goods that the peoples carried between the valleys. The bridge was a wonder of the world, and the peoples who used it prospered from the exchange that it made possible.

## The Use of the Bridge

The Bridge of Starlight was used by the mountain peoples for many generations, the tale says, and the exchange of knowledge and goods and culture that the bridge made possible transformed the mountain regions. The peoples of the different valleys learned from one another, and they shared the techniques of mining and smelting and weaving and building that each valley had developed in its isolation. The bridge was the great highway of the mountains, and the peoples who travelled upon it carried not only their goods but their songs and their stories and their ways of understanding the world.

The bridge was especially important for the dwarven peoples of the mountains, the tale says, for the bridge connected the great dwarven halls that were scattered across the different ranges and that had been isolated from one another since the awakening. The dwarves used the bridge to exchange the knowledge of the stone and the metal, and they developed the craft of stone-singing to a level that had not been possible when the halls were isolated. The dwarven versions of the tale emphasise this aspect of the bridge's use, and they describe in detail the exchanges of knowledge that the bridge made possible.

The elven versions of the tale, preserved among the mountain elves of Morvind, describe the bridge as a place of meeting and celebration, where the peoples of the different valleys gathered for festivals and where the songs and the dances of the different communities were shared. The elves say that the bridge was lit by the stars themselves, and that the light of the bridge was a visible sign of the connections that bound the peoples of the mountains to one another.

## The Destruction of the Bridge

The destruction of the Bridge of Starlight is attributed to different causes in the different versions of the tale. The northern versions say that the bridge was destroyed by the Winter King, who saw the bridge as a challenge to his power and who sent a great storm that shattered the span and scattered the fragments across the sky. The eastern versions say that the bridge was destroyed by a war between the mountain peoples, who forgot the gift that the bridge represented and who turned their weapons against one another. The southern versions say that the bridge simply faded, that the light of the stars grew dimmer over time and that the bridge could not sustain itself without the brightness of the ancient sky.

The destruction of the bridge is always described as a loss that was felt by all the mountain peoples, and the tale describes the grief of the communities that could no longer visit one another. The valleys returned to their isolation, the tale says, and the knowledge that had been shared was lost or fragmented, and the mountain peoples became strangers to one another once again.

## The Fragments in the Sky

The stars that appear as a band across the night sky, the tale says, are the fragments of the Bridge of Starlight, the pieces that were scattered when the bridge was destroyed. The mountain peoples look upon the band of stars as a reminder of what was lost and as a promise of what may be restored. The stars are the remains of the great span that once connected the peaks, and they shine in the sky as a memory of the time when the peoples of the mountains were one.

The dwarves say that the fragments of the bridge fell to the earth as well as remaining in the sky, and that the metals that the dwarves mine from the mountains contain the essence of the starlight that the bridge was made of. The dwarven smiths who work with the metals that came from the sky are said to be able to produce a finish that glows with a faint inner light, and the objects that they craft are prized throughout the known world for this quality.

## The Hope of the Rebuilding

The tale of the Bridge of Starlight is not only a story of loss; it is also a story of hope, and the mountain peoples look forward to the day when the bridge will be rebuilt. The versions of the tale differ in their descriptions of the rebuilding, but they all agree that the bridge will be restored when the mountain peoples have learned the lesson of the destruction: that the connection between the communities is more precious than the differences that divide them.

The dwarven versions of the tale say that the bridge will be rebuilt by the stone-singers, who will weave the light of the stars into a new span using the techniques that they have preserved from the ancient time. The elven versions say that the bridge will be rebuilt by the return of the Star-Weaver, who will descend from the sky when the mountain peoples are ready to receive the gift again. The human versions say that the bridge will be rebuilt by the cooperation of all the peoples, who will work together to restore the connection that was lost.

The hope of the rebuilding is the hope that sustains the mountain peoples through the long winters and the periods of isolation, and the tale of the Bridge of Starlight is told to each new generation as a reminder that the isolation that the peoples of the mountains endure is not the natural order of the world. The bridge existed once, the tale says, and it will exist again, and the stars that shine in the night sky are the promise that the light of the bridge has not been extinguished.
