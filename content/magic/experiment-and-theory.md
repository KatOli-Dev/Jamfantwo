## The Systematic Study of the Art

The Art is not only a practice but also a subject of systematic study, and the traditions have developed methods of experiment and theory by which the principles of the Art may be investigated and understood. The study of the Art is conducted in the libraries and the laboratories of the traditions, and the findings of the scholars are published for the instruction of practitioners across the known world.

This page describes the practice of experiment and theory in the study of the Art, beginning with the methods by which the Art is studied, continuing with the principal discoveries that have been made through systematic study, and concluding with the disputes that remain unresolved.

## The Methods of Study

The study of the Art is conducted through two principal methods: the experimental and the theoretical. The experimental method involves the performance of workings under controlled conditions, with the results recorded and compared with the predictions of the theory. The theoretical method involves the development of accounts of the Art that explain the observed phenomena and predict the outcomes of workings that have not yet been performed.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) is the tradition most committed to the experimental method, and the Council has published more accounts of experiments than any other tradition. The Council's practitioners perform workings under conditions that are designed to isolate the variables that affect the outcome, and the Council's records of failed experiments are as valuable to the study of the Art as the records of successful ones.

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) are the tradition most committed to the theoretical method, and the academies have produced the most systematic treatments of [the four laws](/content/magic/the-four-laws-detailed) that any tradition has achieved. The academies' scholars develop accounts of the Art that are more precise than those of the northern traditions, and the academies' critiques of the doctrines of other traditions are among the most cited texts in the study of the Art.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) and the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) are less committed to systematic study, holding that the Art is best understood through practice rather than through theory. The Court and the Fellowship have produced fewer accounts of experiments and theories than the Council and the academies, but the accounts they have produced are valued for the depth of the understanding they reveal.

## The Laboratory and the Library

The experimental study of the Art is conducted in the laboratory, a space that is designed for the performance of workings under controlled conditions. The laboratory is furnished with the tools and materials that the practitioner requires: the subjects upon which workings are performed, the instruments by which the workings are measured, and the records by which the workings are documented.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) maintains several laboratories in the cities of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), each equipped with the tools and materials that the Council's practitioners require for their experiments. The Council's laboratories are open to practitioners of other traditions who wish to conduct experiments, and the Council encourages the exchange of experimental findings among the traditions.

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) maintain laboratories in each of the academies, and the academies' laboratories are among the most advanced of any tradition. The academies' scholars have developed instruments for the measurement of patterns that are more precise than any other instruments in the known world, and the academies' laboratories are the site of the most rigorous experimental work in the study of the Art.

The theoretical study of the Art is conducted in the library, a space that houses the records of workings that have been performed and the accounts of the principles that the traditions have developed. The library is furnished with the texts of the traditions, the records of experiments that have been conducted, and the commentaries of scholars who have studied the texts.

The [Eldreth Library](/content/magic/relic/library-of-eldreth) is the principal repository of theoretical works in the study of the Art, and the library houses the most extensive collection of texts on the Art of any institution in the known world. The library's collection includes the original manuscript of the [Known-World Codex](/content/magic/relic/codex-of-the-known-world), the [Letters on Pattern](/content/magic/relic/letters-on-pattern), and the records of experiments conducted by practitioners of every tradition.

## The Principal Discoveries

The systematic study of the Art has produced several discoveries that have shaped the practice of every tradition. The most important of these discoveries is the formulation of the four laws in their present form, which was achieved by the chroniclers of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) through the study of the workings of many practitioners over many generations.

The second most important discovery is the observation that resonance is not measurable in the manner of weight or length and cannot be stored in a vessel. The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) conducted a series of experiments in which attempts were made to gather resonance into a containment and release it at need, and the experiments demonstrated conclusively that resonance is not a substance. The Compact's experiments are cited by every tradition as the strongest evidence for the nature of resonance.

The third important discovery is the observation that the patterns of living things resist reading and reshaping with a vigour that the patterns of dead matter do not. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) conducted a series of experiments in which the resistance of various subjects was measured, and the experiments demonstrated that the resistance increases with the complexity of the subject and the vigour of its will.

The fourth important discovery is the observation that the tendency of patterns to settle back toward their original state, called reverberation, is a property of the order of the world rather than a failure of the Art. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) conducted a series of experiments in which the reverberation of reshaped patterns was measured over extended periods, and the experiments demonstrated that reverberation is present in every working and that it cannot be eliminated through any known method.

## The Disputes That Remain

The systematic study of the Art has not resolved all disputes among the traditions, and several questions remain the subject of ongoing debate. The principal dispute concerns the question of whether sympathy may be deepened by practice. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds that it may, while the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) holds that it is fixed. The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) have not taken a position, treating the question as one that cannot be settled by the evidence currently available.

The second dispute concerns the nature of resonance. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds that resonance is a single principle that manifests differently in different contexts, while the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) hold that the observed phenomena are better explained by several distinct principles that work together.

The third dispute concerns the classification of workings. The traditions have developed different systems of classification, and the scholars of the academies have not been able to agree on a single system that accommodates the workings of every tradition. The dispute is of greater interest to the theorists than to the practitioners, but the lack of a common classification complicates the exchange of findings among the traditions.

## The Publication of Findings

The findings of experimental and theoretical study are published for the instruction of practitioners across the known world. The traditions publish their findings in the form of monographs and treatises, and the publications are distributed to the libraries and the academies through the networks of correspondence that the traditions maintain.

The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) publishes its findings more freely than any other tradition, and the Compact's accounts of experiments are among the most widely cited texts in the study of the Art. The Compact holds that the Art benefits from the open exchange of findings and that a finding that is kept secret is a finding that is lost to the practice.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) also publishes its findings freely, and the Council's records of failed experiments are as valuable to the study of the Art as the records of successful ones. The Council holds that a failed experiment is as instructive as a successful one, and the Council's practitioners are trained to publish their failures as well as their successes.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) does not publish its findings, holding that the Art is best transmitted from master to apprentice through demonstration and practice rather than through texts. The Fellowship's reserve has given it a reputation for secrecy that its members do not encourage but do not actively dispel.

## The Future of Study

The systematic study of the Art continues in the laboratories and the libraries of the traditions, and the scholars of the present age have access to more knowledge of the Art than any previous generation. The development of new methods of measurement and new methods of analysis has opened areas of study that were not accessible to earlier generations, and the scholars of the present age are confident that the unresolved disputes will yield to further investigation.

The study of the Art is not limited to the principal traditions. The dwarven practitioners of the mountain holds have developed methods of working with pattern that differ from the methods of the surface traditions, and the dwarven methods are the subject of growing interest among the scholars of the academies. The exchange of findings between the surface traditions and the dwarven practitioners is one of the most promising developments in the study of the Art in the present age.
