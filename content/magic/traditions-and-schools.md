## A Varied Landscape of Practice

The Art is not practised in a single manner but in a variety of traditions, each built around a particular reading of [the four laws](/content/magic/the-four-laws-detailed) and a particular discipline of attunement. A tradition is distinguished less by the effects it produces than by the order in which a practitioner reads, attunes, and reshapes. Two traditions may achieve the same outward result through entirely different disciplines, and a practitioner trained in one tradition cannot readily be substituted for a practitioner of another. The principal traditions whose disciplines have been recorded are the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court), the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship), the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council), the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), and the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach). Each maintains its own lineage of instruction, its own body of recorded workings, and its own understanding of the [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) and [costs](/content/magic/law/cost) of the Art.

This page describes each tradition in turn, together with the dwarven approaches that follow a different pattern, and concludes with a summary of the points on which the traditions agree and disagree. The disputes between the traditions are not adjudicated here; they are set down as the traditions themselves present them.

## The Sylvan Court

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) is the oldest recorded tradition of the Art, its origins predating the written records of any other institution that has survived to the present age. The chroniclers of the Court were the first to set down the four laws in their present form, though the principles themselves are older than any court. The Court is closely attuned to the forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and its practitioners have developed a discipline centred upon the reading and reshaping of woodland patterns over many centuries. The Court's understanding of the Art is shaped by the patterns it most commonly encounters: the slow growth of trees, the seasonal cycles of the forest, and the complex interdependence of living things within the woodland ecosystem. A practitioner of the [Sylvan](/content/language/pseudo/sylvan) Court learns first to read the pattern of a forest as a whole before attempting to read the pattern of any tree or creature within it.

The discipline of the Sylvan Court is characterised by patience and long observation. A practitioner may spend months reading the pattern of a single grove before undertaking any reshaping, and the Court's recorded workings are notable for the care with which they are prepared. The Court holds that the [Source](/content/magic/law/source) of the Art is resonance understood as a path between like things and that this path may be deepened by long practice. A practitioner of sufficient skill may reach patterns that an untutored practitioner cannot, and the Court's most accomplished practitioners are said to read the pattern of a forest as readily as a scribe reads a page.

The Sylvan Court maintains the most extensive archives of the Art of any tradition. Its libraries contain records of workings performed over many centuries, and the Court's chroniclers have produced the most widely cited formulations of each of the four laws. The Court's influence on the other traditions is considerable, for the vocabulary it introduced — the terms [source](/content/magic/law/source), [medium](/content/magic/law/medium), [limit](/content/magic/law/limit), and [cost](/content/magic/law/cost) — has been adopted by most academies founded since. The Court is not, however, the final authority on the meaning of these terms, and the other traditions have developed their own readings of each law.

## The Heartwood Fellowship

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) is a tradition closely attuned to the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood), the most extensive and most ancient temperate forest in the known world. The Fellowship is smaller and more conservative than the Sylvan Court, and its practitioners prefer the older vocabulary of the Art, speaking of sympathy rather than resonance and of pattern rather than medium. The Fellowship holds that the older terms carry a meaning that the newer vocabulary has diluted and that a practitioner who uses the older terms is less likely to misunderstand the nature of the Art.

The discipline of the Heartwood Fellowship is built upon deep attunement to a single place. A practitioner of the Fellowship typically spends many years in the Heartwood before undertaking any working of consequence, learning to read the pattern of the forest in all its aspects. This attunement to a single place gives the Fellowship's practitioners a depth of understanding that practitioners of other traditions rarely match when working within the Heartwood but limits the Fellowship's reach beyond the forest. A practitioner of the Fellowship who travels to the cities of the coast or the mountains of the north must spend a considerable period of reattunement before the Art can be practised with confidence.

The Fellowship is known for its reserve. It does not publish the workings of its members, and its archives are not open to scholars of other traditions. The disputed [Letters on Pattern](/content/magic/relic/letters-on-pattern) are attributed to a practitioner of the Fellowship, but the Fellowship itself has never confirmed or denied the attribution. This reserve has given the Fellowship a reputation for secrecy that its members do not encourage but do not actively dispel.

## The Khalisar Council

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) is the tradition of the inland cities and the routes of trade, its practitioners attuned to the patterns of commerce, law, and the built environment. The Council is the most practical of the major traditions, and its discipline is shaped by the demands of urban life and the movement of goods across the continents. A practitioner of the [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) Council learns to read the patterns of trade routes, marketplaces, and the flow of people through the city, and the Council's workings are directed toward the resolution of disputes, the improvement of navigation, and the maintenance of the infrastructure upon which commerce depends.

The Council's understanding of the Art is notably pragmatic. Its practitioners hold that the four laws are best understood as descriptions of what works rather than as fundamental truths about the order of the world. The Council is less interested in theoretical questions than in the practical applications of the Art, and its recorded workings are among the most extensively documented of any tradition. The Council has published several accounts of failed experiments, a practice that other traditions have commended for its contribution to the shared knowledge of the Art.

The practitioners of the Khalisar Council are particularly skilled in the reshaping of dead matter. The construction of buildings, the maintenance of roads, and the management of water supplies in the desert cities of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) all benefit from the Council's discipline, and the Council's practitioners are in high demand among the merchants and magistrates of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast. The Council maintains a register of licensed practitioners who may be hired for particular workings, a practice that distinguishes it from the more insular traditions of the north.

## The Velden Compact

The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) is the maritime tradition of the Art, its practitioners attuned to the patterns of the sea, the wind, and the weather. The Compact was founded by the mariners of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), who recognised that the Art could be of use in navigation and the reading of weather long before the formal traditions of the north had codified their disciplines. The practitioners of the Compact are the most mobile of any tradition, for the sea does not hold still to be read, and a practitioner must learn to read patterns in constant motion.

The discipline of the [Velden](/content/location/settlement/city/velden) Compact is built upon the reading of currents, winds, and the behaviour of birds and sea creatures. A practitioner of the Compact learns to attend to the pattern of a stretch of water as a whole, reading the movement of the surface, the colour of the water, and the behaviour of the creatures within it. The workings of the Compact are directed toward the improvement of navigation, the prediction of weather, and the maintenance of the vessels that cross the seas. The Compact's practitioners are also skilled in the reshaping of wood and rope, for the patterns of a vessel must be maintained and repaired if the vessel is to survive the crossing.

The Compact publishes its findings more freely than any other tradition. Its accounts of failed experiments in the gathering and storage of resonance are among the most widely cited texts in the study of the [Source](/content/magic/law/source), and its guidelines for the periods of rest required between workings are adopted by practitioners of every tradition.

## The Academies of the Southern Reach

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) are the youngest of the major traditions, and they are also the most scholarly. The academies are found in the far south of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), in a region of inland seas and temperate highlands that has remained isolated from the northern continents for much of its history. The practitioners of the Southern Reach are attuned to the patterns of the mountains and the seas, and their discipline is shaped by the peculiar geography of their home.

The academies emphasise theory and documentation above all other aspects of the Art. A practitioner of the Southern Reach spends years in study before being entrusted with a working of any consequence, and the academies' libraries contain the most systematic treatments of the four laws that any tradition has produced. The academies have developed a terminology of the Art that is more precise than that of the northern traditions, and their scholars have published detailed critiques of the doctrines of the Sylvan Court and the Heartwood Fellowship.

The Southern Reach has produced several works that have influenced the study of the Art across the known world. The academies' treatment of the [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) of the Art is among the most thorough that has been written, and their accounts of the [cost](/content/magic/law/cost) of reshaping have been adopted by practitioners of every tradition. The academies maintain regular correspondence with the Sylvan Court and the Khalisar Council, and the exchange of texts between the south and the north has enriched the study of the Art in both regions.

## Dwarven Approaches

The dwarven peoples of the mountain holds do not maintain a unified tradition of the Art in the manner of the Sylvan Court or the Khalisar Council. The dwarven approach to the Art is less systematic and more artisanal, reflecting the craft traditions that have shaped dwarven culture across the centuries. A dwarven practitioner does not typically undergo a formal apprenticeship in the Art as the human and elven traditions understand it but instead learns the Art as an extension of a craft, whether smithing, stoneworking, or the working of metals.

This artisanal approach has produced workings that the traditions of the surface regard with a mixture of admiration and unease. Dwarven stonework that has been shaped by the Art is among the most enduring of any tradition, and the deep halls of the mountain holds are said to bear the traces of reshapings performed centuries ago that have not settled back toward their original state. The dwarves do not write down their workings in the manner of the academies, and the knowledge of the Art is passed from master to apprentice through demonstration and practice rather than through texts.

The dwarven understanding of the [Source](/content/magic/law/source) is distinctive. Dwarven practitioners hold that resonance is not a property of all things equally but is strongest in the deep places of the earth, where the stone has been undisturbed for ages. The mountains themselves are the source of the Art in the dwarven view, and a practitioner who works in the depths accomplishes more than one who works on the surface. This view is not accepted by any of the major traditions, but the evidence of dwarven workings is difficult to dismiss.

## Points of Agreement and Disagreement

The traditions agree on the existence of the four laws as categories of the Art, though they disagree about the relative weight to assign to each. The Sylvan Court and the Heartwood Fellowship treat the [Source](/content/magic/law/source) and the [Medium](/content/magic/law/medium) as the foundations of the Art, with the [Limit](/content/magic/law/limit) and the [Cost](/content/magic/law/cost) following from them. The Khalisar Council treats the Limit and the Cost as the primary considerations, holding that the Source and the Medium are best understood through the boundaries they impose. The Velden Compact treats the four laws as equal in standing, while the academies of the Southern Reach treat all four as subjects of ongoing investigation.

The traditions agree that the Art is not supernatural and that it is a property of the order of the world. They agree that resonance is the principle by which the Art operates and that pattern is the substance upon which it works. They agree that the practitioner must attune before reshaping and that the reshaping exacts a cost that must be borne. These agreements form the foundation of the shared practice of the Art, and the disputes that remain are conducted within the boundaries that these agreements establish.
