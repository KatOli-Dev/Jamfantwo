## The Many Forms of Reshaping

A working is a single, named act of the Art: a particular reshaping performed upon a particular pattern in a particular way. The same effect may be achieved by workings of different traditions, and a single tradition may produce effects that resemble the workings of another. The working is therefore described as the practitioners describe it, with the pattern and the discipline named together.

This page describes the principal types of workings that the traditions have recorded, grouped by the nature of the reshaping they perform. Some workings are widely shared among the traditions, while others are held by a single tradition or by a single lineage within a tradition. A few workings are recorded only in the relics of the Art, their practitioners long dispersed.

## Shaping

Shaping is the most common type of working, in which the practitioner reshapes the pattern of a subject to a more desirable configuration. The reshaping is always partial and always within the [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) that the order of the world imposes. The practitioner does not impose a new pattern upon the subject from outside but reaches through the path of [Source](/content/magic/law/source) and adjusts the configuration from within.

The shaping of dead matter is the simplest form and the first that apprentices learn. A stone may be shaped to a more useful form, a piece of wood may be shaped to a smoother surface, and a quantity of metal may be shaped to a sharper edge. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has recorded more than two hundred distinct shapings of dead matter, each with its own name and its own conditions of performance.

The shaping of living matter is more demanding and is undertaken only by practitioners who have undergone extensive training. A tree may be shaped to grow in a particular direction, a plant may be shaped to produce more fruit, and an animal may be shaped to a more robust constitution. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) is the tradition most skilled in the shaping of living things, and the Court's practitioners have developed methods of shaping that work with the natural patterns of growth rather than against them.

The shaping of places is the most demanding of the shaping disciplines, for the pattern of a place is a pattern of patterns, and the practitioner who would shape a place must read and reshape many patterns at once. The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) has recorded several shapings of places in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood), and the Fellowship's practitioners are among the few who have attempted the shaping of a place with consistent success.

## Binding

Binding is a working in which the practitioner fixes a pattern in a particular configuration, reducing the tendency of the pattern to settle back toward its old shape. Binding is often performed after shaping, for a shaped pattern left unbound will eventually return to its original configuration through the effect of [Cost](/content/magic/law/cost).

The binding of a pattern requires that the practitioner attune to the subject and fix the new configuration through an act of will that is sustained over a period of time. The duration of the binding depends on the complexity of the pattern and the depth of the attunement. A simple binding of a stone may last for years, while a binding of a living thing may last for only a season.

The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) has recorded several methods of binding that involve the use of relics. A practitioner who works through a relic may achieve a binding that is stronger and more enduring than one achieved through direct working. The Compact's practitioners are particularly skilled in the binding of wood and rope, for the patterns of a vessel must be bound against the constant movement of the sea.

The dwarven practitioners of the mountain holds have developed methods of binding that are unlike any recorded in the surface traditions. The deep halls of the mountain holds are said to bear the traces of bindings performed centuries ago that have not settled back toward their original state, and the dwarven methods of binding are the subject of considerable interest among the scholars of the academies.

## Revealing

Revealing is a working in which the practitioner reads a pattern that is hidden or obscured, bringing the configuration to the awareness of the practitioner or of others. Revealing is not a reshaping but a reading, and some traditions do not classify it as a working at all. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) treats revealing as a part of the discipline of reading rather than as a separate type of working.

The revealing of hidden patterns is a skill that the traditions value highly. A practitioner who can read the traces of a reshaping that was performed long ago may learn the history of a place or an object, and a practitioner who can read the pattern of a person's health may detect the early signs of illness before the person is aware of them.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has developed several workings of revealing that are used in the resolution of disputes. A practitioner of the Council may read the pattern of a document to determine whether it has been altered, or read the pattern of a building to determine whether its structure has been damaged. The Council's workings of revealing are among the most practical of any tradition and are in high demand among the merchants and magistrates of the Sahrani coast.

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) have developed theoretical accounts of revealing that are more systematic than those of any other tradition. The academies treat revealing as a form of reading in which the practitioner attunes to the traces of past reshapings rather than to the present configuration of the subject, and the academies' accounts of the method are among the most cited texts in the study of the Art.

## Warding

Warding is a working in which the practitioner establishes a pattern that resists the entry of other patterns or of the attention of other practitioners. Warding is distinct from shaping and binding in that the practitioner does not reshape an existing pattern but rather establishes a new pattern in the space around a subject.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) has recorded several methods of warding that are used to protect the sacred groves of the forest. The Court's wards are established through the reading of the forest's pattern and the attunement of the ward to the natural resonance of the place. A ward that is properly attuned to the forest will resist the entry of unwanted influences without disturbing the natural patterns of the woodland.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has developed methods of warding that are used in the protection of buildings and the storage of valuable goods. The Council's wards are more systematic than the wards of the forest traditions and are designed to resist the attention of other practitioners as well as the entry of physical threats.

The practice of warding is described in greater detail in the page on [protective workings](/content/magic/protective-workings), where the methods and limits of the discipline are treated more fully.

## Unbinding and Unshaping

Unbinding is a working in which the practitioner releases a pattern that has been bound, allowing it to settle back toward its original configuration. Unshaping is a working in which the practitioner reverses a shaping that has been performed, returning the pattern to its earlier state.

The traditions do not agree on whether unbinding and unshaping are distinct types of working or whether they are simply the reversal of binding and shaping. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds that unbinding is a distinct working that requires a different discipline from binding, for the practitioner who unbinds a pattern must read the original binding and find the point at which the binding may be released. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) holds that unbinding is simply binding performed in reverse and does not require a separate discipline.

Unshaping is more difficult than unbinding, for the pattern that has been shaped has been changed in its configuration, and the practitioner who would unshape it must read the original configuration and restore it through an act of will. The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) holds that unshaping is the most demanding of the working types and should be undertaken only when the original shaping has caused harm, a position accepted by most of the traditions.

## The Limits of Working Types

The types of working described here are not exhaustive, and the traditions continue to discover new workings as the Art is practised in new contexts and with new patterns. The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) has recorded several workings that do not fit neatly into the categories described here, and the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) have proposed a classification of workings that differs from the one presented in this page.

The shared understanding of the types of working is part of the common vocabulary of the Art, and the practitioner who would communicate with practitioners of other traditions must be familiar with the types as they are commonly understood. The disputes about the classification of workings are of interest to the academies and to the theorists of the Art, but the practitioner at work is concerned less with classification than with the practical question of which working will produce the desired effect.
