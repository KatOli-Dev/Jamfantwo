## Wards, Shields, and the Defence of Pattern

The Art may be applied to the protection of places, objects, and persons through workings that resist the entry of unwanted influences. These protective workings are called wards by the forest traditions and shields by the urban traditions, but the principle is the same: the practitioner establishes a pattern that resists the patterns that would do harm.

This page describes the practice of protective workings as it is performed across the traditions, beginning with the principles that govern the establishment of protective patterns, continuing with the methods by which wards and shields are created and maintained, and concluding with the limits and risks that the practitioner must understand.

## The Principles of Protection

A protective working is a pattern that the practitioner establishes in the space around a subject, and the pattern is designed to resist the entry of other patterns that would harm the subject. The protective pattern does not reshape the subject but rather stands between the subject and the influences that would harm it.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) has developed the most extensive body of protective practice of any tradition, for the Court's practitioners have long protected the sacred groves of the forest from the encroachment of those who would harm them. The Court holds that a protective working should be established in harmony with the patterns of the place it protects, for a ward that is not attuned to the place will be less effective and may cause unintended harm.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has developed methods of protection that are more systematic than those of the forest traditions. The Council's practitioners protect the buildings, the marketplaces, and the storerooms of the cities, and the Council's wards are designed to resist the entry of thieves, pests, and the attention of other practitioners.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) does not ordinarily practise protective workings, holding that the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) itself provides sufficient protection for those who live within it. The Fellowship's practitioners may establish a ward in exceptional circumstances, but the practice is rare within the Fellowship.

## The Establishment of Wards

A ward is established through a working in which the practitioner reads the pattern of the place to be protected, attunes to the resonances of the place, and establishes a protective pattern that stands between the place and the influences that would harm it. The establishment of a ward requires that the practitioner be familiar with the place and that the practitioner understand the nature of the influences from which the place is to be protected.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) teaches the establishment of wards through a method called walking the boundary, in which the practitioner walks the perimeter of the place to be protected, reading the pattern of the boundary and establishing the ward as the boundary is walked. The Court holds that a ward established in this manner is stronger than one established from a single point, for the boundary of a place is as important as the interior in determining the pattern of protection.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) teaches the establishment of wards through a method called fixing the threshold, in which the practitioner establishes the ward at the points of entry to the place to be protected. The Council holds that the points of entry are the points of vulnerability, and a ward that protects the points of entry protects the whole.

The duration of a ward depends on the depth of the attunement and the skill of the practitioner. A ward that is established by a practitioner who has attuned to the place over many years may endure for decades, while a ward that is established by a practitioner who attunes to the place for the first time may endure for only a season. The ward must be maintained through periodic renewal, for the reverberation of the pattern tends to weaken the ward over time.

## The Establishment of Shields

A shield is a protective working that is established around a person rather than around a place. The shield is a pattern that the practitioner establishes in the space around the person, and the pattern is designed to resist the entry of influences that would harm the person.

The establishment of a shield is more demanding than the establishment of a ward, for the person who is to be protected is a living pattern that resists the working. The practitioner who would establish a shield around a person must read the pattern of the person, attune to the person through the path of [Source](/content/magic/law/source), and establish the shield in harmony with the person's pattern rather than against it.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has developed the most extensive body of shield practice of any tradition, for the Council's practitioners are frequently called upon to protect the merchants and magistrates of the cities from the attention of other practitioners. The Council's shields are designed to resist the reading of the person's pattern as well as the entry of harmful influences, and the Council's methods are among the most effective of any tradition.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) does not ordinarily practise the establishment of shields around persons, holding that a person who requires a shield is a person who is out of harmony with the patterns of the forest. The Court's practitioners may establish a shield in exceptional circumstances, but the practice is rare within the Court.

## Protective Workings against the Art

The most common application of protective workings is the protection of places and persons from the attention of other practitioners. A ward that is established around a place will resist the reading of patterns within the place by a practitioner who is outside the ward, and a shield that is established around a person will resist the reading of the person's pattern by another practitioner.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has developed several methods of protection against the Art that are used in the storage of valuable goods and the protection of sensitive documents. The Council's methods are based on the principle that a pattern that is shielded from the attention of one practitioner may be more difficult for another practitioner to read, and the Council's practitioners are skilled in the establishment of shields that resist the reading of patterns by practitioners of other traditions.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) does not ordinarily practise protection against the Art, holding that a practitioner who would read the patterns of the forest without permission would be detected by the forest itself and that the forest's own patterns provide sufficient protection. The Court's practitioners may establish a ward against the Art in exceptional circumstances, but the practice is rare within the Court.

## Protective Workings against Natural Forces

Protective workings may also be established against natural forces such as fire, flood, and storm. The practitioner who would protect a place from fire establishes a pattern that resists the entry of flame, and the practitioner who would protect a place from flood establishes a pattern that resists the entry of water.

The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) is the tradition most skilled in protective workings against natural forces, for the Compact's practitioners must protect their vessels from the constant threat of storm and sea. The Compact has developed methods of protection that are among the most effective of any tradition, and the Compact's records contain several accounts of vessels that were saved from destruction by the protective workings of the Compact's practitioners.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has also developed methods of protection against natural forces, particularly against the fires that threaten the cities of the Sahrani interior. The Council's practitioners are skilled in the establishment of wards that resist the spread of flame, and the Council's methods have saved several cities from destruction in the recorded history of the region.

## The Limits of Protective Workings

Protective workings are bounded by the same limits that govern the Art as a whole. The practitioner who would establish a ward or a shield must attune to the place or the person to be protected, and the strength of the protection depends on the depth of the attunement. A practitioner who has attuned to a place over many years may establish a ward that endures for decades, while a practitioner who attunes to a place for the first time may establish a ward that endures for only a season.

The conservation of pattern also bounds protective workings. The practitioner who establishes a ward or a shield cannot create a pattern of protection from nothing but must draw upon the pattern of the place or the person and establish the protective pattern in relation to it. The drawing exacts a [cost](/content/magic/law/cost) that the practitioner bears.

The principal limit on protective workings is the tendency of patterns to settle back toward their original configuration. The ward or shield that has been established will weaken over time through reverberation, and the practitioner must maintain the protection through periodic renewal. A ward that is not maintained will eventually fade, and the place or person that was protected will become vulnerable once again.

## The Risks of Protective Workings

The principal risk of protective workings is that the practitioner may establish a ward or a shield that is too strong, isolating the protected place or person from the influences that are necessary for health and growth. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) has recorded cases in which a ward that was established to protect a grove from the attention of other practitioners also prevented the grove from receiving the sunlight and the rain that were necessary for the health of the trees.

The second risk is that the practitioner may establish a ward or a shield that is attuned to the wrong patterns, protecting the place or person from influences that are beneficial while admitting influences that are harmful. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has recorded cases in which a shield that was established to protect a person from the attention of other practitioners also prevented the person from receiving the healing attention of a practitioner of the Art.

The practice of protective workings therefore requires that the practitioner understand not only the patterns of the Art but also the patterns of the place or person to be protected and the patterns of the influences from which the protection is sought.
