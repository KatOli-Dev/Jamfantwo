## Overview

The Art is paid for, and the cost is recorded in the body and mind of the practitioner, in the pattern of the subject, and in the world that lies between them. The chroniclers of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) state the cost in a form that has come to be the most widely cited, and the cost is treated here under five headings. The cost is not a penalty, and the cost is not a punishment. The cost is the price of the [reshaping of the medium](/content/magic/law/medium), and the practitioner who would practise the Art must be prepared to pay it.

This page sets down the five costs as they are commonly stated, with the principal variants of the formulation and the principal disputes among the traditions. The page notes the cases in which the costs have been borne to a degree that has been remarked upon by the chroniclers, and the page notes the cases in which the costs have been recorded as the cause of a practitioner's withdrawal from the Art.

## The Cost of Effort

The first cost is the effort of the reading and the reshaping. The reading of a [pattern of the medium](/content/magic/law/medium) is a work of attention, and the work requires a stillness of body and of mind that the practitioner must acquire before any other skill can be of use. The reshaping is a work of will, and the work requires a concentration of the practitioner upon the subject that the practitioner cannot long maintain. The effort is borne in the body as fatigue and in the mind as a kind of weariness that the traditions have named differently in different ages. The chronicler of the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has recorded that a practitioner of even moderate skill is exhausted by a working of any consequence, and the chronicler of [Avaros](/content/location/settlement/city/avaros) has recorded that a practitioner of advanced years is more easily exhausted than a practitioner in the prime of the discipline.

The effort is mitigated by the long apprenticeship that the traditions require, and the effort is mitigated by the discipline of attunement that the practitioner learns in the apprenticeship. A practitioner who has attuned to a subject may accomplish more in a working than a practitioner who has not, and the attunement is part of the foundation of the [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) within which the Art is practised. The chronicler of the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) has remarked that the effort of a working is the measure of the practitioner's attunement, and the remark has been taken up by the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) and by several of the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach).

## The Cost of Strain

The second cost is the strain that the reshaping places upon the practitioner. The reshaping is a redistribution of the [pattern of the medium](/content/magic/law/medium), and the redistribution is recorded in the practitioner's body and mind as a kind of tension that the traditions have named strain. The strain is not a pain in the ordinary sense, and the strain is not a wound in the ordinary sense. The strain is a disturbance of the practitioner's pattern that the practitioner feels as a stiffness of the joints, a heaviness of the limbs, and a slowness of the mind. The strain passes with rest in most cases, and the strain leaves no permanent mark upon the practitioner in most cases. The chronicler of [Velden](/content/location/settlement/city/velden) has recorded that the strain of a working of any consequence is the principal reason that the traditions require a period of rest between workings, and the chronicler of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) has recorded that the period of rest is observed by every tradition of which the Compact has knowledge.

The strain is greater when the reshaping is more extensive, and the strain is greater when the subject is more complex. A practitioner who reshapes the pattern of a stone bears a small strain, and a practitioner who reshapes the pattern of a [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) bears a greater strain, and a practitioner who reshapes the pattern of a person bears the greatest strain of all. The strain is the principal reason that the traditions are cautious in the reshaping of the patterns of the living, and the strain is one of the principal reasons that the reshaping of a person is undertaken only with the consent of the subject and under the supervision of a master of the tradition.

## The Cost of Backflow

The third cost is the backflow, the sympathetic effect of the reshaping upon the practitioner. The reshaping is performed through the [path of the Source](/content/magic/law/source) of resonance that connects the practitioner to the subject, and the path is a two-way path. The reshaping that travels from the practitioner to the subject is matched by a smaller reshaping that travels from the subject to the practitioner, and the smaller reshaping is the backflow. The backflow is greater the closer the sympathy between the practitioner and the subject, and the backflow is greater the more extensive the reshaping.

The backflow is the source of the particular care that the traditions take in the reshaping of the patterns of persons. A practitioner who reshapes the pattern of a person is in turn reshaped by the reshaping, and the reshaping of the practitioner is not always under the practitioner's control. The chronicler of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) has recorded several cases in which a practitioner of the reshaping of persons has been so deeply marked by the backflow that the practitioner has been unable to continue in the discipline, and the chronicler of [Avaros](/content/location/settlement/city/avaros) has recorded that the backflow is the principal reason that the traditions insist upon a long apprenticeship in the reshaping of persons.

The backflow is mitigated by the discipline of attunement, and the backflow is mitigated by the practice of the relic that the traditions teach. A practitioner who works through a relic bears a smaller backflow than a practitioner who works directly, and the relic is, in this sense, a buffer between the practitioner and the subject. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) is the principal keeper of the relics that the traditions use for this purpose, and the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) has published several accounts of the practice of relic making that are the foundation of the modern discipline.

## The Cost of Drift

The fourth cost is the drift, the cumulative marks that the practitioner bears from a career of practice. Every reshaping leaves a trace in the practitioner, and the traces accumulate over a career. The accumulated traces constitute a kind of history of the practitioner's practice, and the history may be read by a practitioner of sufficient skill. A practitioner of advanced years is a pattern of considerable complexity, and the complexity is a mark of the practitioner's experience and a mark of the practitioner's discipline.

The drift is not a harm in the ordinary sense, and the drift is not a wound in the ordinary sense. The drift is a part of the practitioner, and the drift is taken by the traditions as a sign of the practitioner's standing in the Art. The chronicler of the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has remarked that a practitioner of advanced years is more to be trusted than a practitioner of the first flush of the discipline, and the remark has been taken up by the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) and by the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach). The drift is, however, a limit upon the practitioner, and the drift is the reason that the traditions require a period of withdrawal from the Art in the later years of a practitioner's career. The chronicler of [Avaros](/content/location/settlement/city/avaros) has recorded several cases in which a practitioner of advanced years has been unable to continue in the discipline because of the drift, and the cases are taken by the traditions as evidence of the wisdom of the period of withdrawal.

## The Cost of Reverberation

The fifth cost is the reverberation, the tendency of a reshaped pattern to settle back toward its old shape. A pattern that has been reshaped is not at rest in the new shape, and the pattern tends to return to the old shape over time. The returning is the reverberation, and the reverberation is greater the more extensive the reshaping. The reverberation is observed in every working of the Art, and the reverberation is the reason that the traditions insist upon the maintenance of a reshaped pattern in the years after the working.

The reverberation is not a failure of the Art, and the reverberation is not a sign of the practitioner's lack of skill. The reverberation is a property of the order of the world, and the reverberation is observed in every case in which a pattern is reshaped. The chronicler of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) has remarked that the reverberation is the reason that the relics of the Art are valued, for the relics are patterns that have been so deeply reshaped that the reverberation has been overcome. The remark is taken by the traditions as the foundation of the practice of relic making, and the remark is the source of the particular care that the traditions take in the making of relics.

The reverberation is mitigated by the discipline of attunement, and the reverberation is mitigated by the long apprenticeship that the traditions require. A practitioner who has attuned to a subject may reduce the reverberation of a reshaping, and the practitioner may accomplish more in a working than a practitioner who has not. The chronicler of the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) has recorded that the reverberation is the principal reason that the traditions insist upon a period of rest between workings, and the period of rest is observed by every tradition of which the Heartwood Fellowship has knowledge.

## The Practice of the Cost

The cost is borne in the body and in the mind, and the cost is borne over the years of a practitioner's career. The traditions teach the practitioner to bear the cost, and the traditions teach the practitioner to mitigate the cost by the discipline of attunement, by the long apprenticeship, and by the period of rest that the traditions require. The chronicler observes that the cost is the measure of the Art, and the cost is the reason that the Art is taught only to those who are prepared to bear it. The chronicler records the cost for the reader's consideration, and the chronicler does not adjudicate the disputes that the traditions carry on about the proper management of the cost.
