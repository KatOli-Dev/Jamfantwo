## The Substance upon Which the Art Operates

The medium of the Art is pattern, the shape of a thing considered as a whole. A practitioner learns first to read the pattern of a thing, then to reshape it within the [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) that the order of the world imposes. Reading is a discipline of attention; reshaping is a discipline of will. The medium is not a substance or a force but the configuration of a thing in all of its aspects, and the practitioner is one who has learned to perceive this configuration and to alter it.

This page sets down what the traditions mean when they speak of pattern, how pattern is read, and how pattern is reshaped. It notes the principal variants of the doctrine and the principal disputes among the traditions.

## Configuration Considered as a Whole

The chroniclers of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) define the pattern of a thing as its configuration in all of its aspects, taken together. The configuration is not a list of properties or a sum of parts but the shape of the thing as a whole, including the relations of the parts to one another as well as the parts themselves. A stone has a pattern, and a [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) has a pattern, and a [storm](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) has a pattern, and a moment has a pattern. The patterns differ in complexity and in coherence but are all of the same kind in the sense the traditions require.

The pattern of a thing is not the thing itself, and practitioners are at pains to mark the distinction. The stone endures, and its pattern endures with it. The stone is broken, and its pattern is broken with it. The stone is ground to powder, and its pattern is dispersed among the grains. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has likened pattern to the footprint relative to the foot, an analogy taken up by several academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach).

## The First Discipline: Patient Attention

The reading of a pattern is the first discipline of the practitioner, taught in every tradition. Reading demands a stillness of body and mind that the practitioner must acquire before any other skill can be of use. The practitioner sits or stands with the subject before them and attends to it until the configuration of the subject as a whole is perceived. This perception is not a vision or a hearing but a faculty the traditions name differently: the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) calls it the inner eye, the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) calls it the listening, and the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) call it the attunement.

The first reading of a pattern is rarely complete. The practitioner must return to the reading many times before the pattern is perceived in its full configuration. The libraries of [Avaros](/content/location/settlement/city/avaros) record that a pattern of even moderate complexity may require a season of patient attention before the practitioner has perceived all its aspects, and the records of [Velden](/content/location/settlement/city/velden) indicate that a pattern of a living person is rarely fully perceived in less than a year. The time required is part of the [cost](/content/magic/law/cost) of the Art and one of the principal reasons the traditions insist upon a long apprenticeship before a practitioner is entrusted with a working of any consequence.

## The Second Discipline: Willed Adjustment

The reshaping of a pattern follows the reading and is the second discipline of the practitioner. Reshaping is a work of will performed through the [path of the Source](/content/magic/law/source) of resonance that connects practitioner to subject. The practitioner does not impose a new pattern upon the subject from outside but reaches through the path of resonance and adjusts the configuration from within, in the manner of a hand that turns a dial rather than a hand that strikes a blow. The reshaping is always partial and always within the [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) that the order of the world imposes.

The traditions disagree about the extent to which reshaping may be called creation. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds that the practitioner may only reshape and that nothing new is made in the strict sense. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) holds that reshaping is a kind of making and that the new pattern, once fixed in the subject, is a thing that did not exist before. The two positions are not far apart in practice: both agree that the practitioner cannot create a pattern from nothing and cannot destroy a pattern entirely.

## Dead Matter, Living Things, Places, and Persons

The patterns of the world vary widely in character, and the traditions distinguish several kinds of particular interest to the practitioner. The patterns of dead matter are the simplest and the most easily read and reshaped. The patterns of living matter are more complex and resist reading and reshaping with a vigour the practitioner must overcome. The patterns of persons are the most complex and the most resistant, for the pattern of a person is bound up with the will of the person, and the will is itself part of the pattern.

The patterns of places are of a different order and are the subject of much of the practice of the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) and the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court). The pattern of a place is the configuration of the place in all of its aspects, including the soil, the waters, and the creatures that dwell within it. It is, in this sense, a pattern of patterns, and reshaping a place is a reshaping of the patterns of which the place is composed. The practitioners of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) hold that reshaping a place is the most demanding of the disciplines of the Art and also the most enduring.

## Traces, History, and the Relics of the Art

The patterns of the world retain memory, one of the most important features of the medium for the practitioner. A pattern that has been reshaped retains the traces of the reshaping, and these traces may be read by a practitioner of sufficient skill. A pattern that has been repeatedly reshaped accumulates the traces of many shapings, constituting a kind of history. The relics of the Art are, in this sense, patterns of great age and accumulated reshaping, and the reading of a relic is one of the most demanding of the disciplines of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court).

The memory of a pattern is also the source of the [drift of the Cost](/content/magic/law/cost). A practitioner who reshapes a pattern leaves a trace of themself in the pattern, and a practitioner who is reshaped by another practitioner carries a trace of the other. These traces accumulate over a career, constituting a history of the practitioner's practice. The libraries of [Avaros](/content/location/settlement/city/avaros) observe that a practitioner of advanced years is a pattern of considerable complexity, a remark received by the traditions with the customary mixture of agreement and polite dissent.
