## Overview

The medium of the Art is pattern, the shape of a thing considered as a whole. A practitioner of the Art learns first to read the pattern of a thing, and then to reshape the pattern within the [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) that the order of the world imposes. The reading is a discipline of attention, and the reshaping is a discipline of will. The medium is not a substance, and the medium is not a force. The medium is the configuration of a thing in all of its aspects, and the practitioner is one who has learned to perceive the configuration and to alter it.

This page sets down what the traditions mean when they speak of pattern, how the pattern is read, and how the pattern is reshaped. The page notes the principal variants of the doctrine and the principal disputes among the traditions.

## The Pattern of a Thing

The chroniclers of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) define the pattern of a thing as the configuration of the thing in all of its aspects, taken together. The configuration is not a list of properties, and the configuration is not a sum of parts. The configuration is the shape of the thing as a whole, and the shape includes the relations of the parts to one another as well as the parts themselves. A stone has a pattern, and a [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) has a pattern, and a [storm](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) has a pattern, and a moment has a pattern. The patterns differ in their complexity and in their coherence, but the patterns are all of the same kind in the sense that the traditions require.

The pattern of a thing is not the thing itself, and the practitioner of the Art is at pains to mark the distinction. The stone endures, and the pattern of the stone endures with it. The stone is broken, and the pattern of the stone is broken with it. The stone is ground to powder, and the pattern of the stone is dispersed among the grains. The chronicler of the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has remarked that the pattern is to the thing as the footprint is to the foot, and the remark has been taken up by several of the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach).

## Reading a Pattern

The reading of a pattern is the first discipline of the practitioner, and the reading is taught in every tradition. The reading is a work of attention, and the work requires a stillness of body and of mind that the practitioner must acquire before any other skill can be of use. The practitioner sits or stands with the subject before the practitioner, and the practitioner attends to the subject until the practitioner perceives the configuration of the subject as a whole. The perception is not a vision, and the perception is not a hearing. The perception is a faculty that the traditions name differently: the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) calls it the inner eye, the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) calls it the listening, and the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) call it the attunement.

The first reading of a pattern is rarely complete, and the practitioner must return to the reading many times before the pattern is perceived in its full configuration. The chroniclers of [Avaros](/content/location/settlement/city/avaros) have recorded that a pattern of even moderate complexity may require a season of patient attention before the practitioner has perceived the pattern in all of its aspects, and the chroniclers of [Velden](/content/location/settlement/city/velden) have recorded that a pattern of a living person is rarely fully perceived in less than a year. The time required is part of the [cost](/content/magic/law/cost) of the Art, and the cost is one of the principal reasons that the traditions insist upon a long apprenticeship before a practitioner is entrusted with a working of any consequence.

## Reshaping a Pattern

The reshaping of a pattern follows the reading, and the reshaping is the second discipline of the practitioner. The reshaping is a work of will, and the work is performed by the practitioner through the [path of the Source](/content/magic/law/source) of resonance that connects the practitioner to the subject. The practitioner does not impose the new pattern upon the subject from outside. The practitioner reaches through the path of resonance and adjusts the configuration of the subject from within, in the manner of a hand that turns a dial rather than a hand that strikes a blow. The reshaping is always partial, and the reshaping is always within the [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) that the order of the world imposes.

The traditions disagree about the extent to which the reshaping may be said to be a creation. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds that the practitioner may only reshape, and the Court holds that nothing new is made in the strict sense. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) holds that the reshaping is a kind of making, and the Council holds that the new pattern, once it has been fixed in the subject, is a thing that did not exist before. The two positions are not in practice far apart, for both agree that the practitioner cannot create a pattern from nothing, and both agree that the practitioner cannot destroy a pattern entirely. The dispute is a matter of vocabulary, and the dispute is preserved by the chroniclers in deference to the traditions.

## Varieties of Pattern

The patterns of the world vary widely in their character, and the traditions distinguish several kinds that are of particular interest to the practitioner. The patterns of dead matter are the simplest, and the patterns of dead matter are the most easily read and reshaped. The patterns of living matter are more complex, and the patterns of living matter resist the reading and the reshaping with a vigour that the practitioner must overcome. The patterns of persons are the most complex of all, and the patterns of persons are the most resistant, for the pattern of a person is bound up with the will of the person and the will of the person is itself a part of the pattern.

The patterns of places are of a different order, and the patterns of places are the subject of much of the practice of the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) and the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court). The pattern of a place is the configuration of the place in all of its aspects, including the configuration of the soil and the waters and the creatures that dwell within the place. The pattern of a place is, in this sense, a pattern of patterns, and the reshaping of a place is a reshaping of the patterns of which the place is composed. The practitioners of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) hold that the reshaping of a place is the most demanding of the disciplines of the Art, and the practitioners of the Heartwood hold that the reshaping of a place is also the most enduring.

## Pattern and Memory

The patterns of the world have a memory, and the memory is one of the most important features of the medium for the practitioner of the Art. A pattern that has been reshaped retains the traces of the reshaping, and the traces may be read by a practitioner of sufficient skill. A pattern that has been repeatedly reshaped accumulates the traces of the many shapings, and the accumulated traces constitute a kind of history that may be read by the practitioner who has learned to read it. The relics of the Art are, in this sense, patterns of great age and of accumulated reshaping, and the reading of a relic is one of the most demanding of the disciplines of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court).

The memory of a pattern is also the source of the [drift of the Cost](/content/magic/law/cost) that is one of the costs of the Art. A practitioner who reshapes a pattern leaves a trace of the practitioner in the pattern, and the practitioner who is reshaped by another practitioner leaves a trace of the other in the practitioner. The traces accumulate over a career, and the accumulated traces constitute a kind of history of the practitioner's practice. The chronicler of [Avaros](/content/location/settlement/city/avaros) has remarked that a practitioner of advanced years is a pattern of considerable complexity, and the remark has been received by the traditions with the customary mixture of agreement and of polite dissent.
