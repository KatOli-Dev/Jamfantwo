## Overview

The Art is bounded by limits that no tradition has been able to exceed, and the limits are observed with a consistency that has been remarked upon by the chroniclers of every age. The limits are not imposed by any authority, and the limits are not the consequence of any failure of skill. The limits are properties of the order of the world, and the practitioner of the Art is one who has learned to work within them. The chroniclers of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) state the limits in a form that has come to be the most widely cited, and the limits are treated here under three headings.

This page sets down the three limits as they are commonly stated, with the principal variants of the formulation and the principal disputes among the traditions. The page notes the cases in which the limits have been tested, and the page notes the cases in which the limits have been reported, by the traditions or by their critics, to have been exceeded.

## The First Limit: Like Affects Like

The first limit is the principle that resonance acts only between like things. A practitioner of the Art may only reach a subject through a [path of the Source](/content/magic/law/source) of sympathy, and the path exists only between the pattern of the practitioner and the pattern of the subject. The closer the pattern of the practitioner is to the pattern of the subject, the more direct the path, and the more readily the practitioner may reshape the subject. The more distant the patterns, the weaker the path, and the less the practitioner may accomplish.

The limit is observed in every working of the Art, and the limit is the foundation of the disciplines of attunement that the traditions teach. A practitioner who wishes to reshape a [pattern of the medium](/content/magic/law/medium) must first bring the practitioner into sympathy with the pattern, and the attunement is performed by a discipline of attention and of will. The chronicler of the [Forest Fellowship of the Heartwood](/content/government/local/forest-fellowship-of-the-heartwood) has recorded that a practitioner who has not first attuned to the subject will accomplish nothing, and the chronicler of [Avaros](/content/location/settlement/city/avaros) has recorded that an attempt to reshape a pattern without attunement is a common cause of harm to the practitioner.

The limit is the source of the specialisation of the traditions. The [Forest Fellowship](/content/government/local/forest-fellowship-of-the-heartwood) is closely attuned to the patterns of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood), and the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) is closely attuned to the patterns of the forests more generally. The practitioners of the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) are attuned to the patterns of the mountains and the seas, and the practitioners of [Khalisar](/content/government/local/council-of-khalisar) are attuned to the patterns of the inland cities and the routes of trade. The attunement is part of the long apprenticeship that the traditions require, and the attunement is one of the principal reasons that practitioners of one tradition are not readily interchangeable with practitioners of another.

## The Second Limit: Pattern Is Conserved

The second limit is the principle that the total of pattern in the world is conserved. A practitioner of the Art may reshape a pattern, but the practitioner may not create a pattern from nothing, and the practitioner may not destroy a pattern entirely. The reshaping is a redistribution, and the redistribution is constrained by the conservation of the total. The chronicler of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) has remarked that the total of pattern is as fixed as the total of weight in a balance, and the remark has been taken up by several of the academies.

The limit is the source of the [costs](/content/magic/law/cost) of the Art. A practitioner who reshapes a pattern takes the substance of the pattern from the practitioner's own pattern or from some other pattern that is in sympathy with the practitioner. The redistribution is recorded in the practitioner's body and mind, and the redistribution is one of the principal reasons that the Art is taught only to those who are prepared to bear the [cost](/content/magic/law/cost). The chroniclers of [Velden](/content/location/settlement/city/velden) have recorded several cases in which an untaught practitioner attempted a reshaping without the discipline of attunement, and the cases agree that the practitioner was harmed in the attempt.

The limit is also the source of the practice of relic making. A relic is an object into which a practitioner has poured a portion of pattern, and the relic is, in this sense, a concentration of pattern at the expense of the practitioner's own pattern. The relics of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) and the [Forest Fellowship](/content/government/local/forest-fellowship-of-the-heartwood) are among the most concentrated of the relics of the Art, and the relics are valued by the traditions for the discipline that produced them and for the burden that the producing practitioner bore.

## The Third Limit: The Living Resist

The third limit is the principle that the patterns of living things resist the reading and the reshaping with a vigour that the patterns of dead matter do not. A practitioner of the Art may read a stone with comparative ease, and a practitioner may reshape a stone within the bounds of the first two limits with little difficulty. A practitioner who attempts to read or to reshape a [pattern of the medium](/content/magic/law/medium) of a living thing encounters a resistance that is not encountered in the patterns of dead matter, and the resistance is greater the more complex the living thing and the more vigorous its will.

The limit is the source of the particular care that the traditions take when the subject of a working is a living person. The reshaping of the pattern of a person is the most demanding of the disciplines of the Art, and the reshaping is undertaken only with the consent of the subject and under the supervision of a master of the tradition. The [Council of Khalisar](/content/government/local/council-of-khalisar) has been particularly insistent on this point, and the Council has published several accounts of practitioners who have undertaken the reshaping of a person without the consent of the person and have been harmed in the attempt.

The chronicler observes that the resistance of the living is not a moral limit, and the resistance is not imposed by any authority. The resistance is a property of the order of the world, and the resistance is observed in every case in which a living pattern is read or reshaped. The chronicler notes that the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) has published an account attributed to a practitioner of the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) who claimed to have reshaped the pattern of a person without the consent of the person, and the chronicler notes that the account is disputed by the academies and is received by the traditions with caution. The chronicler records the account for the reader's consideration, and the chronicler does not adjudicate the dispute.

## Edge Cases and Disputes

The traditions agree on the three limits as they are stated above, and the traditions agree that the limits are not to be exceeded. The traditions disagree about the edges of the limits, and the traditions disagree about the cases in which the limits are said to have been approached. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds that the first limit may be deepened by long practice, and the Court holds that a practitioner of sufficient skill may reach patterns that an untutored practitioner cannot. The [Council of Khalisar](/content/government/local/council-of-khalisar) holds that the first limit is fixed, and the Council holds that the practitioner may only follow the paths that already exist.

The chronicler observes that the disputes about the edges of the limits are of greater interest to the traditions themselves than to the practitioner at work. The practitioner at work is occupied with the reading and the reshaping, and the practitioner at work has little time for the questions that occupy the chroniclers. The questions are not, however, without importance, for the answers that the traditions give shape the disciplines that the traditions teach, and the disciplines in turn shape the workings that the traditions make their own. The chronicler records the disputes for the reader's consideration, and the chronicler does not adjudicate.
