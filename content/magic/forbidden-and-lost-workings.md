## Workings That Are Proscribed or No Longer Known

The traditions of magic have recorded workings that are forbidden, either because they cause harm to the practitioner or to the subject, because they violate the ethical constraints that the traditions have established, or because they approach the [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) of magic in ways that are not safe. There are also workings that are lost, recorded only in the relics of magic or in the accounts of traditions that have not survived to the present age.

This page describes the forbidden and lost workings that the traditions have recorded, beginning with the principles that govern the prohibition of workings, continuing with the forbidden workings that are known, and concluding with the lost workings that scholars have sought to recover.

## The Principles of Prohibition

A working may be forbidden for any of three reasons. The first is that the working causes harm to the practitioner that is disproportionate to the benefit of the working. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has forbidden several workings of this kind, holding that no working should exact a [cost](/content/magic/law/cost) that exceeds the value of the reshaping that the working performs.

The second reason is that the working causes harm to the subject that the traditions cannot accept. The reshaping of a person without consent is forbidden by every tradition, and the reshaping of a living thing without cause is forbidden by the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) and the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship). The traditions have also forbidden workings that would cause lasting harm to the pattern of a place or an object.

The third reason is that the working approaches the limits of magic in ways that are not safe. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) has forbidden several workings that would attempt to exceed the conservation of pattern, and the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) has forbidden workings that would attempt to sever the path of resonance between a practitioner and a subject.

The traditions do not agree on all prohibitions. A working that is forbidden by one tradition may be permitted by another, and the disputes about the boundaries of permissible practice are among the most vigorous in the study of magic. The prohibitions described in this page are those that are accepted by most of the principal traditions, with the points of disagreement noted where they are known.

## Working upon a Person without Consent

The most universally forbidden working is the reshaping of a person without the person's consent. The traditions agree that the pattern of a person is the most complex and the most resistant of any subject and that reshaping a person without consent is a violation of the order of magic itself. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has been particularly insistent on this point and has published several accounts of practitioners who undertook the reshaping of a person without consent and were harmed in the attempt.

The prohibition extends, in a lesser degree, to the reshaping of a person with consent that was given under duress. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds that a person who consents to a reshaping under threat or under the influence of another's will has not truly consented, and a practitioner who acts upon such consent is in violation of the ethical constraints of magic.

The reading of a person without consent is also forbidden by most traditions, though the prohibition is less universally accepted. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) holds that the reading of a person without consent is a violation of the person's pattern and is therefore forbidden, while the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) hold that the reading of a person without consent is permissible if the reading is necessary for the protection of the person or of others.

## The Forging of Compelled Sympathy

A working that would compel sympathy between a practitioner and a subject that does not naturally share sympathy is forbidden by every tradition. The forging of compelled sympathy would violate the first [limit](/content/magic/law/limit) of magic, which holds that resonance acts only between like things, and a practitioner who attempts to forge a sympathy that does not exist would be attempting to exceed the order of the world.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) has recorded several cases in which practitioners attempted to forge compelled sympathy and were harmed in the attempt. The accounts describe practitioners who attempted to reach patterns that were distant from their own and who suffered a tearing of the pattern that left them unable to practise magic for extended periods.

The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) has published an account attributed to a practitioner of the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) who claimed to have forged a sympathy that did not naturally exist. The account is disputed by the academies and received by the traditions with caution, for the working described would violate the first limit of magic if the account were accurate.

## The Severing of Pattern

A working that would sever the pattern of a living thing from its configuration, causing death or lasting harm, is forbidden by every tradition. The severing of pattern would violate the second limit of magic, which holds that the total of pattern in the world is conserved, and a practitioner who severs a pattern would be attempting to destroy a pattern entirely.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) has recorded several accounts of severing that were performed in the early history of magic, before the traditions had established the ethical constraints that govern the practice of the present age. The accounts describe practitioners who severed the patterns of living things and caused death, and the Fellowship holds that the practitioners who performed these severings were themselves harmed by the cost of the working.

The severing of pattern is distinguished from the reshaping of pattern in that severing destroys the coherence of the pattern, while reshaping alters the configuration while preserving coherence. The distinction is taught in every tradition, and the apprentice is trained to recognise the boundary between permissible reshaping and forbidden severing.

## The Binding of Will

A working that would bind the will of a person, compelling the person to act against the will, is forbidden by every tradition. The binding of will would violate the third [limit](/content/magic/law/limit) of magic, which holds that the patterns of living things resist reading and reshaping with a vigour that the patterns of dead matter do not. The will of a person is part of the pattern, and the binding of the will would be an attempt to overcome the resistance of the living pattern in a manner that the traditions cannot accept.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has recorded several cases in which practitioners attempted to bind the will of a person and were harmed in the attempt. The accounts describe practitioners who attempted to compel the obedience of another and who suffered a tearing of the pattern that left them unable to practise magic for extended periods.

The binding of will is distinguished from the persuading of will through the ordinary means of speech and argument. The practitioner who persuades another through speech is working within the ordinary order of human interaction, while the practitioner who binds another through magic is attempting to exceed the limits of magic and the ethical constraints of the traditions.

## Lost Workings

The traditions have recorded workings that are lost, known only from the accounts of traditions that have not survived to the present age or from the relics of magic that have preserved the traces of the workings. The recovery of lost workings is one of the principal subjects of the academic study of magic, and the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) have devoted considerable resources to the search for lost workings.

The [Letters on Pattern](/content/magic/relic/letters-on-pattern) contain several passages that describe workings that are not recorded in any surviving tradition. The letters describe methods of reading and reshaping that the author attributes to practitioners of the early age of magic, and the scholars of the academies have attempted to reconstruct these methods from the descriptions in the letters. The reconstructions have been only partially successful, for the descriptions in the letters are not sufficiently detailed to allow a practitioner to perform the workings without additional instruction.

The [Known-World Codex](/content/magic/relic/codex-of-the-known-world) also contains accounts of workings that are lost, for [Vaelen Eldreth](/content/people/historical/vaelen-eldreth) recorded the practices of peoples who no longer exist and whose traditions have not been preserved. The Codex describes workings of healing, shaping, and binding that were performed by the peoples of the southern continents before the arrival of the northern traditions, and the scholars of the academies have sought to recover these workings from the descriptions in the Codex.

The [Star-Seeker](/content/magic/relic/star-seeker) is a relic that may preserve lost workings within its pattern. The vessel was built and sailed by [Kaelen Navigator](/content/people/historical/kaelen-navigator), who was not a practitioner of magic in the strict sense but whose discipline of reading the stars and the currents produced effects that resemble workings of magic. The practitioners of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) have studied the [Star-Seeker](/content/magic/relic/star-seeker) for centuries, seeking to recover the methods by which Kaelen achieved the first crossing of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean).

## The Search for Lost Workings

The search for lost workings is conducted through the study of relics, the reading of ancient texts, and the investigation of traditions that have not been recorded. The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) are the principal institutions engaged in the search, and their scholars have travelled to every continent in search of records of lost workings.

The search is complicated by the fact that many lost workings are known only from accounts that are themselves incomplete or unreliable. The scholar who would recover a lost working must read the accounts critically, distinguishing the description of the working from the interpretation of the account's author, and must reconstruct the working from the traces that survive.

The traditions do not encourage the search for lost workings that are known to be forbidden. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds that a working that has been lost may have been lost for a reason, and the Court counsels caution in the search for workings that the traditions have not preserved. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) takes a more practical view, holding that a lost working that is found may be of use even if it was lost for a reason, and the Council supports the search for lost workings among its practitioners.
