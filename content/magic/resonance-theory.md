## The Principle Understood across Traditions

Resonance is the principle from which the Art draws, the property of the world by which like things act upon like. The theory of resonance is the most extensively developed of the theoretical branches of the Art, and every tradition has produced its own account of the nature and behaviour of resonance. The accounts disagree on many points of detail, but they agree on the fundamental observation that the world is a fabric of patterns in which every part is in sympathy with every other part, and the practitioner is one who has learned to read the fabric and follow the threads.

This page describes the theory of resonance as it is understood across the principal traditions, beginning with the nature of resonance itself, continuing with the pathways by which resonance operates, and concluding with the disputes that remain unresolved.

## The Nature of Resonance

The chroniclers of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) define resonance as the principle of sympathy by which like things reach like. The definition is the most widely cited of any in the literature of the Art, and it has been adopted by every tradition that has produced a written account of its doctrine. The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) prefers the older term of sympathy, but the two words are taken by most traditions to mean the same thing.

Resonance is not a substance that can be measured, stored, or transferred from one vessel to another. The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) has published several accounts of failed experiments in which an attempt was made to gather resonance into a containment and release it at need, and the accounts agree that the containment gathered nothing and the release produced nothing. The Compact's experiments are cited by every tradition as the strongest evidence that resonance is not a substance.

Resonance is not a force that acts upon things from outside. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has been particularly insistent on this point, holding that resonance is the relation between things rather than a thing in itself. A practitioner who speaks of resonance as a force misunderstands the nature of the Art and will find the workings resistant and the costs higher than necessary.

Resonance is not the gift of any god, nor the property of any chosen people, nor the result of any historical event. It is a property of the order of the world, and it has been present in the world for as long as the world has had order. The traditions agree on this point, and the agreement is one of the foundations of the shared practice of the Art.

## The Pathways of Resonance

Resonance operates through pathways that connect patterns that are like each other. The pathways are not physical nor visible to the uninitiated. They are the relations between patterns, and the practitioner follows a pathway from the pattern to the subject through attention and attunement.

The older accounts describe the practitioner as one who walks the paths of resonance, a description preserved in the discipline of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) and the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship). The paths are not fixed like roads but shifting like the paths of water through a marsh, and the practitioner who would walk them must learn to read the shifting of the pathways as the patterns shift.

The practitioners of the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) prefer a different figure: the practitioner as one who adjusts a tuning until practitioner and subject vibrate together. The two figures describe the same discipline in different terms and are taken to be equivalent in practice. The choice of figure has been a matter of dispute between the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) and the academies, carried on for several generations without resolution.

The pathways of resonance are not all equally direct. Some patterns are close to each other in the sense that the pathway between them is short and easily followed; others are distant, and the pathway between them is long and difficult. The closeness of patterns is determined by the degree of likeness between them: two stones of the same kind are closer than a stone and a tree, and two trees of the same kind are closer than a tree and a person. The practitioner who would reach a distant pattern must find the series of intermediate patterns that bridge the distance, a discipline that requires extensive knowledge of the patterns of the world.

## Whether Sympathy Can Be Deepened

The most important dispute in the theory of resonance concerns the question of whether sympathy may be deepened by practice. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds that it may, and the Court's practitioners devote considerable effort to the deepening of sympathy with the patterns of the forest. A practitioner of the Court who has walked the same grove for many years will find that the pathway to that grove is more direct than the pathway to a grove the practitioner has never visited, and the Court takes this observation as evidence that sympathy may be deepened.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) holds a similar position, and the Fellowship's practice of prolonged attunement to the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) is based on the assumption that sympathy deepens with continued attention. The Fellowship does not, however, hold that sympathy may be deepened indefinitely, and its practitioners recognise a limit beyond which further attention produces no further deepening.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) holds the opposing position, maintaining that sympathy is fixed in the order of the world and that the practitioner may only follow the pathways that already exist. The Council observes that a practitioner who has walked the same marketplace for many years is more skilled at reading that marketplace than a newcomer, but the Council attributes the difference to the practitioner's knowledge of the patterns rather than to any deepening of sympathy. The two positions produce the same behaviour in practice, for both agree that the practitioner who has practised in a place for many years will accomplish more in that place than a newcomer.

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) have not taken a position on the dispute, treating the question as one that cannot be settled by the evidence currently available. The academies have published several studies that attempt to measure the effect of practice on sympathy, but the results have been inconclusive, and the dispute continues.

## Resonance at Many Scales

The principle of resonance is observed at many scales beyond the Art, and the traditions do not regard resonance as peculiar to the Art alone. The [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) responds to the moon in a pattern that resembles the response of water in a bowl, and the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) responds to the wind in a pattern repeated at every scale. These phenomena are not workings of the Art; they are properties of the order of the world, and the practitioner who reads them is reading the same patterns that the Art employs. A more extensive discussion of the moon's influence on resonance is given in the account of the [Moon-Weave](/content/mythology/the-moon-weave).

The traditions take these observations as evidence that resonance is a fundamental property of the world rather than a product of the practitioner's will. The practitioner does not create resonance but rather learns to read it and to walk the pathways it provides. The Art is the discipline by which the practitioner learns to do this, and the theory of resonance is the account of what the practitioner is doing when the Art is practised.

## Resonance in the Relics

The theory of resonance is also the foundation of the understanding of relics. A relic is an object whose pattern has been so deeply reshaped, or has so absorbed the resonance of repeated workings, that it has become a working in itself. The [Known-World Codex](/content/magic/relic/codex-of-the-known-world) is a relic of this kind, for the accumulated attention of its readers has fixed the pattern of the text in a manner that approaches the condition of a working.

The [Letters on Pattern](/content/magic/relic/letters-on-pattern) are a relic of a different kind, for the resonance of the text is carried not by the physical object but by the patterns that the text describes. A practitioner who reads the Letters may attune to the patterns they describe, and the attunement may be deepened with each reading. The [Eldreth Library](/content/magic/relic/library-of-eldreth) holds the original manuscript of the Letters, and scholars who consult the manuscript report that the resonance of the text is stronger in the original than in the copies.

The [Star-Seeker](/content/magic/relic/star-seeker) is a relic whose resonance is of particular interest to the theory of resonance, for the vessel was built and sailed by [Kaelen Navigator](/content/people/historical/kaelen-navigator) in the first recorded crossing of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The vessel bears the symbols of the constellations upon its hull, and the practitioners of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) have suggested that the painting of these symbols was itself a working of the Art, fixing the vessel's pattern in sympathy with the stars. The suggestion is consistent with the observed behaviour of the vessel in the centuries since its construction.

## The Unresolved Questions

The theory of resonance is the most developed of the theoretical branches of the Art, but several questions remain unresolved. The traditions do not agree on whether resonance is a single principle or a family of related principles. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds that resonance is one thing, manifesting differently in different contexts. The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) hold that the observed phenomena are better explained by several distinct principles that work together.

The traditions do not agree on whether resonance may be blocked or interrupted. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) holds that resonance cannot be blocked, for the pathways of sympathy are properties of the order of the world and cannot be severed by any act of practitioner or of nature. The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) holds that resonance may be interrupted by certain materials and has published accounts of workings that failed when the practitioner attempted to reach a subject through a layer of lead or of running water.

The disputes are conducted with the civility that characterises the academic study of the Art, and the traditions continue to investigate the nature of resonance with the same discipline that they apply to the practice of the Art itself.
