## The First Discipline of the Practitioner

The reading of a pattern is the first discipline taught in every tradition, the foundation upon which all later work is built. Reading demands a stillness of body and mind that the practitioner must acquire before any other skill can be of use. The practitioner who cannot read a stone with patience cannot hope to read a living creature, and the practitioner who cannot read a living creature cannot hope to read a person. The progression from simple to complex patterns is the structure upon which every tradition builds its curriculum.

This page describes the discipline of pattern reading as it is practised across the traditions, beginning with the nature of the act itself, continuing with the methods by which patterns are read in different media, and concluding with the limits and risks of reading.

## The Act of Reading

The chroniclers of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) define reading as the perception of the configuration of a subject as a whole. The perception is not a vision or a hearing but a faculty the traditions name differently: the Sylvan Court calls it the inner eye, the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) calls it the listening, and the academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) call it the attunement. Despite the different names, the faculty is the same: the practitioner attends to the subject until the configuration is perceived as a unity.

The first reading of a pattern is rarely complete. The practitioner must return to the reading many times before the pattern is perceived in its full configuration. The libraries of [Avaros](/content/location/settlement/city/avaros) record that a pattern of even moderate complexity may require a season of patient attention before the practitioner has perceived all its aspects, and the records of [Velden](/content/location/settlement/city/velden) indicate that the pattern of a living person is rarely fully perceived in less than a year.

The reading is not a passive observation, for the reading itself changes the reader. The practitioner who has read a pattern deeply enough to reshape it is no longer the same practitioner who began the reading, and this change is part of the [cost](/content/magic/law/cost) of the Art. The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) holds that the change is the price of knowledge, and the practitioner who would know the pattern must be prepared to bear the change.

## Reading Dead Matter

The patterns of dead matter are the simplest and the most easily read. A stone, a piece of wood, a quantity of water, and a metal object all have patterns that the practitioner may read with comparative ease. The simplicity of these patterns makes them the subject of the first exercises in every tradition.

The practitioner who reads a stone attends to the configuration of the stone as a whole: its shape, its weight, its density, its colour, its temperature, and the internal structure that gives it coherence. The reading is not a list of properties but a perception of the stone's unity in all of its aspects. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) teaches the apprentice to read a stone by holding it in the hand and attending to the feel of the stone in the palm, letting the perception of the stone's configuration arise without forcing the attention.

The practitioner who reads water attends to the configuration of the water as a whole: its movement, its temperature, its clarity, and the patterns of light and shadow that play upon its surface. The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) teaches the apprentice to read water by standing in the shallows and letting the movement of the water against the skin become the focus of attention, reading the pattern of the current through the body rather than through the eyes.

The practitioner who reads metal attends to the configuration of the metal as a whole: its composition, its grain, its temper, and the traces of the working that shaped it. The dwarven practitioners of the mountain holds are particularly skilled in the reading of metal, for their craft traditions have developed methods of reading that the surface traditions have not recorded.

## Reading Living Matter

The patterns of living matter are more complex than the patterns of dead matter and resist reading with a vigour that the practitioner must overcome. A tree, a bird, a fish, and a beast all have patterns that are more intricate than the patterns of stone or water, and the practitioner who would read them must bring greater patience and greater discipline to the task.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) is the tradition most skilled in the reading of living things, for the Court's practitioners spend their lives in the forest and have developed methods of reading the patterns of trees and creatures that no other tradition has matched. The Court teaches the apprentice to read a tree by sitting with it for a full day, attending to the pattern of the tree as it changes with the movement of the sun and the wind. The apprentice who has read a single tree thoroughly will find that the reading of other trees is easier, for the patterns of trees share a common structure that the apprentice learns to recognise.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) teaches the reading of living things through prolonged stillness in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood). The apprentice sits in a single place and lets the patterns of the living things in that place enter the awareness without forcing the attention. The method is slower than the method of the Sylvan Court, but the Fellowship holds that the reading achieved through stillness is deeper than the reading achieved through active attention.

The resistance of living patterns to reading is greater the more complex the living thing and the more vigorous its will. A tree offers less resistance than a bird, and a bird offers less resistance than a person. The practitioner who would read a living person must overcome a resistance that is unlike any other in the Art, for the will of the person is itself part of the pattern, and the will resists the attention of the reader.

## Reading Persons

The reading of a person is the most demanding of the reading disciplines and is undertaken only by practitioners who have undergone extensive training. The pattern of a person is bound up with the will of the person, and the will is itself part of the pattern. The practitioner who reads a person must read not only the configuration of the body but also the configuration of the mind, the memory, and the intention.

The traditions agree that the reading of a person should not be undertaken without the subject's consent, for the reading of a person without consent is a violation of the ethical constraints of the Art. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has been particularly insistent on this point and has published several accounts of practitioners who attempted to read a person without consent and were harmed in the attempt.

The practitioner who reads a person with consent proceeds with care, attending to the configuration of the person as a whole. The reading is not a violation of the person's privacy but a perception of the person's unity, and the practitioner who reads a person deeply will find that the reading reveals not only the person's present state but also the traces of the person's history.

## Reading Places

The patterns of places are of a different order from the patterns of individual things and require a different discipline of reading. The pattern of a place is the configuration of the place in all of its aspects, including the soil, the waters, the creatures that dwell within it, and the history of the workings that have been performed there. The practitioner who reads a place reads a pattern of patterns.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) is the tradition most skilled in the reading of places, for the Fellowship's practitioners spend their lives in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) and have developed methods of reading the pattern of the forest as a whole. The Fellowship teaches the apprentice to read a place by sitting in stillness and letting the pattern of the place enter the awareness, a method that requires weeks of practice before the first results are apparent.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) also teaches the reading of places, but the Court's method differs from the Fellowship's. The Court teaches the apprentice to walk the bounds of a place, reading the pattern of the place from the boundaries inward rather than from a single point outward. The Court holds that the boundaries of a place are as important as the interior in determining the pattern of the place as a whole.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) teaches the reading of urban places, attending to the patterns of the streets, the buildings, and the flow of people through the city. The Council's method is more active than the methods of the forest traditions, for the Council holds that the pattern of a city is constantly shifting and that the practitioner who reads a city must read the movement as well as the structure.

## The Limits of Reading

Reading is bounded by the same [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) that govern the Art as a whole. A practitioner may only read a subject through a path of sympathy, and the path exists only between patterns that are alike. The closer the pattern of the practitioner is to the pattern of the subject, the more readily the practitioner may read the subject. A practitioner who has attuned to the forest cannot readily read the sea, and a practitioner who has attuned to the city cannot readily read the deep places of the earth.

The reading is also limited by the memory of the pattern. A pattern that has been reshaped retains the traces of the reshaping, and the traces may obscure the original configuration of the pattern. The practitioner who reads a pattern that has been deeply reshaped must read through the traces to reach the original configuration, a discipline that requires considerable skill.

The reading of a pattern that has absorbed the resonance of many workings over many years is the most demanding of the reading disciplines, for the traces of the workings accumulate and complicate the pattern. The relics of the Art are patterns of this kind, and the reading of a relic such as the [Known-World Codex](/content/magic/relic/codex-of-the-known-world) or the [Letters on Pattern](/content/magic/relic/letters-on-pattern) is a discipline that only the most skilled practitioners can perform with confidence.

## The Risks of Reading

Reading carries risks that the practitioner must understand before attempting the discipline. The principal risk is that the practitioner may read a pattern that carries harmful traces and that these traces may enter the practitioner's pattern through the reading. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has published several accounts of practitioners who read objects that had been reshaped by another practitioner of questionable skill and were harmed by the traces.

The second risk is that the practitioner may become lost in the reading, losing the distinction between the pattern and the self. The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) has recorded cases in which a practitioner who read the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) too deeply began to experience the pattern of the forest as the pattern of the self, a condition that required the intervention of the master to restore.

The reading of a pattern is therefore a discipline that the practitioner approaches with respect and caution. The practitioner who reads with patience and discipline will find that the pattern reveals the configuration, and the practitioner who reads with carelessness and haste will find that the pattern resists the reading and may harm the reader.
