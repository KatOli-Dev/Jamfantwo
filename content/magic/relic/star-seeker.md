## Overview

The Star-Seeker is the vessel in which [Kaelen the Navigator](/content/people/historical/kaelen-the-navigator) made the first recorded crossing of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) from the eastern coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) to the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), a passage of seventeen days at sea that opened the way for the intercontinental trade that has since defined the economy and the politics of both continents. The vessel was built in the modest harbour of what is now the city of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) in the early years of the second age, and the vessel was fitted for the voyage with a reinforced hull and a deep hold for provisions, for Kaelen was resolved to venture beyond the coastal waters that the mariners of his age knew, and the vessel was built to outlast the storms and the calms of the open ocean. The chronicler of the maritime age records that the Star-Seeker was the first ship to bear the symbols of the constellations upon her hull, and the painting of the ship is the foundation of the custom that the vessels of the [Auric Passage](/content/location/route/trade/auric-passage) have observed in the centuries since.

## Construction and Design

The Star-Seeker was built in the boatyard of a shipwright of the harbour of Eldreth, and the vessel was constructed of oak timbers that had been seasoned for two years in the customary manner. The hull was reinforced with an inner skin of larch planking, and the reinforcement gave the vessel a strength that the coastal craft of the period did not possess. The keel was laid in the spring of the year that the chronicles of the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) record as the year of Kaelen's preparation, and the hull was completed before the autumn gales set in. The chronicler of the maritime age records that the vessel was the largest that the harbour of Eldreth had produced to that date, and the vessel was a source of local curiosity throughout the months of her construction.

The design of the vessel was based upon the coastal craft of the eastern Aethelaran shore, with modifications that Kaelen had specified after his years of study. The hull was deeper than the customary proportion, and the depth gave the vessel a stability that the shallower craft of the period did not possess. The single mast was raked slightly aft of the midships point, and the rake allowed the vessel to carry a larger sail without the heeling that the upright masts of the period produced. The sail was of a new cut that Kaelen had devised, with a deeper foot and a flatter head than the sails of the coastal craft, and the cut allowed the vessel to draw a steadier course in the variable winds of the open ocean. The chronicler records that the design was the foundation of the vessels of the Auric Passage that followed, and the design was refined by the generations of shipwrights who came after.

The hold of the Star-Seeker was fitted with the provisions that the crossing would require, and the provisions were stored with a care that the chronicler has described as the foundation of the success of the voyage. The water casks were sealed with pitch and were stowed along the centreline of the hold, where the motion of the vessel would keep the water from shifting. The provisions included salted meat, hard bread, dried fruit, and the small store of spices that Kaelen had selected to preserve the meat and to render the hard bread palatable. The chronicler records that the provisions were sufficient for a voyage of twenty-five days, and the sufficiency was a margin of safety that proved its value in the return voyage, when the vessel was driven far to the west of her intended course and the provisions had to last for thirty-two days in all.

## The Painted Constellations

The Star-Seeker was painted with the symbols of the constellations that Kaelen had studied in his years of preparation, and the painting of the vessel is the most distinctive feature of the relic that has survived to the present day. The symbols were painted upon the hull in a deep blue that the painters of the harbour had compounded from indigo and the white of chalk, and the symbols were placed at the bow and the stern of the vessel, with the principal constellations running in a band along the upper strake of the hull on either side. The chronicler of the maritime age records that the symbols were not merely decorative, and the symbols were the working vocabulary of the navigational method that Kaelen had developed, and the symbols allowed the crew to read the position of the vessel by the stars that passed over the painted figures in the night.

The principal constellation painted upon the hull was the Wanderer, a figure that the mariners of the eastern coast had long associated with the voyages of the open sea. The Wanderer was painted at the bow of the vessel, and the figure was rendered with a staff in one hand and a star in the other, in the iconography that the coastal painters had long employed. The secondary constellations were painted along the sides, and the figures included the Twin Stars, the Crown, the Serpent, and the Boat, each of which the chronicler of the maritime age has described in detail. The painting was completed before the vessel was launched, and the painting was the custom that the vessels of the [Auric Passage](/content/location/route/trade/auric-passage) would follow in the centuries after, with each vessel bearing upon her hull the figures that her master had chosen to mark the passage.

The chronicler notes that the painting of the Star-Seeker has been preserved by the careful stewardship of the relic, and the symbols are still legible upon the hull where the relic is housed. The paint has been renewed at intervals across the centuries, and the renewal has been conducted by the painters of the harbour of Eldreth in the same colours and the same figures that Kaelen had specified. The chronicler records that the painting is the foundation of the custom of the painted vessels, and the custom is observed by the ships of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and by the vessels of the eastern Aethelaran ports to the present day. The custom is one of the most distinctive of the maritime traditions of the known world.

## The Outward Voyage

The Star-Seeker departed from the harbour of Eldreth on the morning of the spring equinox, and the departure was attended by a crowd that the chronicler has recorded as the largest that the harbour had ever gathered. Kaelen took his place at the helm as the vessel cleared the harbour mouth, and he stood at the helm for the first full day and night of the open crossing, refusing to rest until the last trace of the Aethelaran coast had disappeared from the view of the lookouts in the crow's nest. The vessel sailed southward along the coast for three days before turning into the open ocean at a point where the land fell away to the west and the water stretched unbroken to the horizon. The chronicler of the maritime age records that the turn was made at the place that Kaelen had chosen after long study of the currents, and the place has been marked upon the charts of the Auric Passage ever since.

The outward crossing of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) required seventeen days, and the chronicle of the voyage records that the crew endured storms, becalming, and a period of severe rationing when the provisions that had been estimated for a shorter crossing proved insufficient. Kaelen maintained the discipline of the crew through a combination of firm command and the example of his own endurance, and he shared the rations equally and took his turn at the oars when the wind failed. The navigational method that Kaelen employed was based on the observation of the sun by day and the stars by night, and he kept a careful log of the angles and the bearings that would allow him to retrace his course and, more importantly, to guide others across the same waters.

The first landfall was sighted on the morning of the eighteenth day, and the coast that the Star-Seeker had reached was the northern shore of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), at a point that later scholars have identified as the vicinity of the modern port of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara). Kaelen and his crew landed on the beach, and they were greeted by the local people, who were the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the northern [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast. The exchange that followed was the first recorded contact between the peoples of the two continents across the open ocean, and the Star-Seeker was the vessel upon which the contact was made. The chronicler records that the vessel remained at anchor in the Sahrani harbour for six months, and the vessel was the centre of the exploration of the northern shoreline and the river valleys that Kaelen conducted during his stay.

## The Return

The return voyage was more difficult than the outward crossing, for the winds of late autumn were less favourable, and the Star-Seeker was driven far to the west of her intended course by a storm that the chronicles have recorded as among the most severe of the season. The crew despaired of ever sighting the Aethelaran coast again, for the provisions were running low and the water casks were beginning to leak. Kaelen held to his bearings, trusting in the observations he had recorded in his log, and after twenty-three days at sea the Star-Seeker raised the coast of Aethelar at a point south of the harbour from which she had departed. The return was celebrated by the people of Eldreth, and the news of the discovery spread along the coast and into the interior, carried by the traders and the messengers who travelled the routes of the eastern lowlands.

The chronicler of the maritime age records that the Star-Seeker was repaired in the boatyard of her construction, and the vessel was fitted for two further voyages that Kaelen made across the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) in the years that followed. The vessel carried more settlers on each successive voyage, and the vessel was the foundation of the first permanent human settlements on the Sahrani coast. The vessel's final voyage was made in the twentieth year of Kaelen's career, and the vessel was then laid up in the harbour of Eldreth, where the relic has been preserved by the careful stewardship of the city and of the institutions that have maintained the maritime tradition.

## The Disposition of the Vessel

The Star-Seeker was laid up in the harbour of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) at the conclusion of Kaelen's final voyage, and the vessel was housed in a purpose-built shed upon the quay that has borne the vessel's name in the centuries since. The chronicler of the maritime age records that the vessel was preserved with a care that was reserved for the most treasured relics of the city, and the care included the renewal of the paint of the constellations at intervals of twenty years, the replacement of the timbers that had begun to rot, and the maintenance of the rigging and the sails in a state that would allow the vessel to be put to sea if the need arose. The vessel was visited by every maritime delegation that came to Eldreth, and the vessel was the centre of the ceremonial observances of the harbour on the anniversaries of Kaelen's voyages.

The relic was transferred to the custody of the [Eldreth Library](/content/magic/relic/library-of-eldreth) in the centuries after the foundation of the library, and the transfer was conducted with a ceremony that the chronicler of the library has recorded at length. The vessel was moved from the quayside shed to a hall that the library had built for the purpose, and the hall was constructed to the dimensions of the vessel with a clearance of a hand's breadth upon every side. The hall was roofed with glass to admit the light, and the light fell upon the painted constellations of the hull in a manner that the chronicler has described as the most moving of the spectacles of the library. The vessel has been maintained by the library in the same condition that the quayside custodians had observed, and the vessel is one of the most visited of the relics that the library houses.

## Significance

The Star-Seeker is the foundation of the maritime tradition of the [Auric Passage](/content/location/route/trade/auric-passage), and the vessel is the relic in which the first crossing of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) was made. The passage that [Kaelen the Navigator](/content/people/historical/kaelen-the-navigator) charted in the Star-Seeker became the principal route of trade between the northern and the southern continents, and the trade that flows along the passage is the foundation of the prosperity of every port that the route serves. The painting of the constellations upon the hull is the foundation of the custom of the painted vessels, and the custom is observed by the ships of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and by the vessels of the eastern Aethelaran ports to the present day. The vessel is the most visited of the relics that the Eldreth Library houses, and the vessel is a place of pilgrimage for the mariners who still sail the passage that Kaelen discovered. The chronicler finds in the Star-Seeker one of the most venerated and least displaced of the relics of the known world, preserved not by a ruling house or a priesthood but by the steady stewardship of the city that bore the vessel and of the library that now maintains her. The relic endures, in the hall of glass upon the quay of Eldreth, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the open sea.
