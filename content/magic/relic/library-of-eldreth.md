## Overview

The Library of Eldreth is the principal repository of natural-philosophical work in the known world, the institution founded by [High Chronicler Vaelen](/content/people/historical/high-chronicler-vaelen) in the latter years of the second age and maintained without interruption in the city of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) through the centuries that have followed. The library houses the original manuscript of the Codex of the Known World, together with the journals, the drawings, and the specimens that Vaelen collected during the thirty-three years of his travels, and the library is the most important institution of natural philosophy on the continent of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The chronicler of the academies records that the library has been the model for the institutions of natural philosophy that have been established in the cities of every continent, and the library is the foundation of the tradition of systematic observation that the natural sciences of the present age still employ.

## Founding and the High Chronicler

The Library of Eldreth was founded by [High Chronicler Vaelen](/content/people/historical/high-chronicler-vaelen) in the latter years of his long life, and the foundation was the culmination of the work of observation and classification that the High Chronicler had pursued for more than three decades. The library was established in a building that the city of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) had granted to the High Chronicler for the purpose, and the building was situated upon a courtyard that the chronicler has described as one of the most pleasant of the city's interior spaces. The High Chronicler deposited in the library the original manuscript of the Codex of the Known World, together with the journals of his travels, the drawings of the species and the places that he had observed, and the specimens that he had collected and preserved in the customary manner. The chronicler of the library records that the High Chronicler appointed the first librarian from among the scholars who had assisted him in the compilation of the Codex, and the appointment was the foundation of the succession of scholars who have maintained the library in the centuries since.

The founding of the library was accompanied by a charter that the council of the [Kingdom of Aldreth](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-aldreth) granted to the institution, and the charter established the library as an independent body under the protection of the crown. The charter provided for the maintenance of the building and the collection from the revenues of the city, and the charter provided for the appointment of the librarian by the council of scholars that the High Chronicler had established. The chronicler records that the charter has been renewed and extended by each successive generation of rulers, and the charter is the foundation of the independence of the library from the political pressures that have affected the other institutions of the kingdom. The library has endured the sieges that the city has suffered, and the library has been preserved by the careful stewardship of the scholars who have maintained the institution through the most difficult of times.

The High Chronicler himself lived in the library for the last years of his life, and the High Chronicler worked in the reading room that the library had built for him upon the upper floor of the building. The chronicler of the library records that the High Chronicler received the scholars who came to consult the Codex and the journals, and the High Chronicler answered their questions with the patience that the chronicler has described as the most striking feature of his character. The High Chronicler died in the library in the one hundred and fourth year of his life, and his body was interred in the courtyard of the library that he had founded, beneath a simple stone that bears only his name and the title of his great work. The chronicler notes that the grave is the most visited of the tombs of the city, and the grave is the centre of the ceremonial observances of the library upon the anniversary of the High Chronicler's death.

## The Holdings

The principal holding of the library is the original manuscript of the Codex of the Known World, and the manuscript is the most valuable single document in the natural-philosophical collections of the known world. The manuscript is housed in a sealed case in the central chamber of the library, and the case is opened only upon the formal request of the senior scholars of the institution. The chronicler of the library records that the manuscript is in a state of preservation that the careful stewardship of the librarians has ensured, and the manuscript is consulted by the scholars who come to the library from every continent. The manuscript is the standard against which the copies that are held in the other libraries of the world are compared, and the manuscript is the foundation of the textual tradition of the Codex.

The library also houses the journals of the High Chronicler's travels, and the journals are the second most valuable holding of the institution. The journals comprise the daily records that [High Chronicler Vaelen](/content/people/historical/high-chronicler-vaelen) kept during his thirty-three years of travel, and the journals are the raw material from which the Codex was compiled. The journals are consulted by the scholars who study the method of the High Chronicler, and the journals are the foundation of the tradition of systematic observation that the natural sciences of the present age still employ. The chronicler records that the journals are preserved in the same careful manner as the Codex, and the journals are housed in a separate chamber that the library has built for the purpose.

The drawings that the High Chronicler made during his travels are the third principal holding of the library, and the drawings are the most beautiful of the works that the High Chronicler produced. The drawings comprise the portraits of the species of plants and animals that the High Chronicler observed, the maps of the regions that he visited, and the diagrams of the structures and the processes that he studied. The drawings are the work of the High Chronicler's own hand, and the drawings are supplemented by the drawings of the assistants who accompanied the High Chronicler upon his later travels. The chronicler of the library records that the drawings are the foundation of the tradition of natural-philosophical illustration that the academies of the present age still follow, and the drawings are the model for the illustrations that accompany the natural-philosophical works of the present day.

The specimens that the High Chronicler collected are the fourth principal holding of the library, and the specimens are the most fragile of the works that the institution preserves. The specimens comprise the dried plants, the preserved animals, the mineral samples, and the other materials that the High Chronicler gathered during his travels. The specimens are housed in a chamber that the library has fitted with the customary controls of temperature and humidity, and the chamber is the most carefully maintained of the rooms of the institution. The chronicler records that the specimens are consulted by the natural philosophers who come to the library to verify the observations that the High Chronicler recorded, and the specimens are the foundation of the tradition of the preservation of materials that the natural sciences of the present age still observe.

## The Library's Practice

The library maintains a practice of consultation that is open to the scholars of every continent, and the practice is the foundation of the reputation of the institution as the most important centre of natural philosophy in the known world. The scholar who wishes to consult the holdings of the library must apply to the librarian in writing, and the application must set out the purpose of the consultation and the materials that the scholar wishes to examine. The chronicler of the library records that the applications are considered by the librarian in consultation with the senior scholars of the institution, and the applications are approved unless the materials are of a fragility that the consultation would endanger. The approved application entitles the scholar to a seat in the reading room of the library and to the assistance of the librarians in the consultation of the materials.

The library also maintains a practice of correspondence with the institutions of natural philosophy in the other cities and the other continents, and the correspondence is the foundation of the network of scholarship that the institution has established. The librarians prepare copies of the works that the scholars request, and the copies are transmitted to the requesting institutions by the customary means. The chronicler records that the correspondence is the most extensive of any institution of natural philosophy in the known world, and the correspondence is the foundation of the unity of the natural-philosophical tradition across the wide range of the world's settlements.

The library maintains a practice of publication, and the practice is the foundation of the reputation of the institution as a centre of original work in natural philosophy. The scholars of the library publish the results of their observations and their classifications in the form of monographs and treatises, and the publications are distributed to the other institutions of the world by the customary means. The chronicler records that the publications of the library are the most widely read of any natural-philosophical works, and the publications are the foundation of the tradition of the open dissemination of knowledge that the natural sciences of the present age still observe.

## The Scholars and the Succession

The library is maintained by a succession of scholars who have continued the work of [High Chronicler Vaelen](/content/people/historical/high-chronicler-vaelen), and the succession is the foundation of the continuity of the institution across the centuries. The senior scholar of the library is the librarian, and the librarian is appointed by the council of scholars from among the fellows of the institution. The librarian holds office for life, and the librarian is responsible for the maintenance of the holdings, the conduct of the practice, and the appointment of the junior scholars. The chronicler of the library records that the succession of librarians has been unbroken since the foundation of the institution, and the succession is the foundation of the tradition of stewardship that the library embodies.

The junior scholars of the library are the fellows, and the fellows are appointed by the librarian from among the scholars who have applied for admission to the institution. The fellows are appointed for a term of years, and the fellows may be reappointed at the discretion of the librarian. The fellows are responsible for the daily work of the library, including the consultation of the holdings, the preparation of the copies, and the maintenance of the specimens. The chronicler records that the fellows are drawn from the academies of every continent, and the fellows return to their home institutions upon the conclusion of their terms. The circulation of the fellows is the foundation of the network of scholarship that the library has established, and the circulation is the foundation of the unity of the natural-philosophical tradition across the wide range of the world's settlements.

## Relation to the Academies

The library maintains a close relation with the academies of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) and of the other cities of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and the relation is the foundation of the tradition of natural-philosophical education that the continent has maintained. The senior scholars of the library give lectures at the academies upon the subjects of their specialisation, and the lectures are open to the students of the academies and to the public. The chronicler records that the lectures are the most widely attended of any natural-philosophical instruction in the known world, and the lectures are the foundation of the reputation of the city of Eldreth as a centre of learning.

The library also maintains a relation with the institutions of the other continents, and the relation is conducted through the correspondence and the exchange of scholars that the library has established. The scholars of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league), the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the [Deep Realm of Karhold](/content/government/national/deep-realm-of-karhold), and the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) are frequent correspondents of the library, and the scholars of these institutions consult the holdings of the library by the customary means. The chronicler records that the relation is the foundation of the international character of the natural-philosophical tradition, and the relation is one of the most distinctive features of the institution.

## Significance

The Library of Eldreth is the principal repository of natural-philosophical work in the known world, and the library is the most important institution of natural philosophy on the continent of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The holdings of the library, including the original manuscript of the Codex of the Known World and the journals and the drawings of [High Chronicler Vaelen](/content/people/historical/high-chronicler-vaelen), are the foundation of the natural-philosophical tradition that the academies of the present age still observe. The practice of the library, including the consultation of the holdings, the correspondence with the other institutions, and the publication of the original work of the scholars, is the model that the other institutions of the world have followed. The chronicler finds in the Library of Eldreth one of the most distinguished and least displaced of the institutions of the known world, maintained not by a ruling house or a priesthood but by the steady stewardship of the scholars who have continued the work of the High Chronicler through the centuries. The library endures, in the building upon the courtyard in the city of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth), as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the systematic observation of the natural world.
