## The World's Own Resonances

The phenomena that the traditions of the Art study are not produced by practitioners. They are properties of the order of the world, observable in places where resonance is unusually strong, in the behaviour of the seas and the skies, and in objects that have absorbed the attention of generations. The Art does not explain these phenomena; it is the discipline by which a practitioner learns to perceive them and, in some cases, to work within them. The phenomena are the context within which the Art is practised, and they have shaped the development of every tradition.

This page describes the principal phenomena that have been recorded by the traditions, beginning with the places where resonance is strongest, continuing with the natural resonances observable in the behaviour of the continents and the seas, and concluding with phenomena that have no clear explanation. It also notes the relationship between phenomena and the relics of the Art.

## Places of Strong Resonance

Resonance is not evenly distributed across the world. There are places where the paths of sympathy are more direct and more readily perceived, and these places have been the subject of study by every tradition. The most notable of these are the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood), the deep places of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and the [Drowned Isles](/content/location/natural/feature/drowned-isles).

The Aethelar Heartwood is the most extensively documented site of strong resonance in the known world. The forest has been inhabited by [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) for many centuries, and the elven practitioners of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) have recorded the resonance of the Heartwood in greater detail than any other place. The resonance of the Heartwood is felt by practitioners as a presence beneath the ordinary perceptions of the forest: a clarity of pattern that makes the reading of trees, creatures, and soil more immediate than in the woodlands outside the Heartwood. The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) holds that the resonance of the Heartwood is the source of the deep attunement that its practitioners achieve, and the Fellowship's critics have observed that its members are reluctant to practise the Art anywhere else.

The deep places of the Kharesh, the great desert of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), are said to possess a resonance of a different character. The nomads of the Kharesh speak of places where the sand sings and the air shimmers with a heat that has no source, and the caravans that cross the desert avoid these places without being able to say why. The practitioners of the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) have made several expeditions into the deep desert to study these phenomena, and their accounts describe a resonance that is difficult to read and harder to attune to, as though the pattern of the desert resists the attention of the practitioner in a manner that the pattern of the forest does not. The Khalisar Council has published several accounts of these expeditions, and the disputes that have arisen over the interpretation of the findings have been among the most vigorous in the study of the [Source](/content/magic/law/source) in the present century.

The Drowned Isles, off the western coast of the principal continent, are a chain of small, rocky islands where the resonance is said to shift with the tides. The practitioners of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) have studied the islands more thoroughly than any other tradition, and their records describe a resonance that is strongest at low tide and weakest at high tide, as though the water itself dampens the paths of sympathy. The islands have been the site of several documented workings that produced effects different from those the practitioner intended, and the Compact's accounts of these workings are among the most cited texts in the study of the [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) of the Art.

## Natural Resonances

The behaviour of the oceans and the skies exhibits patterns that the traditions regard as natural resonances, distinct from the workings of practitioners but governed by the same principles. The [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) responds to the moon in a pattern that the chroniclers of the Sylvan Court have likened to the response of water in a bowl, and the practitioners of the Velden Compact have charted this response with a precision that has made the crossing of the Auric safer than it was in the centuries before the Compact's studies. The response of the ocean to the moon is not a working of the Art; it is a property of the order of the world, and the practitioner who reads it is reading the same patterns that the Art employs.

The [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea), the shallow body of water that separates [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) from [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), exhibits weather patterns that the traditions have studied for as long as the Art has been practised. The storms of the Stormfall are not random; they follow a pattern that repeats at every scale, from the ripples on the surface of a puddle to the gales that sweep across the whole of the sea. The practitioners of the Velden Compact have developed methods of reading the weather of the Stormfall that are among the most accurate of any tradition, and the navigators who cross the sea rely upon these readings for the safety of their vessels.

The Northern Lights, visible in the high latitudes of Morvind, are a phenomenon that has resisted explanation by every tradition that has studied it. The lights shift and flow across the sky in patterns that resemble the reading of a working, but no practitioner has been able to find a subject that corresponds to the patterns. The dwarven practitioners of the mountain holds have suggested that the lights are a resonance of the world itself, a pattern without a subject that the practitioner may read but never reshape. This suggestion has not been accepted by any of the major traditions, but no alternative explanation has been proposed that accounts for the observed behaviour of the lights.

## Phenomena and the Founding of Traditions

The phenomena of the world have shaped the development of the traditions as surely as the four laws have. The Sylvan Court arose in the forests of Aethelar because the resonance of the woodland was the pattern to which the earliest practitioners attuned. The Court's discipline of patient observation and slow reshaping reflects the character of the forest itself, where the patterns change over seasons and years rather than over moments and days. The Court would not have developed in the form it has if its practitioners had attuned to the sea or the desert.

The Velden Compact arose on the coasts of the Velden Strait because the patterns of the sea and the weather were the ones that the mariners needed to read. The Compact's discipline of reading patterns in constant motion reflects the character of the sea, which does not hold still for the practitioner. The Compact would not have developed its methods of reading currents and winds if its founders had been forest-dwellers or city-dwellers.

The Khalisar Council arose in the inland cities of Sahrana because the patterns of trade and settlement were the ones that the urban populations needed to understand. The Council's practical approach to the Art reflects the character of the city, where the patterns are made by people and may be reshaped by people. The Council would not have developed its emphasis on dispute resolution and the improvement of infrastructure if its founders had attuned to the forest or the sea.

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) arose in the far south of Sahrana, beyond the Kharesh, in a region where the resonance of the mountains and the inland seas produced phenomena that the northern traditions had not recorded. The academies' emphasis on theory and documentation reflects the isolation of the Southern Reach, where practitioners had to develop their own understanding of the Art without the guidance of the older traditions.

## Unexplained Phenomena

The traditions have recorded several phenomena that have no clear explanation within the framework of the four laws. The most notable of these is the behaviour of certain sites where the resonance does not follow the patterns that the practitioner expects. The chroniclers of the Sylvan Court have recorded several places within the Aethelar Heartwood where a working performed in the morning produces a different effect from the same working performed in the evening, with no change in the pattern of the subject or the condition of the practitioner. The Khalisar Council has recorded similar phenomena in the deep desert, where a working that succeeds on one day fails on the next for no apparent reason.

The Velden Compact has recorded a phenomenon that its practitioners call the shifting floor, observed in certain stretches of the Stormfall Sea where the resonance appears to change direction at irregular intervals. A practitioner who has attuned to the pattern of the water may find that the attunement is no longer valid after a period of time, as though the water itself has changed its sympathy. The Compact has not been able to predict when the shifting will occur, and its accounts of the phenomenon are among the most puzzling in the literature of the Art.

The [Letters on Pattern](/content/magic/relic/letters-on-pattern) contain a passage that suggests that the unknown author was aware of phenomena that resist explanation and that the Letters treat these phenomena as evidence of the limits of the Art. The passage has been the subject of extensive debate among the traditions, with some scholars arguing that the author meant that the Art has not yet developed the tools to read these phenomena and others arguing that the author meant that the phenomena are beyond the reach of the Art entirely.

## Phenomena and Relics

The relationship between phenomena and relics is close. A relic is an object whose pattern has been so deeply reshaped, or has so absorbed the resonance of repeated workings, that it has become a working in itself. The most notable relics — the [Known-World Codex](/content/magic/relic/codex-of-the-known-world), the [Letters on Pattern](/content/magic/relic/letters-on-pattern), the [Eldreth Library](/content/magic/relic/library-of-eldreth), and the [Star-Seeker](/content/magic/relic/star-seeker) — are all objects that have been shaped by the attention of practitioners over extended periods.

The Star-Seeker is of particular interest to the study of phenomena, for the vessel was built and sailed by [Kaelen Navigator](/content/people/historical/kaelen-navigator) in the first recorded crossing of the Auric Ocean. The vessel bears the symbols of the constellations upon its hull, and the practitioners of the Velden Compact have suggested that the painting of these symbols was itself a working of the Art, fixing the vessel's pattern in sympathy with the stars. The suggestion is consistent with the observed behaviour of the vessel in the centuries since its construction: the Star-Seeker responds to the movement of the stars in a manner that other vessels of similar age and construction do not.

The Known-World Codex, compiled by [Vaelen Eldreth](/content/people/historical/vaelen-eldreth), is a relic of a different kind. The Codex is not an object that has been reshaped by the Art but a text that has absorbed the attention of readers for many centuries. The practitioners of the Sylvan Court have observed that the Codex exhibits a resonance that other books do not, as though the accumulated attention of its readers has fixed the pattern of the text in a manner that approaches the condition of a working. The observation has been disputed by the academies of the Southern Reach, which hold that the resonance of the Codex is a property of the text's content rather than of the accumulated attention of its readers.

The study of phenomena is one of the principal subjects of the [Magic Overview](/content/magic/magic-overview), and the traditions continue to investigate the phenomena of the world with the same discipline that they apply to the practice of the Art itself.
