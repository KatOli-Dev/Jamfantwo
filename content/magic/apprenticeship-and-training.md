## The Making of a Practitioner

Magic is not learned from books alone, nor can it be acquired through solitary experiment. The discipline of reading and reshaping pattern is transmitted from master to apprentice through years of guided practice, and the traditions of the known world have developed distinct methods of instruction that reflect the patterns to which each tradition is attuned. The training of a practitioner is the foundation upon which every working of magic is built, and the traditions agree that no practitioner should be entrusted with a working of any consequence before the discipline has been thoroughly learned.

This page describes the apprenticeship and training of practitioners across the principal traditions, beginning with the common elements that every tradition shares, continuing with the distinctive methods of each tradition, and concluding with the transition from apprentice to licensed practitioner.

## The Selection of Apprentices

The traditions do not accept every applicant for apprenticeship. The masters of each tradition observe applicants over a period of months before accepting any into the first year of instruction, and applicants who show signs of impatience or carelessness are turned away. The selection is conducted with care because magic exacts a [cost](/content/magic/law/cost) that the practitioner must bear, and a practitioner who undertakes the discipline without the necessary patience will be harmed by the practice rather than improved by it.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) selects its apprentices from among the children of the forest villages, observing them in the ordinary activities of village life before approaching those who show the stillness of attention the Court requires. The Court also accepts applicants from the cities, but these applicants must spend a full year living in the forest before the Court will consider them, and many find the isolation more than they can bear.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) selects its apprentices from among those who have lived in or near the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) for at least three years, for the Fellowship holds that attunement to the Heartwood cannot be taught to one who has not already begun to feel the resonance of the place. The Fellowship does not accept applicants from outside the Heartwood region and has maintained this policy for as long as its records extend.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) selects its apprentices from the cities and towns of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) interior, observing them in the marketplaces and the streets before approaching those who show the practical intelligence the Council requires. The Council also accepts applicants from the villages and the forest margins, but these applicants must spend a year in the city before the Council will consider them.

The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) selects its apprentices from among the maritime communities of the coasts and the islands, observing them in the harbours and aboard the vessels before approaching those who show the adaptability the Compact requires. The Compact is the most flexible of the traditions in its selection and has accepted apprentices from as far inland as the desert cities of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana).

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) select their students from among the literate population of the southern regions, requiring a demonstrated aptitude for systematic study before admission. The academies are the most formal of the traditions in their selection and maintain a written examination that applicants must pass before they are admitted to the first year of instruction.

## The First Years: Reading and Stillness

The first years of apprenticeship are devoted to the discipline of reading, taught in every tradition through exercises that appear simple to the uninitiated. The apprentice learns to attend to a subject with a stillness of body and mind that is the foundation of all later work. The apprentice who cannot read a stone with patience cannot hope to read a living creature, and the apprentice who cannot read a living creature cannot hope to read a person. The progression from simple to complex patterns is the structure upon which every tradition builds its curriculum.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) teaches reading through exercises in the forest. The apprentice is given a single tree and required to sit with it for a full day, attending to the pattern of the tree in all its aspects. The apprentice returns to the same tree for a full season, and the master tests the apprentice's reading by asking questions that only a careful observer could answer. The apprentice who passes the test of the single tree proceeds to the reading of a grove and then to the reading of the forest as a whole.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) teaches reading through exercises in the deep forest. The apprentice is required to sit in a single place in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) for three days without moving, letting the pattern of the place enter the awareness gradually. The master visits the apprentice at intervals to test the depth of the reading, and the apprentice who passes the test of the single place proceeds to the reading of a larger territory.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) teaches reading through exercises in the marketplaces and the streets. The apprentice is required to observe the movement of people through a busy square, attending to the patterns of the crowd without focusing on any individual. The master tests the apprentice's reading by asking questions about the flow of the crowd and the points at which the flow is interrupted.

The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) teaches reading through exercises on the water. The apprentice is required to attend to the movement of the sea in a single place, reading the pattern of the waves and the currents without distraction. The master tests the apprentice's reading by asking questions about the direction of the current and the behaviour of the creatures within the water.

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) teach reading through exercises in the library and the laboratory. The apprentice is given a pattern of dead matter and required to describe it in terms that the academies have standardised, producing a written account that the master may examine and correct.

## The Middle Years: Attunement and Vocabulary

The second phase of apprenticeship is devoted to attunement, the process by which the practitioner brings the pattern into sympathy with the subject. Attunement is taught through exercises that vary by tradition, and the apprentice who has learned to read a pattern must now learn to reach it through the paths of [Source](/content/magic/law/source).

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) teaches attunement through exercises in which the apprentice walks the forest until the pattern of the trees, the soil, and the creatures within it are felt as a single configuration. The apprentice learns to follow the paths of sympathy that connect one pattern to another, and the master tests the apprentice's attunement by observing which patterns the apprentice can reach without effort.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) teaches attunement through exercises in the deep forest. The apprentice learns to let the pattern of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) enter the awareness without forcing the attention, and the master tests the apprentice's attunement by observing the duration of the attunement before the apprentice tires.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) teaches attunement through exercises in the built environment. The apprentice learns to read the pattern of a building or a street and to bring the pattern into sympathy through the discipline of attention, and the master tests the apprentice's attunement by observing the apprentice's ability to read the pattern of a place that is unfamiliar.

The apprentice also learns the vocabulary of magic in the language of the tradition during this phase. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) teaches the terms of resonance and pattern; the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) teaches the older vocabulary of sympathy and attunement; the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) teaches the practical terminology of the working and the cost. The apprentice learns to speak of magic with precision, for imprecision in speech reflects imprecision in understanding, and imprecision in understanding leads to harm.

## The Final Years: Reshaping under Supervision

The third phase of apprenticeship is devoted to reshaping, the act of the working itself. Reshaping is not taught until the master is satisfied that the apprentice has learned to read and to attune with sufficient reliability, for an attempt to reshape without proper reading and attunement is a common cause of harm to the practitioner.

The apprentice begins with the reshaping of simple patterns of dead matter. A stone may be reshaped to a more useful shape, a piece of wood may be reshaped to a smoother surface, and a quantity of water may be reshaped to a clearer state. The master supervises each working closely, observing the apprentice's reading, attunement, and the working itself, and correcting any error before harm is done.

The apprentice proceeds to the reshaping of more complex patterns as the master judges the readiness. Living matter is not reshaped until the apprentice has demonstrated reliability with dead matter, and persons are not reshaped at all during apprenticeship. The reshaping of persons is reserved for the licensed practitioner who has undergone additional training beyond the apprenticeship.

The apprenticeship concludes with a working performed under the supervision of the master. The working is typically modest in scope, and the apprentice who performs it successfully is recognised as a licensed practitioner of the tradition. The recognition is recorded in the registers of the tradition, and the practitioner may thereafter undertake workings independently, subject to the oversight of the governing body.

## Variations across Traditions

The duration of apprenticeship varies across the traditions. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) requires a minimum of ten years before a student may attempt a first working; the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) sets the minimum at eight years; the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) requires that the apprentice spend at least three years in the forest before any instruction in reshaping begins. The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) requires a minimum of seven years, but the Compact allows the apprentice to begin the reshaping of simple patterns of wood and rope after only three years, provided the master is satisfied with the apprentice's reading.

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) require a minimum of twelve years of study before a student may attempt a first working, the longest of any tradition. The academies hold that the theoretical foundation of magic must be thoroughly understood before any practical work is attempted, and their graduates are among the most knowledgeable of any tradition in the theory of magic.

The dwarven approaches to magic follow a different pattern of training, reflecting the artisanal traditions of the mountain holds. A dwarven practitioner does not undergo a formal apprenticeship in magic as the human and elven traditions understand it but instead learns magic as an extension of a craft, whether smithing, stoneworking, or the working of metals. The training is less systematic and more practical, and the dwarven practitioner emerges from training not as a scholar of magic but as a craftsperson who happens to practise magic as part of the craft.

## The Transition to Licensed Practitioner

The transition from apprentice to licensed practitioner is marked by a formal ceremony in most traditions. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds the ceremony in the forest where the apprentice was trained, with the master and the elders of the Court in attendance. The apprentice performs a working of moderate complexity before the assembly, and the master declares the apprentice ready for independent practice.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) holds the ceremony in the hall of the Council, with the master and the councillors in attendance. The apprentice presents a written account of the training and the working performed, and the councillors question the apprentice on the theory and practice of magic.

The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) holds the ceremony aboard a vessel at sea, with the master and the captain in attendance. The apprentice performs a working that improves the vessel or the condition of the crew, and the master declares the apprentice ready for independent practice.

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) hold the ceremony in the great hall of the academy, with the faculty and the senior students in attendance. The apprentice defends a written thesis on a subject of magic, and the faculty votes on whether to admit the apprentice to the register of licensed practitioners.

The licensed practitioner is thereafter bound by the ethical constraints of magic and subject to the oversight of the tradition's governing body. The practitioner who violates the ethical constraints may be removed from the register and prohibited from practising, a sanction that the traditions enforce with consistency across all the known world.
