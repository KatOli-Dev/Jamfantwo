## The Relation between Making and Reshaping

The Art bears a name that suggests a connection to the making of beautiful things, and the traditions have long recognised that the practice of the Art is related to the practice of craft. The practitioner who reshapes a pattern is, in a sense, a maker, and the skills that the craftspeople of the known world develop through years of practice in their trades are related to the skills that the practitioner develops through the discipline of the Art.

This page describes the relationship between artistic craft and magical practice as it is understood across the traditions, beginning with the nature of craft in the Art, continuing with the traditions that have developed the closest connections between craft and the Art, and concluding with the disputes that remain about the nature of the relationship.

## The Craft of Reshaping

The reshaping of a pattern is an act of making in the same sense that the carving of a stone or the weaving of a cloth is an act of making. The practitioner who reshapes a pattern does not create a pattern from nothing but rather guides the pattern into a new configuration, and the act of guiding requires the same attention to the material, the same understanding of the possibilities and the limits of the medium, that the craftsperson brings to the work.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) has long recognised the connection between craft and the Art, and the Court's practitioners are trained to observe the methods of the craftspeople of the forest. The weaver who understands the pattern of the cloth may read the pattern more readily than one who does not, and the carver who understands the grain of the wood may reshape the wood more effectively than one who does not. The Court holds that the practitioner who is also a craftsperson is more skilled in the Art than one who is not.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has developed the most systematic account of the relationship between craft and the Art. The Council holds that the craft of reshaping is the same as the craft of making, differing only in the medium upon which the practitioner works. The craftsperson who works in wood or stone or metal is reshaping the pattern of the material through physical means, while the practitioner of the Art reshapes the pattern through the path of [resonance](/content/magic/law/source). The two methods are different in their operation but the same in their intention: both seek to bring the material into a configuration that is more useful or more beautiful.

## The Dwarven Approaches

The dwarven peoples of the mountain holds have developed the closest connection between craft and the Art of any tradition. The dwarven approach to the Art is not a separate discipline but an extension of the craft traditions that have shaped dwarven culture across the centuries. A dwarven practitioner does not undergo a formal apprenticeship in the Art as the human and elven traditions understand it but instead learns the Art as an extension of a craft, whether smithing, stoneworking, or the working of metals.

The dwarven smith who forges a blade does so with an attention to the pattern of the metal that a practitioner of the Art would recognise as reading. The smith reads the grain of the metal, the temperature of the forge, and the response of the blade to the hammer, and the smith who has practised the craft for many years reads these patterns without conscious effort. The reshaping of the metal through the Art is a deepening of the reshaping that the smith performs through physical means, and the dwarven smith who practises the Art produces blades that are more enduring and more effective than any that could be produced through physical means alone.

The dwarven stoneworker who carves a hall in the mountain does so with an attention to the pattern of the stone that is the foundation of the Art. The stoneworker reads the grain of the rock, the stresses that the excavation will produce, and the response of the stone to the chisel. The reshaping of the stone through the Art is a deepening of the reshaping that the stoneworker performs through physical means, and the dwarven stoneworker who practises the Art produces halls that are more enduring and more beautiful than any that could be produced through physical means alone.

The dwarven approach to the Art is less systematic than the approach of the surface traditions, but it is also more integrated into the daily life of the dwarven communities. The dwarven practitioner does not set aside time for the practice of the Art but practises the Art as part of the craft, and the Art is as natural to the dwarven craftsperson as the hammer and the chisel.

## The Craft of Relic-Making

The making of relics is the point at which craft and the Art are most closely joined, for a relic is an object whose pattern has been shaped by the Art and that endures as a working in itself. The making of a relic requires the skills of the craftsperson and the skills of the practitioner together, and the traditions that have produced the most enduring relics are those that have developed the closest connections between craft and the Art.

The [Known-World Codex](/content/magic/relic/codex-of-the-known-world) is a relic that was made through the craft of the scribe and the attention of the scholar. [Vaelen Eldreth](/content/people/historical/vaelen-eldreth) was not a practitioner of the Art in the strict sense, but the Codex that he compiled has become a relic through the accumulated attention of its readers over many centuries. The Codex is a work of craft in the sense that the writing and the illumination of the text required the skills of the scribe, and it is a relic in the sense that the pattern of the text has been fixed by the resonance of the readings it has received.

The [Star-Seeker](/content/magic/relic/star-seeker) is a relic that was made through the craft of the shipwright and the discipline of [Kaelen Navigator](/content/people/historical/kaelen-navigator). The vessel was built in the boatyard of Eldreth with the same skills that the shipwrights of the coast had developed over generations, and the vessel was painted with the symbols of the constellations that Kaelen had studied. The painting of the symbols was itself a working of the Art, fixing the vessel's pattern in sympathy with the stars, and the vessel has endured as a relic because the craft of the shipwright and the working of the practitioner were joined in the making.

## The Craft of Reading

The reading of a pattern is itself a craft, requiring the same attention to the subject that the craftsperson brings to the material. The practitioner who reads a pattern must attend to the configuration of the subject in all of its aspects, and the discipline of attention is the same discipline that the craftsperson develops through years of practice.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) teaches the reading of patterns through exercises that are derived from the crafts of the forest. The apprentice who learns to read the pattern of a tree is taught to attend to the tree in the same way that the woodcarver attends to the grain of the wood, and the apprentice who learns to read the pattern of a stream is taught to attend to the stream in the same way that the fisher attends to the movement of the water.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) teaches the reading of patterns through exercises that are derived from the crafts of the city. The apprentice who learns to read the pattern of a marketplace is taught to attend to the movement of the crowd in the same way that the merchant attends to the flow of goods, and the apprentice who learns to read the pattern of a building is taught to attend to the structure in the same way that the builder attends to the joining of the timbers.

## The Craft of Teaching

The teaching of the Art is itself a craft, requiring the same attention to the apprentice that the craftsperson brings to the material. The master who teaches the Art must read the pattern of the apprentice, attune to the apprentice through the path of resonance, and guide the apprentice into the configuration of understanding through the same discipline that the practitioner brings to the reshaping of a pattern.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) has developed the most systematic account of the craft of teaching, holding that the master who teaches the Art is engaged in a form of reshaping that is as demanding as any working upon the medium. The master who guides the apprentice through the years of training is reshaping the apprentice's pattern of understanding, and the cost of this reshaping is borne by the master as surely as the cost of any working upon a person.

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) have developed methods of teaching that are more systematic than those of any other tradition, and the academies' accounts of the craft of teaching are among the most cited texts in the study of the Art. The academies hold that the teaching of the Art is a discipline that must be studied as carefully as the practising of the Art, and the academies require that their masters undergo training in the craft of teaching before they are entrusted with the instruction of apprentices.

## The Disputes

The traditions do not agree on the nature of the relationship between craft and the Art. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) holds that the Art is a form of craft and that the practitioner is a craftsperson who reshapes pattern through the path of resonance. The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) hold that the Art is a discipline of a different order from craft and that the practitioner who reshapes pattern is engaged in an act that is fundamentally different from the making of the craftsperson.

The dispute is not merely verbal, for the position one takes affects the way one practises the Art. The practitioner who treats the Art as a form of craft will approach the working with the same attention to material and method that the craftsperson brings to the workshop, while the practitioner who treats the Art as a discipline of a different order will approach the working with a different attention entirely.

The dispute has not been resolved, and the traditions continue to practise the Art according to their own understanding of the relationship between craft and the Art. The dwarven practitioners of the mountain holds, who have developed the closest connection between craft and the Art, do not participate in the dispute, holding that the question is one that can be answered only through practice and that the practitioner who practises the Art with attention and discipline will find the answer in the working itself.
