## The Foundation of Every Working

Attunement is the process by which a practitioner brings their own pattern into sympathy with the pattern of a subject. It is the foundation of every working of the Art, and the traditions teach attunement as the second discipline after reading. A practitioner who has not attuned to the subject will accomplish nothing, and an attempt to reshape without attunement is a common cause of harm to the practitioner. The discipline of attunement is the means by which the practitioner walks the paths of [Source](/content/magic/law/source) that connect one pattern to another, and the depth of attunement determines the extent of what the practitioner may accomplish.

This page describes the practice of attunement as it is taught and performed across the traditions, beginning with the nature of the act itself, continuing with the methods by which attunement is achieved and deepened, and concluding with the limits that attunement observes and the risks that accompany it.

## The Nature of Attunement

Attunement is not a meditation or a trance, though the uninitiated sometimes describe it as such. It is a disciplined act of will by which the practitioner aligns the configuration of the pattern to the configuration of the subject. The alignment is not a merging or a loss of identity; the practitioner does not become the subject but rather reaches a state in which the two patterns vibrate together, in the manner of two strings tuned to the same pitch. The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) prefers the older term of sympathy, speaking of the practitioner as one who comes to feel the pattern of the subject as the subject feels it. The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) speaks of attunement as a tuning of the inner ear to the music of the subject's configuration.

The attunement is not permanent. A practitioner who has attuned to a subject must maintain the attunement through continued attention, and the attunement fades when the practitioner's attention is withdrawn. The duration of attunement depends upon the depth of the practice and the complexity of the subject: a practitioner of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) may maintain attunement to a stretch of water for several hours, while a practitioner of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) who has lived in the same forest for decades may maintain attunement to the forest for days.

The attunement is also specific. A practitioner who has attuned to one subject cannot assume that the attunement extends to another, even if the two subjects appear similar. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has recorded several cases in which a practitioner who had attuned to a particular building attempted to read a second building of similar construction and found that the attunement did not carry over. The Council holds that each pattern must be attuned separately, and the principle is accepted by every tradition.

## The Methods of Attunement

The traditions have developed distinct methods of attunement, each shaped by the patterns to which the tradition is most commonly attuned. The methods differ in the detail but share the same underlying structure: the practitioner brings the attention to rest upon the subject, the practitioner reads the pattern of the subject through the discipline of reading, and the practitioner wills the pattern into alignment with the subject's configuration.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) teaches a method called walking the grove, in which the practitioner moves slowly through the forest while attending to the patterns of the trees, the soil, and the creatures within it. The movement is essential to the method, for the Court holds that the pattern of a forest is not static and that the practitioner who does not move with the forest will not attune to it. The practitioner walks the grove for a full day, and the attunement is achieved when the practitioner can feel the pattern of the forest without conscious effort.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) teaches a method called sitting the root, in which the practitioner sits in a single place in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) and lets the pattern of the place enter the awareness gradually. The practitioner does not force the attention but rather waits for the pattern to reveal itself, and the attunement is achieved when the practitioner can feel the pattern of the Heartwood as a presence in the awareness.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) teaches a method called reading the flow, in which the practitioner attends to the movement of people or goods through a space and reads the pattern of the movement as a whole. The practitioner stands in a fixed place and lets the movement pass through the field of attention, and the attunement is achieved when the practitioner can feel the pattern of the flow without following any particular part of it.

The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) teaches a method called feeling the tide, in which the practitioner attends to the movement of water and lets the pattern of the current enter the body. The practitioner stands in the water or upon a vessel that moves with it, and the attunement is achieved when the practitioner can feel the pattern of the water in the limbs and the breath.

The academies of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) teach a method called systematic attunement, in which the practitioner proceeds through a series of steps that the academies have standardised. The practitioner first reads the subject through the discipline of reading, then adjusts the attention through a series of progressive focuses, then wills the alignment through an act of concentration. The method is slower than the methods of the other traditions but is more reliable, and the academies hold that a practitioner who follows the systematic method will achieve attunement even with subjects the practitioner has never encountered before.

## Deepening Attunement

Attunement may be deepened through continued practice, and the traditions have developed methods by which the practitioner may increase the depth of attunement over time. A deeper attunement allows the practitioner to accomplish more in a working and reduces the [cost](/content/magic/law/cost) that the working exacts.

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) deepens attunement through repeated practice in the same place. A practitioner who walks the same grove day after day will find that the attunement comes more quickly and that the depth of the attunement increases with each session. The Court holds that the practitioner who has attuned to a single grove for a full year has achieved a depth that the practitioner who attunes to a different grove each day cannot match.

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) deepens attunement through prolonged stillness. A practitioner who sits in the same place for many days will find that the attunement deepens as the practitioner's resistance to the pattern diminishes. The Fellowship holds that the deepest attunement is achieved when the practitioner no longer distinguishes the pattern from the subject, a state that the Fellowship calls the opening of the root.

The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) deepens attunement through varied practice. A practitioner who reads the flow of many different marketplaces will find that the attunement becomes more flexible and that the practitioner can attune to a new marketplace more quickly with each repetition. The Council holds that the deepest attunement is achieved when the practitioner can read the pattern of any flow without preparation.

The [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) deepens attunement through practice in varied conditions. A practitioner who feels the tide in calm water and in rough water, in the morning and in the evening, will find that the attunement becomes more robust and that the practitioner can maintain the attunement through changing conditions. The Compact holds that the deepest attunement is achieved when the practitioner can feel the pattern of the water even when the body is occupied with other tasks.

## The Limits of Attunement

Attunement is bounded by the same [limits](/content/magic/law/limit) that govern the Art as a whole. A practitioner may only attune to a subject through a path of sympathy, and the path exists only between patterns that are alike. The closer the pattern of the practitioner is to the pattern of the subject, the more direct the path and the more readily the practitioner may attune. A practitioner who has attuned to the forest cannot readily attune to the sea, and a practitioner who has attuned to the city cannot readily attune to the deep places of the earth.

The limit of sympathy is the reason that practitioners specialise by tradition. A practitioner of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) who must attune to a subject outside the forest undergoes a period of reattunement, a process that may require weeks or months depending on the distance between the practitioner's accustomed patterns and the patterns of the new subject. The traditions do not encourage cross-attunement, for the discipline of attunement is built upon the patterns the practitioner has learned to read, and a practitioner who attempts to attune to a pattern too far from the accustomed patterns risks confusion and error.

## The Risks of Attunement

Attunement carries risks that the practitioner must understand before attempting the practice. The principal risk is that the practitioner may attune too deeply and lose the distinction between the pattern and the subject. The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) has recorded several cases in which a practitioner who attuned too deeply to the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) began to exhibit the behaviour of the forest, moving slowly and speaking in rhythms that matched the growth of the trees. The practitioners affected were restored through the discipline of separation, but the cases are cited by the traditions as evidence of the danger of deep attunement.

The second risk is that the practitioner may attune to a subject that is harmful. A practitioner who attunes to a pattern that carries traces of harmful reshaping may find that the traces enter the practitioner's pattern through the path of attunement. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) has published several accounts of practitioners who attuned to objects that had been reshaped by another practitioner of questionable skill and were harmed by the traces. The Council recommends that a practitioner always read the pattern of a subject thoroughly before attuning, for the traces of previous reshapings may be read before the attunement is attempted.

## Attunement in Daily Practice

The practitioner who practises the Art regularly maintains a state of partial attunement to the patterns most commonly encountered. A practitioner of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) who lives in the forest is always partly attuned to the forest, and the attunement deepens with each day of practice. A practitioner of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) who sails regularly is always partly attuned to the water, and the attunement shifts with the tides.

The partial attunement is the foundation of the practitioner's skill, allowing the practitioner to read and reshape with less effort than one who must achieve full attunement before each working. The partial attunement is also the source of the [Cost](/content/magic/law/cost) that accumulates over a career, for the practitioner who maintains a partial attunement to many patterns over many years will find that the patterns leave traces that are not easily shed.

The practice of attunement is therefore a discipline that the practitioner must maintain throughout a career. The practitioner who neglects attunement will find the Art increasingly difficult, and the practitioner who maintains attunement with care will find that the Art becomes more reliable with each passing year.
