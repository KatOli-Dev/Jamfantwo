## Overview

Saltreach is a western fishing settlement of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), situated at the southern edge of the cliff coast where the land begins to slope toward the lowland plains of the south. The town is the smallest of the major settlements of the [Grey Coast Holdings](/content/grey-coast-holdings), and its harbour is a narrow cove tucked between two headlands that provide shelter from the worst of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/greyring-ocean) swell. Saltreach is the last port of call on the western coast before the cliffs give way to the open beaches of the south, and its fishermen work some of the most dangerous waters on the coast, for the transition from cliff to beach is marked by reefs and shoals that have wrecked many vessels.

## The Harbour and the Fishing

The harbour of Saltreach is a small, deep cove that is accessible only through a narrow entrance between the headlands, and the passage requires careful navigation, for the currents that run through the entrance are strong and the rocks are close on either side. The harbour is sufficient for the fishing boats of the town, but it is too small for the larger vessels that call at [Greycliff](/content/greycliff) or [Misthaven](/content/misthaven), and the trade of Saltreach is limited to the export of its catch and the import of the few goods that the town cannot produce for itself.

The fishing grounds off Saltreach are among the most productive and the most dangerous on the western coast, for the reefs and shoals that mark the transition from cliff to beach are rich in fish but treacherous to navigate. The fishermen of Saltreach are skilled in the reading of the water and the location of the fish, and their boats are small and manoeuvrable, designed for the conditions of the reef rather than the open ocean. The catch is landed at the harbour, salted and dried in the salting houses that line the cove, and exported overland to the inland markets, where the fish of Saltreach is valued for its quality and its freshness.

The seasonal patterns of the fishing grounds are well understood by the fishermen of Saltreach, for the movement of the fish through the reef is governed by the currents and the temperature of the water, and the most productive grounds shift from season to season. The spring run of the cod is the most valuable catch of the year, and the salting houses work through the nights during the spring season to process the volume of fish that is landed. The autumn catch is dominated by the mackerel that pass through the reef on their migration southward, and the dried mackerel of Saltreach is a valued commodity in the inland markets during the winter months.

## The Town

Saltreach is a small, rugged town, and its buildings are constructed of the grey stone of the coast in the manner of the other western settlements. The town is clustered around the harbour, and the streets are steep and narrow, climbing the slope from the cove to the cliff top, where a lighthouse marks the entrance to the harbour and the position of the town for vessels approaching from the south. The population of Saltreach is small and entirely [human](/content/humans), and the people are known for their hardihood and their skill in the difficult waters they work.

The salt for which the town is named is gathered from the shallow pools that form on the cliff tops above the cove, where the spray of the ocean evaporates in the rare sunshine and leaves a deposit of salt that is collected and used in the preservation of the catch. The salt of Saltreach is a minor export, but it is valued for its quality and its whiteness, and the salt-gathering is an important secondary activity of the town.

## History and the Coast

The settlement of Saltreach is old, though its founding is not recorded in any chronicle, for the town was never large enough to attract the attention of the historians of the [Grey Coast Holdings](/content/grey-coast-holdings). The earliest settlers were fishermen who established a seasonal camp at the cove, drawn by the richness of the reef and the shelter that the headlands provided. The permanent settlement grew from these seasonal beginnings, and the salting houses and the lighthouse were the first substantial buildings to be constructed. The town has remained small throughout its history, for the harbour is too narrow to support a larger population, and the prosperity of Saltreach has always been modest compared to that of [Greycliff](/content/greycliff) or [Misthaven](/content/misthaven).

The relationship between Saltreach and the other settlements of the Grey Coast Holdings is one of mutual dependence, for the catch of Saltreach is a valued supplement to the fish and the whale products of the larger ports, and the goods that Saltreach cannot produce for itself are imported from the other settlements. The town is a quiet member of the Holdings, and its people are content with their modest place in the economy of the western coast.

## The Lighthouse and the Cliffs

The lighthouse of Saltreach stands on the cliff top above the harbour, and it is the most prominent structure in the town. The lighthouse is a tower of grey stone that is visible for many miles along the coast, and its beacon is lit every night to guide the fishing boats home and to warn the vessels that pass along the coast of the reefs and shoals that lie below the cliffs. The lighthouse is maintained by the town, and the keeper is a respected figure in the community, for the safety of the fishermen and the passing vessels depends upon the reliability of the beacon.

The cliffs that surround Saltreach are among the highest on the western coast, and the grey stone of the cliffs is the same stone that is used in the construction of the buildings of the town. The cliff tops are windswept and barren, but the salt pools that form in the hollows of the rock are the source of the salt that gives the town its name, and the gathering of the salt is a daily activity during the dry season. The cliff path that runs northward along the coast is the only overland route that connects Saltreach to the other settlements of the Grey Coast Holdings, and the path is narrow and treacherous, passable only on foot or by the pack animals that carry the salt and the dried fish to the inland markets.

## Culture and the Sea

The culture of Saltreach is shaped by the sea, for the town is oriented entirely toward the waters that sustain it, and the rhythms of the community are the rhythms of the fishing and the tides. The people of Saltreach are known for their hardihood and their skill, and the community is bound together by the shared dangers of the reef and the shared labour of the fishing. The festivals of the town are modest affairs, for the population is small and the resources are limited, but the annual feast of the first catch is a cherished tradition that marks the beginning of the spring season and the return of the cod to the reef.

The songs and the stories of Saltreach are the songs and the stories of the sea, and the fishermen of the town have a rich tradition of ballads and tales that are passed down from generation to generation. The stories tell of the great storms that have struck the coast, the wrecks that have been found on the reef, and the skill of the pilots who have guided the fishing boats through the narrow entrance to the harbour in the worst of the weather. The tradition of storytelling is a valued part of the cultural life of the town, and the evenings in the tavern of Saltreach are spent in the sharing of the old tales and the singing of the old songs.

## Significance

Saltreach is the southernmost of the settlements of the [Grey Coast Holdings](/content/grey-coast-holdings), and its harbour is the last refuge on the western coast before the open beaches of the south. The town is small and its trade is modest, but its fishermen are among the most skilled on the coast, and the catch that is landed at Saltreach is a valued supplement to the fish and the whale products of the larger ports. The town is a quiet and self-sufficient community, oriented toward the sea and the difficult waters that sustain it, and its people are proud of their independence and their ability to thrive in one of the most demanding environments on the coast. The reputation of Saltreach for the quality of its catch and the skill of its fishermen is a source of pride for the community, and the town is a valued member of the [Grey Coast Holdings](/content/grey-coast-holdings), contributing its fish and its salt to the economy of the western coast and maintaining the lighthouse that guides the vessels that pass along the shore.
