## Origins

The origins of the centaurs are a subject of considerable interest to naturalists, for the centaur body is unlike that of any other sapient people, combining as it does the upper torso of a humanoid form with the lower body and legs of a large quadruped. Some scholars have proposed that centaurs represent a truly ancient lineage, distinct from all other peoples and shaped by conditions that no longer exist in the known world. Others have speculated that centaurs share a distant kinship with [satyrs](/content/satyrs), citing the partly animal form of both peoples and their shared preference for the woodland fringes and grasslands. The centaurs themselves preserve rich oral traditions that speak of their creation by the spirits of the plains, but they offer these traditions to outsiders only rarely and with great ceremony.

What is certain is that centaurs have inhabited the central grasslands of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar) for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands is the oldest and most enduring feature of the region's history. The grasslands bear the mark of centaur culture in their ancient trackways, their ring-shaped encampments, and the careful management of grazing land that has sustained both the centaurs and the herds of wild horses with which they share the plains.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Centaurs are a large and striking people, and their form is immediately recognisable. From the waist up, they present a humanoid torso with a head, shoulders, and arms, broadly similar in proportion to a [human](/content/humans) of substantial build. From the waist down, the body continues as the trunk and four legs of a large equine, muscular and built for speed and endurance. The junction between the two halves is seamless, the musculature of the humanoid lower back flowing into the equine shoulders in a manner that admits no comparison with any other creature. The whole form is large, for a centaur stands taller than a [troll](/content/trolls) when upright, and the weight of the body is considerable.

The centaur body is built for movement, and the speed and endurance of a centaur on open ground are unmatched by any other sapient people. A centaur can sustain a canter for many hours and cover distances in a day that would take a human on foot the better part of a week. Their legs are powerful and their hooves are hard, and they are sure-footed on grass and firm ground, though they are less confident on rocky or steep terrain. The humanoid arms are strong and capable, used for the manipulation of tools, weapons, and goods, and the hands are broad and calloused from a life of practical work.

Centaur skin and coat vary with the region, ranging from dark brown to dun, bay, and grey, and the equine coat is often marked with patterns of spots or stripes that hold cultural significance among centaur kindreds. The hair of the head is coarse and long, typically dark, and it is worn in braids or bound with leather ties. Centaur eyes are large and set wide, granting a broad field of vision that suits a people whose safety depends on awareness of the open plains. Their hearing is keen, and their sense of smell is well developed, for the equine portion of their body retains the sensitivities of a prey animal.

## Life Cycle

Centaurs are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of seventy to one hundred years under favourable conditions. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late teens, though they continue to fill out and strengthen well into their twenties. Centaur women carry their young for approximately eleven months before giving birth, and births are usually single. The foal is able to stand and walk within an hour of birth, and it can keep pace with the herd within days, a necessity for a people whose way of life demands constant movement.

Centaur populations grow steadily when grazing is sufficient and the plains are at peace, and the young are raised collectively by the kindred, with all adults sharing in their care and training. The education of a centaur foal is long and thorough, encompassing riding, archery, the history of the kindred, and the knowledge of the plains, and a centaur is not considered fully adult until they have demonstrated competence in all of these. The elders of a centaur kindred are respected for their accumulated knowledge of the plains and its ways, and their counsel guides the movements and decisions of the community.

## Habitat and Distribution

Centaurs are found chiefly in the central grasslands of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), where the open plains provide the space and the grazing that their way of life requires. They are the dominant people of the grasslands, and their kindreds range across the full extent of the central plains, from the foothills of the mountain spine in the west to the river valleys of the east. The [geography](/content/geography) of the grasslands, with their gentle terrain and abundant grass, is ideally suited to the centaur form, and the centaurs have shaped the plains over centuries through their management of grazing and their maintenance of trackways.

Smaller populations of centaurs are found on the warm plains of southern Aethelar and in the grassy uplands of the Sahrani interior, where the terrain is open enough to support their way of life. Centaurs are absent from the forests, the mountains, and the coasts, for their large quadrupedal bodies are ill suited to dense vegetation, steep ground, or the confined spaces of a ship. Their distribution is thus governed by the availability of open ground, and the centaur range is the most restricted of any people relative to the breadth of climates they could tolerate.

## Diet

The centaur diet is a mixture of gathered plant foods and hunted game, reflecting the resources of the plains they inhabit. Grasses, grains, and roots form the staple, gathered from the wild and supplemented by small-scale cultivation in the more fertile portions of the grasslands. The centaurs are not farmers in the manner of humans, but they tend the plains deliberately, encouraging the growth of useful plants and managing the grazing of wild herds to maintain the health of the grassland.

Meat is provided by the hunting of the large game animals of the plains, including wild cattle, deer, and the horses with which the centaurs share the grasslands. The hunt is a central activity of centaur life, both as a source of food and as a test of skill, and centaurs are formidable mounted archers, though the term mounted hardly applies to a people who are their own mount. The centaur approach to food is practical and communal, with meals prepared and shared by the entire kindred, and the hospitality of a centaur encampment is extended freely to those who approach in peace.

## Ecology

Centaurs occupy the niche of a large-bodied nomadic herbivore and predator in the grassland ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on the plains is considerable, for their movements, their grazing, and their management of the land shape the vegetation and the wildlife of the entire region. The centaurs regard the grasslands as their charge, and their traditions of stewardship are comparable in their effect to those of the [elves](/content/elves) over their forests, though operating on a shorter timescale and over a more open terrain.

Centaurs share the grasslands with [humans](/content/humans), and the relationship between the two peoples is generally one of cautious coexistence. The settled human communities of the plains and the nomadic centaur kindreds have long maintained an arrangement of mutual benefit, trading the products of cultivation for the products of the hunt, and disputes over grazing and land are resolved through negotiation rather than conflict. [Felids](/content/felinids) share portions of the centaur range, and the two peoples maintain a wary respect, for their needs overlap but their ways of life are distinct.
