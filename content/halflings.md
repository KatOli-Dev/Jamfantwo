## Origins

The origins of the halflings are a matter of some debate among naturalists, for the halfling people present a puzzling case. They are a small folk, smaller even than [gnomes](/content/gnomes), and their bodies show no strong mark of any single environment, suggesting a generalised lineage similar in its way to that of [humans](/content/humans). Some scholars have proposed that halflings share a distant kinship with humans, citing the versatile and unspecialised form of both peoples, and the capacity of each to thrive in a range of climates. Others have argued for a connection to gnomes, pointing to the small stature and the preference for sheltered, pastoral country that both share. The halflings themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their having always dwelt in the lowland valleys, but these traditions offer no detail on the question of origins.

What is generally accepted is that halflings are an old people, well established in the lowland valleys of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar) long before the earliest human chronicles were written. Their communities are among the oldest settled populations in the temperate regions, and the patient cultivation of their valley lands has shaped the landscape over centuries in ways that are often mistaken for natural features by those who do not look closely.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Halflings are the smallest of the sapient peoples, standing no taller than half the height of an average [human](/content/humans). Their frames are compact and proportioned much like those of humans in miniature, though they tend toward a broader build and a softer musculature than their larger cousins. Their bones are light but sturdy, and their bodies are well padded, a quality that suits them poorly for combat but admirably for the endurance of long walks and the patience of quiet labour. Halflings are notably sure of foot, possessing a balance and steadiness that larger peoples often lack, and they can move through rough or narrow ground with a confidence that belies their unimposing stature.

Halfling senses are adequate and well balanced, lacking the specialisation seen in other peoples. Their vision is keen at close range, suited to the fine work of cultivation and craft, though they are short-sighted by comparison with [elves](/content/elves) or [orcs](/content/orcs). Their hearing is competent, and their sense of smell and taste are notably acute, a refinement that reflects the importance of food and hospitality in halfling culture. Halfling skin ranges from fair to lightly tanned, depending on the region, and their hair is most commonly brown or auburn, worn long and simply by both sexes. Their feet are a distinctive feature, for the soles are thick and leathery, and the tops are covered in a growth of coarse hair, and many halflings dispense with footwear entirely, preferring the direct contact with the earth that their feet allow.

## Life Cycle

Halflings are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of one hundred to one hundred and thirty years under favourable conditions. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late teens, but they retain their vigour well into their later years and decline only gradually. Halfling women carry their young for approximately eight months before giving birth, and births are usually single, though twins are not uncommon. Infant mortality is low in settled communities, owing to the sheltered and well-provisioned nature of halfling life, and the young are cared for with great attentiveness by the entire community.

The halfling life cycle is characterised by steady growth and slow decline, and halfling populations tend to increase gradually but persistently when food and peace are sufficient. The combination of moderate longevity and reliable reproduction gives halfling communities a stability that is the envy of less settled peoples, and the halfling way of life has changed little over many centuries. The elders of a halfling community are numerous and respected, and their counsel is sought on all matters of importance, for the accumulated wisdom of a long life is valued above all else in halfling culture.

## Habitat and Distribution

Halflings are found chiefly in the lowland valleys of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), where the land is gentle, the soil is fertile, and the climate is mild. They favour the pastoral country between the forests and the plains, where the ground is well watered but not prone to flooding, and where the natural bounty of the land can be supplemented by careful cultivation. Halfling settlements are small and inconspicuous, for they are built into the landscape rather than upon it, and the dwellings are typically half-sunken or set into the sides of low hills, with rounded doors and windows that face the sun and the garden.

Smaller populations of halflings are found in the temperate lowlands of [Morvind](/content/morvind), where they inhabit the sheltered river valleys of the coast, and in the foothills of the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/sahrana), where the climate is mild enough to support their way of life. In each region, halflings seek out the same qualities of gentleness, fertility, and shelter, and the [geography](/content/geography) of their settlements is governed by the availability of good land rather than by any attachment to a particular continent.

## Diet

The halfling diet is the most varied and carefully prepared of any people, reflecting the importance of food in halfling culture and the richness of the lands they inhabit. Halflings are accomplished cultivators, and their gardens and fields produce a great variety of grains, vegetables, fruits, and herbs, each tended with a care that borders on devotion. Bread is the staple, baked fresh each day in every household, and the halfling reputation for fine baking is recognised throughout the regions where they are known.

Meat is eaten in moderation, chiefly poultry, pork, and fish, and the halfling approach to cookery is patient and refined. Every meal is an occasion, and the hospitality of a halfling household is extended freely to guests, for the sharing of food is the highest expression of halfling culture. Fermentation is widely practised, both for the preservation of food and for the brewing of ale and beer, which are consumed in quantity and with great satisfaction. The halfling palate is discerning, and their knowledge of flavour, texture, and the pairing of foods is unmatched by any other people.

## Ecology

Halflings occupy the niche of a small-bodied cultivator and forager in the pastoral lowlands they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is subtle but pervasive, for the halfling way of life reshapes the land gradually through cultivation, hedging, and the patient management of water and soil. A valley long inhabited by halflings bears the mark of their care in every hedge and terrace, though the effect is so harmonious with the natural landscape that it is often mistaken for the work of nature itself.

Halflings share their habitats with [humans](/content/humans) across much of their range, and the two peoples coexist closely, for their needs and preferences are complementary. Humans settle the larger towns and trade routes, while halflings tend the smaller valleys and hamlets, and each benefits from the presence of the other. [Gnomes](/content/gnomes) and halflings occasionally share the same country, and the two peoples maintain a friendly if distant relationship, each respecting the other's preference for quiet and unobtrusive life.
