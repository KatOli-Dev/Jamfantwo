## Overview

Ashford is a northern city of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), situated at a major crossing of the river that flows through the northern river valleys. The city stands at the point where the river is at its narrowest and most shallow, and the ford that gives the city its name has been a crossing point since the earliest days of human settlement in the region. The bridge that now spans the river is one of the oldest and the most substantial in the north, and the roads that converge on Ashford from all directions make the city a natural hub of communication and trade between the northern valleys and the rest of the continent.

## The Crossing and the Roads

The ford at Ashford was the original reason for the settlement, and the crossing has been the most important point of passage on the northern river for as long as humans have inhabited the region. The river is broad and deep for most of its course, but at Ashford it contracts and shallows, and the bed is firm enough to support the passage of wagons and livestock when the water is low. The bridge that was built at the crossing in a later age is a substantial structure of stone, wide enough for two wagons to pass, and it is one of the finest examples of northern engineering.

The roads that converge on Ashford are the other foundation of the city's prosperity, for the crossing is the meeting point of the routes that connect the northern valleys to each other and to the rest of the continent. The north road runs to [Thornwick](/content/thornwick) and the older settlements of the valley, the south road runs to the central grasslands and the southern cities, the east road runs to the coast and the ports of the eastern shore, and the west road climbs into the foothills of the mountain spine. The traffic that passes through Ashford is constant and varied, and the city's inns, taverns, and stables are a significant part of its economy.

## The City

Ashford is a bustling, well-connected city, and its architecture reflects its function as a crossroads settlement. The city is built on both banks of the river, connected by the bridge and by a second, newer bridge that was built to accommodate the growing traffic of recent centuries. The streets are broad and well-paved, for the wagons and the livestock that pass through the city require room and a firm surface, and the public buildings are situated near the bridge, where the traffic is heaviest and the commerce is greatest.

The market square of Ashford is one of the busiest in the north, and the market that is held there is a daily affair, for the constant traffic of the roads ensures a steady supply of goods and customers. The population of Ashford is chiefly [human](/content/humans), with a small community of [dwarves](/content/dwarves) who are involved in the trade of metalwork and the maintenance of the bridge and the roads.

## Significance

Ashford is the primary hub of communication and trade in the northern river valleys, and its bridge and its roads are the arteries through which the commerce of the north flows. The city is the most important settlement of the region after [Thornwick](/content/thornwick), and its position at the crossing of the river gives it a strategic significance that has been recognised since the earliest days of human settlement. The city is a key point in the road network of the [Northern Marches](/content/northern-marches), and its prosperity is a direct consequence of the traffic that passes through its streets and over its bridge.
