## Overview

Morvind lies to the north of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar) across the [Stormfall Sea](/content/stormfall-sea). It is a rugged and mountainous continent, its interior dominated by ranges that hold their snow well into the spring. The coastal lowlands are narrow but support fishing and trading settlements that have endured for centuries, their harbours sheltered by the same cliffs that protect them from the open ocean. Morvind's mineral wealth has long drawn prospectors and traders, and the continent's reputation for fine metalwork is recognised throughout the known world. Though smaller and less varied than Aethelar, Morvind has a character and a history all its own, shaped by the mountains that define its every feature.

## Climate and Terrain

The interior ranges of Morvind form a labyrinth of valleys and high passes, many of which become quiet during the winter months but reopen with the thaw. Snow lies deep upon the higher slopes for much of the year, and the passes are navigable only during the brief but vivid summer, when the alpine meadows burst into flower and the streams run heavy with meltwater. Between the peaks lie sheltered plateaus where close-knit communities tend flocks of mountain sheep and cultivate cold-resistant crops during the bright growing season. These plateaus are isolated from one another by the ridges that separate them, and each has developed its own distinct customs and dialects, though all share a common resilience in the face of the long winters.

The lower slopes of the mountains are clothed in coniferous forest, dense and dark, which thins toward the summits and gives way to bare rock and scree. These forests provide timber and game for the communities of the uplands, and they shelter a variety of wildlife that is well adapted to the cold. The trees are among the hardiest in the known world, capable of surviving the deep freezes and short growing seasons that characterise the Morvind interior, and their wood is prized for its density and straight grain.

The rivers that descend from Morvind's heights are swift and clear, fed by glacial meltwater, and they carve scenic gorges through the rock before reaching the sea. These waterways have historically provided the most reliable routes inland, and settlements along their banks remain the most populous in the continent, their people known for hospitality to travellers. The largest of these rivers is navigable for much of its length, and its valley forms the principal corridor between the coast and the interior, carrying goods, people, and news between the two.

## Coasts and Lowlands

The coastal lowlands of Morvind are narrow, seldom extending more than a few miles inland before the mountains rise. The shore is marked by high cliffs and deep inlets, and the harbours that nestle among them are well sheltered from the storms that sweep across the [Stormfall Sea](/content/stormfall-sea) during the autumn and winter. The coastal communities are fishing and trading peoples, and their contact with the sea has shaped every aspect of their lives, from their diet to their architecture to their oral traditions.

The climate of the coast is milder than that of the interior, warmed by the waters of the Stormfall and sheltered from the worst of the mountain weather. Fog is common in the autumn, and the long winter nights are lit by the aurora that gives the nearby [Auric Ocean](/content/auric-ocean) its name, a phenomenon that the peoples of Morvind regard with reverence and have woven into their oldest traditions. The short but intense summers are a time of feverish activity, as the coastal communities fish, trade, and prepare for the long months when the sea may be impassable.

## Peoples

[Dwarves](/content/dwarves) are the most numerous of the sapient peoples of Morvind, and the continent holds the greatest concentration of dwarven population in the known world. Their settlements are carved into the mountains of the interior, where vast underground networks offer stable temperatures, refuge from the weather above, and access to the ores and stone that dwarven life requires. The dwarven halls of Morvind are the grandest and most extensive of their kind, and their craftsmanship in metal and stone is the foundation of the continent's wealth and reputation.

[Humans](/content/humans) settle the coastal lowlands, where the climate is milder than the interior uplands. Their communities are oriented toward the sea, and their livelihoods depend upon fishing, trade, and the transport of dwarven goods to the markets of Aethelar. The humans of Morvind are hardy and self-reliant, accustomed to the long winters and the demands of a coast that offers little arable land, and their maritime skills are among the finest in the known world.

Smaller populations of [elves](/content/elves) are found in the temperate uplands, where they dwell in the sheltered valleys and lower slopes of the interior ranges. These folk have grown somewhat stockier and more tolerant of cold than their Aethelaran kin, and their communities are small and secluded, maintaining a quiet existence in the wooded valleys that the dwarves have no interest in claiming. [Orcs](/content/orcs) are present in the remote highlands and cold steppes of the far interior, where few other peoples venture, and [goblins](/content/goblins) may be found in scattered pockets along the coast and in the foothills.

## Significance

Morvind's mineral wealth and the skill of its metalworkers have made it a cornerstone of trade in the northern world. The continent's ores and crafted goods flow south across the [Stormfall Sea](/content/stormfall-sea) to Aethelar and beyond, and its ports bustle with merchants during the calmer months. The [Strait of Velden](/content/strait-of-velden), which connects the Stormfall Sea to the [Auric Ocean](/content/auric-ocean), lies along the southeastern coast of Morvind and has long been a point of strategic and commercial importance, as the wealth of the continent must pass through this narrow channel to reach the markets of the south and east.
