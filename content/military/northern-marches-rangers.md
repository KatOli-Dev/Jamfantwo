## The Guardians of the Wilderness

The [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) occupy
the northernmost region of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar),
a land of dense forests, fast-flowing rivers, and hills that rise toward the
foothills of the [Whitewall Range](/content/location/natural/feature/whitewall-range).
The Marches are sparsely populated, and the settlements that exist are small
and isolated, separated by stretches of wilderness that can take days to cross.
The Rangers are the force that patrols this wilderness, protecting the
settlements from the dangers that lurk in the deep woods and on the high moors.

The Northern Marches Rangers are not a conventional military force. They are
woodsmen, hunters, and trackers who have turned their skills to the service of
the realm. They operate in small groups, moving silently through the forest,
living off the land, and appearing where they are least expected. They are the
eyes and ears of the Marches, and they are the first line of defence against
the threats that come from the wild.

## Organisation

The Rangers are organised into companies, each responsible for a sector of the
Marches. There are six companies, each commanded by a Ranger-Captain who
reports to the Lord Ranger of the Northern Marches. The Lord Ranger is
appointed by the Council of the Marches, and he is responsible for the
coordination of the Rangers' activities and for the allocation of resources.

Each company consists of approximately eighty Rangers, divided into patrols of
eight to twelve. The patrols operate independently, spending weeks or even
months in the wilderness. The Rangers are given considerable autonomy in the
conduct of their patrols, for they are the experts in their sectors and they
know better than any distant commander how to respond to the threats they
encounter.

## Recruitment

Recruitment for the Rangers is a matter of selection rather than enlistment.
Potential Rangers are identified by the existing Rangers, who watch for
individuals who possess the skills and the temperament that the service
requires. A candidate must be an expert woodsman, a skilled archer, and a
reliable companion. He must be comfortable with solitude and capable of making
difficult decisions without supervision.

The recruitment process includes a probationary period during which the
candidate serves with an experienced patrol. The probation lasts for a year,
and at the end of it the patrol decides whether to accept the candidate as a
full member. The standards are high, and many candidates are rejected. Those
who are accepted join a brotherhood that is bound by ties of loyalty and mutual
trust.

## Skills and Training

A Ranger must master a wide range of skills. He must be able to navigate by the
sun, the stars, and the signs of the natural world. He must be able to track
animals and men across any type of terrain, following a trail that may be days
old. He must know the plants and animals of the Marches, which are safe to eat
and which are poisonous, which can be used for medicine and which for other
purposes.

Weapons training focuses on the bow, which is the primary weapon of the Ranger.
A Ranger must be able to shoot accurately at moving targets, at long range, and
in poor light. He must also be proficient with the knife and the spear, for
there are times when the bow cannot be used. The Rangers do not wear heavy
armour, for it would impede their movement and make too much noise in the
forest. They rely on stealth and skill for their protection.

## The Green Patrol

The Green Patrol is the name given to the Rangers' continuous watch over the
northern wilderness. The patrols move constantly, covering their sectors in a
pattern that is designed to maximise the chances of detecting threats. The
pattern is irregular, for the Rangers know that a predictable patrol can be
avoided by an intelligent enemy. The patrols change their routes, their timing,
and their methods to keep potential enemies uncertain.

The Green Patrol is maintained in all seasons and in all weathers. The Rangers
operate in winter as well as summer, travelling on skis when the snow is deep
and camping in improvised shelters when the cold is intense. The Rangers pride
themselves on their ability to operate in conditions that would confine other
soldiers to their garrisons, and they use this ability to gain an advantage
over enemies who expect to be safe in the winter months.

## Intelligence Gathering

The Rangers are the primary source of intelligence for the Northern Marches.
They report on the movement of people and animals, on the condition of the
trails and passes, and on any signs of unusual activity. The intelligence they
gather allows the Council of the Marches to make informed decisions about the
deployment of resources and the response to potential threats.

The Rangers maintain a network of informants among the trappers, hunters, and
hermits who live in the wilderness. These informants provide the Rangers with
information that they could not gather themselves, and they are rewarded with
supplies, money, or other forms of payment. The informant network is the
Rangers' most valuable asset, and it is protected with the utmost secrecy.

## Relations with the Settlements

The Rangers are respected and admired throughout the Northern Marches. They are
the protectors of the settlements, and they are welcomed wherever they go. The
Rangers provide assistance to the settlers, helping them to deal with problems
ranging from predatory animals to bandits. They also serve as messengers and
guides, linking the isolated communities of the Marches to one another and to
the wider world.

The relationship between the Rangers and the settlers is one of mutual
dependence. The settlers provide the Rangers with food, shelter, and
information. The Rangers provide the settlers with protection and assistance.
The bond between them is strong, and it has sustained the Northern Marches
through centuries of hardship.

## The History of the Rangers

The Rangers were established during the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era),
when the first settlers of the Northern Marches realised that they needed a
force that could operate in the wilderness and respond to threats that were
beyond the capacity of the local militias. The early Rangers were hunters and
trappers who volunteered to serve as scouts and watchmen, and the organisation
grew from these humble beginnings.

The Rangers have served with distinction in every conflict that has touched the
Northern Marches. They fought in the [Three-Banners War](/content/history/three-banners-war),
providing intelligence and conducting raids behind enemy lines. They played a
key role in the [Stoneheim Pass War](/content/history/stoneheim-pass-war),
guiding dwarven forces through the passes of the Whitewall Range. Their
reputation for skill, courage, and reliability is unmatched, and they are
regarded as one of the finest light infantry forces in the known world.
