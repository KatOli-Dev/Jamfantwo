## The Horse in War

The use of horses in warfare has shaped the military history of the known world
from the earliest conflicts of the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era)
to the present day. A mounted warrior can move faster, carry more equipment,
and strike from a greater height than a soldier on foot. The realms that have
mastered mounted warfare have held advantages over their neighbours that could
not be overcome by numbers alone. The plains of Aethelar, the deserts of
Sahrana, and the grasslands of the interior have all produced cavalry
traditions that reflect the conditions of their environments.

Not every realm has embraced cavalry to the same degree. The [Stoneheim
Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) fight primarily on foot,
their dwarven warriors finding little advantage in the use of mounts. The river
kingdom of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) uses cavalry primarily for
scouting and pursuit, relying on its infantry for the main work of battle. But
for the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation),
the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), and the
[Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), the horse is
the centrepiece of the military system, and everything else is arranged around
it.

## Types of Cavalry

Cavalry in the known world can be divided into several broad categories based
on their equipment, their training, and their tactical role. Light cavalry are
mounted on fast, agile horses and are armed with bows or javelins. They are
used for scouting, screening, and harassment, and they avoid prolonged close
combat with heavier forces. The riders of the Vashari Pact and the [Kharesh
Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) are primarily light
cavalry, relying on speed and missile weapons to wear down their enemies.

Medium cavalry ride somewhat heavier horses and wear armour of leather or mail.
They carry spears or lances in addition to bows, and they are capable of both
missile combat and close action. The [Northern
Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) field medium cavalry
that can scout ahead of the army and fight in the battle line when required.

Heavy cavalry are the shock arm of a mounted force. They ride the largest and
strongest horses, wear armour of mail or plate, and carry heavy lances and
long swords. The heavy cavalry of the [Southern
Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) and the [Amurra
Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) can break an infantry
formation by the weight of their charge alone, though they are vulnerable to
disorder and to attacks on their flanks.

## Horse Breeds

The quality of a cavalry force depends as much on its horses as on its riders.
The horses of the Plains Confederation are small and hardy, capable of living
on the grass of the prairie and of travelling long distances without
foundering. They are not fast by the standards of the southern breeds, but they
possess an endurance that no southern horse can match.

The desert horses of Sahrana are the fastest in the known world, capable of
bursts of speed that leave northern horses behind. They are more lightly built
than the plains ponies, and they require careful feeding and watering, but
their speed makes them invaluable for the hit-and-run tactics of the Vashari
cavalry.

The heavy horses used by the southern realms are bred from stock that was
brought from the northern continent during the early years of settlement. They
are large, strong, and steady in battle, capable of carrying an armoured rider
at a charge without slowing. These horses are expensive to maintain, requiring
large quantities of grain and hay, and they are reserved for the wealthiest
cavalry forces.

## The Charge

The shock charge is the most dramatic manoeuvre in mounted warfare. The cavalry
forms up in a line or a wedge, with the riders knee to knee and the lances
levelled. At a signal from the commander, the cavalry advances at a walk,
then a trot, and finally a gallop, the speed increasing as the riders approach
the enemy. The impact of a heavy cavalry charge can shatter an infantry
formation, sending soldiers flying and breaking the morale of those who survive
the initial collision.

The charge is effective only when the cavalry can reach the enemy in good
order. If the horses are tired, if the ground is broken, or if the enemy
maintains their formation, the charge will fail. Experienced infantry know how
to receive a charge, forming a dense hedge of spears and presenting a wall of
points that no horse will willingly impale itself upon.

## Horse Archers

The horse archer is the cavalryman of the steppes and the deserts. Mounted on a
fast, agile horse, the horse archer rides toward the enemy, looses an arrow,
and wheels away before the enemy can respond. The horse archer can shoot in any
direction, including behind the horse, a technique that allows the archer to
strike while appearing to retreat.

The horse archers of the Plains Confederation and the Vashari Pact are trained
from childhood in the use of the bow from horseback. They can hit a target at
full gallop and can maintain a rate of fire that keeps the enemy under
continuous pressure. A force of horse archers can defeat a larger infantry
force by attrition, shooting the enemy down from a distance where the infantry
cannot reply.

## Logistics of Cavalry

Cavalry is expensive to maintain. Horses require large quantities of food and
water, and they cannot be kept in the field indefinitely without resupply. A
cavalry army must plan its campaigns around the availability of fodder,
camping near water and moving on when the grass is exhausted. The horsemen of
the Plains Confederation carry their own fodder with them in the form of dried
grass, but even this supply is limited.

The logistics of cavalry limit the scale and duration of mounted campaigns. A
large cavalry force cannot remain concentrated for long without denuding the
pasture around its camp. The Plains Confederation levy disperses after a few
weeks of campaigning for this reason, the riders returning to their homes to
rest and feed their horses before they can be called out again.

## Countering Cavalry

Infantry forces have developed a range of tactics to counter cavalry. The spear
formation, with the front rank kneeling and presenting their spears at an
angle, can stop a charge if the formation holds. Archers can shoot the horses
from a distance, reducing the cavalry before it reaches the infantry line.
Terrain can be used to neutralise the advantage of mobility, channelling the
cavalry into ground where it cannot manoeuvre.

The most effective counter to cavalry is other cavalry. A mobile force that can
match the enemy's speed and outflank their manoeuvres is the surest way to
defeat a mounted opponent. The realms that have neglected their own cavalry
have often found themselves at a disadvantage against neighbours who have
maintained strong mounted forces.
