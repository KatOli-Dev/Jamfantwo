## The Warrior Clans

The [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) inhabit the
coldest and most inhospitable regions of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind),
a land of frozen tundra, glacial valleys, and windswept coasts where the
struggle for survival is the central fact of existence. The war bands of the
Frostwall Clans are not a standing army in the manner of the southern realms.
They are gatherings of warriors who follow a successful chieftain, bound by
ties of kinship, loyalty, and the promise of plunder. Every adult male in the
clans is a warrior, trained from childhood in the use of weapons and hardened
by the environment in which he lives.

The war bands are the means by which the clans conduct their conflicts with one
another, defend their territories against outsiders, and raid the settled lands
of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) and the
northern coasts of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar).
They are feared throughout the known world for their ferocity, their endurance,
and their willingness to fight in conditions that would immobilise any other
army.

## Organisation of a War Band

A Frostwall war band is organised around a chieftain who has demonstrated his
courage, his skill in battle, and his ability to provide for his followers. The
chieftain is advised by a council of the older and more experienced warriors,
and he is supported by his household warriors, the huscarls, who serve as his
bodyguard and as the core of his war band around which the other warriors
gather.

The size of a war band varies according to the resources of the chieftain and
the nature of the campaign. A small raid might involve thirty or forty
warriors, while a major invasion could mobilise several thousand. The warriors
bring their own weapons and armour, and they provide their own provisions for
the first part of the campaign. After that, the chieftain is expected to feed
his followers from the plunder they capture.

## Arms and Armour

The warriors of the Frostwall Clans are armed with weapons that are effective
in close combat and that can be used in conditions of extreme cold. The
primary weapon is the long axe, a weapon with a broad blade that can deliver a
devastating blow against an armoured opponent. The axe is supplemented by a
spear, a sword for those who can afford one, and a knife for use in the closest
quarters.

Armour is heavy and practical. A mail hauberk, worn over a thick gambeson, is
the standard protection, covered by a coat of fur or leather that provides
additional insulation. The helmet is of iron, with a nasal guard and cheek
pieces that protect the face from the wind and from enemy weapons. Shields are
round, made of wood and covered with leather, with an iron boss at the centre.

## The Shield Wall

The signature formation of the Frostwall war bands is the shield wall, a line
of warriors standing shoulder to shoulder, shields overlapping, presenting a
barrier of wood and iron that is difficult to break. The shield wall is used
for defence against cavalry and infantry alike, and it is the formation from
which the warriors launch their attacks.

The shield wall is not a static formation. The warriors advance and retreat as
a unit, maintaining the cohesion of the line while the chieftain and his
huscarls direct the movement. When the shield wall meets the enemy, the
fighting is close and brutal, with the warriors pushing against each other and
striking with axes and spears over the tops of the shields.

## The Berserkers

Among the Frostwall Clans, there exist warriors who are said to fight in a
state of battle frenzy, feeling no pain and showing no fear. These berserkers
are feared by their enemies and respected by their comrades. They are the shock
troops of the war bands, used to break the enemy formation at the critical
moment of the battle.

The berserker state is induced through a combination of ritual, meditation, and
the use of certain substances that are known only to the initiates of the cult.
The berserkers wear the skins of bears and wolves, and they are said to howl
like animals when they fight. Their effectiveness is undeniable, but the cost
is high. Many berserkers die young, and those who survive are often troubled by
the memories of the things they have done.

## Raiding

The Frostwall war bands are most active in the summer months, when the weather
is mild enough to allow travel by sea. The war bands use longships to cross the
stormy northern waters, raiding the coasts of the Karhold Realm and the
northern shores of Aethelar. The raids are swift and brutal, with the war
bands landing at dawn, taking what they can carry, and sailing away before the
defenders can organise a response.

The targets of the raids are coastal settlements, monasteries, and trading
posts that are isolated and poorly defended. The raiders take food, weapons,
valuables, and captives who can be ransomed or sold as slaves. The raids are
condemned by the settled realms, but the Frostwall Clans see them as a
legitimate means of acquiring the resources they need to survive.

## Winter Campaigning

The Frostwall war bands are capable of campaigning in winter, when the armies
of the southern realms are confined to their garrisons. The warriors are
accustomed to the cold, and they know how to travel, fight, and live in
conditions that would kill an unprepared soldier. Winter campaigns are mounted
against enemies who expect to be safe from attack until the spring, and the
element of surprise often gives the war bands a decisive advantage.

Winter campaigning requires careful preparation. The warriors carry supplies
of dried meat and fish, and they know how to find shelter in the frozen
landscape. They travel on skis or on foot, moving silently across the snow,
and they attack at dawn when the cold is at its most intense and the defenders
are least alert.

## The Chieftain's Authority

The authority of a Frostwall chieftain is not absolute. He holds his position
by the consent of his followers, and he can be deposed if he proves
incompetent, cowardly, or unlucky. The chieftain must demonstrate his worth in
every campaign, leading from the front and sharing the dangers of his
warriors. A chieftain who is seen as weak or selfish will lose his followers,
and a chieftain who loses his followers is nothing.

The relationship between the chieftain and his warriors is one of mutual
obligation. The warriors owe the chieftain their loyalty and their service; the
chieftain owes the warriors protection, justice, and a share of the plunder.
This relationship is the foundation of the war band, and it has sustained the
Frostwall Clans through centuries of hardship and conflict.
