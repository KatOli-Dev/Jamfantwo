## The Island Defence

The [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) are a scattered
archipelago in the eastern reaches of the [Auric
Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), a chain of islands that
stretches for hundreds of miles across waters that are rich in fish and
frequented by merchant ships on the long voyage between the continents. The
Isles are not a single realm in the manner of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth)
or [Vael](/content/government/national/vael); they are a loose confederation of
island communities, each with its own customs and its own leaders, bound
together by a common need for defence against the pirates and raiders who have
always been a threat in these waters.

The coastal patrol of the Auric Isles is the means by which this defence is
maintained. It is not a large force, for the population of the Isles is modest,
but it is well adapted to the conditions of the archipelago. The patrol's
vessels are small, fast, and manoeuvrable, capable of navigating the narrow
channels and shallow harbours that are common in the Isles. The patrol's crews
are expert sailors and fishermen who know the waters of the archipelago as well
as they know their own villages.

## Vessels of the Patrol

The typical vessel of the Auric Isles coastal patrol is a shallow-draft cutter
of about forty feet in length, with a single mast carrying a large
fore-and-aft sail and a crew of twelve to fifteen men. The cutter is fast and
weatherly, capable of sailing close to the wind and of manoeuvring in confined
spaces. It carries a light catapult on the foredeck for use against enemy
vessels, and the crew are armed with bows and boarding axes.

Alongside the cutters, the patrol operates a number of smaller boats that are
used for inshore work. These boats are rowed or sailed, and they are used to
patrol the shallow waters around the islands where the cutters cannot go. The
small boats are also used to carry messages and supplies between the islands,
a task that requires constant attention in a region where the weather can
change rapidly.

## Organisation

The coastal patrol is organised into districts, each covering a group of
islands. Each district is commanded by a Warden of the Coast, who reports to
the Council of the Auric Isles. The Warden is responsible for the patrol
vessels based in his district, for the lookout stations that watch the
approaches to his islands, and for the coordination of the defence with the
local militia.

The patrol maintains a central depot on the largest island of the group, where
vessels can be repaired and where supplies can be stored. The depot is staffed
by shipwrights, sailmakers, and armourers who keep the patrol's vessels in
fighting condition. The depot also serves as the headquarters of the Admiral of
the Coast, the senior officer of the patrol, who is elected by the Council
from among the Wardens.

## The Lookout Network

The Auric Isles are surrounded by treacherous waters, and the approaches to the
archipelago are watched by a network of lookout stations that are positioned
on the headlands and highest points of the islands. The lookouts watch for the
sails of approaching vessels, reporting their sightings to the nearest Warden
by signal fire or by messenger boat.

The lookout network is the first line of defence for the Isles. A pirate
squadron that approaches the archipelago can be spotted while it is still a day
away from the nearest island, giving the patrol time to prepare and the
settlements time to take shelter. The lookouts are volunteers from the island
communities, men and women who serve for the satisfaction of protecting their
homes and for the small stipend that the Council provides.

## Tactics

The coastal patrol relies on speed and local knowledge. When a threat is
reported, the patrol vessels converge on the area from their various stations,
using their knowledge of the currents and the winds to arrive faster than the
enemy expects. The patrol does not seek a fleet action against a superior
force; instead, it harries the enemy, cutting off stragglers, attacking at
night, and using the confusing geography of the archipelago to conceal its
movements.

Against pirate vessels, which are often larger and more heavily armed than the
patrol's cutters, the patrol uses hit-and-run tactics. A group of cutters
attacks the pirate vessel from multiple directions, closing rapidly to fire
their catapults and bows, then withdrawing before the pirates can board. The
cutters are agile enough to avoid the pirate's attempted rammings, and they can
escape into shallow water where the heavier pirate vessels cannot follow.

## The Militia

Every island in the Auric Isles maintains a militia of able-bodied men and
women who are trained in the use of weapons and who can be called up to defend
their homes. The militia is not a professional force, but the islanders are
tough and self-reliant, and they are motivated by the knowledge that they are
fighting for their families and their property.

The militia is organised by the village elders, who appoint captains and
sergeants from among the most experienced members of the community. The militia
meets for training once a month, practising with the spear, the bow, and the
knife. In the event of an attack, the militia mans the defensive positions that
have been prepared on each island, while the coastal patrol intercepts the
enemy at sea.

## Smuggling and the Patrol

The coastal patrol is also responsible for the suppression of smuggling, a
problem that has plagued the Auric Isles for as long as they have been settled.
The archipelago's many hidden coves and inlets provide ideal locations for
smugglers to land their goods, and the patrol spends as much time chasing
smugglers as it does defending against pirates.

The relationship between the patrol and the smugglers is complex. The smugglers
are often well regarded in their local communities, for they provide goods that
would otherwise be unavailable or unaffordable. The patrol must balance its
duty to enforce the law with the need to maintain the goodwill of the islanders.
A patrol that is seen as an occupying force will not receive the cooperation it
needs to function effectively.

## The History of the Patrol

The coastal patrol of the Auric Isles was established in the aftermath of a
particularly devastating pirate raid that destroyed three settlements and
carried away over two hundred captives. The island communities realised that
they could not depend on the navies of the larger realms to protect them, and
they pooled their resources to create a force of their own. The patrol has
grown and changed since then, but its mission has remained the same: to protect
the people of the Auric Isles from the dangers of the sea.

The patrol has been tested many times over the centuries. It has fought off
pirates, defeated raiders from the northern seas, and even repelled an invasion
fleet from one of the mainland realms. Each victory has added to the reputation
of the patrol, and the patrol's vessels are now welcomed in every port of the
archipelago.
