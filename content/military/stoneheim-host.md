## The Dwarven Military Tradition

The host of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds)
is the most formidable heavy infantry force in the known world. Dwarven
warriors, armed and armoured in the products of their own forges and trained
from youth in the arts of war, form the core of a military tradition that
stretches back to the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The
Stoneheim Host is not a standing army in the human sense; it is a gathering of
warriors from the individual holds, each hold contributing a contingent that
reflects its population and its wealth. The host has never been defeated in a
pitched battle, a record that the dwarves maintain with considerable pride.

The military strength of the Stoneheim Holds rests on three foundations: the
quality of their equipment, the discipline of their soldiers, and the skill of
their engineers. Dwarven smiths produce armour and weapons that are superior to
those of any human realm, using techniques that have been refined over
centuries of metallurgical advancement. Dwarven soldiers are drilled to a
standard of precision that human armies rarely achieve, and they fight in
formations that make the best use of their strength and stamina. Dwarven
engineers are without equal in the construction of siege engines, fortifications,
and the mining tunnels that have decided more than one campaign.

## Organisation

The Stoneheim Host is organised around the concept of the phalanx, a dense
formation of heavy infantry armed with long spears and fighting in close order.
The basic unit is the company, consisting of one hundred warriors commanded by
a Captain of the Hold. Ten companies form a regiment, and three regiments form
a division. A full gathering of the host includes five divisions, though it is
rare for all five to be called out at once. The reserves and the garrisons of
the individual holds account for an additional three divisions' worth of
trained warriors who can be called up in an emergency.

Each hold sends its own contingents, commanded by a War-Thane appointed by the
Hold-Lord. The War-Thanes coordinate their efforts through the Council of
Hosts, a body of senior commanders who meet in [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim)
to plan campaigns and allocate resources. The Council is chaired by the High
Thane, a position that is traditionally held by the ruler of the strongest hold
at the time of the gathering.

## Armour and Weapons

The standard dwarf warrior wears a full hauberk of chain mail, supplemented by
plate armour on the shoulders, arms, and legs. The helmet is of steel, with a
full visor that can be lowered to protect the face, leaving only a narrow slit
for vision and breathing. The weight of this armour would be crippling to a
human soldier, but dwarves bear it with relative ease, their stout frames and
powerful musculature allowing them to fight for hours in full equipment.

The primary weapon of the host is the long spear, twelve feet in length, with a
broad head of hardened steel. In addition to the spear, each warrior carries a
short sword or a heavy axe for use when the formation closes with the enemy.
A round shield of iron-bound wood, three feet in diameter, is carried on the
left arm, providing additional protection against missiles and the weapons of
enemy infantry. The shield is painted with the emblem of the warrior's hold, a
practice that allows commanders to identify units on the battlefield.

## Siege Engineers

The siege engineers of Stoneheim are renowned throughout the known world and
are often hired by human realms to conduct sieges or to construct fortifications.
The engineers are organised into a separate guild, the Order of the Stone, whose
members are trained in the mathematics of engineering, the properties of stone
and wood, and the techniques of excavation and construction. The Order maintains
workshops in each of the major holds and keeps a library of plans and treatises
that is consulted by engineers from every realm.

The siege engines produced by the Order include trebuchets, ballistae, and
battering rams of great size and power. The trebuchets built by dwarven
engineers can hurl stones weighing three hundred pounds over a distance of four
hundred yards, and they are accurate enough to strike the same spot on a wall
repeatedly. The ballistae are smaller but more precise, used to target the
crews of enemy engines and to clear the battlements of defenders.

## Mining and Tunnelling

The dwarves of Stoneheim are the finest miners in the known world, and they
have applied their mining skills to military purposes with devastating effect.
Dwarven sappers can excavate tunnels beneath enemy fortifications at a rate
that no human engineer can match, collapsing walls and towers by removing their
foundations. During the [Stoneheim Pass War](/content/history/stoneheim-pass-war),
dwarven tunnels were used to outflank enemy positions that had been thought
impregnable, and the technique has been refined in the centuries since.

Tunnelling operations are conducted by specialist teams that include miners,
surveyors, and timber-men who shore up the excavation as it progresses. The
tunnels are driven from a starting point that is screened from enemy view,
often at night or behind the cover of an ongoing siege. When the tunnel reaches
its target, the miners remove the final supports, and the weight of the
structure above collapses the void.

## The Forge-Masters

The weapons and armour of the host are produced by the forge-masters of
Stoneheim, smiths whose skill is passed down through families and whose
reputation rests on the quality of their work. Each hold maintains its own
forges, but the greatest concentration of smithing talent is found in Stoneheim
itself, where the constant ringing of hammers is heard from dawn until dusk.

The forge-masters use ores from the mountains that surround the holds, ores
that are richer in iron and less contaminated with sulphur than those found
elsewhere. The smelting process is carefully controlled, and the resulting
metal is worked and folded repeatedly to produce blades that hold a sharp edge
and resist breaking. Armour produced by the forge-masters is tested by being
struck with a war hammer at full force; if the armour cracks or the wearer is
injured, the armour is rejected and the smith must begin again.
