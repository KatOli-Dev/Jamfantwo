## A League of Ships

The navy of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league)
is a combined force that draws ships and crews from the member cities of the
League, each of which contributes according to its wealth and its maritime
tradition. The League was founded on the principle of mutual defence against
piracy and against the naval ambitions of larger realms, and the navy is the
primary instrument through which that defence is maintained. The League has no
single capital; its fleet is administered from the city that holds the rotating
presidency of the League Council, and the fleet's main anchorage shifts as the
presidency changes.

The Thessaran League Navy operates primarily in the [Greater Inland
Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/greater-inland-sea), where the calm
waters and numerous islands create ideal conditions for galley warfare. The
fleet's ships are designed for speed and manoeuvrability rather than for
long-range cruising, and they rely on oars as their primary propulsion, with
sails reserved for downwind passages and for conserving the strength of the
rowers. The League navy is not the largest in the known world, but it is among
the most experienced, and its crews are familiar with every current, shoal, and
anchorage in their home waters.

## Ship Types

The primary warship of the Thessaran League is the trireme, a galley with three
banks of oars on each side, crewed by one hundred seventy rowers and
approximately thirty marines and deck crew. The trireme is built for speed and
agility, capable of ramming an enemy vessel and withdrawing before the damage
can be returned. The prow is reinforced with a bronze-sheathed ram that can
stave in the planking of an enemy hull, and the deck is kept clear of
obstructions to allow the marines to move freely during boarding actions.

Alongside the triremes, the League maintains a number of smaller vessels.
Penteconters, with fifty rowers, serve as scouts and dispatch boats, while the
larger quinqueremes, with five banks of oars, serve as flagships and as
transports for marine detachments. The quinquereme is slower than the trireme
but carries a larger complement of marines and can serve as a platform for
light catapults.

## Organisation

The League Navy is organised into squadrons of ten to fifteen ships, each
squadron commanded by a Nauarch appointed by the League Council. The squadrons
are assigned to patrol zones that cover the approaches to the member cities and
the trade routes that cross the Greater Inland Sea. A reserve squadron is
maintained at the main anchorage, ready to respond to any threat that exceeds
the capacity of the patrol squadrons.

The command structure of the League Navy is designed to prevent any single city
from dominating the fleet. The Nauarchs are drawn from different member cities,
and the overall command rotates among them on a monthly basis during active
campaigns. This system ensures that no admiral can accumulate enough power to
challenge the League Council, but it has the disadvantage of creating
discontinuity in command during prolonged operations.

## The Rowers

The rowers of the Thessaran League Navy are free men who serve for pay, not
slaves chained to the benches. The League Council determined early in its
history that free rowers fight harder and row more reliably than pressed
labour, and the policy has been maintained ever since. The rowers are organised
into guilds that negotiate their pay and conditions with the League Council,
and they are represented in disputes by elected spokesmen.

Training of rowers begins on land, where recruits learn the rhythm and
coordination required for galley propulsion on specially constructed benches.
Only after they have mastered the basic stroke are they allowed to take their
place on a ship. Experienced rowers are highly valued and can command wages
that compare favourably with those of skilled craftsmen ashore.

## History and Major Actions

The Thessaran League Navy has been involved in most of the major conflicts that
have touched the Greater Inland Sea. During the [Twin-Reaches
Wars](/content/history/twin-reaches-wars), the League fleet defeated a larger
enemy force at the Battle of the Narrow Straits, a victory that is still
celebrated in the member cities. The battle demonstrated the effectiveness of
the League's tactical doctrine, which emphasised ramming and speed over
boarding and close combat.

In the [Three-Banners War](/content/history/three-banners-war), the League Navy
was tasked with maintaining the blockade of enemy ports, a duty that it
performed with mixed success. The blockade was effective in the early years of
the war, but the enemy developed countermeasures, including the use of fire
ships and midnight sorties, that reduced its impact. The League lost twelve
ships during the conflict, a loss that represented a significant portion of its
strength.

## The Arsenal

The main arsenal of the League Navy is located on an island near the centre of
the Greater Inland Sea, a location that is equally accessible to all member
cities. The arsenal includes covered ship sheds where triremes can be stored
out of the weather, workshops for the repair and maintenance of equipment, and
warehouses that hold sufficient timber, cordage, and canvas to outfit an entire
squadron.

The arsenal is administered by a board appointed by the League Council, and it
is staffed by craftsmen from all the member cities. The shipwrights of the
arsenal are skilled in the construction of galleys, and they have developed
techniques for prefabricating components that allow a damaged ship to be
repaired quickly. A trireme that is towed into the arsenal with a shattered
ram can be ready for sea again in under a week, a speed of repair that has
saved more than one campaign from disaster.

## The Marine Complement

The marines of the Thessaran League Navy are drawn from the infantry forces of
the member cities, and they are among the most experienced boarding troops in
the known world. Each trireme carries a complement of approximately thirty
marines, who are armed with short swords, boarding pikes, and javelins. The
marines are trained to fight in the confined space of a ship's deck, where
there is little room for manoeuvre and where a fall overboard is likely to be
fatal.

The marines are organised into squads of ten, each led by a sergeant who is
responsible for the training and discipline of his men. The marines train
alongside the rowers, practising boarding actions against dummy ships and
learning to coordinate their movements with the manoeuvres of the galley. A
well-trained marine squad can board an enemy vessel within seconds of the
grappling hooks being thrown, overwhelming the defenders before they can
organise an effective resistance.

## The League in Wartime

When the Thessaran League is at war, the navy takes on an expanded role. The
patrol squadrons are reinforced with ships that are laid up in peacetime, and
the rowers are supplemented by additional volunteers who are drawn from the
coastal population. The navy's primary mission in wartime is to protect the
trade routes that are the lifeblood of the League, but it is also used to
transport armies, to blockade enemy ports, and to raid the coasts of hostile
realms.

The League Council appoints a War Admiral for the duration of the conflict,
granting him authority over all naval forces and over the shipyards that
support them. The War Admiral is chosen from among the most experienced
Nauarchs, and he is given a mandate to conduct the naval campaign as he sees
fit. The War Admiral reports to the Council at regular intervals, and he can be
removed if the Council loses confidence in his leadership.

## The Legacy of the Navy

The navy of the Thessaran League has been a model for other naval forces
throughout the known world. The League's system of shared command, its use of
free rowers, and its emphasis on ramming tactics have been studied and imitated
by the navies of other realms. The League's triremes have set the standard for
galley design, and the League's shipwrights are sought after by every realm
that maintains a galley fleet.
