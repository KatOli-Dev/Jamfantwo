## Origin and Purpose

The army of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) is the principal land
force of the river kingdom and has been a permanent institution since the reign
of [Halric Vael](/content/people/historical/halric-vael), who recognised that a
realm built along the banks of a great river system required a standing force
to control its length and defend its settlements. Before Halric's reforms, the
kingdom relied on noble levies and hired mercenaries, a system that proved
unreliable during the early stages of the [Three-Banners
War](/content/history/three-banners-war). The army that exists in the present
age traces its foundation to that period of crisis and has changed continuously
since.

The strategic position of Vael demands a force capable of rapid movement along
the rivers and equally rapid concentration at threatened points. The kingdom
stretches along both banks of the Vael for much of its length, with
[Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) at its delta and
numerous smaller towns and fortifications spaced at intervals upstream. An
enemy advancing from the east must cross the river at one of the few bridging
points, all of which are heavily fortified. An enemy approaching from the west
must march through the floodplain, where the army of Vael can use its
knowledge of the terrain to maximum advantage.

## Organisation

The Vael Army is organised into divisions called reaches, each responsible for
a section of the river. There are six reaches in total: the Delta Reach,
guarding Vaelmouth and the approaches from the [Stormfall
Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea); the Lower Reach,
covering the fertile middle valley; the Upper Reach, responsible for the
headwaters and the border with neighbouring territories; the Eastern Reach,
watching the approaches from [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth)
and the eastern plains; the Western Reach, patrolling the western bank and the
foothills of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range);
and the River Guard, a specialised force that operates the patrol boats and
river forts.

Each reach is commanded by a Reach-Lord appointed by the Crown, and within each
reach the soldiers are organised into companies of approximately two hundred
men. A company is divided into four platoons, and each platoon into four
squads of twelve to fourteen soldiers. This structure allows a reach to
detach companies for independent operations while retaining the ability to
concentrate its full strength at need. The system has proved flexible enough to
handle everything from pirate hunting on the delta to full-scale field battles
against invading armies.

## Recruitment and Training

Service in the Vael Army is voluntary for the majority of soldiers, though
every able-bodied male citizen is required to serve a minimum of two years
between the ages of eighteen and twenty-five. This compulsory period provides
basic military training and creates a pool of trained reserves who can be
called up in times of emergency. After completing their compulsory service,
many soldiers choose to remain in the army for a further term, and a
significant number make soldiering their lifelong profession.

Training takes place at garrisons spaced along the river, with the largest
training grounds located outside Vaelmouth. Recruits are instructed in the use
of the spear, the short sword, and the javelin, and they learn to fight in the
close formation that is the hallmark of Vael infantry. River-crossing drills
are a particular specialty, since the army must be able to move its forces
across the Vael quickly when the situation demands. Every soldier learns to
swim and to handle a small boat, skills that are as important as weapon
training for a force that operates in a riverine environment.

## Equipment and Arms

The standard Vael infantryman carries a spear of ash or oak, eight feet in
length, with a broad leaf-shaped head. A short stabbing sword, the vaelspatha,
is worn on the right hip for use when the spear is broken or discarded. Body
armour consists of a leather jerkin reinforced with iron scales sewn in
overlapping rows, a style that balances protection with the need to stay afloat
in the water. Helmets are of iron, with a nasal guard and a short brim that
shields the eyes from the sun's glare off the river.

Archers in the Vael Army carry composite bows of horn and sinew, shorter than
the longbows used in [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) but
powerful enough to penetrate armour at close range. They serve both as
skirmishers and as supporting troops in battle, and they are trained to shoot
from the decks of riverboats as well as on solid ground. Each company includes
a complement of twenty archers, and the best marksmen are organised into elite
units that operate independently under the direct command of the Reach-Lord.

## Riverine Operations

The River Guard is the most distinctive element of the Vael Army. It operates a
fleet of shallow-draft patrol boats, each crewed by a dozen soldiers who serve
as both sailors and fighters. These boats carry a small mast and sail for
downwind travel but are primarily propelled by oars, allowing them to move
against the current. The River Guard patrols the full navigable length of the
Vael, collecting tolls, inspecting cargo, and deterring river pirates. In
wartime, the Guard boats serve as scouts, messengers, and transports,
ferrying soldiers and supplies to wherever they are needed most.

The army has also developed specialised techniques for pontoon bridges and
assault crossings. Each reach maintains a supply of prefabricated bridge
sections that can be assembled quickly to span the river. These sections are
stored at fortified depots and can be transported by wagon or boat to the
crossing point. A well-practised crew can assemble a bridge capable of
supporting infantry in under an hour; a bridge for cavalry and wagons takes
roughly twice as long.

## Fortifications

The river forts of Vael are built to control the crossing points and to serve
as bases for the reaches. Each fort is positioned on high ground overlooking
the river, with walls of stone on the landward side and a palisade or wall on
the river side. Most forts include a protected dock where patrol boats can
moor in safety, and some of the larger forts have dry docks where boats can be
repaired. The forts are garrisoned by two or three companies, with additional
space for reinforcements to be housed when the reach is under threat.

The largest fortification in Vael is the Citadel at Vaelmouth, a massive
structure of stone and brick that guards the entrance to the river delta. The
Citadel's walls are thirty feet high on the landward side and twenty feet high
on the river side, and they are studded with towers that give overlapping
fields of fire. A chain boom stretches across the main channel of the delta,
supported by stone piers on either bank, and can be raised to prevent enemy
ships from entering the river. The Citadel has never been taken by assault.

## Relation with the Crown

The army of Vael is ultimately commanded by the King or Queen of Vael, but the
day-to-day administration is handled by the Marshal of the River, a senior
noble appointed by the Crown. The Marshal oversees recruitment, training,
supply, and the appointment of Reach-Lords. The position has historically been
held by members of the royal family or by nobles who have demonstrated their
loyalty over many years of service. The Marshal reports directly to the Crown
and attends the royal council.

The relationship between the army and the nobility of Vael is carefully
balanced. The Reach-Lords are drawn from the noble houses, but their authority
derives from the Crown rather than from their birth. This system prevents any
single noble from accumulating enough military power to challenge the throne,
while ensuring that the army benefits from the experience and resources of the
landed families. It is not a perfect system — there have been disputes and even
brief conflicts over appointments — but it has maintained the stability of the
kingdom through several centuries of change.
