## The Horsemen of the Desert

The [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) controls the most
effective cavalry force in [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana),
a mobile army of mounted archers that can cross the desert faster than any
infantry can march and strike where the enemy least expects them. The Pact was
formed by a coalition of oasis cities and nomadic clans who recognised that
their survival in the harsh environment of the southern continent depended on
their ability to move quickly and strike decisively. The desert cavalry is the
product of that recognition, and it has been refined over centuries of warfare
against the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), the
settled realms of the north, and the internal rivals who have occasionally
threatened the unity of the Pact.

The cavalry of the Vashari Pact is not a single force but a combination of
contingents contributed by the member cities and the nomadic clans that have
sworn allegiance to the Pact. Each contingent is commanded by its own leader,
and the overall command is exercised by a War-Captain elected by the Pact
Council. The system has its tensions — the interests of the oasis cities do not
always align with those of the nomadic clans — but it has proved durable enough
to survive for many generations.

## The Desert Horse

The horse of the Sahrana desert is a distinct breed, smaller and leaner than
the horses of Aethelar but possessed of a stamina that no northern horse can
match. A Vashari desert horse can travel forty miles in a day under a rider,
carrying water and provisions, and can maintain that pace for a week or more if
necessary. The horses are fed on dates, barley, and dried grass, and they are
watered sparingly, a practice that conditions them to the conditions of the
desert.

The horses are trained from a young age to tolerate the noise and confusion of
battle. They are exposed to the sound of drums, the clash of weapons, and the
shouting of men, and they are taught to respond instantly to the pressure of
the rider's knees and the tug of the reins. A well-trained desert horse will
carry its rider into the midst of a fight without hesitation, and it will
stand quietly when the rider dismounts to shoot from the ground.

## Camel Mounts

Alongside the horses, the Vashari Pact fields a significant number of camel
cavalry. The camel is slower than the horse but far more resilient in the
harshest desert conditions. A camel can carry a heavier load, travel for days
without water, and subsist on the thorny acacia scrub that grows in the driest
wadis. Camel cavalry are used primarily for long-range patrols and for the
escort of merchant caravans, duties that require endurance rather than speed.

Camel riders are armed with the same bows and swords as the horse cavalry, but
their tactics are adapted to the capabilities of their mounts. A camel-borne
force advances at a steady pace that it can maintain for hours, covering ground
that a horse would find exhausting. When contact is made with the enemy, the
camel riders dismount to fight on foot, using the camels as a mobile
fortification behind which they can take cover.

## Arms and Armour

The standard equipment of the Vashari cavalryman includes a composite bow, a
straight sword, and a dagger, with a round shield of hardened leather for
defence. Armour is minimal, consisting of a padded jacket or a light hauberk of
mail for those who can afford it. The heat of the desert makes heavy armour
unbearable for long periods, and the Vashari have accepted that protection must
be sacrificed for mobility.

The composite bow used by the Vashari is a masterpiece of craftsmanship, built
from layers of horn, sinew, and wood glued together under pressure. The bow is
short enough to be used from horseback, but it delivers an arrow with enough
force to penetrate mail at close range. A skilled archer can shoot twelve to
fifteen arrows in a minute, maintaining a rate of fire that allows a single
troop of cavalry to keep up a continuous barrage.

## The Feigned Retreat

The signature tactic of the Vashari desert cavalry is the feigned retreat. The
cavalry advances on the enemy, exchanges a volley of arrows, and then turns and
rides away as if defeated. If the enemy pursues, the cavalry leads them into a
prepared position, where fresh troops are waiting in ambush or where the ground
has been chosen to favour the defenders. The feigned retreat has been used
successfully against enemies of every kind, from the heavy infantry of the
northern realms to the raiding parties of the Kharesh Tribes.

The success of the tactic depends on the discipline of the cavalry and on the
credibility of the retreat. The riders must appear genuinely panicked, dropping
weapons and equipment to sell the deception, and they must keep their
formation loose enough to suggest disorder while retaining the ability to
reform quickly. The feigned retreat requires practice and courage, for the
riders must trust that their commanders will not let the deception become a
real defeat.

## Training and Recruitment

Recruits for the Vashari cavalry begin their training in childhood, learning to
ride before they learn to read and to shoot before they learn to write. The
nomadic clans produce the finest riders, for their children spend their lives
in the saddle, but the oasis cities also contribute experienced horsemen who
have learned their craft in the competitive arena of the desert trade routes.

Formal training is conducted in camps established outside the major oasis
cities. Recruits are instructed in the care of horses and camels, in the use of
the bow from horseback, and in the tactics that the cavalry employs. The
training is hard and the standards are high, for the desert punishes
incompetence without mercy. A cavalryman who cannot shoot accurately while
riding at a gallop will be of little use in a fight, and such men are sent home
before they can endanger their comrades.

## The Caravan Escort

In peacetime, the primary duty of the Vashari desert cavalry is the protection
of the merchant caravans that cross the desert. The Pact's prosperity depends
on trade, and the caravans that carry goods between the oasis cities and the
ports of the coast must be protected from raiders. The cavalry escorts the
caravans along the most dangerous sections of the route, maintaining a watch
for raiders and responding quickly when an attack occurs.

The caravan escort duty is popular with the cavalry, for it offers the
opportunity to visit the oasis cities and to earn extra pay from the merchants.
It also provides valuable experience in desert navigation and in the
management of long journeys, experience that is directly applicable to
military operations.
