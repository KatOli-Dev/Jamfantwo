## The Market for Arms

Mercenary companies have been a feature of warfare in the known world since
before the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era) and have grown in
importance as the wealth of the established realms has increased. A
well-trained company of mercenaries can provide a ruler with experienced
soldiers who are ready to fight at a moment's notice, without the need to train
them or to maintain them in peacetime. The cost is high — mercenaries expect
regular pay, good provisions, and a share of the plunder — but for a realm that
faces a sudden threat or that needs specialised troops it lacks, the expense is
often justified.

The mercenary trade is regulated by custom and by the terms of the [Greywash
Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact), which established standards for
contracts, payment, and conduct. A company that violates its contract faces
sanctions from the signatories of the Compact, including the loss of the right
to operate in their territories. In practice, enforcement is uneven, and
companies that are strong enough to ignore the sanctions do so with impunity.

## The Iron Hall Company

The Iron Hall Company is the oldest and most prestigious mercenary force
operating in [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). Founded in the aftermath of the [Three-Banners
War](/content/history/three-banners-war), the company was originally composed
of veterans from the defeated armies, men who had no homes to return to and who
turned their military skills into a livelihood. The company grew rapidly,
attracting recruits from every realm on the continent, and it now maintains a
permanent strength of two thousand infantry and five hundred cavalry.

The Iron Hall Company is headquartered in a fortress in the [Crown
Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range), a position that allows
it to respond quickly to contracts in any part of Aethelar. The company is
governed by a Captain-General who is elected by the senior officers, and the
Captain-General holds authority over all aspects of the company's operations.
The company maintains its own armoury, its own training grounds, and its own
hospitals, and it is said that the discipline in the Iron Hall Company is
stricter than in many standing armies.

## The Sand Wolves

The Sand Wolves are a mercenary company that operates primarily in
[Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), though they have been
known to accept contracts in the [southern reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach)es of Aethelar as well. The Sand
Wolves specialise in desert warfare and are composed mainly of riders from the
[Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) and the [Kharesh
Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), supplemented by
adventurers from the northern realms who have adapted to the desert conditions.

The Sand Wolves are mounted on horses and camels, and they are armed with the
composite bows and curved swords of their homelands. They are employed
primarily as scouts, raiders, and flank guards, and they are valued for their
ability to operate in terrain that is difficult for northern infantry. The
company has a reputation for reliability that is unusual among [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana)-based
mercenaries, and it has served the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the [Amurra
Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), and the merchant
princes of the southern cities.

## The Free Spears

The Free Spears are a company of infantry that originated in the [Northern
Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) but now operates across
the whole of the known world. The company is known for its discipline and for
its willingness to take on the most dangerous assignments. The Free Spears
recruit from the hard frontier population of the Marches, and their reputation
for toughness is well earned.

The company is organised into four battalions, each of five hundred spearmen,
with a supporting force of archers and light cavalry. The Free Spears fight in
the northern style, with long spears and round shields, and they are trained to
hold their formation against cavalry charges and infantry assaults alike. The
company has served in every major conflict of the past century and has
established a record of reliability that commands high fees.

## The Black Coursers

The Black Coursers are a cavalry company that specialises in the shock charge.
They ride the heaviest horses and wear the finest armour, and they are used to
break enemy formations at the decisive moment of a battle. The Black Coursers
are expensive — their fees are the highest of any mercenary company in the
known world — but they have never failed to earn their pay.

The company was founded by a former knight of the [Southern
Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) who gathered around him a
band of dispossessed noblemen and ambitious younger sons. The Black Coursers
maintain the traditions of chivalry that their founder established, and they
observe a code of conduct that includes the humane treatment of prisoners and
the protection of non-combatants. This code has made them popular with
employers who wish to avoid the reputation for brutality that follows some
other companies.

## Contracting and Payment

A mercenary contract specifies the duration of the engagement, the payment to
be made, and the obligations of both parties. The company agrees to provide a
specified number of soldiers for a specified period, and the employer agrees to
pay a specified sum, usually in gold or silver, at specified intervals. The
contract also specifies what happens to plunder taken during the campaign,
with the typical arrangement being that the company keeps half and the employer
receives half.

Payment is a frequent source of dispute. Employers who are short of funds may
delay payment, and companies that are not paid may withdraw from the campaign
or, in extreme cases, change sides. The [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact) established that a
company that is not paid within thirty days of the due date may terminate its
contract without penalty, but companies that exercise this right often find it
difficult to obtain future contracts.

## The Risks of Mercenary Service

Service as a mercenary is dangerous, and the life expectancy of a common
soldier in a mercenary company is shorter than that of a soldier in a standing
army. Mercenaries are often employed in the most hazardous roles, and they may
find themselves abandoned by their employers when the situation becomes
difficult. The best companies take care of their wounded and provide pensions
for those who are crippled in service, but many companies do not.

The moral hazards of mercenary service are considerable. A company that serves
a tyrant, that commits atrocities, or that changes sides at a critical moment
acquires a reputation that may prevent it from finding future employment. The
most successful companies are those that balance the pursuit of profit with the
maintenance of a reputation for reliability and honour, a balance that is not
easy to maintain.
