## The Citizen Soldier

The legion of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic)
is the largest and most disciplined standing army in the southern regions of
Aethelar. It is a force of citizen soldiers, men who serve not for pay or
plunder but out of obligation to the Republic that grants them their rights and
protects their property. The legion is the embodiment of the Republican ideal:
that those who enjoy the benefits of citizenship must be willing to defend them
with their lives.

The Amurra Republic was founded on the principle that military power should be
vested in the citizenry rather than in a professional class of warriors or a
hereditary nobility. Every male citizen of the Republic is required to serve in
the legion for a period of six years between the ages of eighteen and thirty,
and he remains subject to recall until he reaches the age of fifty. This system
produces a force that is large, motivated, and deeply connected to the society
it defends.

## Organisation

The legion is organised into ten cohorts, each of approximately five hundred
men. The cohort is the basic tactical unit, capable of independent action and
of operating in any type of terrain. Each cohort is divided into five
centuries of one hundred men, and each century is divided into ten squads of
ten men. The squad is the smallest unit and is led by a sergeant who is
responsible for the training, discipline, and equipment of his men.

The legion as a whole is commanded by a Legate, who is appointed by the
Republican Council for a term of five years. The Legate is assisted by a staff
of tribunes, each of whom commands a pair of cohorts, and by a prefect of the
camp, who is responsible for logistics and administration. The command
structure is designed to ensure that the legate's orders are communicated
quickly and clearly to every soldier in the legion.

## Equipment and Weapons

The legionary carries a heavy throwing spear, the pilum, which is designed to
bend on impact, making it impossible for the enemy to throw it back. He also
carries a short stabbing sword, the gladius, which is used for close combat in
the shield wall. Body armour consists of a cuirass of metal plates, a helmet
of iron or bronze, and a large rectangular shield, the scutum, which provides
cover for the entire body.

The legionary is equipped for endurance as well as combat. Each man carries his
own provisions, cooking equipment, and tools for digging trenches and building
palisades. A legion on the march looks like a moving village, with each soldier
carrying the equipment he needs to construct a fortified camp at the end of
the day's march. The ability to build a defensible camp every night is one of
the hallmarks of the Amurra legion, and it has saved the legion from disaster
on more than one occasion.

## Training

Training in the legion is continuous and demanding. Recruits undergo a basic
training that lasts four months and that includes instruction in weapons
handling, formation drill, and the construction of camps. After the basic
training, the recruits are assigned to their cohorts, where they continue to
train alongside the veteran soldiers.

The training includes regular route marches of twenty miles or more, carrying
full equipment, to build the stamina that the legion requires. Weapons training
is conducted with wooden swords and wicker shields, using posts and dummies as
targets. The drills are repeated until the movements become automatic, so that
the legionaries can execute complex manoeuvres under the stress of battle.

## The Legion in Battle

The Amurra legion fights in a formation that is flexible enough to adapt to
changing circumstances. The cohort is deployed in three lines: the first line
of four cohorts, the second line of three, and the third line of three. The
first line engages the enemy, fighting until it is exhausted or until the
commander decides to rotate it. The second line then advances through the gaps
in the first line, taking up the fight while the first line reforms in the
rear. The third line is held in reserve, committed only when the battle hangs
in the balance.

The legion's tactical doctrine emphasises the importance of maintaining order
and cohesion. Legionaries are trained to hold their positions, to support their
comrades, and to advance and retreat in good order. The legion does not rely on
individual heroism but on the collective discipline of the formation. A legion
that holds its formation cannot be defeated, the Republic believes.

## Logistics and Camp Construction

The legion's logistical system is the most efficient in the known world. Each
legion is accompanied by a train of supply wagons and pack animals that carry
grain, fodder, and other necessities. The supply train is organised by the
prefect of the camp, who ensures that the legion is fed, equipped, and paid on
a regular schedule.

At the end of each day's march, the legion constructs a fortified camp. The
camp is surrounded by a ditch and a palisade, with gates on each side and
sentries posted at regular intervals. The construction of the camp takes about
three hours, and it is carried out by the legionaries themselves, who are
trained in the work. The camp provides a defensible position from which the
legion can operate and a refuge to which it can retreat if the battle goes
against it.

## The Reserve System

When a legionary completes his six years of service, he is discharged from the
active legion but remains liable for recall until he reaches the age of fifty.
These veterans form the reserve, a pool of trained soldiers who can be called
up in an emergency. The reserve system allows the Republic to field a much
larger army than its active legion alone would suggest, and it has been
instrumental in the Republic's ability to survive major crises.

The veterans of the reserve are required to maintain their equipment and to
report for periodic training. They are exempt from the taxes that other
citizens pay, a benefit that compensates them for the obligation of continued
service. Veterans who are called up serve in their original cohorts, where
their experience is invaluable in stiffening the resolve of the younger
soldiers.
