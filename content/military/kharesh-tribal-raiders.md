## The Raiding Tradition

The [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) of
[Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) are not a nation in the
sense that [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) or
[Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) are nations. They are a
collection of clans and kinship groups bound together by shared language,
shared customs, and a shared approach to warfare that has remained largely
unchanged since before the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The
raiding tradition is central to Kharesh identity. Young warriors prove their
worth by participating in raids, successful war leaders attract followers and
wealth, and the constant movement of raiding parties prevents any single clan
from accumulating enough power to dominate the others.

Raiding serves an economic purpose as well as a cultural one. The lands
inhabited by the Kharesh Tribes are marginal for agriculture, and the herds of
goats, sheep, and camels that form the basis of the tribal economy are
vulnerable to drought and disease. Raids supplement the tribal income with
captured goods, livestock, and slaves, and they provide a means of acquiring
resources that would otherwise be unavailable. The settled realms of the north
regard the Kharesh raiders as a menace, but from the perspective of the tribes,
raiding is a rational response to the conditions in which they live.

## Organisation of a Raiding Party

A Kharesh raiding party is organised around a war leader, a man who has
demonstrated his skill and courage in previous raids. The war leader attracts
followers through his reputation, promising them a share of the plunder in
return for their service. The size of a raiding party varies according to the
target and the resources of the war leader. A small raid might involve no more
than twenty warriors, while a major expedition could include several hundred.

The warriors who join a raiding party are drawn from the war leader's own clan
and from allied clans. They are armed with the weapons they possess and
mounted on the horses or camels that they own. There is no uniform equipment in
the Kharesh tradition; a warrior carries whatever he can afford or capture, and
the quality of his arms is a reflection of his status and his success. Older
and more experienced warriors are better armed than the young men who are
making their first raid.

## Weapons and Tactics

The typical Kharesh raider carries a composite bow, a curved sword, and a small
round shield of hide stretched over a wooden frame. The bow is the primary
weapon, used to soften a target before the charge and to cover the withdrawal
when the raid is complete. The sword is used for close combat, and the shield
provides protection against the arrows and spears of the defenders.

The tactics of the Kharesh raiders are built around mobility and deception. A
raiding party approaches its target by night or through difficult terrain,
using its knowledge of the desert to avoid detection. The attack is launched at
dawn, when the defenders are tired from a night of watches and when the light
is poor enough to conceal the number of the attackers. The raiders strike hard,
gather what they can, and withdraw before the defenders can organise an
effective pursuit. A prolonged fight is avoided, for the raiders have no
interest in a stand-up battle against a prepared enemy.

## Horses and Camels

The Kharesh Tribes raise two types of mount: the desert horse and the camel.
The desert horse is a lean, long-limbed animal that can carry a rider at speed
over short distances but requires careful feeding and watering. The camel is
slower but far more enduring, capable of travelling for days without water and
subsisting on the thorny scrub that grows in the driest regions.

Raiding parties use both animals in combination. The camels carry supplies,
water, and the plunder taken from the target, while the horses are kept fresh
for the attack. When the raiding party reaches the vicinity of its target, the
warriors dismount from the camels and transfer to the horses, leaving the
camels in the care of a small guard. After the raid, the warriors ride the
horses back to the camels and begin the journey home.

## The War Leader

The war leader of a Kharesh raiding party holds his position by consent of his
followers. He must demonstrate courage in battle, wisdom in planning, and
generosity in the distribution of plunder. A war leader who is seen as cowardly,
foolish, or stingy will soon find himself leading an empty camp, for his
followers will transfer their allegiance to a more capable man.

The war leader is responsible for the planning of the raid, for the discipline
of the party during the approach and the attack, and for the safe return of the
warriors to their homes. He settles disputes among his followers, decides how
the plunder is to be divided, and represents the party in negotiations with the
clans whose territory they cross. The position carries no formal authority
beyond what the war leader can command through his personal reputation, and a
war leader who fails too often will not be followed again.

## Defences Against Raiders

The settled realms that border the Kharesh lands have developed a range of
defences against tribal raiders. Watchtowers are spaced at intervals along the
border, and garrisons are maintained at strategic points where water is
available. Caravans are escorted by armed guards when they pass through
territory where raiders are active, and the merchants who trade with the tribes
pay protection money to ensure the safety of their goods.

The most effective defence against Kharesh raiders has proven to be a policy of
engagement rather than confrontation. Realms that establish regular trading
relations with the tribes, that offer legitimate markets for their goods, and
that employ tribal warriors as mercenaries in their own armies find that the
raids diminish in frequency and intensity. The tribes raid when they have no
other means of acquiring the goods they need; when trade provides an
alternative, the raids become less attractive.

## Relations with Other Realms

The relationship between the Kharesh Tribes and the settled realms is complex
and changeable. Periods of peace, during which trade flows freely and raiding
is suppressed, alternate with periods of conflict when the tribes are driven by
drought, population pressure, or the ambitions of a particularly effective war
leader. The tribes have no central authority with whom a lasting treaty can be
made, and the settled realms must deal with each clan and each war leader
individually.

Some of the most successful Kharesh war leaders have become figures of lasting
renown, their names remembered in the songs of the tribes and in the chronicles
of the realms they raided. Others have been forgotten, their raids unsuccessful
and their followers dispersed. The raiding tradition continues because it is
woven into the fabric of Kharesh society, and it will continue as long as the
tribes exist.
