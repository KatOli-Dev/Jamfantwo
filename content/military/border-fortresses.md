## The Guardians of the Frontiers

The borders of the realms of the known world are marked by fortresses that have
stood for centuries, guarding passes, controlling river crossings, and
watching the approaches to the settled lands. These fortresses are not merely
military installations; they are symbols of the power of the realms that built
them, and they serve as centres of administration, trade, and settlement. The
garrisons that man them are often the only representatives of central authority
that the inhabitants of the border regions ever see.

The distribution of fortresses reflects the strategic geography of each realm.
[Vael](/content/government/national/vael) fortifies the crossing points of its
great river. The [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach)
guards the passes through the [Thunderback
Range](/content/location/natural/feature/thunderback-range). The [Northern
Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) maintains a chain of
forts along the edge of the wilderness that lies beyond the settled lands. Each
fortress is designed for the specific conditions of its location, and each
presents unique challenges to an attacker.

## The Dwarf-Wall of Stoneheim

The most formidable fortification in the known world is the Dwarf-Wall, the
great barrier that guards the approaches to the [Stoneheim
Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds). The Wall is a continuous
rampart of dressed stone, forty feet high and twenty feet thick, that stretches
for fifteen miles across the mouth of the valley that leads to the heart of the
dwarf realm. The Wall is studded with towers at regular intervals, and its
gate is protected by a barbican of exceptional strength that forces any
attacker to pass through a killing ground swept by the defenders' weapons.

The Dwarf-Wall was built in the aftermath of the [Stoneheim Pass
War](/content/history/stoneheim-pass-war), when the dwarves determined that
they would never again allow an enemy to reach their homeland. The Wall was
constructed by dwarven masons over a period of thirty years, and it is said
that the cost would have bankrupted any human realm. The Wall has never been
breached, and the dwarves maintain that it never will be.

## Fort Vaelmouth

The Citadel of [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) is the
most powerful fortress in the river kingdom and one of the strongest in all of
[Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). Built at the point where the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) River meets the [Stormfall
Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea), the Citadel commands the
approaches to the river and controls access to the interior of the kingdom. The
walls are of dressed stone, thirty feet high, and are defended by twenty-seven
towers that provide overlapping fields of fire.

The Citadel contains a deep-water dock where river patrol boats can moor, a
garrison of fifteen hundred soldiers, and storehouses that can hold a year's
supply of provisions. The chain boom that stretches across the main channel of
the river can be raised to block the passage of enemy ships, and the
mechanism for raising the chain is protected by a tower that is itself a minor
fortress. The Citadel has never been taken by assault, and only one siege in
its history came close to success.

## The Thornwall

The Thornwall is the name given to the chain of fortresses that guard the
eastern border of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach)
against incursions from the less settled lands beyond. The Thornwall is not a
continuous wall but a series of strongpoints spaced at intervals of five to ten
miles, each within sight of its neighbours. The strongpoints are linked by a
patrol road that allows the garrisons to communicate and to reinforce one
another.

Each fort of the Thornwall is built on a hill or a rise, with walls of stone
on the exposed side and a palisade on the protected side. The forts are
designed to hold out for a month against a determined siege, and they are
stocked with provisions, water, and ammunition for that purpose. The garrisons
range from fifty to two hundred soldiers, depending on the importance of the
position.

## The Northern Chain

The Northern Chain is the line of fortresses that protects the settled lands of
the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) from the
raiders and wild beasts that come from the trackless forests and mountains to
the north. The Chain consists of twelve forts, each positioned at a strategic
point where the terrain channels movement through a narrow pass or across a
fordable river.

The forts of the Northern Chain are built of timber and earth, materials that
are abundant in the region and that can be repaired by the garrisons without
the need for skilled masons. The walls are of piled earth faced with logs, and
they are surrounded by ditches that are filled with sharpened stakes. The forts
are not designed to withstand a prolonged siege by a professional army, but
they are more than adequate to hold off the raiding parties that are the most
common threat.

## The Desert Keeps

The [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) maintains a
network of fortresses along the caravan routes of the [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) desert. These
keeps are built at oases, where water is available and where the caravans
must stop. The keeps serve as bases for the desert cavalry, as refuges for
travellers, and as symbols of the Pact's authority over the desert.

The desert keeps are built of mud brick, a material that is cheap and readily
available but that requires constant maintenance. The walls are thick, keeping
the interior cool, and they are topped with crenellations behind which the
defenders can shelter. The keeps are supplied with water from wells that have
been dug deep into the desert floor, and they store provisions that can sustain
a garrison for several months.

## The Siege of Border Fortresses

The reduction of a border fortress is a difficult and time-consuming operation.
The attacker must surround the fortress, cut off its supply lines, and then
breach its walls by bombardment, mining, or assault. The defender will use
every means available to delay the attacker, including sorties, night attacks,
and the destruction of the surrounding countryside to deny the attacker
supplies.

The siege of a border fortress often determines the outcome of a campaign. An
army that cannot take the forts that guard the enemy's border cannot advance
into the enemy's heartland, and an army that leaves enemy fortresses in its
rear risks having its supply lines cut. The great commanders of the known world
have understood this, and they have planned their campaigns accordingly.
