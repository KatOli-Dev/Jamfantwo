## Origins

The elves are accounted the eldest of the thinking peoples, and it may be so, for their bodies bear the marks of great age as a lineage. They are plainly a people of the forest, shaped in every feature by the deep woods of Aethelar, where their oldest and largest populations are found. Whether the first elves emerged from the forest itself, as some of their traditions hold, or came from elsewhere and were changed over long ages by the woodland they chose to inhabit, no naturalist can say with certainty. The antiquity of the elven line is supported by the great variation to be found among them, for a people of long standing tends to accumulate more differences between its branches than a younger one, and the elves show more such variation than any other sapient folk.

The relationship between elven origins and those of [humans](/content/humans) and [dwarves](/content/dwarves) is a matter of ongoing study. Comparison of their forms suggests a more distant common stock than that shared by humans and dwarves, as though the elven line parted ways from the others at a much earlier time. Some naturalists have proposed that the elven body reflects a life spent originally among the trees, citing the long limbs, flexible joints, and strong grasping hands that elves share with many tree-dwelling creatures. Whether the earliest elves lived chiefly in the canopy or upon the forest floor remains a subject of learned argument.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Elves are a tall and slender people, typically standing several inches above the average human, with long limbs and a natural grace of movement that other peoples often remark upon. Their bones are lighter than those of a human, thinner but more flexible, a quality that reduces the weight of the body and aids agility but offers less protection against violent impact. Their musculature is lean and built for endurance rather than sudden effort, suited to sustained activity over many hours rather than brief feats of strength.

Elven senses are the keenest of any people. Their eyes are large and well suited to the shifting light of forests, performing well in both the dappled brightness of the canopy and the deep shade beneath it. Their colour vision is remarkably acute, and they are said to perceive hues that other peoples cannot distinguish, a gift that serves them well in reading the subtle signs of plant life and forest health. Their hearing extends into registers higher than those audible to humans or dwarves, and they can detect faint sounds at a distance that seems improbable to other peoples. The pointed ears of an elf are not merely ornamental, for they are mobile and capable of subtle adjustment, turning toward the source of a sound to aid in locating it.

Elven skin is generally fair, varying from pale in the deep-forest folk to more tanned among the upland populations of Morvind. Hair is typically fine and straight, most commonly dark, though lighter shades are found among some northern groups. Elves move with a fluidity that other peoples find remarkable, and their reflexes are quick, qualities that reflect a body evolved for a complex and three-dimensional environment.

## Life Cycle

Elves are the longest-lived of the sapient species, with lifespans measured in centuries rather than decades. An elf who has seen three hundred years is not uncommon, and accounts of individuals living beyond five hundred are found in elven records, though the reliability of such claims is debated by scholars of other peoples. Elven maturation is slow, and physical adulthood is not reached until the second or third decade of life. Even in adulthood, elves do not cease to change, and their appearance continues to alter subtly over the centuries, though they do not decline in the manner of other peoples.

Elven women carry their young for approximately two years before giving birth, far longer than is the case among humans or dwarves, and births are almost always single. Elven births are very infrequent, and populations grow slowly even under the most favourable circumstances. This low fertility appears to be linked to the great longevity of the species, as though the same quality that sustains a long life also limits the rate at which new life is produced. Elven populations are typically small relative to the territory they inhabit, and they are vulnerable to decline when deaths among adults rise, for the losses cannot be quickly replaced.

## Habitat and Distribution

Elves are most closely associated with the ancient temperate forests of Aethelar, where their largest and oldest populations are found. These forests provide the complex, layered environment to which the elven body and temperament are best adapted. Elves favour mature woodland with a dense canopy and diverse undergrowth, and their dwellings are woven into the forest itself rather than carved from it, so that a traveller may pass through an elven settlement without knowing it.

Smaller populations are found in the temperate uplands of Morvind, where elves settle in the sheltered valleys and lower slopes of the interior ranges. These folk have grown somewhat stockier and more tolerant of cold than their Aethelaran kin, as might be expected of a people long settled in a harsher climate. In Sahrana, small communities are present in the warm and humid northern coastal regions. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region has in turn shaped the character of the elven peoples who dwell there, so that the elves of one continent are not precisely the same in form as those of another.

## Diet

The elven diet is chiefly of plants, reflecting both the forest they inhabit and the nature of their digestion, which is less capable of processing heavy animal flesh than those of humans or dwarves. Fruits, nuts, seeds, and leaves form the greater part of what elves eat, supplemented by fungi, roots, and small amounts of fish or game in some communities. Many plants that elves consume with impunity are mildly poisonous to other peoples, a tolerance that speaks to a long acquaintance between the elven body and the flora of the deep forest. Elven metabolism is slow and efficient, suited to a steady intake of varied plant foods and capable of sustaining long periods of quiet activity.

## Ecology

Elves occupy the niche of a long-lived and slow-breeding people within the forest, and their presence shapes the woodland over centuries in a manner that no other people can match. Forests long inhabited by elves are noted for their great diversity of plant and animal life and for the complexity of their structure, qualities attributed to the careful and sustained manner in which elves tend their surroundings. Unlike humans, who clear and replace, elves modify the forest gradually, and the results are often indistinguishable from wild woodland to the untrained eye.

Elves share their habitats with other peoples along the margins of the deep forest. In Aethelar, elven and [human](/content/humans) populations meet where the woodland gives way to cleared ground and river valley, with humans favouring the open land and elves the trees. In the uplands of Morvind, elven and [dwarven](/content/dwarves) ranges meet along the slope of the mountains, with elves occupying the wooded lower reaches and dwarves the bare heights above.
