## Origins

Elven tradition holds that the elves are the eldest of the thinking peoples, and that their ancestors walked the great forests of Aethelar before the rise of any other civilisation. Whether this claim is literally true is a matter that scholars of other species have occasionally disputed, but the antiquity of elven culture is not in doubt. Elven records and oral histories extend further into the past than those of any other people, and the great libraries of the elven settlements are among the oldest repositories of knowledge in the known world.

The elves themselves do not draw a sharp distinction between their mythical origins and their historical ones. To an elven chronicler, the transition from legend to record is gradual rather than sudden, and the early centuries of elvenhood are treated as a continuum in which the people and the forests grew together. This view is sometimes puzzling to [human](/content/humans) and [dwarven](/content/dwarves) scholars, who prefer to separate what can be verified from what cannot, but it reflects a deeply held elven conviction that their identity is inseparable from the land they inhabit.

## Characteristics

Elves are a tall and slender people, typically standing several inches above the average human, with a natural grace of movement that other species often remark upon. They are long-lived, with lifespans measured in centuries rather than decades, and an elf who has seen three hundred years is not uncommon. This longevity shapes every aspect of elven culture, from the pace of their scholarship to the character of their relationships with shorter-lived peoples.

Elven senses are keen, particularly in the domain of sight and hearing, and they are closely attuned to the natural environments in which they dwell. They are widely regarded as among the finest craftsmen, scholars, and musicians in the known world, and their work in wood, textile, and song is prized across every continent. Elves do not rush their craft, and an elven work may take decades to complete, but the results are enduring in both quality and reputation. This deliberation can appear as patience or as aloofness depending on the observer.

## Distribution and Settlement

Elves are most closely associated with the ancient forests of Aethelar, where their largest and oldest settlements are found. These communities are built in and among the trees, with dwellings and gathering places integrated so thoroughly into the woodland that a traveller may pass through an elven town without realising it. The forests of northern and central Aethelar support the greatest concentration of elven population, and these woodlands have been tended by elven hands for generations beyond count.

Smaller elven communities are found in the temperate uplands of Morvind, where they settle in the sheltered valleys and lower slopes of the interior ranges. These Morvindian elves have adapted to a harsher climate than their Aethelaran kin, and their culture reflects the influence of long winters and proximity to dwarven communities. In Sahrana, elves are present in several of the northern city-states, where they participate in trade and scholarship alongside humans and gnomes. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region has in turn shaped the character of the elven peoples who dwell there.

## Society and Governance

Elven society is organised around extended households and councils of elders, with decisions made through deliberation and consensus rather than through the authority of a single ruler. This is not to say that elven communities lack leadership. Elders of great age and experience are accorded significant respect, and in times of crisis a council may appoint a temporary leader with the authority to act swiftly. But the general preference is for patience and collective consideration, and elven governance moves at a pace that can try the patience of shorter-lived peoples.

The long elven lifespan means that changes in leadership are infrequent, and elven institutions tend to be stable over centuries. A council that has governed a forest community for two hundred years is not unusual, and its members may include elves who can remember the founding of the settlement. This continuity gives elven society a depth of institutional memory that other peoples cannot easily match, but it can also make elves slow to respond to new circumstances. When elven communities do change course, however, they do so with a thoroughness that reflects generations of reflection.

## Relations with Other Peoples

Elves maintain cordial but measured relations with most other peoples. Their long lives mean that they interact with several generations of humans, dwarves, and others within a single elven lifetime, and this can lend their dealings with shorter-lived peoples a certain detachment. An elf who has traded with the grandparents and parents of a human merchant may find it difficult to regard the current generation as strangers, yet the merchant will never have met the elf before. This asymmetry is a source of both warmth and misunderstanding.

Despite these differences, elves are respected across the known world for their scholarship and craft, and their communities are frequent hosts to scholars and traders of other species. Elven libraries and workshops are valued points of exchange, and many a human or dwarven scholar has spent productive years in elven company. Relations with dwarves have historically been more reserved, owing to differences in temperament and in the environments each people prefers, but the two species have found common ground in scholarship and trade often enough to maintain a working respect.
