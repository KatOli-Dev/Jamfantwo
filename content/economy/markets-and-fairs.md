## The Gathering of the Buyers and the Sellers

The market is the place where the buyers and the sellers gather to exchange the goods and the services that the economy of the known world requires. The market is a regular institution, meeting on the appointed day of the week in the market square of the town or the village, and the market is the principal point of contact between the producers of the region and the consumers who need the goods that the producers supply. The fair is a larger and a less frequent gathering, held once or twice a year in the larger towns and the cities, and the fair attracts the merchants and the buyers from the entire region and from the distant cities.

The market and the fair are regulated by the guilds and the councils of the towns and the cities where they are held. The guild of the merchants sets the rules of the trading in the market, the conditions under which the goods may be sold, and the fees that must be paid for the use of the market stalls. The council of the town grants the charter for the holding of the fair and appoints the officials who maintain the order and collect the tolls.

## The Weekly Market of the Town

The weekly market is the regular event that serves the needs of the population of the town and the surrounding countryside. The farmers bring their produce to the market, the craftsmen bring their goods, and the merchants display the wares that they have imported from the distant regions. The market is the place where the butter and the cheese and the eggs are sold, where the meat and the fish are offered, and where the cloth and the tools and the household goods are exchanged.

The market square is the centre of the town, and the stalls are arranged around the square in the pattern that the guild has established. The stalls of the butchers are set apart from the stalls of the fishmongers, and the stalls of the cloth merchants are set apart from the stalls of the ironmongers. The arrangement of the stalls is designed to make the inspection of the goods easier and to prevent the fraud and the deception that might arise from the confusion of the different trades.

The market day is the busiest day of the week in the town, and the population of the town swells with the influx of the farmers and the villagers from the surrounding countryside. The inns and the taverns are full, the streets are crowded, and the noise of the bargaining and the haggling fills the air from the morning until the evening.

## The Periodic Fair of the City

The fair is a larger and a grander event than the weekly market, held once or twice a year in the larger towns and the cities. The fair attracts the merchants from the distant regions, who bring the goods that are not available in the ordinary markets. The fair is the occasion for the trade in the luxury goods, the spices and the dyes and the silks and the precious stones that are the products of the distant lands.

The fair of Eldreth is the largest and the most famous of the fairs of the known world, held in the autumn after the harvest and attended by the merchants from every region of the three continents. The fair of Eldreth is held in the great market square of the city, and the stalls and the booths fill the square and the adjoining streets for a period of two weeks. The fair is the occasion for the exchange of the grain and the cloth and the metalwork and the spices and the dyes and the countless other goods that pass through the harbour of the city.

The fair of Khalisar is the second great fair of the known world, held in the spring before the sailing season and attended by the merchants from the Sahrani coast and the interior and from the northern cities. The fair of Khalisar is the occasion for the trade in the spices and the dyes and the precious stones of the Sahrani interior, and the merchants who attend the fair are the wealthiest and the most influential of the southern cities.

## The Organisation of the Fair

The organisation of the fair is the work of the fair court, which is the body of the officials appointed by the council of the city to manage the event. The fair court is responsible for the allocation of the stalls, the collection of the fees, the maintenance of the order, and the resolution of the disputes that arise among the merchants.

The fee for the stall is determined by the location and the size of the stall, and the fees that the fair court collects are the principal source of the revenue that the city derives from the fair. The merchants who attend the fair must also pay the toll on the goods that they bring into the city, and the toll is collected at the gates by the officers of the guild.

The fair court also has the authority to adjudicate the disputes that arise among the merchants, and the court of the fair is the place where the disputes about the quality and the price and the delivery of the goods are resolved. The court of the fair is a court of the special jurisdiction, and the judgments of the fair court are binding on the merchants who have submitted to the authority of the court.

## The Traders and the Merchants

The traders and the merchants who attend the market and the fair are the agents of the exchange that connects the producers to the consumers. The trader who stands behind the stall in the weekly market is a local person, known to the farmers and the craftsmen who supply the goods and to the customers who buy them. The merchant who attends the fair is a wealthier and a more distant figure, conducting the business of the long-distance trade that connects the regions.

The trader's knowledge is the knowledge of the local conditions, the prices that the farmers will accept and the prices that the customers will pay. The merchant's knowledge is the knowledge of the distant markets, the condition of the routes, and the prices that the goods will fetch in the cities of the coast.

The relationship between the trader and the merchant is a relationship of the mutual dependence, for the trader provides the merchant with the access to the local market and the merchant provides the trader with the access to the goods of the distant regions. The system of the fairs and the markets is the system that connects the local and the distant, the farmer and the merchant, and the producer and the consumer.

## The Regulation of the Trade

The guild of the merchants in each city regulates the trade that is conducted in the markets and the fairs, setting the rules for the standards of the quality and the weights and the measures and the prices. The guild of the merchants of Eldreth is the most powerful of the guilds of the known world, and the regulations that the guild has established are the model upon which the guilds of the other cities are based.

The guild also maintains the register of the bills of exchange, in which the names of the merchants who have dishonoured their obligations are recorded. The discipline of the register is the most effective mechanism of the self-regulation in the known world, and the merchant who is recorded in the register is excluded from the trade in every city that recognises the authority of the guild.

## The Fairs of the Different Regions

The fair of Goldmeadow is the principal fair of the Plains Confederation, held in the summer after the harvest of the grain and attended by the merchants from the entire region. The fair of Goldmeadow is the occasion for the trade in the grain and the wool and the leather of the plains, and the merchants who attend the fair are the representatives of the great merchant houses of the coastal cities.

The fair of Karhold is the principal fair of the mining regions, held in the autumn before the winter closes the passes and attended by the merchants from the Stoneheim Holds and the Karhold Realm. The fair of Karhold is the occasion for the trade in the metalwork and the minerals and the dwarf-forged steel that is the principal product of the region.

The fair of Amurra is the principal fair of the Sahrani coast, held in the spring and attended by the merchants from the interior and the coastal cities. The fair of Amurra is the occasion for the trade in the spices and the precious stones and the luxury goods that are the products of the Sahrani interior.
