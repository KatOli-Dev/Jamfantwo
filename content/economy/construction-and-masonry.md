## The Building of the Works of Stone and Timber

The construction trades are the means by which the dwellings, the temples, the fortifications, and the infrastructure of the known world are built and maintained. The mason who cuts the stone and the carpenter who frames the timber are the principal craftsmen of the construction trades, and their work is the foundation of the material civilisation that the peoples of the three continents have built. The construction trades are concentrated in the cities and the towns where the demand for the new buildings is greatest, and the guilds of the masons and the carpenters are among the oldest and the most respected of the trade guilds.

The materials that are used in the construction are the stone that is quarried from the hills and the mountains, the timber that is felled in the forests, the brick that is fired from the clay, and the lime that is burned for the mortar. The choice of the material is determined by the availability of the resources in the region and by the traditions of the builders who work in the region. The stone buildings of the dwarven holds, the timber halls of the northern peoples, and the brick houses of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast are each the expression of the resources and the traditions of the region in which they are built.

## The Quarrying of the Stone

The quarry is the place where the stone is extracted from the earth, and the quarrymen are the labourers who cut the stone from the face of the rock. The quarry is a pit or a cutting in the side of the hill, and the face of the rock is divided into blocks by the cutting of the channels with the picks and the wedges. The block that is separated from the face is then moved to the workshop where it is cut to the dimensions that the builder requires.

The stone that is used for the construction is of the types that are found in the rock formations of the region. The limestone is the most common building stone in the regions where the sedimentary rocks are exposed, and the limestone is easily cut and carved and is durable enough for the walls of the houses and the public buildings. The sandstone is used in the regions where the limestone is not available, and the sandstone is a softer stone that is easier to work but that weathers more quickly than the limestone.

The granite is the hardest and the most durable of the building stones, and it is used for the foundations and the lower courses of the walls where the strength is most needed. The granite is difficult to cut and requires the labour of the most skilled of the quarrymen, and the cost of the granite is higher than the cost of the limestone or the sandstone.

## The Mason and the Stonecutter

The mason is the craftsman who shapes the stone that the quarryman has extracted, cutting the stone to the dimensions and the shapes that the builder requires. The mason works in the lodge, which is the workshop where the stone is cut and dressed, and the mason's tools are the hammer and the chisel and the square and the level that the mason uses to measure and to shape the stone.

The ashlar is the stone that has been cut to the precise dimensions and the smooth faces, and the ashlar masonry is the highest quality of the stonework. The rubble masonry is the stone that has been used in the rough form, and the rubble walls are less expensive but less durable than the walls of the ashlar.

The mason also carves the decorative elements of the building, including the mouldings, the capitals, and the corbels that adorn the public buildings and the temples. The carving of the stone is the highest skill of the mason's trade, and the carver who produces the finest work is a master of the craft who is respected in every city where the building is undertaken.

## The Carpenter and the Timberwork

The carpenter is the craftsman who works in the timber, framing the roofs and the floors and the other structural elements of the building that are not made of the stone. The carpenter's tools are the saw, the axe, the adze, and the chisel, and the carpenter's trade is the second of the principal construction trades.

The timber frame is the structure of the building that supports the roof and the floors, and the frame is assembled from the beams and the joists and the rafters that are cut from the trunks of the trees. The joints that connect the timbers are the mortice and the tenon, and the pegs of the hardwood that are driven through the joints hold the frame together.

The carpenter also produces the doors and the window frames and the shutters that are the fittings of the building, and the work of the carpenter is the complement of the work of the mason. The carpenter who specialises in the fine joinery is a master of the trade, and the cabinets and the furniture that the joiner produces are the valued possessions of the households that can afford them.

## The Foundations and the Walls

The construction of the building begins with the excavation of the foundations, which are the trenches that are dug to receive the lower courses of the walls. The foundations are the most important part of the construction, for the building that is not properly founded will settle and crack and may collapse.

The walls of the building are the vertical elements that support the roof and the floors, and the construction of the walls is the principal work of the mason and the bricklayer. The wall that is built of the stone or the brick is a load-bearing structure that carries the weight of the building to the foundations, and the thickness of the wall is determined by the height of the building and the weight that the wall must support.

The mortar that binds the stones or the bricks together is a mixture of the lime and the sand and the water, and the mortar sets by the absorption of the carbon dioxide from the air. The quality of the mortar determines the strength and the durability of the wall, and the mason who mixes the mortar of the proper consistency is a craftsman of the experience.

## The Roof and the Covering

The roof is the covering that protects the building from the weather, and the roof is the principal work of the carpenter. The roof frame is the structure of the timbers that supports the covering, and the roof is pitched to shed the rain and the snow. The covering of the roof is the material that is laid over the frame, and the choice of the covering is determined by the availability of the materials in the region.

The thatch is the covering of the straw or the reeds, and the thatch is the most common roofing material in the rural regions where the straw is abundant. The tile is the covering of the baked clay, and the tile is the most common roofing material in the towns and the cities where the clay can be fired. The slate is the covering of the stone that is split into the thin sheets, and the slate is the roofing of the highest quality, used on the public buildings and the houses of the wealthy.

## The Public Works and the Fortifications

The construction of the [public works](/content/economy/public-works) and the fortifications is the largest scale of the building that is undertaken in the known world, and the guilds of the masons and the carpenters are the principal contractors for the works. The walls of the city, the bridges over the rivers, and the harbour walls of the ports are the structures that require the greatest skill of the builder and the greatest resources of the patron.

The city wall is the most ambitious of the construction projects that the cities undertake, for the wall must be high enough and thick enough to resist the assault of the enemy. The city wall of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) is the largest and the most complete of the fortifications of the known world, with the walls that rise to a height of thirty feet and the towers that project at intervals to defend the approach.

The bridge is the structure that carries the road over the river, and the building of the bridge is the greatest test of the mason's skill. The arch of the bridge must be built from the centre, with the stones supported by the timber centering until the arch is complete and the stones are locked in place by the weight of the structure. The bridge that is built by the masons of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) is the finest in the known world, with the arches that span the widest rivers and the piers that have stood for centuries against the force of the current.

## The Trades of the Building Industry

The building industry employs a range of trades beyond the masons and the carpenters, including the bricklayers who lay the brick walls, the plasterers who finish the interior surfaces, the glaziers who set the glass in the windows, and the roofers who lay the covering of the roof. Each of these trades is a specialised craft, regulated by its own guild in the cities where the trade is practised.

The bricklayer is the craftsman who builds the walls of the brick, and the bricklayer's trade is the most common of the construction trades in the regions where the clay is abundant. The plasterer is the craftsman who applies the plaster to the walls and the ceilings, and the plaster provides the smooth surface that is the base for the painting and the decoration.

The glazier is the craftsman who cuts and sets the glass in the windows, and the glazier's trade is the most specialised of the construction trades. The glass is made in the glasshouses of the cities, and the glazier who sets the glass in the church windows is an artist as much as a craftsman.
