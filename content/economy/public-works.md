## The Infrastructure That the Sovereign Provides

The public works are the structures and the facilities that are built and maintained by the sovereign or the council of the city for the benefit of the population. The roads and the bridges, the harbours and the breakwaters, the aqueducts and the fountains, the walls and the towers are the public works that make the life of the city possible and that enable the trade that sustains the economy of the region. The funding of the public works is the responsibility of the sovereign, and the revenue that is required is raised from the taxes and the tolls and the duties that the sovereign collects.

The public works are the most visible expression of the sovereign's authority and the most tangible benefit that the sovereign provides to the population. The wall that protects the city from the enemy, the bridge that carries the road across the river, and the harbour that shelters the vessels of the merchants are the works that the people see and use every day, and the quality of the public works is the measure of the sovereign's competence.

## The Walls and the Fortifications

The wall of the city is the most important of the public works, for the wall is the defence that protects the population from the attack of the enemy. The wall of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) is the largest and the most complete of the fortifications of the known world, with the walls that rise to a height of thirty feet and the towers that project at intervals to defend the approach. The wall is built of the stone, with the foundations that are sunk deep into the ground to prevent the undermining by the sappers of the enemy.

The maintenance of the wall is a constant expense, for the stone weathers and the mortar crumbles and the repairs must be made at the regular intervals. The guild of the masons of Eldreth is the contractor for the maintenance of the walls, and the guild employs a team of the masons who work on the walls throughout the year.

The walls of the smaller cities are less elaborate, and many of the towns are not walled at all. The town that is not walled depends on the militia and the local lords for the protection, and the town that is attacked by the enemy must rely on the defence that the houses and the barricades provide.

## The Harbours and the Breakwaters

The harbour is the facility that enables the maritime trade that is the foundation of the prosperity of the coastal cities. The harbour of Eldreth is the largest and the busiest in the known world, with the quays that can accommodate a hundred vessels at the same time and the warehouses that can store the cargoes that the vessels bring. The harbour is protected by the breakwater that extends from the headland, reducing the force of the waves and providing the sheltered water in which the vessels can lie at the anchor.

The maintenance of the harbour is the responsibility of the harbour guild, which collects the tariffs on the cargoes that pass through the quays and uses the revenue for the dredging of the channel and the repair of the quays and the breakwater. The harbour guild is among the most powerful of the institutions of Eldreth, and the master of the guild is a member of the council of the city.

The harbours of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast are smaller but no less important to the trade of the region. The harbour of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) is the principal port of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league), and the harbour has been expanded and improved many times over the centuries. The breakwater of Khalisar is a recent addition, built after the great storm of the century that destroyed the vessels that were anchored in the harbour.

## The Roads and the Bridges

The roads and the bridges are the public works that connect the cities and the towns of the interior to the coast and to each other. The roads of the plains are the most extensive, and the [Grassland Traderoad](/content/location/route/trade/grassland-traderoad) that crosses the plains from the foothills to the coast is the principal route for the transport of the grain and the wool and the leather that the plains produce.

The maintenance of the roads is the responsibility of the sovereign or the council through whose territory the road passes. The tolls that are collected at the gates of the cities are used for the repair of the road and the bridges, and the guild of the waggoners is the institution that monitors the condition of the road and reports the need for the repair to the authorities.

The bridges are the most vulnerable parts of the road network, and the bridge that is washed away by the flood is a disaster for the trade of the region. The bridge of [Goldmeadow](/content/location/settlement/city/goldmeadow) is the largest bridge in the known world, spanning the river that divides the plains in the two parts. The bridge is built of the stone, with the arches that rise high above the water, and the bridge has stood for more than three centuries without the failure.

## The Aqueducts and the Fountains

The aqueduct is the structure that carries the water from the source in the hills to the city where the water is needed. The aqueduct is a channel of the stone or the brick, running at the gentle gradient that keeps the water flowing, and the aqueduct is supported by the arches that carry the channel across the valleys and the depressions.

The aqueduct of Eldreth is the longest in the known world, running for the distance of more than forty miles from the springs in the hills to the cisterns in the city. The aqueduct is the work of the masons of the city, built more than five centuries ago, and the aqueduct still delivers the water that the population of the city requires.

The fountains are the public works that distribute the water to the population of the city, and the fountains of Eldreth are the most elaborate of the known world. The fountain in the market square of Eldreth is a structure of the carved stone, with the basins that catch the water and the spouts that pour the water into the buckets of the people who come to fill them.

## The Drainage and the Sewers

The drainage of the city is the public work that prevents the flooding and the accumulation of the waste that would otherwise make the city uninhabitable. The sewers of Eldreth are the most extensive of the known world, running beneath the streets and the buildings and carrying the waste to the river that flows past the city.

The maintenance of the sewers is the responsibility of the council of the city, which employs the team of the labourers who clean the channels and the traps that prevent the odours from rising into the streets. The council also maintains the registers of the connections that the households have made to the sewers, and the household that is found to have made the illegal connection is fined.

## The Funding of the Public Works

The funding of the public works is the principal challenge of the sovereign and the council, for the cost of the construction and the maintenance is high and the revenue that can be raised from the taxes and the tolls is limited. The wall of the city, the harbour, the aqueduct, and the sewers are the works that the sovereign must build, and the sovereign must find the silver that the works require.

The tolls that are collected at the gates of the city and the tariffs that are collected at the harbour are the principal sources of the revenue for the public works. The sovereign may also impose the special tax for the particular work, such as the wall tax that is imposed when the wall must be rebuilt or the harbour tax that is imposed when the harbour must be dredged.

## The Public Works of the Different Peoples

The dwarven peoples of the mountains are the most accomplished builders of the public works in the known world, and the dwarven holds are served by the roads and the tunnels and the drainage systems that are the most advanced of any region. The dwarven aqueducts are cut through the living rock, running for the miles through the mountains, and the water that the aqueducts deliver is the purest that can be found.

The halfling communities of the valleys maintain the public works that are appropriate to the modest scale of the settlements. The halfling road is a path of the packed earth, lined with the hedges and the ditches, and the halfling bridge is a simple structure of the timber that spans the stream.
