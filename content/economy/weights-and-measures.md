## The Standards by Which Value Is Determined

The weights and measures of the known world are the standards by which the quantity and value of goods traded in [markets and fairs](/content/economy/markets-and-fairs) are determined. The pound of flour, the yard of cloth, the gallon of ale, and the bushel of grain are the units by which buyer and seller agree on the amount exchanged for the silver paid. The accuracy and consistency of weights and measures are the foundation of honest trade, and the maintenance of standards is the responsibility of the guilds and magistrates in every city of any size.

The systems of weights and measures vary across the regions of the known world, reflecting the different traditions and needs of the peoples who developed them. Dwarven measures of the mountain holds are based on the length of the forearm and the weight of stone that can be lifted by the strength of the arm. Human measures of the plains are based on the length of the step and the weight of grain that can be carried in the hand. Centaur measures of the grasslands are based on the distance a horse can travel in a day and the weight a horse can carry on its back.

## The Pound and the Mark

The pound is the standard unit of weight in the trading cities of the known world, defined as the weight of a cylindrical piece of brass or bronze kept in the vault of the guild of money-changers. The pound is divided into sixteen ounces, and the ounce into twenty pennies. The weight of the penny is the standard used for weighing the silver that serves as the currency of trade.

The mark is a larger unit of weight, equivalent to eight ounces. The mark is used for weighing larger quantities of goods, such as grain, metal, and salt. Prices are quoted in [silver mark](/content/religion/silver-mark)s per pound or per bushel, and the merchant who buys goods must know the weight that the silver mark represents.

The standard of weight is maintained by the guild of money-changers, which keeps reference weights in the vault of the guild hall. The reference weights are used to calibrate the weights merchants use in the markets and fairs, and calibrations are conducted at regular intervals. A merchant whose weights are found inaccurate is fined by the guild and may be suspended from the practice of trade.

## The Yard and the Ell

The yard and the ell are the standard units of length used for measuring cloth, rope, and other goods sold by length. The yard is the length of the arm from shoulder to the tip of the middle finger, divided into three feet, and the foot into twelve inches. The ell is a longer unit, used for measuring cloth and equivalent to a yard and a quarter.

The standard of length is maintained by the guild of merchants, which keeps reference rods of iron in the guild hall. The reference rods are used to calibrate the yardsticks and measuring tapes merchants use in markets and fairs.

Measuring cloth is the most common of measuring transactions, and the cloth merchant who sells by length must have a yardstick calibrated to the guild standard. A customer who buys cloth may ask for the measurement to be made in the presence of a guild inspector, who will verify that the length is correct.

## The Bushel and the Gallon

The bushel and the gallon are the standard units of volume used for measuring grain, ale, and other goods sold by volume. The bushel is the volume of grain that fills a cylindrical container of standard dimensions, and the gallon is the volume of ale that fills a similar container of smaller size.

The standard of volume is maintained by the guild of brewers and vintners, which keeps reference vessels of brass in the guild hall. The reference vessels are used to calibrate the measures merchants use in markets and fairs.

Measuring grain is the most important of measuring transactions for the food supply of the population, and the bushel of grain must be measured with the accuracy that fair trade requires. A farmer who brings grain to market must have a bushel calibrated by the guild, and the merchant who buys the grain must use the same measure for determining the quantity.

## The Measures of the Different Regions

The measures of the Aethelaran cities are the most widely used in the known world, for the trade of the continent is the largest and most diverse. The pound, the yard, and the bushel of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) are the standards used in the markets of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation). A merchant who trades between the cities of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) uses the same measures in each city, and the consistency of measures is one of the foundations of inter-city trade.

The measures of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) cities are similar to the Aethelaran measures, but there are differences the merchant must know. The pound of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) is the same weight as the pound of Eldreth, for the two cities agreed on the standard of weight in the treaty that established the Northern Coast League. The yard of Khalisar is the same length as the yard of Eldreth, but the bushel of Khalisar is smaller, for the grain grown in the Sahrani highlands is lighter and the bushel must be adjusted to reflect the difference.

The measures of the dwarven holds are based on different principles and are not compatible with human measures without conversion. The dwarven pound is heavier than the human pound, and the dwarven yard is longer than the human yard. Conversion between dwarven and human measures is knowledge merchants who trade with the holds must possess.

## The Verification of Measures

Verification of weights and measures is the responsibility of the city magistrates and guild officers. The inspector of weights and measures visits market stalls and shops at regular intervals, checking the accuracy of the weights and measures merchants use.

A merchant whose weights and measures are found inaccurate is subject to a fine and confiscation of the false weights. A merchant found cheating customers through false weights is a defrauder of public trust, and the guild will impose a penalty commensurate with the severity of the offense.

A customer who suspects a merchant has used false weights may bring a complaint to the guild, and the guild officers will investigate. The guild maintains a register of complaints, and a merchant who is the subject of repeated complaints is subject to close scrutiny by the inspectors.

## The Role of the Guilds in Maintaining Standards

The guilds are the institutions that maintain the standards of weights and measures in the cities, and a guild's authority to regulate standards is derived from the charter granted by the sovereign or council. The guild of money-changers is the keeper of the standard of weight, the guild of merchants is the keeper of the standard of length, and the guild of brewers and vintners is the keeper of the standard of volume.

The guilds cooperate in maintaining the standards, for the trade of the city depends on the consistency of measures across different trades. The guild of money-changers and the guild of merchants hold joint calibration sessions at regular intervals, and the reference weights and measures are compared to ensure the standards are consistent.

## The Importance of Honest Measure

Honest measure is the foundation of fair trade, and the practice of honest measure is taught in every trading tradition. The [Silver Mark](/content/religion/silver-mark) teaches that the Truemarker is the witness of just measure, and that the merchant who uses false weights defrauds not only the customer but the Truemarker as well. The [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) teaches that the hearth of the honest household is the place where the measure is just, and the [Unwritten Name](/content/religion/unwritten-name) teaches that the honest naming of quantity is the discipline of the noun that the reader must attend to.

A merchant known for honest measure is trusted by customers and correspondents, and the reputation for honest measure is the most valuable asset a merchant can possess. A merchant known for false measure is excluded from the guild and the market, and the trade that merchant once conducted is lost.
