## The Standards by Which Value Is Determined

The weights and the measures of the known world are the standards by which the quantity and the value of the goods that are traded in the markets and the fairs are determined. The pound of the flour, the yard of the cloth, the gallon of the ale, and the bushel of the grain are the units by which the buyer and the seller agree on the amount of the goods that are exchanged for the silver that is paid. The accuracy and the consistency of the weights and the measures are the foundation of the honest trade, and the maintenance of the standards is the responsibility of the guilds and the magistrates in every city of any size.

The systems of the weights and the measures vary across the regions of the known world, reflecting the different traditions and the different needs of the peoples who have developed them. The dwarven measures of the mountain holds are based on the length of the forearm and the weight of the stone that can be lifted by the strength of the arm. The human measures of the plains are based on the length of the step and the weight of the grain that can be carried in the hand. The centaur measures of the grasslands are based on the distance that the horse can travel in the day and the weight that the horse can carry on the back.

## The Pound and the Mark

The pound is the standard unit of the weight in the trading cities of the known world, defined as the weight of a cylindrical piece of the brass or the bronze that is kept in the vault of the guild of the money-changers. The pound is divided into the sixteen ounces, and the ounce is divided into the twenty pennies. The weight of the penny is the standard that is used for the weighing of the silver that is the currency of the trade.

The mark is a larger unit of the weight, equivalent to the weight of eight ounces. The mark is used for the weighing of the larger quantities of the goods, such as the grain and the metal and the salt. The price of the goods is quoted in the silver marks per pound or per bushel, and the merchant who buys the goods must know the weight that the silver mark represents.

The standard of the weight is maintained by the guild of the money-changers, which keeps the reference weights in the vault of the guild hall. The reference weights are used for the calibration of the weights that the merchants use in the markets and the fairs, and the calibrations are conducted at the regular intervals. The merchant whose weights are found to be inaccurate is fined by the guild and may be suspended from the practice of the trade.

## The Yard and the Ell

The yard and the ell are the standard units of the length that are used for the measurement of the cloth and the rope and the other goods that are sold by the length. The yard is the length of the arm from the shoulder to the tip of the middle finger, divided into the three feet, and the foot is divided into the twelve inches. The ell is a longer unit, used for the measurement of the cloth and equivalent to the length of the yard and the quarter.

The standard of the length is maintained by the guild of the merchants, which keeps the reference rods of the iron in the guild hall. The reference rods are used for the calibration of the yardsticks and the measuring tapes that the merchants use in the markets and the fairs.

The measurement of the cloth is the most common of the measuring transactions, and the cloth merchant who sells the cloth by the length must have the yardstick that is calibrated to the standard of the guild. The customer who buys the cloth may ask for the measurement to be made in the presence of the inspector of the guild, and the inspector will verify that the length is the correct length.

## The Bushel and the Gallon

The bushel and the gallon are the standard units of the volume that are used for the measurement of the grain and the ale and the other goods that are sold by the volume. The bushel is the volume of the grain that fills a cylindrical container of the standard dimensions, and the gallon is the volume of the ale that fills a similar container of the smaller size.

The standard of the volume is maintained by the guild of the brewers and the vintners, which keeps the reference vessels of the brass in the guild hall. The reference vessels are used for the calibration of the measures that the merchants use in the markets and the fairs.

The measurement of the grain is the most important of the measuring transactions for the food supply of the population, and the bushel of the grain must be measured with the accuracy that the fair trade requires. The farmer who brings the grain to the market must have the bushel that has been calibrated by the guild, and the merchant who buys the grain must use the same measure for the determination of the quantity.

## The Measures of the Different Regions

The measures of the Aethelaran cities are the most widely used in the known world, for the trade of the continent is the largest and the most diverse. The pound and the yard and the bushel of Eldreth are the standards that are used in the markets of the Northern Coast League and the Plains Confederation. The merchant who trades between the cities of Aethelar uses the same measures in each city, and the consistency of the measures is one of the foundations of the inter-city trade.

The measures of the Sahrani cities are similar to the Aethelaran measures, but there are the differences that the merchant must know. The pound of Khalisar is the same weight as the pound of Eldreth, for the two cities have agreed on the standard of the weight in the treaty that established the Northern Coast League. The yard of Khalisar is the same length as the yard of Eldreth, but the bushel of Khalisar is smaller, for the grain that is grown in the Sahrani highlands is lighter and the bushel must be adjusted to reflect the difference.

The measures of the dwarven holds are based on the different principles, and the dwarven measures are not compatible with the human measures without the conversion. The dwarven pound is heavier than the human pound, and the dwarven yard is longer than the human yard. The conversion between the dwarven and the human measures is the knowledge that the merchants who trade with the holds must possess.

## The Verification of the Measures

The verification of the weights and the measures is the responsibility of the magistrates of the city and the officers of the guilds. The inspector of the weights and the measures visits the market stalls and the shops at the regular intervals, checking the accuracy of the weights and the measures that the merchants use.

The merchant whose weights and measures are found to be inaccurate is subject to the fine and the confiscation of the false weights. The merchant who is found to be cheating the customers by the use of the false weights is a defrauder of the public trust, and the guild will impose the penalty that the severity of the offense requires.

The customer who suspects that the merchant has used the false weight may bring the complaint to the guild, and the officers of the guild will investigate. The guild maintains the register of the complaints, and the merchant who is the subject of the repeated complaints is subject to the close scrutiny of the inspectors.

## The Role of the Guilds in the Maintaining of the Standards

The guilds are the institutions that maintain the standards of the weights and the measures in the cities, and the guild's authority to regulate the standards is derived from the charter that the sovereign or the council has granted. The guild of the money-changers is the keeper of the standard of the weight, the guild of the merchants is the keeper of the standard of the length, and the guild of the brewers and the vintners is the keeper of the standard of the volume.

The guilds cooperate in the maintenance of the standards, for the trade of the city depends on the consistency of the measures across the different trades. The guild of the money-changers and the guild of the merchants hold the joint calibration sessions at the regular intervals, and the reference weights and the measures are compared to ensure that the standards are consistent.

## The Importance of the Honest Measure

The honest measure is the foundation of the fair trade, and the practice of the honest measure is taught in every trading tradition. The Silver Mark teaches that the Truemarker is the witness of the just measure, and that the merchant who uses the false weight is defrauding not only the customer but the Truemarker as well. The Still Flame teaches that the hearth of the honest household is the place where the measure is just, and the Unwritten Name teaches that the honest naming of the quantity is the discipline of the noun that the reader must attend to.

The merchant who is known for the honest measure is the merchant who is trusted by the customers and the correspondents, and the reputation for the honest measure is the most valuable asset that the merchant can possess. The merchant who is known for the false measure is excluded from the guild and the market, and the trade that the merchant once conducted is lost.
