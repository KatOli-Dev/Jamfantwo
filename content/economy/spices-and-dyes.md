## The Treasures of the Sahrani Interior

The spices and dyes of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) are the most valuable single category of goods in the intercontinental trade of the known world, prized in the kitchens of the wealthy, the workshops of the dyers and the painters, and the treasuries of the merchants who deal in luxury goods. The spices of the Sahrani interior include saffron, cardamom, black pepper, and the rare sunroot, while the dyes include indigo, madder, and the crimson of the kermes insect. The trade in these goods is regulated by the merchant houses of the Sahrani coastal cities and the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the spice merchants, whose wealth and influence exceed that of any other commercial institution on the continent.

The production of spices and dyes is concentrated in the interior of Sahrana, where the climate and the soil of the desert oases and the river valleys provide the conditions that the spice plants and the dye-producing insects require. The spices and dyes are carried from the interior to the coast by the caravans of the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) and the [Sahrani Desert Trail](/content/location/route/trade/sahrani-desert-trail), the two great trade routes that connect the interior of the desert to the ports of the coast. The journey from the oasis gardens to the coastal markets can take weeks or months, and the cost of transport and the tariffs and duties that are levied along the routes add significantly to the price of the goods.

## Saffron, Cardamom, and Black Pepper

Saffron is the most valuable spice by weight that is traded in the known world, produced from the dried stigmas of the crocus flower that is cultivated in the irrigated gardens of the Sahrani interior. The crocus flowers are harvested by hand in the early morning of the autumn days when the flowers open, and the three red stigmas of each flower are carefully plucked and dried over a low fire. A single acre of crocus garden yields no more than a few pounds of dried saffron, and the labour of harvesting and processing the spice is intense and skilled. The saffron of Sahrana is recognised as the finest in the known world, with a deep red colour and a strong aroma that are the marks of proper harvesting and drying.

Cardamom is the second most valuable spice of the interior, grown in the shaded gardens of the oases where the ground is moist and the air is warm. The cardamom plant is a member of the ginger family, producing pods that contain the small black seeds that are the spice. The pods are harvested in the dry season, when they have reached their full size but have not yet split open, and they are dried in the sun before being packed into sacks for transport to the coast. The cardamom of Sahrana has a distinctive flavour that is sweeter and more aromatic than the cardamom of the other regions where it is grown, and the Sahrani cardamom commands a premium in the markets of Aethelar.

Black pepper is the most widely traded spice of Sahrana, consumed in every region of the known world where the people can afford it. The pepper vine is grown on trellises in the gardens of the oases, producing clusters of berries that are harvested when they begin to turn red. The berries are dried in the sun, turning black and wrinkled as the moisture leaves them, and the dried peppercorns are packed into sacks for transport. The demand for black pepper in the cities of Aethelar and Morvind is enormous, and the volume of pepper that passes through the ports of the Sahrani coast is measured in thousands of tons each year.

## Sunroot and the Depths of the Kharesh

The sunroot is the rarest and most mysterious of the Sahrani spices, harvested from the deepest parts of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert by the salawa guides who know the locations of the hidden groves where the sunroot plant grows. The sunroot is a tuber that grows beneath the sand, sending up a single leaf to the surface that is visible only to those who know what to look for. The tuber is harvested in the spring, when the plant has stored its energy for the coming season, and it is dried and ground into a powder that has a flavour unlike any other spice in the known world.

The sunroot is used in the preparation of the most elaborate dishes of the Sahrani cuisine, and it is also valued for the medicinal properties that the healers of the interior attribute to it. The price of sunroot in the markets of Khalisar and Amurra is higher than the price of any other spice, and the trade in sunroot is regulated by the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), which controls the access to the groves where the plant grows and the routes by which the harvested tubers are transported to the coast.

The harvest of the sunroot is a ritual that is conducted by the salawa guides in accordance with the traditions of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), and the locations of the groves are passed from generation to generation within the families of the guides. The sunroot is never cultivated, for the plant does not grow in gardens or plantations, and the supply of the spice is limited to what the desert yields naturally. The scarcity of the sunroot is the source of its value, and the merchant houses that deal in sunroot are among the wealthiest of the Sahrani traders.

## Rare Dyes and the Colours of Sahrana

The dyes of Sahrana are as valuable as the spices in the markets of the north, for the colours that the Sahrani dyers produce are brighter and more durable than the colours that the dyers of Aethelar and Morvind can achieve with the plants and insects of their own regions. The most valuable of the Sahrani dyes is the true purple that is extracted from the murex shellfish of the Sahrani coast, a colour that is reserved for the garments of the wealthy and the robes of the sovereigns and the high priests. The murex dye is produced by crushing the shells of the murex and extracting the fluid that turns purple when exposed to the light, and the labour of collecting the shells and processing the dye is intense and skilled.

The indigo of the interior is the second most valuable dye, produced from the leaves of the indigo plant that is cultivated in the irrigated gardens of the oases. The indigo plant is harvested in the summer, and the leaves are fermented and processed to produce the blue dye that is used by the dyers of the whole known world. The indigo of Sahrana is the finest that can be obtained, with a depth and a richness of colour that the indigo of the other regions cannot match.

The red dye that is produced from the dried bodies of the kermes insect is the third of the great Sahrani dyes, used for the dyeing of wool and silk and the painting of the manuscripts that the scribes of the [Reading Cloisters](/content/religion/cloisters-of-reading) produce. The kermes insect lives on the leaves of the kermes oak that grows in the foothills of the mountains of the interior, and the insects are collected by the women and the children of the villages that surround the oak groves. The dried insects are ground to a powder that yields a crimson colour when mixed with a mordant, and the crimson of Sahrana is the standard against which all other red dyes are measured.

## The Caravan Routes to the Coast

The spices and dyes of the interior are carried to the coast by the caravans of the desert, the long trains of camels and donkeys that travel the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) and the [Sahrani Desert Trail](/content/location/route/trade/sahrani-desert-trail) under the guidance of the salawa guides. The caravans leave the interior cities of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) in the cool of the winter months, when the crossing of the desert is least dangerous, and they arrive at the coastal cities of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) after a journey of several weeks.

The caravans are organised by the merchant houses of the interior and the coast, which finance the purchase of the spices and dyes and the hire of the camels and the guides. The caravans are protected by the armed guards of the Pact Guard, who accompany the caravans to defend them from the bandits and the raiders who prey on the trade. The cost of organising and protecting a caravan is substantial, and the price of the spices and dyes at the coast reflects the cost of the journey as well as the value of the goods themselves.

The arrival of the spice caravans at the coastal cities is an event that draws the merchants and the agents of the merchant houses of the whole known world. The spices and dyes are inspected by the officers of the guilds, who certify the quality and the weight of the goods before they are sold to the merchants who will carry them to the markets of Aethelar and Morvind. The sales of the spice caravans are conducted in the halls of the guilds of the spice merchants, and the prices that are set in the halls of Khalisar and Amurra are the reference prices for the entire intercontinental trade.

## The Spice Merchants' Guild and the Vashari Pact

The guild of the spice merchants of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and the guild of the spice merchants of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) are the two most powerful commercial institutions in Sahrana, for they control the trade in the most valuable goods that the continent produces. The guilds regulate the quality of the spices that are exported from the cities, maintaining the standards that ensure the reputation of Sahrani spices in the markets of the north. The guilds also set the prices at which the spices are sold, and the prices that the guilds set are the subject of negotiation with the merchant houses of the interior and the trading partners of the coast.

The [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the alliance of the interior cities that controls the caravan routes and the oases of the desert, is the principal regulator of the spice trade from the production side. The pact sets the duties that are levied on the spices and dyes that pass through the territory of the member cities, and the pact maintains the wells and the fortified stations that make the crossing of the desert possible. The relationship between the pact and the guilds of the coast is a careful balance of interests, with each side dependent on the other for the continuation of the trade that enriches both.

The regulation of the spice trade by the guilds and the pact has maintained the value of the Sahrani spices at a level that sustains the prosperity of the cities and the interior communities that produce them. The prices and livelihood of the people of Sahrana are closely tied to the success of the spice trade, and the fluctuations in the price of pepper and saffron and cardamom are the subject of the most intense attention in the markets of the coast.

## The Value of the Spice Trade

The value of the spice and dye trade is the foundation of the prosperity of the Sahrani coastal cities, and the revenues that the trade generates are the largest single component of the economy of the region. The spices and dyes that are exported from Khalisar and Amurra each year are worth more than the combined value of all the other exports of Sahrana, and the merchant houses that deal in the trade are the wealthiest institutions on the continent.

The trade in spices and dyes is not without its risks, for the crops that produce the spices are vulnerable to the droughts and the pests that afflict the gardens of the interior, and the caravans that carry the goods to the coast are vulnerable to the raiders and the bandits that operate in the desert. The merchant houses that finance the trade are accustomed to the risks, and the rates of return on the spice trade are high enough to compensate for the losses that occur in the bad years. The spice trade is the engine that drives the economy of Sahrana, and the prosperity of the continent depends upon the continued demand for the spices and dyes that the interior produces.
