## The Riches of the Ground

The mining of metals and minerals is the foundation upon which the material civilisation of the known world is built, for without the iron that becomes the tools of the farmer and the weapons of the soldier, the copper that becomes the coin of the merchant and the kettle of the housewife, and the silver and gold that become the currency of trade and the adornment of the wealthy, the economies of the three continents could not function in their present form. The mining industry is concentrated in the mountains of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the ancient forces of the distant past have deposited veins of ore that the miners have followed deep into the earth for centuries. The dwarven peoples of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) are the most accomplished miners and metalworkers in the known world, and the dwarf-forged steel that emerges from the forges of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) is the finest that can be obtained at any price.

The work of the miner is the most dangerous and the most demanding of the common occupations, requiring strength, endurance, and a willingness to labour in conditions that few others would tolerate. The wages of the miner are correspondingly high, reflecting the risk of injury and death that accompanies every shift in the deep workings and the scarcity of labour in the remote mountain settlements where the mines are located. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the mining towns regulate the conditions of work and the payment of wages, maintaining standards that ensure a measure of safety in the most hazardous of trades.

## Dwarven Mining in Morvind

The dwarven kingdoms of Morvind have mined the mountains for longer than the human chronicles record, and the depth and extent of the dwarven workings are the subject of legend and speculation among the surface-dwelling peoples. The [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), which occupy the central massif of Morvind, are the oldest and most extensive of the dwarven mining territories, with workings that descend to depths that are measured in thousands of feet and extend for leagues beneath the peaks. The mines of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) produce iron, copper, tin, silver, and gold in quantities that exceed the production of all the human mines of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) combined, and the dwarven smiths who work the metal in the forges of the holds are the most skilled in the known world.

The [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm), which borders the Stoneheim Holds to the south, is the second great centre of dwarven mining, though the mines of [Karhold](/content/location/settlement/city/karhold) are shallower and more recent than the workings of Stoneheim. The Karhold mines produce primarily iron and copper, with smaller quantities of silver and lead, and the metal from the Karhold mines is of good quality but does not match the purity and consistency of the Stoneheim ore. The [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) is also the centre of the human mining operations in Morvind, for the humans who work in the Karhold mines are employed by the same merchant houses that trade in dwarven metalwork, and the relationship between the human miners and the dwarven smiths is a close and productive one.

The dwarven mining technique is distinguished by the thoroughness with which the dwarves extract the ore from the rock. The dwarven miner does not take only the richest veins and leave the lower-grade ore behind; every particle of metal is recovered from the rock that is brought to the surface, and the waste rock that the dwarven miner discards is truly barren. The dwarves achieve this thoroughness by the use of fire-setting and water-quenching to crack the rock, by the careful sorting of the ore at the working face, and by the repeated crushing and washing of the ore to separate the metal from the gangue. The process is slow and labour-intensive, but the dwarven miner wastes nothing, and the long-term yield of a dwarven mine is far greater than the yield of a human mine of comparable size.

## The Danger of the Work and High Wages

The miner who descends into the deep workings of the Morvind mountains enters a world that is entirely unlike the world of the surface. The temperature rises as the depth increases, and the air grows thick and stale, requiring the constant attention of the ventilation engineers who maintain the flow of air through the shafts and tunnels. The rock above the miner's head is under enormous pressure, and the collapse of a tunnel is a constant risk that no amount of timbering and bracing can eliminate entirely. The water that seeps through the rock must be pumped to the surface, and the failure of a pump or a blockage in a drainage adit can flood a working section in a matter of hours.

The mortality among miners is the highest of any occupation in the known world, with deaths from rockfalls, floods, and the failure of the ropes and ladders that connect the levels of the mine occurring every year in every mining district of any significance. The miner who survives to old age is likely to suffer from the diseases of the lungs that are caused by the dust of the ore and the rock, and the coughing sickness of the miners is a familiar affliction in every mining town. The families of the miners are accustomed to the risk, and the death of a miner is mourned but not unexpected.

The wages of the miner reflect the danger and the difficulty of the work. The [prices and livelihood](/content/economy/prices-and-livelihood) in the mining towns are the highest in the known world, for the miner who works in the deep workings earns a [silver mark](/content/religion/silver-mark) every five days, a wage that is three or four times the wage of a labourer in the agricultural regions of Aethelar. The high wages are necessary to attract workers to the mines and to compensate them for the risk they bear, but they are also a source of the prosperity of the mining towns, for the silver that the miners earn is spent in the shops and taverns and markets of the settlements that serve the mines.

## Mining Towns and Their Character

The mining towns of Morvind are distinctive settlements, built on the slopes of the mountains at the entrances to the valleys that give access to the mines. The towns are dominated by the headframes of the mines, the tall structures of timber and iron that support the ropes and buckets by which the ore is raised from the depths. The noise of the stamp mills, where the ore is crushed to powder before the metal is extracted, is a constant presence in the ears of the inhabitants, and the dust of the crushers settles on every surface in the town.

The population of a mining town is a mixture of dwarves and humans, with a smaller number of salawa and other peoples who have come to the mountains in search of work. The dwarves occupy the upper levels of the town, closer to the mine entrances, while the humans live in the lower levels near the mills and the workshops. The division is not a rigid one, but it reflects the different roles that the two peoples play in the mining industry, with the dwarves providing the skilled labour of the deep workings and the humans providing the unskilled labour of the surface operations.

The mining towns are governed by the councils of the holds, which appoint the magistrates who administer the law and the wardens who oversee the safety of the mines. The guilds of the miners and the smiths have a substantial influence on the councils, for the mines are the source of the wealth of the holds and the guilds are the institutions that organise the labour of the mines. The relationship between the councils and the guilds is a close one, with the guilds providing the technical expertise that the councils need to regulate the industry and the councils providing the legal authority that the guilds need to enforce their standards.

## Iron, Copper, and Precious Metals

The metals that the mines produce are the raw materials of the economy of the known world, and the patterns of production and trade in metals are determined by the natural distribution of the ore deposits and the technological capabilities of the smelters and smiths. Iron is the most important metal, for it is the material from which the tools, weapons, and structural fittings of the world are made. The iron ore of Morvind is of the highest quality, with a low phosphorus and sulphur content that yields a strong and ductile iron that can be forged into steel by the skilled dwarven smith.

Copper is the second most important metal, used for coinage, for the manufacture of kettles and pots and brewing vessels, and for the alloying with tin to produce bronze. The copper mines of the Stoneheim Holds produce copper of high purity, and the dwarven smiths are skilled in the working of copper into sheets and wires and castings. The tin that is alloyed with copper to produce bronze comes primarily from the mines of Aethelar, for the tin deposits of Morvind are limited and the dwarven smiths prefer to import Aethelaran tin for the bronze that they produce.

Silver and gold are the precious metals that are the foundation of the [currency](/content/economy/currency) of the known world, and the mines of Morvind are the principal source of both. The silver mines of Stoneheim produce more silver than all the other mines of the three continents combined, and the silver from the Stoneheim mines is the reference standard against which all other silver is measured. The gold mines of the Karhold Realm produce gold in smaller quantities, but the gold of Karhold is of the highest purity and is sought after by the mints of the trading cities for the striking of the gold crown.

## Dwarf-Forged Steel and the Metalwork Trade

The dwarf-forged steel of Morvind is the most celebrated product of the mining and metalworking industries, and its reputation extends to every corner of the known world. The steel is produced by a process that the dwarven smiths have refined over centuries, combining iron from the Stoneheim mines with charcoal from the forests of Morvind in a furnace that reaches a temperature that the human smiths cannot achieve. The steel is then folded and forged and quenched in a sequence that is the closely guarded secret of the dwarven smithing guilds, and the result is a blade or a tool that holds its edge longer, resists breakage more effectively, and can be sharpened to a finer edge than any other steel in the known world.

The trade in dwarf-forged steel and steel products is the principal export of the Stoneheim Holds, and the value of the steel trade is a significant component of the economy of Morvind. The steel is transported from the forges of the holds to the coastal markets along the [Stoneheim Highland Road](/content/location/route/trade/stoneheim-highland-road) and the [Deep Realm Tradeway](/content/location/route/trade/deep-realm-tradeway), the two great trade routes that connect the interior of Morvind to the ports of the coast. The steel travels in the form of ingots and bars, which are then forged into finished products in the workshops of the coastal cities, and in the form of finished weapons and tools that are made in the dwarven forges themselves.

The price of dwarf-forged steel reflects the quality of the product and the cost of its production. A dwarven steel sword of the standard quality costs fifty silver marks in the markets of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth), a price that is ten times the price of a sword made from ordinary Aethelaran steel. The dwarven steel tool that is used by a carpenter or a smith in the workshops of the cities costs a correspondingly higher price, but the tool lasts longer and performs better than the ordinary tool, and the craftsman who can afford a dwarven steel tool considers it a worthwhile investment.

## Transport of Ore and Metal

The transport of ore from the mines to the smelters and of metal from the smelters to the markets is a significant logistical challenge, for the mines are located in remote mountain regions and the routes that connect them to the coast are difficult and dangerous. The [Stoneheim Highland Road](/content/location/route/trade/stoneheim-highland-road) is the principal route for the transport of metal from the Stoneheim Holds to the coastal ports, a road that follows the valleys and the passes of the mountains for a distance of more than two hundred miles. The road is paved with stone for its entire length, maintained by the guilds of the holds that depend upon it, and guarded by the patrols of the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) against the bandits and the raiders that prey upon the trade.

The [Deep Realm Tradeway](/content/location/route/trade/deep-realm-tradeway) is the second great route for the transport of metal, connecting the mines of the Karhold Realm to the markets of the south and the east. The tradeway is a tunnel road that passes through the mountains for a portion of its length, running through the deep galleries that the dwarven miners have excavated over the centuries. The tunnel road is maintained by the Karhold Realm, and the tolls collected at the gates of the tunnel are the principal source of revenue for the king of the realm.

The transport of ore and metal is conducted by the guilds of the waggoners and the carriers, who maintain the teams of horses and oxen that pull the heavy wagons along the mountain roads. The waggoners of the [Stoneheim Highland Road](/content/location/route/trade/stoneheim-highland-road) are among the most skilled in the known world, for the driving of a fully laden wagon along the narrow, winding road of the mountain pass requires a steady hand and a good knowledge of the route. The guilds of the waggoners set the rates for the transport of ore and metal, and the rates vary with the weight and the value of the cargo and the season of the year.
