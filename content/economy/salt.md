## The White Gold of the Sahrani Coast

Salt is the most essential of the commodities that the intercontinental trade of the known world transports across the seas and the deserts, for without salt the preservation of food for the winter and the long voyages of the merchant vessels would be impossible. The salt that supplies the markets of the three continents is produced primarily along the coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where the hot sun and the shallow waters of the coastal strip provide the conditions that the salt-pans require. The production and distribution of salt are regulated by the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court), an institution that has maintained the quality and the price of [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) salt for longer than any other commercial authority in the known world.

The importance of salt in the economy of the known world cannot be overstated. Every household requires salt for the preservation of meat and fish, for the seasoning of food, and for the preparation of the pickled vegetables that sustain the population through the winter months. The industries that depend upon salt include the fisheries that preserve the catch by salting, the tanneries that cure the hides that become the leather of the workshops, and the manufacturers of cheese and butter who use salt to preserve their products. The demand for salt is constant and universal, and the supply of salt is the foundation upon which the prosperity of the Sahrani coastal region is built.

## The Sahrani Salt Court

The [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court) is the oldest continuously operating commercial institution in [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), established in the first centuries of the intercontinental trade and maintaining its authority through every change of government and dynasty that has occurred on the continent since. The Salt Court is the regulator of the salt industry, with the authority to set the standards of quality for the salt that is produced on the Sahrani coast, to fix the prices at which the salt may be sold, and to adjudicate the disputes that arise among the salt-makers and the merchants who trade in salt.

The Salt Court sits in [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), in a building near the harbour that has been the seat of the court for more than four centuries. The court is composed of the master salt-makers of the coastal salt-pans, the merchants of the salt trade, and the representatives of the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council). The judges of the Salt Court are elected by the members of the court from among the most experienced and respected figures in the salt industry, and they serve for terms of five years before returning to their ordinary occupations.

The Salt Court maintains the standards of quality for Sahrani salt by requiring that every shipment of salt that leaves the coast be inspected by the officers of the court and certified as meeting the standard of purity and dryness that the court has established. The salt that passes the inspection is stamped with the seal of the court, and the sealed salt commands a premium in the markets of the north that the unsealed salt cannot match. The salt that fails the inspection is rejected and must be reworked or sold at a lower price in the local markets where the standards are less stringent.

## Salt-Pan Operations along the Coast

The salt-pans of the Sahrani coast are the most extensive and the most productive in the known world, stretching along the shore of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip) for a distance of more than a hundred miles. The salt-pans are shallow basins that are excavated in the flat ground near the shore, lined with clay to prevent the water from seeping away, and connected to the sea by channels that allow the seawater to flow in at the high tide.

The production of salt from the pans is a seasonal operation that depends upon the heat of the sun to evaporate the water and leave the salt behind. The pans are flooded with seawater at the beginning of the dry season, and the water is allowed to evaporate over a period of weeks, with the salt crystallising on the surface of the brine as the concentration increases. The salt that forms on the surface is skimmed off by the workers, who rake it into piles on the sides of the pans and allow it to drain and dry before it is collected and packed into sacks for transport.

The salt-pans are operated by the families of the salt-makers, who have worked the same pans for generations and who pass the knowledge of the craft from parent to child. The work of the salt-maker is hard and monotonous, requiring long hours in the sun and the constant attention to the state of the brine and the condition of the pans. The salt-makers are organised into a guild that is subordinate to the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court), and the guild sets the wages of the workers and the conditions under which the pans are operated.

The yield of the salt-pans varies with the weather of the dry season, and a season of heavy rain or cloud can reduce the production of salt by half or more. The Salt Court maintains reserves of salt that are held in the warehouses of the court against the years of poor production, and the reserves are released onto the market when the price of salt rises above the level that the court considers just.

## The Saltpan Watch and the Defence of the Pans

The [Saltpan Watch](/content/location/settlement/outpost/saltpan-watch) is the fortified outpost that guards the salt-pans of the Sahrani coast against the raiders and the bandits who would steal the salt or disrupt the production. The watch is maintained by the Salt Court, which pays the wages of the guards and the cost of the fortifications from the revenue of the salt trade. The watch is commanded by a captain who is appointed by the Salt Court, and the garrison of the watch includes a company of fifty men who patrol the coast and the pans.

The [Saltpan Watch](/content/location/settlement/outpost/saltpan-watch) is located on a low hill that overlooks the most extensive of the salt-pans, with a view of the coast for several miles in each direction. The watchtower of the outpost is a stone structure that was built in the early centuries of the salt trade, and the walls of the outpost enclose a compound that includes the barracks, the stables, the storehouses, and the well that provides the garrison with water.

The duty of the Saltpan Watch is to patrol the coast and the pans, to deter the raiders who come by land and by sea, and to report any disturbance to the Salt Court at [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar). The watch also maintains the signal towers that line the coast, which can relay a message from the pans to the city in a matter of hours by the use of fire signals and flags.

## The Importance of Salt for Preserving Food

The preservation of food by salting is the most important use of salt in the economy of the known world, for the salting of meat and fish and vegetables is the means by which the population of the cities and the towns is fed through the winter months and the long voyages of the merchant vessels are provisioned. The salting of fish is the largest single use of salt, for the fisheries of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) and the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) produce a catch that must be preserved within hours of being landed if it is to reach the markets of the interior in an edible state.

The salting of meat is the second largest use of salt, for the animals that are slaughtered in the autumn to avoid the cost of feeding them through the winter must be preserved by salting if the meat is to last until the spring. The salt that is used for the salting of meat is coarser than the salt that is used for the table, and the salt-makers of Sahrana produce a variety of grades of salt that are suited to different uses.

The preservation of vegetables by pickling is the third large use of salt, for the pickled vegetables are a staple of the diet in the winter months when fresh vegetables are not available. The pickling of vegetables requires a brine of a specific concentration, and the salt that is used for the purpose must be pure and free of the impurities that would spoil the pickle.

## The Price of Salt across the Regions

The price of salt varies across the three continents as widely as the distance from the Sahrani coast and the cost of transport along the routes that carry the salt to the interior markets. The salt that is produced on the Sahrani coast is the cheapest in the world at the point of production, for the salt-pans are the most productive and the labour of the salt-makers is the most efficient. A basket of salt of the standard weight of fifty pounds costs one [silver mark](/content/religion/silver-mark) in the markets of Khalisar, where the salt is landed from the pans.

The price of salt rises with the distance from the coast, for the cost of transporting the salt by camel and by wagon and by ship adds to the price at each stage. A basket of salt of the same weight costs two silver marks in the interior towns of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), where the salt has been carried by caravan along the [Sahrani Desert Trail](/content/location/route/trade/sahrani-desert-trail) at a cost that adds significantly to the price. The same basket of salt costs three silver marks in the mountain towns of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the salt has been shipped across the [Auric Passage](/content/location/route/trade/auric-passage) and carried inland along the trade routes of the continent.

The [prices and livelihood](/content/economy/prices-and-livelihood) of the common people in the mountain towns and the interior regions are significantly affected by the price of salt, for the cost of preserving food for the winter is a substantial component of the household budget. The families of the poor in the mountain towns feel the price of salt more keenly than the families of the wealthy, for the wealthy can afford to buy the more expensive cuts of fresh meat throughout the year while the poor must rely on the salted meat and fish that require the salt for their preservation.

## Salt Trade Routes and Distribution

The distribution of salt from the Sahrani coast to the markets of the three continents is conducted by the merchant houses that specialise in the salt trade, working under the authority of the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court) and the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the salt merchants. The salt is transported from the pans to the warehouses of the salt merchants in Khalisar, where it is inspected and certified by the officers of the court before being packed into the sacks and the barrels that will carry it to the markets.

The salt that is destined for the markets of Aethelar is shipped across the [Auric Passage](/content/location/route/trade/auric-passage) aboard the vessels of the salt merchants, who carry the salt to the ports of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) and the other coastal cities of the continent. The salt that is destined for the markets of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) is carried through the [Velden Strait Run](/content/location/route/trade/velden-strait-run) to the ports of [Nordhaven](/content/location/settlement/city/nordhaven) and the other northern harbours. The salt that is destined for the interior markets of Sahrana is carried by caravan along the [Sahrani Desert Trail](/content/location/route/trade/sahrani-desert-trail) and the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) to the towns of the [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) Pact and the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes).

The [trade and tariffs](/content/economy/trade-and-tariffs) that are levied on the salt as it passes through the ports and the frontiers of the different jurisdictions are a significant component of the final price of the salt in the interior markets. The tariffs on salt are a source of revenue for the sovereigns and the councils that levy them, and the rates of the salt tariffs are a matter of constant negotiation between the salt merchants and the authorities of the regions through which the salt passes.
