## The Production of Fermented and Distilled Beverages

The brewing of ale and beer and the distilling of spirits are among the most widespread of the manufacturing trades, for the consumption of alcoholic beverages is a universal feature of the cultures of the known world. The ale and the beer that are brewed from grain are the common drink of the temperate regions, while the wine that is fermented from grapes is the principal beverage of the warmer climates. The spirits that are distilled from the fermented liquors are the strong drink of every region, and the trade in spirits is a significant component of the economy of the towns and the cities where the distillation is practised.

The brewing and the distilling trades are regulated by the guilds in every city of any size, and the standards that the guilds maintain are the guarantee of the quality of the products that the brewers and the distillers produce. The guild of the brewers and the vintners sets the standards of quality for the ale and the wine that are sold in the inns and the taverns, and the guild of the distillers regulates the production of the spirits that are traded in the markets.

## The Brewing of Ale and Beer

The brewing of ale is the most ancient of the fermentation trades, practised in the households of the temperate regions since before the first cities were founded. The ale is brewed from the grain that the farmers produce, most commonly barley, though wheat and oats are also used in the regions where the barley is less abundant. The grain is malted by steeping it in water and then allowing it to germinate, which develops the enzymes that will convert the starches of the grain into the sugars that the fermentation requires.

The malted grain is dried and then crushed in the mill, and the crushed malt is mixed with hot water in the mash tun, where the enzymes convert the starches into sugars. The liquid that results is called the wort, and the wort is boiled in the copper with the addition of the hops that give the ale its characteristic bitterness and preserve the ale against spoilage. The boiled wort is cooled and then fermented by the addition of the yeast, which converts the sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide.

The fermentation of the ale is conducted in the fermenting vessels that are kept in the cellars of the brewery, where the temperature is cool and stable. The ale is left to ferment for a period of several days, and the yeast rises to the surface and is skimmed off for use in the next batch. The ale that has finished fermenting is then racked into the casks and the barrels, where it is left to mature for a period of weeks or months before it is sold.

The quality of the ale is determined by the quality of the grain, the skill of the brewer in the mashing and the boiling, and the condition of the yeast that is used for the fermentation. The ale that is brewed in the towns of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) is the most highly regarded in the known world, for the grain of the plains is of the finest quality and the brewers of the plains are the most skilled in the trade.

## The Brewing of the Hops and the Herbs

The use of hops in the brewing of ale is the practice that distinguishes the ale of the present age from the older forms of the beverage. The hop is a climbing plant that is cultivated in the hop gardens of the temperate regions, and the flowers of the hop are dried and added to the wort in the boiling. The hop imparts a bitterness that balances the sweetness of the malt and a flavour and an aroma that are distinctive to the variety of the hop that is used.

The hop is also a preservative, and the ale that is hopped will keep for longer than the ale that is brewed without the hop. The hop is the principal ingredient that makes the export of ale possible, for the unhopped ale would spoil before it could be transported from the region of the brewing to the distant markets.

The herbs that are used in the brewing of the older forms of ale are still used in the regions where the hop is not cultivated. The herbs that are used include the yarrow, the bog myrtle, and the heather, and the ale that is brewed with these herbs has a flavour and a character that are distinct from the hopped ale. The herb ales are the traditional beverages of the rural regions, and the brewers who produce them are the keepers of the older traditions of the trade.

## The Production of Wine

The production of wine begins with the cultivation of the vine in the vineyards of the warm and the temperate regions. The vine requires a well-drained soil and a sunny aspect, and the vineyards that produce the finest wines are planted on the south-facing slopes where the sun reaches for the longest part of the day. The cultivation of the vine is a labour-intensive process that requires the pruning of the branches, the training of the shoots along the supports, and the careful management of the ripening of the grapes.

The harvest of the grapes is the critical period of the wine-making year, for the grapes must be picked at the moment of the peak of the ripeness to produce the finest wine. The grapes are picked by the vintagers, who carry the baskets of the grapes to the press where the juice is extracted. The press is a heavy beam that is lowered onto the grapes, crushing them and releasing the juice that will become the wine.

The juice of the grapes is set to ferment in the vats and the casks that are kept in the cellars of the wine-making establishment. The fermentation of the wine is conducted by the yeasts that are present on the skins of the grapes, and the sugar of the grape juice is converted into alcohol over a period of weeks. The wine that has finished fermenting is then racked into the casks where it is left to mature for a period of months or years, developing the complexity of the flavour and the aroma that characterise the finest wines.

The regions of the known world that produce the finest wines include the river valleys of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the climate and the soil are ideally suited to the cultivation of the vine, and the coastal hills of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where the terraced vineyards produce wines of a character that is distinct from the wines of the north. The wine trade is a significant component of the commerce of the regions that produce it, and the wine merchants who export the wines of the valleys to the markets of the cities are among the wealthiest of the traders.

## The Distillation of Spirits

The distillation of spirits is a more recent development than the brewing of ale and the making of wine, and the knowledge of the distillation is concentrated in the towns and the cities where the trade is practised. The still is the principal tool of the distiller, and the design of the still determines the quality and the character of the spirit that is produced.

The distiller begins with a fermented liquor, which may be the ale that is brewed from the grain or the wine that is fermented from the grapes or the fruit. The liquor is heated in the still, and the alcohol that evaporates at a lower temperature than the water is collected and condensed into the spirit. The spirit that is collected from the first distillation is called the low wine, and it is distilled again to produce the spirit of the higher strength and the greater purity.

The spirits that are produced by the distillation are consumed in every region of the known world, and the trade in spirits is one of the most lucrative of the trades that are practised in the cities. The spirits that are produced from the grain include the whiskey of the northern regions and the vodka of the eastern plains. The spirits that are produced from the wine include the brandy that is distilled in the wine-producing regions and the fortified wines that are produced by the addition of the spirit to the wine.

## The Regulation of the Trade

The brewing and the distilling trades are regulated by the guilds in every city of any size, and the standards that the guilds maintain are the guarantee of the quality of the products. The guild of the brewers and the vintners sets the standards for the quality of the ale and the wine that are sold in the inns and the taverns, and the guild of the distillers sets the standards for the quality of the spirits that are traded in the markets.

The guild also regulates the prices at which the ale and the wine and the spirits may be sold, protecting the consumer from the excessive charges that the unscrupulous producer might demand. The guild of the brewers and the vintners of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) is one of the oldest and the most respected of the guilds of the trade, and the standards that the guild has maintained for more than two centuries are the model upon which the guilds of the other cities are built.

The inns and the taverns are the principal points of sale for the ale and the wine and the spirits that the brewers and the distillers produce. The innkeeper purchases the beverages from the brewers and the distillers and sells them to the customers who come to the inn for the refreshment and the accommodation. The trade of the innkeeper is regulated by the guild of the innkeepers, and the standards of quality that the innkeeper maintains are the measure of the inn's reputation.

## The Social Role of the Brewing and the Distilling

The consumption of the ale and the wine and the spirits is a social practice that is woven into the fabric of the life of the communities. The gathering at the inn or the tavern after the day's work is the occasion for the exchange of the news and the discussion of the affairs of the community, and the ale that is consumed in the gathering is the lubricant of the conversation.

The festivals and the celebrations of the communities are marked by the consumption of the ale and the wine and the spirits, and the brewing of the special ale for the festival is a tradition that is observed in every region. The ale that is brewed for the harvest festival in the plains, the wine that is opened for the vintage celebration in the vineyards, and the spirits that are distilled for the winter solstice in the northern regions are each a part of the traditions that the people observe.
