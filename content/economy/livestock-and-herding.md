## The Keeping of Beasts for Use and Sustenance

The keeping of livestock is practised in every settled region of the known world, for the animals that the farmer and the herder maintain provide meat, milk, wool, leather, and labour in forms that the cultivation of crops alone cannot supply. The types of livestock kept and the methods by which they are managed vary with the climate, the terrain, and the traditions of the peoples who keep them. The cattle of the plains, the sheep of the highlands, the goats of the rocky hills, the pigs of the woodlands, and the horses that serve as mounts and draught animals each belong to a system of management that has developed over centuries of experience.

The herding of livestock is a way of life as much as a trade, and the peoples who practise it are among the most distinctive in the known world. The centaur kindreds of the Great Grass Plains manage their herds in a pattern of seasonal movement that has shaped the grasslands for as long as any people can remember. The human ranchers of the Plains Confederation keep their cattle in fenced pastures and drive them to the markets of the river towns along the drove roads that cross the plains. The dwarven goatherds of the mountain slopes maintain their flocks in the high pastures of the summer and the shelter of the lower valleys in the winter.

## Cattle and the Economy of the Plains

Cattle are the most important livestock animal in the known world, providing meat, milk, leather, and the draught power that the farmers need for the ploughing of the fields. The cattle of the Great Grass Plains are a hardy breed, adapted to the harsh conditions of the continental climate and capable of surviving the winter on the grass that the summer has dried and preserved. The cattle are kept in herds of several hundred head, managed by the ranchers and the herders who know the condition of each animal and the state of the pastures.

The cattle of the plains are driven to market along the established drove roads, which are routes that have been used for centuries for the movement of livestock from the interior to the coastal cities. The drove road is a wide track of beaten earth, marked by the passage of countless hooves and lined with the watering places and the grazing grounds where the herds may rest on the journey. The drovers who accompany the herds are skilled in the management of the cattle, and the journey from the plains to the coast may take several weeks, depending on the distance and the condition of the animals.

The trade in cattle is a significant component of the economy of the Plains Confederation, and the cattle markets of Goldmeadow and the other river towns are among the busiest in the known world. The price of cattle is determined by the condition of the animals, the season of the year, and the demand in the coastal markets, and the merchants who deal in cattle are among the wealthiest of the plains. The cattle that are not sold for meat are kept for breeding and for the production of milk and leather, and the by-products of the cattle trade are as valuable as the meat itself.

## The Management of Sheep and the Wool Trade

Sheep are kept in the highlands and the hill country of Aethelar and the uplands of Morvind, where the terrain is too steep and the soil too thin for the cultivation of crops. The sheep of the highlands are a hardy breed, capable of surviving the cold winters and the poor grazing of the hills, and they are valued for their wool and their meat. The wool of the highland sheep is of the finest quality, and the cloth that is woven from it is the most sought-after textile in the known world.

The management of sheep is a seasonal cycle that follows the growth of the grass in the hills. The sheep are kept in the lower pastures during the winter, where they are fed on hay and the dried grass of the summer. In the spring, the sheep are moved to the higher pastures, where the new grass is rich and plentiful. The herders who accompany the sheep live in the hills through the summer, moving the flocks from pasture to pasture as the grass is consumed.

The shearing of the sheep is conducted in the late spring, after the weather has warmed and the sheep have shed the heaviest of their winter wool. The wool is washed and combed and spun into thread that is then woven into cloth in the workshops of the wool towns. The wool trade is the principal economic activity of the highland regions, and the wool merchants of the hill towns are the link between the herders of the hills and the weavers of the cities.

The sheep of the Sahrani highlands are a different breed, adapted to the drier conditions and the harsher climate of the interior. The wool of the Sahrani sheep is coarser than the wool of the Aethelaran sheep, but it is hard-wearing and well suited to the making of the cloaks and the blankets that the people of the highlands need for the cold nights of the desert. The sheep of Sahrana are also valued for their meat and their milk, and the cheese that is made from the milk of the Sahrani sheep is a delicacy that is traded along the caravan routes.

## The Versatile Goat

Goats are kept in the rocky regions of the known world where the terrain is too rough for cattle and the grazing is too sparse for sheep. The goat is a versatile animal that can survive on vegetation that other livestock cannot eat, and the goat's milk is a valuable source of nutrition in the regions where the soil is poor and the crops are uncertain. The goats of the mountain regions and the desert fringes are the principal livestock of the peoples who inhabit those regions, and the goat is as important to the economy of the highlands and the deserts as the cattle are to the economy of the plains.

The goat produces milk, meat, and leather, and the hair of the goat is used for the making of rope and the weaving of coarse cloth. The goat is also a pack animal in the regions where the terrain is too steep for the wheeled cart, and the caravans that cross the mountain passes of Morvind are accompanied by trains of goats that carry the goods that the merchants have brought from the coast.

The herding of goats is practised by the salawa peoples of the Kharesh desert, who maintain flocks of goats in the wadis and the oases where the vegetation is sufficient to support them. The salawa herders are skilled in the management of the goats, moving them from one grazing ground to another as the season and the availability of water permit. The goat is the principal source of wealth for the salawa, and the trade in goat products is the foundation of the economy of the desert peoples.

## Pigs and the Woodland Economy

Pigs are kept in the woodland regions of the known world, where the forests provide the mast and the roots and the fungi that the pigs eat. The pig is a prolific animal, producing a large litter of piglets twice a year, and the meat of the pig is an important source of protein in the diets of the woodland peoples. The pig is also a source of leather and of the bristles that are used in the making of brushes.

The management of pigs is less intensive than the management of cattle and sheep, for the pig is capable of foraging for its own food in the woodland and requires only the minimum of attention from the keeper. The pigs are allowed to range through the forest, returning to the settlement at the evening to be fed and to be secured against the predators of the night. The keeper of the pigs is responsible for the health of the animals and for the management of the breeding, but the daily work of the pig keeper is less demanding than the work of the cattle herder or the shepherd.

The trade in pigs and pork products is concentrated in the woodland regions of Aethelar and the forested valleys of Morvind. The pigs are slaughtered in the autumn, after the mast has fattened them, and the meat is preserved by salting and smoking for the consumption of the winter. The bacon and the ham of the woodland regions are traded to the coastal cities, where they are sold in the markets at a price that reflects the quality of the meat and the cost of the transport.

## Horses and the Draught Animal Economy

Horses are the most valuable of the livestock animals, for they serve as mounts for the riders of the plains and the cavalry of the armies and as draught animals for the plough and the wagon. The horses of the Great Grass Plains are the finest in the known world, bred by the centaur kindreds and the human ranchers of the Plains Confederation for speed and endurance and the steadiness of temperament that the rider requires.

The breeding of horses is a specialised trade, requiring the knowledge of the bloodlines and the conformation of the animals that the ordinary livestock keeper does not possess. The horse breeders of the plains maintain stud farms where the stallions are kept and the mares are covered, and the foals that result are raised with the care that the value of the animal demands. The horse trade is the most lucrative of the livestock trades, and a well-trained riding horse from the plains can fetch a price of fifty silver marks or more in the markets of the coastal cities.

The ox is the principal draught animal of the agricultural regions, used for the pulling of the plough and the wagon. The ox is stronger than the horse and less expensive to maintain, for the ox can be fed on grass and hay that the horse cannot digest as efficiently. The ox is also used for the hauling of timber from the forests and the transport of goods along the roads of the interior.

## The Seasonal Movement of Herds

The transhumant herding of livestock is practised in the mountain regions of the known world, where the herders move their animals between the lowland pastures of the winter and the highland pastures of the summer. The movement of the herds follows the pattern of the seasons, with the herders and their families accompanying the animals on the journey that may cover a distance of many miles. The transhumant herder lives in a seasonal settlement, occupying the winter quarters in the valley and the summer camp on the mountain slope.

The transhumant system is a response to the conditions of the mountain environment, where the lowland pastures are insufficient to support the herds through the summer and the highland pastures are too cold for use in the winter. The herders who practise transhumance are skilled in the management of the animals on the journey, and the routes that they follow are marked by the tradition of centuries.

The centaur kindreds of the plains practise a form of herding that is similar to transhumance, though the seasonal movement of the centaurs follows the availability of the grass and the water across the vast expanse of the plains. The centaurs do not keep fixed settlements, moving their herds from one grazing ground to another as the season and the condition of the grass demand. The centaur way of life is the most mobile of the herding traditions, and the centaurs are the most skilled of the herders in the reading of the signs of the plains.
