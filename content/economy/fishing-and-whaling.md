## The Harvest of the Seas

The waters that surround the three continents sustain a harvest that feeds coastal communities from the northernmost reaches of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to the warm shores of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). Fishing and whaling are among the oldest of the extractive industries, predating the settled agriculture of the interior and continuing to provide a substantial portion of the animal protein consumed in the coastal regions. The methods by which the harvest is taken, the preservation techniques that make it possible to transport the catch inland, and the institutions that regulate the taking of fish and whale vary from region to region, but the industry is everywhere shaped by the same fundamental realities: the seasonal patterns of the sea, the danger of the work, and the constant pressure of demand from markets that lie far beyond the sight of the shore.

The [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) and the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) support distinct fisheries, each with its own species, its own seasonal rhythms, and its own communities of fishermen. The cold waters of the Greyring are rich in herring, cod, and the great whales that migrate through the northern seas, while the warmer waters of the Auric yield mackerel, tuna, and a variety of shellfish that are gathered along the shores of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The industry is most developed along the coasts of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the northern coast of Aethelar, where the population density is lower and the agricultural potential of the interior is insufficient to support the coastal towns without the supplement of the sea.

## Coastal Fishing Communities of Aethelar

The coast of Aethelar is dotted with fishing villages that line the shores from the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) in the north to the [Auric Passage](/content/location/route/trade/auric-passage) in the south. The most significant of these communities are clustered where the coastal waters are sheltered and the fishing grounds are accessible within a day's voyage from the harbour. The village of [Brightwater](/content/location/settlement/village/brightwater) on the eastern coast of Aethelar is typical of the larger fishing settlements, with a protected harbour, a fleet of a dozen fishing vessels of various sizes, and a population that is occupied almost entirely in the taking and processing of fish. The fishermen of [Brightwater](/content/location/settlement/village/brightwater) work the inshore waters for cod, haddock, and mackerel, setting lines and nets from small boats that return to harbour each evening with the day's catch.

The fishing villages of the Aethelaran coast are organised into [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) that regulate the taking of fish, the allocation of fishing grounds, and the sale of the catch in the coastal markets. The guild of the fishermen of Brightwater is a local institution chartered by the council of the nearest market town, with the authority to set the seasons during which each species may be taken, the size of the nets that may be used, and the prices at which the catch is sold to the merchants who transport the fish inland. The guild also maintains the harbour, the fish market, and the smokehouses in which a portion of the catch is preserved for transport to the interior towns that cannot obtain fresh fish.

The seasonal cycle of the coastal fisheries follows the migrations of the fish and the rhythms of the weather. The spring herring run is the most important event of the fishing year on the eastern coast of Aethelar, drawing fishermen from villages along the entire coast to the waters where the herring gather in enormous shoals. The herring are taken in nets of fine mesh, hauled aboard the boats in quantities that fill the holds to capacity, and landed at the harbours where the merchants of the interior are waiting to buy them. The catch of a single spring run may equal the catch of the rest of the year combined, and the price of herring in the inland markets falls sharply during the weeks of the run as the supply overwhelms the capacity of the merchants to transport and preserve it.

The summer months are given over to the taking of cod and haddock on the deeper fishing grounds beyond the inshore waters, using lines with baited hooks that are set from boats that remain at sea for several days at a time. The autumn brings the mackerel run, a shorter and less reliable event than the spring herring run but still significant for the communities that depend upon it. The winter months are the lean season for the coastal fisheries, for the storms that sweep across the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the Greyring make it dangerous to put to sea, and the fishing fleet remains in harbour for weeks at a time. The winter is the season for repairing nets and maintaining boats, for smoking and salting the fish that have been laid by for the cold months, and for the small inshore fisheries that operate in the sheltered waters of the river mouths and the estuaries.

## Greyring Whaling Fleets from Morvind

The whaling fleets of Morvind are the most distinctive and the most dangerous branch of the fishing industry, pursuing the great whales of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) in vessels that are built for strength and endurance rather than speed. The whaling settlements of the Morvind coast, including the harbours of [Nordhaven](/content/location/settlement/city/nordhaven) and the smaller towns of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), are the bases from which the whalers operate, launching their expeditions in the spring and returning in the autumn with the products of the season's hunt.

The whale is taken for its oil, its bone, and its meat, all of which are valuable commodities in the markets of the three continents. The oil is rendered from the blubber of the whale and used for lighting, lubrication, and the treatment of leather and wool. The bone is carved into tools and ornaments and used in the manufacture of corsets and other structured garments. The meat is salted or smoked and sold as a foodstuff, primarily in the northern markets where the taste for whale meat is well established and the price is high enough to justify the cost of preserving and transporting it.

The whaling vessel of Morvind is a stoutly built ship of sixty to eighty feet in length, crewed by twenty to thirty men and equipped with harpoons of forged iron and long coils of rope. The whale is pursued in open boats launched from the mother vessel, the harpooner standing in the bow of the boat and striking the whale with a harpoon that is barbed to hold fast. The wounded whale dives and sounds, towing the boat and its crew across the surface of the sea until the whale surfaces again and can be approached for the killing stroke. The work is dangerous and the mortality among whalers is high, for the whale in its death throes can crush a boat or drag it under the surface, and the cold waters of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) kill a man in minutes if he is thrown from the boat.

The whaling fleets of Morvind are regulated by the guilds of the whaling towns, which set the seasons of the hunt, the quotas of whales that may be taken, and the allocation of the catch among the crews. The guilds also maintain the rendering stations where the blubber is boiled down into oil, the smokehouses where the meat is preserved, and the warehouses where the products of the hunt are stored before shipment to the markets of the south. The guilds of Morvind are among the most powerful institutions of the northern coast, for the whaling industry is the principal source of wealth for the towns that depend upon it and the guilds control the access to the harbours and the processing facilities that the whalers require.

## Preservation Techniques: Salting and Smoking

The preservation of the catch is the critical step that determines the value of the fish and the distance to which it can be transported. Fresh fish spoils within a day in the warm climate of Sahrana and within two or three days in the temperate climate of Aethelar, and the markets of the interior are often weeks away from the coast. The preservation of fish by salting and smoking is the solution that the fishing communities have developed over centuries of practice, and the techniques are refined to a high degree of effectiveness.

The salting of fish is the most common preservation method, employed for herring, cod, and the other species that are taken in large quantities. The fish are cleaned and gutted at the harbour, layered with salt in barrels, and left to cure for a period of weeks or months before they are ready for market. The salt used in the preservation of fish comes from the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court) and the salt-pans of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast, and the cost of salt is a significant component of the cost of preserved fish. The salting process draws the moisture from the flesh of the fish, inhibiting the growth of the bacteria that cause spoilage and allowing the fish to be stored for months or even years in a cool, dry place.

The smoking of fish is the second most common preservation method, employed primarily for the species that are taken in smaller quantities and for the parts of the whale that are to be sold as meat. The fish are cleaned, split, and hung in the smokehouse, where they are exposed to the smoke of a slow fire of hardwood for a period of several days. The smoke imparts a distinctive flavour to the flesh and dries it to a degree that prevents spoilage, and the smoked fish can be stored for weeks or months depending on the degree of smoking. The smokehouses of the fishing villages are distinctive structures, with high roofs and louvred walls that allow the smoke to circulate while the fish are hung from the rafters in tiered rows.

The dried fish that is produced by the combined application of salt and air drying is the most durable of the preserved fish products, capable of being stored for years and transported to the most distant markets. The stockfish of the Morvind coast, made from cod that are split, gutted, and hung on racks to dry in the cold, dry air of the northern winter, is a staple of the diet in the mining towns of the interior and the cities of the south that import it as a luxury. The drying racks of the northern villages are a common sight along the coast of Morvind, covered with the pale shapes of the fish that are the foundation of the local economy.

## The Seasonal Cycle of the Fisheries

The fishing year follows the seasons as reliably as the agricultural year, though the rhythms of the sea are less predictable than the rhythms of the land. The winter months are the quiet season for the coastal fisheries, with the fishing fleet in harbour and the fishermen occupied with the maintenance of their boats and gear. The fish that have been preserved during the previous season are sold in the inland markets during the winter, when the demand for protein is highest and the price of fresh food is at its peak.

The spring brings the herring run, the most intense period of the fishing year, with the fishermen working from dawn to dusk and beyond to bring in the catch before the herring move on. The herring are landed at the harbours, where the merchants of the interior and the agents of the guilds are waiting to buy them. The spring herring run is the event that determines the prosperity of the fishing communities for the year, and the catch of the run is the subject of speculation and negotiation among the merchants who trade in preserved fish.

The summer months are the season for the deep-sea fisheries, with the fishermen taking cod, haddock, and other groundfish from the deeper waters beyond the inshore grounds. The summer fisheries are less intense than the spring herring run, but they are more sustained, continuing for weeks at a time and providing a steady supply of fresh fish to the coastal markets and preserved fish to the interior. The summer is also the season for the whaling expeditions of Morvind, with the whalers departing for the Greyring Ocean in the early summer and returning in the autumn with the products of the hunt.

The autumn brings the mackerel run, a shorter and less reliable event than the spring herring run but still significant for the communities that depend upon it. The mackerel are taken in nets and lines, and they are preserved by salting and smoking for the winter market. The autumn is also the season for the preparation of the preserved fish that will be sold during the winter months, with the smokehouses working at full capacity and the salt barrels being filled with the catch of the season.

## Trade and Transport of the Catch

The trade in preserved fish connects the coastal fishing communities to the markets of the interior and the distant ports of the three continents. The merchants of the interior towns buy salted herring and dried cod from the fishing villages, transporting them along the trade routes that connect the coast to the inland population centres. The price of preserved fish in the interior towns is significantly higher than the price at the coast, reflecting the cost of transport and the tariffs and tolls that are levied along the routes.

The trade in whale products connects the whaling towns of Morvind to the markets of Aethelar and Sahrana, where whale oil and whale bone are in demand for a variety of industrial and domestic uses. The whale oil is shipped in barrels aboard the vessels that carry the northern timber and metalwork to the southern markets, and the whale bone is transported in bundles that are loaded into the holds of the same vessels. The trade is regulated by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the whaling towns, which set the prices and the conditions of sale and maintain the quality standards that ensure the reputation of Morvind whale products in the distant markets.

The fishing and whaling industry is a significant component of the economy of the coastal regions, providing employment for a substantial portion of the population and generating a flow of silver that supports the communities that depend upon the harvest of the sea. The industry is also a source of tension between the coastal fishing communities and the interior populations that depend upon the preserved fish that the fisheries produce, for the price of fish in the interior is a matter of constant negotiation between the guilds of the fishermen and the guilds of the merchants who trade in the catch. The system of regulation that the guilds maintain has preserved the fisheries from the overexploitation that would otherwise follow from the intense demand, and the industry continues to sustain the coastal communities as it has for centuries.
