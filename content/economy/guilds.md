## Overview

The guild is the institution by which the merchants and craftsmen of the known world regulate their trades, maintain their standards, and protect their common interests. Every trade of any significance is organised into a guild in every city of any size, from the guild of the vintners and brewers whose standards govern the quality of the ale and wine consumed in the inns of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) to the guild of the waggoners whose rules determine the rates charged for the transport of goods along the [Grassland Traderoad](/content/location/route/trade/grassland-traderoad). The guild is a local institution, established by the practitioners of the trade in a particular city and chartered by the sovereign or the council of that city, and the powers of the guild are limited to the jurisdiction within which the charter operates. The guilds of different cities in the same trade are linked by agreements and customs, and a master of the trade in one city is generally recognised as a master in the cities that have agreements with his home guild, but the guild remains a creature of the city that chartered it.

## The Guild Charter

The guild charter is the document by which the sovereign or the council of a city grants to the practitioners of a trade the right to organise themselves into a guild and to regulate the practice of the trade within the city's jurisdiction. The charter is a contract between the sovereign and the guild, specifying the powers of the guild, the obligations of the guild to the sovereign, and the limits within which the guild may exercise its authority. The charter is typically granted in perpetuity, but it may be revoked by the sovereign if the guild fails to meet its obligations or abuses its powers.

The charter of the guild of the [Vintners and Cellarers](/content/government/local/vintners-and-cellarers), granted by the council of Vassholm more than two centuries ago, is an example of the form. The charter grants to the guild the exclusive right to regulate the production and sale of wine and ale within the city and its territory, the power to set standards of quality for wine and ale, the authority to inspect the premises of vintners and brewers and to confiscate goods that fail the standard, and the right to adjudicate disputes among members of the guild. In return, the guild is required to pay an annual fee to the council, to provide a specified quantity of ale for the council's feasts, and to maintain the guild hall and the cellars in which the standard wines and ales are kept.

The charters of the guilds of the Sahrani coastal cities follow the same pattern, though the sovereign in those cities is the council rather than a single ruler. The guild of the salt-makers, whose charter is held by the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court), is the oldest continuous guild in Sahrana, and its charter has been confirmed by every council of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) since the founding of the city. The guild of the spice merchants of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) holds a charter from the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) that grants the guild the exclusive right to certify the quality of spices exported from the city, and the guild's seal is recognised throughout the known world as the guarantee of authenticity.

## The Ranks of the Guild

The guild is organised into three ranks through which every practitioner of the trade must pass: the apprentice, the journeyman, and the master. The apprentice is a young person bound to a master by a contract of indenture, typically for a term of seven years. The apprentice lives in the master's household, works in the master's workshop, and receives instruction in the trade in return for labour. The master is responsible for the apprentice's food, clothing, and lodging, and the master is required by the guild to provide the apprentice with a complete training in the trade. The contract of indenture is registered with the guild, and the guild has the power to enforce the terms of the contract against both the master and the apprentice.

The journeyman is a practitioner of the trade who has completed the term of apprenticeship and has been admitted to the guild as a full member. The journeyman works for wages, travelling from city to city to gain experience and to learn the different methods of the trade in different regions. The journeyman is required by the guild to produce a masterpiece, a piece of work that demonstrates the journeyman's skill and knowledge of the trade, before being admitted to the rank of master. The masterpiece is judged by the masters of the guild, and the journeyman who fails to meet the standard may attempt again after a specified interval.

The master is a practitioner of the trade who has produced a masterpiece that has been accepted by the guild and who has been admitted to the full rights and privileges of the guild. The master may keep a workshop, take apprentices, and sell goods directly to the public. The master is a member of the guild's governing council, with the right to vote in the election of the guild's officers and to speak in the guild's deliberations. The master is also subject to the guild's discipline, and a master who violates the guild's standards or regulations may be fined, suspended, or expelled from the guild.

## The Guild in the City

The guild is a presence in the daily life of every city, regulating the quality and price of goods, the conditions of labour, and the conduct of the trade. The guild of the bakers sets the weight and price of the loaf, the guild of the butchers sets the standards of meat and the conditions of the slaughterhouse, and the guild of the smiths sets the standards of iron and steel and the prices of the tools and fittings that the smith produces. The guilds of the merchants set the rules of trading in the market, the conditions under which goods may be sold, and the fees that must be paid for the use of the market stalls.

The guilds also serve as the representatives of the trade in the political life of the city. The guild of the merchants of Khalisar elects members to the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council), the governing body of the city, and the voice of the guild is heard in every deliberation of the council that touches upon the trade of the city. The guilds of the other cities of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) hold similar influence, and the guilds of the merchant houses of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) are among the most powerful political forces in the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth). The guild is the institution through which the merchants and craftsmen of the city participate in the governance of the city, and the guild's influence is a check upon the power of the sovereign and the council.

The relationship between the guilds and the sovereign is a balance of power that varies from city to city. In the cities of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), the guilds operate with considerable autonomy, and the council of the city rarely interferes in the internal affairs of the guilds. In the cities of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), the guilds are more closely regulated by the council of the holds, and the sovereign has the power to appoint the master of the guild. In the cities of Sahrana, the relationship is somewhere between these extremes, with the guilds operating under the authority of the council but with a significant degree of independence in their internal affairs.

## The Guild of the Merchant Houses

The guilds of the merchant houses are the most powerful and wealthiest of the guilds of the trading cities, for the merchants control the largest part of the silver that circulates in the economy of the known world. The guild of the merchants of Eldreth is the largest and most influential, with more than three hundred members who trade in every commodity that passes through the harbour of the city. The guild of the merchants of Khalisar is of similar size, and the two guilds maintain a correspondence that is the foundation of the commercial relationship between the two continents.

The guild of the merchants regulates the conditions of trade in the city, setting the fees for the use of the harbour and the market, the standards of measure and weight, and the rules of contract and credit. The guild also maintains the register of bills of exchange, in which the names of merchants who have dishonoured their obligations are recorded, and the discipline of the register is the most effective mechanism of commercial self-regulation in the known world. The guild of the merchants also represents the interests of the merchant houses before the sovereign and the council, and its voice is the most powerful in the commercial policy of the city.

The guilds of the merchant houses of the interior are smaller and less wealthy, but they perform the same functions in their own cities and towns. The guild of the merchants of [Goldmeadow](/content/location/settlement/city/goldmeadow) regulates the trade in grain, wool, and horses that is the foundation of the economy of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), and the guild of the merchants of [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) regulates the caravan trade that connects the interior of Sahrana to the coast. The guilds of the interior are more conservative in their practices than the guilds of the coast, reflecting the more stable and less volatile nature of the inland trade.

## The Guilds of the Dwarven Holds

The guilds of the dwarven holds are organised on a different principle from the guilds of the human cities, reflecting the different traditions of dwarven society. The dwarven guild is a permanent institution, enduring beyond the life of any individual master, and the guild's authority is derived not from a charter granted by the sovereign but from the accumulated tradition of the hold. The dwarven guild of the smiths, the oldest and most respected of the guilds of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim), has regulated the forging of metal in the hold since before the first human settlement on the continent of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar).

The dwarven guild does not have the three ranks of apprentice, journeyman, and master that characterise the human guilds. The dwarven guild admits a member after a period of training and service that may last as long as a human generation, and the member is thereafter recognised as a smith of the guild for life. The dwarven guild does not require a masterpiece, for the work of the dwarven smith is judged by the use to which it is put and the reputation it earns among the other smiths of the hold. The standards of the dwarven guild are enforced by the tradition of the hold, and a smith who produces work of poor quality is not expelled from the guild but is simply given less work and less honour.

The dwarven guilds do not have the same relationship with the sovereign as the human guilds, for the dwarven holds are governed by a council of elders and the guilds are represented on the council. The guild of the smiths of Stoneheim holds seats on the council of the holds, and the voice of the smiths is heard in every deliberation that touches upon the mining and forging of metal. The relationship between the guild and the council is one of partnership rather than regulation, reflecting the dwarven view that the guild is an integral part of the governance of the hold rather than a separate institution.

## The Guilds of the Nomadic Peoples

The nomadic peoples of the known world do not maintain guilds in the form that the settled peoples know, for their trades are not practised in fixed workshops and their commerce is not conducted in fixed markets. The [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert, whose knowledge of the desert and its routes is the foundation of the caravan trade, maintain a tradition of apprenticeship and mastery that serves the same function as the guild in the settled cities. The salawa guide who leads a caravan across the desert is a master of the route, recognised by the other salawa and by the merchant houses of the coast, and the young salawa who accompanies the caravan is an apprentice, learning the route and the skills that the master has learned.

The centaur kindreds of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) maintain a similar tradition, with the older centaurs teaching the younger ones the routes and the watering places and the signs of weather that are the knowledge of the plains. The centaurs do not have guilds in the human sense, but they have customs and traditions that regulate the practice of the trade of guiding caravans and the sharing of the knowledge that is the foundation of the trade. The human merchants of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) recognise the centaur traditions and respect them, for the centaurs are the guardians of the [Grassland Traderoad](/content/location/route/trade/grassland-traderoad) and the knowledge they hold is essential to the safe passage of the caravans.

## The Guilds and the Wider Commerce

The guilds of the trading cities, the dwarven holds, and the nomadic peoples are linked by agreements and customs that connect the local institutions into a system of mutual recognition and cooperation. The guild of the smiths of Eldreth has an agreement with the guild of the smiths of Stoneheim by which a dwarven smith who travels to Eldreth may work in the city as a master of the trade, and a human smith of Eldreth who travels to Stoneheim may work in the holds under the same conditions. The guild of the merchants of Khalisar has agreements with the guilds of the merchants of every city in the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), and a merchant of Khalisar who travels to [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) or [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) is recognised as a member of the guild in those cities.

The system of mutual recognition is one of the foundations of the intercontinental commerce of the known world, for it allows a merchant to travel from Eldreth to [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and to conduct business in the markets of every intermediate city with the assurance of the same rights and protections in each city. The guilds of the different cities do not have identical standards or regulations, but the differences are managed by the agreements and customs that the guilds have established over the centuries of trade, and the system is flexible enough to accommodate the variations that arise from the different traditions of the different regions.
