## The Systems of the Bound Labour

The binding of a person to the service of another is a practice that is found in every region of the known world, though the forms that the binding takes and the conditions under which the bound person lives and works vary widely across the cultures and the legal traditions. The slave is a person who is owned by another, bought and sold as a chattel, and the slave's labour is the property of the owner. The indentured servant is a person who is bound to the service of another by a contract for a fixed term, and the indenture is a form of the obligation that is entered into voluntarily or imposed by the judgment of the court.

The practice of the slavery is concentrated in the Sahrani interior and the warm coastal regions, where the demand for the labour in the spice plantations and the salt pans and the households of the wealthy is high. The practice of the indenture is more common in the Aethelaran regions, where the guilds and the courts have developed the systems of the bound apprenticeship and the debt servitude.

## The Slave Trade of the Sahrani Coast

The slave trade of the Sahrani coast is the largest and the most organised of the slave trades of the known world, supplying the labour that the spice plantations and the salt pans and the households of the wealthy cities require. The slaves are taken from among the prisoners of the wars that the tribes of the interior fight among themselves, and from the families that have been sold into the slavery by the creditors who have seized the debtors and the families of the debtors.

The slaves are brought to the coastal markets along the same routes that the caravans use, and the slave market of Khalisar is the largest in the known world. The slaves are sold in the auction that is conducted in the square of the market, and the price that the slave commands depends on the age and the strength and the skills that the slave possesses.

The conditions of the slave's life depend on the master and the work that the slave is required to perform. The slave who works in the household of the wealthy merchant lives in the relative comfort, fed and clothed and housed in the quarters that are attached to the master's residence. The slave who works in the salt pans or the spice plantations lives in the conditions that are harsh and shortens the life.

## The Indenture of the Debtor

The indenture of the debtor is a form of the bound labour that is more common in the Aethelaran regions, where the courts have the power to impose the servitude on the debtor who cannot repay the debt. The debtor who owes the silver to the creditor and who cannot pay may be bound to the service of the creditor for a term of the years until the debt is worked off.

The indenture of the debtor is regulated by the courts and the guilds, and the term of the service is determined by the amount of the debt and the value of the labour that the debtor can perform. The debtor's labour is valued at a rate that is set by the guild, and the debtor's obligation is reduced by the value of the labour that has been performed.

The indenture of the debtor is a form of the punishment as much as the means of the repayment, and the debtor who is indentured is subject to the discipline of the master. The master may not beat or abuse the debtor, for the indenture is a legal contract and not the ownership of the person, but the master may impose the penalties for the failure to perform the labour that the contract requires.

## The Apprenticeship of the Guild

The apprenticeship of the guild is the form of the indenture that is the most common in the Aethelaran cities, and the apprenticeship is the means by which the young person is trained in the trade that the guild regulates. The apprentice is bound to the master by a contract of the indenture, typically for a term of seven years, and the apprentice lives in the master's household and works in the master's workshop.

The contract of the apprenticeship is registered with the guild, and the guild has the power to enforce the terms of the contract against both the master and the apprentice. The master is required to provide the apprentice with the complete training in the trade, and the apprentice is required to serve the master with the diligence and the obedience.

The apprenticeship is not the slavery, and the apprentice who is mistreated by the master may bring the complaint to the guild. The guild will investigate the complaint and may release the apprentice from the contract if the master has failed to meet the obligations. The apprentice who completes the term of the indenture is admitted to the guild as the journeyman, and the journeyman may then work for the wages and travel from the city to the city.

## The Debt Bondage of the Rural Regions

The debt bondage of the rural regions is a form of the bound labour that is practised in the areas where the money economy is less developed and the access to the credit is limited. The farmer who needs the seed and the tools for the planting may borrow from the landlord or the merchant, and the farmer pledges the labour of the family as the security for the loan.

The debt bondage is the most common form of the bound labour in the rural regions of Aethelar and the Sahrani interior. The farmer who cannot repay the loan is bound to the service of the lender, and the farmer and the farmer's family work the land of the lender until the debt is repaid. The debt may be passed from the parent to the child, and the family may remain in the bondage for generations.

The debt bondage is the subject of the debate among the reformers, who argue that the system keeps the poor in the perpetual servitude and prevents the improvement of the condition of the rural population. The guilds and the councils of the cities have taken some measures to regulate the debt bondage, but the practice remains common in the regions where the guild's authority is limited.

## The Moral and the Legal Debates

The practice of the slavery and the indenture is the subject of the debate among the learned and the pious of the known world. The Still Flame teaches that the warmth of the hearth is the warmth that should be extended to all who come to it, and the hearth-keepers who extend the hospitality to the slave and the indentured are the witnesses of the teaching. The Unwritten Name teaches that the naming of the person as the slave is a naming that the discipline of the nouns must attend to, for the name of the slave is not the name of the person but the name of the condition.

The legal status of the slave varies across the jurisdictions. The slave who escapes from the master in Khalisar may be reclaimed by the master, and the person who harbours the slave is subject to the penalty. The slave who reaches the territory of the Aldreth is free, for the courts of the Aldreth do not recognise the ownership of the person by another.

## The Abolitionist Movements

The movements for the abolition of the slavery have grown in recent generations, particularly in the cities of the Aethelaran coast. The scholars of the Unwritten Name have written the treatises arguing that the condition of the slave is a violation of the discipline of the nouns, for the slave is named as a thing and not as a person. The hearth-keepers of the Still Flame have provided the shelter to the escaped slaves, and the houses of the memory of the Shield and Song have recorded the names of the slaves who have died in the bondage.

The arguments for the abolition have not yet won the wide acceptance, and the slave trade of the Sahrani coast continues to supply the labour that the plantations and the salt pans require. The reformers continue to press for the change, and the debate is one of the most contentious in the public life of the known world.
