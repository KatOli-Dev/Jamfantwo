## The Provision of Shelter and Refreshment for the Traveller

The inns and the hospices of the known world are the establishments that provide the shelter and the refreshment for the travellers who move along the roads and the routes that connect the settlements. The inn is a commercial establishment, offering the bed and the board for the payment of the price, while the hospice is a charitable establishment, offering the shelter without the charge to the traveller who is in need. Both the inn and the hospice are essential to the trade and the travel that sustain the economy of the known world, for the traveller who cannot find the shelter on the road cannot make the journey.

The inn is a familiar presence on every road of any significance, from the coaching inns that serve the travellers on the Grassland Traderoad of the plains to the caravanserais that shelter the merchants on the Kharesh Caravan Way of the desert. The inn provides the stable for the horses and the waggons, the common room for the eating and the drinking, and the dormitory or the private chamber for the sleeping.

## The Inn and the Innkeeper

The innkeeper is the person who manages the establishment, and the innkeeper's trade is a demanding occupation that requires the skill of the host and the business of the merchant. The innkeeper must know the road and the travellers who use it, the rates that the competing inns charge, and the condition of the supplies that the inn requires.

The inn is the place where the travellers gather, and the common room of the inn is the centre of the social life of the road. The merchants who travel together in the caravan will gather in the common room of the evening, exchanging the news of the road and the prices of the markets and the condition of the routes. The innkeeper who keeps a good common room is the host of the conversation as much as the purveyor of the food and the ale.

The rates that the inn charges for the accommodation are regulated by the guild of the innkeepers in the cities where the guild has the authority. The rate for the bed in the dormitory is a copper penny, the rate for the private chamber is three copper pennies, and the rate for the stable and the hay for the horse is a penny for the night. The rates for the food and the ale are set separately, and the innkeeper may charge whatever the market will bear for the refreshment.

## The Hospices of the Religious Orders

The hospices are the establishments that are maintained by the religious orders for the shelter of the travellers who are in need. The hospices are not commercial establishments, and the shelter that they provide is given without the charge to the traveller. The hospice is an expression of the duty of the hospitality that the religious orders teach, and the hospices are the most important source of the shelter for the poor and the pilgrims who travel the roads.

The hearth-halls of the Still Flame are the most numerous of the religious hospices, and the hearth-keepers are required by the doctrine of the faith to extend the warmth of the fire and the shelter of the hall to all who seek it. The hearth-hall of the Still Flame is the refuge of the traveller in the winter, and the hearth-keepers maintain the store of the fuel and the food for the relief of the traveller.

The Reading Cloisters of the Unwritten Name maintain the hospices for the scholars who travel between the cloisters, and the hospitality that the cloisters extend is a sign of the openness that the doctrine teaches. The Memory Houses of the Shield and Song maintain the hospices for the singers who travel between the houses, and the hospitality of the memory house is a sign of the kinship that the order maintains.

## The Caravanserai of the Desert Routes

The caravanserai is the form of the inn that is found on the desert routes of Sahrana, and the caravanserai is a fortified establishment that provides the shelter for the caravans that cross the desert. The caravanserai is built around a central courtyard, with the stables for the camels and the horses on the ground floor and the chambers for the travellers on the upper floor.

The caravanserai is the critical institution of the desert trade, for the caravan that crosses the Kharesh Caravan Way must stop at the caravanserai that are spaced at the intervals of a day's journey along the route. The caravanserai provides the water and the forage for the animals and the food and the shelter for the travellers, and the master of the caravanserai is the person who knows the condition of the road and the safety of the passage.

The caravanserai is also the place where the tolls and the duties are collected, for the Vashari Pact and the Kharesh Tribes maintain the stations for the collection of the revenue from the caravans. The master of the caravanserai is the representative of the sovereign in the desert, and the master's duty is to collect the toll and to report the passage of the caravan to the authorities.

## The Fare of the Inn and the Hospice

The food that is served in the inns and the hospices is the fare of the common table, prepared from the ingredients that are available in the region. The inn of the plains serves the bread and the cheese and the ale that are the staples of the region, with the addition of the meat of the cattle or the sheep that have been slaughtered for the consumption of the travellers. The inn of the coast serves the fish and the bread and the wine that are the fare of the region, with the addition of the spices that are the luxury of the Sahrani trade.

The hospice that is maintained by the religious orders serves the simpler fare, reflecting the doctrine of the moderation that the orders teach. The hospice of the Still Flame serves the bread and the soup and the ale, and the traveller who accepts the hospitality of the hearth-hall is expected to contribute to the maintenance of the hall by the labour of the hands or the offering of the small gift.

## The Social Role of the Inn

The inn is not only the place of the shelter and the refreshment but also the centre of the social life of the road and the market town. The inn is the place where the news is exchanged, where the deals are struck, and where the alliances are formed. The merchants who gather in the common room of the inn are the sources of the information about the condition of the markets and the safety of the routes, and the innkeeper who keeps a well-informed common room is the source of the knowledge that the merchants need.

The inn is also the place where the entertainment is provided, and the inns of the larger towns employ the musicians and the storytellers who perform in the common room of the evening. The singer who performs at the inn of Eldreth is a master of the songs of the Shield and Song, and the story that the teller recites at the inn of Khalisar is the tale of the voyages of the Sea Mother's Court.

## The Inns of the Different Regions

The inns of the plains are the largest and the most comfortable of the inns of the known world, reflecting the prosperity of the grain trade and the volume of the traffic on the Grassland Traderoad. The inn of Goldmeadow is the finest inn of the plains, with the stables that can hold a hundred horses and the chambers that can accommodate fifty travellers.

The inns of the mountains are the most basic of the inns, reflecting the difficulty of the terrain and the scarcity of the supplies. The inn of Karhold is a simple establishment of the stone and the timber, with the common room that serves the ale and the bread and the soup that is the staple of the mining community.

The inns of the desert are the most distinctive, reflecting the conditions of the climate and the traditions of the salawa peoples who maintain them. The tent of the salawa host is a mobile inn that travels with the caravan, and the hospitality of the salawa is extended to the traveller who approaches the tent in peace.

## The Economics of the Innkeeping Trade

The innkeeping trade is a business of the small margins and the high risks, for the innkeeper must maintain the establishment through the seasons of the slack demand and the seasons of the peak demand. The innkeeper who owns the inn in the market town depends on the periodic fairs and the markets for the greater part of the annual revenue, and the innkeeper who owns the inn on the main road depends on the volume of the traffic that passes through.

The guild of the innkeepers regulates the trade in every city of any size, setting the rates that the inns may charge and the standards that the inns must maintain. The guild also provides the system of the dispute resolution, allowing the traveller who has been overcharged or poorly treated to bring the complaint before the guild for the judgment.
