## Spreading Risk Among the Many

Trade in the known world is conducted under constant threat of loss. A vessel sailing the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) may be lost to storm or reef, a caravan crossing the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert may fall to raiders or drought, and a warehouse storing goods may burn or flood. The merchant who bears the risk alone carries a burden that may destroy a fortune accumulated over years of successful trade. Insurance is the means by which risk is spread among the many, so that the loss of one is borne by the contributions of many.

Insurance is not as formalised as [banking and credit](/content/economy/banking-and-credit), and no institution in the known world specialises solely in its provision. Insurance is instead conducted by guilds, merchant houses, and religious institutions, each of which has developed mechanisms for spreading risk among their members.

## Maritime Insurance of the Northern Coast League

The most developed insurance system in the known world is the maritime insurance maintained by the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league). The league's insurance fund is maintained by contributions from the merchant houses of member cities, and the fund compensates the insured for the loss of a vessel or cargo. Insurance is not compulsory, but merchant houses that trade regularly across the [Auric Passage](/content/location/route/trade/auric-passage) typically subscribe to the fund, for the cost of the contribution is small compared to the potential loss of a cargo of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) spices or [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) dyes.

The contribution a merchant pays is called the premium, determined by the value of the vessel and cargo and the risk of the route on which the vessel sails. The premium for a voyage from [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) to [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) in the storm season is higher than for the same voyage in calm weather. The premium for a voyage through the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) is higher than for a coastal voyage, for the passage through the strait is more dangerous and the risk of loss greater.

The league's fund is administered by its admiralty court, which sits permanently in Khalisar. The court adjudicates disputes arising from insurance claims, and its judgments are enforced by the collective authority of the member cities. The court employs assessors who inspect vessels, cargoes, and voyage conditions, and the assessor's report is the basis upon which the court determines the validity of a claim.

## Guild Insurance of the Merchant Houses

The merchant guilds in each city maintain insurance systems for the benefit of their members. The guild of merchants of Eldreth maintains a fund that insures members against loss of goods stored in warehouses and goods carried on vessels sailing from Eldreth's harbour.

The premium a member pays to the guild fund is set by the guild council and adjusted each year according to the losses the fund sustained in the previous year. The guild fund is administered by a committee of guild masters, which appoints assessors to inspect premises, vessels, and cargoes that are insured.

Guild insurance is a form of mutual assistance among guild members, and the fund is not intended to generate profit but to provide the security members need. The guild of merchants of Eldreth has maintained its insurance fund for more than a century, compensating members for losses sustained in fires, floods, and shipwrecks over the years.

## Insurance of the Caravan Trade

The caravan trade of the Sahrani interior is insured through contributions that participating merchants make to a common fund maintained by the caravan master. A merchant shipping goods on a caravan pays a contribution of one part in twenty of the value of the goods into the fund, which compensates those whose goods are lost to raiders or accidents of the road.

The caravan master is responsible for administering the fund, and the master's judgment of loss is final. The caravan master also employs the guards who protect the caravan, the cost paid from the fund contributions.

Caravan insurance is not as formal as the league's maritime insurance, but it effectively spreads risk among the merchants participating in a caravan. A merchant who ships goods on a caravan knows that loss will be compensated from the fund, and this knowledge reduces the hesitation a merchant might feel about undertaking the voyage.

## Religious Institutions and Relief of Loss

Religious institutions also play a role in spreading risk, particularly in relieving losses sustained by the poor and vulnerable. The hearth-halls of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) maintain stores of fuel and food distributed to households that have suffered loss of harvest or fire. The [Sea Mother's Court](/content/religion/sea-mothers-court) in Khalisar maintains a fund that compensates the families of fishermen lost at sea.

Religious institutions do not charge premiums in the manner of insurance funds; the relief they provide is a form of charity rather than insurance. The institutions are supported by offerings of the faithful and by endowments established by wealthy donors who have sought to provide for the relief of the poor.

## Bottomry and Respondentia

Bottomry is a form of maritime loan that combines elements of lending and insurance. The lender advances silver to the owner of a vessel or cargo, and the loan is secured by the vessel or cargo. If the vessel is lost at sea, the loan is forgiven and the lender bears the loss. If the vessel returns safely, the borrower repays the loan with interest that reflects the risk of the voyage.

Bottomry is the oldest form of maritime finance in the known world, regulated by the guild of merchants in the principal ports. The interest charged on a bottomry loan is higher than on an ordinary loan, for the lender bears the risk of loss in addition to the cost of capital.

Respondentia is a similar form of loan secured by the cargo alone, without the vessel. A respondentia loan is made to the merchant who owns the cargo, and the loan is forgiven if the cargo is lost. Respondentia is a more specialised form of finance, used primarily for high-value cargoes such as spices and precious stones.

## Assessment of Risk

Assessing risk is the skill a lender or insurer must possess, for determining the premium or interest depends on accurately assessing the probability of loss. The assessor who works for the admiralty court or the guild fund committee knows the routes, seasons, and condition of the vessels insured, and the assessor's judgment is based on records of losses sustained in the past.

The assessor also considers the reputation of the captain and the owner of the vessel, for a vessel commanded by an experienced master is less likely to be lost than one commanded by a novice. A vessel that is well maintained and properly equipped is less likely to be lost than one in poor condition.

## Limits of Insurance

Insurance is not available for every risk, and merchants who undertake voyages to the most dangerous regions must bear the loss themselves. Trade with the distant islands of the warm seas is too dangerous to be insured at any premium a merchant would be willing to pay, and merchants who undertake that trade accept the risk as the condition of the high profit the trade offers.

Insurance of goods stored in warehouses is more common than insurance of goods in the process of manufacture. The craftsman who works raw material into finished product bears the risk of loss of work in progress, and the craftsman who suffers a fire or flood must bear the loss without compensation.
