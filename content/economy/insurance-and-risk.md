## The Spreading of the Risk Among the Many

The trade of the known world is conducted under the constant threat of the loss, for the vessel that sails across the Auric Ocean may be lost to the storm or the reef, the caravan that crosses the Kharesh desert may be lost to the raiders or the drought, and the warehouse that stores the goods may be lost to the fire or the flood. The merchant who bears the risk of the loss bears a burden that may destroy the fortune that has been accumulated over the years of the successful trade. The system of the insurance is the means by which the risk is spread among the many, so that the loss of the one is borne by the contributions of the many.

The insurance is not a practice that is as formalised as the banking and the credit, and there is no institution in the known world that specialises solely in the provision of the insurance. The insurance is instead a practice that is conducted by the guilds and the merchant houses and the religious institutions, each of which has developed the mechanisms for the spreading of the risk among the members.

## The Maritime Insurance of the Northern Coast League

The most developed system of the insurance in the known world is the maritime insurance that is maintained by the Northern Coast League. The league's insurance fund is maintained by the contributions from the merchant houses of the member cities, and the fund compensates the insured for the loss of a vessel or a cargo. The insurance is not compulsory, but the merchant houses that trade regularly across the Auric Passage typically subscribe to the fund, for the cost of the contribution is small compared to the potential loss of a cargo of the Amurra spices or the Sahrani dyes.

The contribution that the merchant pays is called the premium, and the premium is determined by the value of the vessel and the cargo and the risk of the route on which the vessel is sailing. The premium for the voyage from Khalisar to Eldreth in the season of the storms is higher than the premium for the same voyage in the season of the calm weather. The premium for the voyage through the Velden Strait is higher than the premium for the voyage along the coast, for the passage through the strait is more dangerous and the risk of the loss is greater.

The fund of the league is administered by the admiralty court of the league, which sits permanently in Khalisar. The court adjudicates the disputes that arise from the insurance claims, and the judgments of the court are enforced by the collective authority of the member cities. The court employs the assessors who inspect the vessels and the cargoes and the conditions of the voyages, and the assessor's report is the basis upon which the court determines the validity of the claim.

## The Guild Insurance of the Merchant Houses

The guilds of the merchants in each city maintain the systems of the insurance for the benefit of their members. The guild of the merchants of Eldreth maintains a fund that insures the members against the loss of the goods that are stored in the warehouses and the goods that are carried on the vessels that sail from the harbour of Eldreth.

The premium that the member pays to the guild fund is set by the council of the guild, and the premium is adjusted each year according to the losses that the fund has sustained in the previous year. The guild fund is administered by a committee of the masters of the guild, and the committee appoints the assessors who inspect the premises and the vessels and the cargoes that are insured.

The guild insurance is a form of the mutual assistance among the members of the guild, and the fund is not intended to generate the profit but to provide the security that the members need. The guild of the merchants of Eldreth has maintained the insurance fund for more than a century, and the fund has compensated the members for the losses that have been sustained in the fires and the floods and the shipwrecks that have occurred over the years.

## The Insurance of the Caravan Trade

The caravan trade of the Sahrani interior is insured by the system of the contributions that the merchants who participate in the caravan make to the common fund that is maintained by the caravan master. The merchant who ships the goods on the caravan pays a contribution of one part in twenty of the value of the goods into the fund, and the fund compensates the merchants whose goods are lost to the raiders or the accidents of the road.

The caravan master is responsible for the administration of the fund, and the master's judgment of the loss is final. The caravan master is also responsible for the employment of the guards who protect the caravan, and the cost of the guards is paid from the contributions to the fund.

The system of the caravan insurance is not as formal as the maritime insurance of the league, but it is effective in the spreading of the risk among the merchants who participate in the caravan. The merchant who ships the goods on the caravan knows that the loss of the goods will be compensated from the fund, and the knowledge of the compensation reduces the hesitation that the merchant might feel about the undertaking of the voyage.

## The Religious Institutions and the Relief of the Loss

The religious institutions of the known world also play a role in the spreading of the risk, particularly in the relief of the losses that are sustained by the poor and the vulnerable. The hearth-halls of the Still Flame maintain the stores of the fuel and the food that are distributed to the households that have suffered the loss of the harvest or the fire. The Sea Mother's Court in Khalisar maintains a fund that compensates the families of the fishermen who are lost at sea.

The religious institutions do not charge the premium in the manner of the insurance funds, and the relief that they provide is a form of the charity rather than the insurance. The institutions are supported by the offerings of the faithful and by the endowments that have been established by the wealthy donors who have sought to provide for the relief of the poor.

## The Bottomry and the Respondentia

The bottomry is a form of the maritime loan that combines the elements of the lending and the insurance. The lender advances the silver to the owner of the vessel or the cargo, and the loan is secured by the vessel or the cargo. If the vessel is lost at sea, the loan is forgiven and the lender bears the loss. If the vessel returns safely, the borrower repays the loan with the interest that reflects the risk of the voyage.

The bottomry is the oldest form of the maritime finance in the known world, and the practice is regulated by the guild of the merchants in the principal ports. The interest that is charged on the bottomry loan is higher than the interest on the ordinary loan, for the lender bears the risk of the loss in addition to the cost of the capital.

The respondentia is a similar form of the loan that is secured by the cargo alone, without the vessel. The respondentia loan is made to the merchant who owns the cargo, and the loan is forgiven if the cargo is lost. The respondentia is a more specialised form of the finance, and it is used primarily for the cargoes of the high value such as the spices and the precious stones.

## The Assessment of the Risk

The assessment of the risk is the skill that the lender and the insurer must possess, for the determination of the premium and the interest depends on the accurate assessment of the probability of the loss. The assessor who works for the admiralty court of the league or the committee of the guild fund knows the routes and the seasons and the condition of the vessels that are insured, and the assessor's judgment is based on the records of the losses that have been sustained in the past.

The assessor also considers the reputation of the captain and the owner of the vessel, for the vessel that is commanded by the master of the experience is less likely to be lost than the vessel that is commanded by the novice. The vessel that is well maintained and properly equipped is less likely to be lost than the vessel that is in the poor condition.

## The Limits of the Insurance

The insurance is not available for every risk, and the merchants who undertake the voyages to the regions of the high risk must bear the loss themselves. The trade with the distant islands of the warm seas is too dangerous to be insured at any premium that the merchant would be willing to pay, and the merchants who undertake that trade accept the risk as the condition of the high profit that the trade offers.

The insurance of the goods that are stored in the warehouses is more common, but the insurance of the goods that are in the process of the manufacture is less common. The craftsman who works the raw material into the finished product bears the risk of the loss of the work in the progress, and the craftsman who suffers the fire or the flood must bear the loss without the compensation.
