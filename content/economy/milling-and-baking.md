## The Grinding of Grain and the Making of Bread

The milling of grain into flour and the baking of flour into bread are among the most fundamental of the trades that sustain the populations of the settled regions, for bread is the staple food of every household in the temperate and the cold climates. The miller who grinds the grain that the farmers bring to the mill and the baker who transforms the flour into the loaf are the links between the field and the table, and the reliability of their work is the condition of the daily nourishment of the people. The milling and the baking trades are regulated by the guilds in every city of any size, for the supply of bread is too important to be left to the unregulated enterprise of the individual.

The quality of the bread depends on the quality of the grain, the skill of the miller in the grinding, and the skill of the baker in the mixing and the baking of the dough. The grain that is grown in the fertile soils of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) produces the finest flour, and the bread that is baked from the flour of the plains is the most highly regarded in the known world. The grain of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) highlands is of a lower quality, and the bread that is baked from the Sahrani flour is darker and denser than the bread of the plains.

## The Mill and the Miller

The mill is the building in which the grain is ground into the flour, and the mill is typically located on the stream or the river where the water wheel that drives the millstones is placed. The water wheel is the source of the power that turns the millstones, and the design of the wheel and the arrangement of the gearing that connects the wheel to the stones are the principal technical knowledge that the miller must possess.

The millstones are the heart of the mill, and the quality of the grinding that the millstones perform determines the quality of the flour that the mill produces. The millstones are cut from a single block of hard stone, most commonly the millstone grit that is quarried from the beds of stone that are found in the highlands and the mountains. The surface of the millstone is cut with a pattern of grooves that channel the grain from the centre to the edge of the stone and that produce the cutting action that grinds the grain.

The miller is responsible for the maintenance of the mill and the millstones, and the miller must dress the stones at regular intervals to maintain the sharpness of the cutting surfaces. The dressing of the millstones is a skilled operation that requires the knowledge of the pattern of the grooves and the angle at which the cutting surfaces must be set. The miller who neglects the dressing of the stones produces a flour that is uneven in the grind and that bakes into a bread of an inferior quality.

The miller also determines the fineness of the flour that the mill produces, adjusting the gap between the millstones to produce the coarse flour that is used for the baking of the brown bread or the fine flour that is used for the baking of the white bread. The white flour is more expensive than the brown flour, for the production of the white flour requires the separation of the bran from the endosperm of the grain, and the white bread that is baked from the fine flour is the bread of the wealthy.

## The Types of the Mill

The water mill is the most common type of mill in the regions where the reliable flow of water is available. The undershot water wheel is the simplest design, in which the water flows beneath the wheel and the paddles of the wheel are turned by the force of the current. The overshot water wheel is a more efficient design, in which the water is carried above the wheel and falls onto the paddles, turning the wheel by the force of the falling water.

The wind mill is the type of mill that is used in the regions where the flow of water is not reliable, most commonly on the coastal plains where the wind is strong and steady. The wind mill is a tower of stone or timber, with a cap that rotates to face the sails into the wind. The sails are the arms of the wind mill that are covered with the canvas, and the force of the wind turns the sails and drives the millstones through the gearing.

The hand mill is the type of mill that is used in the households that are too remote from the water mill or the wind mill to bring the grain for the grinding. The hand mill consists of two stones, the lower stone fixed and the upper stone turned by the hand, and the grinding of the grain in the hand mill is a labour that is performed by the women of the household.

## The Baker and the Oven

The baker is the craftsman who transforms the flour into the bread, and the baker's trade is the most publicly visible of the food trades. The baker's shop is a familiar presence in every settlement of any size, and the smell of the baking bread is the sign that the baker is at work. The baker begins the day before the dawn, lighting the oven and mixing the dough while the household sleeps.

The dough is made by the mixing of the flour with the water and the yeast and the salt, and the dough is then kneaded to develop the gluten that gives the bread its structure. The kneading of the dough is a physical labour that requires the strength of the arms and the knowledge of the feel of the dough. The baker who has kneaded the dough for the proper time feels the change in the texture of the dough, from the sticky and the rough to the smooth and the elastic.

The dough is then left to rise in the warm place, where the yeast that is present in the dough produces the carbon dioxide that makes the dough expand. The rising of the dough is the process that the baker must watch with care, for the dough that has risen for too long will collapse and the dough that is not risen enough will produce a dense and heavy loaf.

The oven is the principal tool of the baker, and the design of the oven determines the quality of the bread that the baker produces. The oven is a chamber of brick or stone, heated by the fire that is burned within it. The fire is allowed to burn down to the embers, and the embers are removed before the loaves are placed in the oven. The heat that is stored in the walls of the oven bakes the loaves over the period of the baking.

## The Varieties of the Bread

The bread that is baked in the regions of the known world varies with the grain that is used and the traditions of the peoples who bake it. The white bread that is baked from the fine wheat flour is the most highly regarded, and the white loaf is the bread of the wealthy and the tables of the festivals. The brown bread that is baked from the wholemeal flour is the bread of the common household, and the dark and dense bread that is baked from the rye flour is the bread of the northern regions where the wheat does not grow.

The flat bread that is baked on the griddle is the bread of the regions where the oven is not available, and the flat bread is the staple of the desert peoples who do not have the fuel for the baking of the leavened bread. The unleavened bread that is baked without the yeast is the bread of the travellers and the armies, for the unleavened bread keeps longer than the leavened bread and does not require the time for the rising.

The pastries and the cakes are the products of the baker's craft that are the treats of the households and the celebrations. The pie that is filled with the meat and the vegetables is the principal dish of the winter table in the northern regions, and the tart that is filled with the fruit is the dessert of the summer in the temperate regions. The baker who specialises in the pastries and the cakes is a master of the trade, and the reputation of the pastry baker is the pride of the town.

## The Regulation of the Bread Trade

The guild of the bakers regulates the trade in the bread in every city of any size, setting the weight and the price of the loaf and inspecting the quality of the bread that the bakers produce. The regulations of the guild are the guarantee that the consumer receives a full weight of bread for the price that is paid and that the bread is baked from the flour of an acceptable quality.

The baker who violates the regulations of the guild is subject to the fine and the suspension from the practice of the trade. The baker who sells the loaf that is under the standard weight is fined for each loaf that is found to be deficient, and the baker who consistently violates the weight standards may be expelled from the guild.

The guild also maintains the stocks of the grain that the bakers require, purchasing the grain from the farmers and the merchants in the season of the harvest and storing it for the use of the bakers through the year. The guild of the bakers of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) is among the largest and the most influential of the guilds of the city, for the supply of the bread to the population of the great city is a responsibility that the council of the city takes seriously.

## The Millers and the Bakers of the Different Peoples

The halfling communities of the lowland valleys are the finest bakers in the known world, and the halfling bread is the pride of the halfling household. The halfling baker begins the day before the dawn, mixing the dough with the same care that the craftsman gives to the work of the hands. The halfling oven is a small and efficient structure that is built into the side of the hill, and the halfling bread that emerges from the oven is the finest that can be obtained at any price.

The dwarven peoples of the mountains are the millers of the highland regions, grinding the grain that is grown in the terraced fields of the mountain valleys. The dwarven mill is a structure of stone, built into the side of the mountain and driven by the water that flows from the high peaks. The dwarven flour is of the finest quality, and the dwarven bread is the staple of the mining communities that cannot grow their own grain.
