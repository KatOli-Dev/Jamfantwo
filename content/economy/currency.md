## The Silver Standard and Coin Circulation

The currency of the known world rests upon the silver standard that the trading cities of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) adopted in the first centuries of intercontinental commerce and that has since spread to every market from the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) to the deep [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). Silver is the medium by which the great majority of commercial transactions are conducted, from the purchase of a barrel of ale in a [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) tavern to the settlement of a cargo of spices worth the annual revenue of a small kingdom. Gold circulates among the merchant houses and the treasuries of states, but it is the silver coin that passes from hand to hand in the markets and the counting houses, and it is the silver coin that the [Silver Mark](/content/religion/silver-mark) tradition has made the object of its central observance. The discipline of weights and measures, maintained by the magistrates of every region and enforced by the assayers of every guild, is the foundation upon which the currency of the world depends.

## Weight and Fineness as the Measure of Value

The silver standard is the principle by which the value of a coin is determined by the weight and fineness of the silver it contains. A coin of standard silver is worth its weight in trade, and the merchants and money-changers of the market cities weigh every coin that passes through their hands, testing it against the known weight of the standard. The standard itself varies from region to region, for the three continents have never agreed upon a single weight for the silver coin, but the variation is small enough to be managed by the money-changers who sit at every market of any size. The pound of silver, divided into twelve ounces, is the reference weight used in the assayers' workshops, and the coin most commonly struck from this weight is the silver mark, which contains one ounce of silver of a fineness established by the guild of the mint.

The convenience of the silver standard is that it requires no authority to enforce it beyond the honesty of the assayer and the care of the merchant. A coin that has been clipped or sweated or debased is detected by the scale, and the merchant who accepts such a coin at face value has no one to blame but himself. The tradition of the Silver Mark has given the merchants and assayers of Aethelar a discipline of careful attention to the weight and fineness of coin that is unmatched in the other continents, and the reputation of Aethelaran silver is correspondingly high. The Sahrani mints, though they strike coins of equal fineness by the standard of the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council), are less strictly regulated, and Sahrani silver is routinely discounted by a small percentage in the markets of the north.

## The Silver Mark, Gold Crown, and Copper Penny

The silver mark is the most widely recognised coin in the known world, struck by the mints of every major trading city on the three continents. The mark of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) is the benchmark against which all other marks are measured, for the Eldreth mint is the oldest continuously operating mint in Aethelar and its standards are the most reliable. The mark of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) is slightly lighter by a fraction of a grain, a difference that is accommodated in the exchange rates published by the money-changers. The mark of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) is of the same weight as the Khalisar standard but carries the additional guarantee of the [Amurra Republic's](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) spice inspectors, who assay the silver as part of their regulation of the spice trade. The mark of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) is the heaviest of the principal silver coins, struck from silver mined and refined entirely within the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), and its acceptance in the northern markets is correspondingly high.

The gold crown is the principal coin of high-value transactions, struck by the mints of the great trading cities and the treasuries of the kingdoms. The crown of [Nordhaven](/content/location/settlement/city/nordhaven) is the most commonly encountered gold coin in the northern trade, while the crown of Khalisar dominates the southern markets. Gold coins are not used in everyday commerce, for their value is too great for the ordinary purchase of goods, but they are the medium of settlement between merchant houses, the currency of treaties and dowries, and the store of value that the wealthy keep in their vaults. The ratio between gold and silver varies with the supply of each metal, but the long-term average across the three continents is approximately one gold crown to fourteen silver marks.

The copper penny is the coin of daily life for the common people, struck in vast quantities by the mints of every city and town. The penny has the widest circulation of any coin, for it is the medium in which wages are paid and provisions are bought and small debts are settled. The penny of the Aethelaran mints is a small coin of nearly pure copper, while the pennies of Sahrana are larger and contain a small proportion of tin that gives them a paler colour. The exchange rate between copper pennies and silver marks varies from region to region, for the copper coin is more local in its circulation and its value is subject to the cost of copper in each market. In the cities of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), where copper is mined in significant quantities, the penny goes further than it does in the coastal cities where copper must be imported.

## Where Coins Are Struck and by Whom

The mints of the known world are the workshops in which the coins are struck, operated under the authority of the sovereign of the region or, in the case of the trading cities, under the authority of the guild of the moneyers. The mint of Eldreth is the largest and most productive, striking coins not only for the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) but also under contract for smaller states that lack their own mints. The mint employs a master moneyer, two deputy moneyers, a clerk of the assay, and a staff of smiths, coiners, and labourers who work in the mint buildings near the harbour. The silver for the mint comes from the mines of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) and the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), brought by the trade routes and refined in the mint's own furnaces.

The mint of Stoneheim is the second largest producer of silver coin, though a considerable portion of the dwarven silver is exported as bars rather than struck into coin. The dwarves prefer to trade in weighed silver rather than stamped coin, a practice that reflects the dwarven tradition of direct dealing in the metal itself rather than in the human convention of the minted token. The dwarven silver bars are stamped with the mark of the hold that produced them and the weight and fineness certified by the dwarven assayers, and they are accepted in every market at the value of their silver content. The relationship between the dwarven bar and the human coin is a subject of constant negotiation among the merchant houses, and the exchange rate fluctuates with the supply of dwarven silver and the demand for coined money in the human markets.

The mints of Sahrana are concentrated in the coastal cities of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league), with the largest at Khalisar and a secondary mint at Amurra. Sahrani silver is drawn from the mines of the interior, refined at the coast, and struck into the distinctive Sahrani mark, which bears the emblem of the minting city on one face and the crest of the Northern Coast League on the other. The Sahrani mints are noted for the quality of their dies and the consistency of their striking, and a Sahrani mark in good condition is recognised and accepted in every port. The Sahrani mint in Khalisar also strikes the gold crown that is the currency of the spice trade, and the crown of Khalisar is accepted at the same value as the crown of Nordhaven in the markets of the three continents.

## The Tables, the Touchstone, and the Rates

The money-changer is a fixture of every market in the known world, seated at a table or a stall with a set of weights and measures, a touchstone, and a small collection of reference coins. The money-changer's business is to exchange the coins of one region for the coins of another, to test the weight and fineness of coins brought by merchants and travellers, and to provide the small coin that the larger transactions of the market require. The money-changer charges a small fee for each exchange, the rate of which is set by the guild of the money-changers in each city and published in the register of the guild.

The money-changers of Eldreth are the most numerous and the most influential, for the volume of coin passing through the harbour of Eldreth is greater than that of any other port. The money-changers of Eldreth maintain a daily exchange rate board, updated by the guild in consultation with the merchant houses and the mint, and the rates set in Eldreth are the reference rates for the entire eastern trade. The money-changers of Khalisar maintain their own rates, and the difference between the two sets of rates is the subject of constant arbitrage by the merchant houses that trade on both sides of the [Auric Passage](/content/location/route/trade/auric-passage).

The money-changers of the smaller cities and towns operate under the authority of the local guild, with rates set by reference to the rates of the nearest major city. The rates in the towns of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) follow the rates of [Goldmeadow](/content/location/settlement/city/goldmeadow), which in turn follow the rates of Eldreth, with an adjustment for the cost of transporting coin between the two cities. The rates in the interior of Sahrana follow the rates of Khalisar, with a similar adjustment for the cost of caravan transport across the Kharesh. The system is self-regulating, for the merchant houses that move goods between regions also move coin, and any discrepancy between the exchange rates in different cities is quickly closed by the arbitrage of the merchants.

## Moving Silver without Moving Coin

The weight of silver coin has always been the foundation of the currency of the known world, but the merchant houses and the banking institutions of the great trading cities have long supplemented the coin with instruments of credit that reduce the need to transport physical silver from one city to another. The bill of exchange is the most common of these instruments, a written order by which a merchant in one city instructs his correspondent in another to pay a specified sum to the bearer of the bill. The bill is drawn in the weights and coin of the city of payment, and the exchange rate between the currency of the two cities is determined at the time of drawing.

The bills of exchange are a private instrument, depending for their value on the reputation of the drawer and the reliability of the correspondent. The merchant houses of Eldreth, Khalisar, and Nordhaven maintain correspondents in every significant port, and a bill drawn by a house of good standing is accepted as readily as coin in the markets. The rate at which a bill is exchanged for coin is slightly less than the face value of the bill, the difference reflecting the cost and risk of collecting the payment. The better the reputation of the drawing house, the smaller the discount, and the bills of the oldest and most trusted houses circulate at nearly the value of the silver they represent.

The guild of the money-changers in each city maintains a register of bills of exchange that have been dishonoured, and a merchant house whose bill appears in the register finds itself unable to trade on credit in that city thereafter. The discipline of the register is one of the most effective mechanisms of self-regulation in the commercial life of the known world, for the loss of reputation that follows a dishonoured bill is a greater penalty than any fine the guild could impose. The tradition of the Silver Mark has reinforced this discipline with the authority of the religious teaching that an honest debt is a form of witness to the Truemarker, and the merchant who dishonours a debt dishonours himself in the eyes of the divine.

## Pennies, Barter, and the Scarcity of Coin

The great majority of the people of the known world never handle a gold crown or even a silver mark in the course of a year. The copper penny is the coin of daily life, and the small transactions of the market, the payment of wages, and the settlement of small debts are conducted in pennies. The penny of Eldreth, which is the most widely circulated copper coin in Aethelar, bears on one face the tower of the city and on the other the emblem of the mint. The penny of Khalisar is larger and thinner, bearing on one face the harbour and on the other the crest of the council.

The buying power of the copper penny varies from region to region and from season to season. In the grain-growing regions of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), a penny buys a loaf of bread, a measure of ale, or a day's supply of fuel. In the coastal cities of Sahrana, where food must be imported, a penny buys rather less. In the mining towns of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm), where goods are brought in along the [Deep Realm Tradeway](/content/location/route/trade/deep-realm-tradeway) at considerable cost, a penny buys a smaller portion still. The purchasing power of the penny is the measure of the cost of living in each region.

The absence of coin is a condition the common people know well, for the supply of copper is never sufficient to meet the demand in the busier seasons. When coin is scarce, the market operates by barter, with goods exchanged for goods and the value of each transaction set by negotiation between the parties. The bakers and brewers and butchers of the towns maintain accounts with their regular customers, settled at the end of the week or the month in coin if coin is available or in goods if it is not. The system of accounts is informal but effective, and the merchants and craftsmen who maintain it are the backbone of the local economy.

## Protecting the Currency from Fraud and Theft

The counterfeit of coin is a crime that is prosecuted with the greatest severity in every jurisdiction of the known world, for the counterfeit coin strikes at the foundation of the trust upon which commerce depends. The counterfeiter who casts coins of base metal or of the correct metal but of a weight less than the standard is a threat to every merchant and every money-changer, and the penalty for conviction is the loss of the hand that did the casting. The guild of the money-changers maintains informants in every city who report suspicious coin to the guild's examiners, and the examiners test the suspect coin against the standard and present their findings to the magistrate.

The debasement of the coin by the sovereign is a different matter, and one that the merchants and money-changers have less power to prevent. A sovereign who is short of revenue may reduce the silver content of the coin while ordering that it continue to circulate at the old value, and the effect is a hidden tax on every holder of the debased coin. The practice is rare among the trading cities, where the guild of the moneyers has the power to resist debasement, but it has occurred in the inland kingdoms from time to time, and the memory of each debasement is preserved in the records of the money-changers as a warning to merchants trading in those regions.
