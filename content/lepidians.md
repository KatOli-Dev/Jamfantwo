## Origins

The origins of the lepidians are a subject of considerable interest to naturalists, for the lepidian body is unlike that of any other sapient people, combining a humanoid form with the wings, antennae, and covering of a large moth. Lepidians are found in the temperate forests of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), and their bodies are adapted to a nocturnal life in the wooded environment. Some naturalists have proposed a distant kinship between lepidians and [noctules](/content/noctules), citing the capacity for flight and the nocturnal habits shared by both peoples, though the differences in form and in the nature of their flight are considerable. Others have argued for a connection to [harpies](/content/harpies), pointing to the shared capacity for flight, but the lepidian body is too small and too different in structure for the comparison to be sustained. The lepidians themselves preserve oral traditions that are conveyed through patterns of light and scent rather than speech, and these traditions are unintelligible to other peoples.

What is certain is that lepidians have inhabited the temperate forests of Aethelar for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these woodlands is attested in the oldest elven records. The lepidians and the [elves](/content/elves) have coexisted in the forests for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the overlap in their range and the difference in their habits.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Lepidians are a small to medium people, standing shorter than [humans](/content/humans) but taller than [gnomes](/content/gnomes), with a light and slender frame that is built for flight. Their bones are thin and light, and their musculature is lean, concentrated in the thorax where the flight muscles are anchored. Two pairs of wings extend from the back, covered in a fine, powdery scales that range in colour from pale white to grey, brown, and patterns of striking colour that include eyespots and bands. The wings are large relative to the body, and the lepidian is capable of sustained, silent flight that is markedly different from the swift, agile flight of the [harpies](/content/harpies) or the [noctules](/content/noctules), for the lepidian flies with a slow, deliberate beating of the wings that allows for precise control and quiet movement.

The lepidian head is humanoid in general outline, but it bears two large, compound eyes that are adapted to the low light of the nocturnal forest, and two feathered antennae that are the primary organs of smell and sensory communication. The eyes are large and capable of detecting extremely faint light, and their vision in the dark is among the keenest of any people, but their resolution in bright light is poor, and the lepidians are uncomfortable in direct sun. Their antennae are extraordinarily sensitive, capable of detecting the chemical signatures of plants, other lepidians, and the subtle changes in the air that signal the approach of weather or the presence of other creatures.

The lepidian body is covered in a fine, dense coat of scales that provides insulation and a degree of protection against moisture, and the scales are coloured in patterns that serve as camouflage in the dappled light of the forest. Their hands are small and slender, with long, fine fingers that are capable of delicate work, and their feet are less developed, for the lepidian body is built for the air rather than the ground. The lepidian sense of touch is highly developed, particularly in the tips of the antennae, and the lepidians use their antennae to explore surfaces and materials with a precision that rivals the gnomish sense of touch.

## Life Cycle

Lepidians are short to moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of thirty to fifty years under favourable conditions. They mature quickly, reaching full stature and the capacity for flight within five or six years. Lepidian women lay clutches of fifty to one hundred eggs on the underside of leaves in the forest, and the eggs hatch within weeks into larvae that are wholly different in form from the adult, soft-bodied, legged, and terrestrial, feeding on the leaves of the host plant. The larvae grow rapidly, passing through several moults over a period of months before entering a pupal stage that lasts several weeks, during which the body undergoes a complete transformation into the winged adult form.

The lepidian life cycle is the most complex of any sapient people, for the larval and pupal stages are wholly different from the adult in form, habit, and diet, and the transformation between them is a dramatic and sustained process. The mortality rate among the larvae is high, for they are soft-bodied and vulnerable to predation by the birds, insects, and other creatures of the forest, but the extraordinary number of eggs ensures that enough survive to sustain the population. Lepidian populations fluctuate with the seasons, declining in the winter when the cold reduces the availability of food and the adults become inactive, and increasing rapidly in the spring when the first generation of adults emerges from the pupae.

## Habitat and Distribution

Lepidians are found chiefly in the temperate forests of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), where the trees are large and the undergrowth is thick, and the nights are long enough to support a nocturnal way of life. They favour the mature woodland with a dense canopy and a diverse understorey, where the host plants for their larvae are abundant and the air is still enough for flight. Lepidian settlements are typically built in the upper canopy of the forest, suspended among the branches and the leaves, and they are so well concealed that a traveller may pass beneath a lepidian community without knowing it.

Smaller populations of lepidians are found in the wooded uplands of [Morvind](/content/morvind), where the coniferous forests of the lower slopes provide a suitable if harsher environment, and in the wooded foothills of the [Sahrana](/content/sahrana) northern coast, where the warm, humid forests support a different variety of lepidian. Lepidians are absent from the open plains, the mountains, and the cold regions, for their bodies require the shelter of trees and the moderate temperatures of the forest. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region determines the distribution of lepidian populations, and the lepidian range is restricted to the forested regions where the conditions suit them.

## Diet

The lepidian diet changes dramatically over the course of the life cycle, for the larvae and the adults have wholly different feeding habits. The larvae are herbivorous, feeding on the leaves of the host plants upon which they were laid, and they consume large quantities of foliage as they grow, eating almost continuously until they enter the pupal stage. The adults feed upon nectar, the sap of trees, and the fluids of overripe fruit, and their mouthparts are adapted to a liquid diet, with a long, coiled proboscis that is used to draw fluids from flowers and from the wounds in bark.

The adult lepidian consumes relatively little, for its metabolism is slow and its energy needs are met largely by the reserves built up during the larval stage. The lepidians do not cook or prepare food in any manner, and their diet is entirely raw and liquid. Water is obtained from the dew that forms on leaves and from the moisture in the nectar and fruit that they consume. The lepidian approach to food is simple and individual, for each lepidian feeds itself, and the sharing of meals is unknown among the adults, though the larvae feed communally upon the host plant.

## Ecology

Lepidians occupy the niche of a small-bodied, nocturnal, flying forager in the temperate forest ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is modest, for their numbers are small and their consumption is light, but their role as pollinators is significant, for the lepidians are among the primary nocturnal pollinators of the forest, and their movements between flowers transfer pollen that sustains the reproduction of many plant species. The lepidian presence in a forest is generally associated with a healthy and diverse plant community, and the lepidians are an important component of the forest ecosystem.

Lepidians share the temperate forests with [elves](/content/elves) and [gnomes](/content/gnomes), and the three peoples coexist in a relationship that is generally peaceful and mutually beneficial. The elves tend the forest canopy, the gnomes the understorey and the floor, and the lepidians the night, and each people occupies a different temporal niche within the same woodland. Where lepidians encounter [corvids](/content/corvids), the relationship is wary, for the corvids are diurnal predators of the insects that share the forest with the lepidians, and the lepidians are vulnerable to predation during the hours when they are inactive.
