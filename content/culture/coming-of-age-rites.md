## The Threshold of Adulthood

Coming of age rites are observed by every people of the known world, marking the transition from childhood to adulthood and the acceptance of the responsibilities that adult status entails. The precise form of the rite varies considerably from one culture to another, reflecting the different values and conditions of each community, but the fundamental purpose is the same: the formal recognition that the child has attained the maturity necessary to participate fully in the life of the community.

The age at which the coming of age rite is conducted varies with the physical development of the child and the traditions of the community. The human peoples of the temperate regions typically conduct the rite at the age of twelve to fourteen years, when the child has reached the physical maturity that the community regards as the threshold of adulthood. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) conduct the rite at the age of fifteen years, reflecting the faster development of the dwarven body. The [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the plains conduct the rite at the age of ten years, when the centaur child is considered capable of the responsibilities of adult life.

## The Vaelic Rite of the Still Flame

In the Vael river valley, the coming of age rite is conducted within the tradition of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) and is one of the principal ceremonies of the religious year. The rite is conducted at the temple of the village, presided over by the priest, and is attended by the family of the child and the adult members of the parish.

The rite begins with the child's formal profession of the faith of the Still Flame, a declaration spoken before the altar and witnessed by the assembled congregation. The child acknowledges the eternal flame as the source of all order and life and commits to the observance of the commandments of the faith. The profession is followed by the blessing of the priest, who anoints the child's forehead with oil from the eternal flame and speaks the words that mark the child's acceptance into the adult community of the faithful.

The rite concludes with the presentation of the child to the parish council, which formally recognises the child's attainment of adult status. The newly recognised adult is now entitled to participate in the councils of the parish, to enter into contracts, and to marry according to the custom of [Hands Joined](/content/culture/hands-joined). The rite is followed by a celebration in the child's household, attended by the family and the neighbours, who bring gifts and share in the feast that marks the occasion.

## The Plains Hunt and the Centaur Trial

The coming of age rite of the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) is conducted in the context of the traditional hunt that is the central institution of the centaur culture. The rite is called the First Hunt, and it is the moment when the centaur child is first permitted to join the adult hunters in the pursuit of the great game of the plains.

The First Hunt begins with the preparation of the child by the elders of the tribe, who instruct the child in the rituals and the obligations of the hunt. The child is painted with the traditional patterns that mark the hunter, and the elders bless the child's weapons and the child's horse. The child joins the hunting party at the beginning of the summer season, and the child is expected to participate in the pursuit of the game as a full member of the hunting party.

The successful conclusion of the First Hunt is marked by the presentation of the child to the tribe at the evening fire, where the child recounts the events of the hunt and the elders pronounce the child a full member of the community. The child who fails in the First Hunt is given another opportunity in the following season, but the child who succeeds is recognised as an adult and is entitled to the privileges and the responsibilities of adult status.

## The Descent of the Dwarven Child

The coming of age rite of the dwarves is conducted within the deep chambers of the mountain, a ceremony that marks the child's acceptance into the community of the craftsmen. The rite is conducted at the age of fifteen years, when the dwarven child is considered ready for the binding to the master that will define the child's adult life.

The rite begins with the child's descent into the deepest forge of the hall, a journey that the child must undertake alone. The child carries a rough stone that has been selected by the master of the forge, and the child's task is to shape the stone into a serviceable tool within the space of a single day. The child works at the forge without assistance, applying the skills that have been learned during the years of preparation.

The completion of the tool is the moment of the child's recognition as an adult. The tool is presented to the thane of the hall, who examines it and pronounces it acceptable. The thane then accepts the child into the adult community of the hall, and the child is bound to the master who will oversee the child's further training. The rite is followed by a feast in the hall of feasts, attended by the entire community of the hall, which celebrates the child's achievement and welcomes the child into the adult life of the dwarven people.

## The Sahrani Voyage of the Merchant Child

The coming of age rite of the [Sahrani city-states](/content/culture/sahrani-city-life) is conducted in the context of the trade that is the foundation of the Sahrani civilisation. The rite is called the First Voyage, and it is the moment when the child of the merchant household is first permitted to accompany a trading expedition to a distant port.

The First Voyage begins with the preparation of the child by the elders of the merchant family, who instruct the child in the principles of the trade and the customs of the distant ports. The child is given a small amount of capital, provided by the family, which the child is expected to invest in the purchases that will be made at the destination. The child accompanies the merchant vessel on its journey, learning the skills of the sea and the conduct of the trade.

The successful conclusion of the First Voyage is marked by the return of the child to the family counting house, where the child presents the accounts of the journey and the profits that the child's investments have yielded. The child who has demonstrated the competence to manage the trade is recognised as an adult and is entitled to participate in the family's commercial enterprises as a full partner. The child who has failed to manage the investment successfully is given another opportunity in the following season, but the reputation of the child is diminished until the child demonstrates the competence that the merchant culture requires.

## The Significance of the Rites

Coming of age rites are a recognition of the transition from the dependence of childhood to the autonomy and the responsibility of adulthood. The rites are also a reaffirmation of the values of the community, a public declaration of the qualities that the community regards as essential to adult status. The differences in the form of the rites across the known world reflect the differences in the values that each culture holds most important. The Vaelic rite emphasises faith and community membership, the centaur rite emphasises courage and skill, the dwarven rite emphasises craft and competence, and the Sahrani rite emphasises commercial ability and the management of resources. Each rite serves the same purpose: the integration of the young person into the adult life of the community and the transmission of the values that will sustain the community across the generations.
