## Overview

The Rites of the [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) Holds are the principal seasonal observances of the dwarven communities of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim), the federation of mountain holds that occupies the eastern ranges of the [The Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands). The rites are not a single ceremony but a cycle of observances that extends across the turning of the underground year, and the observances mark the principal events of the life of the mountain and the work of the holds. The rites are observed in every hold of the federation, from the great central halls of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) itself to the most remote of the workings in the high country, though the customs that accompany each observance vary from one hold to another, and the chronicler notes that no two holds observe the rites in precisely the same manner. The rites are above all an expression of the bond between the dwarven people and the mountain that is their home, and the rituals that accompany them are directed toward the formal acknowledgement of the gifts that the mountain provides.

## Origins and the Mountain

The origins of the rites lie in the practical necessities of the dwarven mining life, for the early clans that inhabited the mountains of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) ranges developed a cycle of observances to mark the principal events of the mining year. The early clans observed the opening of the new workings in the spring, the descent into the deeper galleries in the summer, the consolidation of the props and the supports in the autumn, and the sealing of the upper workings against the winter storms in the winter. The observances were refined over the generations into the tradition that is observed in the present age, and the tradition has acquired a meaning that is independent of the practical purposes of the founding. The tradition is not confined to the holds of the eastern ranges, for the dwarven communities of the [Deep Realm of Karhold](/content/government/national/deep-realm-of-karhold) across the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) observe a parallel cycle whose rhythms are recognisably similar to those of the Stoneheim holds, the product of a shared dwarven tradition that has been adapted to the geology of each range.

The rites are conducted in the principal chamber of each hold, and the principal chamber is typically the great hall that occupies the centre of the settlement and that serves as the meeting place of the elders and the craftsmen. The great hall is illuminated by the great lamps that the holds maintain for the purpose, and the lamps burn with a steady flame that is fed by the oil that the holds produce from the minerals of the deep galleries. The elder of the hold presides over the rites, and the elder is assisted by the masters of the principal crafts and the representatives of the families whose members work the galleries of the mountain.

## The Rite of the Opening

The Rite of the Opening is the first of the principal observances of the cycle, and the rite is conducted in the spring at the moment when the snow upon the high passes begins to break and the workings of the new season may safely be opened. The rite is observed in the great hall of the hold, and the rite is attended by the elders, the craftsmen, and the families whose members will descend into the new workings in the weeks that follow. The rite begins with the customary invocation that the elder addresses to the mountain, and the invocation is a formal request for the mountain to permit the opening of the new season's workings and to grant the safety of the galleries to the miners who will work them.

The elder then strikes the floor of the great hall with the ceremonial hammer that the hold maintains for the purpose, and the striking of the hammer is the signal for the commencement of the new season. The hammer is a tool of considerable antiquity that has been preserved in the hold from one generation to the next, and the hammer is struck only at the principal observances of the cycle. The striking of the hammer is answered by the striking of the working hammers of the craftsmen who stand in the gallery that surrounds the hall, and the answering of the strike is the formal acknowledgement of the community that the new season has begun. The chronicler notes that the Rite of the Opening is one of the most solemn of the observances of the cycle, and the occasion is marked by a stillness in the galleries that the customary discipline of the hold requires.

## The Rite of the Vein

The Rite of the Vein is the second of the principal observances of the cycle, and the rite is conducted in the early summer at the moment when the principal vein of the new season has been reached by the workings of the miners. The rite is observed at the face of the vein itself, and the rite is attended by the elder of the hold, the master of the mining craft, and the miners whose work has opened the vein. The rite begins with the customary examination that the elder conducts of the stone that has been broken from the face, and the examination is the formal recognition that the vein is of the quality that the customary standards of the hold require.

The elder then offers the customary thanks to the mountain for the gift of the vein, and the offering is made by the laying of a small portion of the broken stone upon the altar that the miners have erected at the face of the working. The altar is a temporary construction of dressed stone that is assembled for the occasion and that is taken down at the conclusion of the rite, and the laying of the portion upon the altar is the formal moment of the observance. The elder pronounces the customary words as the portion is laid, and the words are an acknowledgement of the bounty of the mountain and a prayer for the continuance of the bounty in the workings that are to follow. The rite is concluded by the customary feast that the hold provides at the face of the working, and the feast is the occasion for the breaking of bread and the sharing of the ale that the miners have carried to the gallery.

## The Rite of the Props

The Rite of the Props is the third of the principal observances of the cycle, and the rite is conducted in the late summer at the moment when the props and the supports of the principal galleries must be renewed in preparation for the stresses that the autumn rains bring to the mountain. The rite is observed in the gallery that has been chosen for the purpose, and the rite is attended by the elder of the hold, the master of the carpentry craft, and the carpenters whose work will renew the supports. The rite begins with the customary examination that the elder conducts of the props that are presently in place, and the examination is the formal recognition of the props that must be replaced and the props that may be left to stand for another season.

The carpenters then erect the new props in the positions that the elder has indicated, and the erection is conducted with the customary forms that the carpentry craft has established. The first prop is set in place by the master of the craft, and the setting is accompanied by the customary words that the master addresses to the assembly. The words are an invocation of the strength of the mountain and a prayer for the endurance of the prop through the season that is coming. The setting of the first prop is the formal moment of the observance, and the moment is marked by the striking of the working hammers of the carpenters who stand in the gallery, the striking being the customary answer that the craft requires. The chronicler notes that the Rite of the Props is an observance of great practical importance, for the safety of the workings through the winter depends upon the props that the rite consecrates.

## The Rite of the Sealing

The Rite of the Sealing is the fourth of the principal observances of the cycle, and the rite is conducted in the autumn at the moment when the upper workings of the hold are sealed against the winter storms. The rite is observed at the mouth of the upper gallery that has been chosen for the sealing, and the rite is attended by the elder of the hold, the masters of the principal crafts, and the miners whose work will conduct the sealing. The rite begins with the customary procession that the community conducts from the great hall to the mouth of the gallery, and the procession is accompanied by the customary songs that the hold maintains for the occasion.

The sealing is conducted by the masons of the hold, who build the wall of dressed stone that will close the mouth of the gallery against the weather of the winter. The first stone is laid by the elder, and the laying is accompanied by the customary words that the elder addresses to the assembly. The words are a formal commendation of the gallery to the care of the mountain until the opening of the next season, and the words are a recognition that the workings of the upper levels may not be safely reopened until the spring. The laying of the first stone is the formal moment of the observance, and the moment is marked by the customary answer that the masons give with the striking of their hammers upon the dressed stone. The chronicler notes that the sealing of the upper workings is a melancholy occasion, for the galleries that are sealed contain the places where many of the miners have laboured through the season, and the sealing is an acknowledgement that the work of those galleries is concluded for the year.

## The Rite of the Deep

The Rite of the Deep is the fifth of the principal observances of the cycle, and the rite is conducted in the winter at the moment when the community gathers in the deepest chambers of the hold to mark the turning of the year. The rite is observed in the chamber that has been chosen for the purpose, and the chamber is typically the lowest of the great halls of the hold, far beneath the surface of the mountain and far from the upper workings that have been sealed against the storms. The rite is attended by the elders, the craftsmen, and the families of the hold, and the gathering is one of the largest of the underground year.

The rite begins with the customary lighting of the great lamps that the chamber maintains, and the lighting is the formal signal that the observance has commenced. The elder then addresses the assembly upon the events of the year that is ending, and the address includes the customary recounting of the principal achievements of the hold and the customary remembrance of the miners who have died in the workings through the year. The remembrance is marked by the striking of the ceremonial hammer upon the floor of the chamber, and the striking is the formal moment of the rite. The striking is answered by the striking of the working hammers of the craftsmen who stand in the gallery that surrounds the chamber, and the answering is the customary recognition that the community pays to the dead. The rite is concluded by the customary feast that the hold provides in the great hall, and the feast is the occasion for the sharing of the food that the hold has stored against the winter.

## The Rite of the Forge

The Rite of the Forge is the sixth of the principal observances of the cycle, and the rite is conducted in the late winter at the moment when the forges of the hold are rekindled after the customary suspension of the work during the darkest weeks of the year. The rite is observed at the principal forge of the hold, and the rite is attended by the elder of the hold, the master of the smithing craft, and the smiths whose work will resume with the rekindling. The rite begins with the customary examination that the elder conducts of the forge and the tools that have been laid aside for the suspension, and the examination is the formal recognition that the forge is in a condition to resume the work.

The master of the craft then kindles the fire of the forge from the embers that have been preserved through the suspension, and the kindling is accompanied by the customary words that the master addresses to the assembly. The words are an invocation of the heat of the mountain and a prayer for the strength of the smiths whose work the forge will support through the year that is coming. The kindling of the fire is the formal moment of the observance, and the moment is marked by the customary answer that the smiths give with the striking of their hammers upon the anvils of the forge. The chronicler notes that the Rite of the Forge is one of the most joyful of the observances of the cycle, for the rekindling of the forge is the signal that the dark weeks of the suspension are at an end and that the customary work of the hold may resume.

## The Role of the Deep Speech

The [Deep Speech](/content/language/pseudo/deep-speech) plays an important part in the observances of the cycle, and the deep rhythms of the stone are used to mark the principal moments of each rite. The elder strikes the floor of the great hall or the wall of the gallery with the ceremonial hammer, and the striking is answered by the striking of the working hammers of the craftsmen in the galleries that surround the place of the observance. The answer travels through the stone of the mountain, and the answer is heard in the distant workings of the hold as clearly as it is heard in the place of the observance itself. The chronicler notes that the deep rhythms are a distinctive feature of the rites, and the rhythms are understood by every dwarf who works the stone of the mountain.

The deep rhythms are also used to coordinate the observances of the different holds of the federation, and a striking at the great hall of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) is answered by the striking of the principal holds of the federation in a sequence that the customs of the cycle have established. The sequence is a measure of the unity of the federation, and the unity is one of the principal assurances that the rites provide. The chronicler notes that the deep rhythms are among the most distinctive of the dwarven customs, and the rhythms are a reminder that the holds of the federation are bound together by the stone that is the common foundation of their life.

## Significance

The Rites of the Stoneheim Holds are the principal seasonal observances of the dwarven communities of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim), and the rites are observed in every hold of the federation from the great central halls of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) to the most remote of the workings in the high country. The rites are an expression of the bond between the dwarven people and the mountain that is their home, and the rituals that accompany them are directed toward the formal acknowledgement of the gifts that the mountain provides. The chronicler notes that the rites have acquired a civic as well as a religious significance that is recognised by the council of the federation and by the elders of the individual holds, and the rites are one of the customary acts of the community at the turning of the underground year. The rites endure as a celebration of the bond between the people and the mountain and as a reminder that the stone that shelters the dwarven communities is the stone that the dwarven communities work, and that the work is the foundation of the life that the mountain sustains.
