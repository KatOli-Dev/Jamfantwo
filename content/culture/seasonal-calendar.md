## The Measurement of Time

The marking of time is a practice of fundamental importance to every people of the known world, the means by which the community organises the activities of the year and maintains the connection between the present and the cycles of the natural world. The calendars of the different cultures vary according to the climatic conditions of each region and the astronomical observations that each people has developed, but the underlying purpose is the same everywhere: the regulation of the agricultural, the commercial, and the religious activities of the community according to the patterns of the seasons.

The year is divided everywhere into the periods that are defined by the movement of the sun and the moon and the stars, the cycles that the peoples of the known world have observed since the earliest period of the recorded history. The solar year of three hundred and sixty-five days is the foundation of the calendars of the settled peoples, supplemented by the lunar months that are used for the regulation of the festivals and the ceremonies.

## The Calendar of the Vael Valley

The calendar of the Vael river valley is the standard solar calendar of the temperate lands, divided into twelve months of thirty or thirty-one days and adjusted by the intercalation of the extra days at the end of the year. The year begins at the spring equinox, the time of the planting of the summer wheat and the observance of the [First-Grain Feast](/content/culture/first-grain-feast).

The months of the Vaelic calendar are named for the agricultural activities that are characteristic of each period. The first month is Seedmonth, the time of the planting of the spring crops. The second month is Growmonth, the time of the growth of the shoots. The third month is Weedmonth, the time of the cultivation of the fields. The fourth month is Haymonth, the time of the cutting of the hay. The fifth month is Heatmonth, the time of the greatest heat of the summer. The sixth month is Reapmonth, the time of the beginning of the harvest. The seventh month is Harvestmonth, the principal month of the harvest and the observance of the [Crown Harvest](/content/culture/crown-harvest). The eighth month is Pressmonth, the time of the pressing of the grapes and the brewing of the ale. The ninth month is Shedmonth, the time of the shedding of the leaves and the preparation of the fields for the winter. The tenth month is Slaughtermonth, the time of the slaughter of the animals and the preserving of the meat. The eleventh month is Coldmonth, the time of the deepest cold. The twelfth month is Restmonth, the time of the [Long Night Festival](/content/culture/long-night-festival) and the rest of the community.

The Vaelic year also includes the intercalary days that are inserted at the end of the Restmonth to align the calendar with the solar year. The intercalary days are a period of the celebration and the exchange of gifts, a time of the liminal space between the old year and the new.

## The Calendar of the Plains

The calendar of the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) and the human herders of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) is based on the cycles of the moon and the movements of the herds that are the foundation of the plains economy. The year is divided into the twelve lunar months, each of which is named for the condition of the grass and the behaviour of the animals.

The months of the plains calendar are named for the stages of the grass and the movements of the herds. The first month is the Greening, the time when the grass begins to grow after the winter. The second month is the Calving, the time when the young are born. The third month is the Flowering, the time when the grasses bloom. The fourth month is the Fattening, the time when the herds grow strong. The fifth month is the Gathering, the time of the [Plains High Days](/content/culture/plains-high-days) and the principal gathering of the tribes. The sixth month is the Hunting, the time of the principal hunt. The seventh month is the Drying, the time when the grass begins to dry. The eighth month is the Moving, the time when the tribes begin the migration to the winter pastures. The ninth month is the Settling, the time when the tribes arrive at the winter camps. The tenth month is the Cold, the time of the coldest weather. The eleventh month is the Waiting, the time of the waiting for the spring. The twelfth month is the Breaking, the time when the ice breaks and the spring begins.

## The Calendar of the Dwarven Halls

The calendar of the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) is based on the cycles of the forge and the rituals of the hall, a system of the timekeeping that reflects the conditions of the underground life. The dwarven year is divided into the twelve periods that are named for the stages of the craftwork and the ceremonies of the hall.

The periods of the dwarven calendar are named for the activities of the forge. The first period is the Kindling, the time of the renewal of the forge fires. The second period is the Forging, the time of the principal work of the smiths. The third period is the Tempering, the time of the cooling and the strengthening of the metals. The fourth period is the Polishing, the time of the finishing of the works. The fifth period is the Trading, the time when the dwarven goods are exchanged for the goods of the surface world. The sixth period is the Mending, the time of the repair of the tools and the structures of the hall. The seventh period is the Quarrying, the time of the extraction of the stone and the metal from the mines. The eighth period is the Smelting, the time of the purification of the ores. The ninth period is the Casting, the time of the pouring of the molten metal into the moulds. The tenth period is the Carving, the time of the decoration of the works. The eleventh period is the Remembering, the time of the [Stoneheim Holds Rites](/content/culture/stoneheim-holds-rites) and the honouring of the ancestors. The twelfth period is the Resting, the time of the quiet and the preparation for the new year.

## The Calendar of the Sahrani Coast

The calendar of the [Sahrani city-states](/content/culture/sahrani-city-life) is based on the cycles of the sun and the stars, the astronomical observations that the Sahrani scholars have developed over the centuries of the navigation and the trade. The year is divided into the twelve months that are named for the constellations that are visible in the night sky of the southern latitudes.

The months of the Sahrani calendar are named for the constellations that rise at the beginning of each month. The first month is the Rising of the Ship, the constellation that marks the beginning of the sailing season. The second month is the Rising of the Scales, the constellation of the balance and the trade. The third month is the Rising of the Salt, the constellation of the salt pans and the harvest of the sea. The fourth month is the Rising of the Palm, the constellation of the date harvest. The fifth month is the Rising of the Camel, the constellation of the caravan season. The sixth month is the Rising of the Sword, the constellation of the summer heat. The seventh month is the Rising of the Star, the constellation of the brightest star of the southern sky. The eighth month is the Rising of the Crescent, the constellation of the moon. The ninth month is the Rising of the Veil, the constellation that marks the beginning of the autumn rains. The tenth month is the Rising of the Gate, the constellation of the ending of the caravan season. The eleventh month is the Rising of the Shroud, the constellation of the winter. The twelfth month is the Rising of the Phoenix, the constellation of the renewal and the beginning of the new year.

## The Calendar of the Northern Coast

The calendar of the northern coast is based on the cycles of the sea and the sun, the patterns that govern the life of the maritime communities. The year is divided into the two principal seasons, the summer of the light and the winter of the darkness, each of which is subdivided into the periods that are marked by the movements of the fish and the storms.

The northern calendar begins at the spring equinox, the time of the breaking of the ice and the opening of the sea. The summer season is divided into the period of the melting, the period of the fishing, the period of the whaling, and the period of the trading. The winter season is divided into the period of the storms, the period of the confinement, the period of the [Long Night Festival](/content/culture/long-night-festival), and the period of the waiting for the spring.

## The Universal Cycle

The calendars of the known world reflect the diversity of the conditions and the values of the different cultures, but the underlying pattern is the same everywhere: the division of the year into the periods that are defined by the cycles of the natural world and the regulation of the activities of the community according to the patterns of the seasons. The calendar is the instrument of the order and the continuity, the means by which the community maintains the connection between the present and the past and between the activities of the people and the cycles of the earth and the sky. The festivals and the ceremonies that mark the turning of the year are the reaffirmation of the bonds that connect the community to the natural world and to the traditions of the ancestors, and the calendar is the record of the time that the community has measured and the time that the community will continue to measure as the ages unfold.
