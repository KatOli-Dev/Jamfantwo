## The Festival of the Deep Singing

The Stone Song Festival is the principal festival of the dwarven peoples of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm), observed at the winter solstice and the summer solstice. The festival marks the awakening of the song within the stone and the shaping of the world that the song accomplished, and it is the occasion upon which the dwarves renew their connection to the song that raised the mountains and formed the deep halls.

The festival is observed in the great halls of the holds and the realm, carved into the living rock of the mountains of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The observance is the most important of the dwarven ceremonial year, and the dwarves who are separated from the great halls by distance or by work make every effort to return to their holds for the festival.

## The Solstice Observance

The Stone Song Festival is observed at the winter solstice and the summer solstice, the two turning points of the year when the sun is at its greatest distance from the mountain and its greatest proximity. The winter solstice observance is the more elaborate of the two, for the winter is the season when the dwarves gather in the great halls and the work of the mines and the forges slows, and the community has the leisure to devote to the ceremonies.

The summer solstice observance is a briefer observance, conducted at the entrances of the holds and the mines where the summer light reaches the underground. The summer observance includes the display of the works that the carvers have completed during the year and the beginning of the work on the new pieces that will be shaped during the following year.

The solstices are chosen for the festival because the song that the dwarves honour is the song that raised the mountains and set the stone in its place, and the sun is the companion of the stone in the dwarven understanding. The relationship between the sun and the stone is the pattern that the dwarven tradition celebrates.

## The Singing of the Deep Songs

The singing of the deep songs is the central observance of the Stone Song Festival, conducted by the stone-singers of the holds and the realm. The stone-singers are the dwarves who have the gift of hearing the song within the stone and of reproducing it in the voices of the community, and they are trained from youth in the discipline of the deep singing.

The deep songs are sung in the great hall of the hold, where the acoustics of the carved stone amplify and enrich the voices of the singers. The songs are not written down but are preserved in the memory of the stone-singers and transmitted from one generation to the next through the practice of the teaching.

The deep songs are the songs that the stone itself sings, the vibrations that the dwarves perceive in the living rock of the mountain. The stone-singers who reproduce these songs in their voices are not creating the music but are translating the music of the stone into a form that the dwarven community can hear and understand. The songs are the means by which the dwarves maintain their connection to the stone and to the song that awakened them.

## The Display of the New Works

The Stone Song Festival is the occasion upon which the carvers and the crafters of the holds display the works that they have completed during the year. The displays are arranged in the great hall and the adjoining chambers, and the community gathers to examine the works and to acknowledge the skill of the makers.

The works that are displayed include the carvings in stone and metal that the dwarven crafters have produced, the furniture and the fittings that the woodworkers have shaped, and the textiles and the tapestries that the weavers have created. The works are displayed without competition, for the dwarven tradition does not rank the works of the makers against one another but acknowledges the contribution that each maker has made to the beauty and the use of the hold.

The ceremony of the new carving is conducted during the display, when the master carvers begin the work on the new pieces that will be completed during the following year. The master carver selects the stone or the metal for the new piece and speaks the words of the opening, acknowledging the song that the material contains and asking for the guidance of the hands. The first stroke of the cutting tool is struck at the moment of the solstice, and the work of the new year begins.

## The Feast of the Shared Stone

The feast of the shared stone is the celebration that follows the singing and the display, held in the great hall of the hold. The feast is prepared from the stores that the hold has laid in during the year and from the contributions of the surrounding communities. The feast includes the roasted meats from the herds of the mountain pastures, the bread baked from the grain that the lowland trade provides, and the ale and the mead that the brewers of the hold have prepared.

The feast is conducted with the formality that the dwarven tradition requires, with the seats arranged according to the precedence of the holds and the households. The eldest stone-singer speaks the blessing of the song before the feast is distributed, and the community responds with the words of the deep singing.

The feast is the occasion for the renewal of the bonds between the holds and between the households, and the toasts that are offered during the feast acknowledge the connections that the dwarven community maintains.

## The Observance of the Ancestors

The Stone Song Festival includes the observance of the ancestors, conducted in the Hall of the Ancestors of each hold. The names of the dwarves who have died since the previous solstice are recited by the stone-singers, and the community acknowledges the departure of the ancestors from the world of the living.

The observance is conducted with the solemnity that the occasion requires, and the stone-singers sing the songs of departure that tradition preserves. The community listens in silence, and the names of the ancestors are carved into the stone of the Hall of the Ancestors in the presence of the community.

The observance is followed by the recommitment of the living to the work of the hold, conducted by the king or the council of the hold. The community renews its oaths to the hold and to the song, and the elders of the hold speak the words of encouragement that tradition requires.
