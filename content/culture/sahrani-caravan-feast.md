## The Desert Trade Cuisine

The [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) Caravan Feast is the principal culinary tradition of the inland cities and caravan routes of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), a body of practice and custom that has grown up over the centuries around the necessities of the desert trade. The feast is not a single dish but a tradition of communal eating accompanying the arrival and departure of caravans at the inland oases, observed by the merchants of the caravan cities and by the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides who conduct the caravans across the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). The feast is one of the most distinctive of the regional cuisines of the known world, and the dishes developed for the occasion are among the most carefully prepared of any in the inland regions.

## The Necessities of the Desert Crossing

The origins of the feast lie in the practical necessities of the caravan trade. Merchants and salawa undertaking the crossing of the desert must take their sustenance in a form that will endure the heat and dryness of the passage. Early caravan masters developed a repertoire of dishes prepared from ingredients available at the oases and from provisions carried upon the animals, refined over generations into the tradition observed in the present age. The custom has been adopted by the settled communities of the inland cities, and the feast is observed upon the arrival and departure of caravans and upon the customary festivals of the inland communities.

The feast is also a social occasion of considerable importance in the life of the inland communities, providing an opportunity for merchants and caravan masters to exchange news of the trade and the conditions of the routes. The feast is conducted in the courtyard of the principal inn of the oasis, with guests seated upon cushions around the perimeter of the courtyard. Seating is arranged according to the customary precedence of the trade, with senior merchants at the head and junior merchants and salawa guides at the foot. The master of the inn presides over the feast and is responsible for the conduct of the service and the observance of customary forms.

## The Stew, Rice, and Sweet Course

The dishes served at the feast are the product of the long development of the caravan cuisine, prepared from ingredients available at the oases and from provisions carried upon the animals. The principal dish is the stew of dried meat and dried vegetables, the staple provisions of the caravan, prepared in a great cauldron set upon the fire in the centre of the courtyard. The stew is flavoured with spices the caravans have carried from the coast, the source of the distinctive aroma of the dish. The stew is served with bread baked in the oven of the inn, broken by the guests and dipped into the stew according to the customary practice.

The second dish is rice prepared with dried fruits and nuts carried from the interior, served as a separate course following the stew. The rice is flavoured with saffron grown in the gardens of the inland oases, giving the dish a colour and fragrance the merchants of the coast associate with the inland trade. The third dish is a sweet prepared with honey and dates, the products of the oases, served at the conclusion of the feast as a mark of the hospitality of the host. The sweet is accompanied by tea brewed with mint grown in the gardens of the oases, the customary beverage of the inland communities.

## Seating, Washing, and the Order of Courses

The service of the feast is conducted according to customary forms that the inland communities have established, observed with a strictness that merchants of the coast have sometimes found surprising. The service begins with the washing of hands, performed by the servants of the inn who bring basins of water to the guests accompanied by the recitation of customary words recognising the dependence of the guest upon the hospitality of the host. The service continues with the presentation of the bread, broken by the senior merchant present and distributed to the guests by the servants.

The service of the stew is the central moment of the feast. The master of the inn places the first portion before the senior merchant present, who receives it and addresses a customary word of thanks. The master replies with the customary word of welcome that formally opens the feast. The stew is then served to the other guests by the servants, accompanied by conversation that is one of the principal pleasures of the occasion. The conversation is not merely social; merchants exchange news of the trade and the conditions of the routes, and the conversation is the principal means by which caravan masters keep themselves informed of the state of the inland trade.

## The Guides' Knowledge and Their Gifts

The [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) are the guides of the caravans and the keepers of the knowledge of the desert, and they play an important role in the customs of the feast. The salawa are seated among the guests and served according to the customary precedence the trade has established, but they are not subject to the precedence the merchants observe among themselves. The salawa are honoured as the bearers of the knowledge the trade depends upon, and they are served before merchants of equivalent rank. This inversion of customary precedence measures the importance the inland communities attach to the knowledge of the desert.

The salawa also contribute to the feast by bringing the products of the desert not available in the settled communities — desert truffles gathered in the deep erg, salt collected from the salt pans of the interior, and wild herbs gathered in the course of their journeys. These contributions are received with customary gratitude expressed by the host, and they measure the bond between the salawa and the settled communities that the trade sustains.

## Different Cities, Different Dishes

The feast varies among different inland cities and caravan routes. The feast of [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) is the most elaborate, including several additional courses not customary in smaller communities. The feast of [Torem](/content/location/settlement/town/torem) is the most cosmopolitan of the southern cities, including dishes adopted from the cuisine of northern traders who pass through the town. The feast of the oasis of [Shurra](/content/location/settlement/village/shurra) is the most traditional of the deep desert, including dishes prepared by the salawa according to recipes handed down through generations.

The feast has also influenced the cuisine of the coastal cities of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and the dishes of the inland trade are served in the inns of the coast catering to merchants who have undertaken the desert crossing. The feast has been adopted by the communities of the strait towns and maritime islands that have frequent contact with the traders of the inland cities, and the dishes of the feast are among the most familiar culinary offerings in the cosmopolitan ports of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden).

## The Feast's Importance to the Inland Trade

The Sahrani Caravan Feast is the principal culinary tradition of the inland cities and caravan routes of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), one of the most distinctive of the regional cuisines of the known world. The feast is a social occasion of considerable importance in the life of the inland communities, providing opportunity for merchants and caravan masters to exchange news of the trade and the conditions of the routes. The feast holds both civic and culinary significance, recognised by the authorities of the inland cities, and it is one of the customary acts of the community at the arrival and departure of caravans. The feast endures as a celebration of the trade that connects the coast to the interior, affirming that the inland trade is the foundation of the prosperity of the inland cities.
