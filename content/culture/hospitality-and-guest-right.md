## The Sacred Bond

Guest right is among the most ancient and most sacred institutions the peoples of the known world maintain. It is the formal recognition of the bond created when a traveller is received into a household, a bond that imposes obligations upon both parties and is enforceable under the customary law of every community. Guest right is the foundation of the travel and trade that connect the peoples of different lands, and its violation is regarded everywhere as one of the most serious offences a person can commit.

Guest right is established by the formal acceptance of a host's hospitality. The traveller who comes to the door and is admitted into the household is entitled to the host's protection for the duration of the visit, and the host is entitled to the guest's respect and observance of the household's customs. Guest right is not a matter of choice or personal inclination; it is a binding obligation enforced by the customary law of every community.

The duty of hospitality requires that the traveller be welcomed, provided with food and shelter, and protected from harm for the duration of the visit. The obligation of the host is matched by the obligation of the guest to respect the household and depart without causing harm or trouble. The forms of hospitality vary from one culture to another, reflecting the different conditions and values of each people, but the underlying principle is the same everywhere: the stranger who comes to the door is to be treated as a member of the household for the duration of the visit.

## The Three Days and the Three Gifts

The customary law of the temperate lands specifies the duration of guest right as three days and three nights, the period considered sufficient for the traveller to rest and conduct the business that brought them to the community. The host must provide the guest with food and shelter for this period, and the guest must accept the hospitality and depart at the conclusion of the period.

Guest right is established by the exchange of traditional gifts that symbolise the bond between host and guest. The host offers the customary cup of water or ale, the gift that signifies acceptance of the guest into the household. The guest offers a token of gratitude and respect. The exchange is the formal moment of establishment, and the bond created is binding upon both parties.

## Hospitality in the Vael Valley

In the Vael river valley, the duty of hospitality is a sacred obligation imposed by the teachings of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame). The faithful are taught that the stranger who comes to the door may be a manifestation of the eternal flame, sent to test the generosity of the household. The household must therefore welcome the stranger as the household would welcome the flame itself.

Guest right is enforced by the [Vael Parish Councils](/content/government/local/vael-parish-councils), which have the authority to impose penalties for its violation. The host who violates guest right by failing to provide required hospitality or by causing harm to the guest is subject to penalties including compensation, exclusion from the community, and in the most serious cases, exile from the valley. The guest who abuses the host's hospitality is subject to the same penalties.

The Vaelic custom begins with the greeting at the door, where the host speaks the traditional words of welcome and offers the guest the customary cup of water or ale. The guest is invited into the central room and offered a seat at the hearth, the place of honour. The host then shares the meal that is the principal expression of hospitality, sharing the food of the household without regard to quantity or quality. The guest who remains beyond the third day is expected to contribute to the work of the household, assisting with tasks appropriate to the guest's abilities.

## Hospitality of the Plains

The hospitality customs of the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) and the human herders of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) are adapted to the conditions of nomadic life, in which travellers are common and the provision of hospitality is a matter of survival. Guest right is established by the sharing of milk or water at the camp fire, the traditional gesture of welcome.

The traveller who comes to the camp is welcomed without question and provided with food and shelter for as long as required. The guest is invited to sit at the fire, and the host shares the food of the camp without regard to the guest's ability to repay. The guest is expected to observe the customs of the camp, respect the host's property, and depart when the guest's business is concluded.

Guest right is enforced by the elders of the tribe, who may impose penalties for violation, the most serious being exclusion from the protection of the tribe. The violator excluded from the tribe is left to the mercy of the elements and the predators of the plains, a fate few survive.

The hospitality of the plains extends also to the animals of the traveller. The horses and camels of the guest are provided with water and pasturage, and the guest is expected to provide the same hospitality to the host's animals when the host travels through the guest's territory. This obligation reflects that animals are essential to survival on the plains.

## Hospitality of the Dwarven Halls

The guest right of the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) is governed by the traditions of the hall, which require that the visitor to the mountain be welcomed with proper ceremonies and provided with the hospitality that dwarven tradition requires. Guest right is established by the formal presentation of the visitor to the thane, who speaks the words that accept the visitor into the hall's protection.

The visitor is received at the gate by the keeper of the gate, who escorts the visitor to the hall of feasts and presents the visitor to the thane. Dwarven hospitality includes the offering of food and ale, the provision of a chamber for the visitor's use, and the conduct of the visitor's business with the appropriate masters of the hall.

Guest right is enforced by the thane and by the authority of the [Stoneheim Thanes Council](/content/government/local/stoneheim-thanes-council) or the [Karhold Warden Councils](/content/government/local/karhold-realm-warden-councils). The violator is subject to penalties including payment of compensation, exclusion from the hall, and reporting of the violation to other dwarven halls. The visitor who abuses the hospitality of the hall is barred from returning and may be reported to the other halls of the dwarven people.

## Hospitality of the Desert

Guest right among the [Kharesh](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and [Vashari](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) desert nomads is governed by customs that regard guest right as the most sacred of obligations. It is established by the sharing of salt, the traditional symbol of the bond between host and guest.

Guest right is enforced by the elders of the tribe and by the customary law of the [Kharesh Blood Debt](/content/law/kharesh-blood-debt). The violator is subject to the blood debt, the obligation that the kin of the violated party are entitled to seek from the kin of the violator. The blood debt is the most serious penalty in the desert culture, and fear of it is the principal guarantee of observance of guest right.

## Hospitality of the Sahrani Coast

Guest right among the [Sahrani city-states](/content/culture/sahrani-city-life) is governed by the laws of the salt, the traditional symbol of the commercial bond that connects the merchants of the coast. It is established by the offering of coffee and dates, the traditional refreshment of Sahrani hospitality.

The traveller who comes to a Sahrani household is welcomed into the courtyard and offered the traditional refreshment. The customs are governed by the rules of the salt, the symbol of the bond between host and guest. The guest who has shared the host's salt is bound by the obligation of gratitude, and the host who has offered the salt is bound by the obligation of protection. The bond of salt is a sacred bond recognised by the customary law of the coast.

Guest right is enforced by the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court), which has the authority to impose penalties for violation. The violator is subject to exclusion from the trade of the city, the most serious penalty in the mercantile culture of the coast. A violator excluded from the trade cannot conduct the business that is the foundation of wealth and status in Sahrani society.

## Hospitality of the Northern Coast

The hospitality customs of the northern coast are adapted to the conditions of maritime life, where travellers are common and the provision of hospitality is a matter of necessity. The traveller who comes to a northern settlement is welcomed at the harbour and escorted to the longhouse of the harbour master, who provides food and shelter for the duration of the visit.

Northern custom includes the offering of food and ale, the provision of a bed in the longhouse, and the assistance of the harbour master in the conduct of the traveller's business. The guest is expected to contribute to the work of the household, assisting with tasks appropriate to the guest's abilities, and to depart when the traveller's business is concluded.

## The Universal Bond

Guest right is the universal bond that connects the peoples of the known world, the institution that makes travel and trade between communities possible. It is the recognition of the common humanity of host and guest, the acknowledgement that the stranger who comes to the door is entitled to the same consideration as a member of the community. Guest right is enforced by the customary law of every people, and its violation is punished by penalties each culture has developed for the protection of the sacred bond. The hospitality a host provides to a guest is the hospitality the host hopes to receive when the host becomes the guest in another land. The exchange of hospitality is the foundation of the relationships that connect the peoples of the known world, a bond of trust and mutual obligation that makes travel and trade possible.
