## The Monotheistic Faith of the Central Lowlands

The Still Flame is the monotheistic religion that has spread through the central and southern regions of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) in the later period of recorded history, a faith centred upon the worship of a single divine principle understood as an eternal and uncreated flame that sustains the order of the world. The faith was founded in the central lowlands by a prophet whose name is recorded in the scriptures of the church but whose historical identity is a matter of debate among scholars who have studied the early texts. The religion has been adopted as the principal faith of the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) and of several smaller kingdoms of the interior, and its influence continues to expand along the trade routes connecting the interior to the coast.

## The Prophet of the Lowlands

The founder of the Still Flame is known in the scriptures of the church as the Prophet of the Lowlands, a figure of obscure origin whose teachings were first recorded in the market towns of the central river valleys. The traditional account holds that the Prophet was a scribe of modest station who received a vision of the eternal flame while copying the chronicles of a minor temple of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/culture/sky-walkers-way). The vision revealed that the gods of the polytheist pantheon were not separate beings but emanations of a single divine principle, and that the worship of many gods was a misunderstanding of the unity that underlay all things.

The Prophet began to preach in the market towns, drawing small crowds of listeners who were attracted by the clarity of the message and the simplicity of its demands. The faith required no sacrifices, no pilgrimages, and no elaborate rituals — only the acknowledgement of the single flame and the conduct of a life in accordance with the order the flame sustained. The simplicity of the teaching was the foundation of its appeal, and the faith spread rapidly through the towns of the river valleys and along the trade routes connecting the interior to the coast.

The Prophet was opposed by the priests of the polytheist temples, who regarded the teaching as a threat to their authority and the social order they upheld. The opposition was strongest in the larger towns where the temples were wealthy and influential. The Prophet was arrested and brought before the magistrates of one of the principal towns of the lowlands, charged with disturbing the peace and undermining the public cult. The trial is recorded in the scriptures of the church with considerable detail. The Prophet defended the teaching with arguments that the magistrates found persuasive, and the charges were dismissed. The acquittal is regarded by the faithful as a sign of the divine favour that attended the mission of the Prophet.

## The Eternal and Uncreated Flame

The central doctrine of the Still Flame is the unity of the divine principle, understood as an eternal flame that is the source of all order and life in the world. The flame is not a physical fire but a metaphysical reality, the principle of unity that holds the world together and sustains the patterns by which the world operates. The flame is eternal in the sense that it has no beginning and no end, and uncreated in the sense that it is not the product of any prior cause. The flame simply is, and the world is an expression of its nature.

The faith teaches that the flame is present in all things, but not in the manner that would permit the worship of any created thing. The flame is present as the principle of order that every created thing manifests, and the acknowledgement of the flame is the recognition of the order that underlies the apparent chaos of the world. The faithful are taught to see the flame in the growth of the grain, the flow of the river, and the light of the sun, but they are also taught that the flame is not identical with any of these. The distinction between the flame and its manifestations is a central concern of the theology of the church.

The faith also teaches that the flame is good and that the order it sustains is a good order. The existence of evil and suffering in the world is a matter of considerable theological debate within the church. The orthodox position holds that evil is not a positive reality but an absence of order, a failure of the world to conform to the pattern the flame sustains. The position is not universally accepted, and dissenting traditions within the church have proposed alternative explanations that attribute greater reality to the principle of evil.

## Spread through the River Valleys

The expansion of the Still Flame from its place of origin in the central lowlands to the surrounding regions was accomplished through the activity of missionaries who travelled along the trade routes and through the patronage of rulers who adopted the faith for their kingdoms. The pattern of expansion was consistent: missionaries arrived in a town, preached the faith to the inhabitants, and established a small community of believers. The community grew gradually as the inhabitants found the teaching persuasive and as the moral conduct of the believers attracted favourable notice.

The adoption of the faith by the rulers of the Aldreth was the decisive event in the spread of the Still Flame through the eastern lowlands. The king of the Aldreth was converted by a missionary who had been sent to the court following a period of illness that the court physicians had been unable to cure. The king recovered after the missionary prayed over him, and the recovery was attributed to the intercession of the flame. The king ordered the establishment of the faith throughout the kingdom, and the temples of the polytheist religions were gradually converted into churches of the Still Flame.

The adoption of the faith by lesser kingdoms of the interior followed a similar pattern, as rulers who had observed the stability and prosperity of the Aldreth under the new faith chose to adopt it for their own realms. The expansion was not accomplished without resistance, and the conflicts between the faithful of the Still Flame and the adherents of the Sky Walkers' Way were most intense in the early period of the faith's expansion. The reconciliation achieved between the adherents of the two religions in the present age is a measure of the maturity the religions have attained.

## Temples and the Hierarchy of the Church

The places of worship of the Still Flame are called temples, though they differ substantially from the temples of the polytheist religions. A temple of the Still Flame is a simple hall oriented toward the east, with an altar at the eastern end upon which a flame is kept burning continuously, symbolising the eternal flame that is the object of the faith. The flame is kindled from the embers of the previous year's flame, and the continuity of the flame from one year to the next is a matter of considerable importance to the faithful. A temple whose flame has been extinguished is regarded as having lost its sanctity until the flame is rekindled by a priest of appropriate rank.

The clergy of the Still Flame are organised into a hierarchy consisting of deacons, priests, and bishops. Deacons are the junior clergy who assist in the conduct of services and the administration of the temple. Priests are the clergy who conduct the principal services of the faith, including the kindling of the flame, the blessing of the faithful, and the administration of the rites. Bishops are the senior clergy who oversee the temples of a region and who are responsible for the ordination of priests and deacons. The head of the church is the First Warder, a title that reflects the understanding of the bishop's role as the guardian of the flame rather than its master.

The hierarchy is governed by a council of bishops that meets at intervals to deliberate upon matters of doctrine and discipline. The council is the highest authority of the church, and its decisions are binding upon the faithful. The council has no authority over secular rulers, and the relationship between the church and the kingdoms in which it is established is governed by agreements that vary from one kingdom to another. In the Aldreth, the church and the crown maintain a close relationship, and the king is crowned by the First Warder in a ceremony that acknowledges the divine origin of royal authority.

## Controversies and Relations with the State

The Still Flame has been the subject of controversy throughout its history, both within the church and in its relations with the secular powers. The principal internal controversy concerns the nature of the flame and the manner in which it is present in the world. The orthodox position holds that the flame is present in all things as the principle of order, but that it is not present in the manner that would permit the worship of any created thing. A dissenting tradition known as the Manifestists holds that the flame is present in certain objects and places in a more concentrated manner, and that these objects and places are therefore worthy of special veneration. The controversy between the orthodox and the Manifestists has been a recurring source of division within the church.

The relationship between the church and the secular powers has been a matter of ongoing negotiation. The church teaches that the flame is the source of all legitimate authority, but that the authority of the flame is exercised through the secular rulers who maintain the order of the world. The teaching has been interpreted differently by different rulers, some of whom have sought to subordinate the church to the authority of the crown, while others have accepted the independence of the church in matters of doctrine and discipline. The balance between the authority of the church and the authority of the crown is a matter that each kingdom has worked out according to its own circumstances and traditions.

## The Faith in the Present Age

The Still Flame is the principal religion of the central and southern regions of Aethelar and has made some progress in the coastal regions of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), though the older animistic traditions of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) have proved more resistant to monotheistic teaching. The faith continues to expand, and the number of the faithful increases with each generation. The expansion of the faith has been accompanied by a deepening of theological reflection, and the scholars of the church have produced a substantial body of commentary upon the scriptures and the doctrines of the faith.

The Still Flame is the fulfilment of the religious traditions that preceded it, and its spread through the kingdoms of Aethelar is a measure of the unity that the faith has brought to the peoples of the continent. The reconciliation with the adherents of the Sky Walkers' Way, the older polytheist religion of the central grasslands, is a sign of the maturity the faith has attained. The faithful of the Still Flame look forward to a future in which the flame will illuminate every corner of the known world and bring all peoples into the unity of the one divine principle.
