## The Festival of the Departing Caravan

The Salt Caravan Festival is the principal festival of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coastal communities, observed at the departure of the first salt caravan of the season from the coastal cities to the interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The festival marks the beginning of the trading season and the departure of the caravans that carry the salt harvested from the coastal salt pans to the peoples of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) and the interior settlements.

The festival is observed in the principal coastal cities of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league), including [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), and [Sahran](/content/location/settlement/city/sahran), and in the smaller communities along the coast that participate in the salt trade. The festival is also observed by the inland communities that receive the caravans, though the observance takes a different form in the interior than it does on the coast.

## The Preparation of the Caravan

The preparation of the caravan begins in the weeks before the festival, when the salt that has been harvested from the coastal salt pans during the dry season is packed into the leather bags and loaded onto the pack animals. The salt is the product of months of labour by the salt-makers of the coastal communities, who have harvested the salt from the evaporation pans and refined it to the standard that the guild requires.

The caravan is assembled in the great square of the city, where the pack animals are arranged in the order of the march and the loads are inspected by the caravan master and the representatives of the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court). The salt is weighed and recorded in the caravan register, and the seals of the guild are affixed to the bags that contain the salt.

The caravan master is responsible for the conduct of the journey and for the safety of the salt and the pack animals. The master is chosen by the guild of the salt-makers from among the experienced caravan leaders and is given the authority to command the caravan and to make the decisions that the journey requires.

## The Blessing of the Caravan

The blessing of the caravan is the central observance of the festival, conducted by the priests of the [Sea Mother's Court](/content/religion/sea-mothers-court) at the harbour or at the edge of the salt pans. The priests invoke the Sea Mother's blessing upon the salt and upon the caravan that carries it, asking that the journey be safe and that the salt reach its destination in good condition.

The blessing is conducted with the formality that the tradition requires, with the priests reciting the prayers of the Sea Mother's Court and the faithful responding with the customary words of assent. The caravan master and the drivers kneel before the priests and receive the benediction, and the pack animals are sprinkled with the water that the priests have blessed.

The blessing is followed by the offering of the first salt, the small portion of the salt that is set aside for the Sea Mother and cast into the waters of the harbour. The offering is the acknowledgment of the gift that the Sea Mother gave, and the salt that is cast into the water is returned to the source from which it came.

## The Departure of the Caravan

The departure of the caravan is the occasion of the great celebration, when the people of the coastal city gather to watch the caravan begin its journey into the interior. The caravan leaves the city through the principal gate, and the people line the road to cheer the drivers and the pack animals as they pass.

The caravan master rides at the head of the column, carrying the banner of the Salt Court and the standard of the guild of the salt-makers. The master is followed by the drivers and the pack animals, arranged in the order of the march that the master has determined. The column stretches for a considerable distance, and the departure of the caravan is one of the most impressive spectacles of the coastal year.

The caravan is accompanied by the music of the pipes and the drums, played by the musicians who travel with the caravan and by the musicians of the city who accompany the caravan to the edge of the settled land. The music is the music of the road, the tunes that the caravan drivers have sung for generations as they cross the desert.

## The Feast of the Salt Bread

The feast of the salt bread is the celebration that follows the departure of the caravan, held in the great square of the city or in the hall of the guild of the salt-makers. The feast is prepared from the contributions of the salt-makers and the merchants and the households of the city, and it is shared by the community and the visitors who have come for the festival.

The principal dish of the feast is the salt bread, the flat loaf that is baked with the salt from the season's harvest. The bread is broken by the head of the guild of the salt-makers and distributed to the guests, and the salt is the symbol of the prosperity that the salt trade brings to the coastal communities.

The feast also includes the salt fish and the salt meat that have been preserved with the salt of the previous season, and the fruits and the vegetables that the coastal climate produces through the year. The feast is accompanied by the songs of the caravan, the ballads that recount the journeys of the great caravans of the past and the deeds of the caravan masters who led them.

## The Evening of Tellings

The evening of the festival is devoted to the tellings, the recitation of the stories of the great caravans and the caravan masters who have crossed the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) in the centuries since the salt trade was established. The tellings are conducted by the elders of the guild of the salt-makers, who recite the stories from the memory that has been passed down through the generations of the guild.

The tellings include the stories of the caravans that were lost in the desert storms and the caravans that were saved by the discovery of the hidden water, the stories of the negotiations with the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the agreements that secured the passage of the caravans, and the stories of the first caravan that crossed the desert and the welcome that it received from the peoples of the interior.

The tellings continue through the evening and into the night, and the community listens with the attention that the stories require. The tellings are the means by which the memory of the salt trade is preserved and the means by which the young learn the traditions of the guild.

## The Observance in the Interior

The Salt Caravan Festival is also observed in the interior communities that receive the caravans, though the observance takes a different form than it does on the coast. The interior communities watch for the arrival of the first caravan of the season, and the arrival is greeted with the same celebration that the departure receives on the coast.

The interior observance includes the blessing of the salt at the mosque or the shrine of the community and the distribution of the salt to the households in the customary portions. The arrival of the caravan is the occasion for the market and the fair, and the interior communities trade their goods for the salt and the other products that the caravan carries.
