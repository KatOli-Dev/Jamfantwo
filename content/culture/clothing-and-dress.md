## The Necessity and the Art

Clothing in the known world serves the dual purposes of protection and expression, shielding the body from the elements while communicating the wearer's identity, status, and adherence to the customs of the community. The materials and the styles of clothing vary considerably from one region to another, reflecting the resources that each environment provides and the values that each culture holds most important.

The production of clothing is one of the principal industries of every settled community, occupying the labour of a substantial portion of the population. The preparation of the fibres, the spinning of the thread, the weaving of the cloth, and the cutting and the sewing of the garments require skills that are transmitted from one generation to the next through the apprenticeship system that governs the craft of the tailor and the weaver. The cloth that the community produces is among the most important of the goods that are traded between the regions.

## The Clothing of the Vael Valley

The clothing of the Vael river valley is adapted to the temperate climate of the region, providing warmth in the winter and protection from the sun in the summer. The principal materials are the wool of the sheep and the flax that the farmers of the valley cultivate for the production of linen. The wool is woven into the heavy cloths that are used for the outer garments of the winter, and the flax is woven into the lighter cloths that are used for the undergarments and the summer clothing.

The typical dress of the Vaelic farmer consists of a linen shirt, breeches of wool or linen according to the season, and a tunic of heavier wool for the outer layer. The colours are generally those of the natural fibres, though the wealthier households use the dyes that are imported from the coast to achieve the brighter colours that indicate the status of the wearer. The blue dye of the woad plant is the most commonly used colour in the valley, producing the shades of blue that are the characteristic colours of the Vaelic dress.

The women of the valley wear a long dress of linen or wool, fastened at the waist with a belt of leather or woven cord. The dress is covered by an apron of heavier cloth, which protects the dress from the dirt of the household work. The married women wear the head covering that signifies their status, a simple cap of linen that covers the hair and is fastened beneath the chin.

## The Clothing of the Plains Peoples

The clothing of the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) and the human herders of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) is adapted to the conditions of the nomadic life and the climate of the open grasslands. The materials are the leather and the fur of the animals that the tribes hunt and the wool of the sheep that they herd. The leather is tanned by the traditional methods of the plains, producing a supple material that is suitable for the clothing that must withstand the constant movement of the nomadic life.

The clothing of the plains peoples is decorated with the beadwork and the embroidery that are the traditional arts of the grassland cultures. The patterns of the decoration indicate the tribe of the wearer, the status within the tribe, and the achievements that the wearer has attained. The patterns are transmitted from the elders to the younger generation through the apprenticeship of the girls to the women who are recognised as the masters of the decorative arts.

The centaurs do not wear clothing on the upper body, as the movement of the four legs requires freedom that the clothing would restrict. The centaurs wear the blankets and the cloaks that are draped over the shoulders and fastened at the chest, providing protection from the rain and the cold without restricting the movement. The centaurs also wear the belts and the harnesses that carry the tools and the weapons of the daily life.

## The Clothing of the Dwarven Halls

The clothing of the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) is adapted to the conditions of the underground life, where the temperature is constant and the light is artificial. The principal materials are the wool of the mountain sheep and the leather of the goats that are pastured on the mountain slopes. The dwarves also import the cotton and the silk of the southern lands for the garments of the wealthy and the ceremonial occasions.

The typical dress of the dwarven craftsman consists of a tunic of wool, breeches of leather, and a leather apron that protects the clothing from the sparks and the dirt of the forge. The dwarves wear the heavy boots of leather and iron that protect the feet from the sharp stones of the mine. The colours of the dwarven clothing are the earthy colours of the mountain: the browns and the greys and the deep reds that are produced by the mineral dyes of the dwarven mines.

The dwarven women wear a long dress of wool, covered by an apron of leather that protects the dress from the work of the household. The dwarven women also wear the head covering that signifies their status, a cap of wool or silk that is fastened with a brooch of the metal that the family's forge produces.

## The Clothing of the Sahrani Coast

The clothing of the [Sahrani city-states](/content/culture/sahrani-city-life) is adapted to the heat of the southern climate, providing protection from the sun while allowing the air to circulate around the body. The principal materials are the cotton that the farmers of the coast cultivate and the silk that is imported from the distant eastern lands. The cloth is woven in the light weights that are suitable for the hot climate, and the colours are generally the light colours that reflect the heat of the sun.

The typical dress of the Sahrani merchant consists of a long robe of cotton or silk, worn over a shirt of lighter cotton. The robe is fastened at the waist with a sash of woven silk, and the head is covered by the turban that protects the head from the sun and indicates the status of the wearer. The colours of the turban indicate the city of the wearer and the family to which the wearer belongs.

The women of the Sahrani coast wear a long dress of cotton or silk, covered by a cloak that is worn when the woman leaves the household. The cloak covers the head and the body, providing privacy and protection from the sun. The materials and the decoration of the cloak indicate the wealth and the status of the woman's family.

## The Clothing of the Northern Coast

The clothing of the northern coast is adapted to the cold and the wet conditions of the northern climate, providing warmth and protection from the storms. The principal materials are the wool of the sheep, the fur of the animals that the hunters catch, and the leather of the seals and the other marine animals that the fishers take. The clothing is heavy and sturdy, designed to withstand the conditions of the sea and the winter.

The typical dress of the northern fisher consists of a tunic of wool, breeches of leather, and a coat of fur or oiled leather that provides protection from the rain and the spray of the sea. The boots are of leather, lined with fur for the warmth. The hat is of fur or wool, covering the ears and the neck in the winter storms.

The women of the northern coast wear a long dress of wool, covered by an apron of leather that protects the dress from the work of the fish processing. The married women wear the head covering that signifies their status, a cap of wool that is fastened beneath the chin.

## The Universal Language of Dress

The clothing that the peoples of the known world wear is a language that communicates the identity of the wearer, the status within the community, and the adherence to the customs of the culture. The materials, the styles, and the colours of the clothing vary according to the conditions of each environment and the values of each culture, but the function of the clothing as a marker of identity is universal. The dress of the individual is the expression of the individual's place within the community, and the customs that govern the clothing are the customs that define the community's sense of order and propriety. The art of the tailor and the weaver is the art of clothing the community in the garments that express the community's values and sustain the community's identity.
