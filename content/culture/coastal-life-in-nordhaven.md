## The Northern Shores

The coastal settlements of the northern lands are scattered along the shores of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) and the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean), a region of rugged cliffs, deep fjords, and narrow beaches that is home to the hardy peoples of the north. [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) is the principal kingdom of this region, a realm of fisherfolk, whalers, and traders who have learned to survive and prosper in a climate that would defeat less determined peoples. The settlements of the northern coast are small and widely spaced, connected by the sea routes that are the principal highways of the region.

The daily life of the coastal settlements is governed by the sea and the weather, the two forces that determine the conditions of existence on the northern shore. The sea provides the food that sustains the population and the trade that connects the north to the wider world. The weather imposes a cycle of activity and confinement that shapes the rhythm of the year. The summer is the season of activity, when the sea is open and the settlements are busy with fishing, trading, and the preparation of stores for the winter. The winter is the season of confinement, when the storms rage across the sea and the settlements huddle behind their walls, enduring the cold and the darkness.

## The Fishing Fleet and the Daily Catch

The fishing fleet is the foundation of the northern economy, and the work of the fleet governs the daily routine of every coastal settlement. The fleet puts to sea at dawn, the boats scattering across the coastal waters to the fishing grounds that each community has used for generations. The boats return in the late afternoon, their holds filled with the catch that will be processed on the shore and distributed to the households of the settlement.

The principal fish of the northern waters are cod and herring, both of which are preserved by salting and drying for consumption through the winter months. The fish are cleaned and prepared on the shore by the women and the children, who work at the fish tables throughout the summer season, processing the daily catch and packing it into barrels for storage. The work is hard and constant, but it provides the food that sustains the population through the winter and the trade goods that the settlements exchange for the products of the [southern lands](/content/location/natural/continent/southern-lands).

## The Whaling and the Sealing

The larger settlements of the northern coast also engage in whaling and sealing, dangerous pursuits that require the cooperation of the entire community. The whaling season begins in the early summer, when the great whales migrate into the northern waters, and continues through the summer months until the whales depart for the open ocean. The whaling boats are larger than the fishing boats, crewed by experienced harpooners and rowers who have learned the skills of the hunt from their elders.

The processing of the whale is a community undertaking, conducted on the shore where the carcass is pulled above the high tide line. The blubber is rendered into oil, the meat is salted and dried for consumption, and the bones and baleen are preserved for use in the manufacture of tools and ornaments. The whale oil is the most valuable product of the northern hunt, traded to the southern lands for the goods that the north cannot produce for itself.

## The Household and the Longhouse

The dwellings of the northern coast are longhouses of timber and stone, designed to contain the extended household and to withstand the storms of the winter. The longhouse is divided into a central hall, where the household gathers for meals and for the evening activities, and the side chambers, where the individual families sleep and keep their possessions. The hearth is the centre of the longhouse, a long fire that burns throughout the day and is banked at night, providing heat and light for the entire household.

The household is the fundamental unit of northern society, comprising the extended family of three or four generations living under a single roof. The head of the household is the senior capable adult, who manages the household's resources and represents the household in the councils of the settlement. The authority of the household head is considerable, but it is balanced by the customary councils that the settlements maintain for the governance of the community.

## The Port Councils and the Governance of the Settlements

The governance of the northern settlements is conducted through the [Nordhaven Port Councils](/content/government/local/nordhaven-port-councils), the assemblies of the free householders who meet to decide the matters that affect the community. The port council is the highest authority in the settlement, responsible for the allocation of the fishing grounds, the maintenance of the harbour works, and the conduct of the community's relations with the other settlements of the coast.

The port council is presided over by an elected harbour master, who serves for a term of years and is responsible for the day-to-day administration of the settlement. The harbour master is assisted by the council of elders, the oldest and most respected members of the community, who are consulted in every matter of importance. The decisions of the port council are made by open vote, and the custom of the north requires that the decision be supported by a substantial majority of the householders.

## The Northern Law and the Sea Code

The legal tradition of the northern settlements is codified in the [Nordhaven Sea Code](/content/law/nordhaven-sea-code), a body of customary law that governs every aspect of the community's relationship with the sea. The Sea Code specifies the rights of the communities to the fishing grounds, the division of the proceeds of the whaling and the sealing, and the penalties for the violations of the customary law. The Sea Code also governs the conduct of the ships at sea and the treatment of the crews by the shipmasters.

The Sea Code is enforced by the port councils and, in the more serious cases, by the courts of the kingdom. The most serious offences under the Sea Code are the theft of another's catch, the failure to render assistance to a vessel in distress, and the violation of the terms of the whaling agreements. The punishments specified by the Sea Code include fines, the forfeiture of the offending vessel, and, in the most serious cases, exile from the community.

## The Spiritual Life of the Northern Coast

The religion of the northern coast is a blend of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame), which has made progress in the southern settlements of the kingdom, and the older traditions of the northern peoples that honour the spirits of the sea and the sky. The [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) and the more remote northern settlements maintain the old religion in its traditional form.

The principal figures of the old religion are the Sea Mother, who is the patron of the fishers and the sailors, and the Storm King, who is the ruler of the weather and the master of the storms. The Sea Mother is honoured at the beginning of the fishing season, when the community gathers at the shore to offer the traditional sacrifices and prayers for a safe and prosperous season. The Storm King is honoured at the beginning of the winter, when the community prays for the storms to be mild and for the settlement to survive the season of confinement.

## The Winter Confinement

The winter in the northern settlements is the season of the long confinement, when the storms rage across the sea and the snow covers the land. The boats are pulled up onto the shore, and the community retires into the longhouses to endure the months of darkness and cold. The winter is the season of indoor labour, when the fishing gear is repaired, the cloth is woven, and the tools of the summer are maintained. The winter is also the season of storytelling and the transmission of the community's traditions, when the elders recite the histories and the legends that preserve the memory of the northern peoples.

The [Long Night Festival](/content/culture/long-night-festival) is the principal celebration of the winter, observed at the time of the winter solstice, the longest night of the year. The festival is an occasion of feasting and the exchange of gifts, a reaffirmation of the bonds between the households of the settlement and a defiance of the darkness that surrounds the community. The festival is also the occasion for the telling of the stories of the ancestors, ensuring that the memory of the community is preserved through the generations.

## The Enduring Coast

The northern settlements endure on the edge of the habitable world, a testament to the courage and the determination of the peoples who make their homes on the northern coast. The life of the coastal settlements is hard, but it is also a life of dignity and purpose, defined by the relationship with the sea that provides the food and the trade that sustains the community. The northern peoples are a people of the shore, their character shaped by the storms and the seasons, their traditions preserved through the long nights of the winter and the brief, intense summers of the northern year.
