## The Shared Meal

The shared meal is one of the most important social institutions of the known world, the occasion at which the bonds of family and community are reinforced and the hierarchies of society are expressed. The customs that govern the preparation and the consumption of food vary considerably from one culture to another, reflecting the different resources and values of each people, but the fundamental purpose of the feast and the meal is the same everywhere: the nourishment of the body and the strengthening of the relationships that hold the community together.

## The Daily Meal in the Vael Valley

The daily meals of the Vael river valley are simple affairs, governed by the rhythm of the agricultural year and the resources that the household can command. The morning meal is taken at dawn, before the work of the fields begins, and consists of bread or porridge accompanied by ale or water. The midday meal is taken in the fields, a simple repast of bread and cheese and the preserved meats that the household has prepared. The evening meal is the principal meal of the day, taken after the work is finished, and consists of a stew or a roast accompanied by bread and ale.

The evening meal is the occasion at which the household gathers together, seated at the table that occupies the centre of the main room. The head of the household presides at the table, carving the meat and distributing the portions according to the custom of the valley. The meal is preceded by the prayer of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame), a moment of silence in which the faithful acknowledge the eternal flame that has provided the food.

## The Festival Feasts of the Vael Valley

The festivals of the agricultural year are the occasions for the great feasts that bring the community together in celebration and thanksgiving. The [First-Grain Feast](/content/culture/first-grain-feast) is observed at the planting of the spring wheat, a feast of the seeds and the preserved stores of the winter that marks the beginning of the new agricultural year. The feast includes the blessing of the seed grain by the priest, followed by a communal meal of the dishes that the tradition of the valley requires.

The [Crown Harvest](/content/culture/crown-harvest) is the principal feast of the year, observed at the bringing in of the summer wheat. The feast lasts for several days and includes the dishes that celebrate the abundance of the harvest: the fresh bread, the roasted meats, and the ale that has been brewed from the previous year's barley. The feast is also the occasion for the settling of debts and the planning of the winter's work, the practical matters that are as important to the community as the celebration itself.

## The Feasts of the Plains

The feasts of the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) and the human herders of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) are conducted at the summer gatherings, when the scattered tribes come together for the [Plains High Days](/content/culture/plains-high-days). The feast is a communal affair, prepared by the families of the tribe and shared by the entire assembly.

The principal dish of the plains feast is the roast meat of the hunt, prepared over the open fires that the tribe maintains throughout the gathering. The meat is accompanied by the dairy products of the herds and the wild plants that the women of the tribe have gathered from the grasslands. The feast is accompanied by the music and the dancing that are the traditional entertainments of the plains, and the celebration continues through the night.

## The Feasts of the Dwarven Hall

The feasts of the dwarven halls are conducted in the hall of feasts, the great chamber where the entire community gathers for the principal meals. The table of the dwarven feast is laden with the dishes that the dwarven tradition requires: the breads and the porridges of the imported grains, the meats of the goats and the sheep that are pastured on the mountain slopes, and the cheeses and the preserves that the dwarves prepare in their own kitchens.

The ale of the dwarves is the principal beverage of the feast, served in the great cups that are the traditional vessels of the dwarven table. The thane presides at the high table, raising the first cup in honour of the ancestors and the founders of the hall. The feast is accompanied by the songs and the recitations that celebrate the history and the achievements of the dwarven people.

## The Dining Customs of the Sahrani City-States

The dining customs of the [Sahrani city-states](/content/culture/sahrani-city-life) reflect the mercantile culture of the coast, in which the shared meal is an occasion for the conduct of business as well as the enjoyment of food. The principal meal of the day is the evening meal, taken after the heat of the day has passed, when the family gathers in the courtyard to share the dishes that the cooks of the household have prepared.

The Sahrani meal is served on a low table, with the diners seated on cushions around the table. The dishes are shared, placed in the centre of the table for all to take. The principal dishes are the spiced stews that are the foundation of the Sahrani cuisine, accompanied by the flat breads that are baked in the ovens of the household. The meal concludes with the fresh or dried fruits that are the traditional dessert of the Sahrani table.

## The Northern Feast

The feasts of the northern coast are conducted in the longhouses of the settlements, where the community gathers to share the food that the sea and the land have provided. The principal dishes of the northern feast are the fish and the meat that the hunters and the fishers have caught, prepared according to the traditional recipes of the northern peoples.

The [Long Night Festival](/content/culture/long-night-festival) is the principal feast of the northern year, observed at the time of the winter solstice. The feast is a celebration of the survival of the community through the winter, a reaffirmation of the bonds that connect the households of the settlement. The feast includes the roasted meats, the fish preserved from the summer, and the ale that has been brewed in the autumn.

## The Customs of the Table

The customs that govern the conduct of the meal are as important as the food itself in the social life of the known world. The order in which the diners are seated, the manner in which the food is served, and the conduct that is expected at the table are all governed by the tradition of each culture. The shared meal is one of the principal occasions at which the hierarchies of the community are expressed and the values of the culture are reinforced. The feast is the celebration of the community's abundance, a reaffirmation of the bonds that connect the members of the community to each other and to the resources that sustain them. The food of the feast is the food of the community, and the customs that govern its preparation and its consumption are the customs that define the community's identity.
