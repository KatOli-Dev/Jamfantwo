## The Science of the Waters

Tide-Reading is the disciplined study of the movements of the seas and oceans, a field of natural philosophy that seeks to understand and predict the patterns of the waters that surround the continents of the known world. The discipline is most highly developed in the academies of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), whose scholars have been recording observations of the tides and currents for more than nine centuries, and it is the foundation upon which the navigators of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) base their practice. The practitioners of Tide-Reading are called tide-readers, and their work informs the operations of every port and shipping lane of consequence in the known world.

## The Foundations of the Discipline

Tide-Reading is built upon the systematic observation and recording of the behaviour of the waters at fixed points over extended periods. The tide-readers of the academies maintain stations at principal harbours and headlands where the height, timing, and force of the tides are measured and recorded according to standardised methods. The records maintained at these stations are among the most comprehensive compilations of natural data in the known world, and the tables of tide predictions published from them are the standard reference of the mariners of the great ports.

The methods of Tide-Reading are empirical and quantitative. The tide-reader measures the rise and fall of the water against a marked staff set in the harbour or upon the shore, recording the height and time of each high and low water through the cycle of the lunar month. The records are kept in ledgers that are preserved in the libraries of the academies and consulted by scholars seeking to understand the patterns and variations that the tides exhibit. The comparison of records from different stations has revealed that the behaviour of the tides varies considerably from one location to another, and the understanding of these variations is a central concern of the discipline.

The principal treatises upon Tide-Reading are those published under the direction of [Halen Amurra](/content/people/notable/halen-amurra), the present chief scholar of the academy of the Amurra Republic, whose work upon the currents of the warm waters of the eastern ocean is the standard reference of the mariners of the Auric. The treatise sets forth the principal currents of the ocean and the rules by which the navigator may predict the set of the water at any season of the year. The work is regarded by the scholars of the academy as the most significant contribution to the study of the waters made in the present century.

## The Relationship with Navigation

Tide-Reading is closely connected to the practice of navigation, and the two disciplines have developed in parallel through the centuries. The navigator of the great ports relies upon the tide-reader's tables to plan the departure and arrival of vessels, to avoid the hazards of shoals and reefs that are uncovered at low water, and to take advantage of the currents that can shorten a voyage or lengthen it. The relationship between the tide-reader and the navigator is one of mutual dependence: the tide-reader provides the knowledge, and the navigator provides the practical experience that refines the knowledge.

The academy of Amurra maintains a school of navigation in which the principles of Tide-Reading are taught to the officers of the merchant fleet of the republic. The instruction includes the use of the tide staff, the reading of the tables, and the calculation of the corrections required for the particular conditions of each harbour. The school is one of the most respected institutions of its kind, and the officers trained there are among the most accomplished navigators in the known world.

The navigator guilds of the harbours of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) and the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) have adopted the tide tables published by the academy of Amurra as the standard of their practice. The tables are copied and distributed to the ports of all three continents, and the navigators who rely upon them carry the learning of the tide-readers into every corner of the known world. The discipline has thus become a common heritage of the maritime peoples, and its methods and findings are shared across the boundaries of nations and the divisions of species.

## The Principal Questions and Debates

The scholars of Tide-Reading are occupied with several questions that have not yet been resolved to the satisfaction of all practitioners. The principal debate concerns the cause of the tides themselves. The orthodox view, held by the majority of the tide-readers of the Amurran academy, is that the tides are caused by the attraction of the moon, which draws the waters of the ocean toward itself as it passes across the sky. The evidence for this view is the close correspondence between the timing of the tides and the position of the moon in the sky, a correspondence that has been observed and recorded at every station where tide-readers have maintained a watch.

A minority of tide-readers hold that the tides are caused by the movement of the waters in response to the rotation of the world, and that the apparent connection with the moon is a coincidence that has not been properly investigated. This view is more common among the scholars of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), whose own traditions of observation differ in some respects from the methods of the Amurran academy. The debate between the two schools has been conducted with the courtesy that characterises scholarly disagreement, and the publication of opposing views is welcomed in the journals of the academies.

The second question concerns the variation of the tides from one location to another. The tide-readers have observed that the height and timing of the tides vary considerably between harbours that are separated by only a short distance, and the explanation of these local variations is a matter of ongoing investigation. The most promising hypothesis holds that the shape of the coastline and the depth of the water in the approaches to each harbour modify the behaviour of the tidal wave as it approaches the shore, and that the local variations are the product of these modifications. The testing of this hypothesis requires the collection of more detailed observations than are presently available, and the tide-readers of the academies are engaged in the extension of the network of observation stations that will provide the data required.

## The Place of Tide-Reading in the Present Age

Tide-Reading is one of the principal achievements of the natural philosophy of the present age, and its practitioners are among the most respected scholars in the known world. The discipline has contributed substantially to the safety and efficiency of maritime trade, and the tables published by the academies are used in every port of consequence. The methods of Tide-Reading have also influenced the development of other branches of natural philosophy, and the approach of systematic observation and quantitative recording is now applied to the study of the winds, the currents, and the movements of the celestial bodies.

The future of Tide-Reading lies in the extension of the network of observation stations to the less frequented coasts of the known world and in the refinement of the methods of prediction that the tide-readers have developed. The scholars of the present generation are confident that the remaining questions will yield to the patient accumulation of observations, and that the discipline will continue to advance the understanding of the waters that surround the continents. The tide-readers look forward to a future in which the behaviour of the waters is as well understood as the behaviour of the air and the patterns of the sky, and in which the knowledge of the tides is the common possession of all the peoples of the known world.
