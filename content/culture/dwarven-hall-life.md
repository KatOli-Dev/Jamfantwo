## The Mountain Halls

The [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) communities of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) dwell in the great halls that the ancestors of the present generation carved from the living rock of the mountains. The principal halls are [Stoneheim](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and [Karhold](/content/government/national/karhold-realm), two ancient centres of dwarven civilisation that have been continuously inhabited since the earliest period of recorded history. The halls are cities in the rock, extending for miles through the mountains, connected by tunnels and passages that the dwarves have excavated over the centuries.

Life in the dwarven halls is life in an entirely artificial environment, carved from the stone and maintained by the constant labour of the inhabitants. The temperature of the deep rock is stable throughout the year, never varying from the comfortable cool that the dwarves regard as the natural condition of existence. The darkness of the deep places is absolute, lit by the lamps and torches that the dwarves maintain with careful attention, and by the glow of the molten metal that flows through the forges at the heart of the mountain. The air is dry and still, circulated through ventilation shafts that the dwarven engineers have designed to bring fresh air from the surface to the deepest chambers.

## The Forge and the Crafting

The forge is the centre of dwarven life, the place where the raw materials of the mountain are transformed into the objects that sustain the community and the goods that are traded to the surface world. The great forge of each hall is a cavern of immense proportions, its roof blackened by the smoke of centuries, its floor covered with the workstations of the smiths and the craftsmen who labour there without interruption.

The day in the dwarven halls begins with the kindling of the forge fires, a ceremony conducted by the master smith of the hall. The fires are stoked to the required temperature, and the craftsmen take up their stations at the anvils and the workbenches. The work of the forge continues through the day, the sound of the hammers echoing through the corridors of the hall, the glow of the molten metal illuminating the faces of the workers. The forges are never allowed to go cold, maintained through the night by the apprentices who tend the fires and prepare the materials for the following day.

## The Stone Tables and the Hall of Feasts

The hall of feasts is the principal social space of the dwarven community, a vast chamber where the inhabitants gather for the communal meals that are the centre of dwarven domestic life. The hall is furnished with long stone tables and benches, arranged in rows that face the high table where the thane and the master smith sit. The meals are served at the appointed hours, prepared in the kitchens that adjoin the hall by the cooks who are among the most respected members of the community.

The dwarven diet is based on the products of the mountain and the trade with the surface. The principal foods are the breads and the porridges made from the grains that the dwarves import from the surface world, the meats of the goats and the sheep that are pastured on the mountain slopes, and the cheeses and the preserves that the dwarves prepare in their own dairies and kitchens. The ale that the dwarves brew is the most famous product of the halls, a dark and strong brew that is aged in the cool of the deep chambers and is regarded as the proper accompaniment to every meal.

## The Thane and the Council

The governance of the dwarven halls is conducted by the thane, the hereditary ruler of the community, who is advised by the [Stoneheim Thanes Council](/content/government/local/stoneheim-thanes-council) or the [Karhold Warden Councils](/content/government/local/karhold-realm-warden-councils) according to the hall. The thane is the commander of the dwarven warriors, the chief judge in the disputes that arise within the community, and the principal authority in the conduct of the hall's relations with the surface world.

The thane is assisted by the master of the forge, who oversees the craftwork of the community, and the keeper of the archives, who maintains the records that preserve the history and the law of the hall. The thane and the master of the forge and the keeper of the archives meet regularly to discuss the matters that affect the welfare of the community, and their decisions are binding upon the inhabitants of the hall.

## The Deep Chambers and the Treasures

The deepest chambers of the dwarven halls are the treasuries, the repositories of the wealth that the dwarves have accumulated over the centuries of mining and crafting. The treasuries are chambers of stone, secured by doors of metal and stone that are the masterworks of the dwarven smiths. The contents of the treasuries are the metal and the gems that the dwarves have extracted from the mountain, the works of art that the craftsmen have produced, and the records and the chronicles that preserve the history of the dwarven people.

The treasuries are not merely storehouses; they are also the places where the dwarves display the achievements of their civilisation. The greatest works of the [Inlay Art](/content/culture/inlay-art) are displayed in the treasuries, the inlaid panels and the decorated vessels that are the pride of the hall. The treasuries are also the places where the dwarves keep the relics of their history, the weapons and the armour of the ancestors, the chronicles and the genealogies that record the descent of the present generation from the founders of the hall.

## The Children of the Hall

The children of the dwarven halls are raised in the community, learning the skills and the traditions that they will need as adults through direct participation in the life of the hall. The children are taught by the elders of the community, who instruct them in the history and the law of the hall, in the proper conduct of the rituals, and in the principles of the craft that they will pursue.

The apprenticeship system is the foundation of dwarven education. The child is bound to a master at the age of twelve or thirteen, and the apprentice serves the master for a period of twenty years or more, learning the craft through direct participation in the work. The apprenticeship is a period of strict discipline, during which the apprentice is required to master the techniques of the craft and to demonstrate the dedication that the dwarves regard as essential to the production of quality work.

## The Deepening Silence

Life in the dwarven halls is a life of order and purpose, governed by the cycles of the forge and the rituals of the community. The silence of the deep places is broken by the sound of the hammers and the voices of the craftsmen, by the chanting of the rituals and the conversations of the feasts. The dwarves are a people of the rock, their character shaped by the materials that they work and the environment that they have created. The halls are the homes of the dwarven people, the places where their ancestors dwelt and where their descendants will dwell in the ages to come, a testament to the endurance of a people who have made their home in the heart of the mountain.
