## The Festival of the Winter Fire

The First Kindling is the principal winter festival of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame), observed in the hearth-halls throughout the lands where the faith is practised on the longest night of the year. The festival marks the awakening of the first fire and the gift of warmth and light that the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) represents, and it is the occasion upon which the faithful renew their connection to the fire that sustains the community through the darkness of the winter.

The festival is observed by the communities of the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) and the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and by the scattered communities of the Still Flame throughout the central and southern regions of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The observance is also maintained by the hearth-keepers of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm), whose smiths and craftfolk have adopted the festival as a complement to their own traditions of the forge fire.

## The Night of the Longest Dark

The First Kindling is observed on the longest night of the year, the night when the darkness is at its greatest and the cold is at its most intense. The hearth-halls are prepared for the observance in the weeks preceding the festival, with the faithful gathering the fuel for the great bonfire that will be kindled at the heart of the community.

The preparation of the fuel is itself a ceremonial act, conducted by the hearth-keepers and the members of the community who gather the wood from the surrounding forests and the peat from the moors and heaths. The fuel is stacked in the shape of a cone at the centre of the hearth-hall or the village square, and the faithful gather around the stack as the sun sets on the longest night.

The hearth-keeper speaks the words of the kindling as the sun disappears below the horizon, recalling the first kindling and the gift of the fire that the Still Flame represents. The hearth-keeper then strikes the flint and kindles the flame, and the faithful watch as the fire catches and grows, casting the light and the warmth across the gathered community.

## The Lighting of the Hearth Fires

From the great bonfire, the faithful kindle their hearth fires for the winter. Each household sends a representative to the bonfire with a taper or a lantern, and the hearth-keeper lights the taper from the great fire. The householder carries the flame back to the home, where the hearth fire is kindled with the flame from the bonfire.

The lighting of the hearth fires is the central observance of the First Kindling, for it renews the connection between the community and its homes and between the Still Flame of the hearth-hall and the flames that burn in every hearth. The household that kindles its hearth fire from the great bonfire is participating in the same act of kindling that the first kindler performed, and the flame that burns in the hearth through the winter is a continuation of the flame that was kindled at the First Kindling.

The hearth fire is kept burning through the night of the longest dark, and the household gathers around the hearth to share the feast and to sing the songs that the Still Flame preserves. The hearth-keeper of the household recites the origin myth of the festival, telling the story of the first kindling and the gift of the fire, and the household listens in the silence that the story requires.

## The Feast of the Kindling

The feast of the First Kindling is the principal meal of the winter season, prepared from the stores that the community has laid in during the autumn. The feast includes the bread that has been baked from the last of the autumn grain, the preserved meats and the salted fish, the root vegetables that have been stored in the cellars, and the dried fruits and the honey that the summer provided.

The feast is shared by the household around the hearth, with the portions distributed according to the customary precedence. The first portion is reserved for the hearth-keeper, who speaks the blessing of the Still Flame over the food before it is distributed. The last portion is reserved for the stranger, for the doctrine of the Open Hand requires that the hospitality of the hearth be extended to any who come to the door during the festival.

The feast is accompanied by the songs of the Still Flame, sung by the household in the order that the hearth-keeper determines. The songs recount the history of the faith and the deeds of the hearth-keepers who have maintained the Still Flame through the generations, and the household joins in the singing with the attention that the songs require.

## The Vigil Through the Night

The vigil is the observance that follows the feast, the period during which the household keeps watch through the night to ensure that the hearth fire does not go out. The vigil is conducted in silence, with the household taking turns to tend the fire and to watch for the first light of the dawn.

The vigil is the occasion upon which the household reflects on the year that has passed and on the work that lies ahead. The hearth-keeper speaks the words of the reflection, recalling the events of the year and the deeds of the household, and the household listens in the silence of the vigil.

The vigil ends with the first light of the dawn, when the household extinguishes the lamps and the candles and allows the hearth fire to burn down to the embers. The hearth-keeper speaks the words of the closing, thanking the Still Flame for the warmth and the light that the festival has provided, and the household prepares for the work of the new year.

## The Gift of the Ember

The First Kindling concludes with the gift of the ember, the practice by which the faithful carry a burning ember from the hearth fire to the households of those who could not attend the festival. The hearth-keeper selects an ember from the fire and places it in a clay pot filled with ash, and the faithful carry the ember to the homes of the sick and the elderly and those who are separated from the community by distance or circumstance.

The gift of the ember is the expression of the Still Flame's teaching that the fire must be shared with those who are in need, and the household that receives the ember kindles its hearth fire from the ember and joins in the observance of the festival even though it could not attend the kindling. The practice is observed with great care, for the ember must be kept alive during the journey and the pot must be tended with attention.

## The Divergent Customs of the Regions

The observance of the First Kindling varies from region to region, reflecting the different conditions and the different traditions of the communities that celebrate it. The communities of the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) observe the festival in the form established by the central lowlands, with the great bonfire kindled at the hearth-hall and the hearth fires lit from the bonfire.

The communities of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) observe the festival with a greater emphasis on the vigil and the reflection, reflecting the contemplative character of the Vaelic tradition. The hearth fires of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) are not kindled from a central bonfire but from a flame that has been preserved from the previous year, and the continuity of the flame is a matter of great importance to the Vaelic communities.

The communities of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) observe the festival in the forges and the workshops, with the forge fire serving as the hearth fire and the smiths and the craftfolk serving as the hearth-keepers. The First Kindling of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) is a more austere observance than the festival of the lowlands, reflecting the practical character of the dwarven tradition.
