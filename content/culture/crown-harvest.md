## The Greatest Gathering

The Crown Harvest is the greatest gathering of the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale), the annual ceremony at which the laws of [Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale) are recited and grain from each district is offered in token of the vale's common inheritance. The Crown Harvest is held at the old coronation hall in the heart of the vale, the hall in which the queen first took the crown and in which the water ordinances that she instituted were first set down. The chronicler notes that the Crown Harvest is regarded by the valley as the central observance of the agricultural year, and the Crown Harvest is held in high esteem by every household of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) that draws its living from the soil of the vale.

The Crown Harvest is not a single ceremony but a cycle of observances that extends over a period of several days, and the observances are conducted at the old coronation hall and at the principal granaries of the districts that surround the hall. The chronicler notes that the Crown Harvest is among the most distinctive of the regional ceremonies of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), and the ceremony is an expression of the bond between the peoples of the vale and the land that sustains them. The Crown Harvest is observed in the late summer, when the grain is gathered in and the granaries are filled, and the timing of the ceremony is the measure of the vale's dependence upon the harvest.

## Origins in the Unification

The Crown Harvest traces its foundation to the early years of the reign of [Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale), the sovereign who united the warring principalities of the central river valleys and established the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) as a stable and prosperous realm. The queen instituted the Crown Harvest as a means of binding the conquered principalities to the laws and the institutions of the new kingdom, and the ceremony was designed to remind the peoples of the vale that their inheritance was held in common. The chronicler notes that the queen is recorded in the chronicles of the kingdom to have presided over the first of the Crown Harvests in person, and the chronicles describe the ceremony in terms that the chroniclers of the present age regard as a model of the kind of observance that binds a kingdom together.

The Crown Harvest was refined over the generations into the tradition that is observed in the present age, and the tradition has been preserved by the governors and the local councils of the vale. The chronicler notes that the Crown Harvest has not been interrupted since the first gathering that the queen presided over, and the continuity of the ceremony is a measure of the durability of the institutions that the queen established. The Crown Harvest has been observed through every season of peace and through every season of conflict that the kingdom has known, and the ceremony is the source of the unity of the vale.

## The Old Coronation Hall

The Crown Harvest is held at the old coronation hall in the heart of the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale), and the hall is the principal repository of the vale's collective memory. The hall was erected in the early years of the reign of [Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale), and the hall is the place in which the queen first took the crown and in which the water ordinances that she instituted were first set down. The chronicler notes that the hall has been maintained by the governors and the local councils of the vale through every generation since the queen's death, and the hall is regarded by the valley as the most sacred of its public buildings.

The hall contains copies of [Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale)'s first water ordinances, the boundary stones of the districts of the vale, the harvest accounts that the local councils have kept, and the agreements by which the former rivals of the queen entered the kingdom. The chronicler notes that the records of the hall are consulted by the governors and the magistrates of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) in every dispute that touches upon the inheritance of the vale, and the records are the source of the vale's knowledge of its own beginnings. The hall is the seat of the Crown Harvest, and the hall is the place to which every household of the vale returns at the close of the harvest.

## The Recitation of the Laws

The central observance of the Crown Harvest is the recitation of the laws of [Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale), and the recitation is conducted in the great chamber of the old coronation hall. The recitation is opened by the senior governor of the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale), and the recitation is continued by the local councils of the districts of the vale in the customary order. The chronicler notes that the recitation is not a reading of the laws in the modern sense but a recitation from memory, and the reciters are chosen from among the elders of the districts for their knowledge of the laws and the customary order.

The recitation is attended by the delegates of every district of the vale, and the delegates are accompanied by such of the householders of the districts as are able to attend. The chronicler notes that the recitation is regarded by the valley as the most important of the public observances of the year, and the recitation is the measure of the vale's continuity with the institutions that the queen established. The recitation is followed by a brief silence in which the laws are held in mind without being spoken, and the silence is regarded by the elders of the vale as the most important part of the recitation.

## The Offering of the Grain

The second of the principal observances of the Crown Harvest is the offering of the grain from each district of the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale), and the offering is conducted at the principal granaries of the vale. The grain is brought to the granaries by the householders of the districts in the customary order, and the grain is offered in token of the vale's common inheritance. The chronicler notes that the offering is not a tax in the modern sense but a free-will offering of the harvest, and the offering is regarded by the householders as a privilege rather than a duty.

The offering is received by the governors of the vale and by the elders of the local councils, and the offering is entered in the harvest accounts that the old coronation hall preserves. The chronicler notes that the harvest accounts are the most complete of the records of any district of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), and the accounts are consulted by the governors and the magistrates in every dispute that touches upon the harvest. The offering is followed by a feast at the great granary of the vale, and the feast is attended by the delegates and the householders of the districts.

## The Reports of the Districts

The third of the principal observances of the Crown Harvest is the reports of the districts of the vale, and the reports are delivered in the great chamber of the old coronation hall. The reports are opened by the local councils of the districts, and the reports set forth the condition of the bridges, the granaries, and the channels of the districts for the year that is past. The chronicler notes that the reports are the means by which the valley maintains the water law that [Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale) instituted, and the reports are the source of the vale's knowledge of the state of the works upon which the prosperity of the kingdom depends.

The reports are received by the governors of the vale and by the delegates of the districts, and the reports are entered in the records of the hall. The chronicler notes that disputes that have resisted settlement in the villages are often concluded during the Crown Harvest, when precedent can be read against the records preserved in the hall. The reports are followed by a brief discussion in which the governors and the delegates consider the measures that the vale must take in the year that is to come, and the discussion is the source of the vale's plans for the maintenance of the works.

## The Crown Harvest and the Kingdom

The Crown Harvest is observed by the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale) as a ceremony of the valley, and the ceremony is not observed by the kingdom at large. The chronicler notes that the Crown Harvest is regarded by the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) as a local observance of the heartland from which the kingdom was founded, and the kingdom is content to allow the vale to maintain the ceremony without the interference of the court. The Crown Harvest is, however, attended by the senior magistrates of the kingdom, and the attendance of the magistrates is the measure of the importance that the kingdom attaches to the inheritance of the vale.

The Crown Harvest is also attended by the ambassadors of the neighbouring kingdoms, and the ambassadors are received by the governors of the vale in the customary manner. The chronicler notes that the ambassadors are shown the records of the hall and the harvest accounts of the vale, and the showing is the source of the ambassadors' knowledge of the institutions of the kingdom. The Crown Harvest is regarded by the ambassadors as a privilege to attend, and the ambassadors are counseled to observe the ceremony without comment or interference.

## The Legacy of Sera

The Crown Harvest is regarded by the valley as the principal legacy of [Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale), and the ceremony is the source of the vale's continuity with the institutions that the queen established. The chronicler notes that the queen is invoked by the governors of the vale in every ceremony of the Crown Harvest, and the queen is remembered by the householders of the districts as the founder of the kingdom and the source of the laws that bind the vale together. The queen's name is entered in the records of the hall beside the names of the governors and the elders who have served the vale since her death, and the queen's name is recited at the opening of every ceremony.

The queen is also remembered in the customary songs that the vale maintains, and the songs are sung at the feasts of the Crown Harvest. The chronicler notes that the songs are a measure of the importance that the vale attaches to the memory of the queen, and the songs are the source of the vale's knowledge of the queen's life and reign. The songs are taught to the young at the household hearth, and the songs are sung at the feasts of the Crown Harvest in the presence of the governors and the delegates of the districts.

## Significance

The Crown Harvest is the greatest gathering of the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale), and the ceremony is the central observance of the agricultural year of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael). The Crown Harvest is held at the old coronation hall, and the ceremony includes the recitation of the laws of [Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale), the offering of the grain from each district, the reports of the districts, and the feasts that follow the observances. The chronicler notes that the Crown Harvest has been the source of the unity of the vale through every generation since the queen's death, and the Crown Harvest is held in high esteem by every household of the kingdom that draws its living from the soil of the vale. The Crown Harvest endures as a ceremony of the valley and as a reminder that the prosperity of the kingdom rests upon the patient cultivation of the land and the just law that the queen established.
