## Overview

The Rite of the First Hunt is the principal coming-of-age ceremony of the human and the elven communities of the temperate and the northern regions of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), observed by the young people of both sexes at the age at which they are reckoned to have reached the maturity that qualifies them for the full responsibilities of the community. The rite is observed in every nation of the temperate zone, though the customs that accompany it vary from one people to another, and the chronicler notes that no two communities conduct the rite in precisely the same manner. The rite is above all a demonstration of the competence of the young person to provide for the community and to defend it against the dangers of the wilderness, and the rituals that accompany it are directed toward the formal acknowledgement of the young person's transition to the status of an adult.

## Origins and Meaning

The origins of the rite are lost in the pre-historic period, and the chroniclers of the different nations offer different accounts of its founding. The scholars of the [Kingdom of Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael) have proposed that the rite was originally a hunting ritual of the forest peoples, a test of the competence of the young to participate in the great hunts of the woodland. The scholars of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) have proposed that the rite is older than the settled agriculture of the river valleys, and that it is descended from a pastoral ritual of the pre-historic peoples of the grasslands. Whatever its origins, the rite has acquired a meaning that is independent of the theories of its founding, and the rite is observed by peoples of every religion and of none.

The rite is reckoned to take place at the age of fifteen or sixteen in most communities, though the precise age varies with the climate and the constitution of the young person. The young people of the colder regions of the north are reckoned to mature later than those of the warmer regions of the south, and the rite is correspondingly delayed in the mountain communities. The young person who has reached the age of the rite is required to undergo the test of the hunt, and the successful completion of the test is the condition of the formal acknowledgement of the young person's transition to the status of an adult. The failure to complete the test is not accounted a disgrace, and the young person who has failed is permitted to attempt the test again in the following year.

## The Preparation

The preparation for the rite begins in the weeks that precede the day of the hunt, and the preparation is conducted under the supervision of the elders of the community. The young person is instructed in the lore of the hunt, including the reading of the signs of the quarry and the conduct of the chase, and the young person is also instructed in the customary observances that accompany the hunt. The instruction is conducted by an elder of the community who has been appointed for the purpose, and the elder is responsible for the assessment of the young person's readiness for the test. The elder's assessment is not the only criterion of readiness, for the parents and the other members of the household are also consulted, and the consent of the household is required for the young person to proceed to the test.

The preparation includes a period of fasting and of contemplation that the young person observes in the days that immediately precede the hunt. The fasting is not a severe one, for the young person is permitted to take a light meal in the evening, but the fasting is sufficient to require the young person to contemplate the seriousness of the occasion. The contemplation is conducted in the household shrine or in some other place of quiet that the community provides, and the young person is encouraged to reflect upon the responsibilities that the completion of the rite will bring. The chronicler notes that the period of contemplation is observed with varying degrees of strictness in different communities, but the custom is sufficiently widespread to be reckoned among the principal elements of the preparation.

## The Test of the Hunt

The test of the hunt is conducted upon a day that is appointed by the elders of the community, and the day is typically in the late spring or the early summer when the weather is favourable and the game is in good condition. The young person is conducted to the place of the hunt by the elder who has overseen the preparation, and the elder remains at a distance to observe the conduct of the test. The young person is required to pursue and to take a single animal of a species that is customary for the test, and the species varies with the community and the terrain. The communities of the forest require the taking of a deer or a wild boar, the communities of the plains require the taking of a hare or a fox, and the communities of the mountain require the taking of a marmot or a similar creature.

The young person is required to take the quarry without assistance, and the only weapon that is permitted is the customary weapon of the community, whether that be the bow, the spear, or the sling. The young person is also required to dress the quarry according to the customary practice and to bring the carcass back to the community, where the elder examines the work and makes the assessment of the success of the test. The examination includes an inspection of the wound that has been inflicted, for the wound is required to be a clean one that has caused a minimum of suffering to the animal. The chronicler notes that a wound that is accounted to have caused unnecessary suffering is grounds for the failure of the test, even if the quarry has been taken.

## Variations among the Peoples

The rite is observed in different ways among the different peoples of the known world, and the variations are a measure of the diversity of the customs of the nations that share the celebration. The dwarves of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/the-morvind-uplands) observe a rite of their own that is adapted to the conditions of the underground life, and the dwarven rite requires the young person to demonstrate the competence in the metalwork and the stonework that are the customary occupations of the dwarven communities. The rite is conducted in the presence of the master of the craft to which the young person is to be bound, and the master makes the assessment of the young person's readiness. The dwarven rite does not include the hunting test of the human and elven communities, for the dwarven communities do not hunt the great game of the surface, but the rite includes a test of the young person's ability to navigate the tunnels and to find the way in the dark.

The elves of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) and the other elven communities of the central woodlands observe a rite that is adapted to the conditions of the forest. The elven rite requires the young person to spend a period of seven days and seven nights alone in the deep forest, and the young person is required to subsist upon the products of the forest and to demonstrate the competence to read the signs of the wood. The young person is observed at a distance by the elders of the community, and the elders make the assessment of the young person's readiness upon the conclusion of the period of solitude. The elven rite is reckoned to be among the most demanding of the coming-of-age ceremonies of the known world, and the completion of the rite is a matter of considerable pride for the young person and the family.

The chronicler notes that the rite is observed by peoples of every religion and of none, and the rite has acquired a civic as well as a religious significance that is recognised by the authorities of the nations in which it is observed. The rite endures as a celebration of the renewal of the community and as a reminder that the responsibilities of the adult are to be entered into with a full understanding of their character.
