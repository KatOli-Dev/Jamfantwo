## The Foundations of Kinship

Marriage and the family are the foundations of social organisation across the known world, the institutions through which the bonds of kinship are established and the continuity of the community is ensured. The forms of marriage and the structure of the family vary considerably from one people to another, reflecting the different conditions and values of each culture, but the fundamental purpose is the same everywhere: the regulation of the relationships between the sexes and the creation of the stable households that are the foundation of society.

The marriage bond is recognised everywhere as a relationship of mutual obligation between the spouses and between the families of the spouses. The rights and the responsibilities of the spouses are specified by the customary law of each community, and the dissolution of the marriage is governed by the same law. The family is the unit through which the property is held and the children are raised, and the family is the primary source of identity and support for the individual.

## Marriage in the Vael Valley

In the Vael river valley, marriage is conducted according to the custom of [Hands Joined](/content/culture/hands-joined), the principal marriage ceremony of the temperate lands. The ceremony is conducted at the temple of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame), presided over by the priest, and is attended by the families of the spouses and the adult members of the parish.

The Vaelic family is a three-generation household, comprising the grandparents, the parents, and the children. The household is headed by the senior capable adult, who manages the household's resources and represents the household in the councils of the parish. The authority of the household head is exercised in consultation with the other adult members of the household, and the decisions of the household are reached through discussion and consensus.

The marriage bond in the Vael valley is a contract between the families of the spouses, specifying the contributions that each family will make to the establishment of the new household and the rights of the spouses in the property of the household. The marriage is indissoluble under the customary law of the valley, though the separation of the spouses is permitted in cases of cruelty or abandonment. The children of the marriage belong to the household of the father, but the rights of the mother in the children are recognised by the custom of the valley.

## Marriage on the Great Grass Plains

The marriage customs of the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) and the human herders of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) are adapted to the nomadic conditions of the grasslands. The marriage is conducted at the summer gathering, when the scattered tribes come together for the [Plains High Days](/content/culture/plains-high-days), and the ceremony is presided over by the elders of the tribes.

The marriage is marked by the exchange of gifts between the families of the spouses, the principal gift being the horses or the livestock that are the foundation of the plains economy. The bride price is paid by the groom's family to the bride's family, compensating the bride's family for the loss of the bride's labour and securing the groom's rights in the children of the marriage. The amount of the bride price varies according to the status of the families and the quality of the bride, but it is always a matter of negotiation between the families.

The family of the plains is an extended kinship network, comprising the spouses, the children, and the grandparents, together with the unmarried siblings of the spouses and the other dependents who are attached to the household. The family is mobile, moving with the herds and the flocks across the grasslands, and the family tent is the centre of the domestic life. The authority of the husband is considerable in the public affairs of the family, but the wife manages the domestic affairs and the children, and her authority in these matters is recognised by the custom of the plains.

## The Dwarven Lineage and the Hold

The marriage customs of the dwarves are governed by the traditions of the [Stoneheim Holds Rites](/content/culture/stoneheim-holds-rites), the ceremonies that mark the principal events of the dwarven life. The dwarven marriage is conducted in the hall of the hold, presided over by the thane, and is attended by the entire community of the hall.

The dwarven family is a lineage rather than a household, a group that extends across multiple generations and multiple households. The lineage is the principal unit of dwarven social organisation, bound together by the obligations of mutual support and the traditions of the craft that the lineage practises. The head of the lineage is the senior master of the craft, who manages the lineage's resources and represents the lineage in the councils of the hold.

The marriage of the dwarves is a contract between the lineages rather than between the individuals, arranged by the elders of the lineages to secure the alliances and the economic advantages that will benefit the community. The marriage is indissoluble under the customary law of the dwarves, and the children of the marriage belong to the lineage of the father. The wife is adopted into the lineage of the husband, and her obligations to her own lineage are assumed by the lineage of her husband.

## Marriage in the Sahrani City-States

The marriage customs of the [Sahrani city-states](/content/culture/sahrani-city-life) are governed by the traditions of the mercantile culture that is the foundation of the Sahrani civilisation. The marriage is conducted at the temple of the city, presided over by the priest, and is attended by the families of the spouses and the merchants who are the principal witnesses of the contract.

The Sahrani marriage is a contract between the families of the spouses, specifying the amount of the dowry that the bride's family will contribute and the settlement that the groom's family will provide. The marriage is a union of commercial interests as well as personal bonds, and the marriages of the merchant families are arranged to consolidate the economic relationships that connect the trading houses of the city.

The Sahrani family is a compound household, comprising the husband, the wife, the children, and the servants and the dependents who are attached to the household. The husband is the head of the household, managing the family's commercial interests and representing the family in the councils of the city. The wife manages the domestic affairs of the household, including the management of the servants and the education of the children.

## Marriage in the Northern Coast

The marriage customs of the northern coast are adapted to the conditions of the maritime life, reflecting the importance of the sea in the lives of the northern peoples. The marriage is conducted at the harbour or at the shore, presided over by the captain of the vessel that the groom commands or by the harbour master of the settlement.

The northern marriage is marked by the exchange of the rope that the custom of the coast requires, a length of braided rope that symbolises the binding of the two households. The rope is later displayed in the household as a token of the marriage bond. The marriage is a partnership of equals, reflecting the conditions of the maritime life in which the wife manages the household while the husband is at sea and in which the wife's competence in the management of the household is essential to the survival of the family.

## The Universal Institution

Marriage and the family are universal institutions of the known world, the foundations upon which the societies of the peoples are built. The forms of marriage and the structure of the family vary according to the conditions of each environment and the values of each culture, but the purposes of marriage and the family are the same everywhere: the regulation of the relationships between the sexes, the creation of the stable households in which the children can be raised, and the transmission of the traditions and the property from one generation to the next. The marriage bond and the family ties are the strongest bonds in human society, and their endurance across the ages is a measure of their importance to the peoples of the world.
