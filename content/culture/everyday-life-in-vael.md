## The Rhythm of the River Valley

The Vael river valley is the heartland of the kingdom of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), a region defined by the slow meander of the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael) through broad floodplains that sustain the densest agricultural settlement of the central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) lowlands. Everyday life for the peoples of the valley follows the cycles of the river and the seasons, a pattern that has remained largely unchanged for centuries despite the expansion of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) and the growing influence of the coastal kingdoms. The valley is home to humans of Vaelic descent, substantial populations of [halflings](/content/species/sapient/halflings) who work the fields and gardens, and scattered communities of [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) who maintain the mills and the smaller workshops of the towns.

The day begins before sunrise in most households of the valley. The first light of dawn finds the farmers already in the fields, the household fires already kindled, and the children already engaged in the morning tasks assigned by the head of the household. The morning meal is eaten together in the central room of the house, a simple affair of bread and cheese or porridge according to the season, accompanied by ale or water drawn from the household well. The meal is preceded by a customary moment of silence observed by adherents of the Still Flame, who acknowledge the kindling of the day's fire as a manifestation of the eternal flame that sustains the world.

## The Agricultural Year

The fields of the Vael valley produce a variety of grains, the most important of which is summer wheat, planted after the spring floods have receded and harvested in the late summer before the autumn rains begin. The wheat is supplemented by barley for brewing, rye for the darker breads of the autumn and winter, and oats for the horses and the porridge that is the customary breakfast of the valley. The fields are worked by extended families, with the halfling households contributing labour in return for a share of the harvest and the right to cultivate the garden plots that surround their burrows.

The growing season is divided by the principal agricultural festivals that mark the rhythm of the year. [First-Grain Feast](/content/culture/first-grain-feast) is observed at the planting of the spring wheat, a day of blessing and celebration that brings the communities of the valley together in the first major gathering after the isolation of winter. [Crown Harvest](/content/culture/crown-harvest) is the principal festival of the autumn, observed at the bringing in of the summer wheat, a celebration that lasts several days and includes the settling of debts and the planning of the winter's work. The winter months are a time of rest and indoor labour, when the farmers repair tools and the household weaves cloth and prepares preserves.

The river itself is a source of food and transport. Fishing is a constant occupation throughout the year, supplying freshwater fish to the tables of the valley and providing a trade good for exchange with the upland communities. The riverboats that ply the Vael carry grain and cloth downstream to the markets of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) and return upstream with salt, metal goods, and the luxuries of the coast. The river also provides the water that irrigates the fields through the network of canals and channels that the farmers of the valley have maintained since prehistoric times.

## The Village and the Household

The settlements of the valley are principally villages of timber and thatch, arranged around a central green that serves as the gathering place for the community. The village is governed by a parish council under the authority of the [Vael Parish Councils](/content/government/local/vael-parish-councils), which supervises the distribution of water rights, the maintenance of the canals, and the resolution of disputes between households. The parish council meets in the village hall, a building of timber and stone that serves also as the storehouse for the community's grain reserve and the meeting place for the observances of the Still Flame.

The household is the fundamental unit of Vaelic society, typically comprising three generations living under a single roof. The head of the household is the eldest capable adult, who manages the household's land and represents the household in the councils of the parish. The authority of the household head is considerable but not absolute, constrained by the customary law of the valley that recognises the rights of other household members to a share of the household's resources and a voice in household decisions. The household is also the primary unit of religious observance, with the hearth fire kept burning continuously through the winter months as a domestic manifestation of the eternal flame.

The daily work of the household is divided by age and ability. The able-bodied adults work the fields, while the elderly tend the younger children and maintain the household fires. The children begin their education in the household tasks from the age of five or six, learning the skills they will need as adults through direct participation in the work. The halfling households that work the fields of human farmers follow a similar division of labour, though the halfling preference for subterranean dwellings gives their domestic life a character distinct from that of the surface-dwelling humans.

## The Crafts and Trades of the Valley

The towns of the valley are centres of craft and trade, supporting a population of artisans who serve the agricultural households of the surrounding countryside. The most important crafts are those of the miller, the smith, the potter, and the weaver, each of whom practises a trade that has been transmitted from master to apprentice through many generations. The mills are situated along the canals and the smaller tributaries of the Vael, grinding the grain that the farmers bring in from the fields and returning a portion of the flour as the miller's fee. The smiths produce and repair the tools of the fields and the household, working iron and bronze in forges that are among the most substantial structures in the towns.

The towns also support a class of merchants who trade along the river and the roads, connecting the valley to the wider world. The merchants of the valley are respected but not accorded the highest social status, which remains with the landowners who hold the most productive fields and the priests of the Still Flame who guide the spiritual life of the community. The merchants purchase the surplus grain of the valley and transport it downstream to the markets of Aldreth, returning with goods that the valley cannot produce for itself.

The market day is the principal social occasion of the week, when the farmers bring their surplus produce to the town and the townspeople gather to buy and sell. The market is held in the central square of the town, a space dominated by the temple of the Still Flame on one side and the town hall on the other. The market is a noisy and crowded affair, conducted in a mixture of [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) and the [Trade Tongue](/content/language/pseudo/trade-tongue), with merchants calling out their prices and customers bargaining for the best deal. The market is also the occasion for the exchange of news and gossip, the arrangement of marriages and apprenticeships, and the settlement of debts and disputes.

## Religion and Community Life

The Still Flame is the principal religion of the valley, and its observances structure the community's life beyond the agricultural cycle. The temple of each village and town is the focal point of community life, the place where the faithful gather for the weekly kindling ceremony and for the major festivals of the religious year. The priest of the Still Flame is a figure of authority in the community, called upon to bless the fields at planting time, to preside at weddings and funerals, and to counsel those in distress.

The valley also retains older traditions that predate the coming of the Still Flame. The veneration of the river is still practised by some of the older families, who make offerings to the Vael at the beginning of the planting season and at the time of the harvest. The [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/culture/sky-walkers-way) retains adherents in the more remote communities of the upper valley, where the older polytheist traditions of the central lowlands have not been entirely displaced. The adherents of the two religions maintain a respectful distance, each regarding the other as misguided but not as hostile.

## The Winter Season

Winter in the Vael valley is a season of confinement and indoor labour. The fields lie fallow under the snow, and the river runs low and slow, sometimes freezing over in the coldest years. The households bring in the stores that will sustain them through the winter: the grain that has been ground and stored in the granaries, the preserves that have been prepared in the autumn, the salted meat and fish that have been cured in the smokehouses. The winter diet is heavier than the summer diet, relying on stored provisions and the produce of the household garden that has been dried or pickled.

The long evenings of winter are a time for storytelling and the transmission of the community's traditions. The older members of the household relate the histories and legends of the valley, teaching the younger generation the deeds of their ancestors and the customs that have governed life in the valley since time immemorial. The winter is also the season of weddings, as [Hands Joined](/content/culture/hands-joined) ceremonies are concentrated in the period between the [Long Night Festival](/content/culture/long-night-festival) and the beginning of the spring planting. The winter is a season of hardship but also of community, a time when the bonds between households are strengthened through the shared experience of enduring the cold.

## The Enduring Pattern

Everyday life in the Vael valley follows a pattern that has been repeated generation after generation, a cycle of planting and harvest, of birth and death, of work and festival that has shaped the character of the Vaelic people. The pattern is not static, and the valley has been transformed by the coming of the Still Flame, the expansion of trade, and the growing influence of the coastal kingdoms. Yet the fundamental rhythms of the river and the seasons continue to govern the daily lives of the valley's inhabitants. The fields are still planted and harvested at the appointed times, the river still carries the goods of the valley to the coast, and the community still gathers in the temple and the market square to conduct the business of life. The valley endures as a place of settled agriculture and stable community, a foundation of the civilisation that the peoples of Aethelar have built.
