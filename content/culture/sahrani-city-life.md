## The Coastal City-States

The [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast of the continent of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) is a narrow strip of fertile land between the sea and the [Sahrani Wastes](/content/location/settlement/region/sahrani-wastes), a region of ancient cities and prosperous ports that have been the centres of civilisation in the [southern lands](/content/location/natural/region/southern-lands) since the earliest period of recorded history. The city-states of the Sahrani coast are independent entities, each governed by its own council and each maintaining its own traditions, but they are united by a common language, a common culture, and a common dependence on the sea and the trade that passes through their harbours.

The most important of the city-states are [Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar), [Vereth](/content/location/settlement/town/vereth), and [Shurra](/content/location/settlement/village/shurra), each of which controls a stretch of the coastal strip and the hinterland that extends into the wastes. The cities are built of stone and whitewashed plaster, their buildings arranged along narrow streets that follow the contours of the coast. The architecture of the Sahrani cities is designed to provide shade and cooling, with interior courtyards and rooftop terraces that catch the sea breeze.

## The Salt Trade and the Caravans

The economy of the Sahrani city-states is based on the [salt](/content/economy/salt) trade and the caravan routes that connect the coast to the interior of the continent. The salt is produced from the salt pans that line the coast, evaporated from the seawater by the heat of the southern sun, and processed in the saltworks that are the principal industry of the region. The salt is traded to the interior kingdoms and to the distant lands beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), carried by the caravans that assemble in the Sahrani cities and depart for the interior at the appointed seasons.

The [Sahrani Caravan Feast](/content/culture/sahrani-caravan-feast) is the principal celebration of the caravan season, observed before the departure of the principal caravans for the interior. The feast is an occasion of blessing and farewell, when the merchants and the caravan masters gather to pray for the success of the journey and to exchange the gifts that will ensure the favour of the desert spirits. The caravan feast is also the occasion for the settling of accounts and the arrangement of the partnerships that will govern the conduct of the caravan.

## The Courtyard Household

The household of the Sahrani city-states is centred on the courtyard, an interior space that serves as the focus of domestic life. The courtyard is open to the sky, planted with shade-giving trees and provided with a fountain or a pool that cools the air. The rooms of the household open onto the courtyard, their doors and windows positioned to catch the breeze that passes through the courtyard and to provide privacy from the view of the neighbours.

The Sahrani household typically comprises the extended family, including the grandparents, the parents, and the children, together with the servants and the dependents who are attached to the household. The head of the household is the senior capable adult, who manages the household's resources and represents the household in the councils of the city. The authority of the household head is exercised in consultation with the other adult members of the household, and the decisions of the household are reached through discussion and consensus.

## The Market and the Suq

The market of the Sahrani city is the suq, a network of covered streets that occupies the central quarter of the city. The suq is divided into sections, each devoted to a particular trade or commodity. The spice merchants occupy one quarter, the cloth merchants another, and the metalworkers a third. The organisation of the suq reflects the organisation of the guilds that regulate each trade.

The suq is the heart of the commercial life of the city, the place where the goods of the interior are exchanged for the goods of the coast and where the merchants conduct the business that sustains the prosperity of the city-states. The suq is governed by the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court), the body that regulates the conduct of trade and resolves the disputes that arise between merchants.

## The Salt Court and the Governance of Trade

The [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court) is the principal authority in the commercial life of the city-states, a council of experienced merchants who oversee the conduct of trade and the maintenance of the standards that govern the quality of the goods. The Salt Court meets in the merchants' hall, a building of stone and marble that stands at the centre of the suq, and its decisions are binding upon the merchants of the city.

The Salt Court is responsible for the certification of the [weights and measures](/content/economy/weights-and-measures) that are used in the trade, the inspection of the goods that are offered for sale, and the resolution of the disputes that arise between merchants. The Salt Court also maintains the register of the debts and the credits that the merchants of the city incur in the course of trade, ensuring that the financial obligations of the merchants are recorded and enforced.

## The Sea and the Ships

The harbour of the Sahrani city is the other centre of the community's life, the place where the ships that carry the salt and the other goods of the coast depart for the distant ports of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles). The Sahrani ships are built for the conditions of the southern sea, with hulls that are designed to withstand the heat and the storms of the southern latitudes.

The daily life of the harbour is governed by the tides and the winds, the forces that determine the schedule of the shipping. The ships depart on the morning tide, carrying the goods of the coast to the distant ports, and they return on the evening tide, bringing the goods of the wider world to the Sahrani markets. The harbour is a place of constant activity, supervised by the harbour master and the customs officers of the Salt Court.

## The Faiths of the Sahrani Coast

The religion of the Sahrani city-states is the older polytheist tradition of the southern lands, combined with elements of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) that have been adopted by some of the merchant families who trade with the northern ports. The principal figures of the Sahrani religion are the Sun Father and the Sea Mother, the deities who govern the conditions that determine the prosperity of the coast.

The temples of the Sahrani cities are buildings of considerable architectural ambition, decorated with the tilework and the carvings that are the distinctive contribution of the Sahrani artists to the civilisation of the known world. The temples are the centres of the religious life of the community, the places where the faithful gather for the daily offerings and the major festivals of the religious year.

## The Heat and the Breeze

Life in the Sahrani city-states is conditioned by the heat of the southern climate, a presence that shapes every aspect of the daily routine. The morning is the principal period of activity, when the streets and the suq are busy with the work of the day. The afternoon is the period of rest, when the heat of the sun drives the inhabitants into the shade of the courtyards and the interior rooms. The evening is the period of social activity, when the families sit on the rooftops and the balconies, enjoying the breeze that rises from the sea and the cooler air of the night.

The Sahrani people have adapted to the heat over the centuries, developing the customs and the practices that make life possible in the climate of the southern coast. The architecture of the cities, the pattern of the daily routine, and the traditions of the diet are all designed to mitigate the effects of the heat and to make the most of the resources that the coast provides. Life in the Sahrani city-states is a life of adaptation to the conditions that the environment imposes, a civilisation built on the edge of the desert, sustained by the sea and the trade that passes through the harbours of the coast.
