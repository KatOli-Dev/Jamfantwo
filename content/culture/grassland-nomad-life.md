## The Great Grass Plains

The [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are the domain of the nomadic peoples who have followed the herds across the grasslands since prehistoric times. The plains are a vast expanse of tall grasses that stretch from the foothills of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) in the east to the banks of the [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river) in the west, a region of open sky and distant horizons that has shaped the character of its inhabitants. The principal peoples of the plains are the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs), the four-legged people who are the oldest inhabitants of the grasslands, and the human herders who have adopted the nomadic way of life over the centuries of their settlement on the plains.

The daily life of the plains peoples is governed by the movement of the herds and the cycle of the seasons. The spring finds the tribes scattered across the southern plains, where the grass grows earliest and the calving herds can be protected from the worst of the weather. The summer is the season of the great gatherings, when the tribes converge on the traditional meeting places for the [Plains High Days](/content/culture/plains-high-days) and the other festivals that mark the turning of the year. The autumn is the season of the hunt, when the tribes follow the herds as they move south before the winter. The winter is the season of the camps, when the tribes settle in the sheltered valleys of the southern plains and endure the cold until the spring.

## The Centaur Herds and the Human Flocks

The economy of the plains is based on the management of animals. The centaurs maintain herds of the semi-domesticated horses that are their kin and their livelihood, the source of the milk and cheese that form the principal food of the centaur diet and the foundation of the trade that connects the plains to the settled lands. The human herders maintain flocks of sheep and goats, the source of the wool and meat that sustain their households and the hides and horns that they trade with the settled peoples of the river valleys.

The management of the herds and the flocks requires a constant attention that governs the daily routine of the plains peoples. The herds must be moved from one pasture to another as the grass is consumed, the waters must be located and the camp moved accordingly, and the predators must be driven off or killed. The work is shared across the tribe, with the younger and more able members taking the greater share of the physical labour and the older members contributing the wisdom of their experience.

## The Mobile Household

The household of the plains peoples is a mobile unit, designed to be packed and moved at short notice when the condition of the pasture requires it. The centaurs do not build dwellings in the manner of settled peoples, but they construct shelters of hide and frame that serve as the centres of their domestic life. The human herders live in tents of felt and wool, erected on frames of wood that are disassembled and transported on the backs of pack animals when the tribe moves.

The interior of the tent or shelter is the domain of the women and the children, who maintain the household and prepare the food. The hearth is the centre of the domestic space, a fire of dried dung or brushwood that is kept burning throughout the day and banked at night. The food of the plains is simple and substantial, based on the products of the herds and the flocks. Milk and cheese from the horses or the goats are the staple foods, supplemented by the meat of the animals that are slaughtered for the pot and the wild plants that the women gather from the grasslands.

## The Tribe and the Kinship Bond

The tribe is the fundamental unit of plains society, a group of related families that move together across the grasslands and share the obligations of mutual defence and mutual support. The tribe is led by a chief, chosen by the elders of the tribe for the wisdom of the chief's counsel and the courage of the chief's leadership in war. The chief is advised by the council of elders, the oldest and most respected members of the tribe, who meet whenever the tribe is in camp to discuss the matters that affect the welfare of the community.

The kinship bond is the strongest tie in plains society, and the obligations of kinship extend to the most distant relatives. The killing of a kinsman is the gravest crime in the customary law of the plains, punished by exile from the tribe or by the blood feud that the kin of the slain are bound to pursue. The blood feud is a recognised institution of plains society, governed by strict rules that limit its scope and duration, but it remains a source of conflict that the chiefs and the elders must constantly manage.

## The Great Gatherings and the Plains High Days

The summer gatherings are the principal social events of the plains year, when the tribes that have been scattered across the grasslands converge on the traditional meeting places. The gatherings are occasions of celebration and competition, when the young people of the tribes test their skills in the contests of speed and strength that are the pride of the plains peoples. The most important contests are the horse races of the centaurs, which are run at full gallop across the open plains, and the wrestling matches of the human herders, which are fought according to the traditional rules that each tribe maintains.

The gatherings are also the occasion for the [Plains High Days](/content/culture/plains-high-days), the principal religious observance of the plains peoples, when the shamans of the tribes conduct the ceremonies that honour the spirits of the grasslands and the ancestors of the peoples. The High Days are the occasion for the telling of the traditional histories, when the elders recite the deeds of the ancestors and the younger members of the tribes learn the stories that define their identity.

## Trade with the Settled Lands

The plains peoples maintain a regular trade with the settled peoples of the river valleys and the coastal cities, exchanging the products of the herds and the flocks for the goods that the grasslands do not produce. The principal exports of the plains are horses, wool, hides, horns, and the dairy products that the settled peoples value as delicacies. The principal imports are grain, metal tools, cloth, and the luxury goods that the plains peoples have come to value as markers of status.

The trade is conducted at the traditional meeting places on the borders of the plains, where the tribes and the merchants of the settled lands assemble at appointed times. The trade is governed by the customary law of the plains, which specifies the terms of exchange and the penalties for the violation of the agreements. The merchants of the settled lands are required to observe the customs of the plains, including the laws of hospitality that govern the treatment of visitors.

## The Spiritual Life of the Plains

The religion of the plains peoples is the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/culture/sky-walkers-way), the polytheist tradition that honours the spirits of the sky and the earth and the ancestors of the peoples. The central figure of the plain's religion is the Sky Father, the god of the open sky and the ruler of the weather, who is invoked in every ceremony of the tribal year. The earth mother is the other principal figure, the goddess of the grasslands and the source of the grass that sustains the herds.

The shamans of the tribes are the intermediaries between the human world and the spirit world, trained in the arts of divination and healing. The shaman is a figure of considerable authority in the tribe, consulted in every matter of importance and respected for the power that the shaman commands. The training of a shaman is a long and demanding process, requiring the apprentice to learn the traditional lore of the tribe and to develop the skills of trance and vision that the shaman uses in the conduct of the ceremonies.

## The Open Sky

Life on the Great Grass Plains is a life lived under the open sky, a condition that the plains peoples regard not as a hardship but as the natural and proper state of human existence. The plains are a world of distance and horizon, where the sun rises and sets over the unbroken line of the grass and the stars wheel overhead through the long nights of the winter. The plains peoples are a people of the horizon, their eyes accustomed to the long view and their minds accustomed to the patient endurance that the life of the nomad requires. The plains have shaped the peoples who live upon them, and the character of the centaurs and the human herders bears the mark of the grasslands as surely as the character of the settled peoples bears the mark of their fields and their cities.
