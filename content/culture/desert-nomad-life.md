## The Deep Sands

The deserts of the known world are vast expanses of sand and rock that occupy the interior of the continent of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) and the [eastern reach](/content/location/natural/continent/eastern-reach)es of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The principal desert peoples are the [Kharesh](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), who inhabit the great sand sea known as the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and the [Vashari](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), who dwell in the rocky wastes and the dry riverbeds of the eastern deserts. The desert is a harsh and unforgiving environment, but the peoples who have made it their home have developed the skills and the traditions that enable them to survive and prosper in conditions that would defeat the unprepared.

The daily life of the desert nomads is governed by the availability of water and the movement of the herds that sustain the nomadic economy. The camps move with the seasons, following the pattern of rainfall and the growth of the sparse vegetation that supports the herds. The summer is the season of the high heat, when the tribes retreat to the mountain refuges that provide shade and water. The winter is the season of the rains, when the desert blooms and the herds fatten on the fresh growth.

## The Camel and the Herd

The camel is the foundation of the desert economy, the animal that carries the nomads across the sands and provides the milk and the meat that sustain them. The desert nomads maintain herds of camels that are the measure of the tribe's wealth and the foundation of the tribe's status. The camel is also the principal means of transport, the animal that carries the goods of the desert to the markets of the coast and the settled lands.

The management of the camel herd is the principal occupation of the desert nomads, the work that determines the routine of the camp. The camels must be watered at the appointed intervals, the milk must be collected and processed, and the young must be protected from the predators that inhabit the desert margins. The camel is the subject of a substantial body of traditional knowledge, transmitted from the elders to the younger generation, that specifies the proper treatment of the animal in every condition of health and disease.

## The Tent and the Camp

The dwelling of the desert nomads is the tent, a structure of woven goat hair and camel hide that is designed to be easily assembled and disassembled for the movement of the camp. The tent is divided into the men's section and the women's section, each with its own hearth and its own area for the preparation of food and the conduct of domestic work.

The camp is the basic unit of desert society, a group of related families that travel together and share the obligations of mutual defence and mutual support. The camp is led by the sheikh, the senior male of the leading family, who is chosen by the elders of the camp for the wisdom of his counsel and the courage of his leadership. The sheikh is advised by the council of elders, the oldest and most respected members of the camp, who meet at the evening fires to discuss the matters that affect the welfare of the community.

## The Water and the Well

The well is the most important resource in the desert, the source of the water that sustains the camp and the herds. The wells are distributed across the desert at distances that reflect the capacity of the camels to travel between them. The ownership of the wells is governed by the customary law of the desert, which specifies the rights of the tribes to the water and the obligations of the well owners to the travellers who pass through their territory.

The well is also a place of social exchange, where the travellers and the tribes meet to exchange news and to conduct the business that the isolation of the desert makes difficult. The well is governed by the laws of hospitality that are the most sacred of the desert customs, requiring the well owner to provide water to the traveller and the traveller to accept the water with gratitude and to depart without causing harm to the well or its owner.

## The Caravan and the Trade

The caravans that cross the desert are the principal means of trade between the coast and the interior, carrying the goods of the [Sahrani city-states](/content/culture/sahrani-city-life) to the kingdoms of the interior and returning with the products of the inland regions. The caravans are organised by the merchants of the coast, who hire the guides and the guards from the desert tribes to conduct the caravan across the sands.

The caravan is a complex organisation, requiring the coordination of the camels, the drivers, the guides, and the guards. The caravan is led by the caravan master, an experienced traveller who knows the routes and the wells and who is responsible for the safety of the caravan and the success of the journey. The caravan master is assisted by the guides, who are members of the desert tribes and who provide the knowledge of the terrain and the relationships with the tribes whose territory the caravan crosses.

## The Blood Debt and the Tribal Law

The customary law of the desert tribes is codified in the [Kharesh Blood Debt](/content/law/kharesh-blood-debt), a body of tradition that specifies the penalties for the offences that arise within the tribe and the obligations that the tribes owe to each other. The blood debt is the most serious of the customary obligations, requiring the kin of a slain person to seek redress from the kin of the slayer. The blood debt is a source of conflict that the tribes must manage through the councils of the elders and the payment of compensation.

The law of the desert also specifies the rights of the travellers and the obligations of the tribes to the travellers who pass through their territory. The traveller is entitled to the protection of the tribe whose territory the traveller crosses, and the tribe is entitled to the respect and the conduct of the traveller. The violation of the obligations of hospitality is one of the most serious offences in the customary law of the desert, punished by the exclusion of the violator from the protection of the law.

## The Spirits of the Desert

The religion of the desert nomads is the tradition of the ancestor spirits and the desert spirits, the older animistic tradition that predates the arrival of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) and the other religions of the settled lands. The desert spirits are the beings that inhabit the wells, the rocks, and the sand dunes, and they are honoured by the offerings and the prayers that the nomads make at the appropriate times.

The shaman of the tribe is the intermediary between the human world and the spirit world, trained in the arts of divination and healing. The shaman is a figure of considerable authority in the camp, consulted in every matter of importance and respected for the power that the shaman commands. The training of a shaman is a long and demanding process, requiring the apprentice to learn the traditional lore of the tribe and to develop the skills of trance and vision that the shaman uses in the conduct of the ceremonies.

## The Stars and the Sands

Life in the desert is a life of movement and adaptation, governed by the rhythms of the seasons and the availability of the resources that sustain the nomadic economy. The desert nomads are a people of the horizon, their eyes accustomed to the distances of the desert and their minds accustomed to the patience that the life of the nomad requires. The desert has shaped the character of the peoples who dwell within it, and the traditions of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) and the [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) bear the mark of the sands and the stars that guide them across the empty spaces of the world. The desert is a harsh and demanding environment, but it is also a place of freedom and of the dignity that comes from the mastery of the conditions of existence.
