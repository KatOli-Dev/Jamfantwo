## The Season of the Ancestral Stories

The Winter Telling is the principal cultural observance of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) of northern [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), the period during the deepest winter when the families gather around the hearth and tell the stories of the ancestors. The Winter Telling begins on the longest night of the year and continues until the first signs of the spring, and the stories that are told during this period are the means by which the memory of the ancestors is preserved and transmitted from one generation to the next.

The Winter Telling is the most important of the Frostwall cultural observances, and the children who hear the stories during the Winter Telling carry the memory of the ancestors into the future. The observance is conducted in the great halls of the clans and in the smaller households of the isolated settlements, and the stories that are told during the Winter Telling are the foundation of the identity of the Frostwall peoples.

## The Longest Night

The Winter Telling begins on the longest night of the year, the night when the darkness is at its greatest and the cold is at its most intense. The families gather in the great hall of the clan or in the principal room of the household, and the hearth fire is kindled with the ceremony that the tradition requires.

The first story of the Winter Telling is always the story of the first winter, the tale of the time when the world was young and the Frostwall ancestors learned to survive the cold. The story is told by the eldest member of the family or by the recognised storyteller of the clan, and the family listens with the attention that the story requires.

The story of the first winter tells of the journey that the ancestors made from the warmer lands of the south to the cold north that became their home. The story describes the hardships that the ancestors endured and the discoveries that they made, and it establishes the pattern of the Winter Telling that will continue through the season.

## The Rhythm of the Telling

The Winter Telling follows a rhythm that is established by the traditions of the clan and the conditions of the winter. The stories are told in the evening, after the work of the day is completed and the family has gathered around the hearth. The stories are told in a sequence that is determined by the storyteller, with each story building upon the stories that have gone before.

The stories of the first weeks of the Winter Telling are the stories of the ancestors of the distant past, the founders of the clans and the heroes who established the traditions of the Frostwall peoples. These stories are the oldest and the most sacred of the Frostwall traditions, and the storyteller recites them with the formality that their age requires.

The stories of the middle weeks of the Winter Telling are the stories of the more recent ancestors, the grandparents and the great-grandparents of the living generation. These stories are told with less formality and with more variation, for the memory of the living is less fixed than the memory of the distant past.

The stories of the final weeks of the Winter Telling are the stories of the year that has passed, the events that the family has experienced and the ancestors who have died since the previous Winter Telling. The stories of the passing year are the most personal and the most emotional of the Winter Telling, and the family shares the grief and the joy that the year has brought.

## The Aurora and the Ancestors

The ancestors are believed to inhabit the northern lights, the aurora that dances across the winter sky of Morvind. The Frostwall peoples watch the aurora for signs of the ancestors' presence, and they interpret the colours and the patterns of the lights as messages from the dead.

The aurora is the visible presence of the ancestors in the winter sky, and the stories that are told during the Winter Telling are the interpretations of the messages that the aurora has sent. The storyteller who sees a pattern of green light in the aurora will tell the story of the ancestor who is associated with that colour, and the family will listen for the message that the ancestor intends.

The aurora is also the occasion for the ceremonial dances that the Frostwall peoples perform during the Winter Telling. The dances are conducted in the open air, beneath the aurora, and the dancers move in the patterns that the lights trace across the sky. The dances are the means by which the living communicate with the ancestors, and the dancers who perform the dances are believed to be possessed by the spirits of the ancestors for the duration of the dance.

## The Hearth as the Stage

The hearth is the stage upon which the Winter Telling is performed. The fire is the source of the light and the warmth that the stories require, and the family gathers around the hearth in the arrangement that tradition prescribes. The storyteller sits closest to the fire, with the younger members of the family arranged behind in order of age.

The hearth fire is tended with the care that the Winter Telling requires, for the fire must be kept burning through the hours of the telling and must not be allowed to go out. The fire is fed with the wood that has been gathered and dried for the purpose, and the family members take turns tending the fire during the stories.

The hearth is also the repository of the objects that the storyteller uses to illustrate the stories. The objects include the tools and the weapons of the ancestors, the clothing and the ornaments that they wore, and the carvings and the weavings that they made. The storyteller displays these objects during the telling of the relevant stories, and the family members examine them with the attention that they require.

## The End of the Telling

The Winter Telling ends with the first signs of the spring, the moment when the snow begins to melt and the daylight begins to lengthen. The final story of the Winter Telling is always the story of the return of the sun, the tale of the first spring after the first winter that the Frostwall ancestors experienced.

The story of the return of the sun is told by the eldest member of the family or by the recognised storyteller of the clan, and the family listens with the attention that the story requires. The story describes the joy that the ancestors felt when the sun returned and the warmth began to return to the land, and it establishes the pattern of the spring that will follow the Winter Telling.

The story is followed by the extinguishing of the hearth fire and the kindling of the new fire for the spring. The new fire is kindled from the embers of the old fire, and the continuity of the flame is the symbol of the continuity of the family and the clan through the winter and into the spring. The Winter Telling is concluded, and the family prepares for the work of the new season.
