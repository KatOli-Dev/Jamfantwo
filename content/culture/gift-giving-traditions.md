## The Language of the Gift

The giving of gifts is among the most important of the social institutions of the known world, the means by which the bonds between individuals and between communities are established and maintained. The gift is a language that speaks of the relationship between the giver and the receiver, expressing the gratitude, the respect, the affection, or the obligation that the giver feels. The customs that govern the giving and the receiving of gifts vary considerably from one culture to another, but the fundamental importance of the gift as a social instrument is recognised everywhere.

The gift creates an obligation of reciprocity, the expectation that the receiver will at some future time provide a gift of equivalent value to the giver. The obligation of reciprocity is the foundation of the gift economy that operates alongside the commercial economy in every culture of the known world. The gift that is given without the expectation of return is the purest expression of the generosity that the community values, but the gift that is given with the expectation of return is the more common form of the exchange.

## The Gifts of the Vael Valley

The gift giving customs of the Vael river valley are governed by the traditions of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) and the customary law of the parish. The principal occasions for the giving of gifts are the festivals of the agricultural year and the ceremonies that mark the passage of the individual through the stages of life.

The [Crown Harvest](/content/culture/crown-harvest) is the principal occasion for the giving of gifts in the Vael valley, when the community gathers to celebrate the bringing in of the harvest and to exchange the gifts that acknowledge the bonds between the households. The gifts are typically the products of the household's labour: the grain, the preserves, the cloth, or the craft goods that the household has produced. The value of the gift is less important than the gesture of the giving, and the household that gives generously is respected for the generosity.

The naming ceremony, the [Hands Joined](/content/culture/hands-joined) marriage ceremony, and the funeral are the principal occasions for the giving of gifts within the family. The gifts that are given at the naming ceremony are the tokens that welcome the child into the community. The gifts that are given at the marriage ceremony are the household goods that the couple will need to establish the new household. The gifts that are given at the funeral are the offerings that honour the memory of the deceased.

## The Gifts of the Plains

The gift giving customs of the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) and the human herders of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) reflect the nomadic conditions of the life and the importance of the horse and the livestock in the economy of the plains. The principal gifts of the plains are the horses, the cattle, and the products of the herd that are the measure of the wealth in the nomadic culture.

The marriage gifts are the most important of the gifts of the plains, the bride price that the groom's family pays to the bride's family and the dowry that the bride's family provides to the couple. The bride price is typically paid in horses or in cattle, the number of the animals reflecting the status of the families and the quality of the bride. The dowry includes the household goods that the bride will bring to the marriage and the tent that will be the home of the couple.

The gifts of the hunt are the other important category of the gifts of the plains. The hunter who has made a successful kill is expected to share the meat with the members of the tribe, distributing the portions according to the traditional rules that govern the division of the game. The sharing of the meat is the expression of the generosity that the culture of the plains values, and the hunter who hoards the meat is regarded with the contempt of the community.

## The Gifts of the Dwarven Halls

The gift giving customs of the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) reflect the importance of the craft and the lineage in the dwarven culture. The principal gifts of the dwarves are the works of the craft that the dwarven smiths and the craftsmen produce, the objects of the metal and the stone that are the measure of the skill in the dwarven culture.

The gifts of the forge are the most valued of the dwarven gifts, the weapons, the tools, and the ornaments that the master smiths produce. The gift of a dwarven weapon is the highest honour that the dwarven culture can bestow, the recognition of the worth of the receiver and the respect of the giver. The gift of the dwarven tool is the acknowledgement of the craft of the receiver and the bond of the shared work.

The gifts of the naming ceremony and the binding ceremony are the tokens that mark the acceptance of the child into the community of the hall. The child is given the tools of the craft that the child will pursue, the gifts that symbolise the beginning of the child's training and the child's connection to the lineage.

## The Gifts of the Sahrani Coast

The gift giving customs of the [Sahrani city-states](/content/culture/sahrani-city-life) reflect the mercantile culture of the coast, in which the gift is an instrument of the commercial relationship. The principal gifts of the Sahrani coast are the goods of the trade, the spices, the silks, and the precious metals that are the currency of the commercial exchange.

The gift of the salt is the most important of the Sahrani gifts, the symbol of the bond that is created by the sharing of the meal. The guest who has shared the salt of the host is bound by the obligation of the gratitude, and the host who has offered the salt is bound by the obligation of the protection. The gift of the salt is the foundation of the trust that the trade requires, and the Sahrani merchants maintain the custom of the salt in every transaction.

The gifts of the festival are the other important category of the gifts of the Sahrani coast. The major festivals of the religious year are the occasions for the exchange of the gifts between the families, the gifts that reaffirm the bonds of the kinship and the friendship. The gifts are typically the luxury goods of the trade, the silks and the spices and the precious metals that are the markers of the status in the Sahrani culture.

## The Gifts of the Northern Coast

The gift giving customs of the northern coast reflect the conditions of the maritime life, in which the gift is an expression of the solidarity of the community. The principal gifts of the northern coast are the catch of the sea, the fish and the whale oil and the seal skins that are the products of the northern hunt.

The gifts of the [Long Night Festival](/content/culture/long-night-festival) are the most important of the gifts of the northern coast, the tokens that are exchanged at the darkest time of the year as the reaffirmation of the bonds between the households. The gifts are typically the products of the household's labour: the woven cloth, the carved bone, the preserved fish. The gift is less important than the gesture of the giving, and the household that gives generously is respected for the generosity.

## The Meaning of the Gift

The gift giving traditions of the known world reflect the values that each culture holds most important. The gift is the language that speaks of the relationship between the giver and the receiver, expressing the values of the generosity, the gratitude, the respect, and the obligation that the community requires. The gift that is given freely and without the expectation of return is the purest expression of the generosity that binds the community together, but the gift that is given with the expectation of return is the more common form of the exchange. The meaning of the gift is the meaning that the giver and the receiver assign to it, and the gift is the instrument of the social bond that connects the individuals and the communities of the known world.
