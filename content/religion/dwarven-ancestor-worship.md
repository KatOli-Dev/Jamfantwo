## The Honouring of the Forebears and the Mountain

The religious practices of the dwarven peoples are centred upon the reverence for the ancestors and the mountain, the two poles around which the spiritual life of the holds revolves. The dwarves do not maintain temples in the manner of the human coastal cities, and they do not have a priesthood or a formal doctrine of the kind that the contemplative orders cultivate. The dwarven reverence is expressed through the practices of the household and the hold, through the preservation of the names and the deeds of the forebears, and through the acknowledgement of the mountain as the source and the sustainer of the dwarven life.

The ancestors are the dead who have gone before, and the dwarves hold that the ancestors remain present in the life of the hold. The ancestor is not a deity to be worshipped in the manner that the Sea Mother is worshipped, but a presence to be honoured through the memory and the observance of the traditions that the ancestor maintained. The ancestor who is forgotten is a loss to the hold, and the dwarves take great care to ensure that the names and the deeds of the forebears are preserved for the generations that follow.

## The Halls of the Memory

The halls of the memory are the chambers that are cut into the rock of the hold for the preservation of the records of the ancestors. The hall of the memory is a long chamber lined with the stone shelves upon which the tablets of the ancestors are placed. The tablet is a piece of the polished stone upon which the name and the deeds of the ancestor are inscribed, and the tablets are arranged in the order of the generations, from the oldest at the far end of the hall to the most recent at the entrance.

The hall of the memory is the most sacred place in the hold, and the dwarves who enter it do so with the quiet reverence. The hall is lit by the lamps that are kept burning at the intervals along the walls, and the names of the ancestors are read aloud at the appointed times by the keeper of the memory.

The keeper of the memory is the dwarf who is charged with the preservation of the tablets and the recitation of the names. The keeper is trained from the youth in the lore of the ancestors, and the keeper's knowledge of the genealogies and the deeds of the forebears is the foundation of the hold's memory.

## The Rites of the Ancestors

The principal rite of the ancestor reverence is the gathering at the hall of the memory, conducted at the turning of the seasons and at the times of the important events in the life of the hold. The dwarves gather in the hall, and the keeper recites the names of the ancestors from the oldest to the most recent. The recitation is followed by the silence in which the living honour the dead and the dead are held in the attention of the living.

The rite of the naming is conducted when a dwarf dies. The body of the dwarf is prepared for the return to the mountain, and the name of the dwarf is added to the tablet that is placed in the hall of the memory. The keeper recites the name of the newly departed dwarf at the next gathering, and the living acknowledge the passage of the ancestor into the company of the forebears.

The rite of the consultation is conducted when the hold faces a difficult decision. The elders of the hold gather in the hall of the memory, and the keeper recites the names of the ancestors who faced the similar challenges. The elders reflect on the deeds and the decisions of the ancestors, seeking the guidance that the example of the ancestors provides.

## The Mountain as the Source

The mountain is the second pole of the dwarven reverence, honoured as the source from which the dwarven life proceeds and the foundation upon which the dwarven civilisation is built. The mountain provides the stone from which the halls are built, the metal from which the tools and the weapons are forged, and the shelter from the dangers of the surface world.

The dwarves do not worship the mountain as a deity, and the reverence that they show to the mountain is not the worship of the divine. The reverence is the acknowledgment of the dependence, the recognition that the dwarf is the child of the mountain and that the dwarf's life is sustained by the mountain's bounty. The dwarf who mines the ore and the dwarf who forges the steel and the dwarf who builds the hall are each engaged in a relationship with the mountain that is both practical and spiritual.

The rite of the mountain is conducted at the opening of a new mine or the cutting of a new hall. The elders of the hold gather at the place where the work is to begin, and the senior miner speaks the prayer of the asking, requesting the mountain's permission for the work that is to be done. The prayer is not a petition to a deity but an acknowledgment of the relationship, a recognition that the work of the dwarf is a collaboration with the mountain.

## The Rites of the Deep Earth

The dwarves also observe the rites that are connected to the deep earth, the realm beneath the lowest workings of the holds. The deep earth is the place from which the first dwarves are said to have emerged, and the dwarves regard the deep earth with the reverence that is due to the origin.

The rite of the deep earth is conducted at the times of the great transitions in the life of the hold. The elders descend to the deepest chamber of the hold, and the keeper of the deep speaks the words that have been transmitted from the oldest times. The words are not spoken aloud to the assembly, for the rite of the deep earth is the most secret of the dwarven observances.

## The Differences Among the Holds

The practices of the ancestor reverence vary among the different dwarven holds, reflecting the different histories and the different conditions of the regions. The Stoneheim Holds, the oldest and the most extensive of the dwarven territories, maintain the most elaborate of the ancestral traditions. The halls of the memory in Stoneheim are the largest and the most complete, and the keepers of the memory in Stoneheim are the most respected of the dwarven scholars.

The Karhold Realm, which borders the Stoneheim Holds to the south, has developed the traditions that reflect the closer contact with the human peoples of the region. The Karhold dwarves have adopted some of the human practices, and the halls of the memory in Karhold are less extensive than the halls of Stoneheim. The dwarves of the more isolated holds maintain the older traditions without the variation, and the elders of the isolated holds regard the Stoneheim practices as the standard against which the other holds are measured.

## The Ancestor Reverence and the Other Traditions

The dwarven ancestor reverence is not a tradition that seeks the converts or that maintains the correspondence with the other faiths of the continent. The dwarves regard the reverence as the practice of the dwarven people, and the other peoples who come to live in the holds are not required to observe the rites of the ancestors.

The dwarves maintain a respectful distance from the human traditions, regarding them as the practices of the surface peoples who do not share the dwarven relationship with the mountain. The dwarves do not criticise the human traditions, and they expect the humans to extend the same courtesy to the dwarven practices.
