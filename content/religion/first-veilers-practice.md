## The Hermits and the Foundations of the Practice

The First Veilers are the founders of the path of the Sunless Veils, the hermits who withdrew from the older market towns of the central highlands of Aethelar and took up residence in the quiet valleys where the sun's rays reach only briefly in the winter months. The First Veilers are remembered only by the title that the path has preserved, and no personal names have survived from the founding generation. The path is content that the identity of the founders should remain uncertain, for the teaching is more important than the teacher, and the discipline is more important than the name that the teacher bore.

The practice that the First Veilers developed is the discipline of the long retreat, the withdrawal from the bright world and the loud voices for the season of the weeks or the months. The practice is the means by which the veils that obscure the deeper reality are lifted, and the practice is the foundation upon which the path of the Sunless Veils is built.

## The Practice of the Withdrawal

The practice of the withdrawal that the First Veilers developed is the central observance of the path. The practitioner withdraws from the ordinary occupations of the life and enters the curtained chamber where the light is dim and the silence is deep. The chamber is furnished with a low couch, a small table, a basin of water, and a single lamp that is kept burning through the hours of the retreat. The curtains are drawn at the beginning of the retreat and are not opened until the close, and the practitioner remains within the chamber for the duration of the season.

The withdrawal is conducted in the silence, and the practitioner is permitted the company only of the senior veiler who attends to the discipline without the conversation. The practitioner is instructed to attend to the thoughts and the feelings that arise in the silence, observing them without the attachment and allowing them to pass without the pursuit. The silence is the means by which the veils are lifted, and the practitioner who persists through the difficulty of the silence begins to experience the stillness that lies beneath the surface of the thoughts.

The First Veilers discovered that the withdrawal from the bright world and the loud voices was the means by which the veils could be lifted, and they refined the practice over the generations of the founding period. The practice has been preserved without the variation in the Veil Cloister and the other cloisters of the path, and the practitioner who undertakes the retreat today follows the same discipline that the First Veilers established.

## The Practice of the Distinction Between Lifting and Breaking

The First Veilers taught the distinction between lifting the veils and breaking them, and this distinction is the heart of the path's discipline. A veil that is lifted can be lowered again, and the practitioner who lifts a veil gains the ability to see what lies beyond it without losing the ability to function in the ordinary world. A veil that is broken cannot be restored, and the practitioner who breaks a veil suffers a loss of the ordinary capacities that the veil makes possible.

The practice of the distinction is learned through the long retreat, where the practitioner is guided by the senior veiler in the art of the lifting. The practitioner learns to touch the veil with the attention, to lift it slowly and with the care, and to allow it to settle back into the place when the lifting is complete. The practitioner who tries to tear the veil aside in the impatience of the seeking will break the veil and suffer the consequences.

The First Veilers taught that the breaking of the veils is the great danger of the path, and they developed the practice of the long retreat as the means by which the practitioner learns to lift the veils without breaking them. The practice is the safeguard that the First Veilers provided for the generations that would follow.

## The Practice of the Oral Tradition

The First Veilers did not write down the teachings extensively, and the practice of the oral tradition is valued above the written in the path. The First Veilers taught through the practice of the retreat and through the example of the lives, and the senior veilers of the present day transmit the discipline by guiding the novices through the same retreat that the First Veilers developed.

The small body of the writings that the path preserves includes the record of the First Veilers' teachings, but the path regards the teachings as pointing toward the practice and not as a substitute for it. The novice who reads the writings without undertaking the retreat will not understand the teaching, and the novice who undertakes the retreat without reading the writings will still learn the discipline.

The practice of the oral tradition is the means by which the path has preserved the teachings through the centuries. The senior veiler who guides the novice through the retreat speaks the words that the senior veiler learned from the senior veiler before, and the chain of the transmission reaches back to the First Veilers themselves.

## The Practice of the Correspondence with the Kindred Traditions

The First Veilers maintained the correspondence with the kindred traditions of the continent, and the practice of the correspondence has been preserved by the path. The letters that the First Veilers exchanged with the early readers of the Unwritten Name are preserved in the library of the Veil Cloister, and the letters are among the most valued texts in the possession of the path.

The practice of the correspondence is the means by which the path has maintained the openness to the wider world. The First Veilers recognised the Unwritten Name as a kindred path that approached the mystery of the divine through the discipline of the language, and the two traditions have maintained the correspondence through the centuries. The practice of the exchange of the practitioners between the traditions is the continuation of the correspondence that the First Veilers established.

## The Practice of the Community

The First Veilers established the first cloisters in the chambers cut into the sides of the hills, and the practice of the community life is the foundation of the path. The cloister is the place where the long retreat is conducted, where the instruction is given, and where the senior veilers gather for the conference. The community of the cloister is the support that the practitioner needs for the undertaking of the difficult discipline.

The First Veilers lived in the community of the cloister, sharing the labour of the maintenance and the silence of the retreat. The practice of the community life is the means by which the practitioner is sustained through the long seasons of the withdrawal, and the practice is the model upon which the cloisters of the present day are built.

## The Legacy of the Practice

The practice that the First Veilers developed has been preserved through the centuries, and the path of the Sunless Veils is the living continuation of the discipline that the First Veilers established. The practitioner who undertakes the long retreat today follows the same practice that the First Veilers followed, and the senior veiler who guides the novice through the retreat speaks the same words that the First Veilers spoke.

The legacy of the First Veilers is the practice itself, preserved in the discipline of the Veil Cloister and the other cloisters of the path. The practitioner who enters the long retreat and learns to lift the veils without breaking them is continuing the work that the First Veilers began in the quiet valleys of the central highlands.
