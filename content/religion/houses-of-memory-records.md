## The Records and the Practices of the Song-Half

The Houses of Memory are the quiet establishments of the song-half of the Shield and Song, the places where the local songs and traditions are maintained and where the singers of the order keep the memory of the communities they serve. The houses are found in the larger market towns and at the more important river crossings of the temperate lowlands of Aethelar, kept by the order's singers who are recognised by the small wooden clasp they wear at the throat. The houses are regarded by the order as the second of the principal institutions of the calling, the seat of the song-half of the order's work in the same measure that the standing of the watch is the seat of the shield-half.

The houses maintain the records of the communities they serve, preserving the names of the dead, the accounts of the events that have shaped the life of the community, and the songs that the order has gathered through the generations. The records are kept in the library of the house, a chamber lined with the shelves of the plain wood upon which the scrolls and the codices of the songs are stored.

## The Singers and the Training

The Houses of Memory are kept by the singers, the principal officers of the song-half of the calling. A singer is required to learn by heart a substantial body of songs and to maintain the accuracy of the transmission from one generation to the next. The tradition holds that a song learned imperfectly is a song lost, and the singers are trained accordingly.

The training of a singer is conducted by a senior singer of the nearest house and is regarded as the chief instrument by which the song-half is preserved. The novice singer attends the teaching sessions at the house, learning the songs that the order has preserved and the technique of the singing that the tradition requires. The novice also accompanies the senior singer on the travels, learning the craft through the observation and the practice.

The training is long and demanding, for the body of the songs that the singer must learn is substantial and the accuracy that the order requires is exacting. The novice who completes the training is presented to the community at the gathering, and the senior singer places the wooden clasp around the novice's throat as the sign of the calling.

## The Records of the House

The records of the House of Memory are the principal means by which the community's history is preserved. The house maintains the register of the births and the deaths, the marriages and the departures, the events that have shaped the life of the community. The register is the foundation of the evening review, for the review depends on the accurate record of the day's events.

The house also maintains the collection of the songs that the singers have gathered through the generations. The songs include the older songs of the order, the local songs of the communities that the house serves, and the songs that the singers have composed in the observance of the calling. The collection is the library of the house, and the songs are the treasure that the house preserves.

The songs of the order are not written down in the ordinary course, for the order values the song learned by heart above the song written down. The songs that are written are the songs that the senior singers have judged to be of the greatest importance, and the written versions are used for the teaching of the novice singers.

## The Practice of the Evening Review

The central practice of the House of Memory is the evening review, conducted in the meeting-hall of the house at the close of the day. The household or the company recalls together the events of the day, giving thanks for the mercies and observing the losses. A short song of account is sung, naming the day's sick and recovered, the day's fed and unfed, the day's spared and the day's fallen.

The review is not a confession and not a punishment, and the order is careful to mark the difference. The sins of the day are observed but not adjudicated, and the remedy lies in the change of the conduct on the morrow rather than in any further observance at the evening. The order teaches that the review is meant to keep the account honest, so that the community may know what it has done and may be moved, in good time, to mend what has been done amiss.

The evening review is the chief instrument by which the song-half of the calling is observed in the household. The singer who leads the review is the keeper of the community's memory, and the review is the occasion on which the memory is renewed and the account is brought up to the date.

## The Practice of the Travel

The singers of the Houses of Memory travel between the houses and between the communities, carrying the songs from one district to the next. The travel is the means by which the songs are spread and the unity of the order across the region is maintained. The singer who travels is the carrier of the news and the knowledge, and the singer's arrival in the community is the occasion for the gathering.

The travel follows the schedule that the house maintains, alternating the seasons of the teaching with the seasons of the travel. The singer who travels in the season of the travel visits the communities that the house serves, conducting the evening review and teaching the songs to the people who gather.

## The Practice of the Kindred Correspondence

The Houses of Memory maintain the correspondence with the houses of the kindred traditions, and the practice of the correspondence is the means by which the order has preserved the kinship with the Still Flame and the other contemplative orders. A singer of the Shield and Song will sometimes attend the evening review of a hearth-hall of the Still Flame, and a hearth-keeper will sometimes attend the singing at a House of Memory.

The practice of the correspondence is a privilege rather than a claim, and the order does not seek to convert the practitioners of the other traditions. The correspondence is the expression of the order's openness to the wider world, and the exchange of the practices enriches both traditions without diminishing the distinctiveness of each.

## The Practice of the Relationship with the Community

The House of Memory is bound to the community it serves by a customary relation that is held to be part of the discipline of the order. The community is required to maintain the house in the provisions and in the small endowment that the singers require, and the house is required to maintain the community in the keeping of the memory and in the instruction of the young in the songs of the order.

The practice of the relationship is the foundation of the trust that the community places in the house. The house is the keeper of the community's memory, and the community is the provider of the house's maintenance. The breach of the mutual obligation is visited by the censure of the community, delivered by the elders of the community in the presence of the senior singer of the house.
