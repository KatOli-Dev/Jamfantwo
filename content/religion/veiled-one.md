## The Stillness Beyond the Last Veil

The Veiled One is the divine reality toward which the path of the [Sunless Veils](/content/religion/sunless-veils) aspires, the stillness that lies beyond the last veil that can be lifted. The Veiled One is not a deity in the manner of the Sea Mother of the warm coasts, to whom prayers and offerings are made at the temple in Khalisar, nor is it a keeping in the manner of the Watcher of the Shield and Song, in which the practitioner shares by bearing hardship and keeping memory. The Veiled One is a stillness, a presence that cannot be described in the language of the bright world, and the path teaches that the practitioner who approaches the Veiled One learns to be content with the approach and to abandon the expectation of arrival.

The name of the Veiled One is itself a concession to the limitations of language, and the path acknowledges that the name is inadequate to the reality it attempts to indicate. The Veiled One is veiled precisely because it cannot be seen or named or grasped by the faculties that the ordinary world cultivates. The veils that the path lifts are the veils of the bright world, and the Veiled One is not another object that becomes visible when the last veil is lifted; the Veiled One is the stillness that the practitioner enters when the lifting of the veils is complete, and the stillness is not an object of knowledge in the ordinary sense.

## The Stillness of the Veiled One's Own Nature

The path of the Sunless Veils teaches that the long retreat is the chief instrument by which the practitioner is prepared to approach the Veiled One, and the discipline of the retreat is designed to cultivate the stillness that the approach requires. The practitioner who enters the long retreat remains within the curtained chamber for a season of weeks or months, and the withdrawal from the bright world and the loud voices of the ordinary life is the means by which the practitioner learns to rest in the stillness that the Veiled One's nature requires.

The Veiled One is not a void, and the path is careful to mark the difference between the stillness of the Veiled One and the emptiness of mere absence. The Veiled One is a presence that the practitioner experiences as stillness, and the stillness is not the absence of sound or movement but the presence of a quality that the ordinary language cannot name. The practitioner who has completed the long retreat speaks of the stillness with the same care that a craftsman speaks of the quality of the work, and the path holds that the practitioner who has experienced the stillness knows the Veiled One as fully as the practitioner can know.

## The Approach Without Arrival

The path teaches that the practitioner never arrives at the Veiled One in the sense of achieving a final union or comprehension. The approach is the practice, and the stillness that the practitioner experiences in the long retreat is a taste of the Veiled One's nature and not a full possession. The practitioner who undertakes the retreat comes forth quieter than the practitioner who entered, and this change is the measure of the approach. The practitioner does not claim to have reached the Veiled One; the practitioner claims only to have come closer, and the path teaches that the coming closer is the purpose of the discipline and that the arrival is not to be expected in the present life.

The path regards the expectation of arrival as a kind of veil in itself, a veil of impatience that the practitioner must learn to lift. The practitioner who demands to reach the Veiled One is still attached to the bright world's habit of achieving goals and acquiring possessions, and the path teaches that this attachment must be relinquished before the practitioner can approach the stillness that the Veiled One offers. The long retreat is the means by which the practitioner learns to relinquish the demand for arrival and to rest in the approach as an end in itself.

## The Veiled One and the First Veilers

The understanding of the Veiled One is traced to the [First Veilers](/content/religion/first-veilers), the hermits who withdrew from the older market towns and developed the discipline of the long retreat. The First Veilers taught that the ultimate reality is not to be found in the bright world of surfaces and voices but in the stillness that lies beyond the veils that the ordinary world places between the soul and the truth. The First Veilers did not leave a systematic account of the Veiled One, and the path has preserved their teaching in the oral tradition and in the small body of writings that the senior veilers maintain.

The path teaches that the First Veilers understood the Veiled One through the practice of the long retreat and not through speculation or argument, and the order has maintained the practice as the principal means by which the understanding of the Veiled One is transmitted from one generation to the next. The novice who enters the long retreat is not taught about the Veiled One in the manner of a lecture or a text; the novice is prepared for the retreat and is allowed to experience the stillness directly, and the senior veiler who attends to the discipline without conversation is present to guide the novice through the experience without imposing an interpretation upon it.

## The Correspondence with the Stillness

The Sunless Veils maintain correspondence with the Unwritten Name and the Still Flame, the kindred traditions of the continent, and the three traditions have exchanged practitioners for many generations. The Unwritten Name, whose discipline of nouns teaches a kindred attention to the limits of language, has been especially close to the Sunless Veils, and the exchange of senior readers and senior veilers has enriched both traditions. A senior veiler will sometimes attend the conference of readers, and a senior reader will sometimes undertake the long retreat, each welcomed by the other with the courtesy of kindred traditions.

The correspondence between the traditions reflects the path's teaching that the Veiled One is approached through stillness and silence, and that the stillness and silence are not the exclusive possession of any single tradition. The Veiled One is the stillness that underlies all the traditions of the continent, and the path teaches that the practitioner who approaches the stillness approaches the Veiled One whether the practitioner names it or not.

## The Veiled One in the Discipline of the Veils

The Veiled One is not named in the daily observances of the path, and the long retreat does not include prayers or invocations addressed to the Veiled One by name. The Veiled One is acknowledged in the silence that closes the conference of the senior veilers and in the stillness that the practitioner enters at the depth of the long retreat. The path teaches that the silence is the most appropriate form of acknowledgment, for the Veiled One is not a being to be addressed in the manner of the temple deities but a stillness to be entered through the discipline of the retreat.

The practitioner who has completed the long retreat comes forth with a quieter understanding of the Veiled One than the practitioner who entered, and this change is the measure of the retreat's success. The path does not require the practitioner to articulate the understanding, and the senior veilers are trained to recognise the stillness in the practitioner's bearing without requiring the practitioner to describe it. The Veiled One remains veiled even to those who have approached it, and the path teaches that the veiling is not a failure of the discipline but a condition of the reality that the discipline approaches.
