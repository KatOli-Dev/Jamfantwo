## The Central Establishment of the Sunless Veils

The Veil Cloister is the central establishment of the [Sunless Veils](/content/religion/sunless-veils), the quiet house in which the order's retreat is conducted and the long discipline of the veils is sustained. The cloister is found in the inland valleys of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the sun's rays reach only briefly in the winter months, and it is a humble building of stone and timber curtained against the brightness of the day. The cloister is regarded by the path as the chief instrument by which the veils are lifted in their order, held in greater esteem than any of the meeting-houses or chapels found in the towns of the hill country.

The cloister is the seat of the senior veilers, the place where the keepers of the veils instruct the novices and oversee the retreats. The cloister is supported by the labour of the community and the small offerings of the faithful of the surrounding hill towns, who regard the long retreat as a service to the wider faith. The cloister has no endowment of consequence, its small revenues derived from the work of the community's hands and the customary gifts of the hill towns.

## The Hermits and the First Veilers

The Veil Cloister traces its foundation to the community of hermits who withdrew from the older market towns of the central highlands and took up residence in the quiet valleys where the sun's rays reach only briefly in the winter months. The hermits taught that the world of bright surfaces and loud voices was a kind of veil, and the community's founders are remembered only by the title of [the First Veil](/content/mythology/the-first-veil)ers. Whether the title belonged to one or to several is left to the chronicler's discretion, and the path is content that the teaching should be remembered whatever the case may be.

The first cloister was a single chamber cut into the side of a hill, curtained against the day. The community's successors added a second chamber and then a third, and the cloister grew in proportion to the number of novices admitted to the long retreat. The path spread quietly through the inland valleys, and each new valley that received the teaching required its own cloister, for the distance between the valleys is such that a single cloister could not serve the whole of the faithful. Though each cloister is modelled upon the first, each is governed by the same discipline the first community established.

## The Curtained Room and the Silence of the Retreat

The central observance of the path is the long retreat, a discipline of withdrawal undertaken at the cloister for a season of weeks or months. The retreat is conducted in silence and in the dim light of a small chamber curtained against the day's brightness, and the practitioner is permitted the company only of a senior veiler who attends to the discipline without conversation. The chamber is furnished with a low couch, a small table, a basin of water, and a single lamp kept burning through the hours of the retreat. The curtains are drawn at the beginning of the retreat and are not opened until the close, and the practitioner remains within the chamber for the duration of the season.

The retreat is not a punishment, and the path is careful to mark the difference. It is held to be the chief instrument by which the veils are lifted in their order, and the elders regard it as the most demanding and rewarding of the path's disciplines. The practitioner who undertakes the retreat comes forth quieter than the practitioner who entered, and this change is regarded by the elders as the measure of the retreat's success. The chamber of the retreat is the heart of the cloister, the place to which every novice aspires and to which every senior veiler returns.

## The Alternating Cycle of Withdrawal and Instruction

The path maintains at the cloister a schedule of study observed throughout the year. The schedule is divided into seasons of withdrawal and seasons of instruction, alternating in a cycle refined over generations. The seasons of withdrawal are set apart for the long retreat, and the seasons of instruction are set apart for reading the older texts and discussing the doctrine of the veils. The schedule is observed without exception, and breaking it is regarded by the elders as a failure of discipline.

The seasons of instruction are conducted by the senior veilers, who lead the novices through the readings the path has preserved. The readings are drawn from the older writings of the path and the commentaries added by senior veilers over the generations. The readings are conducted in the meeting-hall of the cloister, a plain room lit by a single window curtained against the brightness of the day. The meeting-hall is the second of the principal rooms of the cloister, the place in which the doctrine is taught and the discipline of the veils is explained.

## The Small Collection of Older Writings

The cloister maintains a small library of the texts the path has preserved, the third of the principal rooms of the cloister. The library is housed in a chamber lined with shelves of plain wood, holding copies of the older texts and the commentaries the path has produced. The library is open to the senior veilers and to the novices admitted to the seasons of instruction and closed to all others. It is one of the smaller libraries of the inland valleys, for the path values the oral tradition above the written and has been slow to commit its teachings to the page.

The texts held by the library are few in number and are regarded as the foundation of the doctrine. They include the record of the [First Veilers](/content/religion/first-veilers)' teachings, the commentaries of the early senior veilers, and the small body of letters exchanged with the senior readers of the [Unwritten Name](/content/religion/unwritten-name). These letters are among the most valued texts in the library, recording the kinship between the two traditions and the courtesy they have observed toward one another.

## The Silent Reading and the Senior Veiler's Discussion

The reading of the texts is conducted in the library under the direction of the senior veilers, one of the principal disciplines of the path. The reading is conducted in silence, and the reader attends to the text without the interruption of commentary. The reader may mark passages found to be of consequence and must bring them to the senior veiler at the conclusion of the reading. The senior veiler then discusses the marked passages with the reader, the occasion for instruction and correction of misunderstanding. The reading is regarded as a complement to the long retreat, and the path holds that the reader who has not undertaken the retreat is not fitted to interpret the texts.

## The Wandering Veilers and the Hill Town Houses

The cloister is the home of the wanderers of the path, the wandering veilers who travel between the cloisters and the hill towns and maintain the discipline in the wider community. The wanderers are recognised by the small white staff they carry and by their custom of speaking softly and at length only when the company requires it. They are recruited from among the senior novices of the cloister, and the wandering office is regarded as a worthy complement to the long retreat. The wanderers return to the cloister at the close of their wanderings, and the return is the occasion for a brief silence in which their labours are observed without ceremony.

The cloister is also the home of the keepers of the quiet houses that the path maintains in the hill towns. The keepers are appointed by the senior veilers of the nearest cloister, and the office is regarded as a worthy complement to the long retreat. The quiet houses are humble establishments that lack the chamber and silence of the cloister but provide a few days of quiet and counsel to those who cannot leave their households for long. The quiet houses are the principal point of contact between the cloister and the wider community of the hill towns and the source of most of the novices admitted to the long retreat.

## The Annual Gathering of the Senior Veilers

The senior veilers of the several cloisters of the path maintain a conference held at the principal cloister once each year. The conference is conducted with great formality, and the order of speaking is observed without variation. The senior veilers report upon the state of the cloisters, the number of novices admitted to the long retreat, and the affairs of the quiet houses within their charge. The conference closes with a brief silence in which the veils are held in mind without being named, regarded by the senior veilers as the most important part of the conference. The conference is the only occasion upon which the senior veilers of the several cloisters meet and is the source of the unity of the path.

The conference is open to visitors of good faith, and the path welcomes those from other traditions who wish to attend. A senior reader of the Unwritten Name will sometimes attend the conference of senior veilers, and a senior veiler will sometimes attend the conference of readers, each welcomed by the other with the courtesy of kindred traditions. The conference is the measure of the path's openness to the wider world, a privilege rather than a claim.

## The Quiet Growth of the Path in the Hill Country

The Veil Cloister is the central establishment of the Sunless Veils, the seat of the long retreat, the schedules of study, the library of the veils, and the conference of the senior veilers. It is a quiet establishment in the inland valleys of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), supported by the labour of the community and the small offerings of the faithful of the hill towns. The cloister has been the source of the path's slow growth in the hill country, a quiet counterweight to the louder claims of the trading cities. The cloister asks of its practitioners that they attend to the difference between lifting the veils and breaking them, and it offers in return the long retreat by which the lifting is learned and the soul is wrapped, at last, in the corresponding stillness of the [Veiled One](/content/religion/veiled-one)'s own nature.
