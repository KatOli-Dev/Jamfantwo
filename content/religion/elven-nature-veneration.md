## The Spiritual Life of the Forest Peoples

The elven peoples of the known world practise a form of the spiritual life that is centred upon the veneration of the living world, the forests and the waters and the creatures of the woodland that the elves regard as the manifestations of the divine. The elven veneration is not a religion in the manner of the temple faiths of the human coastal cities, for the elves do not maintain the temples or the priests or the formal doctrines. The veneration is a way of the being in the world, a recognition of the sacredness of the natural order and a practice of the attention to the life that surrounds the elven community.

The elves of the ancient temperate forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are the oldest and the most numerous of the elven populations, and the practices that they maintain are the foundation of the elven spiritual life. The elves of the smaller populations in [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) have adapted the practices to the different conditions of the regions, but the core of the veneration remains the same across the elven communities.

## The Forest as the Temple

The forest is the temple of the elven veneration, and the elves do not build the separate structures for the worship. The grove of the old trees, the clearing where the light falls through the canopy, and the bank of the stream where the water runs clear are the places where the elven rites are conducted. The forest is the dwelling of the spirits that the elves honour, and the building of a permanent structure for the worship would be the separation from the spirits that the elves seek to approach.

The oldest trees of the forest are the most sacred of the elven places, for the old tree has witnessed the passage of many generations of the elves and the cycles of the forest life. The elves who gather at the old tree for the rite are gathering at a place that is already sacred, and the presence of the old tree is the presence of the living memory of the forest.

The elves maintain the forest with the care that the temple requires. The paths are kept clear, the invasive plants are removed, and the trees that are diseased or damaged are tended with the same attention that the human priest gives to the fabric of the temple. The elf who walks through the forest is walking through the sacred space, and the elf's conduct in the forest is the conduct of the worshipper in the temple.

## The Spirits of the Living World

The elves honour the spirits of the living world, the presences that they perceive in the trees and the waters and the creatures of the woodland. The spirit is not a deity in the manner of the Sea Mother, and the elves do not pray to the spirits in the manner that the sailors pray for the safe passage. The spirit is the presence that is perceived when the attention is turned to the life of the forest, the quality that the elf experiences in the stillness of the woodland.

The spirit of the tree is the presence that the elf experiences in the company of the old tree. The elf who sits at the base of the old tree and attends to the life of the tree perceives the spirit as a quality of the stillness, a presence that is distinct from the tree itself but inseparable from it.

The spirit of the water is the presence that the elf experiences at the stream or the spring. The water is the source of the life, and the elf who attends to the movement of the water perceives the spirit that animates the flow.

The spirit of the creature is the presence that the elf experiences in the encounter with the deer or the bird or the other inhabitants of the forest. The creature is not a symbol of the spirit but a manifestation of it, and the elf who meets the creature in the forest is meeting the spirit in the form that the spirit has taken.

## The Rites of the Forest

The principal rite of the elven veneration is the gathering at the grove, conducted at the times of the solstice and the equinox and at the times of the important events in the life of the community. The elves gather in the clearing at the centre of the grove, and the elders speak the words that have been transmitted through the generations. The words are not a prayer or a petition; they are the acknowledgment of the presence of the spirits and the renewal of the relationship between the elves and the forest.

The rite of the planting is conducted when a new grove is established or when a tree that has fallen is replaced. The elves gather at the place where the sapling is to be planted, and the elder who plants the sapling speaks the words of the blessing over the young tree. The rite is the acknowledgment that the life of the forest is the life of the elven community, and that the planting of the tree is the act of the faith.

The rite of the passing is conducted when an elf dies. The body of the elf is returned to the forest, and the elves gather to speak the name of the departed and to commit the life to the memory of the spirits. The rite is the acknowledgment that the elf who has died returns to the forest from which the elf came.

## The Elders and the Keepers of the Lore

The elven veneration does not have a priesthood in the manner of the human traditions, but the elders of the community and the keepers of the lore are the persons who are charged with the maintenance of the traditions. The elders are the elves who have lived the longest and who have the deepest knowledge of the forest and the spirits. The elders lead the gatherings at the grove and speak the words that the tradition has preserved.

The keepers of the lore are the elves who have learned the songs and the stories that the elven communities have preserved through the generations. The keepers transmit the lore to the younger elves, ensuring that the traditions are not lost.

## The Elven Veneration and the Other Traditions

The elven veneration is not a tradition that seeks the converts, and the elves do not maintain the correspondence with the human traditions in the manner that the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) maintains the correspondence with the [Unwritten Name](/content/religion/unwritten-name). The elves regard the veneration as the practice of the elven people, and the other peoples who come to live in the elven forests are not required to observe the elven rites.

The elves maintain a distant respect for the human traditions, regarding them as the practices of a people who are more removed from the natural world. The elves do not criticise the human traditions, but they do not see in them the understanding of the spirits that the elves have cultivated through the centuries of the life in the forest.
