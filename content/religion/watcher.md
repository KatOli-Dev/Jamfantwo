## The Divine Keeping of the World

The Watcher is the divine reality that the order of the [Shield and Song](/content/religion/shield-and-song) places at the centre of its understanding of the world. The Watcher is not a deity in the manner of the Sea Mother, who is approached through prayer and offering at the temple in [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar); the Watcher is a keeping, a holding of the world in attention and in care, and the order teaches that the Watcher's keeping is the foundation upon which the existence of the world and the lives of the peoples rest. The Watcher is revealed in two complementary aspects that correspond to the two halves of the order's calling: the shield-half, which is the bearing of hardship for the protection of others, and the song-half, which is the keeping of memory and the maintenance of the meaning of the community's life.

The theology of the Watcher is not elaborated into the kind of systematic doctrine that the academies of the southern cities produce. The [Shield and Song](/content/religion/shield-and-song) is an order of practice rather than of speculation, and its understanding of the Watcher is expressed in the discipline of the [Standing Watch](/content/government/local/standing-watch) and the keeping of the [Memory Houses](/content/religion/houses-of-memory) rather than in treatises and arguments. The Watcher is known through the work of the watch and the song, and the order holds that the practitioner who bears hardship well and who sings the memory of the community faithfully already knows the Watcher as fully as the practitioner needs to know.

## The Two Aspects of the Watcher's Nature

The Watcher is revealed in the shield-half as the one who bears the world. The Watcher holds the world in the same way that the watchman holds the line against the dangers of the frontier: without ceasing, without recognition, and without the relief of knowing that the holding makes any difference. The order teaches that the world continues to exist because the Watcher bears it, and that the hardship that the practitioner bears in the watch is a share in the Watcher's own bearing. The practitioner who stands the watch through the cold and the dark and the long silence is not merely performing a duty; the practitioner is participating in the sustaining of the world.

The Watcher is revealed in the song-half as the one who keeps the memory of the world. The Watcher remembers every life that has been lived, every song that has been sung, every name that has been spoken in the evening review. The order teaches that the memory of the world is the condition of the world's meaning, and that the singer who learns the songs and keeps the names is participating in the Watcher's own remembering. The practitioner who sings the evening review and names the day's sick and recovered, the day's fed and unfed, the day's spared and the day's fallen is not merely reciting a list; the practitioner is keeping the world in the Watcher's attention.

## The Relation Between the Watcher and the Practitioner

The Shield and Song teaches that the relation between the Watcher and the practitioner is not a relation of worship in the sense that the temple traditions understand worship. The practitioner does not pray to the Watcher for favours or protection, and the Watcher does not intervene in the world in response to the practitioner's petitions. The relation is one of participation: the practitioner shares in the Watcher's keeping of the world by bearing hardship and by keeping memory, and the Watcher's keeping of the world is the condition that makes the practitioner's bearing and singing meaningful.

The order teaches that the Watcher does not need the practitioner's service, for the Watcher keeps the world without the practitioner's assistance. The practitioner's service is not a contribution to the Watcher's work but a share in it, and the practitioner who undertakes the calling does so not because the Watcher needs the help but because the sharing of the work is the purpose for which the practitioner exists. The bearing and the singing are not means to an end; they are the end itself, and the practitioner who bears and sings well is already living the life that the Watcher intends.

## The Watcher and the Other Traditions

The Shield and Song does not claim that the Watcher is the only divine reality, and the order maintains correspondence with the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) and the [Unwritten Name](/content/religion/unwritten-name) without asserting that the Watcher is the same reality that the Still Flame honours in the hearth-fire or that the Unwritten Name approaches through the discipline of nouns. The order regards the other traditions of the continent as kindred paths that approach the same keeping from different directions, and the correspondence between the traditions is the means by which the order preserves its openness to the wider world.

The Still Flame, whose teaching of the single light echoes the order's teaching about the Watcher's keeping, has been especially close to the Shield and Song, and the two traditions have exchanged practitioners for many generations. The Unwritten Name, whose discipline of nouns teaches a kindred humility before the mystery of the divine, has also maintained correspondence with the order, and the three traditions together form a network of kindred paths that have enriched one another without diminishing the distinctiveness of each.

## The Watcher in the Life of the Order

The Watcher is not named in the daily observances of the order, and the evening review does not include prayers or invocations addressed to the Watcher by name. The Watcher is acknowledged in the silence that follows the singing of the account, and the order teaches that the silence is the most appropriate form of acknowledgment, for the Watcher is not a being to be addressed in the manner of the temple deities but a keeping to be participated in through the work of the watch and the song.

The Watcher is the reality that gives the order's calling its meaning, and the practitioner who undertakes the calling without understanding the Watcher is not yet fully a practitioner of the Shield and Song. The understanding of the Watcher is not acquired through study or through the reading of texts; it is acquired through the practice of the calling itself, and the novice who stands the watch and learns the songs comes to understand the Watcher in the same way that the apprentice comes to understand the craft: through the work, through the discipline, and through the company of those who have undertaken the same calling before.

## The Watcher's Keeping of the World

The Watcher keeps the world from the beginning to the end, and the order teaches that the keeping is the condition of the world's existence. The Watcher does not create the world in the manner that the [creation myths](/content/mythology/creation-myths-overview) of the southern temples describe; the Watcher holds the world in attention, and the holding is itself the world's existence. The order does not speculate about the beginning or the end of the Watcher's keeping, and the practitioner is taught that the question of the beginning and the end is one of the questions that the calling does not require the practitioner to answer.

The practitioner of the Shield and Song is called to share in the Watcher's keeping, and the share is the bearing and the singing. The bearing of hardship for the protection of others and the singing of the memory of the community are the means by which the practitioner participates in the sustaining of the world, and the order teaches that the practitioner who bears and sings faithfully is already doing all that the Watcher requires.
