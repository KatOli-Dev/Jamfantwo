## The Tenders of the Perpetual Fire

The hearth-keepers of the Still Flame are the men and women who are appointed to tend the central fire of the hearth-halls and to instruct the young in the doctrine of the faith. The hearth-keepers are the principal officers of the Still Flame, and their service is the foundation upon which the life of the faith is built. The hearth-keeper is not a priest in the manner of the temple faiths, for the Still Flame does not maintain a hierarchical priesthood or a formal doctrine of the ordination. The hearth-keeper is a member of the community who has been recognised by the elders for the devotion and the skill in the tending of the fire and the instruction of the young.

The hearth-keepers are drawn from the communities that the hearth-halls serve, and they are appointed by the elders of the community after a period of training and probation. The keepers are expected to labour with their hands as do other folk, and the faith counsels them against the temptation to seek governance or influence beyond the tending of the fire and the instruction of the young. The keepers are the principal point of contact between the hearth-hall and the wider community, and their reputation for hospitality is the measure of the hall's standing in the surrounding country.

## The Training of the Hearth-Keeper

The training of the hearth-keeper is conducted by the senior hearth-keepers of the region, and the training includes the instruction in the doctrine of the Threefold Flame, the care of the central fire, the conduct of the rites of the flame, and the duty of the hospitality. The novice hearth-keeper serves in the hearth-hall for a period of some years, learning the practice of the faith under the direction of a senior keeper.

The training is not a formal curriculum of the kind that the academies maintain, for the Still Flame values the practical knowledge above the theoretical. The novice learns by the doing, attending to the fire and the hall and the people who come to it, and the senior keeper guides the novice through the experience without imposing a rigid interpretation upon it.

The novice who has completed the training is presented to the community at the seasonal gathering, and the elders of the community confirm the appointment. The new hearth-keeper is given the charge of the hearth-hall and the responsibility for the tending of the fire and the instruction of the young.

## The Daily Duties of the Keeper

The daily duties of the hearth-keeper begin before the dawn, with the kindling of the fire from the embers of the previous day. The fire is the heart of the hearth-hall, and the keeper tends it through the day, feeding the wood at the proper intervals and banking it at the proper times. The keeper who allows the fire to die is dismissed from the service, for the fire is the sign of the faith and the tending of the fire is the first duty of the keeper.

The instruction of the young is the second of the keeper's duties. The keeper teaches the children of the community the doctrine of the Threefold Flame, the practice of the morning kindling and the evening banking, and the songs and the prayers that the faith has preserved. The instruction is conducted in the hearth-hall, and the children gather around the fire to hear the teaching that the keeper has prepared.

The hospitality of the hall is the third of the keeper's duties. The keeper must extend the warmth of the fire and the shelter of the hall to all who seek it, regardless of the creed or the origin of the person who comes. The keeper maintains the store of the fuel and the food that the hall provides against the winter, and the keeper must ensure that the store is sufficient for the needs of the travellers who may come.

## The Hearth-Keeper and the Community

The hearth-keeper is a member of the community that the hall serves, and the keeper's life is the life of the community. The keeper does not withdraw from the world in the manner of the veilers of the Sunless Veils, and the keeper does not devote the whole of the time to the service of the hall. The keeper works in the fields or the workshop as do the other members of the community, and the keeper's labour is the source of the keeper's subsistence.

The relationship between the keeper and the community is a relationship of the mutual obligation. The community provides the hall and the fuel and the offerings that support the keeper's service, and the keeper provides the tending of the fire and the instruction of the young and the hospitality that the hall extends. The balance of the obligation is the foundation of the trust that the community places in the keeper.

## The Senior Hearth-Keeper

The senior hearth-keeper is the keeper who has served for the longest time and who is recognised by the other keepers as the leader of the local community of the faith. The senior keeper presides at the seasonal gatherings, conducts the principal rites of the flame, and represents the faith in the relations with the other traditions.

The senior keeper does not hold the authority over the other keepers in the manner of a bishop or a superior. The senior keeper is the first among the equals, and the other keepers seek the counsel of the senior keeper without being bound by it. The discipline of the faith is maintained by the mutual respect of the keepers and by the traditions that have been transmitted through the generations.

## The Hearth-Keepers and the Kindred Traditions

The hearth-keepers maintain the correspondence with the kindred traditions of the continent, and the exchange of the keepers between the traditions is a practice that has continued for many generations. A hearth-keeper of the Still Flame will sometimes attend the evening review of a Memory House of the Shield and Song, and a singer of the Shield and Song will sometimes attend the morning kindling of a hearth-hall.

The keepers also maintain the correspondence with the Forged Flame, whose hearth-keepers teach a discipline of the workshop that the Still Flame regards as a worthy complement to the tending of the hearth. The exchange has enriched both traditions, and the keepers of the Still Flame have learned from the keepers of the Forged Flame the skills of the working of the metal and the firing of the clay that have been applied to the making of the hearth tools and the vessels.

## The Vocation of the Keeper

The vocation of the hearth-keeper is a calling that the keeper undertakes for the life. The keeper who has accepted the charge of the hearth-hall serves the community for the remainder of the days, and the keeper who has served the hall for the long years is honoured at the death by the gathering of the community around the fire that the keeper has tended.

The faith teaches that the keeper who tends the fire with the devotion and the skill is already a full participant in the work of the faith. The keeper does not need to seek the higher knowledge or the deeper understanding, for the tending of the fire is itself the understanding. The keeper who has served the hall for a lifetime has learned the teaching of the Threefold Flame more fully than the scholar who has read all the texts that the faith has produced.
