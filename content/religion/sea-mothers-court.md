## The Great Temple of the Warm Coasts

The Sea Mother's Court is the principal temple of the Sea Mother, a maritime deity venerated along the warm coasts of the known world, and it is among the most significant religious structures in the city of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar). The Court is not a single building but a complex of halls, courtyards, and harbour-side chapels that extends along the waterfront of the old city, and its precincts include the great hall where the principal rites are conducted, the lesser halls where the priests maintain the schedule of offerings and prayers, and the harbour-side chapels where the sailors and the fishermen make their petitions before putting out to sea. The Sea Mother's Court is the most visible institution of the maritime faith in the southern cities, and its influence extends along the trade routes that connect the warm coasts to the ports of the wider world.

The Sea Mother is among the older deities of the warm coastal regions, and her worship is found in every port city of the southern coasts and the islands of the warm seas. The deity is conceived as a maternal figure who holds the sea in her keeping, and the faithful pray to her for safe passage, abundant catches, and protection from the storms that rise without warning from the southern waters. The Sea Mother is not a gentle deity in the manner that the hearth-side traditions imagine maternal figures; the sea that she keeps is indifferent to the lives of those who sail upon it, and the worship of the Sea Mother acknowledges this indifference while seeking the deity's favour through the observance of the proper rites and the making of the proper offerings.

## The Complex of the Court

The Sea Mother's Court occupies a substantial portion of the Khalisaran waterfront, and its buildings are constructed of the pale stone that is quarried from the hills above the city. The great hall is the largest covered space in Khalisar, and its roof is supported by a series of stone pillars carved with the forms of sea creatures and sailing vessels. The hall is open to the seaward side, and the altar stands at the centre of a circular opening in the roof through which the light of the sun and the moon and the rain of the storms fall upon the stone. The altar is a single block of dark stone brought from the sea floor, and it is kept wet with seawater that is carried up from the harbour each morning by the novices of the temple.

The lesser halls surround the great hall on three sides, and they house the priests, the stores of offerings, the libraries of the temple's records, and the chambers in which the priests instruct the novices in the rites and the lore of the Sea Mother. The harbour-side chapels are small structures built at the edge of the water, each dedicated to a particular aspect of the Sea Mother's care: the chapel of the Safe Passage, the chapel of the Abundant Catch, the chapel of the Calm Water, and the chapel of the Returning Sailor. The chapels are tended by the junior priests, and they are the principal point of contact between the temple and the sailors and fishermen who make up the greater part of the Sea Mother's following.

## The Rites of the Sea Mother

The principal rite of the Sea Mother's Court is the offering of the first catch, conducted at the beginning of the fishing season and repeated at the turning of the seasons throughout the year. The priests carry a portion of the first catch of the season to the altar in the great hall and present it to the Sea Mother with prayers for the safety of those who go down to the sea in ships. The rite is attended by the masters of the fishing fleets and the captains of the merchant vessels, and the offerings are distributed to the poor of the city after the conclusion of the prayers.

The second of the principal rites is the lighting of the harbour lamps, conducted each evening at the close of the day. The priests light the great lamps that stand at the entrance to the harbour and recite the prayers for the safe return of those who are still at sea. The lamps are visible from the open water for a considerable distance, and the sailors who see them from their vessels know that the Sea Mother's Court is keeping the watch for their return. The lighting of the lamps is the most visible of the temple's daily observances, and the people of Khalisar regard the lamps as a sign of the city's care for those who sail from its harbour.

## The Place of the Court in Khalisar

The Sea Mother's Court is among the most important institutions of Khalisar, and its influence extends beyond the religious into the political and commercial life of the city. The high priest of the Court is a member of the city council, and the temple's resources are employed in the maintenance of the harbour and the funding of the pilots who guide the ships through the dangerous approaches to the port. The Court has been the site of important political ceremonies, including the signing of the [Twin-Reaches Peace](/content/history/twin-reaches-peace), the treaty that ended the long rivalry between Khalisar and the northern trading cities and established the terms of the trade that has enriched the southern coast for generations.

The Court also maintains the records of the ships that sail from Khalisar, and the temple's library holds the logs of the voyages that have been undertaken from the port for centuries. The records include the account of [Kaelen's Voyage](/content/history/kaelens-voyage), the expedition that first charted the southern islands and established the trade routes that connect Khalisar to the ports of the warm seas. The library is open to scholars and to the captains of the merchant vessels, and the priests regard the keeping of the records as a sacred duty that complements the more visible work of the rites and the offerings.

## The Priests of the Sea Mother

The priests of the Sea Mother's Court are drawn from the families of Khalisar and the surrounding coastal towns, and they undergo a training of some years before they are admitted to the service of the temple. The training includes instruction in the rites and the lore of the Sea Mother, the navigation of the coastal waters, the reading of the weather, and the care of the harbour lamps and the altar. The priests are expected to know the sea as well as they know the prayers, and the temple teaches that the priest who cannot read the weather is as useless as the priest who cannot recite the prayers.

The high priest of the Court is the senior priest of the Sea Mother in Khalisar, and the office is held for life. The high priest is chosen by the council of the senior priests from among their number, and the choice is confirmed by the city council of Khalisar. The high priest presides over the principal rites and represents the Court in the political and commercial affairs of the city, and the office carries considerable influence in the life of the port.

## The Sea Mother Beyond Khalisar

The Sea Mother is venerated beyond Khalisar, and her temples are found in every port city of the southern coasts and the warm islands. The Court in Khalisar is the largest and most influential of the Sea Mother's temples, but it is not the only temple, and the worship of the Sea Mother is maintained by independent priestly communities in each port. The temples maintain correspondence with one another and exchange news of the sea and the trade routes, and the network of the Sea Mother's temples is one of the means by which the news of the southern waters is carried from port to port.

The Sea Mother is not a deity of the inland regions, and her worship does not extend far from the coast. The faithful of the Still Flame and the Shield and Song regard the Sea Mother with the respect that one tradition owes to another, but the maritime faith is too closely bound to the conditions of the coast to take root in the river valleys and the hill country. The Sea Mother's Court remains a temple of the port, and its influence is measured in the safety of the voyages that begin and end in the harbour of Khalisar.
