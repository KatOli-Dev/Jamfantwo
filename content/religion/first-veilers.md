## The Hermits Who Founded the Path

The First Veilers are the founders of the path of the [Sunless Veils](/content/religion/sunless-veils), the hermits who withdrew from the older market towns of the central highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and took up residence in the quiet valleys where the sun's rays reach only briefly in the winter months. The First Veilers are remembered only by the title that the path has preserved, and no personal names have survived from the founding generation. The path is content that the identity of the founders should remain uncertain, for the teaching is more important than the teacher, and the discipline is more important than the name that the teacher bore.

The First Veilers emerged from a period in which the market towns of the central highlands were growing in wealth and influence, and the courts of the early Aethelaran kingdoms were establishing the patterns of governance that would shape the continent for centuries. The hermits who withdrew from the towns did so not because they despised the world of commerce and governance but because they recognised that the bright surfaces and the loud voices of the towns were a kind of veil that obscured the deeper reality from which the world proceeds. The First Veilers taught that the world of the towns and the courts was not false, and the path does not teach that the ordinary world is to be despised or abandoned; the First Veilers taught that the world was veiled, and that the veils could be lifted through the discipline of withdrawal and silence.

## The Withdrawal from the Market Towns

The First Veilers withdrew from the market towns of the central highlands in the generations before the first great human kingdoms arose, and the precise circumstances of the withdrawal are not recorded. The oral tradition of the path preserves the memory of a company of seekers who left the towns one by one or in small groups, each moved by the same recognition that the noise and the brightness of the towns concealed something that could not be found in the markets or the courts. The First Veilers gathered in the quiet valleys where the sunlight was brief and the silence was deep, and they established the first cloisters in the chambers cut into the sides of the hills.

The withdrawal was not a rejection of the towns or the people who lived in them, and the path teaches that the First Veilers maintained a relation of courtesy with the communities they had left. The towns provided the First Veilers with provisions and with the small offerings that supported the first cloisters, and the First Veilers provided the towns with counsel and with the quiet presence of the retreat. The mutual obligation that the path maintains with the hill towns of the present day is traced to the relations that the First Veilers established with the communities of the valleys.

## The Development of the Discipline

The First Veilers developed the discipline of the long retreat that is the central observance of the Sunless Veils. The retreat was not invented by a single figure or established at a single moment; it emerged from the practice of the first hermits who withdrew into the curtained chambers and remained there for days and then weeks and then months, attending to the stillness that the withdrawal made possible. The First Veilers discovered that the withdrawal from the bright world and the loud voices was the means by which the veils could be lifted, and they refined the practice over the generations of the founding period.

The path teaches that the First Veilers did not write down their teachings extensively, and the oral tradition of the path is valued above the written. The First Veilers taught through the practice of the retreat and through the example of their lives, and the senior veilers of the present day transmit the discipline by guiding the novices through the same retreat that the First Veilers developed. The small body of writings that the path preserves includes the record of the First Veilers' teachings, but the path regards the teachings as pointing toward the practice and not as a substitute for it.

## The Teachings of the First Veilers

The First Veilers taught that the world of bright surfaces and loud voices is a kind of veil that the soul must learn to lift. The veils are not evil, and the First Veilers did not teach that the ordinary world is to be despised or abandoned. The veils are the necessary conditions of ordinary life, and the First Veilers recognised that the ordinary world is the world in which the soul must live and work and maintain the relations of the household and the community. The veils are to be lifted, one by one, through the discipline of the retreat, and the practitioner who lifts the veils without breaking them comes to see the world with a quieter and more accurate vision.

The First Veilers taught the distinction between lifting the veils and breaking them, and this distinction is the heart of the path's discipline. A veil that is lifted can be lowered again, and the practitioner who lifts a veil gains the ability to see what lies beyond it without losing the ability to function in the ordinary world. A veil that is broken cannot be restored, and the practitioner who breaks a veil suffers a loss of the ordinary capacities that the veil makes possible. The First Veilers taught that the breaking of the veils is the great danger of the path, and they developed the discipline of the long retreat as the means by which the practitioner learns to lift the veils without breaking them.

## The First Cloisters

The First Veilers established the first cloisters in the chambers cut into the sides of the hills of the inland valleys. The first cloister was a single chamber curtained against the day, and the First Veilers used it for the long retreat and for the instruction of the novices who joined the community. The chamber was furnished with a low couch, a small table, a basin of water, and a single lamp, and the First Veilers maintained the discipline of the retreat in the same form that the path preserves in the present day.

The community of the First Veilers grew slowly, and the successors to the founding generation added a second chamber and then a third as the number of novices increased. The cloisters that the First Veilers established are the models upon which the Veil Cloister and the other cloisters of the path are built, and the discipline that the First Veilers developed is the discipline that the path observes without variation in the present day.

## The Legacy of the First Veilers

The First Veilers are remembered by the path as the founders of the discipline and the source of the teaching. The path does not preserve the names of the First Veilers, and the senior veilers teach the novices that the anonymity of the founders is a fitting first lesson in the discipline of the veils. The names of the founders are among the veils that the path has learned to lift, and the practitioner who insists on knowing the names before undertaking the discipline has not yet understood the first teaching of the path.

The legacy of the First Veilers is preserved in the practice of the long retreat, in the discipline of lifting the veils without breaking them, and in the stillness that the practitioner enters at the depth of the retreat. The [Veil Cloister](/content/religion/veil-cloister) and the other cloisters of the path maintain the discipline that the First Veilers established, and the wandering veilers who travel between the cloisters and the hill towns carry the teaching that the First Veilers first articulated in the quiet valleys of the central highlands.

## The First Veilers and the Kindred Traditions

The First Veilers maintained correspondence with the kindred traditions of the continent, and the letters exchanged with the early readers of the Unwritten Name are preserved in the library of the Veil Cloister. The First Veilers recognised the Unwritten Name as a kindred path that approached the mystery of the divine through the discipline of language, and the two traditions have maintained the correspondence through the centuries. The First Veilers also knew of the Still Flame, whose teaching of the single light echoed the path's own teaching about the stillness that lies beyond the veils, and the three traditions have exchanged practitioners for many generations. The correspondence is the source of the path's openness to the wider world, and the First Veilers set the example of the courtesy that the path maintains toward the kindred traditions of the continent.
