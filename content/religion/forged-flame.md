## The Fire of the Workshop

The Forged Flame is the name given to the tradition of workshop devotion practised by smiths and craftspeople in several regions of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The tradition is not a single organised faith but a family of related practices centred upon the understanding that the act of making is a form of devotion and that the fire of the forge is a sacred presence. The Forged Flame is the fire the smith kindles in the forge, the heat that transforms the metal, and the light that guides the hands of the maker.

The tradition takes its name from the belief that the first fire kindled in [the first forge](/content/mythology/the-first-forge) was a gift from the divine, a flame that was not merely fire but the presence of the maker's spirit made manifest in the world. Every fire kindled in a forge thereafter is a continuation of that first forging, and every object shaped upon the anvil is a participation in the work that began at the first making.

## The Forger as Divine Smith

The central figure of the Forged Flame tradition is the Forger, the divine smith who kindled the first fire and shaped the first object from the raw material of the world. The Forger is not a god in the manner of the temple deities; the Forger does not dwell in a realm apart from the world but is present in every workshop and forge where making is done. The Forger is the pattern of the maker, the hands that guide the hands of every smith and craftsperson who works with attention and skill.

The Forger is described in the tradition as one who labours without ceasing at the forge of the world, shaping the raw material of existence into forms of beauty and use. The Forger does not command or judge but works, and the work of the Forger is the model for the work of every maker. The smith who strikes hot metal upon the anvil is doing what the Forger does, and the object that emerges from the forging is a continuation of the Forger's labour.

## The Practices of the Tradition

The practices of the Forged Flame tradition vary among the workshops and communities that observe it, but certain practices are common to all who follow the way of the Forger. The kindling of the forge fire is the principal ritual of the tradition, and the smith who kindles the fire speaks the words of the kindling, acknowledging the presence of the Forger in the fire and asking for the guidance of the hands.

The words of the kindling are spoken at the beginning of the working day, when the forge is lit and the metal placed in the fire. The smith speaks the words of the opening, addressing the Forger as the companion of the work and asking that the fire burn true and that the metal yield to the shaping. The words are not fixed but are composed by each smith in a manner that reflects the smith's own relationship with the Forger.

The words of the closing are spoken at the end of the working day, when the forge is banked and the tools put away. The smith thanks the Forger for the guidance of the hands and for the work accomplished, and asks that the fire be preserved until morning. The tools of the workshop are cleaned and arranged in the order the smith maintains, and the workshop is left in readiness for the work of the following day.

## The Discipline of Attention

The discipline of attention is the central practice of the Forged Flame tradition, as it is in the [gnomish craft devotion](/content/religion/gnomish-craft-devotion) with which the Forged Flame has maintained a correspondence. The smith who works at the forge must attend to the work with full attention, for metal struck without attention is spoiled, and an object shaped without care fails in its purpose.

The discipline extends beyond the moment of striking to every stage of the making: the selection of the ore, the preparation of the fuel, the building of the fire, the heating of the metal to the precise temperature that the work requires, and the cooling at the proper rate. Each stage demands a different quality of attention, and the smith who masters all of them has mastered the discipline as thoroughly as any scholar has mastered a text.

The attention the smith brings to the work is the same attention the smith brings to the presence of the Forger. The smith absorbed in the work does not think of other things, and absorption in the work is the form of meditation the Forged Flame tradition teaches. The smith who works with attention works in the presence of the Forger, and the object made with attention carries the mark of the Forger.

## The Correspondence with Other Traditions

The Forged Flame has maintained a correspondence with the [gnomish craft devotion](/content/religion/gnomish-craft-devotion), recognising in the gnomish tradition the same understanding that the work of the hands is a form of devotion and that an object well made honours the divine. Smiths of the Forged Flame have exchanged visits with gnomish workshops, and the two traditions have learned from each other the techniques of the craft and the disciplines of attention.

The Forged Flame has also been recognised by the [Memory Houses](/content/religion/houses-of-memory) of the [Shield and Song](/content/religion/shield-and-song), whose singers have attended the kindling ceremonies of the Forged Flame and carried reports of those ceremonies back to their houses. The houses of memory regard the Forged Flame as a kindred tradition, sharing the understanding that the discipline of attention is a form of devotion.

The [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) has also acknowledged the Forged Flame as a kindred tradition, recognising in the forge fire a reflection of the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) that burns in the hearth-halls. The hearth-keepers of the Still Flame have exchanged courtesies with the smiths of the Forged Flame, and the two traditions have maintained a respectful correspondence.
