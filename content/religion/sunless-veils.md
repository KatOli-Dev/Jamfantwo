## The Path of the Lifted Veils

The Sunless Veils are a contemplative order practising the discipline of lifting the veils that the world places between the soul and the reality that lies beyond the bright surfaces of things. The order maintains the [Veil Cloister](/content/religion/veil-cloister) as its central establishment, and its practitioners are found in the inland valleys and the hill country of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the light of the sun reaches only briefly in the winter months and where the long retreat can be conducted in the dim quiet that the path requires. The Sunless Veils are neither a religion in the manner of the Still Flame nor a philosophical doctrine of the kind that the Unwritten Name cultivates; they are a path of withdrawal, a discipline of silence, and a practice of attending to the difference between lifting the veils and breaking them.

The order teaches that the world of bright surfaces and loud voices is a kind of veil that obscures the deeper reality from which the world proceeds. The veils are not evil and are not to be destroyed; they are the necessary conditions of ordinary life, and the path does not teach that the ordinary world is to be despised or abandoned. The veils are to be lifted, one by one, through the discipline of the long retreat and the practice of silence, and the practitioner who lifts the veils without breaking them comes to see the world with a quieter and more accurate vision.

## The Long Retreat

The central observance of the Sunless Veils is the long retreat, a discipline of withdrawal undertaken at the [Veil Cloister](/content/religion/veil-cloister) for a season of weeks or months. The retreat is conducted in silence and in the dim light of a small chamber curtained against the day's brightness, and the practitioner is permitted the company only of a senior veiler who attends to the discipline without conversation. The chamber is furnished with a low couch, a small table, a basin of water, and a single lamp kept burning through the hours of the retreat. The curtains are drawn at the beginning of the retreat and are not opened until the close, and the practitioner remains within the chamber for the duration of the season.

The long retreat is not a punishment, and the path is careful to mark the difference. It is the chief instrument by which the veils are lifted in their order, and the elders regard it as the most demanding and rewarding of the path's disciplines. The practitioner who undertakes the retreat comes forth quieter than the practitioner who entered, and this change is regarded by the elders as the measure of the retreat's success. The retreat teaches the practitioner to distinguish between the veils that are necessary for ordinary life and the veils that can be lifted, and the discipline of the retreat is the means by which the lifting is learned.

## The Difference Between Lifting and Breaking

The Sunless Veils teach that there is a crucial difference between lifting a veil and breaking it, and the distinction is the heart of the order's discipline. A veil that is lifted can be lowered again, and the practitioner who lifts a veil gains the ability to see what lies beyond it without losing the ability to function in the ordinary world. A veil that is broken cannot be restored, and the practitioner who breaks a veil suffers a loss of the ordinary capacities that the veil makes possible.

The path teaches that the veils are not to be torn aside in the impatience of the seeker who wants to see the hidden reality at once and without preparation. The veils are to be lifted slowly, with patience and attention, and the practitioner is to allow each veil to be lowered again before attempting to lift the next. The long retreat is the instrument by which the patience is cultivated and the attention is refined, and the practitioner who completes the retreat comes to understand the difference between lifting and breaking with the same clarity that a craftsman understands the difference between cutting and tearing.

## The Ultimate Goal: The Stillness of the Veiled One

The ultimate goal of the Sunless Veils is to be wrapped, at last, in the corresponding stillness of the [Veiled One's](/content/religion/veiled-one) own nature. The Veiled One is the divine reality toward which the path aspires, the stillness that lies beyond the last veil that can be lifted. The path does not teach that the Veiled One can be known in the manner that ordinary things are known, for the Veiled One is not an object of knowledge in the ordinary sense. The Veiled One is approached through the stillness that the long retreat cultivates, and the practitioner who has lifted all the veils that can be lifted comes to rest in that stillness without claiming to have understood what the stillness is.

The path teaches that the stillness of the Veiled One is not a void or an absence. It is a presence that cannot be described in the language of the bright world, and the practitioner who approaches it learns to be content with the approach and to abandon the expectation of arrival. The long retreat is the means by which the practitioner learns to rest in the approach without demanding the arrival, and the discipline of the path is the cultivation of this resting.

## The Heritage of the First Veilers

The Sunless Veils trace their foundation to the [First Veilers](/content/religion/first-veilers), the hermits who withdrew from the older market towns of the central highlands and took up residence in the quiet valleys where the sun's rays reach only briefly in the winter months. The First Veilers taught that the world of bright surfaces and loud voices was a kind of veil, and they developed the discipline of the long retreat as the means by which the veils could be lifted. Their teachings form the foundation of the path, and the order has preserved their instructions in the small library of the Veil Cloister and in the oral tradition that the senior veilers maintain.

The First Veilers did not leave extensive writings, and the path values the oral tradition above the written. The teachings that have been preserved are regarded as the foundation of the doctrine, and the senior veilers are charged with transmitting them faithfully to the novices who enter the long retreat. The path is content that the identity of the First Veilers should remain uncertain, for the teaching is more important than the teacher, and the discipline is more important than the name.

## The Schedule of Withdrawal and Instruction

The Sunless Veils maintain at the cloister a schedule of study observed throughout the year, divided into seasons of withdrawal and seasons of instruction. The seasons of withdrawal are set apart for the long retreat, and the seasons of instruction are set apart for reading the older texts and discussing the doctrine of the veils. The schedule is observed without exception, and the alternation of withdrawal and instruction is regarded as essential to the discipline, for the path teaches that the retreat without instruction is empty and the instruction without retreat is shallow.

The seasons of instruction are conducted by the senior veilers, who lead the novices through the readings the path has preserved. The readings are drawn from the older writings of the path and the commentaries added by senior veilers over the generations, and they are conducted in the meeting-hall of the cloister under the direction of the senior veiler. The instruction is not a lecture but a guided reading, and the novices are expected to bring their own attention to the texts and to mark the passages that strike them as consequential.

## The Wandering Veilers and the Quiet Houses

The path maintains a network of wandering veilers who travel between the cloisters and the hill towns, carrying the discipline of the path to the communities that cannot support a full cloister. The wandering veilers are recognised by the small white staff they carry and by their custom of speaking softly and at length only when the company requires it. They are recruited from among the senior novices, and the wandering office is regarded as a worthy complement to the long retreat.

The quiet houses are humble establishments that the path maintains in the hill towns, lacking the chamber and the silence of the cloister but providing a few days of quiet and counsel to those who cannot leave their households for long. The quiet houses are the principal point of contact between the cloister and the wider community, and they are the source of most of the novices who are admitted to the long retreat. The path has grown slowly through the hill country, and the quiet houses have been the means by which the discipline of the veils has reached the communities of the valleys and the towns.
