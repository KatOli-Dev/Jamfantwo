## The Domestic Spiritual Practices of the Lowland Valleys

The halfling communities of the lowland valleys maintain a body of the domestic spiritual practices that are centred upon the household and the garden and the table. The halfling hearth traditions are not a religion in the manner of the temple faiths, and the halflings do not maintain the priests or the formal doctrines or the sacred texts. The hearth traditions are the practices of the daily life, the rituals of the preparation of the food and the tending of the fire and the gathering of the family that are woven into the fabric of the halfling existence.

The halflings are a people of the home, and the hearth is the centre of the halfling household. The hearth is the source of the warmth and the light, the place where the food is cooked and the family gathers in the evening. The halfling hearth is the symbol of the domestic life that the halflings value above all else, and the practices that the halflings observe around the hearth are the expressions of the reverence for the home and the family.

## The Spirits of the Home

The halflings believe that the home is inhabited by the spirits that watch over the household and the family. The spirit of the hearth is the most important of the domestic spirits, and the halflings honour the hearth spirit through the daily tending of the fire and the offering of the first portion of the meal. The hearth spirit is not a deity in the manner of the Sea Mother, and the halflings do not pray to the hearth spirit for the favours or the protection. The hearth spirit is a presence that is honoured through the attention that the halfling gives to the home.

The spirit of the garden is the second of the domestic spirits, and the halflings honour the garden spirit through the planting and the tending of the garden. The garden spirit is the presence that ensures the fertility of the soil and the abundance of the harvest, and the halfling who works in the garden is engaged in a relationship with the garden spirit.

The spirit of the threshold is the third of the domestic spirits, and the halflings honour the threshold spirit through the greeting and the farewell that are spoken at the door. The threshold spirit is the guardian of the boundary between the home and the world, and the halfling who crosses the threshold with the proper words is acknowledging the spirit's presence.

## The Daily Practices

The principal daily practice of the halfling hearth traditions is the morning kindling and the evening banking of the fire. The halfling who kindles the fire in the morning speaks the words of the greeting to the hearth spirit, and the halfling who banks the fire in the evening speaks the words of the thanks for the warmth that the fire has provided.

The preparation of the meal is the second of the daily practices. The halfling who prepares the food does so with the attention and the care, and the offering of the first portion is made before the meal is served. The offering is a small portion of the food that is placed at the edge of the hearth, and the halfling who makes the offering speaks the words of the thanks to the spirits of the home.

The gathering of the family at the table is the third of the daily practices. The halfling family gathers at the table for the meal, and the meal is the occasion for the sharing of the news and the stories of the day. The halflings regard the gathering at the table as the most important of the daily observances, and the meal that is eaten alone is a meal that is incomplete.

## The Seasonal Festivals

The halfling hearth traditions include the seasonal festivals that are observed by the entire community, conducted at the times of the planting and the harvest and the turning of the year. The festival of the planting is conducted in the spring, when the gardens are prepared for the new season. The halflings gather in the fields and the gardens, and the elders speak the words of the blessing over the seeds and the soil.

The festival of the harvest is conducted in the autumn, when the produce of the gardens has been gathered. The halflings come together for the feast that is prepared from the bounty of the season, and the spirits of the home and the garden are thanked for the abundance that they have provided.

The festival of the hearth is conducted at the winter solstice, when the nights are the longest and the warmth of the hearth is most needed. The halflings gather at the central hearth of the community, and the fire is kept burning through the longest night. The festival is the occasion for the telling of the stories and the sharing of the food and the renewal of the bonds of the community.

## The Rites of the Passage

The halfling hearth traditions include the rites that mark the important passages of the life. The rite of the naming is conducted when the child is born, and the child is presented to the hearth and to the community. The elders speak the name of the child, and the hearth spirit is asked to watch over the child's life.

The rite of the joining is conducted when the couple is married, and the marriage is solemnised before the hearth of the couple's new home. The couple kindles the fire together, and the elders speak the words of the blessing over the union.

The rite of the passing is conducted when the halfling dies, and the body of the halfling is given to the earth of the garden from which the halfling came. The family gathers at the hearth, and the elders speak the words of the farewell to the departed.

## The Halfling Traditions and the Human Faiths

The halfling hearth traditions are not a faith that seeks the converts, and the halflings do not maintain the correspondence with the human traditions. The halflings regard the hearth traditions as the practices of the halfling people, and the human faiths are the practices of the human peoples.

The halflings live in the close proximity to the human communities, and the halflings have learned to respect the human traditions without adopting them. The halfling who attends the human temple for the marriage or the funeral does so with the respect for the human practices, but the halfling returns to the hearth at the home to observe the halfling rites.
