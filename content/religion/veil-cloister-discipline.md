## The Rules and the Practices of the Central Establishment

The Veil Cloister is the central establishment of the Sunless Veils, and the discipline that is observed at the cloister is the model upon which the disciplines of the other cloisters are based. The cloister is found in the inland valleys of Aethelar, where the sun's rays reach only briefly in the winter months, and the building is a humble structure of the stone and the timber, curtained against the brightness of the day. The discipline of the cloister is designed to cultivate the stillness that the path requires and to prepare the practitioner for the approach to the Veiled One.

The discipline of the cloister is observed without the variation, and the senior veilers are the guardians of the discipline. The novice who enters the cloister submits to the discipline for the duration of the stay, and the novice who cannot observe the discipline is asked to leave.

## The Discipline of the Schedule

The cloister maintains a schedule of the study that is observed throughout the year, divided into the seasons of the withdrawal and the seasons of the instruction. The seasons of the withdrawal are set apart for the long retreat, and the seasons of the instruction are set apart for the reading of the older texts and the discussion of the doctrine of the veils.

The schedule is observed without the exception, and the breaking of the schedule is regarded by the elders as a failure of the discipline. The novice who enters the cloister must attend the withdrawal and the instruction in the proper order, and the novice who attempts to avoid the withdrawal or the instruction is corrected by the senior veiler.

The schedule is the framework within which the discipline is practised, and the alternation of the withdrawal and the instruction is regarded as essential to the discipline. The path teaches that the retreat without the instruction is empty and the instruction without the retreat is shallow.

## The Discipline of the Chamber

The chamber of the long retreat is the principal room of the cloister, and the discipline of the chamber is the most demanding of the practices that the cloister observes. The chamber is a small room that is curtained against the brightness of the day, furnished with a low couch, a small table, a basin of water, and a single lamp that is kept burning through the hours of the retreat.

The novice who enters the chamber for the long retreat must remain within it for the duration of the season, and the curtains must not be opened until the close. The novice is permitted the company only of the senior veiler who attends to the discipline without the conversation, and the novice must observe the silence for the whole of the retreat.

The discipline of the chamber is the cultivation of the stillness that the path requires. The novice who remains in the chamber through the long days and the longer nights learns to attend to the thoughts and the feelings that arise in the silence, observing them without the attachment and allowing them to pass without the pursuit. The stillness that the novice experiences in the depth of the retreat is the taste of the Veiled One's nature.

## The Discipline of the Silence

The silence is the most important of the disciplines that the cloister observes, for the path teaches that the silence is the condition of the approach to the Veiled One. The silence is observed in the chamber of the retreat, in the library, and in the meeting-hall of the cloister. The novice who speaks in the silence is corrected by the senior veiler.

The silence is not the absence of the sound but the presence of the attention. The novice who observes the silence learns to attend to the world without the interference of the words, and the attention that the novice cultivates in the silence is the attention that the path requires for the lifting of the veils.

The discipline of the silence is the most difficult of the practices for the novice who is accustomed to the noise of the ordinary life. The novice who persists through the difficulty of the silence begins to experience the stillness that the path teaches is the nature of the Veiled One.

## The Discipline of the Instruction

The instruction is conducted in the meeting-hall of the cloister, a plain room lit by a single window curtained against the brightness of the day. The instruction is led by the senior veilers, who guide the novices through the readings that the path has preserved.

The readings are drawn from the older writings of the path and the commentaries that have been added by the senior veilers over the generations. The readings are conducted in the silence, with the reader attending to the text without the interruption of the commentary. The reader may mark the passages that are found to be of the consequence and must bring them to the senior veiler at the conclusion of the reading.

The senior veiler then discusses the marked passages with the reader, providing the instruction and the correction of the misunderstanding. The instruction is regarded as a complement to the long retreat, and the path holds that the reader who has not undertaken the retreat is not fitted to interpret the texts.

## The Discipline of the Library

The library of the cloister is the room in which the texts that the path has preserved are kept. The library is a chamber lined with the shelves of the plain wood, holding the copies of the older texts and the commentaries that the path has produced. The library is open to the senior veilers and to the novices who have been admitted to the seasons of the instruction.

The discipline of the library is the discipline of the care. The reader who handles the texts must do so with the clean hands and the careful attention, for the texts are the treasures of the path and the loss of a text is the loss of a part of the tradition. The reader who damages a text is required to report the damage to the senior veiler.

## The Discipline of the Wandering

The wandering veilers of the cloister observe a discipline of the travel that is distinct from the discipline of the cloister. The wanderer who travels between the cloisters and the hill towns carries the small white staff that is the sign of the calling and observes the custom of the speaking softly and at length only when the company requires it.

The discipline of the wandering is the discipline of the exposure. The wanderer is tested by the conditions of the road and by the encounters with the people who do not share the discipline of the path. The wanderer who returns to the cloister at the close of the wandering has been refined by the testing, and the return is the occasion for the silence in which the wanderer's labours are observed without the ceremony.

## The Discipline of the Community

The community of the cloister observes the discipline of the common life, sharing the labour of the maintenance and the silence of the retreat. The veilers who reside in the cloister are required to contribute to the common work, whether it be the cleaning of the chambers, the preparation of the food, or the maintenance of the building.

The discipline of the community is the discipline of the mutual support. The veiler who is weakened by the long retreat is supported by the other veilers, and the veiler who is strengthened by the retreat supports the others in their turn. The community is the condition of the discipline, and the discipline is the condition of the community.
