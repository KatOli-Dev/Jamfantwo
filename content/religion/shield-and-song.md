## The Order of the Two Halves

The Shield and Song is an order whose calling is divided into two complementary halves: the shield-half, which is the bearing of hardship for the protection of others, and the song-half, which is the keeping of memory and the maintenance of the community's meaning through the arts of voice and instrument. The shield-half is embodied in the [Standing Watch](/content/government/local/standing-watch), the body of watchmen who maintain the holding of the beacon and the patrol of the border in the northern frontier. The song-half is embodied in the [Memory Houses](/content/religion/houses-of-memory), the quiet establishments where the singers of the order keep the local songs and traditions and maintain the evening review. The order teaches that the divine [Watcher](/content/religion/watcher) is revealed in both aspects, and that the practitioner who neglects either half of the calling fails to honour the full nature of the Watcher's keeping.

The order is found chiefly in the northern regions of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the frontier between settled land and the ancient forests requires the discipline of the watch, and in the market towns and river crossings of the temperate lowlands, where the communities value the keeping of memory. The Shield and Song is not a religion of the kind that maintains temples or a doctrine of the kind that the academies cultivate; it is a calling, a discipline of bearing and singing that the practitioner undertakes for the duration of life, and the order asks of its members that they bear what they are called to bear and sing what they are called to sing.

## The Shield-Half: The Standing Watch

The shield-half of the calling is the bearing of hardship for the protection of others, and its principal embodiment is the [Standing Watch](/content/government/local/standing-watch), the body of watchmen who maintain the holding of the beacon and the patrol of the border in the northern frontier of Aethelar. The Standing Watch is not a military order in the ordinary sense; the watchmen are not soldiers who go to war but guardians who hold the line against the dangers that emerge from the deeper woodland. The discipline of the watch is the discipline of endurance, and the watchmen are trained to withstand the cold and the dark and the long silences of the frontier without losing the alertness that the watch requires.

The shield-half teaches the practitioner that the protection of others is not a matter of strength or valour in the manner that the sagas celebrate. The protection of others is a matter of bearing: bearing the hardship of the watch without complaint, bearing the weight of the community's safety without seeking recognition, and bearing the knowledge that the failure of the watch may mean the loss of lives that the watchman has never met. The discipline of the shield-half is the discipline of this bearing, and the order holds that the practitioner who bears well is already a full participant in the Watcher's keeping of the world.

## The Song-Half: The Memory Houses

The song-half of the calling is the keeping of memory, and its principal embodiment is the [Memory Houses](/content/religion/houses-of-memory), the quiet establishments where the singers of the order maintain the local songs and traditions and conduct the evening review. The singers are the keepers of the order's living memory, charged with learning by heart a substantial body of songs and with maintaining the accuracy of their transmission from one generation to the next. The tradition holds that a song learned imperfectly is a song lost, and the singers are trained accordingly.

The song-half teaches the practitioner that the memory of the community is not a matter of recording events in the manner that the chroniclers practise. The memory of the community is a matter of singing: singing the names of the dead at the evening review, singing the deeds of the forebears at the gatherings, singing the songs that the community has preserved through the generations. The discipline of the song-half is the discipline of this singing, and the order holds that the singer who sings well is already a full participant in the Watcher's keeping of the world.

## The Evening Review

The central observance of the order is the evening review, conducted at the Memory Houses or at the watch posts of the Standing Watch. At the close of the day, the household or the company recalls together the events of the day, giving thanks for the mercies and observing the losses. A short song of account is sung, naming the day's sick and recovered, the day's fed and unfed, the day's spared and the day's fallen. The review is not a confession and not a punishment; it is an honest accounting, and the remedy for the sins of the day lies in the change of conduct on the morrow rather than in any further observance at the evening.

The evening review is the chief instrument by which the two halves of the calling are united in a single observance. The watchman who has held the line through the day and the singer who has kept the songs of the community both attend the review, and the accounting of the day's mercies and losses is the occasion on which the shield-half and the song-half are seen to be two aspects of a single calling. The order teaches that the review is meant to keep the account honest, so that the community may know what it has done and may be moved to mend what has been done amiss.

## The Kindred Traditions and the Correspondence

The Shield and Song maintains correspondence with the Still Flame and the Unwritten Name, the kindred traditions of the continent, and the three traditions have exchanged practitioners for many generations. A singer of the Shield and Song will sometimes attend the evening review of a hearth-hall of the Still Flame, and a hearth-keeper will sometimes attend the singing at a house of memory. The correspondence is the means by which the order has preserved its kinship with the other traditions of the continent and has maintained the openness to the wider world that the order regards as a privilege rather than a claim.

The order has also exchanged singers with the Forged Flame, whose hearth-keepers teach a discipline of the workshop that the order regards as a worthy complement to the singing of the houses of memory. The Forged Flame is not a kindred tradition in the sense that the Still Flame is a kindred tradition, but the exchange has enriched both orders and has deepened the understanding of the relation between the work of the hands and the work of the voice.

## The Life of the Calling

The Shield and Song is a calling rather than a profession, and the order does not offer worldly advancement or the respect of the courts. The order asks of its practitioners that they bear what they are called to bear and sing what they are called to sing, and it promises them in return that the bearing and the singing are themselves a share in the Watcher's keeping of the world. The practitioner who undertakes the calling does not seek reward or recognition, and the order teaches that the reward of the calling is the calling itself: the discipline of the watch and the song, the community of the practitioners, and the knowledge that the bearing and the singing are a participation in the divine keeping.

The Shield and Song has endured through the centuries because the need for the calling has endured. The frontier still requires the watch, and the communities still require the memory that the singers preserve. The order grows slowly, attracting those who are drawn to the discipline of bearing and singing, and it has maintained its practices through the generations without seeking the world's adherence or the world's approval.
