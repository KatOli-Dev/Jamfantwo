## The Making as a Form of Prayer

The gnomish peoples of the woodland hills practise a form of spiritual life centred upon the craft of making, the work of the hands that transforms raw material into a finished object. Gnomish craft devotion is not a religion in the manner of the temple faiths; [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) maintain no priests, doctrines, or sacred texts. The craft devotion is the understanding that the act of making is a form of prayer, and that an object well made is an honour to the divine.

Gnomes are a people of the hands, and making is the central activity of gnomish life. The gnomish craftsman who works in the forge or the workshop is engaged in a practice both practical and spiritual, and the attention the craftsman brings to the work is the attention the gnome brings to the divine.

## The Spirit in the Material

Gnomes believe that the material from which an object is made contains a spirit that is released and shaped by the work of the craftsman. The spirit of the wood, the spirit of the metal, and the spirit of the stone are the presences the gnome honours in the work, and the gnome who works the material with respect and skill is the gnome who releases the spirit in its best form.

The spirit of the wood is the presence the gnome experiences in the grain and texture of timber. The gnomish woodworker who selects a piece of wood for carving attends to the spirit the wood contains, and the carving that follows is the revelation of the form the spirit has intended.

The spirit of the metal is the presence the gnome experiences in the forge and the anvil. The gnomish smith who heats metal in the forge and shapes it on the anvil is in dialogue with the spirit of the metal, and the object that emerges from the forging is the resolution of that dialogue.

The spirit of the stone is the presence the gnome experiences in the cutting and polishing of a gem. The gnomish lapidary who cuts the stone reveals the light the stone has contained, and the polished gem is the expression of the spirit the stone has held.

## The Workshop as Sacred Place

The workshop is the sacred place of gnomish craft devotion, and the gnome who enters the workshop enters a space set apart for the work of making. The workshop is furnished with the tools and materials the craftsman requires, and the gnome who works in the workshop is engaged in the practice that is the devotion.

The tools of the workshop are the instruments of devotion, and the gnome who uses them does so with care and respect. A tool that is well maintained serves the devotion; a tool that is neglected fails in its service.

The gnome who begins work in the workshop speaks the words of the opening, acknowledging the spirits of the material and the tools and asking for the guidance of the hands. The gnome who finishes work speaks the words of the closing, thanking the spirits for their assistance and the material for its gift.

## The Practice of Attention

The practice of attention is the central discipline of gnomish craft devotion. The gnome who works at the craft must attend to the work with full attention, and the gnome who allows attention to wander produces flawed work.

Attention is the means by which the gnome enters into relationship with the spirit of the material. The gnome who attends to the grain of the wood, the temper of the metal, and the clarity of the stone is the gnome who perceives the spirit the material contains.

The practice of attention is also the means by which the gnome cultivates stillness of mind. The gnome absorbed in the work does not think of other things, and absorption in the work is the form of meditation that the craft devotion teaches.

## The Teaching of the Master

The craft devotion is transmitted from master to apprentice through the practice of teaching. The master gnome who takes an apprentice into the workshop teaches the skills of the craft and the discipline of attention, and the apprentice who learns from the master learns the devotion as well as the trade.

The teaching is not conducted through lectures or texts. The master shows the apprentice how the work is done, and the apprentice learns by watching and doing. The master speaks the words of the opening and the closing, and the apprentice learns the words through repetition.

Apprenticeship is the period during which the apprentice learns the devotion as well as the craft, and the apprentice who completes it is initiated into the practice.

## The Festival of Making

Gnomish communities observe the festival of making at the turning of the year, the occasion on which craftsmen display the work produced through the year. The festival is held in the central clearing of the community, and the gnomes bring the objects they have made to be seen and admired by others.

The festival is not a competition; gnomes do not judge the work of others as better or worse. The festival is the celebration of making, and the gnome who looks at the work of another gnome attends to the spirit the other gnome has released through the craft.

## The Craft Devotion and Other Traditions

Gnomish craft devotion is not a tradition that seeks converts, and the gnomes do not maintain correspondence with human traditions. Gnomes regard craft devotion as the practice of the gnomish people, and the human faiths as the practices of human peoples.

Gnomes respect human traditions without adopting them, and those who trade with human settlements have learned enough of human beliefs to conduct relations with courtesy. Gnomes recognise in the [Still Flame](/content/religion/still-flame) and the [Forged Flame](/content/religion/forged-flame) the kindred understanding that the work of the hands is a form of devotion, and they have exchanged craftsmen with the [Forged Flame](/content/religion/forged-flame) on occasion.
