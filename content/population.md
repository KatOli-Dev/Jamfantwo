## Overview

The known world is home to a diversity of peoples, both those who walk the common paths of humankind and those whose origins lie elsewhere entirely. Several distinct species share the continents and seas, each with their own traditions, settlements, and ways of life. Their interactions have shaped the history of the world, giving rise to great cities, trade networks, and cultural exchanges that span oceans and continents. This page provides a broad survey of the peoples known to chroniclers. Detailed accounts of individual species and their civilisations may be found on their respective pages.

## The Common Peoples

The term common peoples refers to those species whose populations are most widespread and whose civilisations are most extensively documented. They are found on all three continents and across the islands and coasts of the known world, and their languages and customs form the foundation of most recorded history. The common peoples are [humans](/content/humans), [elves](/content/elves), and [dwarves](/content/dwarves). Each of these species has established enduring civilisations on multiple continents, and their histories are deeply intertwined with the broader record of the world.

## The Less Common Peoples

Beyond the common peoples, the world is home to several species whose populations are smaller or more localised. These peoples are no less significant in the history of the world, and their civilisations, though less frequently encountered, have made distinctive contributions to the broader culture of the known world. The less common peoples are gnomes, orcs, and goblins. Their communities tend to be concentrated in particular regions rather than spread across the whole of the world, and their ways of life are correspondingly varied.

## Peoples of the Seas and Coasts

In addition to the land-dwelling peoples, the world is home to species whose lives are closely tied to the oceans and coastlines. These peoples are less frequently described in the chronicles of the interior continents, but they play an important role in the maritime life of the known world. The merfolk are the most widely known of these peoples, inhabiting the coastal waters and shallow seas around all three continents.

## Coexistence and Exchange

The peoples of the known world do not live in isolation from one another. Trade routes, shared borders, and common interests have brought different species into regular contact, and the larger cities of Aethelar and Sahrana are home to mixed communities where several peoples live side by side. Such coexistence is not without its difficulties. Differences in lifespan, custom, and temperament can give rise to misunderstanding, and old grievances are not easily forgotten. Yet the broad current of history has been one of increasing contact and exchange, and the world's greatest achievements have often arisen where peoples of different kinds have worked together.

Relations among the peoples vary by region and by era. In some parts of the world, closely neighbouring species share languages, festivals, and even governance. In others, distance and differing ways of life have kept peoples largely separate, meeting only at the trading post or the border market. The balance between co-operation and separation has shifted many times, and no single pattern prevails across the whole of the known world.
