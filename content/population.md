## Overview

The known world is home to a diversity of peoples, each a distinct species with its own form, nature, and manner of life. Several of these peoples are well known to chroniclers and naturalists, while others are less frequently encountered and more poorly understood. What follows is a broad survey of the sapient species known to inhabit the continents and seas, with attention to their forms, their habits, and the relations between them. Detailed accounts of individual species may be found on their respective pages.

## The Common Peoples

The term common peoples refers to those species whose populations are most widespread and whose bodies and habits are most extensively documented. They are found on all three continents and across the islands and coasts of the known world. The common peoples are [humans](/content/humans), [elves](/content/elves), and [dwarves](/content/dwarves). Each of these species has established enduring populations on multiple continents, and their interactions with one another and with the lands they inhabit have shaped much of the natural history of the known world.

Of the three, humans are the most numerous and the most widely distributed, owing to their short generation time, high birth rate, and generalised constitution, which allows them to thrive in nearly any climate. Elves are the longest-lived but the fewest, for their rate of reproduction is very low and their populations grow slowly. Dwarves stand between the two in both lifespan and fecundity, and their restriction to mountainous and subterranean habitats limits their range relative to humans, though they are well established wherever the terrain suits them.

## The Less Common Peoples

Beyond the common peoples, the world is home to several species whose populations are smaller or more localised. The less common peoples are [gnomes](/content/gnomes), [orcs](/content/orcs), and [goblins](/content/goblins). Their communities tend to be concentrated in particular regions rather than spread across the whole of the world, and their forms and habits are correspondingly varied and less fully recorded.

[Gnomes](/content/gnomes) are a small folk, reported in the woodlands and hills of several continents, and are said to bear some resemblance to dwarves in miniature, though the nature of their kinship, if any, is a matter of speculation. [Orcs](/content/orcs) are a large and powerful people, found chiefly in the wilder and less settled regions of the world, and their physiology suggests a constitution well suited to harsh conditions and violent exertion. [Goblins](/content/goblins) are a smaller and more numerous people, reported in a variety of habitats, often in places that other peoples do not occupy. The natural history of all three is poorly developed compared to that of the common peoples, and much of what is written about them is unreliable.

## Peoples of the Seas and Coasts

In addition to the land-dwelling peoples, the world is home to species whose lives are closely tied to the oceans and coastlines. These peoples are less frequently described in the chronicles of the interior continents, but they are known to those who travel the coasts and work the seas. The [merfolk](/content/merfolk) are the most widely known of these peoples, inhabiting the coastal waters and shallow seas around all three continents. Their bodies are adapted to an aquatic life in a manner that admits no comparison with any land-dwelling people, and their physiology is a subject of particular interest to naturalists, though opportunities for close study are limited.

## Relations Between Peoples

Where the ranges of different peoples overlap, their interactions are shaped by the natures of each. Differences in lifespan, rate of reproduction, and environmental tolerance determine in large part whether species coexist closely or remain separate. Humans, being generalists, share habitat with both elves and dwarves across broad portions of the world, and the chief factor in their coexistence is the tendency of each species to favour different ground within a shared region. Elves and dwarves, being more specialised in their requirements, overlap less frequently, and where they do, they generally separate by elevation and vegetation.

Where species are more particular in their needs, they tend to be more isolated from others. [Orcs](/content/orcs) and [goblins](/content/goblins), favouring wild and marginal lands, have less contact with the common peoples, and the [merfolk](/content/merfolk), bound to the sea, are apart from all land-dwelling species by the nature of their habitat. The degree of contact between peoples is thus governed less by disposition than by the lands they can inhabit and the manner in which their ranges intersect.
