## Overview

Highgate is a city of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar) that commands the most important pass through the mountain spine, the great range that divides the continent into eastern and western halves. The city stands at the narrowest point of the pass, where the mountains contract to a defile that is barely wide enough for a road, and its walls and gates span the full width of the gap, making Highgate the most strategically significant settlement on the overland route between the two coasts. The city has been a point of military and commercial importance for as long as the pass has been used, and its fortifications are among the most extensive and the most impressive in the known world.

## The Pass

The pass that Highgate commands is the lowest and the most direct route across the mountain spine, and it is the only crossing that remains passable throughout the year, though the winter snows can close it for weeks at a time. The road that runs through the pass is the principal overland route between the eastern and western coasts of Aethelar, and the traffic that passes through Highgate includes the trade goods, the travellers, and the armies that have moved between the two halves of the continent for centuries. The pass is narrow, and the walls of the defile rise steeply on either side, funnelling all traffic through the gate that gives the city its name.

The gate itself is a massive structure of stone and iron, built to withstand siege and to control the passage of all who use the road. The gate is the only way through the walls of Highgate, and the city's control of the gate gives it the power to open or close the pass at will, a power that has been exercised many times in the city's long history. The tolls levied on the traffic that passes through the gate are the principal source of the city's wealth, and the toll rates are a subject of negotiation between Highgate and the powers that depend on the pass for their trade.

## History

The strategic value of the pass was recognised in the earliest period of recorded history, and the first fortifications at Highgate were raised long before the city itself took shape. The earliest known structure was a stone watchtower, built by the human settlers of the western lowlands who sought to secure their trade routes to the eastern coast. Over the centuries, the tower was enlarged and reinforced, and the first gate was constructed across the defile to control the passage of travellers and to levy tolls upon the goods that passed through.

The city grew around the gate as the volume of trade between the two coasts increased, and the population of Highgate expanded to include the soldiers, the administrators, the merchants, and the craftsmen who served the needs of the garrison and the travellers. The construction of the present walls and the great gate was undertaken in a period of renewed prosperity, when the trade between the eastern and western coasts was at its height, and the fortifications were built to a standard that has not been surpassed. The walls have been reinforced and extended several times in the centuries since, and the dwarven engineers of the city have contributed significantly to the maintenance and the improvement of the structures.

Highgate has been besieged on several occasions, for the control of the pass has been a prize that the powers on both sides of the mountain spine have sought to claim. The most serious of these sieges was repelled by the garrison without relief from either side, and the endurance of the defenders during this episode is a source of pride for the city and a testament to the strength of the fortifications. The city has maintained its independence throughout these conflicts, and its neutrality has been respected by the powers that border the mountain spine, for the tolls on the pass are paid by all, and the disruption of the trade that flows through the gate is a cost that no power wishes to bear.

## The City and the Fortress

Highgate is a city that is also a fortress, and its fortifications are the dominant feature of the settlement. The walls that span the defile are thick and high, built of the grey stone of the mountains and reinforced with towers and bastions that command the approach from both sides. The city within the walls is compact and densely built, for the space within the defile is limited, and the buildings are tall and close-set, their upper floors projecting over the narrow streets to make the most of the available ground. The garrison of Highgate is among the largest and the most professional in Aethelar, and the soldiers of the city are a visible and constant presence in the streets.

The streets of Highgate are arranged in a pattern that reflects the constraints of the defile, with the main road running east to west through the gate and the side streets climbing the slopes on either side. The buildings are constructed of the same grey stone as the walls, and the architecture is plain and functional, for the city is a fortress first and a place of residence second. The market square is situated near the gate, and it is the commercial centre of the city, where the goods that pass through the pass are exchanged and the provisions for the travellers and the garrison are sold.

The population of Highgate is chiefly [human](/content/humans), with a significant community of [dwarves](/content/dwarves) who are involved in the mining and the metalwork that the mountains provide, and who are valued for their skill in the maintenance of the fortifications and the gate. The [minotaurs](/content/minotaurs) of the labyrinthine highlands are occasional visitors to the city, and the trade between Highgate and the minotaur communities of the uplands is a small but valued part of the city's commerce.

## Trade and Significance

The trade of Highgate is dominated by the tolls on the traffic that passes through the gate, and the goods that flow through the city include the products of the eastern coast, the western coast, and the interior. The city is also a centre for the exchange of goods between the lowlands and the mountains, and the metalwork, the stone, and the mineral products of the mountain spine are traded at Highgate for the grain, the cloth, and the manufactured goods of the lowlands. The market square near the gate is the site of a daily exchange where the merchants of the city and the traders who pass through the gate negotiate the prices and the quantities of the goods that are bought and sold.

The tolls that are levied on the traffic are set by the council of Highgate, a body of elected officials and appointed elders that governs the city and oversees the administration of the pass. The toll rates are a subject of negotiation between Highgate and the powers that depend on the pass for their trade, and the rates have been adjusted many times in response to the changing conditions of the continental trade. The revenue from the tolls is used to fund the maintenance of the fortifications, the payment of the garrison, and the construction of the public works that serve the city and the travellers who pass through it.

Highgate is the most strategically significant settlement on the overland route between the two coasts of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), and its control of the pass gives it a military and a commercial importance that is unmatched by any other city in the mountain spine. The city is an independent polity, allied with but not subject to the powers that border the mountain spine, and its neutrality is the foundation of its prosperity, for the tolls on the pass are paid by all who use it, regardless of their allegiance. The significance of Highgate lies in its gate, which is the single most important structure on the overland route between the two coasts, and the city is a symbol of the commercial and the strategic importance of the mountain pass that it commands.
