## Overview

Highgate is a city of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar) that commands the most important pass through the mountain spine, the great range that divides the continent into eastern and western halves. The city stands at the narrowest point of the pass, where the mountains contract to a defile that is barely wide enough for a road, and its walls and gates span the full width of the gap, making Highgate the most strategically significant settlement on the overland route between the two coasts. The city has been a point of military and commercial importance for as long as the pass has been used, and its fortifications are among the most extensive and the most impressive in the known world.

## The Pass

The pass that Highgate commands is the lowest and the most direct route across the mountain spine, and it is the only crossing that remains passable throughout the year, though the winter snows can close it for weeks at a time. The road that runs through the pass is the principal overland route between the eastern and western coasts of Aethelar, and the traffic that passes through Highgate includes the trade goods, the travellers, and the armies that have moved between the two halves of the continent for centuries. The pass is narrow, and the walls of the defile rise steeply on either side, funnelling all traffic through the gate that gives the city its name.

The gate itself is a massive structure of stone and iron, built to withstand siege and to control the passage of all who use the road. The gate is the only way through the walls of Highgate, and the city's control of the gate gives it the power to open or close the pass at will, a power that has been exercised many times in the city's long history. The tolls levied on the traffic that passes through the gate are the principal source of the city's wealth, and the toll rates are a subject of negotiation between Highgate and the powers that depend on the pass for their trade.

## The City and the Fortress

Highgate is a city that is also a fortress, and its fortifications are the dominant feature of the settlement. The walls that span the defile are thick and high, built of the grey stone of the mountains and reinforced with towers and bastions that command the approach from both sides. The city within the walls is compact and densely built, for the space within the defile is limited, and the buildings are tall and close-set, their upper floors projecting over the narrow streets to make the most of the available ground. The garrison of Highgate is among the largest and the most professional in Aethelar, and the soldiers of the city are a visible and constant presence in the streets.

The population of Highgate is chiefly [human](/content/humans), with a significant community of [dwarves](/content/dwarves) who are involved in the mining and the metalwork that the mountains provide, and who are valued for their skill in the maintenance of the fortifications and the gate. The [minotaurs](/content/minotaurs) of the labyrinthine highlands are occasional visitors to the city, and the trade between Highgate and the minotaur communities of the uplands is a small but valued part of the city's commerce.

## Trade and Significance

The trade of Highgate is dominated by the tolls on the traffic that passes through the gate, and the goods that flow through the city include the products of the eastern coast, the western coast, and the interior. The city is also a centre for the exchange of goods between the lowlands and the mountains, and the metalwork, the stone, and the mineral products of the mountain spine are traded at Highgate for the grain, the cloth, and the manufactured goods of the lowlands.

Highgate is the most strategically significant settlement on the overland route between the two coasts of Aethelar, and its control of the pass gives it a military and a commercial importance that is unmatched by any other city in the mountain spine. The city is an independent polity, allied with but not subject to the powers that border the mountain spine, and its neutrality is the foundation of its prosperity, for the tolls on the pass are paid by all who use it, regardless of their allegiance.
