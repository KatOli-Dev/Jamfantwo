## The Regulation of Violence and Wrongdoing

The criminal law of the known world is concerned with the regulation of conduct that is considered harmful to persons, property, or the social order. Every society, whether governed by a king, a council, or a gathering of elders, has established rules prohibiting certain acts and providing for the punishment of those who commit them. The forms of criminal law and punishment vary widely across the continents, reflecting differences in culture, environment, and political organisation, yet certain principles are common to all systems of criminal justice.

The most fundamental of these principles is that the community has the right and the duty to respond to wrongdoing. A person who kills, injures, steals from, or defrauds another has not merely wronged the individual victim but has disturbed the peace and order of the community as a whole. The response to wrongdoing may take the form of punishment imposed by the sovereign or the council, compensation paid to the victim or the victim's family, or a combination of both. The forms of response reflect the values of the society in which they are employed and the purposes that the society believes punishment should serve.

## The Definition of Crimes in the Eastern Kingdoms

In the kingdoms of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), crimes are defined by the decrees of the sovereign and the decisions of the courts. The criminal law of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) divides crimes into three categories: crimes against the person, crimes against property, and crimes against the crown. Crimes against the person include murder, manslaughter, assault, and the various forms of unlawful restraint. Crimes against property include theft, robbery, burglary, arson, and the fraudulent obtaining of property by deception. Crimes against the crown include treason, sedition, counterfeiting, and the evasion of taxes.

The criminal law of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) follows a similar classification, though the water law of the kingdom creates additional offences unique to the management of the river. The obstruction of a watercourse, the failure to maintain a dyke, and the diversion of water without authorisation are criminal offences in Vael, punishable by fines and, in serious cases, by the forfeiture of the land from which the offence was committed. The water courts of Vael have jurisdiction over these offences, and the penalties imposed by the water courts are enforced by the water commissioners.

## The Definition of Crimes in the Confederations and Leagues

In the confederations and leagues of the known world, crimes are defined by the decisions of the councils and the customs of the member communities. The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) has a body of criminal law that applies throughout its territory, though the enforcement of the law is largely a matter for the individual member communities. The confederation council has the authority to define crimes that affect the interests of the confederation as a whole, including the theft of cattle or horses, the destruction of boundary markers, and the obstruction of the roads and bridges that the confederation maintains.

The [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) has a criminal code that applies within the jurisdiction of the league's courts, primarily concerned with offences arising from maritime commerce. Piracy, the plundering of shipwrecks, the fraudulent overloading of vessels, and the falsification of cargo manifests are crimes under the league's code, punishable by fines, imprisonment, and the forfeiture of the vessel used in the commission of the offence. The league's admiralty court has jurisdiction over these offences, and the penalties imposed by the court are enforced by the collective authority of the member cities.

In the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), the criminal law is primarily concerned with offences arising from the spice trade. The adulteration of spices, the falsification of quality certificates, and the bribery of inspectors are serious crimes in Amurra, punishable by heavy fines and permanent exclusion from the spice trade. The republic's commercial court has jurisdiction over these offences, and the republic's inspectors are empowered to seize and destroy adulterated goods.

## The Definition of Crimes Among the Nomadic Peoples

Among the nomadic peoples of the known world, crimes are defined by the customary law of the kindreds and tribes. The [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) of the Sahrani desert recognise offences against the person, offences against property, and offences against the code of hospitality that is the foundation of the social order of the desert. Murder, assault, and the theft of livestock are the most serious offences under the customary law of the desert, and the penalties for these offences reflect the values of the nomadic peoples.

The code of hospitality is a distinctive feature of the criminal law of the desert. The refusal of hospitality to a traveller in need, the betrayal of a guest who has been received under the protection of the host, and the violation of a guest's trust are serious offences under the customary law of the Kharesh Tribes, punishable by the payment of compensation to the victim and, in the most serious cases, by the expulsion of the offender from the kindred. The code of hospitality is enforced by the elders of the kindreds and by the collective authority of the tribe.

Among the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds of the grasslands, the customary law recognises offences against the person, offences against property, and offences against the seasonal order of grazing and migration. The disruption of the grazing cycle, the destruction of the stone circles that mark the gathering places of the kindreds, and the violation of the boundaries that separate the territories of different kindreds are offences under the customary law of the grasslands, punishable by the payment of compensation in livestock and, in the most serious cases, by the expulsion of the offender from the kindred.

## The Courts and the Administration of Criminal Justice

The courts that administer criminal justice vary widely across the known world. In the kingdoms of Aethelar, criminal justice is administered by the royal courts, presided over by judges appointed by the sovereign. The royal courts have jurisdiction over all criminal offences committed within the territory of the kingdom, and the judges who preside over them have the authority to determine guilt and to impose punishment. The procedure of the royal courts is formal and adversarial, with the prosecution conducted by officials appointed by the crown and the defence conducted by the accused or by a representative chosen by the accused.

In the confederations and leagues, criminal justice is administered by the councils and by the courts established by the councils. The Plains Confederation has a network of local courts that handle minor offences, while more serious offences are tried before the confederation council or before a panel of elders appointed by the council. The Thessaran League has an admiralty court that handles offences arising from maritime commerce, while other offences are handled by the courts of the individual member cities.

Among the nomadic peoples, criminal justice is administered by the elders of the kindreds and by the gatherings of the kindreds. The Kharesh Tribes have no formal courts in the sense that the settled peoples know, but the elders of the kindred have the authority to resolve disputes and to impose penalties. The Sands' Assembly, the gathering of the elders of all the kindreds, may hear the most serious cases and impose penalties that affect the whole of the confederation.

## The Forms of Punishment

The forms of punishment imposed by the courts and councils of the known world are as varied as the legal systems themselves. The most common form of punishment is the fine, a payment of money or goods to the crown, the council, or the victim. Fines are the preferred punishment in most legal systems, for they are easily administered and they compensate the victim and the community for the harm caused by the offence. The amount of the fine is determined by the seriousness of the offence, the means of the offender, and the customs of the community.

Imprisonment is also common in the kingdoms and city-states, though the conditions of imprisonment vary widely. In Aldreth, the prisons are maintained by the crown and are used primarily for the detention of offenders awaiting trial or the enforcement of fines imposed by the courts. Long-term imprisonment is rare, for the cost of maintaining prisoners is high and the purpose of punishment is generally understood to be the deterrence of future offences rather than the isolation of offenders from society.

Corporal punishment, including whipping and branding, is used in some legal systems as a punishment for certain offences. The [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) employ corporal punishment for offences against the mining code, reflecting the dwarven belief that the integrity of the mining operation is of such importance that offences against it must be punished in a manner that is both painful and public. The [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) employ corporal punishment for offences against the martial code, reflecting the martial traditions of the clans.

The most serious punishment in most legal systems is death, reserved for the most serious offences. In Aldreth, the death penalty is imposed for murder, treason, and piracy, and the method of execution is determined by the nature of the offence. In the Plains Confederation, the death penalty is rarely imposed, for the confederation values the life of every member of the community and reserves the death penalty for the most heinous offences, such as the murder of a child or the betrayal of the confederation to its enemies.

## Blood Debt and Compensation Among the Nomadic Peoples

Among the nomadic peoples, the preferred form of punishment for serious offences is the payment of compensation to the victim or the victim's family, known in the customary law of the Kharesh Tribes as the blood debt. The blood debt is the obligation of the offender or the offender's kindred to pay compensation to the victim or the victim's kindred for the harm caused by the offence. The amount of the compensation is determined by the elders of the kindreds, taking into account the nature of the offence, the status of the victim, and the means of the offender.

The blood debt serves several purposes. It compensates the victim or the victim's family for the harm suffered, it provides an incentive for the offender to avoid future offences, and it prevents the cycle of revenge that might otherwise arise from the commission of serious offences. The blood debt is the mechanism by which the customary law of the nomadic peoples maintains social order in the absence of the formal courts and enforcement institutions that the settled peoples rely upon.

The blood debt is also a feature of the customary law of the centaur kindreds of the grasslands, where the compensation for a killing is measured in horses and cattle. The amount of the compensation is determined by the elders of the kindreds, and the payment of the compensation is the responsibility of the offender's kindred. The blood debt is the most serious obligation under the customary law of the grasslands, and the failure to pay the blood debt is regarded as a violation of the social order that may lead to the expulsion of the offender's kindred from the confederation.

## The Purposes of Punishment

The purposes of punishment in the legal systems of the known world are multiple and varied. The most commonly stated purpose is deterrence, the prevention of future offences by the offender and by others who might be tempted to commit similar offences. The fine, the imprisonment, the corporal punishment, and the death penalty are all intended to deter, though they achieve deterrence in different ways and to different degrees.

A second purpose of punishment is retribution, the infliction of suffering on the offender in proportion to the harm caused by the offence. The principle of proportionality is central to the retributive purpose, and legal systems that emphasise retribution typically have detailed scales of punishment that specify the penalty for each offence in proportion to its seriousness.

A third purpose of punishment is rehabilitation, the reform of the offender so that the offender may return to society as a law-abiding member. Rehabilitation is most commonly pursued through the imposition of fines or the requirement that the offender perform labour for the benefit of the community. The water courts of Vael, for example, may require an offender who has failed to maintain a dyke to perform labour on the dyke as part of the penalty, thus both punishing the offender and repairing the harm caused by the offence.

A fourth purpose of punishment is the protection of society by the isolation of offenders who pose a continuing threat to the community. Imprisonment and exile serve this purpose, removing the offender from the community and preventing further offences. The exile of a serious offender from the Kharesh Tribes is the most severe penalty under the customary law of the desert, for the exile is condemned to a life outside the protection of the kindred and the code of hospitality that sustains life in the desert.
