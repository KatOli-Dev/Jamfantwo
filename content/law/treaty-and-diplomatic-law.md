## The Ordering of Relations Between Powers

Treaty and diplomatic law governs the relations between the political entities of the known world, the manner in which agreements between sovereigns and councils are made and enforced, and the rights and obligations of those who conduct the business of diplomacy. The system of treaty and diplomatic law is essential to the maintenance of peace and the conduct of commerce across the boundaries of kingdoms, leagues, and confederations.

The treaties of the known world are the instruments by which political entities establish the terms of their relationship. Treaties may address matters of peace and war, trade and commerce, the delimitation of boundaries, the extradition of criminals, and the mutual recognition of legal judgments. The form and content of treaties reflect the relationship between the parties and the issues that the treaty is intended to address.

## The Forms of Treaty

The most common form of treaty in the known world is the bilateral treaty, an agreement between two political entities that establishes the terms of their relationship. Bilateral treaties are used to establish peace, to regulate trade, to define boundaries, and to provide for mutual assistance in times of need. The treaty between the Kingdom of [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) and the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) governing the trade routes between the coast and the interior is an example of a bilateral treaty that has endured for centuries, regulating the flow of goods and the rights of merchants travelling between the two realms.

Multilateral treaties are agreements among three or more political entities, and they are used to establish the terms of relationship among a group of states that share common interests. The founding charter of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) is a multilateral treaty that established the framework for co-operation among the eastern port cities, defining the rights and obligations of the member cities and the institutions through which the league is governed. The charter of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) serves a similar function for the Sahrani coast.

The treaties that establish leagues and confederations are the most significant treaties in the known world, for they create the political entities that are the primary actors in intercontinental commerce and diplomacy. The Accord of the River that established the governance of [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark), the founding agreements of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), and the charters of the merchant leagues are all treaties that have shaped the political order of the continents.

## The Negotiation and Conclusion of Treaties

The negotiation of treaties is conducted by representatives who are authorised to speak for their political entities. The representatives may be ambassadors, envoys, or other officials who are designated by the sovereign or the council to conduct negotiations. The authority of the representatives is established by credentials, documents that specify the scope of the representative's authority and that are presented to the other party at the commencement of negotiations.

The negotiation of a treaty typically begins with the exchange of proposals between the parties. The proposals are discussed, modified, and eventually agreed, and the agreed text is recorded in writing. The treaty is then signed by the representatives of the parties, indicating that the text is the agreed text and that the parties intend to be bound by its terms.

The signing of a treaty is not the final act in the process of concluding a treaty. In most legal systems, the treaty must be ratified by the sovereign or the council before it becomes binding. The ratification is the formal act by which the political entity confirms its intention to be bound by the treaty, and it is typically expressed in a document that is exchanged between the parties.

The requirement of ratification serves an important function in the treaty process. It ensures that the treaty has been reviewed by the sovereign or the council and that it is consistent with the interests and the legal order of the political entity. It also provides an opportunity for the political entity to consider the treaty carefully before committing to its terms.

## The Enforcement of Treaties

The enforcement of treaties is a matter of the greatest importance and the greatest difficulty in the legal order of the known world. There is no institution above the kingdoms and leagues that can compel compliance with treaty obligations, and the enforcement of treaties depends ultimately on the willingness of the parties to honour their commitments and on the consequences that follow from the breach of a treaty.

The most effective mechanism for the enforcement of treaties is the principle of reciprocity, by which each party performs its obligations in the expectation that the other party will perform its obligations in return. A party that breaches a treaty may find that the other party suspends or terminates its own performance, and the loss of the benefits of the treaty may be a sufficient deterrent to breach.

The second mechanism for the enforcement of treaties is the involvement of third parties who have an interest in the stability of the treaty order. The kingdoms and leagues of the known world have an interest in the maintenance of the treaty system, for the system of treaties is the foundation of the peace and commerce upon which their prosperity depends. A party that breaches a treaty may face the collective disapproval of the community of political entities, and the loss of reputation and trust may have consequences that extend beyond the treaty that has been breached.

The third mechanism for the enforcement of treaties is the provision for arbitration or adjudication of disputes arising from the interpretation or application of a treaty. Many treaties include provisions for the resolution of disputes by a panel of arbitrators chosen by the parties, and the decisions of the arbitrators are binding on the parties. The use of arbitration in treaty disputes is a common feature of the treaty practice of the known world, and it has contributed to the stability of the treaty system.

## Diplomatic Immunity

The principle of diplomatic immunity is one of the most important principles of treaty and diplomatic law. The principle holds that ambassadors and other diplomatic representatives are immune from the jurisdiction of the state to which they are accredited, and that they may not be arrested, detained, or subjected to legal proceedings in that state.

The principle of diplomatic immunity is founded on the necessity of allowing diplomatic representatives to perform their functions without interference from the state to which they are accredited. An ambassador who is subject to the jurisdiction of the host state could be prevented from communicating with the home state, could be arrested on false charges, or could be subjected to pressures that would compromise the ambassador's ability to represent the home state effectively.

The scope of diplomatic immunity varies in the practice of the known world. The broadest immunity is enjoyed by ambassadors and their families, who are immune from all legal proceedings in the host state. The immunity of other diplomatic personnel, including the staff of the embassy and their families, is narrower, extending only to acts performed in the course of their official duties.

The immunity of diplomatic representatives does not extend to acts that are unrelated to their official functions or that are criminal in nature. A diplomatic representative who commits a serious crime in the host state may be declared persona non grata and expelled from the state, and the home state may waive the immunity of the representative to allow the host state to prosecute.

## The Rights of Diplomatic Representatives

Diplomatic representatives enjoy certain rights that are essential to the performance of their functions. The most important of these rights is the right of communication with the home state. The ambassador has the right to send and receive messages, dispatches, and diplomatic bags without interference from the host state, and the ambassador has the right to use codes and ciphers for the protection of diplomatic communications.

The ambassador also has the right to immunity from the search and seizure of the embassy and the ambassador's residence. The premises of the embassy are inviolable, and the authorities of the host state may not enter the embassy without the consent of the ambassador. The archives and documents of the embassy are also inviolable, and they may not be seized or inspected by the host state.

The ambassador has the right to freedom of movement within the host state, subject to the laws and regulations of the state concerning the movement of foreign nationals. The ambassador also has the right to freedom of expression, and may not be punished or silenced for expressing the views of the home state.

## The Treaty Practice of the Major Powers

The treaty practice of the major powers of the known world reflects their different political traditions and their different interests in the treaty system. The Kingdom of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) has a highly developed treaty practice, reflecting its position as the leading commercial power of the eastern coast. The treaties of Aldreth are drafted with precision and care, and they include detailed provisions for the resolution of disputes by arbitration or by reference to the Aldreth Commercial Code.

The treaty practice of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) reflects the confederation's character as a union of settled cities and nomadic kindreds. The confederation's treaties are concluded by the council, but the representatives of the centaur kindreds participate in the negotiations and the treaties must be approved by the elders of the kindreds. The confederation's treaties emphasise the principles of reciprocity and mutual benefit that are the foundation of the confederation's own internal order.

The treaty practice of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) is shaped by the republic's focus on the spice trade. The republic's treaties are primarily commercial in nature, establishing the terms on which the republic's merchants may trade with other states and the rights of foreign merchants in the republic's markets. The republic's treaties include detailed provisions for the quality and authenticity of spices and for the resolution of disputes by the republic's commercial courts.

The treaty practice of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) is shaped by the customary law of the desert. The treaties of the Kharesh Tribes are concluded by the council of elders at the Sands' Assembly, and they are recorded in the oral traditions of the kindreds. The treaties of the Kharesh Tribes emphasise the obligations of hospitality and the rights of passage across the desert, and they are enforced by the collective authority of the kindreds.

## The Law of Diplomacy Among the Dwarven Realms

The treaty and diplomatic practice of the dwarven realms of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) is distinctive in its emphasis on permanence and formality. The treaties of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) are inscribed on metal plates that are kept in the great hall of the capital, and the terms of the treaties are recited at each council session as a reminder of the obligations that the holds have undertaken.

The diplomatic practice of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) reflects the martial traditions of the clans. The ambassadors of the Frostwall Clans are chosen from among the warriors of the clans, and they are expected to conduct themselves with the same honour and courage that they would display in battle. The treaties of the Frostwall Clans include provisions for the exchange of hostages as a guarantee of performance, a practice that reflects the martial values of the clans and their understanding of the importance of personal honour in the maintenance of treaty obligations.

## The Decline and Termination of Treaties

Treaties may decline or terminate in a variety of ways. The most common form of termination is by agreement of the parties, who may agree to terminate a treaty that has served its purpose or that is no longer relevant to their interests. The termination of a treaty by agreement is typically recorded in a new treaty or in an exchange of notes between the parties.

A treaty may also terminate by the performance of its terms. A treaty that requires the payment of compensation or the delivery of goods is terminated when the payment or delivery has been completed. A treaty that establishes a relationship or an institution may endure for as long as the relationship or institution continues to exist, but it may be terminated by the agreement of the parties or by the dissolution of the institution.

A treaty may be terminated by the breach of its terms by one of the parties. The party that is not in breach may suspend or terminate the treaty in response to the breach, and the breach may also give rise to a claim for compensation or for other remedies. The consequences of breach are determined by the terms of the treaty and by the customary law of treaties.

The enduring significance of treaty and diplomatic law in the known world lies in its function as the framework for the relations between political entities. The system of treaties and diplomatic practice allows the kingdoms, leagues, and confederations to conduct their affairs with a degree of certainty and predictability that would not be possible in its absence. The development of treaty and diplomatic law across the centuries has been driven by the needs of peace and commerce, and the principles that have emerged from this development are the foundation of the international order of the known world.
