## The Means of Settling Conflicts Outside the Courts

The resolution of disputes is a function that every society must perform if it is to maintain order and stability. The formal courts of the kingdoms and leagues are the most visible institutions of dispute resolution, but they are not the only institutions, and in many communities they are not the most important. The customary practices of mediation, arbitration, and negotiation that have developed across the known world provide alternative means of resolving disputes that are often faster, cheaper, and more satisfactory to the parties than litigation in the formal courts.

The methods of dispute resolution that are employed in a particular community reflect the values and traditions of that community and the nature of the disputes that most commonly arise within it. The merchant communities of the eastern cities have developed sophisticated methods of commercial arbitration that are tailored to the needs of merchants. The nomadic peoples of the desert and the grasslands have developed methods of mediation that are grounded in the customary law of hospitality and honour. The settled farmers of the river valleys have developed methods of negotiation that reflect the practical necessities of agricultural life.

## Mediation in the Eastern Kingdoms

Mediation is the process by which a neutral third party assists the parties to a dispute in reaching a voluntary agreement. The mediator does not impose a decision on the parties but helps them to communicate, to understand each other's interests, and to find a solution that both can accept. Mediation is the most common method of dispute resolution in the rural communities of the eastern kingdoms, where the formal courts are distant and the cost of litigation is prohibitive for most people.

In the Kingdom of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), mediation is the primary method of resolving disputes arising from the management of water and the maintenance of dykes and channels. The water commissioners, who are appointed by the crown to oversee the water law, serve as mediators in disputes between landowners and communities over the use of water and the maintenance of water works. The commissioners bring the parties together, hear their concerns, and help them to reach an agreement that is consistent with the water law and the needs of the community.

The mediation process in Vael is informal and flexible. The commissioner meets with the parties separately and together, exploring the interests and concerns of each side and proposing possible solutions. The commissioner does not have the authority to impose a decision, but the commissioner's knowledge of the water law and the practical realities of water management gives the commissioner's proposals considerable weight. Most disputes that are referred to mediation are resolved without recourse to the water courts.

## Mediation Among the Nomadic Peoples

Mediation is the primary method of dispute resolution among the nomadic peoples of the known world, for the formal courts of the settled kingdoms are inaccessible to the nomads who range across the deserts and grasslands. The elders of the kindreds serve as mediators in disputes between members of the same kindred and between members of different kindreds, and the mediation process is grounded in the customary law of hospitality and honour.

Among the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) of the Sahrani desert, mediation is conducted by the elders of the kindreds, who are respected for their wisdom and their knowledge of the customary law of the desert. The elders bring the parties together at a neutral location, such as a well or a camp that is not associated with either party, and they guide a discussion of the dispute and the possible means of resolving it.

The mediation process among the Kharesh Tribes is accompanied by rituals that reinforce the solemnity of the occasion and the obligation of the parties to participate in good faith. The parties share salt, a symbol of the hospitality that binds the community together, and they exchange promises to abide by the decision that the elders reach. The elders then propose a solution, which the parties are expected to accept. The solution typically involves the payment of compensation from one party to the other, the performance of a service, or the exchange of goods.

Among the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds of the grasslands, mediation is conducted by the elders of the kindreds at the stone circles that mark the gathering places of the plains. The elders hear the parties, consult with the other members of the kindreds, and propose a solution that is consistent with the customary law of the grasslands and the seasonal needs of the community. The mediation process among the centaurs is quicker and less formal than among the Kharesh Tribes, reflecting the more mobile way of life of the centaurs and the need to resolve disputes before the kindreds move to new grazing grounds.

## Arbitration in the Merchant Communities

Arbitration is the process by which the parties to a dispute submit their disagreement to a neutral third party or panel for a binding decision. Arbitration is distinguished from mediation by the fact that the arbitrator has the authority to impose a decision on the parties, and the decision of the arbitrator is binding and enforceable.

Arbitration is the preferred method of dispute resolution in the merchant communities of the eastern kingdoms and the Sahrani city-states, where the needs of commerce require that disputes be resolved quickly and by persons who understand the customs and practices of the trade. The [Aldreth Commercial Code](/content/law/aldreth-commercial-code) requires that commercial disputes be submitted to arbitration before they may be brought to the commercial courts, and the code provides a detailed framework for the conduct of arbitration proceedings.

The arbitration panel under the Aldreth Commercial Code consists of three arbitrators chosen by the parties. Each party chooses one arbitrator, and the two arbitrators so chosen select a third arbitrator to preside over the panel. The arbitrators are typically merchants or former merchants who have experience in the trade that is the subject of the dispute, and they are expected to bring their practical knowledge to the resolution of the dispute.

The arbitration hearing is conducted in a less formal manner than a court proceeding. The parties present their evidence and arguments to the panel, and the panel asks questions and seeks clarification. The panel may also consult experts in the trade or practice at issue in the dispute, and it may request additional information from the parties.

The decision of the arbitration panel is binding on the parties and may be enforced by the commercial courts. The decision is typically rendered in writing, with the reasons for the decision stated briefly. The decision is not subject to appeal, except on the ground that the arbitrators exceeded their authority or that the decision was procured by fraud or corruption.

## Arbitration in the Thessaran Merchant Courts

The [Thessaran Merchant Courts](/content/law/thessaran-merchant-courts) have developed a system of arbitration that is integrated with the court system of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league). The league encourages the use of arbitration for the resolution of commercial disputes, and the merchant courts have the authority to order the parties to submit to arbitration if the court determines that arbitration would be more efficient and more appropriate than litigation.

The arbitration system of the Thessaran League is administered by the league's admiralty court, which maintains a register of qualified arbitrators who are available to hear disputes. The arbitrators are chosen by the parties from the register, and the arbitration is conducted according to the rules established by the admiralty court. The decision of the arbitrator is binding on the parties and may be enforced by the admiralty court.

The Thessaran arbitration system has been successful in resolving a large number of commercial disputes without the need for litigation, and the league's experience with arbitration has influenced the development of arbitration practices in other regions. The league's rules for arbitration have been adopted or adapted by many of the merchant communities of the eastern coast, and the register of qualified arbitrators maintained by the admiralty court is consulted by merchants throughout the region.

## Negotiation and Customary Settlement

Negotiation is the most common method of dispute resolution in every society of the known world, for most disputes are resolved by the parties themselves without the involvement of a third party. The parties to a dispute may negotiate directly with each other, or they may be represented by family members, friends, or advisors who assist them in reaching an agreement.

The negotiation process is governed by the customs and traditions of the community in which it takes place. In the rural communities of the eastern kingdoms, the negotiation of a dispute is typically conducted by the heads of the families involved, who meet at a neutral location and discuss the dispute until a settlement is reached. The settlement is typically recorded in a written agreement that is witnessed by the elders of the community.

Among the nomadic peoples, the negotiation of a dispute is conducted by the elders of the kindreds, who represent the interests of their kindreds in the negotiation. The negotiation is conducted according to the customs of the desert or the grasslands, and the settlement is typically expressed in the form of an exchange of gifts or the payment of compensation.

## The Role of Honour and Reputation in Dispute Resolution

Honour and reputation play a significant role in the resolution of disputes in many communities of the known world. The parties to a dispute are concerned not only with the material outcome of the dispute but also with their honour and reputation in the community, and the resolution of a dispute often involves the restoration of the honour of the party who has been wronged.

The concern for honour is most apparent in the customary law of the Kharesh Tribes, where the blood debt that is paid in compensation for a killing or a serious injury is measured not only by the material value of the compensation but also by the honour that is restored to the victim's kindred by the payment. The honour that is restored by the payment of the blood debt is as important as the material compensation, and the failure to pay the blood debt is regarded as a continuing dishonour that may lead to further conflict.

The concern for reputation is most apparent in the merchant communities of the eastern cities, where the reputation of a merchant for honesty and fair dealing is the foundation of the merchant's commercial success. A merchant who is found to have acted dishonestly in a dispute may be excluded from the markets of the city, and the loss of reputation may have consequences that are more severe than any penalty that the court could impose.

## The Integration of Dispute Resolution Methods

The methods of dispute resolution that are employed in the known world are not mutually exclusive. The same dispute may be the subject of negotiation, mediation, and arbitration at different stages of its resolution, and the parties may move from one method to another as the circumstances require. The integration of dispute resolution methods is a common feature of the legal systems of the known world, and it allows the parties to choose the method that is best suited to the nature of their dispute and the relationship between them.

The water law of Vael provides a model of the integration of dispute resolution methods. A dispute arising from the management of water is first addressed through negotiation between the parties, with the assistance of the water commissioner if necessary. If negotiation fails, the dispute is referred to mediation by the water commissioner, who attempts to help the parties reach an agreement. If mediation fails, the dispute is referred to the water court, which hears the case and renders a decision.

The integration of dispute resolution methods is also a feature of the commercial law of the Aldreth Commercial Code. A commercial dispute is first addressed through negotiation between the parties, then through arbitration by a panel of merchants, and finally through litigation in the commercial courts if arbitration fails. The integration of methods ensures that the parties have every opportunity to resolve their dispute without the need for litigation, and that litigation is used only as a last resort.

The enduring significance of dispute resolution in the known world lies in its function as the means by which social order is maintained and conflicts are resolved without violence. The methods of mediation, arbitration, and negotiation that have developed across the centuries are among the most important achievements of the legal systems of the known world, for they provide the means by which persons and communities can resolve their differences peacefully and move forward with their lives.
