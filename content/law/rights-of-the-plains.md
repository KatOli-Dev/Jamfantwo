## The Founding Principles of the Confederation

The Rights of the Plains are the fundamental principles upon which the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) is founded, a set of unwritten but binding rules that no council may set aside and that define the relationship between the confederation and its members. The Rights are not a code in the sense of the [Aldreth Commercial Code](/content/law/aldreth-commercial-code) or the [Vael Water Law](/content/law/vael-water-law), for they are not written in a single document or compiled in a systematic form. They are rather a collection of principles, each arising from a specific dispute or grievance that the confederation resolved by extending rather than restricting the voice of its members, and each affirmed by the practice of successive councils over the centuries of the confederation's existence.

The Rights of the Plains are recited at the opening of every council session, at every gathering of the Open Grass Assembly, and at every meeting of the kindred fires where the affairs of the confederation are discussed. The recitation is performed by the eldest representative present, who speaks the Rights from memory, and the assembled members listen in silence until the recitation is complete. The recitation is a reminder of the principles that bind the confederation together and of the limits that those principles place upon the authority of the council and the power of the majority.

## The Right of Speech at the Open Grass Assembly

The first and most fundamental of the Rights of the Plains is the right of every free adult member of the confederation to speak at the Open Grass Assembly, the gathering that precedes each council meeting and that is open to any member of the confederation who wishes to attend. The right of speech is not limited to representatives or elders or property holders or any other category of person. Every free adult who has reached the age of majority, regardless of species, gender, wealth, or place of residence within the confederation, may stand before the assembly and speak on any matter that concerns the affairs of the confederation.

The right of speech is exercised in a specific order at each assembly. The speakers are heard in the order in which they request to speak, and each speaker is allotted a period of time determined by the council according to the number of speakers who have requested to be heard. The speaker stands in the open space before the assembled representatives, and the representatives are required to listen without interruption until the speaker has finished. The council may not silence a speaker who exceeds the allotted time, but subsequent speakers are given the same latitude, and the assembly as a whole decides through its attention or lack of attention whether a speaker has spoken too long.

The right of speech is the foundation of the political order of the confederation, for it ensures that every voice may be heard and every concern may be brought before the council. The right of speech is also the mechanism by which the council is held to account, for any citizen may question a representative about decisions taken at the previous council or about the conduct of the representative since the last meeting. The right of speech is thus both a participatory right and a check upon the power of the representatives.

## The Right of Choice of Representatives

The second of the Rights of the Plains is the right of every member community of the confederation to choose its own representative to the council by whatever method the community prefers. The confederation does not prescribe a uniform method for the selection of representatives, for the communities that make up the confederation are diverse in their customs and traditions, and the imposition of a uniform method would violate the principle of community autonomy that is one of the foundations of the confederation.

The settled cities of the confederation choose their representatives by popular vote of all free adults in the community who have reached the age of majority, regardless of species, gender, or wealth. The elections are held at intervals specified by each city's own laws, and the procedures for voting and counting votes are determined by each city's own customs. Among the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds, the elders who serve as representatives must command the consent of their kindreds to hold their seats, and the elders are chosen through the deliberative process of the kindred fires, where every adult member of the kindred may speak and vote.

The right of choice is the foundation of the representative character of the council. A representative who does not command the confidence of the community that the representative represents cannot long hold the seat, for the community may at any time choose a different representative by the same method by which the original choice was made. The right of choice thus ensures that the council is composed of representatives who enjoy the support of the communities they represent.

## The Right of Petition

The third of the Rights of the Plains is the right of petition, by which any citizen of the confederation may submit a written concern to the council for its consideration. The right of petition may be exercised by any free adult member of the confederation, and the petition may address any matter that falls within the authority of the council. The petition must be submitted in writing, and it must be signed by the petitioner or, if the petitioner cannot write, by a witness who can attest to the petitioner's intention.

The council is required to consider every petition that is submitted to it in accordance with the rules established by the council for the receipt of petitions. The council must respond to the petition within a reasonable time, either by granting the relief requested, by denying the relief with an explanation of the reasons for the denial, or by referring the petition to a committee for further study. The council may not ignore a petition or refuse to consider it, and a council that fails to respond to a petition within a reasonable time may be challenged before a panel of elders drawn from communities not party to the dispute.

The right of petition is the means by which citizens who cannot attend the Open Grass Assembly in person may still bring their concerns before the council. The right of petition is especially valued by the centaur kindreds, whose nomadic way of life makes it difficult for their members to attend every assembly in person. Petitions submitted by members of the centaur kindreds are given particular attention by the council, for the council recognises that the nomadic members of the confederation face challenges that the settled members do not.

## The Right of Recall

The fourth of the Rights of the Plains is the right of recall, by which a community may replace a representative who has lost the trust of the community before the term for which the representative was chosen has expired. The right of recall may be exercised by any member community of the confederation, and the procedure for recall is determined by each community according to its own customs and traditions.

Among the settled cities, the right of recall is exercised by popular vote of the free adults of the community, initiated by a petition signed by a specified number of citizens. If the vote in favour of recall reaches the threshold established by the city's laws, the representative is removed from the council and a new election is held to choose a replacement. Among the centaur kindreds, the right of recall is exercised through the deliberative process of the kindred fires, and a kindred that has lost confidence in its elder may set aside that elder at any gathering and choose another.

The right of recall is the ultimate check upon the power of the representatives. A representative who votes against the interests of the community, who fails to represent the community's views faithfully, or who acts in a manner that brings the community into disrepute may be recalled by the community before the term expires. The right of recall thus ensures that the representatives remain accountable to the communities they represent and that the council does not become a body of officials who are insulated from the consequences of their decisions.

## The Right of Challenge

The fifth of the Rights of the Plains is the right of challenge, by which any council decision found to violate the Rights of the Plains may be challenged before a panel of elders drawn from communities not party to the dispute. The right of challenge may be exercised by any member community of the confederation or by any citizen who can demonstrate that a council decision has directly affected the citizen's rights or interests.

The panel of elders that hears the challenge is drawn from communities that have no direct interest in the dispute, and the panel is composed of elders who are recognised for their knowledge of the Rights and their experience in the governance of the confederation. The panel hears arguments from the challenger and from the council, and the panel decides whether the council decision violates the Rights. If the panel finds that the decision violates the Rights, the decision is void and the council must reconsider the matter in a manner consistent with the Rights.

The right of challenge is the mechanism by which the Rights of the Plains are enforced. The Rights themselves contain no provision for their own enforcement, for they are principles that depend upon the willingness of the confederation to respect them. The right of challenge provides a means by which a community or citizen who believes that the Rights have been violated may obtain a hearing before an impartial panel and may obtain a remedy if the violation is confirmed.

## The Historical Development of the Rights

The Rights of the Plains were not established at a single moment or by a single decision. They developed gradually over the history of the confederation, each addition arising from a specific dispute or grievance that the confederation resolved by extending rather than restricting the voice of its members. The first of the Rights to be recognised was the right of speech, which was established in the early years of the confederation when a group of farmers from the southern plains demanded the right to address the council directly rather than through their representatives. The council of that time, recognising that the grievance was just, granted the right and established the Open Grass Assembly as the institution through which the right would be exercised.

The right of choice was established in the following century, when a dispute arose over the selection of a representative who had been imposed on a community by the council rather than chosen by the community itself. The community refused to recognise the representative, and the council, after a period of tension and negotiation, conceded the right of each community to choose its own representative. The right of petition was established in similar circumstances, when a centaur kindred that could not attend the Open Grass Assembly because of the distance of its summer grazing grounds submitted a written concern that the council initially refused to consider. The kindred persisted, and the council eventually recognised that the written petition was a necessary supplement to the oral speech of the assembly.

The right of recall was established in the aftermath of a dispute over a representative who had betrayed the interests of the community that had chosen him. The community demanded the right to replace the representative before the term expired, and the council, after debating the matter, recognised the right. The right of challenge was the last of the Rights to be established, arising from a dispute over a council decision that some communities believed violated the spirit of the Rights even though it did not violate the letter of any specific provision. The council established the panel of elders as the institution through which such challenges could be heard and resolved.

## The Rights and the Wider Legal Order

The Rights of the Plains are the foundation of the political order of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), but they are not the whole of the confederation's law. The confederation also has a body of written and customary law governing matters such as grazing rights, trade, defence, and the management of the grasslands. The Rights of the Plains stand above this body of law, limiting the authority of the council and the scope of the law that the council may make.

The Rights of the Plains have influenced the development of legal and political institutions beyond the borders of the confederation. The principles of speech, choice, petition, recall, and challenge have been adopted or adapted by other political entities that seek to balance the authority of government with the rights of citizens. The [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) has incorporated elements of the Rights of the Plains into its civil code, and the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) have adopted similar principles in their customary law. The influence of the Rights of the Plains extends across the known world, and the principles that were first developed by the farmers and herders of the grasslands continue to shape the development of law and governance in distant lands.
