## The Law of the Sea Across the Known World

Maritime law governs the conduct of persons and vessels upon the seas, the rights and obligations of shipowners, masters, crews, and passengers, and the resolution of disputes arising from maritime commerce and navigation. The seas of the known world are the highways along which the commerce of the continents flows, and the law that governs them is essential to the prosperity of every kingdom, league, and city-state that depends upon maritime trade.

The maritime law of the known world is not a single body of rules but a collection of customs, codes, and court decisions that have developed in different regions and that reflect the conditions of the waters to which they apply. The maritime law of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea), codified in the [Nordhaven Sea Code](/content/law/nordhaven-sea-code), reflects the harsh conditions of northern waters and the practical concerns of the seafaring communities of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) coast. The maritime law of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) reflects the commercial character of the eastern trade and the influence of the [Aldreth Commercial Code](/content/law/aldreth-commercial-code).

## The Admiralty Courts and Their Jurisdiction

The admiralty courts are the institutions through which maritime law is administered and disputes arising from maritime commerce are resolved. The admiralty courts are distinct from the ordinary courts of the kingdoms and leagues, and they have jurisdiction over a wide range of matters, including salvage, collision, shipwreck, and disputes between the owners, masters, and crews of vessels.

The admiralty court of [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) is the oldest and most respected of the admiralty courts of the known world. The court sits in the port council hall of the capital under the presidency of a judge appointed by the king, and its jurisdiction extends to all matters arising under the Nordhaven Sea Code and to all disputes arising from maritime commerce in the waters of the Stormfall Sea. The decisions of the Nordhaven admiralty court are cited as precedent in maritime courts throughout the known world, and the court's influence on the development of maritime law is second to none.

The admiralty court of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) sits in [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) under a presidency that rotates with the office of the Thessar, the presiding officer of the league council. The court has developed a body of maritime law that draws upon the Aldreth Commercial Code, the customs of the eastern maritime communities, and the decisions of the Nordhaven admiralty court. The Thessaran admiralty court is recognised as a leading authority on maritime law in the Auric Ocean, and its decisions are enforced by the collective authority of the league.

The admiralty court of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) sits in [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and administers a body of maritime law that reflects the conditions of the Sahrani coast and the influence of the Sahrani commercial traditions. The court has jurisdiction over disputes arising from maritime commerce in the waters of the southern Auric Ocean, and its decisions are enforced by the collective fleet of the league.

## The Law of Salvage

The law of salvage is one of the most important branches of maritime law, governing the right of those who recover a vessel or its cargo from peril to receive a reward proportional to the value of the property saved and the risk incurred in saving it. The law of salvage is founded on the principle that the prospect of reward is necessary to encourage mariners to risk their lives and vessels in the rescue of ships in distress, and that the reward should be determined according to the circumstances of each case.

The Nordhaven Sea Code provides a detailed framework for the determination of salvage awards. The code specifies the factors that the admiralty court must consider in determining the award, including the degree of peril to which the vessel and its cargo were exposed, the skill and effort of the salvors, the value of the property risked by the salvors, and the success of the salvage operation. The code also specifies the maximum award that may be granted, which is one-half of the value of the property saved.

The maritime law of the Thessaran League follows similar principles, but the maximum award is limited to one-third of the value of the property saved. The lower maximum reflects the different conditions of the Auric Ocean, where the waters are generally less hazardous than those of the Stormfall Sea and where the volume of shipping is such that salvage opportunities are more frequent.

The law of salvage in the Northern Coast League follows the same principles as the Thessaran League, but the maximum award is determined by the admiralty court according to the circumstances of each case. The court has the authority to grant an award of up to one-half of the value of the property saved in cases of exceptional peril, but awards of more than one-third are rare.

## The Law of Collision

The law of collision governs the liability of vessels that collide with each other or with fixed objects in the sea. The Nordhaven Sea Code establishes the principle that liability for damage arising from a collision is determined by fault, and that each vessel that is at fault bears a share of the liability proportional to its degree of fault.

The code sets out the rules of navigation that vessels are required to follow to avoid collisions. The rules include the requirement that vessels keep to the right when approaching each other head-on, that a vessel overtaking another must keep clear of the vessel being overtaken, and that vessels display lights at night in specified positions and colours. The rules are enforced by the admiralty court, and a vessel that violates the rules and causes a collision is presumed to be at fault.

The maritime law of the Thessaran League follows the same principles, but the league has also adopted a system of compulsory pilotage for vessels entering the harbours of the member cities. The pilots are licensed by the league and are required to guide vessels through the approaches to the harbours, reducing the risk of collision in the congested waters of the port approaches.

The maritime law of the Northern Coast League follows the same principles, and the league has also adopted a system of coastal watchtowers that signal the approach of vessels to each other, reducing the risk of collision in the waters off the Sahrani coast. The watchtowers are spaced at intervals along the shore, and they communicate with each other by polished-metal mirrors during the day and oil lamps at night.

## The Law of Shipwreck and Wreckage

The law of shipwreck and wreckage governs the treatment of vessels and cargo that are lost at sea or cast ashore. The Nordhaven Sea Code establishes the principle that the crown has the right to all wreckage that is not claimed by the owner within a specified period, but that the crown also has the duty to make reasonable efforts to notify the owner of the wreckage and to preserve the wreckage for the owner's benefit.

The code requires that a person who finds wreckage on the shore or in the coastal waters report the finding to the nearest port council within a specified period. The finder who reports the wreckage is entitled to a salvage award if the wreckage is claimed by the owner, and the finder who fails to report the wreckage is liable to the crown for the value of the wreckage and may be subject to criminal penalties.

The code also requires that the bodies of persons who die in shipwrecks be treated with respect and be given a proper burial. The code prohibits the plundering of shipwrecks and the taking of property from the bodies of the dead, and it imposes severe penalties on those who violate this prohibition.

The maritime law of the Thessaran League follows similar principles, but the league has also established a system of wreck commissioners who are responsible for the recovery and preservation of wreckage. The commissioners are appointed by the league council and are empowered to hire salvors, to supervise the recovery of wreckage, and to determine salvage awards.

## The Rights and Obligations of Seafarers

The maritime law of the known world establishes the rights and obligations of the masters, crews, and passengers of vessels. The Nordhaven Sea Code requires that the master of a vessel exercise due diligence to ensure the safety of the vessel and the persons on board, and that the master provide proper food, water, and medical care to the crew. The code also requires that the master pay the crew the wages that have been agreed, and that the master provide the crew with a safe and healthy working environment.

The code establishes the right of the crew to refuse to sail if the vessel is unseaworthy or if the master is incompetent. The crew also has the right to demand that the vessel put into port if a member of the crew is seriously ill or injured and requires medical attention that cannot be provided at sea.

The code also regulates the treatment of passengers, requiring that the master provide passengers with proper accommodation, food, and water, and that the master ensure the safety of passengers during the voyage. The master is liable for injury or loss suffered by a passenger as a result of the master's negligence or the negligence of the crew.

The maritime law of the Thessaran League and the Northern Coast League follows similar principles, with variations that reflect the different conditions of the waters in which the vessels operate. The rights of seafarers are protected by the admiralty courts of the leagues, and a master who violates the rights of the crew or passengers is subject to penalties imposed by the court.

## General Average and the Sharing of Maritime Risk

The principle of general average is one of the oldest and most widely recognised principles of maritime law in the known world. The principle holds that the losses incurred for the common safety of a vessel and its cargo are shared proportionally among all parties with an interest in the voyage. If the master of a vessel orders cargo to be jettisoned to lighten the ship in a storm, the owners of the cargo that is saved contribute to the value of the cargo that is lost, each paying in proportion to the value of their own cargo.

The principle of general average is recognised in the Nordhaven Sea Code, the Aldreth Commercial Code, and the maritime law of the Thessaran League and the Northern Coast League. The principle is applied by the admiralty courts of these jurisdictions, and the decisions of the courts on general average are cited as precedent throughout the known world.

The principle of general average is the foundation of the system of marine insurance that is essential to long-distance commerce in the known world. The insurers who issue policies of marine insurance rely upon the principle of general average to allocate losses among the parties with an interest in the voyage, and the predictability of the allocation allows the insurers to calculate the premiums that they charge for the insurance.

## Maritime Insurance

Marine insurance is the practice by which a merchant transfers the risk of loss to an insurer in return for the payment of a premium. The Aldreth Commercial Code provides a comprehensive framework for the regulation of marine insurance, and the code's provisions have been adopted or adapted by most of the maritime jurisdictions of the known world.

The contract of marine insurance is governed by the principle of utmost good faith, which requires that the merchant seeking insurance disclose to the insurer every fact material to the risk. A failure to disclose a material fact renders the contract voidable at the option of the insurer. The insurer who issues a policy of marine insurance undertakes to indemnify the merchant against losses caused by perils of the sea, including storms, shipwreck, collision, piracy, and fire.

The regulation of marine insurance in the Nordhaven Sea Code follows similar principles, with the addition of provisions specific to the conditions of the Stormfall Sea. The code requires that insurers maintain reserves sufficient to pay claims arising from the policies they have issued, and that insurers submit to the supervision of the admiralty court.

The regulation of marine insurance in the Thessaran League is governed by the league's commercial code, which is based on the Aldreth Commercial Code. The league has also established a system of mutual insurance, by which groups of merchants contribute to a common fund that is used to compensate members who suffer losses at sea. The system of mutual insurance is administered by the league's admiralty court, and it has proven to be an effective means of spreading risk among the merchant community.

The enduring significance of maritime law in the known world lies in its function as the legal framework for the commerce that connects the continents. The seas are the highways along which the goods of the world are carried, and the law that governs them ensures that those who risk their lives and property in the service of maritime commerce are treated fairly and justly. The development of maritime law across the centuries has been driven by the needs of commerce and the practical experience of seafarers, and the principles that have emerged from this development are among the most important legal achievements of the known world.
