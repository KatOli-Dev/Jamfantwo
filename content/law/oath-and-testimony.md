## The Binding Power of the Spoken Word

The oath is the foundation of legal proceedings in the known world, the means by which the truth of testimony is established and the reliability of evidence is assured. The practice of oath-taking is universal across the continents, though the forms of the oath and the consequences of oath-breaking vary from culture to culture. Every legal system, whether codified or customary, recognises the oath as the most solemn and binding commitment that a person can make, and every system provides for the punishment of those who violate their oaths.

The oath serves two functions in legal proceedings. The first function is to bind the conscience of the witness, to remind the witness of the importance of telling the truth, and to invoke the consequences that follow from falsehood. The second function is to assure the court and the parties that the testimony of the witness is truthful, to provide a basis for the court to rely upon the testimony in reaching its decision.

## The Forms of the Oath in the Eastern Kingdoms

In the kingdoms of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), the oath is administered by the court at the commencement of the witness's testimony. The witness raises the right hand and repeats the words of the oath after the judge or the clerk of the court. The words of the oath vary from kingdom to kingdom, but they typically include a promise to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth, and an invocation of the consequences of falsehood.

The oath in the Kingdom of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) is administered in the name of the king, who is the source of justice in the realm. The witness swears by the king's crown and by the witness's own honour to tell the truth in the matter before the court. The oath is recorded in the records of the court, and the witness who is found to have testified falsely is subject to penalties for perjury.

The oath in the Kingdom of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) is administered in the name of the river, reflecting the central role that the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael) plays in the life of the kingdom. The witness swears by the waters of the Vael and by the witness's own place in the community to tell the truth. The river oath is regarded as particularly binding, for the river is the source of the kingdom's prosperity and the symbol of the continuity of the community.

## The Forms of the Oath in the Sahrani City-States

In the city-states of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), the oath is administered according to the customs of each city and the traditions of the religious communities that are influential in the region. The [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) administers the oath in the name of the council and the republic, and the witness swears by the spice trade and the prosperity of the city to tell the truth.

The oath in Amurra is accompanied by a ritual that underscores its solemnity. The witness places the right hand on a container of spices that have been certified by the guild of the spice merchants, symbolising the connection between truth-telling and the authenticity of the spices that are the foundation of the republic's prosperity. The witness who testifies falsely is regarded not only as a perjurer but as a traitor to the spice trade, and the penalties for false testimony include permanent exclusion from the markets of the republic.

## The Forms of the Oath Among the Nomadic Peoples

Among the nomadic peoples of the known world, the oath is administered by the elders of the kindreds and is accompanied by rituals that reflect the values and traditions of the community. The [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) of the Sahrani desert administer the oath in the presence of the elders, and the witness swears by the ancestors of the kindred and by the code of hospitality that is the foundation of the social order of the desert.

The oath among the Kharesh Tribes is accompanied by the sharing of salt, a symbol of the hospitality that binds the community together. The witness takes a piece of salt from the elder who administers the oath and places it on the tongue, symbolising the witness's acceptance of the obligations of truth-telling and the witness's willingness to suffer the consequences of falsehood. The salt oath is regarded as the most binding commitment that a member of the Kharesh Tribes can make, and the violation of the salt oath is one of the most serious offences under the customary law of the desert.

Among the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds of the grasslands, the oath is administered at the stone circles that mark the gathering places of the kindreds. The witness swears by the stones and by the grass of the plains, and the witness places the right hand on one of the stones while speaking the oath. The stone oath is regarded as binding on the witness and on the witness's kindred, and the violation of the stone oath may have consequences that extend beyond the witness to the family and the kindred.

## The Consequences of Perjury

Perjury, the giving of false testimony under oath, is punished severely in every legal system of the known world. The severity of the punishment reflects the importance of the oath to the functioning of the legal system and the need to deter witnesses from testifying falsely.

In the kingdoms of Aethelar, perjury is a criminal offence punishable by fines, imprisonment, and, in the most serious cases, by the forfeiture of the perjurer's goods and the marking of the perjurer's face with a hot iron to warn others of the perjurer's unreliability. The penalty for perjury in Aldreth is determined by the nature of the case in which the false testimony was given: a perjurer who testified falsely in a capital case may be sentenced to death, while a perjurer who testified falsely in a commercial dispute may be fined and barred from testifying in any future proceeding.

In the Amurra Republic, the penalty for perjury is permanent exclusion from the markets of the republic and the forfeiture of the perjurer's goods. The perjurer is also required to pay compensation to any person who suffered loss as a result of the false testimony, and the name of the perjurer is recorded in the register of dishonoured persons that is maintained by the council.

Among the Kharesh Tribes, the penalty for perjury is the payment of compensation to the person who suffered loss as a result of the false testimony, and the expulsion of the perjurer from the kindred. The perjurer who is expelled from the kindred loses the protection of the code of hospitality and is condemned to a life outside the community, a fate that is regarded as worse than death in the harsh conditions of the desert.

## The Evaluation of Testimony

The evaluation of testimony is the function of the judge, the council, or the elders who preside over legal proceedings. The evaluation of testimony involves the assessment of the credibility of the witness, the consistency of the testimony with other evidence, and the probability of the facts to which the witness has testified.

The courts of the eastern kingdoms have developed rules for the evaluation of testimony that reflect centuries of experience in the administration of justice. The most important of these rules is the requirement that testimony be corroborated by other evidence in certain types of cases. In a case involving a serious crime, the testimony of a single witness is not sufficient to establish guilt, and the prosecution must produce additional evidence to support the witness's account.

The courts also consider the character of the witness in evaluating the testimony. A witness who has a reputation for honesty and integrity is given greater weight than a witness who has a reputation for dishonesty or who has been convicted of perjury in the past. The courts may also consider the relationship of the witness to the parties, the interest of the witness in the outcome of the case, and the demeanour of the witness while testifying.

## The Role of Written Evidence

Written evidence plays an increasingly important role in legal proceedings in the known world, particularly in commercial cases where the transactions at issue are complex and the testimony of witnesses may be unavailable or unreliable. The Aldreth Commercial Code provides that written contracts, bills of exchange, and other commercial documents are admissible as evidence in the commercial courts, and that the documents may be used to prove the facts that they record.

The code also provides that the register of the commercial courts, in which the decisions of the courts and the records of commercial transactions are kept, is admissible as evidence of the facts recorded in it. The register is maintained by the clerk of the court, and the entries in the register are presumed to be accurate unless the contrary is proved.

The use of written evidence is less developed in the customary law of the nomadic peoples, where the oral tradition is the foundation of the legal order and where the testimony of witnesses is the primary means of proving facts. The Kharesh Tribes do, however, recognise the validity of written documents in their dealings with the settled peoples, and a written contract that has been witnessed by elders of the kindreds is enforceable in the customary law of the desert.

## Testimony in the Merchant Courts

The merchant courts of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) have developed distinctive rules for the taking of testimony that reflect the needs of commercial litigation. The merchant courts place greater emphasis on written evidence than the ordinary courts, for the nature of commercial transactions is such that the written record is often more reliable than the oral testimony of witnesses.

The merchant courts also permit the testimony of experts, persons who have specialised knowledge of the trade or practice at issue in the case. The expert witness may testify about the customs of the trade, the standards of quality that are expected, and the practices that are followed by merchants in similar transactions. The testimony of expert witnesses is given considerable weight by the merchant courts, for the judges of the merchant courts are themselves merchants who understand the value of specialised knowledge.

The merchant courts also permit the testimony of witnesses who are not members of the league, including foreign merchants and travellers who may have knowledge of the facts at issue in the case. The testimony of foreign witnesses is taken on oath administered according to the customs of the witness's own community, and the merchant courts have the authority to compel the attendance of foreign witnesses who are within the jurisdiction of the league.

## The Oath in Dispute Resolution

The oath also plays a role in the resolution of disputes outside the formal court system. The mediation and arbitration processes that are common in the customary law of the nomadic peoples and the merchant communities of the cities often begin with the taking of an oath by the parties, who swear to participate in the process in good faith and to accept the decision of the mediator or arbitrator.

The oath that is taken at the commencement of a mediation or arbitration is different from the oath that is taken by a witness in court. The oath of good faith is a promise to participate honestly and openly in the process, to disclose all relevant facts, and to accept the decision that is reached. The oath of good faith is the foundation of the mediation and arbitration process, and the party who violates the oath may be subject to the same penalties as a witness who commits perjury.

## The Future of Oath and Testimony

The role of the oath and testimony in legal proceedings continues to evolve as the legal systems of the known world develop and adapt to changing conditions. The increasing use of written evidence, the growth of the merchant courts, and the development of new forms of dispute resolution have all affected the way in which testimony is taken and evaluated.

Yet the oath remains the foundation of legal proceedings in every legal system of the known world. The spoken word, solemnly pledged before the court, the council, or the elders, continues to be the most reliable means of establishing the truth in legal proceedings, and the consequences of perjury continue to be among the most serious that any legal system can impose. The oath is a reminder that the administration of justice depends ultimately upon the willingness of witnesses to tell the truth, and that the legal system cannot function without the trust that the oath represents.
