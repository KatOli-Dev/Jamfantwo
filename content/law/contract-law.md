## The Binding of Promises Across Cultures

Contract law governs the formation, performance, and enforcement of agreements between persons. Every society in the known world has developed means by which promises may be made binding and by which those who break their promises may be held to account. The forms of contract law vary widely, from the sophisticated codes of the commercial kingdoms to the customary practices of the nomadic peoples, but the underlying principles are remarkably consistent across cultures.

The most fundamental of these principles is that a promise that is made freely and with the intention of creating a binding obligation ought to be kept. This principle is expressed in the commercial codes of the eastern kingdoms, in the customary law of the nomadic peoples, and in the decisions of the merchant courts that resolve disputes arising from the trade that flows across continents. The specific rules by which the principle is applied differ from one legal system to another, but the principle itself is recognised wherever commerce is conducted and wherever persons deal with one another in the expectation of good faith.

## The Formation of Contracts in the Commercial Kingdoms

In the commercial kingdoms of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), a contract is formed when one person makes an offer and another person accepts that offer, and both persons intend that the agreement shall be binding and enforceable by law. The [Aldreth Commercial Code](/content/law/aldreth-commercial-code) provides detailed rules for the determination of when an offer has been made, when an acceptance has been communicated, and when the agreement is binding on the parties.

An offer is a proposal made by one person to another that indicates the terms on which the offeror is willing to enter into an agreement and that invites the offeree to accept those terms. The offer must be communicated to the offeree, and it must be sufficiently certain in its terms to be capable of being accepted. An offer may be revoked by the offeror at any time before it is accepted, but the revocation must be communicated to the offeree to be effective.

An acceptance is a communication by the offeree to the offeror that the offeree agrees to the terms of the offer. The acceptance must be communicated to the offeror, and it must be made in the manner prescribed by the offer or, if no manner is prescribed, in a manner that is reasonable in the circumstances. The acceptance takes effect when it is communicated to the offeror, and the contract is formed at the moment of communication.

The code requires consideration for the validity of a contract. Consideration is the benefit that each party receives from the agreement, or the detriment that each party suffers, and it is the exchange of consideration that distinguishes a binding contract from a mere promise. A promise that is not supported by consideration is not enforceable as a contract, though it may be enforceable under other principles of law.

## The Formation of Contracts in the Sahrani City-States

The contract law of the Sahrani city-states follows the same basic principles as that of the eastern kingdoms, but with significant variations that reflect the influence of the spice trade and the commercial customs of the region. The [Amurra Republic Civil Code](/content/law/amurra-republic-civil-code) governs the formation of contracts in the republic, and its provisions are similar to those of the Aldreth Commercial Code, but with a greater emphasis on the formalities of contracting and the documentation of agreements.

The Amurra Civil Code requires that certain contracts be made in writing to be enforceable, including contracts for the sale of spices, contracts for the carriage of goods by caravan, and contracts for the loan of money for the purpose of trade. The requirement of writing reflects the importance of documentation in a commercial system in which goods are traded across long distances and disputes are resolved by courts that are distant from the place where the agreement was made.

The code also requires that contracts be registered with the republic's commercial registry to be enforceable against third parties. The registry is maintained by the council of the republic, and it records the terms of contracts, the names of the parties, and the dates of the agreements. The registry is open to inspection by any person who has a legitimate interest in the contract, and it provides a means by which the terms of an agreement can be verified if a dispute arises.

## The Formation of Contracts Among the Nomadic Peoples

Among the nomadic peoples of the known world, contracts are formed by the exchange of spoken promises in the presence of witnesses. The [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) of the Sahrani desert do not require writing or registration for the validity of a contract, for the oral traditions of the desert are the foundation of the legal order of the kindreds, and a promise made before witnesses is as binding as a written document in the legal systems of the settled kingdoms.

The contract of the desert is accompanied by the exchange of gifts or tokens that symbolise the agreement. The parties to a contract may exchange a small gift, such as a piece of salt or a length of cord, to signify the binding nature of their agreement. The exchange of gifts serves the same function as the consideration required by the commercial codes of the eastern kingdoms, providing a tangible symbol of the agreement and a means by which the agreement can be remembered and proved.

The witnesses to a contract among the Kharesh Tribes are chosen from among the elders of the kindreds, and their presence at the making of the agreement is essential to its validity. The witnesses are responsible for remembering the terms of the agreement and for testifying to those terms if a dispute arises. The reliability of the witnesses is the foundation of the contractual order of the desert, and the elders who serve as witnesses take their responsibility seriously, for their reputation as trustworthy witnesses is essential to the functioning of the system.

## The Performance of Contracts

The performance of contracts is governed by the principle that the parties must perform their obligations in accordance with the terms of the agreement. The Aldreth Commercial Code requires that the parties perform their obligations in good faith, and that they do not take advantage of each other through fraud, misrepresentation, or the concealment of material facts.

The code provides detailed rules for the determination of when performance is due, where performance is to be rendered, and what constitutes proper performance. A party who performs in accordance with the terms of the contract is entitled to receive the benefit of the other party's performance, and a party who fails to perform in accordance with the terms of the contract is liable for breach of contract.

The code also provides for the discharge of contracts by performance, by agreement of the parties, by frustration of the purpose of the contract, and by operation of law. A contract that has been performed by both parties is discharged and the parties are released from their obligations. A contract that has been discharged by agreement of the parties is also terminated, and the parties are released from their obligations if they agree to the discharge.

## The Enforcement of Contracts

The enforcement of contracts is the function of the courts that have jurisdiction over the parties and the subject matter of the contract. In the kingdoms of Aethelar, contract disputes are resolved by the royal courts or by the commercial courts, depending on the nature of the dispute and the status of the parties. The Aldreth Commercial Code gives the commercial courts jurisdiction over disputes arising from commercial contracts, and the procedure of the commercial courts is designed to be swift and efficient.

The remedies available for breach of contract include damages, specific performance, and the rescission of the contract. Damages are the most common remedy, and they are intended to compensate the innocent party for the loss suffered as a result of the breach. The measure of damages is the difference between the value of the performance that the innocent party expected to receive and the value of the performance that was actually received, together with any additional losses that were reasonably foreseeable at the time the contract was made.

Specific performance is an order of the court requiring the party in breach to perform the contract in accordance with its terms. Specific performance is available only in cases in which damages would not be an adequate remedy, such as contracts for the sale of unique goods or contracts for the sale of land. The court has discretion to order specific performance, and it will not do so if the order would be difficult to enforce or if it would cause injustice to the party in breach.

Rescission is the cancellation of the contract and the restoration of the parties to the positions they occupied before the contract was made. Rescission is available when the contract was induced by fraud, misrepresentation, or duress, or when the contract is voidable for some other reason. The party seeking rescission must act promptly, and the party must be able to restore the other party to the position that party occupied before the contract was made.

## The Merchant Courts

The merchant courts of the known world are the institutions through which commercial contracts are enforced and commercial disputes are resolved. The [Thessaran Merchant Courts](/content/law/thessaran-merchant-courts) are the most developed of these institutions, with a system of courts that operates throughout the territory of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) and that applies a body of commercial law derived from the Aldreth Commercial Code and the customs of the eastern maritime communities.

The merchant courts are presided over by judges who are chosen from among the senior merchants of the league's member cities. The judges have practical experience of commerce, and they understand the needs of merchants and the customs of the trade. The procedure of the merchant courts is informal and flexible, designed to resolve disputes quickly and efficiently without the delays and formalities that characterise the ordinary courts.

The merchant courts have jurisdiction over disputes arising from contracts for the sale of goods, contracts for the carriage of goods by sea, contracts of insurance, and contracts for the loan of money for commercial purposes. The courts also have jurisdiction over disputes between merchants arising from any commercial transaction, whether or not the transaction is governed by a specific provision of the commercial code.

The decisions of the merchant courts are enforced by the collective authority of the league. A merchant who fails to comply with a decision of a merchant court may be excluded from the markets of the league, and the name of the merchant is recorded in the register of dishonoured traders that is maintained by the league. The social and economic consequences of exclusion from the register are such that the threat of registration is a powerful deterrent to non-compliance.

## Contracts Among the Dwarven Holds

The contract law of the dwarven holds is distinctive in its emphasis on the written form and the permanence of agreements. The [Stoneheim Mineral Rights](/content/law/stoneheim-mineral-rights) code requires that contracts for the extraction of minerals be recorded in writing and inscribed on metal plates that are kept in the great hall of the hold. The metal plates are the permanent record of the agreement, and they may be consulted by the parties and by the courts if a dispute arises.

The dwarven understanding of contract is shaped by the dwarven conception of time, which is measured in generations and centuries rather than in years. A contract that is made by the dwarves is intended to endure for as long as the conditions that gave rise to it persist, and the dwarves are reluctant to modify or terminate a contract once it has been made. The permanence of the written record reinforces the permanence of the obligation, and the dwarves regard the breaking of a contract as a serious offence that may have consequences extending beyond the parties to the agreement.

The enforcement of contracts among the dwarven holds is the function of the council of the hold, which has the authority to hear disputes and to order the performance of contracts. The council applies the written provisions of the contract and the customary law of the holds in resolving disputes, and the decisions of the council are enforced by the authority of the king of the holds.

## The Customary Contract Law of the Plains

The contract law of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) is a mixture of written and customary elements, reflecting the dual character of the confederation as a union of settled cities and nomadic kindreds. The confederation council has issued decrees governing certain aspects of contract law, including the sale of grain and livestock, the carriage of goods along the roads of the confederation, and the lending of money for commercial purposes. These decrees are binding on all members of the confederation, and they are enforced by the confederation's courts.

The customary contract law of the centaur kindreds continues to operate alongside the written decrees of the confederation council. Contracts between members of the centaur kindreds are formed by the exchange of spoken promises in the presence of witnesses, and they are enforced by the authority of the kindred elders. Contracts between centaurs and settled farmers are governed by the written decrees of the confederation council, ensuring that the legal framework for commerce between the two communities is uniform and predictable.

The enduring significance of contract law in the known world lies in its function as the foundation of commerce and exchange. The system of contracts allows merchants to plan their affairs with confidence, knowing that the promises made to them will be kept or that they will have recourse to the courts if the promises are broken. The development of contract law across the continents has been driven by the needs of commerce, and the principles that have emerged from this development are among the most important legal achievements of the known world.
