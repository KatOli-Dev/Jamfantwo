## The Contract Between Sovereign and Trade

The guild charter is the document by which the sovereign or the council of a city grants to the practitioners of a trade the right to organise themselves into a guild and to regulate the practice of the trade within the city's jurisdiction. The charter is a contract between the sovereign and the guild, specifying the powers of the guild, the obligations of the guild to the sovereign, and the limits within which the guild may exercise its authority.

The guild charter is the foundation of the guild system that is the primary institution of commercial regulation in the cities and towns of the known world. Every trade of any significance is organised into a guild in every city of any size, and the charter that establishes each guild is the source of its authority and the framework within which it operates. The charter is typically granted in perpetuity, but it may be revoked by the sovereign if the guild fails to meet its obligations or abuses its powers.

## The Content of the Guild Charter

The guild charter typically contains several provisions that define the rights and obligations of the guild. The first of these provisions is the grant of the exclusive right to regulate the practice of the trade within the city's jurisdiction. The charter provides that no person may practise the trade within the city who is not a member of the guild, and that the guild has the authority to determine the conditions under which persons may be admitted to membership.

The second provision of the charter is the grant of the power to set standards of quality for goods produced or sold by members of the guild. The guild has the authority to inspect the premises of members and to confiscate goods that fail to meet the standards. The standards of quality established by the guild are binding on all members, and the guild may impose penalties on members who violate the standards.

The third provision of the charter is the grant of the right to adjudicate disputes among members of the guild. The guild has the authority to hear disputes between members arising from the practice of the trade, and to make decisions that are binding on the parties. The guild may impose fines, suspend members, or expel members from the guild for violations of the guild's rules.

The fourth provision of the charter specifies the obligations of the guild to the sovereign or the council. The guild is typically required to pay an annual fee to the sovereign, to provide goods or services to the sovereign at specified times, and to maintain the guild hall and the facilities necessary for the practice of the trade. The guild may also be required to provide a specified number of trained craftsmen for the sovereign's service in times of need.

The fifth provision of the charter specifies the limits of the guild's authority. The guild may not exercise its powers in a manner that violates the laws of the kingdom or the city, and the guild may not discriminate against persons who are eligible for membership on grounds that are prohibited by law. The guild may also be required to submit its rules and decisions to the sovereign or the council for review.

The charter has been the foundation of the prosperity of the wine and ale trade in Vassholm. The guild's charter has been the foundation of the reputation of Amurra spices in the markets of the known world. The assurance of quality and authenticity that the guild's seal provides has made Amurra spices the most trusted and the most valuable in the world, and the guild's regulation of the trade has been a significant factor in the prosperity of the republic.

The charter of the smiths' guild is not granted by the sovereign but is derived from the accumulated tradition of the hold, and the guild's authority is recognised by the king and the council as a matter of custom rather than by written grant.

The charter of the smiths' guild is inscribed on a metal plate that is kept in the great hall of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim), recording the rights and obligations that the smiths have enjoyed since the founding of the hold. The charter grants to the guild the exclusive right to regulate the forging of metal in the hold, including the standards of quality for weapons, tools, and decorative metalwork.

The guild is organised on a different principle from the human guilds. The dwarven guild does not have the three ranks of apprentice, journeyman, and master that characterise the human guilds, but admits a member after a period of training and service that may last as long as a human generation. The member is thereafter recognised as a smith of the guild for life, and the member's work is judged by the use to which it is put and the reputation it earns among the other smiths of the hold.

The guild is represented on the council of the hold, and the voice of the smiths is heard in every deliberation that touches upon the mining and forging of metal. The relationship between the guild and the council is one of partnership rather than regulation, reflecting the dwarven view that the guild is an integral part of the governance of the hold.

## The Enforcement of Guild Charters

The enforcement of guild charters is the function of the sovereign or the council that granted the charter. A guild that exceeds its powers or fails to meet its obligations may be subject to penalties imposed by the sovereign or the council, including fines, the suspension of the charter, or the revocation of the charter.

The sovereign or the council may also intervene in the internal affairs of the guild if the guild fails to maintain the standards of quality that are expected of it, or if the guild engages in practices that are contrary to the interests of the public. The sovereign or the council may appoint commissioners to oversee the guild's operations, or may suspend the guild's authority and assume direct regulation of the trade.

The guild also has the right to enforce its charter against its members and against persons who practise the trade without being members of the guild. The guild may impose fines, suspend members, or expel members from the guild for violations of the guild's rules. The guild may also seek the assistance of the sovereign or the council in enforcing its authority against persons who are not members of the guild.

## The Relationship Between Guild Charters and the Wider Legal Order

Guild charters are part of the wider legal order of the kingdoms and cities in which they operate. The guild charter is subject to the laws of the kingdom or the city, and the guild may not exercise its powers in a manner that violates those laws. The guild charter is also subject to the jurisdiction of the courts, and a person who is aggrieved by a decision of the guild may seek review of the decision by the courts.

The relationship between guild charters and the wider legal order is a balance of power that varies from city to city. In the cities of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), the guilds operate with considerable autonomy, and the council of the city rarely interferes in the internal affairs of the guilds. In the cities of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), the guilds are more closely regulated by the council, and the sovereign has the power to appoint the master of the guild. In the cities of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), the relationship is somewhere between these extremes, with the guilds operating under the authority of the council but with a significant degree of independence in their internal affairs.

## The Decline or Revocation of Guild Charters

A guild charter may be revoked by the sovereign or the council if the guild fails to meet its obligations or abuses its powers. The revocation of a charter is a serious step, and it is typically preceded by warnings and by attempts to reform the guild's practices. The revocation of a charter is also subject to review by the courts, and the guild has the right to challenge the revocation on legal grounds.

The revocation of a guild charter may have significant consequences for the trade that the guild regulated. The trade may be opened to all practitioners, or the sovereign or the council may assume direct regulation of the trade. The experience of the known world suggests that the regulation of a trade by a guild is generally more effective than regulation by the sovereign or the council, and the revocation of a guild charter is typically followed by the establishment of a new guild under a new charter.

The enduring significance of guild charters in the known world lies in their function as the foundation of the system of commercial regulation that sustains the prosperity of the cities and towns. The guild charter provides the legal framework within which the practitioners of a trade can organise themselves, maintain standards, and resolve disputes, and it is the institution through which the sovereign or the council can delegate the regulation of a trade to those who know it best. The guild charter is a flexible instrument that can be adapted to the needs of different trades and different cities, and it has proven to be one of the most enduring and effective legal institutions in the known world.
