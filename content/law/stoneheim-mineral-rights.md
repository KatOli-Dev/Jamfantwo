## The Law of the Deep Veins

The [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) Mineral Rights code is the body of law that governs the extraction of mineral wealth in the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), establishing the rights of the crown, the holds, and individual miners to the ores, precious metals, and rare minerals that are the foundation of the dwarven economy. The code is the oldest and most comprehensive body of mining law in the known world, and its provisions have been developed over centuries of experience in the extraction of mineral wealth from the depths of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) mountains.

The code is inscribed on metal plates that are kept in the great hall of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim), the capital of the holds, and the plates are the authoritative record of the law. Additions to the code require the assent of the king of Stoneheim, and the king's decree is inscribed on a new plate that is added to the collection. The code is divided into chapters dealing with distinct areas of mining law, and each chapter is further divided into sections that organise the provisions by topic.

## The Ownership of Mineral Rights

The code establishes the principle that all mineral wealth beneath the surface of the holds belongs to the king, who holds the mineral rights as trustee for the dwarven people. No person or hold may extract minerals from the earth without the authorisation of the king, and the king has the power to grant, deny, or revoke the right to extract minerals from any portion of the holds.

The king grants mineral rights to the holds through a system of concessions, by which a hold receives the exclusive right to extract minerals from a specified area for a specified period. The concession is recorded in the code and is binding on the king and the hold. The hold that receives a concession is required to pay a portion of the value of the minerals extracted to the king, and the hold is required to conduct the extraction in accordance with the standards established by the code.

The hold that receives a concession may grant sub-concessions to individual miners or to groups of miners, who then have the right to extract minerals from a specified portion of the hold's concession. The sub-concession is recorded in the hold's register, and the miner who receives a sub-concession is required to pay a portion of the value of the minerals extracted to the hold.

## The Allocation of Mining Claims

The code establishes a system for the allocation of mining claims within the areas covered by concessions. A mining claim is the right of a miner or a group of miners to extract minerals from a specified portion of the earth, and the claim is subject to the authority of the hold and the king.

The allocation of mining claims is governed by the principle of priority, as established by the code. The first miner who discovers a vein of ore and registers the discovery with the hold has the prior right to claim the vein. The miner must register the discovery within a specified period of the discovery, and the miner must commence extraction within a specified period of the registration. A miner who fails to register a discovery or to commence extraction loses the priority.

The code also provides for the allocation of claims by auction in cases in which multiple miners seek the right to extract minerals from the same area. The auction is conducted by the hold, and the miner who offers the highest share of the value of the minerals to the hold receives the claim. The auction system is used primarily for areas that are known to contain valuable minerals but that have not yet been claimed by a discoverer.

## The Standards for Extraction

The code establishes detailed standards for the extraction of minerals from the earth, reflecting the dwarven understanding of the importance of safety and efficiency in mining operations. The standards address the construction and maintenance of mine shafts, tunnels, and chambers, the ventilation of the mines, the drainage of water from the mines, and the disposal of waste materials.

The code requires that mine shafts be constructed with proper supports to prevent collapse, and that the supports be inspected at regular intervals by inspectors appointed by the hold. The code also requires that the mines be ventilated to provide fresh air to the miners and to prevent the accumulation of dangerous gases. The ventilation systems must be maintained by the miners and inspected by the inspectors.

The code requires that water be drained from the mines to prevent flooding and to protect the health of the miners. The drainage systems must be constructed and maintained by the miners, and the water that is drained from the mines must be disposed of in a manner that does not damage the surrounding land or water sources.

The code also requires that waste materials from the extraction of minerals be disposed of in a manner that does not damage the surrounding environment. The waste materials must be stored in designated areas, and the storage areas must be maintained to prevent the release of harmful substances into the environment.

## The Rights and Obligations of Miners

The code establishes the rights and obligations of the miners who extract minerals from the holds. The miner has the right to extract minerals from the area covered by the miner's claim, subject to the standards established by the code. The miner also has the right to use the surface of the land above the mine for the purposes of the mining operation, including the construction of buildings, roads, and other facilities.

The miner has the obligation to conduct the extraction in accordance with the standards established by the code, and the miner is liable for any damage caused by the miner's failure to comply with the standards. The miner is also required to pay the share of the value of the minerals to the hold and to the king as specified in the concession or the claim.

The miner has the right to sell the minerals that are extracted from the mine, subject to the payment of the shares to the hold and the king. The miner may sell the minerals to any buyer, but the miner must report the sale to the hold and pay the shares within a specified period.

## The Regulation of the Mineral Trade

The code regulates the trade in minerals extracted from the holds, ensuring that the shares due to the king and the holds are paid and that the quality of the minerals is maintained. The code requires that all minerals extracted from the holds be weighed and assayed at the hold's weighing station before they are sold. The assayer determines the quality and value of the minerals, and the miner is required to pay the shares based on the assayer's determination.

The code also requires that the minerals be transported from the holds to the markets in a manner that preserves their quality and prevents theft. The minerals must be transported by licensed carriers who are registered with the hold, and the carriers are required to provide security for the value of the minerals during transport.

The code also regulates the export of minerals from the holds to foreign markets. The export of minerals requires the authorisation of the king, and the king may impose restrictions on the export of certain minerals to protect the interests of the holds. The code also provides for the imposition of export duties on minerals that are sold to foreign buyers.

## The Resolution of Disputes

The code establishes a system for the resolution of disputes arising from the extraction and trade of minerals. The disputes are heard by the mining court of the hold, which is presided over by a judge appointed by the hold. The mining court has jurisdiction over disputes between miners, between miners and the hold, and between miners and the buyers of minerals.

The mining court applies the provisions of the code and the customs of the holds in resolving disputes. The court has the authority to order the payment of compensation, the adjustment of claims, and the suspension or revocation of concessions and sub-concessions. The decisions of the mining court may be appealed to the king, who has the authority to confirm, modify, or overturn the decision.

The code also provides for the arbitration of disputes between the holds, which are heard by the council of the holds under the presidency of the king. The council applies the provisions of the code and the traditions of the dwarven people in resolving disputes, and the decisions of the council are binding on the holds.

## The Historical Development of the Code

The Stoneheim Mineral Rights code developed over centuries of experience in the extraction of mineral wealth from the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) mountains. The earliest provisions of the code were established by the first kings of Stoneheim, who recognised that the extraction of minerals required a legal framework that balanced the interests of the crown, the holds, and the individual miners.

The code has been expanded and revised by successive kings as new mining techniques have been developed, new minerals have been discovered, and new challenges have arisen. The code now contains provisions governing the extraction of more than fifty different minerals, the use of advanced mining techniques, and the environmental impact of mining operations.

The code has been influenced by the practices of other mining communities, including the dwarven communities of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) and the human miners of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm). The king of Stoneheim maintains a correspondence with the leaders of these communities, and the exchange of knowledge and experience has contributed to the development of the code.

## The Significance of the Code

The Stoneheim Mineral Rights code is the foundation of the dwarven economy and the legal order of the holds. The code ensures that the extraction of mineral wealth is conducted in an orderly and efficient manner, that the interests of the crown, the holds, and the individual miners are balanced, and that the minerals that are the foundation of the dwarven economy are extracted and traded in accordance with the law.

The code has also influenced the development of mining law in other regions of the known world. The principles of the code have been adopted or adapted by the mining communities of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) and the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches), and the code is cited as authority in mining disputes throughout the known world.

The enduring significance of the code lies in its demonstration that the extraction of mineral wealth can be conducted in a manner that is both efficient and just. The code represents the dwarven understanding that the mineral wealth of the earth is a common heritage that must be managed for the benefit of all, and that the legal framework within which that wealth is extracted must be carefully designed and faithfully enforced. The code is a testament to the dwarven genius for order and organisation, and it remains the model for mining law throughout the known world.
