## The Legal Order of the Deep Interior

The Frostwall Clans Customary Law is the body of unwritten law that governs the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), the dwarven clans of the deep interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The customary law is preserved in the memory of the clan elders and recited at the council sessions held at [Grimhold](/content/location/settlement/city/grimhold), the principal fortress-city of the clans. Certain provisions have been inscribed on stone tablets kept in the fortress halls, but the law remains primarily an oral tradition, transmitted across generations by the elders who are its guardians.

The customary law of the Frostwall Clans reflects the conditions of the deep interior and the values of the dwarven people who inhabit it. The law is shaped by the demands of the harsh environment, the martial traditions of the clans, and the need for collective action in defence of the interior ranges. The law is also shaped by the relationship between the clans and the other peoples of Morvind, including the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and the human settlements of the coast.

## The Sources of the Customary Law

The customary law of the Frostwall Clans derives from several sources, each of which contributes to the body of rules that govern the life of the clans. The first and most important source is the tradition of the clans themselves, the accumulated wisdom of generations of dwarves who have lived and died in the deep interior. The traditions of the clans include the rules of conduct that have been developed to ensure the survival and prosperity of the community in the demanding conditions of the interior.

The second source of the customary law is the decisions of the council of clan chiefs, which meets at Grimhold to resolve disputes and to make rules for the governance of the clans. The decisions of the council are recorded in the oral traditions of the clans, and they are cited as precedent in future disputes. The council has the authority to make new rules when the existing law is silent or when the conditions of the interior require a change in the law.

The third source of the customary law is the agreements between the clans and the other political entities of Morvind. The treaties and agreements that the clans have made with the Stoneheim Holds, with the human settlements of the coast, and with the other peoples of the interior are part of the customary law, and they are binding on the clans.

## The Clan and the Individual

The customary law of the Frostwall Clans is founded on the principle that the clan is the primary unit of society and that the individual is defined by membership in the clan. The clan is responsible for the protection and support of its members, and the member owes loyalty and service to the clan. The relationship between the clan and the individual is governed by a body of rules that specify the rights and obligations of each.

The clan is responsible for the defence of its members against external threats, and the clan is required to provide assistance to members who are in need. The clan is also responsible for the actions of its members, and the clan may be held liable for the harm caused by a member who acts within the scope of the member's duties to the clan.

The individual member of the clan has the right to the protection and support of the clan, and the member has the right to participate in the decisions of the clan through the clan's councils and gatherings. The member also has the obligation to contribute to the defence and prosperity of the clan, to obey the decisions of the clan's chiefs and elders, and to conduct the member's affairs in a manner that does not bring dishonour to the clan.

## The Law of the Forge

The law of the forge is a distinctive branch of the customary law of the Frostwall Clans, governing the practice of metalwork that is the foundation of the dwarven economy and the source of the clans' reputation for craftsmanship. The law of the forge establishes the standards of quality that must be met by the smiths of the clans, the rules for the training of apprentices, and the rights and obligations of smiths in the practice of their trade.

The law of the forge requires that every smith of the clans meet the standards of quality established by the elders of the clan. The smith who produces work of poor quality may be required to redo the work, to pay compensation to the person who commissioned the work, or to submit to additional training. A smith who consistently produces work of poor quality may be prohibited from practising the trade for a specified period.

The law of the forge also governs the training of apprentices, who are bound to a master smith by a contract that specifies the term of the apprenticeship and the obligations of the master and the apprentice. The master is required to provide the apprentice with a complete training in the trade, and the apprentice is required to serve the master faithfully and to obey the master's instructions.

The law of the forge also governs the rights of smiths to use the techniques and designs that they have developed. A smith who develops a new technique or design has the exclusive right to use it within the clan for a specified period, after which the technique or design becomes the common property of the clan. The exclusive right encourages innovation while ensuring that the benefits of new techniques and designs are ultimately shared by the community.

## The Law of Defence

The law of defence is the branch of the customary law that governs the military organisation of the clans and the conduct of war. The law of defence is the most developed branch of the customary law, reflecting the martial traditions of the clans and the constant threat that the wild peoples of the mountains pose to the interior.

The law of defence requires that every able-bodied adult of the clans be trained in the use of arms and be available for service in the defence of the clan and the confederation. The training is conducted by the veterans of the clan, who teach the younger members the skills of combat and the traditions of the warrior.

The law of defence also governs the conduct of war, including the declaration of war, the conduct of military operations, and the treatment of prisoners and non-combatants. The law requires that war be declared by the council of clan chiefs before military operations may begin, and that the council be informed of the progress and outcome of the operations.

The law of defence also governs the treatment of prisoners, who must be treated with respect and may not be subjected to torture or cruel treatment. The prisoners may be exchanged for prisoners held by the other side, or they may be ransomed for a payment of goods or livestock. The law also prohibits the killing of non-combatants, including women, children, and the elderly, except in the most extreme circumstances.

## The Law of the Passes

The law of the passes governs the maintenance and use of the high passes that connect the interior to the coast. The passes are the only overland routes between the eastern and western portions of the Morvind ranges, and their maintenance is essential to the survival and prosperity of the clans.

The law of the passes requires that each clan maintain the portion of the passes that passes through its territory, clearing the snow and debris that accumulate during the winter months and repairing the damage caused by the weather. The law also requires that the clans maintain the cairns that mark the passes and that provide shelter for travellers.

The law of the passes also governs the use of the passes by travellers and traders. The clans have the right to charge tolls for the use of the passes, and the tolls are used to fund the maintenance of the passes and the shelters. The clans also have the right to refuse passage to persons who are judged to be a threat to the safety of the clans or the confederation.

## The Resolution of Disputes

The resolution of disputes under the customary law of the Frostwall Clans is the function of the clan chiefs and the council of clan chiefs. Minor disputes between members of the same clan are resolved by the chief of the clan, who hears the parties and renders a decision based on the customary law. Major disputes between members of different clans are resolved by the council of clan chiefs, which hears the parties and renders a decision that is binding on all.

The resolution of disputes is conducted in a formal manner, with the parties presenting their cases before the chief or the council and the witnesses testifying under oath. The chief or the council may also consult the elders of the clans for their knowledge of the customary law and the traditions of the dwarven people.

The decision of the chief or the council is announced in a gathering of the affected clans, and the decision is recorded in the oral traditions of the clans. The party who fails to comply with the decision may be subject to penalties imposed by the chief or the council, including fines, the forfeiture of property, and the expulsion from the clan.

The customary law of the Frostwall Clans is related to the law of the Stoneheim Holds, but the two legal systems are distinct and independent. The Frostwall Clans are a confederation of independent clans, not a unified kingdom, and each clan retains its own legal traditions and its own authority to make and enforce law.

The customary law of the Frostwall Clans has, however, been influenced by the law of the Stoneheim Holds, and the two legal systems share many principles and practices. The law of the forge, the law of defence, and the law of the passes are common to both legal systems, and the decisions of the Stoneheim courts are sometimes cited as persuasive authority in the councils of the Frostwall Clans.
