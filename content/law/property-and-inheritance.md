## The Ordering of Ownership Across Cultures

Property law is concerned with the rights of persons to control, use, and dispose of land, goods, and other resources. The systems of property law that have developed across the known world reflect the different ways in which different societies understand the relationship between persons and things. In the settled kingdoms, property is primarily understood as individual ownership, a right that may be acquired, transferred, and inherited according to rules established by the sovereign and enforced by the courts. Among the nomadic peoples, property is understood differently, as a right of use or access that is held by the kindred or the tribe and that is subject to seasonal and environmental conditions.

Inheritance law governs the transmission of property from one generation to the next. The rules of inheritance reflect the values of the society in which they operate, determining which members of a family or community succeed to the property of a deceased person and in what shares. The inheritance customs of the known world vary from the primogeniture of the eastern kingdoms, in which the eldest son inherits the whole of the family's land, to the equal division of the Sahrani city-states, in which all children share in the inheritance.

## Land Tenure in the Eastern Kingdoms

In the kingdoms of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), land is held by individual owners who have the right to use, lease, sell, or bequeath their land as they see fit, subject to the authority of the crown and the provisions of the law. The ownership of land is recorded in the land registers maintained by the royal administration, and the transfer of land requires the registration of the transfer in the registers to be effective against third parties.

The land law of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) is the most developed in Aethelar, reflecting the commercial character of the kingdom. Land is treated as a commodity that may be bought, sold, and leased like any other, and the Aldreth Commercial Code contains detailed provisions governing the sale and lease of land. The code requires that contracts for the sale of land be in writing and registered with the royal chancery, and it provides for the enforcement of contracts for the sale of land through the commercial courts.

The land law of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) is shaped by the water law of the kingdom. The ownership of land bordering the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael) is subject to the obligations imposed by the water law, and a landowner who fails to maintain the dykes or channels bordering the land may forfeit the land to the crown. The water law also limits the right of landowners to divert water or obstruct watercourses, and these limitations are enforced by the water courts.

The inheritance of land in the eastern kingdoms is governed by the principle of primogeniture, by which the eldest son inherits the whole of the family's land. Primogeniture is the foundation of the stability of the landed estates of the eastern kingdoms, for it prevents the fragmentation of estates that would result from the equal division of land among multiple heirs. The younger sons of landed families must seek their fortunes in trade, the military, or the clergy, and the daughters of landed families receive dowries in money or goods rather than shares of the land.

## Land Tenure in the Confederations and Leagues

The land law of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) is shaped by the needs of the grassland economy and the relationship between the settled cities and the nomadic [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds. The settled cities of the confederation hold their territories as communal property, administered by the city councils on behalf of the citizens. The agricultural land surrounding the cities is divided into plots that are allocated to individual farmers, but the land remains the property of the city and may be reallocated by the council if the farmer fails to cultivate it properly.

The grasslands that lie between the settled cities are held in common by the confederation, and the use of the grasslands is governed by the grazing rights system that is one of the distinctive features of the confederation's legal order. The [Plains Confederation Grazing Rights](/content/law/plains-confederation-grazing-rights) establish the rights of the centaur kindreds and the settled farmers to graze their livestock on the grasslands, and the system is administered by the confederation council.

The [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) has a land law that reflects the commercial character of the member cities. Land in the league's cities is held by individual owners who have the right to buy, sell, and lease their property. The league does not regulate the ownership of land within the member cities, for each city governs its own affairs in this matter. The league does, however, regulate the ownership of vessels and cargo, which are the most valuable forms of property in the maritime commerce of the league.

## Land Tenure in the Sahrani City-States

The land law of the Sahrani city-states is shaped by the concentration of population in the coastal cities and the control of the valuable trade routes that connect the coast to the interior. In the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), land within the city is held by individual owners, but the ownership of land is subject to the authority of the council and the needs of the spice trade. The council has the power to expropriate land for the construction of warehouses, harbours, and other facilities necessary for the spice trade, and the owner of expropriated land is entitled to compensation determined by the council.

In the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league), the land law of the member cities varies according to the customs of each city. The city of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) follows the same principles as Amurra, while the city of [Taretha](/content/location/settlement/city/taretha) has a land law that reflects the influence of the scholarly institutions that are the foundation of the city's prosperity. The ownership of land in Taretha is subject to the claims of the libraries and academies that hold extensive properties in and around the city.

## Property Among the Nomadic Peoples

Among the nomadic peoples of the known world, property is understood differently from the settled kingdoms and city-states. The [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) of the Sahrani desert do not recognise the individual ownership of land, for the land of the desert is the common heritage of the kindreds and cannot be owned by any individual. The right to use the land for grazing, for water, or for passage is held by the kindreds, and the rights are allocated by the council of elders according to the needs of the kindreds and the conditions of the desert.

The most valuable forms of property among the Kharesh Tribes are livestock, water rights, and the knowledge of desert routes and water sources. Livestock are owned by individuals and families, and they may be bought, sold, or inherited according to the customary law of the desert. Water rights are held by the kindreds, and the allocation of water rights among the members of a kindred is governed by the elders of the kindred. The knowledge of desert routes and water sources is the most valuable property of all, and this knowledge is held by the elders and the guides who have learned it through years of experience.

Among the centaur kindreds of the grasslands, the understanding of property is similar to that of the Kharesh Tribes. The land of the grasslands is the common heritage of the kindreds, and no individual or kindred may claim exclusive ownership of any portion of the grasslands. The right to graze livestock in a particular area is allocated by the confederation council according to the seasonal grazing rights system, and the right to use the stone circles that mark the gathering places of the kindreds is held by the kindreds jointly.

## Inheritance Among the Nomadic Peoples

The inheritance customs of the nomadic peoples reflect the values of their societies and the conditions of their environment. Among the Kharesh Tribes, the inheritance of livestock and personal property is governed by the customary law of the desert, which provides for the equal division of the deceased's property among the children of the deceased, with the eldest child receiving a double share as compensation for the responsibility of caring for the surviving parent.

The inheritance of water rights is more complex, for water rights are held by the kindreds rather than by individuals. The right to draw water from a particular well or spring is allocated by the elders of the kindred to the members of the kindred according to their needs, and the right does not pass by inheritance but by allocation. A family that has drawn water from a particular source for generations may have a strong claim to continue drawing water from that source, but the claim is not a right of ownership and may be adjusted by the elders if the needs of the kindred require it.

Among the centaur kindreds of the grasslands, the inheritance of property follows similar principles. The herds of livestock that are the primary wealth of the kindreds are divided equally among the children of the deceased, with the eldest child receiving a double share as compensation for the responsibility of leadership. The knowledge of the routes and the traditions of the kindred is transmitted from the elders to the younger members of the kindred, and the transmission of knowledge is the most important form of inheritance among the centaurs.

## Inheritance in the Eastern Kingdoms

The inheritance law of the eastern kingdoms is more formal and more detailed than the customary law of the nomadic peoples. In [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth), the inheritance of property is governed by the royal decrees that establish the rules of succession to land and goods. The principle of primogeniture governs the inheritance of land, by which the eldest son inherits the whole of the family's land. The principle of equal division governs the inheritance of personal property, by which all the children of the deceased share equally in the goods of the deceased after the debts and expenses of the estate have been paid.

The testator, the person who makes a will, has the power to dispose of the personal property by will as the testator sees fit, subject to the rights of the children to a share of the estate. The law of Aldreth requires that a will be in writing and witnessed by two persons who are not beneficiaries of the will, and that the will be registered with the royal chancery to be effective. The testator may not, however, dispose of land by will in a manner that defeats the right of the eldest son to inherit the land, for the principle of primogeniture is considered to be a matter of public policy that cannot be overridden by the will of the testator.

The inheritance law of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) follows the same principles as that of Aldreth, with the additional consideration that land subject to the water law may not be divided in a manner that would make it difficult or impossible for the heir to maintain the dykes and channels bordering the land. The water courts of Vael have the authority to review the division of land by inheritance and to order adjustments if the division would impair the management of the water works.

## Inheritance in the Confederations and City-States

The inheritance law of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) is governed by the civil code of the republic, which provides for the equal division of property among all children of the deceased, regardless of sex or birth order. The principle of equal division reflects the commercial character of the republic, for the spice trade is conducted by families that cooperate across generations, and the equal division of property ensures that all children of the family have the resources to participate in the trade.

The testator in Amurra has the power to dispose of property by will, but the will must respect the right of each child to a share of the estate. The testator may not disinherit a child entirely, for the civil code requires that each child receive a minimum share of the estate, known as the legitimate portion. The legitimate portion is one-half of the share that the child would have received if the deceased had died without a will, and the remainder of the estate may be disposed of by the testator as the testator sees fit.

The inheritance law of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) follows the customs of the member communities rather than a uniform code. The settled cities of the confederation generally follow the principle of equal division of property among the children of the deceased, while the centaur kindreds follow the principle of equal division of livestock with a double share for the eldest child. The confederation does not regulate the inheritance of land, for the land of the confederation is held by the cities and the kindreds rather than by individuals.

## The Enforcement of Property and Inheritance Rights

The enforcement of property and inheritance rights is the function of the courts and councils that administer the legal systems of the known world. In the eastern kingdoms, property disputes are resolved by the royal courts, which have the authority to determine the ownership of land and goods and to order the delivery of property to the person entitled to it. The royal courts also have the authority to interpret wills and to determine the distribution of estates.

The water courts of Vael have a role in property disputes that involve the water law, and the decisions of the water courts on matters of property rights are binding on the royal courts. The commercial courts of Aldreth have jurisdiction over property disputes arising from commercial transactions, and the decisions of the commercial courts are enforced by the royal administration.

Among the nomadic peoples, property disputes are resolved by the elders of the kindreds and by the gatherings of the kindreds. The elders have the authority to determine the ownership of livestock and the allocation of water rights, and the gatherings of the kindreds have the authority to resolve disputes that cannot be resolved by the elders alone. The enforcement of the decisions of the elders and the gatherings is achieved through the collective authority of the kindred, and the threat of expulsion from the kindred is the ultimate sanction for those who refuse to accept the decisions of the elders.
