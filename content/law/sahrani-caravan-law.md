## The Law of the Desert Routes

The Sahrani Caravan Law is the body of customary and written law that governs the operation of caravans across the deserts of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), the rights and obligations of caravan masters, guides, merchants, and drovers, and the regulation of the trade routes that connect the coast to the interior. The caravan law is one of the most important legal institutions of the Sahrani continent, for the caravans that cross the deserts are the lifeline of the interior cities and the foundation of the intercontinental trade in spices, dyes, and other valuable goods.

The caravan law is not a single code but a collection of customs, agreements, and regulations that have developed over centuries of desert travel. The law draws upon the customary law of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), the commercial codes of the coastal city-states, and the agreements between the tribes and the trading cities that have been negotiated over generations.

## The Formation of the Caravan

The formation of a caravan is governed by the caravan law, which establishes the rights and obligations of the participants. The caravan master is the person who organises the caravan, recruits the merchants and drovers, hires the guides, and arranges for the purchase of provisions and the hiring of pack animals. The caravan master is responsible for the safety and success of the caravan, and the caravan master's authority over the participants is recognised by the law.

The merchants who entrust their goods to the caravan enter into contracts with the caravan master that specify the terms of the carriage, the fees to be paid, and the allocation of risk. The contracts are governed by the principles of the [Aldreth Commercial Code](/content/law/aldreth-commercial-code) and the commercial customs of the coastal cities, and they are enforceable in the courts of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic).

The drovers who accompany the caravan are employed by the caravan master under contracts that specify their wages, their duties, and the conditions of their employment. The drovers are responsible for the care of the pack animals and the loading and unloading of the goods, and they are subject to the authority of the caravan master during the journey.

## The Role of the Guides

The guides who lead the caravan across the desert are the most important participants in the caravan, for their knowledge of the routes, water sources, and weather patterns is essential to the success of the journey. The guides are typically members of the Kharesh Tribes, who have inherited the knowledge of the desert from generations of ancestors who have crossed the sands.

The contract between the caravan master and the guide is governed by the customary law of the Kharesh Tribes, which requires that the guide be treated with respect and that the guide's authority over the navigation of the caravan be recognised. The guide has the right to determine the route, the schedule, and the location of camps, and the guide's decisions may not be overridden by the caravan master or the merchants.

The guide is entitled to a fee that is determined by the length and difficulty of the journey, the value of the goods being carried, and the reputation of the guide. The fee is paid by the caravan master, who collects a share of the fees paid by the merchants to cover the cost of the guide and other expenses.

The guide is also entitled to the protection of the caravan and the assistance of the drovers in the event of an emergency. The guide may call upon the merchants and drovers to assist in the defence of the caravan against bandits or raiders, and the guide may require the caravan to stop or to change course if the guide determines that the conditions of the desert require it.

## The Rights and Obligations of the Merchants

The merchants who travel with the caravan or who entrust their goods to it have rights and obligations under the caravan law. The merchants have the right to expect that their goods will be carried safely and delivered in the same condition in which they were entrusted to the caravan. The caravan master is liable for loss or damage to the goods caused by the negligence of the caravan master or the drovers, but the caravan master is not liable for loss or damage caused by perils of the desert, including sandstorms, flash floods, or attacks by bandits.

The merchants have the right to inspect their goods at each stop along the route, and to verify the condition of the goods and the number of packages. The merchants also have the right to communicate with the guide about the route and the schedule, and to raise concerns about the safety of the goods or the progress of the journey.

The merchants are required to pay the fees agreed with the caravan master, and to provide the caravan master with accurate information about the value and nature of the goods being carried. A merchant who provides false or misleading information about the goods is liable for any loss or damage that results from the false information.

## The Code of Hospitality and the Protection of the Caravan

The code of hospitality that is the foundation of the social order of the Kharesh Tribes also governs the protection of the caravan during its journey across the desert. The caravan that passes through the territory of a kindred is entitled to the protection of that kindred, and the kindred is obligated to provide the caravan with safe passage, access to water sources, and assistance in the event of an emergency.

The obligation of protection is reciprocal. The caravan is required to respect the customs of the kindreds whose territory it crosses, to avoid damaging the water sources or the grazing grounds of the kindreds, and to provide assistance to members of the kindreds who are in need. The caravan that violates the code of hospitality may lose the protection of the kindreds and may be refused access to water sources or assistance in an emergency.

The caravan master is responsible for ensuring that the members of the caravan comply with the code of hospitality. A merchant or drover who violates the code may be expelled from the caravan, and the caravan master may be held liable by the kindred for the actions of the members of the caravan.

## The Regulation of the Caravan Routes

The caravan routes that cross the Sahrani desert are regulated by agreements between the Kharesh Tribes and the trading cities of the coast. The agreements establish the rights of the caravans to use the routes, the fees that the caravans must pay to the tribes for the use of the routes, and the obligations of the tribes to maintain the routes and to protect the caravans from bandits and raiders.

The fees paid to the tribes for the use of the routes are a significant source of income for the Kharesh Tribes, and the agreements that govern the routes are negotiated with care and enforced by the collective authority of the tribes. A caravan that fails to pay the agreed fees may be refused passage or may be charged higher fees on subsequent journeys.

The routes are marked by cairns and other landmarks that guide the caravans across the featureless desert. The tribes are responsible for maintaining the markers and for ensuring that the routes remain passable. The tribes also maintain shelters at intervals along the routes, where the caravans may rest and replenish their supplies of water.

## The Resolution of Disputes

Disputes arising from the operation of caravans are resolved by the elders of the Kharesh Tribes and by the courts of the coastal cities. Disputes that arise during the journey across the desert are typically resolved by the elders of the kindred whose territory the caravan is crossing, applying the customary law of the desert and the principles of the code of hospitality.

Disputes that arise from the contracts between the merchants and the caravan master are resolved by the commercial courts of the coastal cities, applying the commercial codes of the cities and the principles of the Aldreth Commercial Code. The decisions of the commercial courts are enforced by the authority of the cities and the league.

Disputes between the tribes and the trading cities over the regulation of the caravan routes are resolved by negotiation between the elders of the tribes and the representatives of the cities. The agreements that result from these negotiations are recorded in writing and are binding on both parties.

## The Caravan Law and the Intercontinental Trade

The Sahrani Caravan Law is essential to the intercontinental trade that connects Sahrana to the markets of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The spices, dyes, and other goods that are carried by the caravans from the interior to the coast are the foundation of the trade that flows through the harbours of the Northern Coast League, and the caravan law provides the legal framework within which this trade is conducted.

The caravan law ensures that the caravans can cross the desert safely, that the goods are carried reliably, and that the rights of the participants are protected. The law also ensures that the Kharesh Tribes are compensated for their role in guiding and protecting the caravans, and that the relationship between the tribes and the trading cities remains stable and mutually beneficial.

The enduring significance of the Sahrani Caravan Law lies in its adaptation to the conditions of the desert and the needs of the peoples who cross it. The law is a product of centuries of experience in desert travel, and its provisions reflect the practical realities of life in the most demanding environment in the known world. The caravan law is a testament to the ingenuity and resilience of the peoples of Sahrana, who have developed a legal system that allows them to conduct commerce across a desert that would defeat any people less determined and less skilled.
