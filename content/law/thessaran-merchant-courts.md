## The Commercial Justice of the Eastern Ports

The Thessaran Merchant Courts are the institutions through which commercial disputes are resolved within the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league), a confederation of eastern coast port cities of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The merchant courts are the most developed and respected commercial courts in the known world, and their decisions are cited as precedent in merchant courts throughout the continents.

The merchant courts were established by the founding charter of the Thessaran League, which recognised that the prosperity of the member cities depended upon the swift and reliable resolution of commercial disputes. The courts are distinct from the ordinary courts of the member cities, and they have jurisdiction over disputes arising from commercial transactions between merchants of the league and between merchants of the league and foreign traders.

## The Structure of the Merchant Courts

The merchant courts of the Thessaran League are organised in a hierarchy that reflects the nature and value of the disputes they hear. The lowest level of the hierarchy is the local merchant court, which sits in each member city of the league and has jurisdiction over disputes involving amounts below a specified value. The local merchant court is presided over by a judge who is chosen from among the senior merchants of the city, and the court applies the commercial code of the league and the customs of the trade.

The intermediate level of the hierarchy is the regional merchant court, which sits in the principal cities of the league and has jurisdiction over disputes involving amounts above the value specified for the local courts. The regional merchant court is presided over by a panel of three judges, each chosen from a different member city, and the court applies the commercial code of the league and the decisions of the higher courts.

The highest level of the hierarchy is the league merchant court, which sits in [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) under the presidency of the Thessar, the presiding officer of the league council. The league merchant court has jurisdiction over disputes involving the largest amounts and over appeals from the regional merchant courts. The decisions of the league merchant court are final and binding on all parties, and they are recorded in the league's register of decisions, which is consulted by merchants and courts throughout the known world.

## The Judges of the Merchant Courts

The judges of the merchant courts are chosen from among the senior merchants of the league's member cities. The judges are required to have a minimum of ten years of experience in commerce, and they must be known for their integrity, their knowledge of the trade, and their ability to resolve disputes fairly and efficiently.

The judges of the local merchant courts are appointed by the councils of the member cities, and they serve for terms of five years. The judges of the regional merchant courts are appointed by the league council, and they serve for terms of seven years. The judges of the league merchant court are elected by the league council from among the judges of the regional courts, and they serve for terms of ten years.

The judges are assisted by clerks who manage the records of the courts and by assessors who provide expert knowledge of the trade or practice at issue in the dispute. The assessors are chosen from among the merchants of the league who have specialised knowledge of the matter before the court, and they advise the judges on the customs and practices of the trade.

## The Jurisdiction of the Merchant Courts

The merchant courts have jurisdiction over a wide range of commercial disputes, including disputes arising from contracts for the sale of goods, contracts for the carriage of goods by sea, contracts of insurance, contracts for the loan of money for commercial purposes, and disputes between partners in commercial ventures. The courts also have jurisdiction over disputes arising from the sale or charter of vessels and from the employment of masters and crews.

The merchant courts have jurisdiction over disputes between merchants of the league, between merchants of the league and foreign traders, and between merchants of the league and the guilds that regulate the trades in the member cities. The courts also have jurisdiction over disputes between the member cities themselves, arising from commercial matters, and over disputes between the league and its members.

The merchant courts do not have jurisdiction over criminal matters, over disputes involving land or inheritance, or over disputes between persons who are not engaged in commerce. These matters are heard by the ordinary courts of the member cities, which apply the laws of the individual cities rather than the commercial code of the league.

## The Procedure of the Merchant Courts

The procedure of the merchant courts is designed to be swift, efficient, and accessible to merchants who may not have legal training. The courts do not require the parties to be represented by legal counsel, and the judges take an active role in the proceedings, questioning the parties and the witnesses and seeking to understand the facts and the issues in the case.

The proceedings begin with the filing of a complaint by the party who seeks relief from the court. The complaint is a written document that states the facts of the case, the legal basis for the claim, and the relief that the party seeks. The complaint is filed with the clerk of the court, who delivers a copy to the other party.

The other party files a response to the complaint within a specified period, and the response may include a counterclaim against the party who filed the complaint. The court then schedules a hearing at which the parties present their evidence and arguments.

The hearing is conducted in a less formal manner than the proceedings of the ordinary courts. The parties speak directly to the judges, presenting their evidence and arguments in their own words. The witnesses are examined by the parties and by the judges, and the judges may ask questions to clarify the facts or to explore the issues.

At the conclusion of the hearing, the judges deliberate and render a decision. The decision is announced in open court, and the reasons for the decision are stated briefly. The decision is recorded in the records of the court, and the parties are bound by it.

## The Evidence in the Merchant Courts

The merchant courts place great emphasis on written evidence, reflecting the commercial character of the disputes they hear. The courts accept contracts, bills of exchange, promissory notes, letters of credit, invoices, and other commercial documents as evidence of the transactions at issue in the dispute. The courts also accept the records of the guilds and the registers of the commercial courts as evidence of the facts recorded in them.

The courts also accept oral testimony from the parties and from witnesses. The testimony is taken under oath, and the courts apply the same rules of perjury that apply in the ordinary courts. The courts also accept the testimony of expert witnesses who have specialised knowledge of the trade or practice at issue.

The courts may also inspect the goods that are the subject of the dispute, and they may order the production of documents or other evidence that is relevant to the case. The courts may also appoint assessors to investigate the facts and to report their findings to the court.

## The Enforcement of Decisions

The decisions of the merchant courts are enforced by the collective authority of the league. A party who fails to comply with a decision of a merchant court may be subject to a range of penalties, including fines, the seizure of goods, and the suspension or revocation of the party's right to trade in the league's markets.

The most effective enforcement mechanism is the register of dishonoured traders, which is maintained by the league. The name of a merchant who fails to comply with a decision of a merchant court is recorded in the register, and the registration has serious consequences for the merchant's ability to conduct business in the league's markets. The registered merchant may be excluded from the markets of the cities of the league, and the merchant may be refused credit by the merchants of the league.

The decisions of the merchant courts are also enforced by the courts of the member cities, which have the authority to seize the goods of a party who fails to comply with a decision and to sell the goods to satisfy the judgment. The courts of the member cities also have the authority to imprison a party who fails to comply with a decision, although imprisonment is used only as a last resort.

## The Relationship with the Aldreth Commercial Code

The merchant courts of the Thessaran League apply the commercial code of the league, which is based on the [Aldreth Commercial Code](/content/law/aldreth-commercial-code). The league's commercial code incorporates many of the provisions of the Aldreth code, including the rules for the formation and enforcement of contracts, the regulation of insurance, and the principles of general average.

The merchant courts also apply the decisions of the Aldreth commercial courts as persuasive authority, and the judges of the merchant courts regularly consult the Aldreth decisions when they are faced with novel questions of commercial law. The relationship between the merchant courts of the league and the commercial courts of Aldreth is one of mutual respect and co-operation, and the two systems of commercial law have developed in parallel for centuries.

The influence of the Aldreth Commercial Code on the merchant courts of the Thessaran League is a testament to the power of the code and the respect that it commands throughout the known world. The code is the most influential body of commercial law in Aethelar, and its adoption by the league has contributed to the uniformity of commercial law across the eastern coast.

## The Significance of the Merchant Courts

The Thessaran Merchant Courts are among the most important institutions of the league, and they are essential to the prosperity of the member cities. The courts provide a swift, efficient, and reliable means of resolving commercial disputes, and they give merchants the confidence to conduct business across the boundaries of the member cities.

The merchant courts have also contributed to the development of commercial law in the known world. The decisions of the courts are recorded and preserved, and they are consulted by merchants and courts throughout the continents. The courts have developed a body of commercial law that is practical, flexible, and responsive to the needs of commerce, and their decisions have shaped the development of commercial law in regions far beyond the borders of the league.

The enduring significance of the Thessaran Merchant Courts lies in their demonstration that commercial disputes can be resolved effectively by institutions that are independent, impartial, and knowledgeable about the trade. The courts represent the principle that commerce is best regulated by those who understand it, and that the law of commerce must be adapted to the needs of merchants rather than imposed upon them by authorities that do not understand the realities of trade. The merchant courts of the Thessaran League are a model of commercial justice that has inspired the development of similar institutions throughout the known world.
