## Overview

The Thessaran Coastal Route is the chain of coastal waters, harbour roads, and short overland links connecting the port cities of eastern [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). It follows the shore of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) from the northern approaches of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) through [Caerwyn](/content/location/settlement/city/caerwyn) and the southern ports, with branches toward the river valleys and lower passes of the interior. The route is governed and protected largely by the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league), whose member cities share the cost of lighthouses, pilots, harbour works, and naval patrols. It is a busy corridor rather than a fixed line, for vessels keep close to shore in calm weather, seek shelter in coves during storms, and transfer cargo to roads whenever a headland makes land journey more efficient.

The route is the eastern coast's answer to the great ocean passages. Large ships use the deep harbours and carry cargo between principal ports, while smaller vessels serve fishing villages, river mouths, and settlements lacking quays for ocean-going hulls. Barges and wagons extend the trade inland, making the coastal route a network reaching farther than the salt water itself. Grain from the southern valleys, timber from the northern forests, iron from the mountain spine, and imported goods from [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) all move along its connected stages. The prosperity of the eastern coast rests upon the regularity of this traffic.

## The Shape of the Coast

The northern course begins where the coast opens around Eldreth, whose harbour receives vessels from the wider Auric trade. The shore is low in some places and broken by rocky promontories in others, with river mouths creating mudflats that change after every season of heavy rain. Pilots steer between shoals marked by the league's black-and-white beacons. South of Eldreth the coast becomes more varied. Cliffs alternate with sheltered beaches, and wooded valleys reach the sea through narrow inlets. The harbour of [Caerwyn](/content/location/settlement/city/caerwyn) lies behind a natural rock breakwater and serves as the most important shipbuilding centre on the route, its system separating merchant berths from construction slips. The surrounding coast provides timber, fresh water, and sheltered anchorages, making Caerwyn a natural midpoint for ships that cannot safely complete the full route without replenishment. Farther south the shore opens into broad bays and low riverlands, where [Sallowmere](/content/location/settlement/city/sallowmere) receives the produce of the warm southern country and sends grain, fruit, oil, and woven cloth northward.

## Harbours and Coastal Stations

Every major harbour maintains a quay district, a storehouse quarter, and a refuge anchorage beyond the main breakwater. The large cities provide cranes, shipwrights, sailmakers, and licensed brokers, while smaller ports specialise in provisions and local cargoes. Harbour masters use signal flags and lanterns to assign berths. Thessaran Toll is one of the smallest but most carefully placed stations on the route, standing beside a headland road where coastal cargoes are transferred from ships to wagons, allowing merchants to avoid a difficult reef or storm-exposed stretch of water. The outpost weighs goods, records their destination, and collects the contribution funding nearby lights and road repairs.

At the northern edge of the route, [Stormfall](/content/location/natural/continent/stormfall) Pier serves vessels approaching from colder waters and needing to exchange cargo before entering the Auric coast. Its pier is built of heavy timber braced with iron, and the settlement stands behind a low seawall protecting its sheds from the strongest tides. Fishers, pilots, and freight handlers make up most of the permanent population.

## Goods and Commerce

Southbound traffic carries grain, timber, tools, iron fittings, and manufactured goods from the northern and central ports. Caerwyn's ships are themselves a major export. The route distributes paper, glass, metal instruments, and cloth imported through Eldreth. Smaller vessels divide these cargoes into parcels suitable for river landings and village markets. Northbound traffic includes rice and fruit from the southern lowlands, oil, dyed cloth, salt fish, and products of the inland valleys. Imported spices and precious stones change hands repeatedly along the route.

The league regulates measures and seals so that a bale purchased in one port can be recognised in another. Brokers record origin, date, and condition of a shipment, and harbour courts hear complaints about damaged cargo or dishonest weights.

## Mariners and Communities

The crews are chiefly human, though every major harbour includes dwarven smiths, gnomish instrument-makers, and visiting traders. The mariners learn the route in stages, serving first aboard fishing boats and small cargo vessels. Their knowledge includes the position of submerged rocks, the behaviour of river currents, and the signals by which one harbour warns another of a storm. The league's collective fleet is a familiar sight during the active season. Its patrols protect merchant ships from raiders and escort convoys through exposed waters, but the fleet also performs practical work: carrying repair crews, delivering lighthouse stores, and rescuing crews from vessels damaged on the reefs.

## Weather and Navigation

The route is most heavily used during late spring, summer, and early autumn. Winter storms move in from the Stormfall waters and can drive a vessel onto the coast. The autumn gales are especially dangerous near headlands, where wind from the open ocean meets currents flowing out of river mouths. Captains study cloud shapes, the swell beneath a calm surface, and the behaviour of seabirds before deciding whether to leave harbour. Fog is common near the northern headlands and in the mornings after rain. The league's lighthouses use different patterns of shutters or coloured glass so that pilots can identify them when the shore is hidden.

## History and Significance

The individual sections of the route are older than the league. The Thessaran League formed after raiding and disorder threatened commerce of the coast, and its founding charter gave the separate cities reason to maintain shared lights, measures, and patrols. The Thessaran Coastal Route remains the principal connection among the eastern ports, carrying common necessities as steadily as luxury goods and joining surface farms, mountain workshops, shipyards, and ocean commerce into one system. No single cargo sustains it, and no single city can replace all the others. The route's enduring strength comes from this variety.
