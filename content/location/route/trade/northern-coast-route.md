## Overview

The Northern Coast Route is the coastal shipping passage following the northern shore of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), connecting the chain of city-states and trading ports lining the fertile strip between the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert. The route runs from [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) in the west to [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir) in the east, following the curve of the coast through warm and sheltered waters that have been the highway of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) maritime civilisation since the earliest records were kept. The route is the primary artery of the coast, carrying the goods, peoples, and ideas that sustain the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league), the alliance of city-states that controls the most valuable stretch of maritime real estate in the southern world. The route is a corridor rather than a single path, a band of water running close to shore where conditions are favourable and harbours frequent.

## The Geography of the Coast

The western portion of the route, from Nessara to the vicinity of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), passes through waters sheltered by offshore islands that break the force of the ocean swell and provide a lee for vessels travelling close to shore. The coast here is low and gently sloping, the fertile fields of the coastal strip running down to beaches lined with fishing villages and small ports serving the agricultural communities of the hinterland. The waters are shallow and sandy, and vessels must be careful to avoid sandbars shifting with seasonal currents.

The central portion, from Amurra eastward toward [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), passes through the busiest and most prosperous stretch of the Sahrani coast, where the largest cities and most important harbours are concentrated. The coast is more varied, with rocky headlands, sheltered bays, and the mouths of major rivers draining the coastal plain. The waters are deeper and the current stronger, and the traffic is dense enough that vessels must observe navigational protocols established by the Northern Coast League to prevent collisions. The eastern portion, from Khalisar to Qassir, passes through a region where the coast becomes more arid and settlements are more scattered, shifting from dense urban commerce to the more measured pace of the eastern frontier.

## The Ports and the Harbours

[Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) is the western terminus, a city at the boundary between the fertile coast and the arid west. Its harbour is a natural anchorage sheltered by a small offshore island, its quays lined with warehouses holding the products of western farms and goods arriving from the interior through caravan routes. [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) is the greatest port on the central coast and the most important single node of the route, its harbour the primary point of export for the spices that are the most valuable commodity in the known world. The spice trade of Amurra is the foundation of the city's extraordinary wealth. The harbour is divided into the inner basin, where larger vessels are unloaded, and the outer roadstead, where smaller vessels and fishing boats lie at anchor.

Khalisar is the largest city on the Sahrani coast and the de facto capital of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league). Its harbour is a broad, sheltered bay protected by a long spit of land curving around the anchorage, and the quays lining the inner shore can accommodate a fleet at any state of the tide. Khalisar is the primary point of transhipment for goods of the interior arriving through caravan routes, and its markets are the most diverse and cosmopolitan on the Sahrani coast.

## The Goods of the Route

The eastward traffic carries wine and oil of the Nessara region, spices and dyes of the Amurra hinterland, and cloth and manufactured goods of the Khalisar workshops. The westward traffic carries fish and salt of the eastern coast, timber and resins of the eastern highlands, and goods from the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles). The most distinctive cargo is the salt harvested from the salt pans of the eastern coast, essential to food preservation in coastal cities and provisioning of vessels crossing the ocean.

## The Peoples of the Route

The mariners of the Sahrani coast are among the most skilled in the known world, their knowledge of coastal conditions and seasonal weather patterns the product of a lifetime of experience on these particular waters. The captains commanding coastal vessels are drawn from families that have sailed the route for generations. The merchants trading along the route represent the trading houses of every city-state in the Northern Coast League, their competition conducted within the framework of the league's regulations. The [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) of the desert interior are frequent passengers, for the route is the fastest and safest means of travel between the coastal cities.

## The Hazards of the Coast

The most common hazard is the shifting sandbar forming at the mouths of rivers and entrances to harbours, capable of grounding a vessel and holding it fast for days. The sandbars are marked on charts but shift with every major storm and seasonal flood. The storms of the Auric Ocean sweep into coastal waters with a force driving vessels to seek shelter in the nearest harbour. The storm season on the Sahrani coast runs from late summer into autumn, and vessels travelling during this period must be prepared to make for shelter at short notice. The network of refuge harbours lining the coast is a critical feature of the route, and the location and condition of these refuges are among the most important pieces of knowledge a coastal mariner must possess.

## Economic and Cultural Significance

The Northern Coast Route is the principal artery of the Sahrani coastal economy. The route connects the cities and regions into a single commercial network, and the flow of goods along it is the foundation of the prosperity of every participating city-state. The route serves as a channel for exchange of culture and ideas, and the movement of peoples along its waters has shaped the languages, customs, and artistic traditions of the Sahrani civilisation. The cohesion of the Northern Coast League is sustained by the route, and the regular contact between member cities is critical to the political and commercial unity of the alliance.
