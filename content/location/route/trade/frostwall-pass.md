## Overview

The Frostwall Pass is the principal summer crossing through the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches), joining the settled ranges of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to the cold high valleys beyond the escarpment. The pass is not one narrow defile but a succession of shelves, gates, and ravines rising through the dark stone face known as the Frostwall. It carries ore, worked metal, grain, salt, and messages connecting the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) to the wider dwarven realms. During the months when snow retreats, guarded caravans travel between [Grimhold](/content/location/settlement/city/grimhold), the lesser clan settlements, and the roads joining the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds). During the long winter closure, the pass is a line of sealed doors and buried waymarkers, remembered by the communities that depend upon its return.

The pass has strategic as well as commercial value. The Frostwall stands between the central ranges and remote plateaus where travelling peoples, wild animals, and sudden weather movements can approach the settled holds. The clan council treats the road as a defended work, assigning patrols, refuge keepers, and engineers to it before the summer traffic begins. The route is open to peaceful merchants under clan protection, but every convoy accepts the authority of the wardens and follows rules designed for mountain safety.

## The Approach to the Wall

The southern approach begins in valleys where roads from the Stoneheim ranges converge. The route climbs through country of dark fir, exposed scree, and narrow pasture, passing abandoned adits and seasonal shelters of goat herders. The lower road is broad enough for wagons, though its gradients remain severe, with retaining walls supporting the outer edge wherever the slope falls toward a gorge. In spring the meltwater crosses the road in many places, and the first work of the clan crews clears the channels and rebuilds small bridges shifted by winter ice. The road wardens place fresh white stones at each crossing, making the safe line visible even when mist lies low over the valley.

The upper approach follows a shelf beneath the first cliff of the Frostwall. The rock is dark, fine-grained, and streaked with pale ice that remains after the surrounding snow has gone. Here the road narrows, and the outer side is protected by a waist-high parapet of carefully fitted blocks that gives animals a solid edge to follow when wind drives snow across the surface. Small alcoves cut into the inner wall at regular intervals allow a traveller or led animal to move aside while a loaded convoy passes. The first gate is reached after a bend called the Three Hammers, where three slabs of stone rise beside the road. The gate controls the transition from lower clan territories into the defended march, its doors made of timber faced with iron, and the chambers above contain stores of rope, lamp oil, and dried food. The gate wardens record the names of caravan masters, the number of animals, and the declared destination of each convoy.

## The High Road and Its Stations

Beyond the first gate, the route enters a series of steep-sided ravines connected by engineered causeways. Some causeways are cut directly into rock, while others rest on arches spanning gullies filled with snow. Dwarven masons inspect the arches every year, tapping the stone and listening for the hollow note signalling a hidden crack. Human merchants sometimes regard this practice as excessive, but the clan engineers point to the age of the structures and the weight of the traffic they bear. The pass has no room for a failed bridge, for a blocked ravine can divide the marches for weeks.

The principal inhabited station is [Frostwall Outpost](/content/location/settlement/outpost/frostwall-outpost), a small refuge built where the high road passes beneath an overhanging shelf. The outpost maintains a heated hall, a stable gallery, a cistern, and a signal tower whose roof can be seen from the next ridge in clear weather. It is staffed by wardens from several clans rather than belonging to one family, a practice intended to keep the station neutral during disputes. Higher still, the road passes a chain of refuge halls cut into the wall itself, each containing sleeping platforms, a hearth, and a sealed store of grain and fuel.

## Traffic and Cargoes

Southbound traffic consists chiefly of ore, metalwork, and stone from the remote valleys. Raw ore moves in compact sledges during the early opening of the pass, when patches of old snow make sled runners easier than wheels. Later, when the surface is dry, wagons become more efficient, and the road is crowded with loads bound for the lower ranges. The clans send iron bars, copper, silver-bearing stone, tools, armour, and fittings for mines and fortifications. Northbound traffic carries supplies the high country cannot produce in sufficient quantity: grain and salt are most important, followed by cloth, lamp oil, leather, and timber.

Travel is arranged in measured stages. A convoy leaves one station only after the wardens have confirmed that the next refuge has room and that weather permits the higher crossing. The speed of the whole train is set by the slowest loaded wagon, and the clans discourage merchants from racing ahead to gain a place at the next gate.

## Weather and Defence

Snow is the pass's most obvious hazard, though the danger comes as much from its movement as from its depth. A slope that appears secure can release after a warm afternoon, carrying ice and stone over a road that was clear at dawn. Wind can also fill a cut road within an hour, and the fresh surface hides holes, loose stone, and the edges of ravines. The first convoy of the season travels with engineers and does not carry valuable cargo; its task is to sound the road and reopen the route for those who follow. The pass is defended by watchtowers and patrols rather than by a single large garrison, with small clan parties moving between the gates and signal fires or mirrors passing warnings from one height to another.

## History and Significance

The Frostwall Pass is older than the present clan council. The oldest sections are narrow tracks cut into the cliff, their makers unknown, though dwarven inscriptions appear on several retaining walls later incorporated into the road. The clans enlarged the crossing as their settlements expanded beyond the wall, constructing the first refuge halls and permanent gates. Grimhold became the administrative centre of the route because its fortress could gather workers, soldiers, and stores in one place. The pass remains the defended hinge of the deep interior, giving the remote clans access to markets, tools, and allies while bringing the products of their mines to the wider world. The Frostwall Pass is not merely a road through difficult country but the seasonal promise that the high valleys remain part of a larger realm.
