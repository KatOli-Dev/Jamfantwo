## Overview

The Stoneheim Highland Road is the principal overland route between the central halls of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) and the coastal markets of Morvind, descending from the mountain interior through high valleys and broken foothills before reaching the harbours of the Stormfall shore. The road is a chain of engineered tracks rather than a single uninterrupted highway, for its builders have followed ridges, stream terraces, and shelves of sound rock wherever the mountains allow. It carries the forged iron, steel, tools, weapons, and worked stone of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) outward, while grain, salt, cloth, timber, and coastal goods travel inward to the dwarven halls. Its traffic is most abundant during the open season, yet the road remains important in winter because the stores gathered along it determine whether the highland communities pass the dark months in comfort or scarcity.

The name refers to Stoneheim as the source and destination of the greatest portion of its traffic, though the road serves many holds and mining settlements beyond the capital. Caravans use different branches according to the season, the condition of the snow, and the destination of their cargo. The eastern branch reaches [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar) and the iron workings of the lesser holds, while the western branch joins the routes that descend toward [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch) and the strait trade. The road therefore functions as a spine within a larger network, and its importance is measured not only by the wagons that travel its marked sections but also by the mines, halls, ferries, and refuge stations that depend upon its existence.

## The Highland Course

The road begins beneath the eastern escarpment of Stoneheim, where the last broad galleries of the city open toward a stone-paved approach cut into the mountain face. Dwarven engineers maintain the first miles with particular care, setting retaining walls against the slopes and carving drains beside the wheel tracks. The air remains cool and dry near the city, and the road passes through a succession of gatehouses that record the departure of ore trains and the arrival of supply caravans. These gates are not intended to hinder ordinary traffic. They mark the boundary between the protected halls and the exposed road, and they allow the wardens to account for every convoy before it enters the high country.

Beyond the maintained approaches, the road climbs through valleys where the slopes are too steep for fields but suitable for hardy goats and patches of mountain barley. The surface changes from fitted stone to compacted gravel, with timber bridges spanning the streams that descend from the upper snows. In the early summer the water runs high and brown with melt, and a caravan may wait half a day for the bridge crews to lower temporary braces and inspect the foundations. The road avoids the deepest valley floors, where floods and falling stone are common, and instead crosses high shelves that offer wider views of the surrounding ranges. The climb is demanding for animals, but the shelves provide firm ground and allow the wagons to keep their loads level.

The central course passes near [Ironhollow](/content/location/settlement/town/ironhollow), whose furnaces supply much of the raw iron used in the Stoneheim forges. A branch road connects the settlement's ore yards to the main road, and convoys often divide there according to the weight and urgency of their cargo. Raw ore moves in heavy wagons drawn by teams of broad-backed mountain beasts, while finished metalwork travels in smaller, better-guarded carts. The distinction has shaped the road itself. The eastern shelves are broad enough for ore trains to pass one another, while the western descent is narrower and contains frequent lay-bys where loaded wagons can be drawn aside for lighter traffic.

## Stations and Road Custom

The principal refuge on the upper road is [Stoneheim Pick](/content/location/settlement/outpost/stoneheim-pick), a small inhabited station at a shoulder of dark rock where several highland branches meet. The outpost provides fodder, lamp oil, rope, and covered space for the repair of harness and wheels. Its storehouses also hold a reserve of grain and salt maintained by the holds, for a blocked pass can leave travellers at the station longer than any ordinary schedule allows. Stoneheim Pick is not a town in the lowland manner. Its buildings cling to the slope around a cistern and a walled yard, and its population rises and falls with the road traffic, though a permanent corps of wardens, smiths, cooks, and stable keepers remains throughout the year.

Road custom requires a caravan master to declare the nature of a load at the first gatehouse and to present the same account at the principal stations. The practice is less a matter of taxation than of safety, since the wardens need to know which wagons carry unstable ore, volatile furnace materials, or goods that require shelter from snow. The [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) levy a measured road contribution, and the money is spent on bridges, retaining walls, beacon stores, and the wages of the wardens. Merchants sometimes complain about delays caused by inspection, but the same merchants are quick to defend the system when a bridge fails or a snowfield covers the route. Records from the road show that the system prevents more losses than it imposes.

Caravans usually travel in groups of six to twenty wagons, joined by pack trains from the smaller holds. The larger convoys carry their own smiths and masons, while small companies rely upon the station workers and the hospitality of the highland communities. Dwarven route keepers walk ahead of the wagons with measuring rods and hammers, testing the road surface and the edges of bridges. Human merchants from the coast are common during the summer, and their knowledge of harbour prices gives the highland traders a useful account of conditions beyond the mountains. The two peoples have developed a careful division of responsibility: dwarven officers govern the road, while human agents often manage the sale of cargo once it reaches the shore.

## Goods and Exchange

The outward trade of the road begins in the mines and forges. Bars of iron and steel are packed in straw and sealed against moisture, while finished blades, fittings, and tools are wrapped in oiled cloth. Stoneheim also sends engraved plates, hinges, cooking vessels, chain, and the durable hardware used in the construction of halls and fortifications. The finest objects travel under separate guard, not because the common road is lawless but because the value of a single chest of decorated metalwork can equal the yearly output of a small mining settlement. Merchant houses maintain marked wagons for these loads, and the mark is recognised from Stoneheim to the coastal warehouses.

The return traffic is more varied and less conspicuous. Grain from the river valleys forms the largest portion, followed by salt from the shore, wool and woven cloth from the coastal settlements, and timber selected for beams and pit supports. The mountain holds need little imported metal, but they depend upon coastal shipwrights for tar, rope, sailcloth, and certain hardwoods that do not grow above the tree line. Dried fish is also prized in the deepest halls, where fresh food is scarce during the closed season. In return, the coastal merchants value the regular supply of tools and iron fittings that makes their own building and shipping possible.

## Hazards and Protection

The road's chief hazards arise from the mountain rather than from hostile travellers. Rockfalls follow thawing weather, avalanches descend from slopes that appear stable in the morning, and streams can destroy a bridge within an hour of heavy rain. The wardens maintain signal poles at exposed bends, and each refuge station keeps a set of iron bells whose patterns distinguish a blocked road from a request for military aid. During the summer, crews work continuously to clear debris and reinforce the shelves. The road is never considered finished, because frost and water alter the mountain every year.

Banditry is uncommon on the central road because the holds patrol it closely, but theft still occurs at crowded stations and during the confusion of a river crossing. The greater danger comes from creatures and raiders of the remote uplands, who sometimes test the outer branches when the garrisons are thin. Convoys therefore place the most valuable wagons in the centre and keep armed guards at the front and rear. A caravan that loses an animal is expected to report the loss at the next station, both to assist recovery and to prevent a frightened beast from wandering onto a bridge or into a mine yard. The discipline of the road is practical and mutual, founded upon the knowledge that every traveller may depend upon the same system on a later journey.

## History and Significance

The first sections of the Stoneheim Highland Road were little more than mule tracks between old mine entrances. As the holds expanded, the need to move ore in greater quantity led to the construction of retaining walls, drainage channels, and the first permanent bridges. The work was carried out by several holds under the direction of Stoneheim engineers, and the agreements that governed labour and tolls became an early foundation of the present realm. Later improvements joined the separate tracks into a recognisable road, though the older branches remain in use where they offer a safer route around a damaged section.

The road has also served as a diplomatic path. Councillors, inspectors, smiths, and envoys travel it between the holds, and disputes over mining rights or road contributions are often settled at the same stations where merchants weigh their cargoes. The connection to the Stormfall ports gives the dwarven realm an influence beyond the mountains, while the supplies moving inward prevent the highland communities from becoming isolated enclaves. The Stoneheim Highland Road is therefore both a commercial artery and a civic work. Its bridges and walls express the practical strength of the holds, and its continuing traffic binds the deep halls to the coast and to every market that values the products of dwarven labour.
