## Overview

The Northern Coast Route is the coastal shipping passage that follows the northern shore of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), connecting the chain of city-states and trading ports that line the fertile strip between the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert. The route runs from [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) in the west to [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir) in the east, following the curve of the coast through the warm and sheltered waters that have been the highway of the Sahrani maritime civilisation since the earliest records were kept. The route is the primary artery of the coast, carrying the goods, the peoples, and the ideas that sustain the League of the Northern Coast, the alliance of the city-states that controls the most valuable stretch of maritime real estate in the southern world. The Northern Coast Route is not a single path but a corridor, a band of water that runs close to the shore where the conditions are favourable and the harbours are frequent, and the vessels that travel it are the lifeblood of the coastal civilisation.

## The Geography of the Coast

The northern coast of Sahrana is a study in contrasts, and the route that follows it must adapt to the changing character of the shore at every stage of the journey. The western portion of the route, from Nessara to the vicinity of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), passes through waters that are sheltered by the offshore islands that break the force of the ocean swell and provide a lee for the vessels that travel close to the shore. The coast in this region is low and gently sloping, the fertile fields of the coastal strip running down to the beaches that are lined with the fishing villages and the small ports that serve the agricultural communities of the hinterland. The waters are shallow and the bottom is sandy, and the vessels that use this portion of the route must be careful to avoid the sandbars that shift with the seasonal currents and the storms that occasionally reshape the coastline.

The central portion of the route, from Amurra eastward toward [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), passes through the busiest and the most prosperous stretch of the Sahrani coast, where the largest cities and the most important harbours are concentrated. The coast here is more varied, with rocky headlands, sheltered bays, and the mouths of the major rivers that drain the coastal plain. The waters are deeper and the current is stronger, and the traffic is dense enough that the vessels must observe the navigational protocols that the [League of the Northern Coast](/content/government/national/league-of-the-northern-coast) has established to prevent the collisions that would otherwise be a daily occurrence. The eastern portion of the route, from Khalisar to Qassir, passes through a region where the coast becomes more arid and the settlements are more scattered, and the character of the route shifts from the dense urban commerce of the central coast to the more measured pace of the eastern frontier. The waters off the eastern coast are open and exposed, and the vessels that travel this portion of the route must be prepared to face the full force of the ocean weather that sweeps in from the open Auric.

## The Ports and the Harbours

The Northern Coast Route is punctuated by a series of ports and harbours that serve as the nodes of the coastal trade, each with its own character and its own role in the economy of the coast. [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) is the western terminus of the route, a city that stands at the boundary between the fertile coast and the arid west, and its harbour is the point of departure for the vessels that carry the wine, the oil, and the hardy grains of the western coastal strip to the markets of the central cities. The harbour of Nessara is a natural anchorage sheltered by a small offshore island, and its quays are lined with the warehouses that hold the products of the western farms and the goods that arrive from the interior through the caravan routes that converge at the city.

[Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) is the greatest port on the central coast and the most important single node of the Northern Coast Route, its harbour the primary point of export for the spices that are the most valuable commodity in the known world. The spice trade of Amurra is the foundation of the city's extraordinary wealth, and the vessels that call at its harbour are drawn from every nation that trades in the Auric Ocean. The harbour of Amurra is a work of engineering as much as a natural formation, its breakwaters and its quays constructed over centuries of investment and maintained by the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) with the revenue from the spice trade. The harbour is divided into the inner basin, where the larger vessels are unloaded, and the outer roadstead, where the smaller vessels and the fishing boats lie at anchor, and the constant movement of the shipping is a spectacle that draws the admiration of every visitor to the city.

Khalisar is the largest city on the Sahrani coast and the de facto capital of the [League of the Northern Coast](/content/government/national/league-of-the-northern-coast), and its harbour is the busiest on the route after Amurra. The harbour of Khalisar is a broad, sheltered bay that is protected by a long spit of land that curves around the anchorage, and the quays that line the inner shore are capable of accommodating a fleet of vessels at any state of the tide. Khalisar is the primary point of transhipment for the goods of the interior that arrive through the caravan routes, and the markets of the city are the most diverse and the most cosmopolitan on the Sahrani coast.

## The Goods of the Route

The cargoes that move along the Northern Coast Route are a reflection of the diversity of the Sahrani coast itself, and the goods that are carried between the ports are as varied as the climates and the cultures of the regions that the route connects. The eastward traffic carries the products of the western and the central regions to the markets of the east, including the wine and the oil of the Nessara region, the spices and the dyes of the Amurra hinterland, and the cloth and the manufactured goods of the Khalisar workshops. The westward traffic carries the products of the eastern regions to the central and the western markets, including the fish and the salt of the eastern coast, the timber and the resins of the eastern highlands, and the goods that arrive from the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) and the distant reaches of the ocean.

The most distinctive cargo that travels the Northern Coast Route is the salt that is harvested from the salt pans of the eastern coast, a product that is essential to the preservation of the food of the coastal cities and the provisioning of the vessels that cross the ocean. The salt of the eastern pans is among the purest in the known world, and the trade in salt is a significant component of the economy of the eastern ports. The vessels that carry the salt westward along the route are a common sight, their holds filled with the white crystals that are the most humble but the most necessary of the Sahrani exports.

## The Peoples of the Route

The population that travels and works along the Northern Coast Route is drawn from the rich diversity of the Sahrani peoples, and the vessels that ply the coastal waters are crewed by the sailors of every city and every region that the route touches. The mariners of the Sahrani coast are among the most skilled in the known world, their knowledge of the coastal conditions and the seasonal patterns of the weather the product of a lifetime of experience on these particular waters. The captains who command the coastal vessels are drawn from the families that have sailed the route for generations, and their knowledge of the harbours, the hazards, and the commercial practices of each port is the foundation of the efficiency and the reliability of the coastal trade.

The merchants who trade along the route are a similarly diverse group, representing the trading houses of every city-state that is a member of the League of the Northern Coast. The competition between the merchants for the best cargoes and the most favourable terms is a constant feature of the coastal commerce, but the competition is conducted within the framework of the league's regulations, which are designed to prevent the abuses and the conflicts that would disrupt the trade. The [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) of the desert interior are frequent passengers on the coastal vessels, for the route is the fastest and the safest means of travel between the cities of the coast, and the presence of the salawa traders and the travellers on the vessels is a reminder of the connection between the coast and the desert that is the foundation of the Sahrani economy.

## The Hazards of the Coast

The hazards of the Northern Coast Route are less dramatic than those of the open ocean passages, but they are no less real, and the mariners who sail the coastal waters must be constantly alert to the dangers that the shore and the weather can present. The most common hazard is the shifting sandbar that forms at the mouths of the rivers and the entrances to the harbours, a danger that can ground a vessel and hold it fast for days while the tide and the current work to free it. The sandbars are marked on the charts that the mariners use, but the charts can never be entirely up to date, for the sandbars shift with every major storm and every seasonal flood.

The storms of the Auric Ocean are a hazard for the coastal route as well, for the same weather systems that trouble the open ocean can sweep into the coastal waters with a force that drives the vessels to seek shelter in the nearest harbour. The storm season on the Sahrani coast runs from the late summer into the autumn, and the vessels that travel the route during this period must be prepared to make for the shelter of the harbours at short notice. The network of refuge harbours that line the coast is a critical feature of the route, and the location and the condition of these refuges are among the most important pieces of knowledge that a coastal mariner must possess.

## Economic and Cultural Significance

The Northern Coast Route is the principal artery of the Sahrani coastal economy, and its significance to the civilisation of the Sahrani coast is beyond measure. The route connects the cities and the regions of the coast into a single commercial network, and the flow of goods along the route is the foundation of the prosperity of every city-state that participates in the trade. The route also serves as a channel for the exchange of culture and of ideas between the regions of the coast, and the movement of the peoples along its waters has shaped the languages, the customs, and the artistic traditions of the Sahrani civilisation. The cohesion of the League of the Northern Coast is sustained by the route, and the regular contact that the coastal vessels provide between the member cities is a critical factor in the maintenance of the political and the commercial unity of the alliance. The Northern Coast Route is a symbol of the prosperity and the connectedness of the Sahrani coast, and its continued operation is essential to the future of the civilisation that has grown along its waters.
