## Overview

The Grassland Traderoad is the network of overland routes that cross the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), connecting the settled cities of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) to one another and to the markets of the eastern coast and the southern frontier. The traderoad is not a single paved highway but a system of routes that shift with the seasons, the condition of the ground, and the movements of the herds that are the foundation of the plains economy. The routes follow the lines of the rivers and the ridges, crossing the open grass at the points where the ground is firmest and the water is most accessible, and the traffic that moves along them is a constant procession of the merchants, the herders, the travellers, and the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds whose way of life is inseparable from the open country that the traderoad crosses.

## The Geography of the Traderoad

The Grassland Traderoad is woven into the geography of the plains, following the natural lines of communication that the land provides and the peoples of the plains have used for centuries. The principal route runs from east to west, connecting the eastern cities that lie at the edge of the plains with the interior settlements that are scattered across the open grass. The eastern terminus of the traderoad is at [Stagford](/content/location/settlement/city/stagford), a city that commands the primary river crossing of the central grasslands and controls the flow of the traffic that moves between the plains and the eastern coast. From Stagford, the route strikes westward across the open grass, following the line of the river that gives the city its name and crossing the plains through the country that is the most productive and the most densely settled of the grassland region.

The central hub of the traderoad is [Goldmeadow](/content/location/settlement/city/goldmeadow), a city that stands at the meeting of the river and the road at the heart of the plains. Goldmeadow is the largest and the most prosperous of the interior cities, and its market is the primary point of exchange for the goods that move along the traderoad. The routes that converge at Goldmeadow include the east-west passage from Stagford, the northern route that connects the city to the timbered country of the northern plains, and the southern route that strikes toward the frontier towns of [Dustford](/content/location/settlement/town/dustford) and [Redhollow](/content/location/settlement/town/redhollow). The convergence of these routes at Goldmeadow is the foundation of the city's prosperity, and the market of the city is the most important commercial gathering in the interior of Aethelar.

The southern extension of the traderoad runs from Goldmeadow through [Sunmere](/content/location/settlement/city/sunmere), the largest city of the southern plains, and continues southward to the frontier at Dustford, where the grasslands give way to the warm, dry country of the far south. This southern route is the most important of the traderoad's branches for the long-distance trade, for it connects the Plains Confederation to the markets of the south and the caravans that cross the country beyond the grasslands. The route passes through the country that is the home of the southern centaur kindreds and the settled communities of the southern plains, and the traffic that moves along it is a mix of the local trade in livestock and the grain and the long-distance commerce in the goods that are carried to and from the frontier.

## The Goods of the Traderoad

The cargoes that move along the Grassland Traderoad are the products of the plains themselves, and the trade that flows along the route is a reflection of the agricultural and the pastoral economy of the grassland region. The eastward traffic carries the goods of the interior to the markets of the coast, including the grain that is grown in the fertile soil of the plains, the wool and the leather that are produced from the herds that graze upon the grass, and the livestock that is driven eastward to the slaughterhouses and the markets of the coastal cities. The grain of the plains is the most important of these exports, for the fertility of the grassland soil is the foundation of the agricultural wealth of the interior, and the grain that is carried eastward along the traderoad feeds the populations of the coastal cities that cannot grow enough for themselves.

The westward traffic along the traderoad carries the goods of the coast and the wider world to the markets of the interior, including the cloth and the metal goods that are manufactured in the eastern cities, the salt that is harvested from the coastal pans, and the exotic goods that arrive from [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) through the ports of the east. The trade in the horses of the plains is a distinctive feature of the traderoad, for the horses that range wild upon the grass are the finest in the known world, and the trade in the tamed and trained horses is a significant component of the commerce that moves along the route. The horses of the plains are sought after by every nation that can afford them, and the caravans that carry them eastward are among the most valuable and the most carefully guarded of the traderoad traffic.

## The Peoples of the Traderoad

The Grassland Traderoad is travelled by a diverse population of peoples whose lives are shaped by the rhythms of the plains and the demands of the trade that the route sustains. The merchants who organise the caravans and manage the trade are based chiefly in the cities of the Plains Confederation, and their counting houses and their warehouses are concentrated at the commercial hubs of Stagford, Goldmeadow, and Sunmere. These merchants are drawn from the settled [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) population of the plains, and their wealth and their influence are the foundation of the commercial power of the confederation.

The centaur kindreds of the plains are the other great population that moves along the traderoad, and their way of life is inseparable from the routes that cross the grass. The centaurs do not travel the traderoad as merchants in the same sense as the humans, but their movements across the plains follow the same routes, and their knowledge of the country is the most comprehensive of any people who live upon the grass. The centaurs are the guardians of the traderoad, for they know the condition of the river crossings, the location of the springs and the watering holes, and the state of the grass that the caravans must cross. The relationship between the centaurs and the human merchants is one of mutual benefit, for the centaurs provide the knowledge and the guidance that the caravans need, and the humans provide the goods and the markets that the centaurs cannot obtain from the plains alone.

The [halflings](/content/species/sapient/halflings) of the river valleys are a third population that contributes to the traderoad, for their settlements line the watercourses that the route follows, and the halflings are the innkeepers, the ferrymen, and the provisioners of the road. The halflings are noted for their hospitality and their skill in the preparation of the food and the drink that sustain the travellers who pass through their communities, and the inns of the river valleys are among the most welcome sights on the traderoad for the merchants and the herders who have spent days in the open grass.

## The Hazards of the Traderoad

The hazards of the Grassland Traderoad are those of the plains themselves, and the travellers who cross the grass must contend with the weather, the wildlife, and the bandits that are the perennial threats to the commerce of the interior. The weather of the plains is the most immediate and the most constant hazard, and the storms that sweep across the open grass in the summer and the blizzards that descend in the winter are the causes of the greatest losses on the traderoad. The summer storms bring lightning and hail that can kill the livestock and the travellers who are caught in the open, and the winter blizzards can strand a caravan for days while the snow piles high around the wagons and the tents. The caravans that travel the traderoad are equipped for the extremes of the plains weather, and the knowledge of the signs that precede the storms is among the most valued skills that the guides and the centaur kindreds possess.

The predators of the plains are another hazard, and the packs of the grey plains wolf that range across the grass are a threat to the livestock that travels with the caravans. The wolves are cautious and they seldom attack the larger caravans that are well guarded, but the smaller parties and the solitary travellers are vulnerable to the attention of the wolf packs, and the loss of a draft animal to the wolves can be a catastrophe for the caravan that depends upon it. The plains lion is a less common but more fearsome threat, and the caravans that travel the southern routes are careful to keep the fires burning through the night when the lions are known to be in the vicinity.

The bandits of the plains are the third great hazard, and the history of the traderoad is marked by the recurring challenge of the outlaws who prey on the commerce that passes through their territories. The bandits operate from the hidden camps that are concealed in the river valleys and the groves of the windbreak trees, and they strike at the slower and the less well-defended caravans with a precision that suggests careful planning and a thorough knowledge of the road. The Plains Confederation maintains a force of road guards who patrol the traderoad and respond to the reports of bandit activity, and the presence of the guards has reduced the threat but has not eliminated it entirely.

## Economic and Cultural Significance

The Grassland Traderoad is the economic backbone of the Plains Confederation, and its significance to the political and the commercial life of the central grasslands is beyond dispute. The trade that flows along the route is the foundation of the prosperity of the confederation's member cities, and the revenue that is generated by the tolls and the commerce of the traderoad sustains the administration and the defence of the alliance. The route is also a channel for the exchange of culture and of ideas between the settled and the nomadic peoples of the plains, and the movement of the centaur kindreds and the human merchants along its length has shaped the traditions and the customs of both populations. The traderoad is a symbol of the connection between the peoples of the plains, and the routes that cross the grass are the threads that bind the diverse communities of the interior into a single civilisation. The future of the traderoad will depend upon the continued co-operation between the settled cities and the centaur kindreds, and the agreements that govern the use of the route will continue to evolve as the trade and the population of the plains grow and change.
