## Overview

The [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) Caravan Way is the network of overland routes crossing the Kharesh desert of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), connecting the fertile coastal cities of the north to the scattered oases, upland settlements, and distant reaches of the interior. The way is not a single road but a web of routes, each chosen for access to water, favourable terrain, and avoidance of the worst hazards the desert offers. The caravans travelling these routes are the lifeline of the interior, carrying goods of the coast inland and returning with spices, minerals, precious stones, and other products of the deep desert that have made the fortune of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) trade. The Kharesh Caravan Way is among the oldest and most demanding trade routes in the known world.

## The Geography of the Routes

The Caravan Way begins at the chain of coastal cities lining the northern shore of Sahrana, with primary termini at [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), and [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), each serving as the point of departure for caravans striking southward into the desert. The routes from these cities converge within a few days of travel, joining into a smaller number of main arteries. The principal route runs south-south-west from the coast, following the line of seasonal watercourses providing the most reliable access to water in the marginal zone between fertile coast and deep desert. This route passes through [Dunesend](/content/location/settlement/outpost/dunesend), serving as the final staging point before the true desert, and from there strikes southward into the great erg, the vast field of shifting dunes at the heart of the Kharesh.

Beyond the erg, the route reaches rocky uplands rising from the sands like islands in a sea of stone. The uplands of the central Kharesh are a chain of massifs running from north-west to south-east, their sheltered valleys and perennial springs providing water and shade for settled communities and visiting caravans. The most significant are the oasis towns of [Kheled](/content/location/settlement/village/kheled) and [Shurra](/content/location/settlement/village/shurra), each situated at the base of a massif where groundwater emerges. Beyond the central uplands, the route continues southward toward the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) and the inland seas that lie beyond the Kharesh.

## The Goods of the Caravan Trade

The southward caravans carry manufactured goods, cloth, tools, and foodstuffs the interior communities cannot produce for themselves, including grain of the coastal strip, iron and metal goods imported from [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and fine cloth woven in the coastal cities. The northward caravans carry the products that have made Sahrana the most sought-after source of luxury goods: spices gathered from desert plants and rocky uplands, dyes extracted from insects and minerals of the interior, and precious stones mined from veins running through upland massifs. The most valuable cargo is the spice known as sunroot, a rare and aromatic substance found only in the deepest and most inaccessible parts of the desert, harvested by nomads who trade it to the caravans for goods of the coast.

## The Peoples of the Way

The merchants organising the caravans are based chiefly in the coastal cities, where they finance expeditions and arrange for sale of returning cargoes. These merchants are members of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the two great commercial alliances dominating Sahrani trade. The guides who lead caravans across the desert are drawn from the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa), the nomadic people whose knowledge of the Kharesh is the most comprehensive of any sapient inhabitants. Their understanding of shifting dunes, hidden water sources, and seasonal patterns of desert weather is the foundation upon which the caravan trade depends.

The nomads inhabiting the deep desert, including the salawa and the [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) tribes ranging across the erg, are the primary suppliers of goods the caravans carry northward. Their exchanges with the caravans are conducted according to traditions older than any written law, and the terms of trade are established through negotiation and mutual recognition.

## The Hazards of the Crossing

The crossing of the Kharesh is among the most dangerous journeys any traveller can undertake. The most immediate and relentless hazard is the lack of water. The distances between reliable sources in the deep desert are measured in days and weeks, and the margin between sufficient provision and fatal shortage is narrow in the extreme. The caravans carry water in goatskins and sealed clay vessels, and the journey's schedule is dictated by the locations of wells and springs. The wells of the Kharesh are the most precious resource of the desert, protected by traditions and agreements among the peoples who use them. A well that is polluted or a spring that has dried up can be a catastrophe for a caravan with no alternative source within reach.

The heat of the Kharesh is the second great hazard. The combination of intense sun by day and freezing cold by night tests the endurance of every traveller. The caravans travel by night during the hottest months. The seasonal winds generate sandstorms capable of burying a caravan in hours. The bandits and raiders of the desert are the third great hazard, drawn from marginal communities on the fringes of the desert. The caravans are defended by armed guards recruited from coastal cities and desert tribes.

## Economic and Cultural Significance

The Kharesh Caravan Way is the economic backbone of the Sahrani interior. The trade it sustains is the foundation of the prosperity of the coastal cities, for the spices, minerals, and precious stones of the interior are the primary exports flowing through the harbours of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) to markets beyond. The Caravan Way is also a channel for exchange of culture and ideas between the coast and the interior, and the stories told around the campfires of the way blend the traditions of both worlds.

## The Future of the Caravan Way

The growth of maritime trade has shifted some traffic from overland routes to the sea, and the caravans travelling the Kharesh are fewer than in earlier centuries. The knowledge of the desert that is the foundation of the Caravan Way remains a resource that cannot be replaced, and the salawa and other nomadic peoples holding that knowledge will continue to sustain the trade for as long as there is value in the goods the interior provides.
