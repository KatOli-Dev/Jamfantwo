## Overview

The [Velden](/content/location/settlement/city/velden) Strait Run is the maritime passage through the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), the narrow channel that connects the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) to the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) and separates [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) from [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The run is the only navigable passage between the two great bodies of water that lie on either side of the central continent, and every vessel that travels between the northern and the southern seas must pass through its narrow and demanding waters. The strait is a point of concentration for the maritime traffic of three continents, and the wealth that passes through its channel is beyond any easy calculation. The towns that line its shores, including [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch) on the Morvind side and [Southport](/content/location/settlement/town/southport) on the Aethelaran side, have grown prosperous from the services that they provide to the vessels that navigate the passage, and their fortunes are bound up with the commerce that flows through the strait.

## The Geography and the Conditions

The Velden Strait is a narrow and twisting channel that runs between steep shores, its width varying from as little as two miles at the narrowest point to something more generous where the channel opens into the broader waters at either end. The passage runs roughly north-east to south-west, connecting the warmer and the calmer waters of the Auric Ocean to the colder and the more turbulent waters of the Stormfall Sea. The meeting of these two bodies of water at the narrowest point of the strait produces conditions that are among the most challenging for mariners anywhere in the known world. The warm current of the Auric, flowing steadily northward, presses against the cooler and the more variable waters of the Stormfall, creating eddies and tide-rips that can spin a vessel around and hold it against the current for hours at a time. The tidal range within the strait is considerable, and the direction and the strength of the current reverse with each tide, so that a vessel entering the strait at the wrong moment may find itself unable to make any headway against the flow, forced to anchor and wait for the change that may not come for many hours.

The shores of the strait are steep and rocky on both sides, though the character of the two coasts is distinct and each presents its own challenges to the navigator. The Morvind shore rises sharply from the water, the mountains of the continent descending almost to the beach in a series of cliffs and headlands that cast long shadows across the channel. The Aethelaran shore is gentler in its slope, a coast of low cliffs and sheltered coves that provide anchorages for the vessels that must wait for the tide to turn, but the waters off this shore are studded with the submerged rocks and the shoals that have wrecked many vessels in the course of the centuries. The navigation of the strait requires a detailed and current knowledge of the conditions on both sides, and the pilots who guide the vessels through the passage are trained to read the water and the weather with a precision that is the product of long and intensive study.

## The Towns and the Governance

The settlement and the governance of the strait are divided between the two shores, with the towns of Veldenwatch and Southport serving as the primary centres of the passage. Veldenwatch is the older and the more substantial of the two, its stone buildings rising from the rocky Morvind shore in a compact mass that reflects the wealth that the strait trade has brought. The town is the seat of the pilotage guild of the northern shore, and its harbour is the principal refuge for the vessels that are waiting to make the passage from the Stormfall Sea into the Auric Ocean. Southport, on the Aethelaran shore, is the counterpart of Veldenwatch, its harbour serving the vessels that approach the strait from the south, and the two towns together administer the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), the joint governance that regulates the passage through the strait and divides the revenue from the tolls and the pilotage fees between the two shores.

The Velden Compact is the political framework that has sustained the strait trade for more than a century, and its provisions govern every aspect of the passage, from the training and the licensing of the pilots to the collection and the distribution of the tolls. The compact is administered by a joint council that meets alternately in Veldenwatch and Southport, and the council is composed of the representatives of the pilotage guilds, the merchant communities, and the civic authorities of both towns. The decisions of the council are binding on all the parties to the compact, and the mechanisms for the resolution of the disputes that inevitably arise from the competition for the strait trade are well established and respected by all the parties. The compact has endured through periods of tension between [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and Morvind that might have closed the strait to the traffic of one or the other, and the mutual benefit that the arrangement provides has been sufficient to keep the channel open to all peaceful vessels regardless of their origin or their destination.

## The Goods of the Run

The cargoes that pass through the Velden Strait are a cross-section of the commerce of three continents, and the variety and the value of the goods that move along the run are a reflection of the diversity of the trade networks that converge at the channel. The vessels that approach the strait from the south carry the products of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) and the eastern coast of Aethelar, including the spices, the dyes, and the precious stones of the southern continent and the grain, the cloth, and the manufactured goods of the eastern Aethelaran kingdoms. These vessels pass through the strait into the Stormfall Sea, carrying the goods of the south to the markets of Morvind and the northern coast of Aethelar. The vessels that approach from the north carry the products of Morvind and the northern Aethelaran coast, including the metalwork and the minerals of the Morvind interior and the fish, the timber, and the furs of the northern kingdoms.

The most valuable single cargo that passes through the strait is the dwarf-forged metalwork of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), and the vessels that carry this cargo are among the most carefully guarded that navigate the passage. The smiths of Stoneheim, [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar), and the lesser holds produce the finest steel and the most intricate metalwork in the known world, and the demand for their products in the markets of the south is insatiable. The dwarves do not sail the seas themselves, but they maintain agents in Veldenwatch and Southport who oversee the shipment of their goods through the strait, and the counting houses that these agents occupy are among the most substantial and the most secure buildings in either town. The trade in dwarf-forged metalwork is the foundation of the strait economy, and the revenue that it generates is a significant component of the income of both Veldenwatch and Southport.

## The Peoples of the Run

The population that travels and serves the Velden Strait Run is among the most cosmopolitan in the known world, reflecting the concentration of the maritime traffic at this single point of passage. The pilots who guide the vessels through the strait are the most visible and the most respected of the strait peoples, their profession the product of generations of training and experience in the difficult waters of the channel. The pilots of Veldenwatch and Southport are organised into guilds that are among the oldest and the most powerful institutions in the strait towns, and the skills that they possess are passed from parent to child, refined and adapted by each generation's experience of the changing conditions of the passage. The pilots are drawn from the families that have lived on the shores of the strait for centuries, and their knowledge of the currents, the rocks, and the weather patterns is a resource that cannot be acquired from any chart or manual, learned only through years of practice on the water.

The merchants who trade through the strait are a similarly diverse group, representing the trading houses of every nation that has an interest in the intercontinental commerce. The markets of the strait towns offer goods from Aethelar, Morvind, and Sahrana side by side, and the merchants who gather there are accustomed to dealing with the representatives of every people and every nation. The [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) of the strait towns are a notable presence, their workshops producing the fine instruments and the navigational tools that are essential to the vessels that navigate the passage. The gnomes of Southport, in particular, are known for the quality of their chronometers and their compasses, and the instruments that are produced in the gnomish workshops of the strait are sought after by every captain who sails the waters of the known world.

## The Hazards of the Run

The hazards of the Velden Strait Run are as varied as they are formidable, and the vessels that pass through the channel must contend with the natural dangers of the water and the human dangers of the commerce that concentrates at the strait. The natural hazards are dominated by the currents and the weather, and the combination of the strong tidal flows and the narrow channel can produce conditions that test the skills of even the most experienced pilots. The storms that sweep across the Stormfall Sea can drive waves and winds through the strait with a force that makes the passage impossible, and the vessels that are caught in the channel during the worst of the weather must anchor in the shelter of the coves and wait for the conditions to improve. The winter months are the most dangerous period for the strait run, and the volume of the traffic during this season is reduced to a fraction of the summer peak.

The pirates and the raiders of the strait are the other great hazard, and the history of the passage is marked by the recurring challenge of the outlaws who prey on the vessels that pass through the narrow waters. The geography of the strait, with its hidden coves and its steep shores, provides the opportunities for the ambush that the raiders need, and the vessels that are forced to wait for the tide or the weather are vulnerable to attack. The joint naval patrols of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) and the navies of the [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) maintain a constant presence in the strait, but the pirates are resourceful and the rewards of a successful capture are sufficient to attract the desperate and the ambitious to the trade.

## Economic and Cultural Significance

The Velden Strait Run is one of the most significant trade routes in the known world, and its importance to the economies of the three continents that it connects is beyond dispute. The passage is the chokepoint through which the trade between the northern and the southern seas must flow, and the control of the strait is a matter of strategic interest to every nation that participates in the maritime commerce of the world. The revenue that is generated by the tolls and the services of the strait is a significant component of the wealth of the [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) and the Aldreth, and the strait towns themselves are among the most prosperous communities of their respective continents. The cultural significance of the strait is no less profound, for the channel is the meeting point of the peoples and the traditions of three continents, and the mingling of these traditions has produced a culture that is distinct from any single source. The strait towns are known for their tolerance and their openness, qualities that have been forced upon them by the sheer diversity of the traffic that passes through their harbours, and the exchange of ideas and customs that takes place along the run has enriched the cultures of every nation that borders the waters that the strait connects.
