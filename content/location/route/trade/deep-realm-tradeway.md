## Overview

The Deep Realm Tradeway is the principal commercial road through the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), connecting the underground cities of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) to the lowland markets and the eastern coast. The tradeway begins in the halls of [Karhold](/content/location/settlement/city/karhold), follows a chain of high valleys through the mining country, and descends by engineered roads toward [Highgate](/content/location/settlement/city/highgate), where the mountain traffic joins the great continental passage. Branches lead to [Deepreach](/content/location/settlement/town/deepreach), to the western exchange at [Brackenford](/content/location/settlement/town/brackenford), and to lesser settlements whose names appear in the account books of the dwarven merchants even when their buildings are absent from the lowland maps.

The road carries the wealth of the mountain and the necessities of the surface. Dwarven metalwork, stone, ore, and precious minerals travel outward, while grain, cloth, salt, timber, and manufactured goods travel inward. Unlike a simple mining track, the tradeway is built for regular and heavily loaded traffic. Its retaining walls, drainage channels, tunnels, and bridges are maintained by the realm under agreements with the lowland communities, and the tolls collected at its gates pay for works that benefit many peoples. The road has made the Deep Realm a power in the wider economy of Aethelar, but it has also made the kingdom dependent upon secure passage beyond its own halls.

## The Mountain Course

The upper road begins at the eastern halls of Karhold, where cargoes pass from subterranean storehouses to surface wagons. The transition is carefully managed because the temperature, light, and road surface change within a short distance. Goods that have been stored in the dry chambers of the city are wrapped before they meet the damp air of the high valleys, and draft animals are allowed time to adjust after entering the bright exterior. The first mile is broad and paved, with covered galleries protecting travellers from falling stone. Dwarven masons maintain carved markers along the gallery walls, recording distances to the principal gates and the height of the road above the valley floor.

From Karhold the route crosses a series of ridges that descend gradually toward the central highlands. The road follows the firmest shelves rather than the shortest line, and its many bends reflect the need to keep gradients within the strength of loaded animals. In places the mountain wall is cut back to form a road wide enough for two wagons; elsewhere one convoy must wait in a lay-by while another passes. The route keepers regard patience as part of sound commerce, for damage to a wagon or a mule can delay the whole road more seriously than a measured halt. Travellers learn to read the carved signs that indicate a passing place, a water trough, or a section where loose rock requires a slower pace.

The branch to Deepreach climbs into a basin rich in iron, copper, and silver-bearing stone. Ore from the settlement is sorted before it joins the main flow, and the heaviest loads travel down during the cooler months of the open season. The road there is steeper and more confined than the main course, so sledges and pack animals are common even when wheels carry most of the trade elsewhere. Merchants who visit Deepreach also bring news and letters from the lower markets, and their arrival is an important event for a community whose contact with the surface depends upon only a few annual convoys.

## The Mouth and the Gates

The most important surface station is Deep Realm Mouth, an outpost established where the upper tunnels open into a broad valley road. The settlement is small, but its position gives it responsibility for weighing cargoes, changing teams, and directing traffic toward the several branches of the tradeway. Its main buildings stand around a paved yard with a covered scale, a stable gallery, and a gatehouse that connects to the first road tunnel. The outpost's name refers to the passage into the realm rather than to any natural cavern. Dwarven officials call it the mouth because every surface merchant who enters the mountain trade passes through its records.

The road gates are both practical and ceremonial. The gatehouse at Deep Realm Mouth checks seals and road licences, while higher stations confirm that the recorded cargo has not been altered during the climb. The practice protects buyers and sellers from disputes about quality, weight, and origin. It also prevents unauthorised excavation along the road, since the realm regards mineral rights as a matter of law rather than an opportunity open to every passing prospector. Lowland merchants may purchase ore and metalwork, but they may not cut new workings without permission from the Deep King's officials and the council of elders.

The largest gate lies near the approach to Highgate. There the highland road meets the fortified passage through the mountain spine, and the traffic from the Deep Realm joins caravans, river carts, and travellers from both coasts. Highgate's toll officers distinguish between loads that merely cross the pass and loads that have been bought or stored within the city. The arrangement has required repeated negotiation, but the two authorities recognise their mutual dependence. The Deep Realm requires a reliable outlet, while Highgate gains more from a prosperous road than from a closed gate or an overburdened market.

## Goods and Merchant Houses

The outward cargoes of the tradeway are dominated by the products of the mountain. Bars of iron and steel are the most numerous, followed by tools, mining equipment, nails, hinges, chains, weapons, armour, and worked stone. Karhold sends finished goods of exceptional quality, while Deepreach and the lesser settlements supply ore and practical metalwork for ordinary use. Decorative objects, engraved vessels, and precious metal fittings are carried in smaller consignments under seal. The merchants place these loads in the centre of a convoy, where they are protected from weather and watched by guards, but the ordinary cargoes remain the true foundation of the road's regular traffic.

The inward trade answers the needs of the underground cities. Grain arrives in sealed sacks and is transferred to cool storehouses before it reaches the deeper halls. Salt, cloth, leather, lamp oil, timber, and wax are equally important, while books, medicines, and fine foodstuffs serve the households that maintain contact with the surface. The mountain communities cannot grow enough grain within their own valleys, and the road's grain traffic is measured in terms of future seasons rather than immediate profit. A disruption that lasts only a month may be manageable, but a failed crossing before winter can alter the whole distribution of stores.

The merchant houses of Karhold organise much of the trade, though independent human traders and gnomish instrument-makers have established their own contracts along the lower road. Dwarven agents know the quality marks of the forges and the rights attached to each mine, while surface merchants understand the shifting prices of grain, timber, and coastal freight. Their partnership is sometimes tense, especially when a lowland buyer presses for a lower price or a dwarven factor refuses a shipment that has absorbed too much damp. The road's accounting customs provide a common language. Every bale, bar, and chest receives a weight, a source mark, and a destination, and disputes are settled by comparing the records at the gates.

## The People of the Road

Most permanent workers on the upper tradeway are dwarves of the Deep Realm, including masons, miners, waggoners, engineers, gate officers, and innkeepers. The lower sections host a larger human population, for the valleys contain farms and surface villages that supply food to the stations. [Gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) are frequent visitors and maintain workshops near Highgate, where their instruments and mechanisms are sold to merchants who travel both coasts. The road is therefore less divided by species than the deep halls themselves. Every station has customs for receiving outsiders, though the details of lodging, trade, and speech vary from one valley to the next.

Travelling families form a recognisable part of the road population. Some maintain mule trains for generations, others specialise in fragile cargoes, and several have contracts to carry letters between the Deep Realm and the coastal cities. Their knowledge of weather and road conditions is prized by officials, who may consult a caravan leader before ordering a high gate opened. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the realm possess the authority to govern the road, but the human route keepers often know the exposed slopes better, and the most successful stations make use of both forms of experience.

## Hazards and Maintenance

The mountain itself provides the greatest dangers. Rockfall, flooding, fog, and winter ice can close a section without hostile action, while the deep road tunnels require constant attention to ventilation and drainage. The engineers inspect bridges and retaining walls at the start of every trading season, and crews remain near the most difficult bends until the traffic has passed. Temporary markers are placed after a landslip, and no convoy is permitted to remove them until a mason has declared the road secure. This cautious practice increases the length of the journey but has prevented many larger disasters.

Raiding occurs chiefly on the outer branches, where a convoy may pass beyond the immediate protection of the realm. The guards of the merchant houses travel with the most valuable loads, and the gate stations share reports about suspicious activity. The Deep Realm prefers patrols and agreements to permanent military occupation of every valley, since the presence of large forces can disturb local farms and mining work. When a threat becomes serious, the king's officials coordinate with Highgate and the neighbouring lowland powers. The tradeway is too important to one realm to be defended by one garrison alone.

## History and Significance

The oldest part of the road was a sequence of mine approaches used by the first dwarven settlements of the mountain spine. The construction of the great road from Karhold to the lowlands transformed those tracks into a dependable commercial route, and the work is remembered as one of the Deep Realm's greatest public undertakings. Dwarven engineers cut galleries through unstable slopes while human builders widened the lower valleys, and the agreement between them established customs of joint maintenance that still shape the road. Later generations extended the route to Deepreach and the smaller mines, ensuring that their output could reach the main markets.

The Deep Realm Tradeway is the kingdom's connection to the surface world. It moves the materials that make lowland building and warfare possible, while bringing the food and supplies that sustain the halls. Its gates also create a meeting ground where the traditions of the mountain and the lowlands are exchanged without either fully replacing the other. The road's stonework is a record of dwarven skill, but its continuing life depends upon human farms, gnomish workshops, and the many hands that keep a convoy moving. In that shared labour lies the tradeway's lasting significance.
