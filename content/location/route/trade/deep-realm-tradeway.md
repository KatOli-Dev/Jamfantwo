## Overview

The Deep Realm Tradeway is the principal commercial road through the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), connecting the underground cities of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) to the lowland markets and the eastern coast. The tradeway begins in the halls of [Karhold](/content/location/settlement/city/karhold), follows a chain of high valleys through the mining country, and descends by engineered roads toward [Highgate](/content/location/settlement/city/highgate), where the mountain traffic joins the great continental passage. Branches lead to [Deepreach](/content/location/settlement/town/deepreach), to the western exchange at [Brackenford](/content/location/settlement/town/brackenford), and to lesser settlements.

The road carries the wealth of the mountain and the necessities of the surface. Dwarven metalwork, stone, ore, and precious minerals travel outward, while grain, cloth, salt, timber, and manufactured goods travel inward. Its retaining walls, drainage channels, tunnels, and bridges are maintained by the realm under agreements with the lowland communities, and the tolls collected at its gates pay for works benefiting many peoples. The road has made the Deep Realm a power in the wider economy of Aethelar, but it has also made the kingdom dependent upon secure passage beyond its own halls.

## The Mountain Course

The upper road begins at the eastern halls of Karhold, where cargoes pass from subterranean storehouses to surface wagons. The transition is carefully managed because temperature, light, and road surface change within a short distance. The first mile is broad and paved, with covered galleries protecting travellers from falling stone. Dwarven masons maintain carved markers recording distances to the principal gates and the height of the road above the valley floor.

From Karhold the route crosses a series of ridges descending gradually toward the central highlands. The road follows the firmest shelves rather than the shortest line, keeping gradients within the strength of loaded animals. In places the mountain wall is cut back to form a road wide enough for two wagons; elsewhere one convoy must wait in a lay-by while another passes. Travellers learn to read carved signs indicating a passing place, a water trough, or a section where loose rock requires a slower pace.

The branch to Deepreach climbs into a basin rich in iron, copper, and silver-bearing stone. Ore from the settlement is sorted before joining the main flow, and the heaviest loads travel down during the cooler months. The road there is steeper and more confined than the main course, so sledges and pack animals are common even when wheels carry most of the trade elsewhere.

## The Mouth and the Gates

The most important surface station is Deep Realm Mouth, an outpost established where the upper tunnels open into a broad valley road. The settlement is small, but its position gives it responsibility for weighing cargoes, changing teams, and directing traffic toward the several branches. Its main buildings stand around a paved yard with a covered scale, a stable gallery, and a gatehouse connecting to the first road tunnel. Dwarven officials call it the mouth because every surface merchant who enters the mountain trade passes through its records.

The road gates are both practical and ceremonial. The gatehouse at Deep Realm Mouth checks seals and road licences, while higher stations confirm that the recorded cargo has not been altered. The practice protects buyers and sellers from disputes about quality, weight, and origin and prevents unauthorised excavation along the road. The largest gate lies near the approach to Highgate, where highland road and fortified passage meet.

## Goods and Merchant Houses

The outward cargoes are dominated by mountain products. Bars of iron and steel are the most numerous, followed by tools, mining equipment, nails, hinges, chains, weapons, armour, and worked stone. Karhold sends finished goods of exceptional quality, while Deepreach and the lesser settlements supply ore and practical metalwork. The inward trade answers the needs of the underground cities. Grain arrives in sealed sacks and is transferred to cool storehouses. Salt, cloth, leather, lamp oil, timber, and wax are equally important, while books, medicines, and fine foodstuffs serve the households maintaining contact with the surface.

The merchant houses of Karhold organise much of the trade, though independent human traders and gnomish instrument-makers have established their own contracts along the lower road. Dwarven agents know the quality marks of the forges and the rights attached to each mine, while surface merchants understand the shifting prices of grain, timber, and coastal freight. The road's accounting customs provide a common language: every bale, bar, and chest receives a weight, a source mark, and a destination.

## The People of the Road

Most permanent workers on the upper tradeway are dwarves of the Deep Realm, including masons, miners, waggoners, engineers, gate officers, and innkeepers. The lower sections host a larger human population, for the valleys contain farms and surface villages supplying food to the stations. [Gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) are frequent visitors and maintain workshops near Highgate. Travelling families form a recognisable part of the road population, some maintaining mule trains for generations, others specialising in fragile cargoes.

## Hazards and Maintenance

The mountain itself provides the greatest dangers. Rockfall, flooding, fog, and winter ice can close a section without hostile action, while the deep road tunnels require constant attention to ventilation and drainage. The engineers inspect bridges and retaining walls at the start of every trading season. Temporary markers are placed after a landslip, and no convoy is permitted to remove them until a mason has declared the road secure.

Raiding occurs chiefly on the outer branches. The guards of the merchant houses travel with the most valuable loads, and gate stations share reports about suspicious activity. The Deep Realm prefers patrols and agreements to permanent military occupation of every valley. When a threat becomes serious, the king's officials coordinate with Highgate and neighbouring lowland powers.

## History and Significance

The oldest part of the road was a sequence of mine approaches used by the first dwarven settlements. The construction of the great road from Karhold to the lowlands transformed those tracks into a dependable commercial route. Dwarven engineers cut galleries through unstable slopes while human builders widened the lower valleys. Later generations extended the route to Deepreach and the smaller mines.

The Deep Realm Tradeway is the kingdom's connection to the surface world. It moves materials that make lowland building and warfare possible while bringing the food and supplies that sustain the halls. Its gates create a meeting ground where traditions of the mountain and the lowlands are exchanged without either fully replacing the other. The road's stonework is a record of dwarven skill, but its continuing life depends upon human farms, gnomish workshops, and the many hands keeping a convoy moving.
