## A Great Emptiness Far from Empty

The Kharesh is the vast desert interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), covering the greater part of the continent from the fertile northern coast to the highlands at the threshold of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) in the far south. The name is given by the inhabitants themselves, translating roughly as "the great open." To the peoples of the northern coast, the Kharesh is known chiefly through the accounts of caravans and nomads. To those who dwell within it, the desert supports small but consistent populations at the oases and upland settlements, with abundant animal and plant life concentrated near water sources. The total nomadic population of the Kharesh is estimated at between twenty and thirty thousand, distributed across several distinct tribes.

## Erg, Uplands, and the Rocky Massifs

The Kharesh is a desert of many characters, and its appearance changes dramatically from one region to another. In the north, where the desert meets the foothills of the coastal highlands, the land is a scrubby semi-arid plain crossed by seasonal watercourses that carry the winter rains inland. This marginal country is the gateway to the deep desert, and here the caravans make their final preparations before venturing into the true Kharesh.

Beyond the marginal zone lies the great erg, a vast field of dunes that shifts with the seasonal winds, reshaping the landscape from one year to the next. The dunes may rise to heights of several hundred feet, their crests sharp and their flanks smooth, and the interdune corridors between them are floored with hard-packed sand and gravel where water may sometimes be found beneath the surface. The dunes migrate slowly across the desert, driven by the prevailing winds, and occasionally they reveal the remains of old caravan stops, wells, and even entire settlements abandoned when the sands advanced. The shifting nature of the dunes means that no map of the deep Kharesh remains accurate for long, and the nomads who travel the erg rely upon memory, landmark, and the position of the stars rather than upon any written chart.

The rocky uplands that rise from the sands provide shelter, water, and minerals, and several of these outcrops have been settled for centuries. These massifs are remnants of an ancient landscape that the desert has otherwise buried, and their bare summits and sheltered valleys sustain life at isolated oases. Springs fed by deep aquifers emerge at the base of the uplands, sustaining date palms, figs, and other crops cultivated in terraced gardens. The uplands are also rich in minerals, including copper, salt, and precious stones that are among the most valued products of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The largest known oasis settlement is Dahara, with a permanent population of roughly eight hundred and extensive date plantations in the surrounding terraces.

## Scorching Days and Freezing Nights

The climate of the Kharesh is one of extremes. Daytime surface temperatures on the dunes reach 140 degrees in the summer months, and exposure without shade is fatal within hours. At night, the sand radiates its heat rapidly, and the temperature may drop to near freezing even in summer. The air is dry throughout the year, and rainfall is rare and unpredictable, arriving in brief storms that may flood the interdune corridors before draining into the sand. The seasonal winds are the most consistent feature, dividing the year into distinct periods that the nomads use to structure their movements: the northern winter wind (November to February), the spring transition (March to April), the southern summer wind (May to August), and the autumn calm (September to October).

## Nomads, Upland Settlements, and Desert Routes

Nomadic peoples traverse the Kharesh following routes passed down through generations of oral tradition, moving between oases and seasonal grazing lands. The routes are not fixed paths but a series of waypoints adjusted according to water conditions, game movement, and dune shifts. The nomads travel in family groups, sometimes joining into larger parties for safety when crossing the most exposed sections of the erg. Their camps are temporary structures of cloth and hide that can be erected and dismantled within an hour. The principal tribes include the Banu Harim, the Tal Rashad, and the Dhu'l Qadar, each following a distinct annual circuit of roughly seven to twelve oases.

The settled communities of the uplands cultivate hardy crops and maintain trade with both the nomads and the coastal cities to the north. These communities are small but resilient, their populations stable over long periods, and their gardens and springs are the fixed points around which the life of the desert revolves. The upland settlements are connected to one another and to the coast by the caravan routes, and the goods that pass through them sustain a modest but enduring economy.

[Humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) form the bulk of the population, both settled and nomadic, and their adaptation to the demands of the desert is a mark of the versatility that characterises the species. [Orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) are present in the wilder and more remote regions of the Kharesh, beyond the range of the caravans, where they pursue a nomadic existence similar in form but distinct in tradition from that of the human nomads. [Goblins](/content/species/sapient/goblins) are found in scattered pockets along the desert fringes, where they scavenge and trade as opportunity allows.

## Salt, Spices, and the Caravan Undertaking

Ancient caravan routes cross the Kharesh, connecting the northern coast to the interior oases and the upland settlements. These routes carry salt, minerals, and crafted goods inland and bring out the rare dyes and spices that are found only in the desert and its fringes. The most valuable of these products are the dyes extracted from desert plants such as the [Date Palm](/content/species/flora/date-palm) and the spices gathered from the rocky uplands, both commanding extraordinary prices in the markets of the northern coast and beyond.

The crossing of the Kharesh is a serious undertaking, undertaken only by those who know the desert well. The caravans that make the journey are organised and led by experienced guides who have spent their lives learning the routes, and they are equipped with provisions for a journey that may last many weeks. Water is carried in skins and supplemented by the wells at the oases, but the margin between sufficiency and disaster is narrow, and a delayed arrival at the next water source can be fatal for the entire party. The caravans are a lifeline to the communities that dwell within the Kharesh, and their arrival is the most significant event in the life of an oasis or upland settlement, bringing news, goods, and contact with the wider world. The desert is also home to distinctive creatures such as the [Kharesh glass spider](/content/species/fauna/kharesh-glass-spider), whose webs are sometimes encountered among the rocky outcrops of the uplands.
