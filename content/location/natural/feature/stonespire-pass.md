## Saddle Between the Two High Peaks

At the head of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), where the range narrows to a saddle between two of the highest peaks, a broad, rocky pass carries the road from the cold north into the warm coast. The pass has been a landmark of the inland valleys since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era) and is called the [Stonespire](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire) Pass for the peaks flanking it on either side. It is bounded on the north by the cliffs of the higher [Stonespire](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire), on the south by the descent to the foothills of the warm coast, on the east by the peak called the Sentinel, and on the west by the peak called the [Watcher](/content/religion/watcher).

The pass has been the principal route of the land trade between the cold north and the warm coast since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era) and has been the subject of careful engineering by the magistrates of the towns of both regions. The pass sits at an elevation of approximately 4,200 feet and accommodates two wagons abreast along its widest section. An estimated four thousand travellers and eight hundred laden wagons cross the pass each year, according to toll records maintained by the magistrates of the towns of both regions.

## Paved Road and the Winter Closures

The pass is broad enough to accommodate the road and the small streams running off the slopes to either side, and it has been the site of the road that the [Laying the Stone Roads](/content/history/laying-the-stone-roads) constructed. The road is paved with granite quarried from the surrounding peaks, laid in blocks averaging two feet square and eight inches thick, and descends from the pass to the foothills of the warm coast in a winding course of eleven miles that the engineers of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) cut through the native rock. The road was completed in the year 487 of the Founding Era and has required resurfacing on average once every thirty years since.

The pass is subject to occasional closure when winter snow blocks the road, and these closures have been the source of much of the hardship for merchants and travellers who must wait for spring to reopen the road. The closures are recorded in the records of the magistrates of the towns of both regions as exceptional events, and the records describe the labour organised to clear the road and reopen the pass. The closures have been the foundation of the careful management of the road that the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) maintains.

## Sentinel, Watcher, and the Hardy Vegetation

The peaks flanking the pass are among the highest of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) and are the home of the hardy vegetation that the climate supports. The lower slopes support the conifers that the Reading the Ground has catalogued, and the higher slopes support only the lichens and mosses that the cold allows. The peaks are the home of smaller mammals that the [Golden Eagle](/content/species/fauna/golden-eagle) feeds upon and the wild goats that the hunters of the holds pursue in season.

The peaks have been the subject of careful survey by the pilots of the Distance-Craft, establishing their heights with care. The Stonespire summit stands at 7,830 feet and the Sentinel at 7,210 feet, both surveyed in 623 FE by the pilots of the Distance-Craft. They serve as primary navigation references for travellers crossing the inland valleys.

## Garrison and the Chain of Signals

The watchtowers of the pass were erected by the magistrates of the towns of the cold north in the early generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace) and have been maintained by the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) ever since. They stand at the northern and southern ends of the pass and serve as refuge for travellers caught upon the road in time of storm. The garrisons consist of a sergeant and four men, holding the towers for a tour of two months. The watchtowers are positioned at intervals of three miles along the road, providing the garrisons with sightlines covering the entire length of the pass.

The watchtowers are connected by a chain of signals exchanged with neighbouring towers, informing the garrisons of the state of the road and the approach of any large body of men. This signal chain, employing smoke by day and lanterns by night, can relay a message from the northernmost to the southernmost tower in under two minutes. The system is maintained by the [Standing Watch](/content/government/local/standing-watch).

## Stone Shelters for Travellers

The quarters of the pass were erected by the magistrates of the towns of both regions in the early generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace) and are maintained by the [Standing Watch](/content/government/local/standing-watch). They are small buildings of stone providing shelter for travellers caught upon the road in time of storm and are the home of the small garrisons maintaining the watchtowers. The quarters measure approximately twelve feet by eighteen feet and can accommodate up to eight travellers in addition to the garrison. Each is stocked with firewood, preserved food sufficient for three weeks, and medical supplies for the treatment of frostbite and injury.

The quarters are furnished with supplies that the garrisons require for the tour, delivered by the magistrates of the towns of both regions at the beginning of every season. The cost of maintaining the quarters and watchtowers is borne jointly by the towns of the cold north and the warm coast, divided in proportion to the volume of trade passing through each town's territory as recorded in the toll ledgers.

## Birds, Goats, and the Mountain Streams

The pass supports a rich variety of wildlife forming the foundation of the ecology of the pass. The peaks support smaller mammals that birds of prey feed upon, and the slopes support the wild goats that the hunters of the holds pursue in season. The streams running off the slopes support the trout and grayling that the fishermen of the holds pursue and are the source of the water that the quarters and watchtowers require. The wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the Bestiaries and Herbals, who have recorded the patterns of the mammals and fish in their catalogues.

## Foundation of Land Trade

Stonespire Pass remains the primary overland trade route between the cold north and the warm coast. The merchants and travellers who use the road mark the anniversary of its opening each spring with a ceremony at the northern watchtower, a tradition recorded in the magistrates' ledgers since the year 512 FE. The ceremony includes a toll of the season's first crossing and a public accounting of the road's condition, attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every town of both regions.
