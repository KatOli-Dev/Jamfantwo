## Overview

At the head of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), where the range narrows to a saddle between two of the highest peaks, a broad, rocky pass carries the road from the cold north into the warm coast. The pass has been a landmark of the inland valleys since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era) and is called the [Stonespire](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire) Pass for the peaks flanking it on either side. It is bounded on the north by the cliffs of the higher Stonespire, on the south by the descent to the foothills of the warm coast, on the east by the peak called the Sentinel, and on the west by the peak called the Watcher.

The pass has been the principal route of the land trade between the cold north and the warm coast since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era) and has been the subject of careful engineering by the magistrates of the towns of both regions. It is one of the most distinctive natural features of the three continents and is held in particular esteem by the merchants and travellers who make their living upon the road.

## The Pass

The pass is broad enough to accommodate the road and the small streams running off the slopes to either side, and it has been the site of the road that the [Laying the Stone Roads](/content/history/laying-the-stone-roads) constructed. The road is paved with the hard stone of the surrounding peaks and is one of the most reliable roads of the three continents. It descends from the pass to the foothills of the warm coast in a long, winding course that the engineers of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) cut into the rock with care. The road is the most remarkable of the works of the road-building programme and the foundation of the safety of the land trade between the two regions.

The pass is subject to occasional closure when winter snow blocks the road, and these closures have been the source of much of the hardship for merchants and travellers who must wait for spring to reopen the road. The closures are recorded in the records of the magistrates of the towns of both regions as exceptional events, and the records describe the labour organised to clear the road and reopen the pass. The closures have been the foundation of the careful management of the road that the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) maintains.

## The Peaks

The peaks flanking the pass are among the highest of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) and are the home of the hardy vegetation that the climate supports. The lower slopes support the conifers that the [Reading the Ground](/content/belief/science/physical/reading-the-ground) has catalogued, and the higher slopes support only the lichens and mosses that the cold allows. The peaks are the home of smaller mammals that birds of prey feed upon and the wild goats that the hunters of the holds pursue in season.

The peaks have been the subject of careful survey by the pilots of the [Distance-Craft](/content/belief/science/physical/distance-craft), establishing their heights with care. They are the most distinctive landmarks of the inland valleys and are held in high esteem by the magistrates and merchants of the towns of both regions.

## The Watchtowers

The watchtowers of the pass were erected by the magistrates of the towns of the cold north in the early generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace) and have been maintained by the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) ever since. They stand at the northern and southern ends of the pass and serve as refuge for travellers caught upon the road in time of storm. The garrisons consist of a sergeant and four men, holding the towers for a tour of two months. The watchtowers are the foundation of the safety of the pass and are held in high esteem by the merchants and travellers of the road.

The watchtowers are connected by a chain of signals exchanged with neighbouring towers, informing the garrisons of the state of the road and the approach of any large body of men. These signals have been the foundation of the safety of the pass and are the measure of the careful management that the Standing Watch maintains.

## The Quarters

The quarters of the pass were erected by the magistrates of the towns of both regions in the early generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace) and are maintained by the Standing Watch. They are small buildings of stone providing shelter for travellers caught upon the road in time of storm and are the home of the small garrisons maintaining the watchtowers. The quarters are the foundation of the safety of the pass and are held in high esteem by the merchants and travellers of the road.

The quarters are furnished with supplies that the garrisons require for the tour, delivered by the magistrates of the towns of both regions at the beginning of every season. This delivery has been the foundation of the careful management of the quarters, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of both regions.

## The Wildlife

The pass supports a rich variety of wildlife forming the foundation of the ecology of the pass. The peaks support smaller mammals that birds of prey feed upon, and the slopes support the wild goats that the hunters of the holds pursue in season. The streams running off the slopes support the trout and grayling that the fishermen of the holds pursue and are the source of the water that the quarters and watchtowers require. The wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the [Bestiaries and Herbals](/content/belief/science/physical/bestiaries-and-herbals), who have recorded the patterns of the mammals and fish in their catalogues.

## Significance

Stonespire Pass is one of the most distinctive natural features of the three continents and the foundation of the land trade between the cold north and the warm coast. The road, the watchtowers, the quarters, the peaks, and the streams are the inheritance that the pass has bequeathed to the present age. The pass is held in particular memory by the merchants and travellers who make their living upon the road, and they mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. These ceremonies are among the most important public observances of the three continents, attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every town of both regions.
