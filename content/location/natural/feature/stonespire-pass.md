## Overview

At the head of the [The Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), where the range narrows to a saddle between two of the highest peaks, a broad, rocky pass carries the road from the cold north into the warm coast. The pass has been a landmark of the inland valleys since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities), and the chroniclers of the academies have named it the [Stonespire](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire) Pass for the peaks that flank it on either side. The pass is bounded on the north by the cliffs of the higher Stonespire, on the south by the descent to the foothills of the warm coast, on the east by the peak that the locals call the Sentinel, and on the west by the peak that the locals call the Watcher.

The pass has been the principal route of the land trade between the cold north and the warm coast since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities), and the pass has been the subject of careful engineering by the magistrates of the towns of both regions. The pass is one of the most distinctive of the natural features of the three continents, and the pass is held in particular esteem by the merchants and the travellers who make their living upon the road.

## The Pass

The pass is broad enough to accommodate the road and the small streams that run off the slopes to either side, and the pass has been the site of the road that the [Building of the Stone Roads](/content/history/building-of-the-stone-roads) constructed. The road is paved with the hard stone of the surrounding peaks, and the road is one of the most reliable of the roads of the three continents. The road descends from the pass to the foothills of the warm coast in a long, winding course that the engineers of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) cut into the rock with care. The chronicler notes that the road is the most remarkable of the works of the road-building programme, and the road is the foundation of the safety of the land trade between the two regions.

The pass is subject to the occasional closure when the snow of the winter blocks the road, and the closures have been the source of much of the hardship of the merchants and the travellers who must wait for the spring to reopen the road. The chronicler notes that the closures are recorded in the chronicles of the magistrates of the towns of both regions as exceptional events, and the chronicles describe the labour that the magistrates have organised to clear the road and to reopen the pass. The chronicler observes that the closures have been the foundation of the careful management of the road that the [Standing Watch of the Northern Roads](/content/government/local/standing-watch-of-the-northern-roads) maintains.

## The Peaks

The peaks that flank the pass are among the highest of the The Stoneheim Highlands, and the peaks are the home of the hardy vegetation that the climate supports. The lower slopes support the conifers that the [Grammar of Stones and Soils](/content/belief/science/physical/grammar-of-stones-and-soils) has catalogued with care, and the higher slopes support only the lichens and the mosses that the cold allows. The peaks are the home of the smaller mammals that the birds of prey feed upon, and the peaks are the home of the wild goats that the hunters of the holds pursue in the season.

The chronicler notes that the peaks have been the subject of careful survey by the pilots of the [Surveying of Heights and Distances](/content/belief/science/physical/surveying-of-heights-and-distances), and the surveys have established the heights of the peaks with care. The chronicler observes that the peaks are the most distinctive of the landmarks of the inland valleys, and the peaks are held in high esteem by the magistrates and the merchants of the towns of both regions.

## The Watchtowers

The watchtowers of the pass were erected by the magistrates of the towns of the cold north in the early generations of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities), and the watchtowers have been maintained by the [Standing Watch of the Northern Roads](/content/government/local/standing-watch-of-the-northern-roads) ever since. The watchtowers stand at the northern and southern ends of the pass, and the watchtowers are the refuge of the travellers who are caught upon the road in time of storm. The garrisons of the watchtowers consist of a sergeant and four men, and the garrisons hold the towers for a tour of two months. The chronicler notes that the watchtowers are the foundation of the safety of the pass, and the watchtowers are held in high esteem by the merchants and the travellers of the road.

The watchtowers are connected by a chain of signals that the garrisons exchange with the neighbouring towers, and the signals inform the garrisons of the state of the road and the approach of any large body of men. The chronicler notes that the signals have been the foundation of the safety of the pass, and the signals are the measure of the careful management that the Standing Watch maintains.

## The Quarters

The quarters of the pass were erected by the magistrates of the towns of both regions in the early generations of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities), and the quarters are maintained by the Standing Watch. The quarters are small buildings of stone that provide shelter for the travellers who are caught upon the road in time of storm, and the quarters are the home of the small garrisons that maintain the watchtowers. The chronicler notes that the quarters are the foundation of the safety of the pass, and the quarters are held in high esteem by the merchants and the travellers of the road.

The quarters are furnished with the supplies that the garrisons require for the tour, and the supplies are delivered by the magistrates of the towns of both regions at the beginning of every season. The chronicler notes that the delivery has been the foundation of the careful management of the quarters, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of both regions.

## The Wildlife

The pass is the home of a rich variety of wildlife, and the wildlife is the foundation of the ecology of the pass. The peaks support the smaller mammals that the birds of prey feed upon, and the slopes support the wild goats that the hunters of the holds pursue in the season. The streams that run off the slopes support the trout and the grayling that the fishermen of the holds pursue, and the streams are the source of the water that the quarters and the watchtowers require. The chronicler notes that the wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the [Cataloguing of Flora and Fauna](/content/belief/science/physical/cataloguing-of-flora-and-fauna), and the scholars have recorded the patterns of the mammals and the fish in their catalogues.

## Significance

Stonespire Pass is one of the most distinctive of the natural features of the three continents, and the pass is the foundation of the land trade between the cold north and the warm coast. The road, the watchtowers, the quarters, the peaks, and the streams are the inheritance that the pass has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the pass is held in particular memory by the merchants and the travellers who make their living upon the road, and the merchants and the travellers mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the three continents, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every town of both regions.
