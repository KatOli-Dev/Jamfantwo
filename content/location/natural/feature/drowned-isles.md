## Overview

Off the western coast of the principal continent, beyond the cliffs that mark the limit of the [Ashen Heath](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/ashen-heath), a chain of small, rocky islands runs northward into the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean). The islands take their name from the steep, broken coastlines that the waves have cut into them, and the chroniclers of the academies have named the chain the Drowned Isles for the way the spray of the ocean seems to overwhelm them in foul weather. The islands are bounded on the east by the cliffs of the mainland, on the west by the open ocean, on the south by the waters that wash the cliffs of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), and on the north by the colder waters that lead to the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast).

The islands have been sparsely populated since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era), and the population is concentrated in a string of small fishing villages along the sheltered bays of the eastern side of the chain. The villages are connected by the small boats that ferry passengers and goods between the islands, and the boats are the principal means of communication between the villages and the towns of the mainland coast. The chronicler notes that the islands are one of the most distinctive of the landscapes of the cold north, and the islands are held in particular esteem by the fishermen who make their living upon them.

## The Islands

The islands of the chain vary in size from the larger islands that support the principal villages to the smaller rocks that rise only a few feet above the water and that the chroniclers of the [Bestiaries and Herbals](/content/belief/science/physical/bestiaries-and-herbals) have catalogued as the home of the seabirds that breed upon them. The islands are composed of the same ancient stone that forms the cliffs of the mainland, and the stone is exposed in the cliffs that drop to the water on every side. The islands are covered with a thin soil that supports a tough vegetation of grasses and low shrubs, and the vegetation is the foundation of the limited agriculture of the larger islands.

The islands are the home of a small population of farmers who cultivate the higher ground and a larger population of fishermen who work the waters around the islands. The farmers exchange their grain and their wool for the fish that the fishermen take from the waters, and the exchange is the foundation of the economy of the islands. The chronicler notes that the islands are the source of much of the fish that the towns of the mainland coast require, and the islands are held in high esteem by the magistrates and the merchants of the towns of the coast.

## The Bays

The sheltered bays of the eastern side of the chain are the principal anchorages of the islands, and the bays have been the home of the fishing villages since the founding of the settlements. The bays are small and the water is deep, and the bays are protected from the prevailing winds by the cliffs that rise to the west. The bays are the principal landing places for the small boats that serve the islands, and the boats carry the supplies that the villages require.

The chronicler notes that the bays have been the subject of careful survey by the pilots of the [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch), and the pilots maintain the beacons that mark the entrances to the bays. The chronicler observes that the bays are the foundation of the safety of the navigation around the islands, and the navigation is the source of much of the contact between the islands and the mainland.

## The Wildlife

The islands are the home of a rich variety of wildlife, and the wildlife is the foundation of the ecology of the chain. The cliffs support the seabirds that breed upon the ledges in the spring, and the birds include the gulls, the cormorants, and the smaller auks that the chroniclers of the [Bestiaries and Herbals](/content/belief/science/physical/bestiaries-and-herbals) have catalogued with care. The interior of the larger islands supports the smaller mammals that the birds of prey feed upon, and the waters around the islands support the fish that the fishermen pursue.

The wildlife is the source of much of the food of the villages, and the wildlife is the foundation of the careful management of the fishery that the elders of the villages maintain. The chronicler notes that the wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies, and the scholars have recorded the patterns of the birds and the fish in their catalogues.

## The Fishery

The waters around the islands support a rich fishery, and the fishery has been the foundation of the prosperity of the villages since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The principal fish are the cod and the haddock that the fishermen take from the deeper waters, and the herring that the fishermen take from the shallower waters in the summer. The fishery is regulated by the elders of the villages in consultation with the pilots of the [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch), and the regulations set the seasons of the catch, the methods that the fishermen must use, and the price that the fishermen may charge.

The chronicler notes that the fishery has been the subject of long debate between the villages of the islands and the towns of the mainland coast, and the debate has been settled by the careful management that the Watch maintains. The regulations set the proportion of the catch that each party may take, and the proportion has been the foundation of the careful management of the fishery. The chronicler observes that the management is the measure of the public spirit of the magistrates and the merchants of the towns of the coast.

## The Lighthouses

The lighthouses of the islands were erected by the council of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) in the early generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace), and the lighthouses have been maintained by the [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch) ever since. The lighthouses stand upon the highest points of the larger islands, and the lights are visible far out to sea in clear weather. The keepers of the lighthouses are senior seamen of the Watch who have been relieved of duty afloat, and the keepers maintain the fires, trim the wicks at dusk, and signal the state of the tide by the colour of the lights that they show.

The chronicler notes that the lighthouses have been the foundation of the safety of the navigation around the islands, and the lighthouses have been the refuge of many a pilot who has been caught upon the coast in time of storm. The lighthouses are among the most important of the public works of the cold north, and the lighthouses are held in high esteem by the pilots and the merchants of every city of the coast.

## Significance

The Drowned Isles are one of the most distinctive of the landscapes of the cold north, and the isles are the foundation of the fishery and the navigation of the western coast. The islands, the bays, the villages, and the lighthouses are the inheritance that the isles have bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the isles are held in particular memory by the fishermen and the pilots who make their living upon them, and the fishermen and the pilots mark the anniversaries of the founding of the lighthouses with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the cold north, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every town of the coast.
