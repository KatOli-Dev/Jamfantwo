## Three Channels and Two Islands of Silt

At the western edge of the principal continent, the Greywash divides into three principal channels that descend through a broad delta to the sea at [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara). The three channels are the eastern, the central, and the western, and they enclose two large islands of silt and marsh between them. The delta covers several days of travel from north to south and from east to west, and it is the home of a rich variety of landscapes called the Greywash Mouths. The Mouths are bounded on the east by the lower slopes of the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash), on the north by the sea, on the west by the cliffs marking the limit of the delta, and on the south by the open water of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean).

The Mouths are one of the most distinctive landscapes of the cold north and have been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). They are the foundation of the fishery of the lower Greywash and the source of much of the fish that the towns of the cold north require. The Mouths are held in particular esteem by the fishermen and merchants of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), and they mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies.

## Eastern, Central, and Western

The three principal channels of the delta are the eastern, the central, and the western. The eastern channel is the deepest and most reliable, the principal route by which larger vessels enter the river on their way to [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) and the towns of the upper river. The central channel is shallower and shifts more frequently, used by the smaller vessels that work the delta itself. The western channel is the most variable, used by fishing boats working the coast to the west of the delta.

The channels are maintained by the river warden that the [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact) established, who inspects the channels at regular intervals and supervises the dredging required to keep them navigable. This maintenance has been the foundation of the safety of navigation in the delta, and the navigation is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of the lower river.

## Low-Lying Land and the Spring Floods

The two islands between the channels are the home of a small population of farmers and fishermen, connected to the mainland and to each other by bridges and by the small boats that ferry passengers and goods across the channels. The islands are low-lying and subject to floods that the river brings when the spring rains are heavy, and these floods have been the source of much of the hardship of the islands. The floods are recorded in the chronicles of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) as exceptional events, and the records describe the labour that the magistrates of the town have organised to repair the damage.

The islands support a population of farmers who cultivate the higher ground and fishermen who work the channels. The farmers exchange their grain and vegetables for the fish taken from the channels, and this exchange is the foundation of the island economy. The islands are the source of much of the food of the lower river and are held in high esteem by the magistrates and merchants of the towns of the coast.

## Salmon, Eel, and the Managed Catch

The Mouths support a rich fishery that has been the foundation of the prosperity of the villages along the delta since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The principal fish are the salmon running up the channels in the spring and autumn, the eel taken from the deeper channels, and the smaller fish taken from the shallows in summer. The fishery is regulated by the river warden in consultation with the elders of the villages, with regulations setting the seasons of the catch, the methods fishermen must use, and the prices they may charge.

The fishery has been the subject of long debate between the towns of the upper river and the towns of the lower river, settled by the careful management that the [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact) established. The treaty sets the proportion of the catch that each town may take, and this proportion has been the foundation of the careful management of the fishery.

## Herons, Bitterns, and the Channel Margins

The Mouths support a rich variety of wildlife forming the foundation of the ecology of the delta. The channels support the fish that the fishermen pursue and the smaller creatures that the birds feed upon. The islands support nesting birds that breed in the marshes in summer, including the herons and bitterns that the Bestiaries and Herbals have catalogued. The margins of the channels support the deer and wild pig that the hunters of the villages pursue in season.

The wildlife is the source of much of the food of the villages and the foundation of the careful management of the chase that the river warden maintains. The wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies, who have recorded the patterns of the birds and fish in their catalogues.

## Beacons and the Route to the River

The Mouths are the principal entrance to the Greywash for larger vessels sailing from the harbours of the warm coast, and they have been the subject of careful navigation since the founding of the towns. The pilots of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) maintain the beacons marking the channels and report the state of the channels to the harbormaster of the town. This navigation has been the foundation of the trade of the lower river, and the trade is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of the coast.

The Mouths are also the principal entrance for vessels sailing from the cold north to the harbours of the warm coast and have been the subject of careful survey by the pilots of the Distance-Craft. The Mouths are the most useful of the entrances to the river and the foundation of the safety of navigation on the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean).

## Foundation of the Delta

The Greywash Mouths are one of the most distinctive landscapes of the cold north and the foundation of the fishery and navigation of the lower Greywash. The channels, the islands, the villages, and the bridges are the inheritance that the Mouths have bequeathed to the present age. The Mouths are held in particular memory by the fishermen and merchants who make their living upon them, and they mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. These ceremonies are among the most important public observances of the cold north, attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every town of the lower river.
