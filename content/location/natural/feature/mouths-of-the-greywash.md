## Overview

At the western edge of the principal continent, the Greywash divides into three principal channels that descend through a broad delta to the sea at [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara). The three channels are the eastern, the central, and the western, and the three channels enclose two large islands of silt and marsh between them. The delta covers several days of travel from north to south and from east to west, and the delta is the home of a rich variety of landscapes that the chroniclers of the academies have named the Mouths of the Greywash. The Mouths are bounded on the east by the lower slopes of the [Reed Marshes of the Greywash](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash), on the north by the sea, on the west by the cliffs that mark the limit of the delta, and on the south by the open water of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean).

The Mouths are one of the most distinctive of the landscapes of the cold north, and the Mouths have been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities). The Mouths are the foundation of the fishery of the lower Greywash, and the Mouths have been the source of much of the fish that the towns of the cold north require. The chronicler notes that the Mouths are held in particular esteem by the fishermen and the merchants of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), and the fishermen and the merchants mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies.

## The Channels

The three principal channels of the delta are the eastern, the central, and the western. The eastern channel is the deepest and the most reliable, and the eastern channel is the principal route by which the larger vessels enter the river on their way to [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) and the towns of the upper river. The central channel is shallower and shifts more frequently, and the central channel is used by the smaller vessels that work the delta itself. The western channel is the most variable, and the western channel is used by the fishing boats that work the coast to the west of the delta.

The channels are maintained by the river warden that the [Treaty of the Greywash](/content/history/treaty-of-the-greywash) established, and the river warden inspects the channels at regular intervals and supervises the dredging that is required to keep them navigable. The chronicler notes that the maintenance has been the foundation of the safety of the navigation of the delta, and the navigation is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of the lower river.

## The Islands

The two islands between the channels are the home of a small population of farmers and fishermen, and the islands are connected to the mainland and to each other by bridges and by the small boats that ferry passengers and goods across the channels. The islands are low-lying and subject to the floods that the river brings when the spring rains are heavy, and the floods have been the source of much of the hardship of the islands. The chronicler notes that the floods are recorded in the chronicles of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) as exceptional events, and the chronicles describe the labour that the magistrates of the town have organised to repair the damage.

The islands support a population of farmers who cultivate the higher ground and a population of fishermen who work the channels. The farmers exchange their grain and their vegetables for the fish that the fishermen take from the channels, and the exchange is the foundation of the economy of the islands. The chronicler notes that the islands are the source of much of the food of the lower river, and the islands are held in high esteem by the magistrates and the merchants of the towns of the coast.

## The Fishery

The Mouths support a rich fishery, and the fishery has been the foundation of the prosperity of the villages along the delta since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities). The principal fish are the salmon that run up the channels in the spring and the autumn, the eel that the fishermen take from the deeper channels, and the smaller fish that the fishermen take from the shallows in the summer. The fishery is regulated by the river warden in consultation with the elders of the villages, and the regulations set the seasons of the catch, the methods that the fishermen must use, and the price that the fishermen may charge.

The chronicler notes that the fishery has been the subject of long debate between the towns of the upper river and the towns of the lower river, and the debate has been settled by the careful management that the [Treaty of the Greywash](/content/history/treaty-of-the-greywash) established. The treaty sets the proportion of the catch that each town may take, and the proportion has been the foundation of the careful management of the fishery. The chronicler observes that the treaty is the measure of the public spirit of the magistrates and the merchants of the towns of the river.

## The Wildlife

The Mouths are the home of a rich variety of wildlife, and the wildlife is the foundation of the ecology of the delta. The channels support the fish that the fishermen pursue and the smaller creatures that the birds feed upon. The islands support the nesting birds that breed in the marshes in the summer, and the birds include the herons and the bitterns that the chroniclers of the [Cataloguing of Flora and Fauna](/content/belief/science/physical/the-cataloguing-of-flora-and-fauna) have catalogued with care. The margins of the channels support the deer and the wild pig that the hunters of the villages pursue in the season.

The wildlife is the source of much of the food of the villages, and the wildlife is the foundation of the careful management of the chase that the river warden maintains. The chronicler notes that the wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies, and the scholars have recorded the patterns of the birds and the fish in their catalogues.

## The Navigation

The Mouths are the principal entrance to the Greywash for the larger vessels that sail from the harbours of the warm coast, and the Mouths have been the subject of careful navigation since the founding of the towns. The pilots of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) maintain the beacons that mark the channels, and the pilots report the state of the channels to the harbormaster of the town. The chronicler notes that the navigation has been the foundation of the trade of the lower river, and the trade is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of the coast.

The chronicler of the [Watch of the Inner Sea](/content/government/local/watch-of-the-inner-sea) notes that the Mouths are also the principal entrance for the vessels that sail from the cold north to the harbours of the warm coast, and the Mouths have been the subject of careful survey by the pilots of the [Surveying of Heights and Distances](/content/belief/science/physical/the-surveying-of-heights-and-distances). The chronicler observes that the Mouths are the most useful of the entrances to the river, and the Mouths are the foundation of the safety of the navigation of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean).

## Significance

The Mouths of the Greywash are one of the most distinctive of the landscapes of the cold north, and the Mouths are the foundation of the fishery and the navigation of the lower Greywash. The channels, the islands, the villages, and the bridges are the inheritance that the Mouths have bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the Mouths are held in particular memory by the fishermen and the merchants who make their living upon them, and the fishermen and the merchants mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the cold north, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every town of the lower river.
