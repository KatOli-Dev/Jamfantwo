## Overview

The Ember Ridge is the broken volcanic range that traverses the continent of [Kharos](/content/location/natural/continent/kharos) from the northwestern headlands to the southeastern coast, a chain of ridges, vents, and hot springs that has shaped the climate, the rivers, and the customs of every people who dwell within sight of its smoke. The range is not high throughout its length, and several of its lower passes can be crossed without great difficulty, but it is among the most troublesome ranges in the known world to traverse as a whole, for the ridges are separated by dry gorges and shelves of loose stone that frustrate the traveller at every stage. The chronicler of the western cartographers has set down that the Ember Ridge is the spine of [Kharos](/content/location/natural/continent/kharos) in a more practical sense than many a greater range, for the few rivers that the highlands send down to the eastern plains are the principal sources of water for the broad basins, and the springs of the range sustain a settled life that would be impossible in their absence.

The range takes its name from the vents and the hot springs that mark its length, and the steam that rises from these openings is visible from the coast on the clearest mornings. The native chroniclers of [Kharos](/content/location/natural/continent/kharos) prefer an older name that translates as the broken backbone, and they use that name in the formal records of the mountain councils, but the name Ember Ridge has entered the common speech of the traders and the mariners who visit the eastern ports. The range is sometimes confused in foreign accounts with the volcanic ranges of the far south, but the Ember Ridge is wholly distinct, and the chronicler notes that a traveller who has crossed the range will recognise it at a glance from any other highland in the known world.

## Ridges and Vents

The ridges of the Ember Ridge are not continuous. They are broken by gorges, by shelves of dark volcanic rock, and by the vents that mark the course of the range from end to end. Several of the highest peaks retain snow until late in the warm season, and the meltwater that descends from these summits feeds the rivers that run to the eastern plains. The lower ridges are dry and dusty, and the loose stone that covers them shifts underfoot, so that a path that was clear in the morning may be buried by the afternoon. The chronicler of the mountain patrols has recorded that the range is crossed by few roads, and that the roads that do exist are maintained by the communities of the high valleys rather than by any central authority.

The vents of the range are of two kinds. Some are quiet, releasing a steady plume of steam that the highland communities use to warm their houses and to extend the growing season in the sheltered gardens near the springs. Others are noisy and unreliable, throwing stones and ash at irregular intervals, and the highland communities give these a wide berth. The most active of the vents are concentrated in the central section of the range, and the chronicler notes that the activity of the vents has increased in living memory, though the increase is not yet understood by the naturalists of the coastal academies. The hot springs are found throughout the range, and the water that rises from them is rich in minerals that the chroniclers of the mountain baths have long held to be beneficial to the skin and to the joints.

The dark volcanic rock that covers the shelves of the range is the most distinctive feature of the highland, and the rock gives the Ember Ridge its character. The stone is black or deep grey, often veined with red or yellow where the minerals have settled in the cracks, and the surfaces of the larger shelves ring underfoot like iron. The stone splits readily into flat plates that the highland communities use for paving and for the walls of the more substantial buildings, and the plates are exported down the eastern slope as a building material that is valued for its fire-resistant qualities. The chronicler records that the volcanic stone of the Ember Ridge is among the most durable in the known world, and that the buildings of the high valleys have outlasted the generations that raised them.

## Climate and Seasons

The Ember Ridge divides the climate of [Kharos](/content/location/natural/continent/kharos) more sharply than its modest height would suggest, for the range lies across the path of the seasonal sea winds and forces them to rise against the western slopes. The western rivers are short and swift, for the rain that falls upon the outer face of the range reaches the coast before it can gather into a substantial stream, and many of the smaller channels disappear beneath the gravel before they reach the sea. The eastern rivers are longer and slower, for they descend through the sheltered valleys to the broad basins that open to the eastern plains, and they carry the water that sustains the settlements of the interior.

The climate of the range is governed by three seasons rather than four, a distinction that the inland cartographers have recorded as one of the defining features of the continent. The wet season comes when the sea winds turn inland, filling the high valleys and sending sudden floods through the lower ravines. The following bright season is dry and clear, with strong sunlight by day and cool air after sunset, and the inhabitants store water in covered cisterns and line their fields with stone against the return of the rains. A shorter storm season arrives when hot air from the southern basins meets the cold winds descending from the range, and thunderstorms gather over the interior with a speed that the chronicler of the mountain patrols has described as the most sudden weather in the known world. A single night of rain can alter the course of a river, and the highland communities have learned to read the signs of the approaching storms with a precision that the foreign visitors seldom acquire.

The chronicler notes that the three-season cycle of [Kharos](/content/location/natural/continent/kharos) is more reliable than the four-season cycle of the colder continents, for the transitions between the seasons are governed by the position of the sun and the direction of the prevailing winds, and the highland communities have marked the dates of the transitions with stones and cairns that survive the generations. The shepherds of the high valleys move their flocks between the open grass of the upper slopes and the sheltered meadows of the lower valleys according to the season, and the timing of the movements is fixed by the customary law of the mountain councils.

## The Glass Groves of the Southern Face

The Kharesh Glass Groves occupy a series of basins along the southern face of the Ember Ridge, and the chronicler of the western cartographers has set down that the groves are among the most remarkable woodlands in the known world. The trees of the groves have fibrous trunks that harden into translucent plates as they mature, producing a woodland that glitters in the morning and becomes almost invisible at dusk. The trees are living organisms rather than mineral growths, though their bark contains silica and other substances that give it unusual strength, and the groves are a living forest that has adapted to the dry conditions of the southern slope in a manner that no other tree is known to do.

The roots of the glass trees spread close to the surface, forming a woven mat that catches silt and channels rain toward shaded hollows. In dry years the groves remain green after the surrounding scrub has faded, and their shade provides the principal refuge for animals moving between the uplands and the southern plains. The Kharesh glass spider, the great arachnid of crystalline carapace whose range extends across the rocky uplands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), is found in the drier margins of the groves, where the broken ground offers suitable burrows, and the spider is a familiar presence to the grove communities. The chronicler records that the spider is feared but respected, and that the grove communities maintain a careful distance from the lairs that the spiders establish beneath the larger stones.

The groves are harvested carefully. Fallen plates are collected for windows, lantern covers, and the coloured inlay used by Kharoshi artisans, while living trunks are rarely cut. The grove communities maintain boundary stones and seasonal paths that distinguish gathering grounds from protected stands, and the oldest agreements limit felling and require that every damaged stand be replanted with cuttings taken from mature trees. The chronicler notes that the groves are not a limitless resource, and that the communities that have observed the customary limits have maintained the productivity of the groves over many generations, while those that have exceeded them have seen the basins exposed to erosion and the springs reduced to a trickle.

## Rivers and Coasts

The rivers that descend from the Ember Ridge are of two principal kinds, and the distinction is the foundation of the economy of the continent. The western rivers are short and swift, fed by the rain that falls upon the outer face of the range, and they cut through the dark stone in narrow gorges before they reach the coast. The gorges open into small bays of shingle and pebble, and the harbours of the western coast are few and difficult of access, set in the mouths of the larger rivers where the water is deep enough to shelter a vessel of moderate draught. The western rivers are unsuitable for navigation beyond the first few miles of their course, and the communities of the western coast rely upon the sea for the bulk of their long-distance transport.

The eastern rivers are longer and slower, and they descend from the highland valleys through a series of terraces before they reach the broad basins that open to the eastern plains. The largest of these rivers runs through a series of narrow gorges and then spreads into braided channels across the eastern basin, supporting reeds, fruit trees, and fields of grain along its banks. The floodplain is broad and fertile, and the high banks carry vineyards and groves of the bitterleaf that the Kharoshi healers use in their medicines. The chronicler records that the eastern rivers are dependable through most of the year, though the flood of the wet season can be severe, and the communities of the eastern basin have learned to build their villages above the ordinary flood line and to withdraw to raised roads and stone platforms when the water rises.

The coast of [Kharos](/content/location/natural/continent/kharos) is shaped by the rivers and the currents that the Ember Ridge determines. The western coast supports kelp beds, seals, shore crabs, and long-bodied fish that follow the cold current along the continental shelf. The eastern waters are warmer, and the sheltered bays contain coral shelves of considerable extent. [Merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) communities are present in both regions, though their customs differ according to the temperature and the depth of the water. The rocky headlands are home to [cancrids](/content/species/sapient/cancrids), whose tidal settlements are built into the crevices above the reach of the largest waves, and the chronicler records that the Kharoshi fishers trade with both peoples, and that the oldest coastal agreements establish the seasons when nets are forbidden in the breeding grounds that are shared by the land and the sea dwellers.

## Settlements and Peoples

The settlements of the Ember Ridge are built near the springs, for the water that the range provides is the foundation of every community that dwells within sight of the smoke. The villages of the high valleys are small and self-reliant, their houses of stone and timber set upon the sheltered shelves where the steam from the vents warms the air through the cold months. The gardens of the villages are planted with the hardy crops that the long bright season ripens, and the orchards produce fruit that is exchanged with the lowland communities for the grain and the cloth that the high country cannot supply. The chronicler notes that the villages are spaced at the distance of a day's walk, and that the customary hospitality of the highland communities ensures that a traveller who arrives at any village before nightfall will find shelter and a meal.

[Humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) form the largest settled population of the range, living in the high valleys and the eastern basin towns, and their architecture makes extensive use of the dark volcanic stone that the range provides. [Elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) inhabit the older glass groves and the wetter northern valleys, where they tend the woodland and protect the headwaters of the rivers. [Gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) are common in the foothills, where their workshops use the heat of the springs and the plates of the glass trees for pottery and metalwork. The chronicler records that the three peoples exchange goods at the seasonal markets, and that mixed communities are especially common along the rivers where no single group can prosper without the others.

The highland communities maintain their own councils and their own customary law, and the chronicler notes that the law of the Ember Ridge is among the most practical of any in the known world. The councils meet in the bright season, when the passes are open and the communities can send their representatives to the appointed places. The decisions of the councils are recorded in small books that are kept by the elders, and the records are consulted whenever a dispute arises concerning the use of the springs, the grazing grounds, or the passes. The chronicler records that the councils have settled their disputes without the intervention of any distant authority, and that the system has endured for many generations.

## Significance

The Ember Ridge is the spine of [Kharos](/content/location/natural/continent/kharos), and its broken ridges, its hot springs, its glass groves, and its rivers are the inheritance that the continent has received from the volcanic highland. The range is at once a barrier and a watershed, a source of water and a source of the dark stone that the builders of the lowland value. The chronicler of the western cartographers sets down that the prosperity of the continent is bound up with the health of the range, and that the maintenance of the springs, the groves, the rivers, and the customary law that governs them is a duty that is shared by every people whose lands depend upon the water and the stone that the highland provides. The Ember Ridge endures as the living backbone of [Kharos](/content/location/natural/continent/kharos) and as the inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the ancient heat of the western continent.
