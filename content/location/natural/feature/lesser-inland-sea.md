## Smaller and Warmer of the Two Inland Seas

South of the larger sea to the north and separated from it by a low saddle of cultivated land, the Lesser Inland Sea is the smaller of the two inland seas of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach). It lies in the far south of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and its shore is the centre of the agriculture of the southern basin. The Lesser has a milder climate than the greater sea, its surrounding lowlands are highly productive, and the orchards and grain fields ringing its shore supply food to the lakeside communities.

## Low, Warm Depression and the Alluvial Fans

The basin of the Lesser Inland Sea is a low, warm depression sheltered from the prevailing winds by the highlands rising on every side, and the resulting climate is the mildest in the far south. The summers are long and the winters are short, and the rainfall is sufficient to sustain agriculture without irrigation through most years. The rivers descending from the surrounding highlands are shorter than those feeding the greater sea to the north, but they are dependable, and the silt they carry has built broad alluvial fans reaching almost to the water's edge. The soil is deep and loamy, and the shore is well suited to the cultivation of figs, dates, and the citrus fruits produced in the south.

The land rises gradually from the shore to the foothills, climbing in turn to the highlands marking the southern limit of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach). The lower slopes are clothed in open woodland of evergreen oak, wild olive, and carob, giving way higher up to the open grassland supporting the highland herds. The mosaic of woodland, cultivation, and pasture reflects the fertility of the basin.

## Ashaar and the Fig and Date Orchards

[Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar) is the principal port of the Lesser Inland Sea and the southernmost settlement of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), its harbour a natural anchorage sheltered by a curving headland on the eastern side of the water. The town is the centre of the fishing and agriculture of the southern basin, and the boats moored at its quays are the most numerous of any on the inland seas. The other lakeside settlements are smaller than [Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar), but several are of considerable age and known for particular products, whether figs, dates, or the citrus fruits of the southern shore. The smaller harbours are connected to Ashaar by a network of boat routes that is the principal means of travel around the southern shore.

The fields and orchards surrounding Ashaar are the foundation of the food supply of the basin, cultivated by the farmers of the town and the surrounding villages. The fields are watered by the rivers flowing from the highlands into the sea, and the orchards are planted upon the lower slopes where the soil is deepest and the drainage is best. The agriculture of Ashaar is highly productive within the Southern Reach, and the surplus that the basin produces is traded to the other communities of the federation by boat and by the inland routes connecting the two seas.

## Broad Beams, Low Masts, and the Ashaar Guild

The boats travelling the Lesser Inland Sea and the connecting routes to the [Greater Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/greater-inland-sea) are broad and shallow-draught vessels designed for the calm waters and frequent shallows of the lakes. The boats are of a distinctive type, with a low freeboard, a wide beam, and a single mast that can be lowered when entering a narrow channel or passing beneath a low bridge. The boatbuilders of [Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar) are the primary builders in the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), and their vessels are valued by the communities of both seas for their stability, capacity, and ease of handling in the light breezes of the basin.

The boatyard of Ashaar is a prominent feature of the harbour, and the construction of boats is a visible part of the town's industry. The boats are built from the timber of the surrounding woodlands, with ribs of oak and planking of cedar, and the craftsmen who build them are organised into a guild that maintains the standards of the trade and supervises the training of apprentices. A boat built in Ashaar typically serves for thirty years, compared to fifteen to twenty years for boats from other yards, and the vessels of the southern sea are the preferred choice of the boatmen travelling between the lakes.

## The Tortolans of the Southern Shore

The [tortolans](/content/species/sapient/tortolans) of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) are concentrated on the shores of the Lesser Inland Sea, and the community at [Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar) is the most numerous of any settlement in the federation. The warm, sheltered waters of the southern sea and the mild climate of the basin are well suited to the tortolan constitution, and the slow, deliberate pace of their lives is in keeping with the rhythm of the agricultural communities of the shore. The tortolans inhabit a quarter of Ashaar set apart from the harbour and the market, and their long-lived, unhurried presence is a familiar feature of the town.

The relationship between the tortolans and the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the southern shore is one of quiet cooperation, and the two communities have shared the waters and the land for as long as the oldest inhabitants can remember. The tortolans contribute to the fishery with their slow and patient methods, maintaining knowledge of the breeding grounds and seasonal movements of the fish that the human fishers respect. Their elders hold a long perspective on the history of the basin, and the councils of the lakeside communities often call upon the tortolan elders for counsel on matters of water and weather, for the long memory of the tortolans records patterns that the human communities have not had time to observe.

## Wool and Leather from the Southern Uplands

South of the Lesser Inland Sea, the foothills and highlands marking the southern limit of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) are the home of the highland herdsmen, whose flocks of [sheep and goats](/content/species/fauna/goats) move between winter pasture in the sheltered valleys and summer grazing upon the high meadows. The wool and leather of the highland herds are among the principal products of the southern basin, traded through the lakeside communities and dispatched to the markets of the north by the caravan routes descending from the highlands to the harbour of [Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar).

The herdsmen are a distinct community within the Southern Reach, their way of life shaped by the demands of the terrain and the climate of the uplands. They live in seasonal camps of stone and turf, moving their flocks according to the rhythm of the pastures and the weather. The highland wool is particularly valued in the markets of the north for its softness and strength, and the leather of the highland goats is used by the craftsmen of the federation for the finest bound goods produced in the southern basin. The trade between the herdsmen and the lakeside towns is one of the foundations of the economy of the southern shore, and the goods it produces are a valued component of the commerce flowing northward through the caravan routes.

## Breadbasket of the Federation

The Lesser Inland Sea is the smaller and warmer of the inland waters of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), and its shore is the centre of agriculture in the federation. The basin supports a population of approximately 8,500 across eleven recognised settlements, with [Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar) accounting for nearly 2,000. The orchards of the southern shore produce 200 tons of figs and 150 tons of dates annually, while the grain fields yield two harvests per year of [wheat](/content/species/flora/wheat) totalling approximately 600 tons. The boatyard of Ashaar launches an average of fifteen vessels each year. The highland herds to the south support a wool trade of roughly 30 tons annually, carried by caravan northward through [Torem](/content/location/settlement/town/torem).
