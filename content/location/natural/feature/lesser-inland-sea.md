## Overview

South of its larger sister and separated from it by a low saddle of cultivated land, the Lesser Inland Sea is the smaller of the two inland seas of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), and the most sheltered, the warmest, and the most fertile of the deep-south waters. It lies in the far south of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and its shore is the centre of the agriculture of the southern basin. The chronicler of the inland seas sets down that the Lesser is, in many respects, the better favoured of the two waters, for its smaller size gives it a milder climate, its surrounding lowlands are the most productive in the federation, and the orchards and the grain fields that ring its shore are the breadbasket of the lakeside communities.

## The Southern Basin

The basin of the [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea) is a low, warm depression that is sheltered from the prevailing winds by the highlands that rise on every side, and the climate that results is the mildest in the far south. The summers are long and the winters are short, and the rainfall is sufficient to sustain agriculture without irrigation through most years. The rivers that descend from the surrounding highlands are shorter than those that feed the greater sea to the north, but they are dependable, and the silt that they carry has built broad alluvial fans that reach almost to the water's edge. The soil is deep, dark, and rich, and the chronicler notes that no other shore in the Southern Reach is so well suited to the cultivation of figs, dates, and the citrus fruits that are the most prized produce of the south.

The land rises gradually from the shore to the foothills, and the foothills climb in turn to the highlands that mark the southern limit of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach). The lower slopes are clothed in open woodland of evergreen oak, wild olive, and carob, and the woods give way higher up to the open grassland that supports the highland herds. The mosaic of woodland, cultivation, and pasture is the visible sign of the fertility of the basin, and the chronicler remarks that no traveller who has seen the orchards of the southern shore in fruit can doubt that the basin is the most generously endowed of any in the deep south.

## Ashaar and the Lakeside Settlements

[Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar) is the principal port of the [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea) and the southernmost settlement of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), and its harbour is a natural anchorage that is sheltered by a curving headland on the eastern side of the water. The town is the centre of the fishing and the agriculture of the southern basin, and the boats that are moored at its quays are the most numerous of any on the inland seas. The other lakeside settlements are smaller than Ashaar, but several are of considerable age and are noted for particular products, whether figs, dates, or the citrus fruits for which the southern shore is famed, and the smaller harbours are connected to Ashaar by a network of boat routes that is the principal means of travel around the southern shore.

The fields and the orchards that surround Ashaar are the foundation of the food supply of the basin, and the cultivation is carried out by the farmers of the town and the surrounding villages. The fields are watered by the rivers that flow from the highlands into the sea, and the orchards are planted upon the lower slopes where the soil is deepest and the drainage is best. The chronicler notes that the agriculture of Ashaar is the most productive in the Southern Reach, and that the surplus that the basin produces is traded to the other communities of the federation by boat and by the inland routes that connect the two seas.

## Boats and the Lake Routes

The boats that travel the [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea) and the connecting routes to the [Greater Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/greater-inland-sea) are broad and shallow-draught vessels that are designed for the calm waters and the frequent shallows of the lakes. The boats are of a distinctive type, with a low freeboard, a wide beam, and a single mast that can be lowered when the boat enters a narrow channel or passes beneath a low bridge. The boatbuilders of [Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar) are the most skilled in the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), and their vessels are valued by the communities of both seas for their stability, their capacity, and their ease of handling in the light breezes of the basin.

The boatyard of Ashaar is a prominent feature of the harbour, and the construction of the boats is a visible part of the town's industry. The boats are built from the timber of the surrounding woodlands, with ribs of oak and planking of cedar, and the craftsmen who build them are organised into a guild that maintains the standards of the trade and supervises the training of the apprentices. The chronicler records that a boat built in Ashaar will outlast one built elsewhere by a generation, and that the boats of the southern sea are the preferred vessels of the boatmen who travel between the lakes.

## The Tortolans of the Southern Shore

The [tortolans](/content/species/sapient/tortolans) of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) are concentrated on the shores of the [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea), and the community at [Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar) is the most numerous of any settlement in the federation. The warm, sheltered waters of the southern sea and the mild climate of the basin are well suited to the tortolan constitution, and the slow, deliberate pace of their lives is in keeping with the rhythm of the agricultural communities of the shore. The tortolans inhabit a quarter of Ashaar that is set apart from the harbour and the market, and their long-lived, unhurried presence is a familiar feature of the town.

The relationship between the tortolans and the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the southern shore is one of quiet co-operation, and the two communities have shared the waters and the land for as long as the oldest inhabitants can remember. The tortolans contribute to the fishery with their slow and patient methods, and they maintain a knowledge of the breeding grounds and the seasonal movements of the fish that the human fishers respect. Their elders hold a long perspective on the history of the basin, and the chronicler notes that the councils of the lakeside communities often call upon the tortolan elders for counsel on matters of water and weather, for the long memory of the tortolans is a record of patterns that the human communities have not had the time to observe.

## The Highland Herdsmen

South of the [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea), the foothills and the highlands that mark the southern limit of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) are the home of the highland herdsmen, whose flocks of sheep and goats move between the winter pasture in the sheltered valleys and the summer grazing upon the high meadows. The wool and the leather of the highland herds are among the principal products of the southern basin, and the goods are traded through the lakeside communities and dispatched to the markets of the north by the caravan routes that descend from the highlands to the harbour of [Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar).

The herdsmen are a distinct community within the Southern Reach, and their way of life is shaped by the demands of the terrain and the climate of the uplands. They live in seasonal camps of stone and turf, and they move their flocks according to the rhythm of the pastures and the weather. The chronicler records that the highland wool is particularly valued in the markets of the north for its softness and its strength, and that the leather of the highland goats is used by the craftsmen of the federation for the finest of the bound goods that are produced in the southern basin. The trade between the herdsmen and the lakeside towns is one of the foundations of the economy of the southern shore, and the goods that it produces are a valued component of the commerce that flows northward through the caravan routes.

## Significance

The [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea) is the most sheltered and the most productive of the inland waters of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), and its shore is the breadbasket of the federation. The orchards and the grain fields of the basin are the principal source of the food that sustains the lakeside communities, the wool and the leather of the highland herds are a valued product of the trade with the north, and the boatbuilders of [Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar) produce the vessels that maintain the connections of the basin. The chronicler sets down that the lesser sea, despite its smaller size, is the most fertile of the two inland waters, and that the prosperity of the Southern Reach is founded in no small measure upon the warmth, the shelter, and the abundance of the southern shore.
