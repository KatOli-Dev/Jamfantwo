## The Principal River of the Cold North

The Greywash is the principal river of the cold north, the long waterway that gathers the streams of the southern flank of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) and carries them south and west to the sea at [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara). The river runs for several days of travel from the headwaters in the upland meadows to the broad delta that the chroniclers of the academies have named the [Mouths of the Greywash](/content/location/natural/feature/mouths-of-the-greywash), and the river has been the principal highway of the inland valleys since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities). The chronicler of the river records has set down that the Greywash is the most useful of the rivers of the cold north, and that the cities and the towns of its banks are the foundation of the public order of the present age.

The name of the river is older than the present kingdoms of the cold north, and the name is preserved in the chronicles of the academies of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm). The chronicler notes that the name was applied to the river by the earliest of the chroniclers, and that the name has been retained in every subsequent record. The academies preserve the older tablets that show the river under a different name, but the chronicler has set down that the older name is no longer in use, and that the river is universally known by the name that the present chronicles employ.

## The Headwaters and the Upper Reaches

The Greywash rises in the southern flank of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), in the meadows and the high pastures where the snowmelt gathers in the spring. The headwaters are several distinct streams that join within a day's ride of the highlands, and the joined river descends through a series of narrow valleys before it enters the broader country of the inland valleys. The chronicler of the highland records has set down that the upper reaches of the river are swift and clear, and that the water of the upper river is the purest of any of the rivers of the cold north.

The upper reaches of the Greywash pass through country that is the home of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim), the great dwarven realm of the interior, and the dwarven engineers have worked the upper river for many generations. The engineers have built the roads that follow the upper river, the bridges that cross the tributary streams, and the cisterns that store the water for the use of the highland communities. The chronicler of the dwarven records has recorded that the work of the engineers has been the foundation of the safety of the upper river, and that the roads and the bridges of the upper Greywash are among the most reliable of the dwarven works.

## The Inland Valleys and the Towns

Below the upper reaches, the Greywash enters the inland valleys, the broad and fertile country that is the home of the principal cities of the cold north. The river flows through the valleys in a long and winding course, and the towns of the river are strung along the banks at intervals of a day's ride. The chronicler of the river towns has set down that the towns are the most prosperous of any of the settlements of the cold north, and that the prosperity is built upon the trade that the river carries and the agriculture that the river's waters sustain.

The principal towns of the Greywash are [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm), the academies of which have preserved the records of the river, and the smaller market towns that serve the farmers and the fishermen of the banks. The towns are connected by the road that the [Building of the Stone Roads](/content/history/building-of-the-stone-roads) constructed upon the embankments that the magistrates of the river towns have raised over the centuries, and the road is one of the most reliable of the roads of the cold north. The chronicler of the inland cartographers has recorded that the road of the Greywash is the model for the principal roads of the other rivers of the cold north, and that the engineers of the river towns are held in particular esteem for the quality of their work.

## The Treaty of the Greywash

The [Treaty of the Greywash](/content/history/treaty-of-the-greywash) is the agreement that the magistrates of the principal towns along the river signed in the middle of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities) for the management of the river, the protection of the fisheries, and the regulation of the navigation. The treaty was negotiated over the course of several years, and the negotiation was conducted through the mediators of the academies. The chronicler of the treaty has set down that the negotiation was a model of patient and careful diplomacy, and the treaty is recorded in the chronicles of the academies as the most comprehensive of the river agreements of the present age.

The treaty took its name from the river at whose confluence the magistrates convened to sign it, and the chronicler of the river records has noted that the name of the treaty has since passed into the general usage of the river towns, so that the agreement is now known by the river's name as much as by its own. The treaty established the office of the river warden, and the river warden is appointed by the magistrates of the principal towns to oversee the implementation of the treaty's terms. The chronicler of the inland administration has recorded that the office of the river warden has been the foundation of the success of the treaty, and the river warden is held in high esteem by the magistrates and the merchants of the river towns.

## The Greywash Falls

The river descends from the inland valleys to the lower country by means of a series of cascades that the chroniclers of the academies have named the [Greywash Falls](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-falls). The falls lie within a day's ride of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), and the falls are one of the most distinctive of the natural features of the cold north. The falls are bounded on the east by the lower slopes of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale), on the west by the upper reaches of the [Reed Marshes of the Greywash](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash), on the north by the cliffs that mark the descent, and on the south by the calmer water below.

The falls have been a landmark of the river since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities), and the falls have been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies. The falls are the home of a small population of millers and fishermen, and the mills that the falls have powered have been the source of much of the flour that the towns of the lower river require. The chronicler notes that the falls are held in particular esteem by the merchants and the millers who make their living upon them, and the millers and the merchants mark the anniversaries of the founding of the mills with appropriate ceremonies.

## The Reed Marshes and the Delta

Below the falls, the Greywash broadens and slows, and the silt that the river carries settles in a broad belt of shallow water and saturated ground that the chroniclers have named the [Reed Marshes of the Greywash](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash). The marshes cover several days of travel from east to west, and the marshes are the home of a community of fishermen and reed-cutters who make their living upon the channels and the embankments. The marshes are bounded on the north by the gentle slopes of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale), on the south by the low hills that mark the southern limit of the Greywash basin, on the east by the rapids where the river descends to the plain, and on the west by the estuary that opens to the sea.

The delta of the Greywash, the [Mouths of the Greywash](/content/location/natural/feature/mouths-of-the-greywash), lies at the western edge of the principal continent, where the river divides into three principal channels that descend through a broad delta to the sea at [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara). The delta is the home of a rich variety of landscapes, and the delta is the foundation of the fishery of the lower Greywash. The chronicler of the river records has set down that the Mouths are held in particular esteem by the fishermen and the merchants of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), and the fishermen and the merchants mark the anniversaries of the founding of the delta with appropriate ceremonies.

## The Bridge of the Greywash

The bridge of the Greywash at [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) is one of the most substantial of the bridges that the [Building of the Stone Roads](/content/history/building-of-the-stone-roads) constructed, and the bridge is the principal work of engineering upon the lower river. The bridge was built of stone and timber, and the bridge was set upon piers that were founded upon the bedrock of the river. The chronicler of the road-builders has recorded that the bridge was built to last, and the bridge has been maintained by the road wardens of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) ever since. The bridge has endured for several generations, and the chronicler notes that the bridge is among the most reliable of the crossings of the cold north.

## Significance

The Greywash is one of the most useful of the natural features of the cold north, and the river is the foundation of the public order, the trade, and the agriculture of the inland valleys. The headwaters, the inland valleys, the treaty that bears the river's name, the falls, the reed marshes, the delta, and the bridge of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) are the inheritance that the river has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler of the river records has set down that the river is held in particular memory by the magistrates and the merchants of the river towns, and the magistrates and the merchants mark the anniversaries of the founding of the principal works upon the river with appropriate ceremonies. The Greywash endures, in the water that descends from the highlands and in the silt that renews the fields, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the principal river of the cold north.
