## Principal River of the Cold North

The Greywash is the principal river of the cold north, gathering the streams of the southern flank of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) and carrying them south and west to the sea at [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara). The river runs for several days of travel from the headwaters in the upland meadows to the broad delta called the [Greywash Mouths](/content/location/natural/feature/mouths-of-the-greywash), and it has been the principal highway of the inland valleys since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The Greywash is the most useful of the rivers of the cold north, and the cities and towns of its banks are the foundation of the public order of the present age.

The name of the river is older than the present kingdoms of the cold north, preserved in the chronicles of the academies of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm). The name was applied to the river by the earliest chroniclers and has been retained in every subsequent record. The academies preserve older tablets showing the river under a different name, but the older name is no longer in use, and the river is universally known by the name that the present records employ.

## The Headwaters and the Upper Reaches

The Greywash rises in the southern flank of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), in the meadows and high pastures where the snowmelt gathers in the spring. The headwaters are several distinct streams that join within a day's ride of the highlands, and the joined river descends through a series of narrow valleys before entering the broader country of the inland valleys. The upper reaches of the river are swift and clear, and the water of the upper river is the purest of any of the rivers of the cold north.

The upper reaches pass through country that is the home of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), the great dwarven realm of the interior, and the dwarven engineers have worked the upper river for many generations. They have built the roads that follow the upper river, the bridges crossing the tributary streams, and the cisterns storing water for the use of the highland communities. The work of the engineers has been the foundation of the safety of the upper river, and the roads and bridges of the upper Greywash are among the most reliable of the dwarven works.

## Khalisar, Vassholm, and the Stone Road

Below the upper reaches, the Greywash enters the inland valleys, the broad and fertile country that is the home of the principal cities of the cold north. The river flows through the valleys in a long and winding course, and the towns of the river are strung along the banks at intervals of a day's ride. These towns are the most prosperous of any settlements of the cold north, and their prosperity is built upon the trade that the river carries and the agriculture that the river's waters sustain.

The principal towns of the Greywash are [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm), the academies of which have preserved the records of the river, and the smaller market towns serving the farmers and fishermen of the banks. The towns are connected by the road that the [Laying the Stone Roads](/content/history/laying-the-stone-roads) constructed upon the embankments that the magistrates of the river towns have raised over the centuries, and this road is one of the most reliable of the cold north. The road of the Greywash is the model for the principal roads of the other rivers of the cold north, and the engineers of the river towns are held in particular esteem for the quality of their work.

## The River Warden and the Historic Accord

The [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact) is the agreement that the magistrates of the principal towns along the river signed in the middle of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace) for the management of the river, the protection of the fisheries, and the regulation of the navigation. The compact was negotiated over the course of several years through the mediators of the academies. The negotiation was a model of patient and careful diplomacy, and the compact is recorded in the chronicles of the academies as the most comprehensive of the river agreements of the present age.

The treaty took its name from the river at whose confluence the magistrates convened to sign it, and the name has since passed into the general usage of the river towns. The treaty established the office of the river warden, appointed by the magistrates of the principal towns to oversee the carrying out of the treaty's terms. The office of the river warden has been the foundation of the success of the treaty, and the river warden is held in high esteem by the magistrates and merchants of the river towns.

## Rapids and the Mills at the Descent

The river descends from the inland valleys to the lower country by means of a series of cascades called the [Greywash Falls](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-falls). The falls lie within a day's ride of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) and are one of the most distinctive natural features of the cold north. They are bounded on the east by the lower slopes of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale), on the west by the upper reaches of the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash), on the north by the cliffs marking the descent, and on the south by the calmer water below.

The falls have been a landmark of the river since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era) and have been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies. They are the home of a small population of millers and fishermen, and the mills that the falls have powered are the source of much of the flour that the towns of the lower river require. The falls are held in particular esteem by the merchants and millers who make their living upon them, and they mark the anniversaries of the founding of the mills with appropriate ceremonies.

## Broad Belt of Shallow Water and Saturated Ground

Below the falls, the Greywash broadens and slows, and the silt that the river carries settles in a broad belt of shallow water and saturated ground called the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash). The marshes cover several days of travel from east to west and are the home of a community of fishermen and reed-cutters who make their living upon the channels and embankments. They are bounded on the north by the gentle slopes of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale), on the south by the low hills marking the southern limit of the Greywash basin, on the east by the rapids where the river descends to the plain, and on the west by the estuary opening to the sea.

The delta of the Greywash, the [Greywash Mouths](/content/location/natural/feature/mouths-of-the-greywash), lies at the western edge of the principal continent, where the river divides into three principal channels descending through a broad delta to the sea at [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara). The delta is the home of a rich variety of landscapes and the foundation of the fishery of the lower Greywash. The Mouths are held in particular esteem by the fishermen and merchants of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), and they mark the anniversaries of the founding of the delta with appropriate ceremonies.

## The Stone Crossing at Nessara

The bridge of the Greywash at [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) is one of the most substantial bridges that the [Laying the Stone Roads](/content/history/laying-the-stone-roads) constructed, and it is the principal work of engineering upon the lower river. The bridge was built of stone and timber, set upon piers founded upon the bedrock of the river. It was built to last and has been maintained by the road wardens of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) ever since. The bridge has endured for several generations and is among the most reliable crossings of the cold north.

## Enduring Water, Enduring Works

The Greywash is one of the most useful natural features of the cold north, and the river is the foundation of the public order, trade, and agriculture of the inland valleys. The headwaters, the inland valleys, the treaty that bears the river's name, the falls, the reed marshes, the delta, and the bridge of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) are the inheritance that the river has bequeathed to the present age. The river is held in particular memory by the magistrates and merchants of the river towns, and they mark the anniversaries of the founding of the principal works upon the river with appropriate ceremonies. The Greywash endures, in the water descending from the highlands and in the silt that renews the fields, as the living inheritance of those whose work and life are bound up with the principal river of the cold north.
