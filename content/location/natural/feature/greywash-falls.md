## Overview

Where the Greywash descends from the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) to the lower reaches of the river, the channel narrows and the water drops in a series of cascades that the chroniclers of the academies have named the Greywash Falls. The falls lie within a day's ride of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), and the falls are one of the most distinctive of the natural features of the cold north. The falls are bounded on the east by the lower slopes of the vale, on the west by the upper reaches of the [Reed Marshes of the Greywash](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash), on the north by the cliffs that mark the descent, and on the south by the calmer water below.

The falls have been a landmark of the river since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities), and the falls have been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies. The falls are the home of a small population of millers and fishermen, and the mills that the falls have powered have been the source of much of the flour that the towns of the lower river require. The chronicler notes that the falls are one of the most distinctive of the natural features of the cold north, and the falls are held in particular esteem by the merchants and the millers who make their living upon them.

## The Cascades

The cascades of the falls descend in three principal drops over a distance of several hundred yards, and the cascades are separated by stretches of calmer water where the river runs over the bedrock of the plateau. The upper cascade is the highest, and the upper cascade drops in a single sheet of water that the chroniclers of the academies compare to a curtain of glass. The central cascade is broken into several channels by the rocks that have fallen from the cliffs above, and the central cascade is the most picturesque of the three. The lower cascade is the smallest, and the lower cascade is the principal site of the mills that the river supports.

The cascades are the source of the power that the mills require, and the mills have been a feature of the falls since the founding of the towns of the lower river. The chronicler notes that the mills are the source of much of the flour that the towns require, and the mills are the foundation of the careful management of the water that the [Treaty of the Greywash](/content/history/treaty-of-the-greywash) established.

## The Cliffs

The cliffs that flank the falls are composed of the same ancient stone that forms the plateau of the upper river, and the cliffs are exposed in the faces that drop to the water below. The cliffs are the home of the seabirds that breed upon the ledges in the spring, and the birds include the gulls and the cormorants that the chroniclers of the [Cataloguing of Flora and Fauna](/content/belief/science/physical/the-cataloguing-of-flora-and-fauna) have catalogued with care. The cliffs are covered with a tough vegetation of shrubs and small trees that the salt spray has shaped into low, wind-bent forms, and the vegetation is the foundation of the ecology of the falls.

The cliffs are the source of the small streams that run off the slopes to the river below, and the streams are the only source of fresh water upon the cliffs. The chronicler notes that the streams are small and unreliable, and the streams are sufficient for the small millers' village above the falls but not for any larger settlement.

## The Mills

The mills of the falls have been a feature of the river since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities), and the mills have been the source of much of the flour that the towns of the lower river require. The mills are built upon the lower cascade, where the water is most reliable and the head is greatest, and the mills are turned by water-wheels that the millers have constructed with care. The chronicler notes that the mills are the most productive of the mills of the cold north, and the mills are held in high esteem by the magistrates and the merchants of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara).

The mills are maintained by the millers in consultation with the river warden that the [Treaty of the Greywash](/content/history/treaty-of-the-greywash) established, and the river warden supervises the distribution of the water among the mills. The chronicler notes that the distribution has been the foundation of the careful management of the mills, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of the lower river.

## The Fishery

The waters below the falls support a rich fishery, and the fishery has been the foundation of the prosperity of the villages along the river below the falls. The principal fish are the salmon that run up the river in the spring and the autumn, the trout that the fishermen take from the deeper pools below the falls, and the smaller fish that the fishermen take from the shallows in the summer. The fishery is regulated by the river warden in consultation with the elders of the villages, and the regulations set the seasons of the catch, the methods that the fishermen must use, and the price that the fishermen may charge.

The chronicler notes that the fishery has been the subject of long debate between the towns of the upper river and the towns of the lower river, and the debate has been settled by the careful management that the [Treaty of the Greywash](/content/history/treaty-of-the-greywash) established. The treaty sets the proportion of the catch that each town may take, and the proportion has been the foundation of the careful management of the fishery.

## The Wildlife

The falls are the home of a rich variety of wildlife, and the wildlife is the foundation of the ecology of the falls. The cliffs support the seabirds that breed upon the ledges in the spring, and the birds include the gulls and the cormorants that the chroniclers of the [Cataloguing of Flora and Fauna](/content/belief/science/physical/the-cataloguing-of-flora-and-fauna) have catalogued with care. The slopes support the smaller mammals that the birds of prey feed upon, and the waters below the falls support the fish that the fishermen pursue.

The wildlife is the source of much of the food of the villages along the river, and the wildlife is the foundation of the careful management of the chase that the river warden maintains. The chronicler notes that the wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies, and the scholars have recorded the patterns of the birds and the fish in their catalogues.

## Significance

The Greywash Falls are one of the most distinctive of the natural features of the cold north, and the falls are the foundation of the milling and the fishery of the lower river. The cascades, the cliffs, the mills, the villages, and the streams are the inheritance that the falls have bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the falls are held in particular memory by the millers and the fishermen who make their living upon them, and the millers and the fishermen mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the cold north, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every town of the lower river.
