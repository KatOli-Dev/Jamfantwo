## The Peak That Names the Pass

The higher Stonespire is the great peak of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) that gives its name to the [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass) at whose head it stands, and it is a prominent summit flanking the principal overland route between the cold north and the warm coast. The Stonespire rises from a saddle in the range where the highlands narrow between two of the highest crests, and its snow-streaked flanks are visible from the foothills of the warm coast on a clear day and from the upper valleys of the cold north throughout the brightest weeks of summer. The higher Stonespire is the dominant peak of the central [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim), and the lesser summits surrounding it are known to the highland shepherds and the dwarven road-wardens by their own names.

The name of the peak is older than the present kingdoms of the cold north, preserved in the chronicles of the academies of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and in the older inscriptions that the highland road-wardens maintain at the principal crossings. The name was applied to the peak by the earliest of the dwarven chroniclers and has been retained in every subsequent record. The highland communities apply the name to the single dominant peak and not to the surrounding summits; the lesser peaks are known by the names of local features, the Sentinel to the east and the [Watcher](/content/religion/watcher) to the west, rather than by the great name of the Stonespire.

## Single Sharp Summit of Dark Stone

The higher Stonespire is a single, sharp summit of dark stone, with a southern face that drops in a long, unbroken cliff toward the foothills of the warm coast, and a northern face that is more broken but equally steep. The peak rises above the line of perpetual snow, and the snowfields upon its flanks feed streams descending in two directions, southward to the warm coast and northward to the headwaters of the rivers of the cold north. The shape of the peak is distinct from the other summits of the Stoneheim, and it can be identified from any of the lower ridges that look upon it.

The flanks of the peak support the hardy vegetation that the climate permits. The lower slopes are covered by the conifers that the Reading the Ground has catalogued with care, and the higher slopes support only the lichens and mosses that the cold allows. The peak is the home of smaller mammals that the [Golden Eagle](/content/species/fauna/golden-eagle) feeds upon and the wild goats that the hunters of the holds pursue in season. The peak has been the subject of careful survey by the pilots of the Distance-Craft, and the surveys have established the height of the Stonespire with precision.

## Saddle Between Stonespire and the Sentinel

The [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass) lies at the head of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), in the saddle between the higher Stonespire to the north and the lesser peak called the Sentinel to the south. The pass is broad enough to accommodate the road and the small streams running off the slopes to either side, and it has been the site of the road that the [Laying the Stone Roads](/content/history/laying-the-stone-roads) constructed. The road is paved with the hard stone of the surrounding peaks and is one of the most reliable roads of the cold north.

The road descends from the pass to the foothills of the warm coast in a long, winding course that the engineers of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) cut into the rock with care. The engineer who oversaw the design and construction was [Bron Stoneheim](/content/people/historical/bron-stoneheim), the master engineer of the road-building programme whose work is the foundation of the land trade between the cold north and the warm coast. Bron's road across the pass is a significant work of the road-building programme and has supported the land trade for several generations.

## Bron Stoneheim and the Road Across the Pass

The Stonespire was the principal challenge of the [Laying the Stone Roads](/content/history/laying-the-stone-roads), and the road crossing the pass is a notable work of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace). The road was built upon a foundation of large stones set into a trench, covered with a layer of smaller stones packed tight, and finished with a layer of gravel rolled smooth, crowned slightly so that water would run off to the ditches cut along the sides. The engineering was adapted to the terrain, and the engineers of the Stoneheim faced challenges that the engineers of the warm coast did not.

The engineers of the Stoneheim cut the road into the rock of the steepest sections and built retaining walls to hold the road in place upon the steeper slopes. The cuttings on the southern face of the pass were cut by the dwarven engineers of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) over the course of more than twenty years and are maintained to the present day by the road wardens of the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch).

## Refuge Towers and the Garrison Quarters

The watchtowers of the pass were erected by the magistrates of the towns of the cold north in the early generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace) and have been maintained by the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) ever since. The watchtowers stand at the northern and southern ends of the pass and serve as refuge for travellers caught upon the road in time of storm. The garrisons consist of a sergeant and four men, holding the towers for a tour of two months. The watchtowers are the foundation of the safety of the pass and are maintained in good order by the merchants and travellers of the road.

The quarters of the pass are small buildings of stone providing shelter for travellers caught upon the road in time of storm, and they are the home of the small garrisons maintaining the watchtowers. The quarters are furnished with supplies that the garrisons require for the tour, delivered by the magistrates of the towns of both regions at the beginning of every season. The quarters are the foundation of the safety of the pass and are maintained by the merchants and travellers of the road.

## The Great Dwarven Realm of the Interior

The [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) is the great dwarven realm of the interior, the principal political entity of the cold north. The realm encompasses the underground cities, mining settlements, and mountain territories inhabited and governed by the dwarven people of the continent, and its engineers have built the mountain roads of the region since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The engineers of the holds have been the foundation of the road-building programme since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era) and are noted for the quality of their work upon the [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass).

[Bron Stoneheim](/content/people/historical/bron-stoneheim) was born into a household of miners and engineers serving the holds for several generations, trained as an engineer in the workshops of the senior holds before being appointed chief engineer of the road-building programme. The road that Bron designed and oversaw was the work of more than twenty years, opened to traffic in the middle of Bron's service. Bron's road across the Stonespire is a significant work of the road-building programme and the foundation of the land trade of the cold north for several generations.

## Trout and the Descending Streams

The Stonespire supports a rich variety of wildlife forming the foundation of the ecology of the peak. The slopes support smaller mammals that birds of prey feed upon and the wild goats that the hunters of the holds pursue in season. The streams running off the slopes support the trout and grayling that the fishermen of the holds pursue and are the source of the water that the quarters and watchtowers require. The wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the Bestiaries and Herbals, who have recorded the patterns of the mammals and fish in their catalogues.

The streams descending from the Stonespire are the source of several of the principal rivers of the cold north and the foundation of the water supply of the towns of the upper valleys. The streams are carefully managed by the highland communities, a communal obligation enforced by the customary law of the highland. The streams are the source of the water that the towns require, and the towns mark the anniversaries of the founding of the customary law with appropriate ceremonies.

## Living Inheritance of the Highland Road

The Stonespire is the principal peak of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), and it is the foundation of the engineering and land trade of the cold north. The peak rises to 9,400 feet above sea level, with the snow line at approximately 7,000 feet. The [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass) crosses the saddle at an elevation of 6,800 feet, and the road spanning it runs for 18 miles between the northern and southern approach stations. Two watchtowers are maintained at the northern and southern ends of the pass, each garrisoned by a sergeant and four soldiers of the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch), serving two-month tours. The road carries an average of 120 caravans per year between the cold north and the warm coast, with the heaviest traffic concentrated in the summer months when the pass is free of snow.
