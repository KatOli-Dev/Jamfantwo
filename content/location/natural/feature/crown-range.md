## Overview

The Crown Range is the set of highlands whose ridges form a broad crescent around the central valleys of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), and the range is the spine and the watershed of the continent. The mountains are not continuous walls. They open into passes, stair-stepped plateaus, and high meadows, allowing roads to cross the continent while still dividing its waters, and the chronicler of the inland cartographers has set down that the Crown Range is at once a barrier and a corridor, for the same openings that allow the passage of the roads also divide the rivers that descend to the two coasts. The range must not be confused with the [Sunken Crown](/content/location/natural/feature/sunken-crown) of [Ashara](/content/location/natural/continent/ashara), a separate and quite different highland of the warm continent to the south, for the two ranges are distinguished by climate, by geology, and by the peoples who inhabit their slopes.

## Ridges and Highlands

The ridges of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) rise in a broad arc from the northern capes of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara) to the southern hills, and the central peaks are high enough to retain snow through much of the warm season. The summits are bare and wind-scoured, and the snowfields that lie upon them feed the rivers that descend to the two coasts. The high meadows below the snowline are the domain of the shepherds of the range, who move their flocks between the summer pasture on the open grass and the winter shelter of the sheltered valleys, and the Thessaran Highlands begin above the last orchards and extend across the upper valleys, where the thin soils and the strong winds favour grasses, juniper, and the low flowering shrubs that complete their bloom before the grazing herds arrive.

The lower slopes of the range are covered in pine and cedar, and the woods thin with altitude until they give way to the open meadows and the bare rock of the highest crests. The range is crossed by many valleys, but the great majority of them are too steep or too narrow for roads, and the routes that the caravans and the merchants follow are confined to the few passes that the chronicler of the highland roads has recorded with care. The stair-stepped plateaus that interrupt the descent from the summits to the lower slopes are a peculiar feature of the range, and the plateaus are the sites of the highland villages and the summer pastures that sustain the communities of the upper valleys.

## The Western Slope

The western slope of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) is steep and wet, for the prevailing weather of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara) drives the rains against the western face of the range, and the rivers that descend from the highland snows are short, swift, and full through most of the year. The rivers cut through dark stone in narrow gorges, and the gorges open at the coast into narrow estuaries bordered by marsh and reed beds, where the fresh water meets the salt of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) in a tangle of channels. The estuaries are difficult to navigate, and the harbours of the western coast are few and small, set in the mouths of the larger rivers where the water is deep enough to shelter a vessel of moderate draught.

The forests of the western slope are dense, and the lower valleys are the home of the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) who inhabit the northern cedar woods and the high valleys where the streams remain cool throughout the summer. The elven settlements are small and widely spaced, and their forest management favours mixed stands rather than a single useful tree, for the elven principle is to take without diminishing the woodland. The chronicler records that the elven presence upon the western slope is among the most ancient in [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), and that the elves of the range are held in particular respect by the highland communities for their knowledge of the weather and the water.

## The Eastern Slope

The eastern slope of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) is gentler and drier than the western, for the rains have already fallen upon the western face, and the air that descends to the east is comparatively warm and dry. The rivers are longer and slower, and they cross broad terraces and long alluvial fans before reaching the eastern coast. The terraces are the most densely settled lands of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), and the villages that occupy them grow grain, olives, and fruit upon the deep soils that the rivers have laid down over the centuries. The alluvial fans at the mouths of the highland valleys are the richest of the eastern farmlands, and the orchards that are planted upon them produce the figs, the grapes, and the olives that are among the most valued products of the continent.

The human population of the eastern slope is the largest in [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), and the villages are arranged around communal wells and along the roads that follow the terraces. The architecture of the slope reflects the climate, with low buildings of stone, broad eaves, and shaded courtyards that catch the breeze in the heat of the summer. The chronicler notes that the terraces require constant maintenance, for the rivers that built them are capable of taking them away, and the terrace walls are rebuilt and strengthened after every flood that the summer storms bring down from the highland valleys.

## The Central Basin and the Roads

Between the two slopes of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) lies a basin of pale limestone, honey-coloured in the afternoon light, whose level floor and gentle gradient have served as the principal route across the continent since the first settled communities appeared. The basin is not a single valley but a chain of linked plains, each separated from the next by a low ridge of harder stone, and the roads that follow the basin connect the eastern and the western coasts by way of the passes that descend from the highland meadows. The chronicler of the inland roads records that the basin has been a highway for so long that the wear of the wheels and the hooves is visible in the surface of the limestone, and that the road is one of the oldest in [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara).

The towns of the basin are staging points for the caravans that cross the range, and the merchants of the basin maintain the inns, the warehouses, and the workshops that the road requires. The [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) of the lower passes have established workshops in the basin towns, producing survey instruments, glazed pottery, and the small water devices that the farmers of the terraces and the highland meadows require. The chronicler notes that the basin is the most cosmopolitan part of the range, for the road brings together the highland shepherds, the eastern farmers, the western fishermen, and the merchants of the coastal cities, and the markets of the basin towns are among the most varied in [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara).

## The Passes

The passes of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) are the openings that allow the road across the continent to follow its course, and the chronicler of the highland roads has set down that the most reliable of the central passes is the [Amber Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/amber-pass), whose road is closed only during the worst winter storms. The [Amber Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/amber-pass) is not the highest of the central crossings, but its stone floor drains well, its springs remain available through the dry season, and its approaches are gentle enough that laden animals can traverse them in safety. The other passes of the range are more difficult, and several are open only during the warmest weeks of the summer, when the snow has retreated from the high meadows and the rivers that cross the roads are low enough to be forded.

The passes are maintained by the highland communities that inhabit the approaches, and the maintenance is a communal obligation that the chronicler of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) has recorded as a foundation of the trust between the highland and the lowland peoples. Refuge halls are maintained at the highest points of the principal passes, stocked with dried meat, peat, and spare timber for the use of travellers who are caught by the weather, and the highland wardens keep the roads clear of snow during the weeks when the crossings are open. The chronicler records that the loss of a pass is a disaster for the communities on both sides of the range, and that the maintenance of the crossings is a duty that is not neglected in any season.

## Mineral Wealth and the Dwarven Works

The [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) is rich in iron, tin, and the coloured stone that the craftsmen of the continent value, and the mines that have been opened in the highland valleys are among the most productive in [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara). The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the range have worked the veins of metal and stone for many generations, and their galleries and shafts are a network of passages that extends beneath several of the principal ridges. The dwarf miners have also driven drainage galleries and built cisterns beneath the highland meadows, and the water that they collect is the source of the streams that feed the headwaters of the eastern rivers. The chronicler records that the dwarven works are among the most extensive in the continent, and that the miners of the range are held in particular respect for the skill and the patience that their craft requires.

The metals and the stone that the dwarves extract are transported down the eastern slope to the coastal cities, where they are exchanged for the grain, the cloth, and the manufactured goods that the highland mines cannot produce. The caravans that carry the metal and the stone are among the heaviest that travel the roads of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), and the drovers who handle them are a distinct and a valued body of craftsmen. The chronicler notes that the dwarven metalwork of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) is of a quality that is recognised throughout the continent, and that the iron and the tin of the range are the foundation of the tool-making and the metalwork of the eastern cities.

## The Agreement with the Valleys

The drainage galleries and the cisterns that the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) have built beneath the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) are subject to agreements that keep the underground channels open to the downstream villages of the eastern valleys. The agreements were drawn up in the early years of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league), and they are renewed at the regular meetings of the highland wardens and the valley councils. The terms of the agreements require that the dwarven works do not divert the water from the channels that supply the villages, and that the galleries are kept open so that the water that does not enter the dwarven workings continues to flow to the streams that the farmers depend upon. The chronicler records that the agreements have been observed without serious interruption for many generations, and that the trust between the highland miners and the lowland farmers is a foundation of the prosperity of the continent.

The agreement is administered by panels composed of representatives from upstream and downstream communities, and the panels meet each year to review the state of the water and the state of the galleries. The chronicler of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) notes that the agreement is the model for the other arrangements that the league maintains between the highland and the lowland communities, and that the observation of the terms is regarded as a measure of the public spirit of the parties. The dwarves accept the limits upon their works because the works cannot be maintained without the goodwill of the farmers, and the farmers accept the presence of the works because the metal and the stone of the range are a resource that they themselves require.

## Significance

The [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) is the spine and the watershed of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), and its ridges, its passes, its meadows, and its mines are the inheritance that the continent has received from the highland. The range is at once a barrier and a corridor, a watershed and a source of water, a treasury of metal and a foundation of agreement. The chronicler of the inland cartographers sets down that the prosperity of the continent is bound up with the health of the range, and that the maintenance of the passes, the drainage, and the mining works is a duty that is shared by every people whose lands depend upon the water and the stone that the highland provides.
