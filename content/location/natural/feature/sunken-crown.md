## Overview

The Sunken Crown is the principal mountain range of [Ashara](/content/location/natural/continent/ashara), a long and ancient highland whose weathered ridges occupy the entire western half of the warm continent. The range is not a single wall but a broad and broken chain of uplifts whose outer slopes have been worn over countless ages into stepped terraces and whose upper crests are still high enough to draw rain from the warm sea winds. The mountains are the source of the silt that has built the lowland basins, the home of the oldest forests on the continent, and the watershed from which the great rivers descend to the eastern lowlands. The chronicler of the inland cartographers has set down that the Sunken Crown is the spine of [Ashara](/content/location/natural/continent/ashara) in the same measure that the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) is the spine of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), and that the two ranges, though they share a name and an orientation, are wholly distinct in climate, in geology, and in the peoples who inhabit their slopes.

The range is sometimes confused in foreign accounts with the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), and the two are indeed so similar in their east-west sweep across their respective continents that the older chronicles occasionally treat them as a single feature of the world. They are not. The Sunken Crown is older, lower, and warmer than its Thessaran counterpart, and the conditions of its slopes are governed by the wet season and the dry season rather than by the snow and the high meadow. The chronicler notes that a traveller who crosses both ranges in succession will recognise a kinship in their form but a difference in every other respect, and the customs of the two highland peoples are as distinct as the climates that have shaped them.

## Ridges and Terraces

The outer slopes of the Sunken Crown are the most distinctive feature of the range, for they have been worn into broad steps that the chronicler of the western cartographers has described as a stairway from the lowland to the cloud forest. The lower steps are wide enough to contain villages and terraced fields, the middle steps support the hardwood forests, and the upper steps are the platform of the cloud forest and the high grassland. The stone of the steps is the dark volcanic and metamorphic rock of the ancient range, broken by countless seasons of rain and sun into ledges that retain the moisture of the wet season long after the rains have passed.

The terraces are not the work of any single generation. They are the cumulative result of patient cultivation by the highland communities, each terrace rebuilt or strengthened after the heavy rains that the wet season delivers. The chronicler records that the maintenance of the terraces is a communal obligation, and the villages of the higher slopes set aside a portion of the labour of the dry season for the repair of the walls and the channels that carry the water from one terrace to the next. The terraces that are not maintained revert to scrub within a few seasons, and a community that neglects them loses the use of the slope above.

The upper ridges of the range are covered by the cloud forest, a high and misty woodland that draws its moisture from the warm sea winds and the cool air of the highest crests. The trees of the cloud forest are shorter than the giants of the lower slopes, for the wind and the cold of the upper ridges check their growth, but the mosses, the orchids, and the ferns are larger and more abundant than anywhere else in [Ashara](/content/location/natural/continent/ashara). The forest is the home of birds and small mammals that are found in the lowlands only as occasional visitors, and the streams that descend from the cloud forest carry a steady supply of clean water to the lower slopes through every month of the year.

## Forests and Woodlands

The hardwood forests of the lower and middle slopes of the Sunken Crown are among the most diverse in the known world. Tall sailwood, rain cedar, and black mahogany form the upper canopy, while broad-leaved palms and flowering shrubs fill the middle levels, and vines and epiphytes gather moisture from the air and create deep soils around the roots of the largest trees. The forests have been inhabited for many generations by the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of [Ashara](/content/location/natural/continent/ashara), whose small garden settlements lie beneath the canopy in places that an outsider might pass without recognising them as habitations. The elves cultivate medicinal plants without clearing the surrounding woodland, and their knowledge of tree diseases is valued by the human farmers and the shipwrights of the lowland rivers.

The high grassland of the upper slopes is the domain of the shepherds and the herders of the high country, who move their flocks between the open grass and the sheltered valleys according to the season. The grassland is too high for cultivation, but it supports a wealth of small game and a variety of useful plants, and the herders who tend the flocks have developed a body of customary knowledge that governs the use of the high pasture and the protection of the streams. The chronicler notes that the high grassland is the seasonal home of no settled community, for the winters are too cold and the summers too brief for permanent habitation, but the herders return to the same grounds year after year in a pattern that has changed little over the generations.

The forests and the grasslands of the Sunken Crown are joined by a transitional zone in which the trees thin and the grass begins to dominate, and the zone is the principal site of the controlled burning that the highland communities practise to maintain the open ground. The burning is conducted in the late dry season, when the undergrowth is at its driest and the fire can be contained within the boundaries that the community has established. The practice has been refined over many generations into a system of fire management that the chronicler of the highland forestry has recorded as one of the most successful examples of communal land management on the continent.

## Rivers and Water

The rivers that descend from the Sunken Crown are the arteries of [Ashara](/content/location/natural/continent/ashara), and the chronicler of the inland cartographers has set down that the continent is shaped by its water in a manner that no other landmass in the known world quite matches. The rivers begin in the cloud forest and the high grassland, where the rain is abundant, and they descend through the hardwood forests and the cultivated terraces in a series of falls and rapids that are gathered for irrigation before they reach the lower basins. The water is dependable because the headwaters receive rain at every elevation, and the rivers that flow out of the range are not subject to the violent floods that afflict rivers whose sources lie in snowmelt alone.

The principal rivers of the range are the three that the inland cartographers have named in their oldest records, each of which gathers its waters from a different section of the highland and carries them to a different basin of the lowland. The northern river waters the warm and densely cultivated basin of the north, the central river crosses the open woodland and the seasonal lakes of the middle basin, and the southern river feeds the wetter lowland and the broad marsh that lies at the mouth of the southern slope. The chronicler notes that the three rivers are not equal in volume, and the southern river is the broadest and the most generous, while the northern is the swiftest and the most easily diverted for irrigation.

The water rights of the rivers are among the most carefully managed in the known world, and the river clerks who travel between the settlements before the rains keep a record of the customary allocations that govern the use of the water. The rights are measured by household, by field, and by boat, and a single family may hold different rights over each use. The chronicler records that the system has its disputes, as any system of common management must, but that the disputes are settled through the river councils and the customary law of the water rather than through the intervention of any distant authority.

## Mines and Minerals

The Sunken Crown is rich in minerals, and the mines that have been opened in the weathered rock of the outer slopes are among the most productive in [Ashara](/content/location/natural/continent/ashara). The principal metals are iron and tin, both of which are found in veins that lie close to the surface of the weathered stone, and the coloured stone that the craftsmen of the lowland cities value for building and for inlay work. The mines are generally shallow, for the richest veins lie near the surface where the rainwater has carried the lighter minerals away and concentrated the heavier ones in pockets that are easily reached. The chronicler notes that this shallowness has made the mines more accessible to small communities than the deeper workings of the colder ranges, and that the mining of the Sunken Crown has remained a craft of families and villages rather than of great companies.

The deposits of amber resin are a particular feature of the range, for the resin that has hardened in the soil of the highland forests is gathered in pieces of considerable size and clarity, and the craftsmen of the lowland cities work the amber into beads, ornaments, and the small objects that are exchanged across the continent. The amber is gathered by the forest communities in the late dry season, when the ground is at its hardest and the resin is most easily distinguished from the surrounding soil. The chronicler records that the gathering is governed by customary rules that limit the taking to a measure that the forest can renew, and that the amber is not removed from the upper slopes in quantities that would expose the roots of the trees to the weather.

The mines and the amber works are connected to the lowland markets by the Amber Pass, the principal crossing of the range and the route by which the highland minerals reach the rivers and the ports. The pass is not the highest of the crossings, but its stone floor drains well, its springs remain available through the dry season, and its approaches are gentle enough that laden animals can traverse them in safety. The chronicler of the highland cartographers has set down that the pass has been a highway for so long that the wear of the wheels and the hooves is visible in the surface of the stone, and that the road is one of the oldest in [Ashara](/content/location/natural/continent/ashara).

## Settlements and the Highland Communities

The highland communities of the Sunken Crown are distributed across the slopes according to the elevation and the resources that each section provides. The villages of the cultivated terraces are the most numerous, and they cluster around the springs and the streams that descend from the upper ridges. The settlements of the hardwood forest are fewer and more widely spaced, for the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the forest prefer to live in small garden communities that are scattered through the trees rather than in the larger villages of the terraces. The shepherds of the high grassland maintain no permanent settlement at all, but they return to the same grounds season after season and the grounds are marked by the small shrines and the boundary stones that the customary law requires.

The architecture of the highland villages reflects the conditions of the range. Houses are built of stone and timber, with broad eaves and steep roofs that shed the heavy rain of the wet season, and they are set upon raised foundations that allow the water to pass beneath rather than collect around the walls. The villages maintain granaries above the flood line, smoke rooms for the preservation of the meat of the highland flocks, and the storage houses for the amber and the metal that the highlands produce. The chronicler notes that the highland villages are self-reliant in most matters of daily life, and that the long ascent from the lowland has made the highland communities careful in their use of the goods that the lower country provides.

The relationship between the highland communities and the lowland farmers is governed by agreements that have been renewed at the customary gatherings of the mountain councils. The highland communities provide metal, amber, wool, and the dried herbs of the upper slopes, and the lowland farmers provide grain, salt fish, and the manufactured goods that the high country cannot produce. The exchanges are conducted at the markets that are held at the foot of the Amber Pass, and the markets are the principal occasions of the meeting between the highland and the lowland peoples. The chronicler records that the meetings have been conducted without serious interruption for many generations, and that the trust between the two communities is the foundation of the prosperity of the range.

## Significance

The Sunken Crown is the principal mountain range of [Ashara](/content/location/natural/continent/ashara), and its weathered slopes, its cloud forests, its rivers, and its mines are the inheritance that the continent has received from the highland. The range is at once a barrier and a corridor, a watershed and a source of water, a treasury of metal and a foundation of the customary law that governs the use of the rivers and the terraces. The chronicler of the inland cartographers sets down that the prosperity of the continent is bound up with the health of the range, and that the maintenance of the terraces, the forests, the rivers, and the mines is a duty that is shared by every people whose lands depend upon the water and the stone that the highland provides. The Sunken Crown endures as the spine of [Ashara](/content/location/natural/continent/ashara) and as the inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the ancient mountains of the warm continent.
