## Grey Rings of Fog and the Western Sea

The Greyring Ocean lies to the west of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and to the south of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). It is the cooler of the two oceans, with steady currents flowing from the polar south toward the equator. Few established trade routes cross the Greyring, and much of its expanse remains unexplored. Sailors who have ventured into its waters speak of long rolling swells and banks of mist that shroud the ocean in grey. The name Greyring is said to derive from the grey rings of fog that encircle the waters on the horizon, visible from the western cliffs of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) on all but the clearest days.

## Cold Northward Flow and the Nutrient Upwelling

The cold currents of the Greyring flow northward from the southern polar regions, chilling the air above them and giving the western coasts of Aethelar a cooler and damper climate than the eastern shores. Fogs are common along the coast, particularly in the autumn and winter months, when the contrast between the cold water and the warmer air inland produces dense banks of mist that may persist for days. The peoples of the western coast have learned to navigate by sound and memory when the mist obscures the landmarks, using bells and horns placed along the shore to guide vessels safely to harbour. The fog is both a hazard and a shelter, for it has on many occasions concealed the western coast from fleets approaching with hostile intent.

The ocean's waters are rich with marine life, nourished by the upwelling of cold, nutrient-laden water from the deep. This upwelling occurs along the edge of the continental shelf, where the sea floor drops away and the currents draw water upward from the abyssal depths. The abundance of nutrients supports vast shoals of fish, which in turn attract larger predators, including the [greyring whale](/content/species/beasts/greyring-whale) and other species, and the fishing grounds of the Greyring are among the most productive in the known world, though also among the most dangerous.

## Whaling and the Hardiest Fishermen

The coastal peoples of western Aethelar have long harvested the cold waters of the Greyring for fish and whales. These fisheries sustain several thriving port towns whose harbours nestle among the cliffs of the western coast, towns that are smaller and more rugged than their counterparts on the eastern shore but no less proud of their maritime heritage. The catches of the Greyring are a vital source of food and trade for the western coast, and the fishermen who work its waters are regarded as among the most skilled and hardy in the known world, accustomed to cold, fog, and heavy seas that would deter lesser mariners.

Whaling is a particularly important activity along the Greyring coast, and the great whales that migrate through its waters are the source of oil, bone, and other materials traded inland and across the continent. The whalers of the western coast venture far from land in pursuit of their quarry, and their voyages are the closest thing to deep-water exploration that the Greyring has seen, for the whaling grounds lie beyond the continental shelf in the open ocean where few other vessels venture.

## Grey Stone, Narrow Streets, and the Peat Fires

The western coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) is a rugged and sparsely settled region defined by the ocean along its shores. High cliffs of grey stone rise from the water, their faces scoured by the storms and spray of centuries, and the harbours serving the coast are few and difficult of access, tucked into narrow inlets and sheltered coves concealed from the open sea. The communities inhabiting the coast are small and self-reliant, their lives oriented toward the ocean that sustains them, and their culture is distinct from that of the inland regions and the eastern shore.

The architecture of the western coast reflects the demands of the environment, with houses built of the same grey stone that forms the cliffs, their walls thick and their windows small, designed to withstand the wind and rain that the Greyring delivers throughout the year. The roofs are steep and weighted with stone to resist the gales, and the chimneys are broad to carry the smoke of the peat fires that warm the interiors through the long, damp winters. The streets of the coastal towns are narrow and winding, sheltered from the wind, and the buildings huddle together as if for warmth.

The people of the western coast are known for their hardiness and independence. They are fishermen and whalers first, and their relationship with the ocean is one of respect tempered by necessity, for the Greyring is generous in its bounty but unforgiving of error. The loss of a vessel is a common experience in the coastal communities, and the traditions of the western shore are marked by an awareness of the ocean's power that the peoples of the gentler eastern coast do not share. The folk songs of the western coast are full of the ocean, its moods and its dangers, and the ballads of lost vessels and their crews are the most treasured part of the coastal cultural heritage.

## Trenches, Mist, and the Strange Discoveries

Beyond the continental shelf, the ocean floor drops to extraordinary depths, and the few soundings that have been attempted suggest trenches as vast as the mountains of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) are tall. The depth of the Greyring is a source of speculation and unease among the peoples who border it, for the ocean's floor is as unknown as the surface of the land was before the first maps were drawn. Strange creatures are occasionally washed ashore or caught in deep-water nets, forms that no naturalist has been able to classify, and these rare discoveries have fuelled a rich tradition of legend and speculation about what may dwell in the lightless depths.

The peoples of the western coast regard the Greyring with a mixture of respect and wonder, and many a legend has been woven around its mists and its depths. Tales are told of islands seen in the fog that vanish when approached, of lights beneath the water that rise from the deep, and of great shapes that surface alongside ships and stare with eyes that are not the eyes of any whale. What may lie in the unexplored reaches of the Greyring remains a matter of speculation, and no reliable account exists of the ocean's western shores, if indeed any lie beyond the endless waters.
