## Grey Rings of Fog and the Western Sea

The Greyring Ocean lies to the west of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and to the south of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). It is the cooler of the two oceans, with steady currents flowing from the polar south toward the equator. Few established trade routes cross the Greyring, and much of its expanse remains unexplored. The name derives from the grey rings of fog that appear on the horizon, visible from the western cliffs of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) on all but the clearest days. The ocean's maximum depth is unknown; the deepest sounding on record, taken by a Nordhaven expedition in 4878, reached 2,400 fathoms without touching bottom.

## Cold Northward Flow and the Nutrient Upwelling

The cold currents of the Greyring flow northward from the southern polar regions, chilling the air above them and giving the western coasts of Aethelar a cooler and damper climate than the eastern shores. Fogs are common along the coast, particularly in the autumn and winter months, when the contrast between the cold water and the warmer air inland produces dense banks of mist that may persist for days. The peoples of the western coast have learned to navigate by sound and memory when the mist obscures the landmarks, using bells and horns placed along the shore to guide vessels safely to harbour. The fog is both a hazard and a shelter, for it has on many occasions concealed the western coast from fleets approaching with hostile intent.

The ocean's waters are rich with marine life, nourished by the upwelling of cold, nutrient-laden water from the deep. This upwelling occurs along the edge of the continental shelf, where the sea floor drops away and the currents draw water upward from the abyssal depths. The abundance of nutrients supports vast shoals of fish, which in turn attract larger predators, including the [greyring whale](/content/species/fauna/greyring-whale) and other species, and the fishing grounds of the Greyring are among the most productive in the known world, though also among the most dangerous.

## Whaling and the Hardiest Fishermen

The coastal peoples of western Aethelar have long harvested the cold waters of the Greyring for fish and whales. Several port towns serve the fisheries, their harbours located in sheltered inlets among the western cliffs. The catches are a vital source of food and trade for the western coast, and the fishermen who work these waters are regarded as among the most skilled in the known world, operating in cold, fog, and heavy seas for much of the year. The largest such port, Westmarch, processes over four hundred tons of fish annually.

Whaling is a particularly important activity along the Greyring coast, and the great whales that migrate through its waters are the source of oil, bone, and other materials traded inland and across the continent. The whalers of the western coast venture far from land in pursuit of their quarry, and their voyages are the closest thing to deep-water exploration that the Greyring has seen, for the whaling grounds lie beyond the continental shelf in the open ocean where few other vessels venture.

## Grey Stone, Narrow Streets, and the Peat Fires

The western coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) is a rugged and sparsely settled region. High cliffs of grey stone rise from the water, and the harbours are few and difficult of access, tucked into narrow inlets and sheltered coves. The communities are small and self-reliant, oriented toward the ocean, with a culture distinct from that of the inland regions and the eastern shore. Houses are built of the same grey stone as the cliffs, with thick walls and small windows to withstand the wind and rain. Roofs are steep and weighted with stone against the gales, and broad chimneys carry the smoke of peat fires that warm interiors through the long, damp winters. Streets in the coastal towns are narrow and winding, sheltered from the wind. The loss of a vessel to weather is a common experience, recorded in local ballads that form the principal oral tradition of the western shore. Vessels lost at sea with all hands are commemorated at an annual ceremony held in every port on the first day of winter, known as the Tolling of the Grey.

## Trenches, Mist, and the Strange Discoveries

Beyond the continental shelf, the ocean floor drops to depths comparable to the mountains of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The deepest sounding recorded, at 2,400 fathoms, failed to reach the bottom. Unidentified creatures are occasionally washed ashore or caught in deep-water nets, including a specimen of translucent-jellied mass weighing some three hundred pounds that was landed at Westmarch in 4901 and classified by the academy as incertae sedis. No expedition has returned with reliable intelligence of land beyond the Greyring's western horizon, despite three recorded attempts by Nordhaven mariners between 4800 and 4920. The last such expedition, led by Captain Eirik Steadfast in 4918, encountered continuous fog banks after twenty days' sailing and turned back with the loss of two crewmen to sickness.

## Economic Significance

The Greyring fisheries sustain approximately thirty communities along the western Aethelaran coast, with a combined population of roughly twelve thousand. The annual catch includes an estimated eight thousand tons of cod and herring, of which roughly half is salted and exported to interior markets. Whale oil production from the Greyring whaling fleet averages one thousand barrels per year, with the principal whaling ports of Westmarch, Blackbay, and Stormhead accounting for three-quarters of the total. The Western Whaling Accord of 4760, which established annual quotas and seasonal restrictions, is administered by a council of the twelve principal fishing towns that meets each spring at Westmarch. Revenue from Greyring fisheries and whaling constitutes approximately one-fifth of the western coast's total economic output, with the remainder coming from peat cutting, coastal grazing, and limited agriculture in the sheltered valleys. The Greyring also supports a modest trade in kelp, harvested from the shallow waters of the continental shelf and dried for use as fuel, animal feed, and a source of potash for glassmaking, with an annual harvest of approximately six hundred tons.
