## Overview

The Cape of the First Light is the easternmost headland of the principal continent, where the long curve of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/the-aethelar-heartwood) terminates in a high, rocky point that the chroniclers of the academies have named for the light that the dawn casts upon it. The cape rises several hundred feet above the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), and the cliffs drop almost sheer to the water below. The cape is bounded on the west by the forested slopes of the heartwood, on the south and east by the ocean, and on the north by a small bay where the fishing boats of the coast find shelter in foul weather.

The cape has been a landmark of the pilots of the warm coast since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities), and the pilots have used the cape as a mark for setting their course when sailing east along the coast. The chroniclers of the [Watch of the Inner Sea](/content/government/local/watch-of-the-inner-sea) note that the cape is the most distinctive of the headlands of the warm coast, and the cape has been the subject of careful survey by the pilots of the [Surveying of Heights and Distances](/content/belief/science/physical/the-surveying-of-heights-and-distances).

## The Headland

The headland is composed of the same ancient stone that forms the spine of the heartwood, and the stone is exposed in the cliffs that drop to the ocean. The cliffs are the home of the seabirds that breed upon the ledges in the spring, and the birds include the gulls and the cormorants that the chroniclers of the [Cataloguing of Flora and Fauna](/content/belief/science/physical/the-cataloguing-of-flora-and-fauna) have catalogued with care. The headland is covered with a tough vegetation of shrubs and small trees that the salt spray has shaped into low, wind-bent forms, and the vegetation is the foundation of the ecology of the cape.

The headland is the source of the small streams that run off the slopes to the bay, and the streams are the only source of fresh water upon the cape. The chronicler notes that the streams are small and unreliable, and the streams are sufficient for the small fishing village at the bay but not for any larger settlement. The streams have been the foundation of the limited habitation of the cape since the founding of the village.

## The Lighthouse

The lighthouse at the cape was erected by the council of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the early generations of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities), and the lighthouse has been maintained by the [Watch of the Inner Sea](/content/government/local/watch-of-the-inner-sea) ever since. The lighthouse stands upon the highest point of the headland, and the light is visible far out to sea in clear weather. The keeper of the lighthouse is a senior seaman of the Watch who has been relieved of duty afloat, and the keeper maintains the fire, trims the wicks at dusk, and signals the state of the tide by the colour of the light that he shows above the doorway of the lantern room.

The chronicler notes that the lighthouse has been the foundation of the safety of the navigation along the coast, and the lighthouse has been the refuge of many a pilot who has been caught upon the coast in time of storm. The lighthouse is one of the most important of the public works of the warm coast, and the lighthouse is held in high esteem by the pilots and the merchants of every city of the coast.

## The Bay

The bay lies upon the northern side of the headland, and the bay is sheltered from the prevailing winds by the cliffs that rise to the south. The bay is small and the water is deep, and the bay is the home of a small fishing village that has occupied the site since the founding of the cities. The village is the home of a small community of fishermen and their families, and the village is the centre of the limited habitation of the cape. The chronicler notes that the village is one of the smallest of the settlements of the warm coast, and the village is held in particular esteem by the pilots and the merchants of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar).

The bay is the principal anchorage of the cape, and the bay is used by the fishing boats of the village and by the larger vessels that call for shelter in foul weather. The bay is also the landing place for the small boats that serve the lighthouse, and the boats carry the supplies that the keeper requires. The chronicler notes that the bay has been the foundation of the contact between the cape and the towns of the coast, and the contact is the source of the prosperity of the village.

## The Wildlife

The cape is the home of a rich variety of wildlife, and the wildlife is the foundation of the ecology of the cape. The cliffs support the seabirds that breed upon the ledges in the spring, and the birds include the gulls and the cormorants that the chroniclers of the [Cataloguing of Flora and Fauna](/content/belief/science/physical/the-cataloguing-of-flora-and-fauna) have catalogued with care. The slopes support the smaller mammals that the birds of prey feed upon, and the streams support the fish that the fishermen of the village pursue. The ocean supports the larger fish that the fishermen take from the boats that they launch from the beach.

The wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies, and the scholars have recorded the patterns of the birds and the fish in their catalogues. The chronicler notes that the wildlife is the source of much of the food of the village, and the wildlife is the foundation of the careful management of the fishery that the elders of the village maintain.

## The Pilot's Mark

The cape has been a landmark of the pilots of the warm coast since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities), and the pilots have used the cape as a mark for setting their course when sailing east along the coast. The pilots read the bearing of the cape against the sun at noon, and the bearing is entered in the log alongside the time and the weather. The chronicler notes that the bearing has been the foundation of the navigation of the coast, and the navigation is the source of the prosperity of the merchants of the coast.

The chronicler of the [Watch of the Inner Sea](/content/government/local/watch-of-the-inner-sea) notes that the cape is also the mark that the pilots use when sailing west from the harbours of the coast, and the pilots set their course by the cape until the cape drops below the horizon. The chronicler observes that the cape is the most useful of the marks of the coast, and the cape is the foundation of the safety of the navigation of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean).

## Significance

The Cape of the First Light is one of the most distinctive of the headlands of the warm coast, and the cape is the foundation of the navigation along the eastern coast. The headland, the lighthouse, the bay, the village, and the streams are the inheritance that the cape has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the cape is held in particular memory by the pilots and the fishermen who make their living upon it, and the pilots and the fishermen mark the anniversaries of the founding of the lighthouse with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the warm coast, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every town of the coast.
