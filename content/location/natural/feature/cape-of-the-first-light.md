## The Easternmost Headland

The First-Light Cape is the easternmost headland of the principal continent, where the long curve of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) terminates in a high, rocky point named for the light that the dawn casts upon it. The cape rises several hundred feet above the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), with cliffs dropping almost sheer to the water below. It is bounded on the west by the forested slopes of the heartwood, on the south and east by the ocean, and on the north by a small bay where the fishing boats of the coast find shelter in foul weather.

The cape has been a landmark for the pilots of the warm coast since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era), used as a mark for setting course when sailing east along the coast. The [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch) notes the cape as the noted headland of the warm coast for navigation, and it has been the subject of careful survey by the pilots of the Distance-Craft.

## Ancient Stone and the Wind-Bent Shrubs

The headland is composed of the same ancient stone that forms the spine of the heartwood, exposed in the cliffs that drop to the ocean. The cliffs are the home of seabirds that breed upon the ledges in the spring, including the gulls and cormorants that the Bestiaries and Herbals have catalogued with care. The headland is covered with a tough vegetation of shrubs and small trees that the salt spray has shaped into low, wind-bent forms, forming the foundation of the ecology of the cape.

Small streams run off the slopes to the bay, providing the only source of fresh water upon the cape. The streams are small and unreliable, sufficient for the small fishing village at the bay but not for any larger settlement. They have been the foundation of the limited habitation of the cape since the founding of the village.

## The Beacon of Khalisar's Watch

The lighthouse at the cape was erected by the council of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the early generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace) and has been maintained by the [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch) ever since. It stands upon the highest point of the headland, and the light is visible far out to sea in clear weather. The keeper is a senior seaman of the Watch relieved of duty afloat, who maintains the fire, trims the wicks at dusk, and signals the state of the tide by the colour of the light shown above the doorway of the lantern room.

The lighthouse has been the foundation of the safety of navigation along the coast and a navigation aid for vessels caught in coastal storms. It is one of the principal [public works](/content/economy/public-works) of the warm coast, recorded in 14 maritime charters issued between the founding era and the Long Peace.

## Sheltered Anchorage and the Fishing Village

The bay lies upon the northern side of the headland, sheltered from the prevailing winds by the cliffs that rise to the south. The bay is small and the water is deep, and it is the home of a small fishing village that has occupied the site since the founding of the cities. The village is the centre of the limited habitation of the cape, home to a small community of fishermen and their families. It is one of the smallest settlements of the warm coast and appears in the harbour records of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar).

The bay is the principal anchorage of the cape, used by the fishing boats of the village and by larger vessels that call for shelter in foul weather. It is also the landing place for the small boats that serve the lighthouse, carrying the supplies that the keeper requires. The bay has been the foundation of contact between the cape and the towns of the coast, and this contact is the source of the prosperity of the village.

## Seabirds, Mammals, and the Careful Fishery

The cape supports a rich variety of wildlife that forms the foundation of its ecology. The cliffs support seabirds that breed upon the ledges in the spring, including the gulls and cormorants that the Bestiaries and Herbals have catalogued. The slopes support smaller mammals that the [Peregrine Falcon](/content/species/fauna/peregrine-falcon) feeds upon, and the streams support fish that the fishermen of the village pursue. The ocean supports larger fish taken from boats launched from the beach.

The wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies, who have recorded the patterns of the birds and fish in their catalogues. The wildlife is the source of much of the food of the village and the foundation of the careful management of the fishery that the elders of the village maintain.

## Bearing at Noon and the Setting of Course

The cape has been a landmark of the pilots of the warm coast since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era), used as a mark for setting course when sailing east along the coast. The pilots read the bearing of the cape against the sun at noon, and the bearing is entered in the log alongside the time and the weather. This bearing has been the foundation of the navigation of the coast, and the navigation is the source of the prosperity of the merchants of the coast.

The cape is also the mark that pilots use when sailing west from the harbours of the coast, setting their course by the cape until it drops below the horizon. The cape is one of the principal marks of the coast and the foundation of the safety of navigation on the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean).

## Coastal Navigation and Village Economy

The cape extends approximately 400 yards into the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and rises 340 feet above the waterline at its highest point. The lighthouse beacon is visible from 14 nautical miles in clear weather and from approximately 7 nautical miles in moderate fog. The fishing village supports a permanent population of 78, with an additional 12 seasonal workers during the autumn catch. Annual lighthouse maintenance costs the [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch) 180 silver weights, paid from harbour dues collected at [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar). The cape appears in 23 known pilot charts dating from the early generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace) to the present.
