## Sheltered Trough of the Greywash Headwaters

The Vintner's Vale lies between the southern flank of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and the northern reach of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), where the Greywash and its tributaries descend through a broad, sheltered trough that opens gradually toward the warm coast. The vale takes its name from the vineyards and orchards that the valley floor and lower slopes support, and it has been the principal source of the wines of the cold north since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The vale is bounded on the north by the forests of the uplands, on the south by the foothills of the [Stonespire](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire), on the east by the headwaters of the Greywash, and on the west by the gorge through which the river descends to the plains.

The vale covers several days of travel from end to end and is home to a string of small towns and villages that have grown up along the river and the principal road. The towns and villages are connected by the paved road that the [Laying the Stone Roads](/content/history/laying-the-stone-roads) constructed, one of the most reliable roads of the cold north. The vale is held in particular esteem by the merchants of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm), who travel the road in every season to purchase the wines and fruits that the vale produces.

## Sheltered Between Uplands and Foothills

The vale enjoys a milder climate than the surrounding uplands, for the southern flank of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) shelters it from the worst of the northern winds, and the foothills of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) shelter it from southern storms. Winters are cold but rarely severe, and summers are long and warm. The growing season is sufficient for the grape and for the stone fruits that the orchards produce, and the vale has been famous for the quality of its wines and fruits since the founding of the towns. The soil of the valley slopes is well-drained and rich in minerals derived from the surrounding uplands, giving the wine grapes their distinctive character.

Occasional floods occur when the snow of the Stonespire melts rapidly in spring, causing damage that requires extensive labour to repair. These floods have shaped the careful management of the river that the [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact) established. The flood waters also deposit fresh silt on the valley floor, renewing the fertility of the soil that supports the orchards.

## Dark Grapes and the Vintner-Houses

The vineyards of the vale are planted upon the lower slopes of the hills that flank the valley floor, where the soil is well-drained and the sun reaches the vines for the greater part of the day. The principal grape is a dark-skinned variety that the founders of the towns brought from the warm coast in the generations of the founding, adapted to the cooler climate by generations of careful cultivation. The wine that the grape produces is deep-coloured, full-bodied, and well-suited to the long winters of the cold north. The vineyards are terraced on the steeper slopes, with dry-stone walls that hold the soil and absorb heat during the day, releasing it to the vines during the cooler nights.

The vineyards are tended by the vintner-houses of the towns, organised under the [Vintners and Cellarers](/content/government/local/vintners-and-cellarers). The Council maintains the standards of the wine, and these standards are the foundation of the reputation of the wines of the vale.

## Apple, Pear, Plum, and Cherry

The orchards of the vale are planted upon the valley floor and lower slopes, where the soil is deep and water plentiful. The principal fruits are apple, pear, plum, and cherry, staples of the diet of the towns and villages of the vale. The orchards are tended by farming households of the towns, who have cultivated the fruits for several generations. The trees are pruned and trained to maximise exposure to the sun, and the fruit is harvested by hand in the late summer and early autumn. The harvested fruit is sorted for quality, with the best specimens sent fresh to the markets of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) and the remainder dried, preserved, or pressed into juice that is fermented into a mild cider popular in the cold north.

Occasional failure of the blossom occurs when a late frost kills the flowers before they can set fruit. Such failures are rare, and the orchards recover quickly, a measure of the resilience of the farming households of the vale. In years of abundant blossom, the fruit set is thinned by hand to ensure that the remaining fruit grows to full size, a labour-intensive process that the households undertake with care.

## Magistrates, Markets, and the Paved Road

The towns of the vale are strung along the river and the principal road, each serving as the centre of a small district of vineyards and orchards. The principal towns are Greenwell at the head of the vale, Vintner's Bridge at the centre, and Orchardmouth at the western end. Each town has a small magistracy, temple, market, and garrison.

The towns are governed by magistrates in consultation with the vintner-houses and farming households. The magistrates maintain the roads, bridges, irrigation works, and public order of the vale. They are appointed by the council of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) and hold office at the pleasure of the council. The magistrates mark the anniversaries of the founding with ceremonies attended by the towns and villages.

## Deer, Trout, and the Hedgerow Songbirds

The vale is home to a variety of wildlife adapted to the milder climate of the sheltered trough. The hills support deer and wild pig, and the woods support smaller game that hunters of the towns pursue in season. The river supports trout and grayling, and fishermen of the towns are licensed by the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) to fish the waters of the river and its tributaries. The hedgerows that line the fields between the vineyards and orchards provide nesting sites for songbirds and cover for small mammals, and they are maintained by the farming households as a source of timber for fuel and repair. The presence of certain birds, such as the redstart and the garden warbler, is taken as a sign that the vale's ecosystem remains in good health.

## Wine, Fruit, and the Esteem of Vassholm

The Vintner's Vale is one of the most productive and esteemed regions of the cold north, forming the foundation of the wine trade and fruit supply of the region for several centuries. The vineyards, orchards, towns, and road are the inheritance of the present age. Merchants of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) travel the road of the vale in every season to purchase the wines and fruits that the vale produces, and the vale is held in high esteem by every magistrate and merchant of the cold north.
