## High Cliffs, Deep Fjords, and the Narrow Plains

The [Stormfall](/content/location/natural/continent/stormfall) Coast is the rugged southern shoreline of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) that faces the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea), a region of high cliffs, deep fjords, and narrow coastal plains shaped by the relentless action of wind and water over geological ages. The coast runs for several hundred miles from the western headlands that jut into the open waters of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) to the eastern approaches of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), presenting a face of stone and water that has defied easy settlement while rewarding those who have learned its ways with shelter, food, and passage. The settlements of the coast are small and resilient, oriented toward the sea for their livelihood and connection to the wider world, and the character of the region is shaped by the storms that give it its name.

## Ancient Granite, Glacial Fjords, and the Fertile Shelf

The underlying geology of the Stormfall Coast is ancient, the rock being among the oldest exposed in the known world, and the landforms are the product of glacial carving and marine erosion acting upon a foundation of hard granite and basalt. The cliffs that define the coast rise in many places to a height of several hundred feet, their faces sheer and their summits bare, broken at intervals by the mouths of fjords that penetrate deep into the interior of Morvind. The fjords themselves are long and narrow, their sides steep and their waters deep, extending inland for distances that range from a few miles to more than twenty, their heads marked by gravel deltas where mountain streams enter the sea.

The coast is not uniformly steep. In the lee of headlands and at the heads of fjords occur narrow flats where land meets the sea in a gentler fashion. These flats are composed of sediment carried down from the interior by rivers and glacial melt, and they support the greater part of the settlement and agriculture of the region, meagre though these are by the standards of more temperate shores. The beaches are of dark sand and rounded cobbles, narrow and seldom extending more than a few paces before the ground rises into rocky slopes.

The offshore waters are shallow along the continental shelf, and the sea floor is composed of rock and gravel rather than fine sediment. The shelf is rich in marine life, for the mixing of cold waters of the Greyring with warmer currents of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) creates conditions of great fertility, and the waters of the Stormfall Sea are among the most productive fishing grounds in the known world. The seabed is marked by ridges and troughs that are remains of ancient glacial valleys, channeling currents and concentrating nutrients that sustain the web of marine life.

## Long Winters, Brief Summers, and the Dominant Fog

The climate of the Stormfall Coast is cold and maritime, moderated by the waters of the Stormfall Sea but subject to the violent weather that gives the region its name. Winters are long and dark, with the sun climbing only a few degrees above the horizon even at midday, and the storms that sweep across the sea during the winter months are the defining feature of the coastal climate. These storms arrive with little warning, bringing winds that can tear the roof from a well-built house and seas that can dash a ship against the cliffs within minutes.

Summer is brief but intense, with long days and a sun that never fully sets in the northernmost reaches. The storms of winter give way to periods of calm and fog, and the waters grow calm enough for boats to venture out in safety. Fog that rolls in from the sea during summer is frequent and dense, reducing visibility to a few paces and rendering navigation a matter of sound and memory rather than sight.

Precipitation is abundant, falling as rain in summer and as snow in winter, and the moisture supports lush vegetation despite the harshness of the climate. Snow that falls upon the higher slopes of the coastal mountains accumulates through winter and feeds streams that run clear and cold through summer, and the meltwater that pours down cliffs and into fjords is a constant presence in the life of the coast.

## Windshrub, Mountain Birch, and the Dwarf Fir

The plant life of the Stormfall Coast is adapted to the twin pressures of salt-laden wind and shallow, rocky soil, and the vegetation that thrives here is low-growing and tough. The coastal heath that covers the cliff tops and upper slopes of the fjords is composed of dwarf shrubs, mosses, and lichens, with a scattering of hardy grasses that flower in the brief summer. The heath is dominated by the windshrub, a woody plant that grows no higher than a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) knee and spreads by underground runners, forming mats of vegetation that hold the thin soil against wind and rain. The leaves of the windshrub are small and leathery, covered in a waxy coating that reduces water loss.

In the shelter of the fjords, where cliffs provide protection from the worst of the wind, vegetation becomes more substantial. Stunted trees of mountain birch and wind-bent pine find a foothold in crevices and on slopes, their forms shaped by the prevailing wind into permanent postures of submission to the elements. These trees seldom exceed the height of a [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) even in the most sheltered locations, and their wood is dense and slow-grown, prized for its resistance to rot.

The lower slopes of the coastal mountains support a denser forest of coniferous trees, including black spruce and Morvind fir, the latter being one of the hardiest of all timber trees. These forests are dark and quiet, their floors carpeted with needles and moss, marking the transition between the open coast and the closed interior of the continent.

## Kelp, Cod, and the Coastal Whale

The waters of the Stormfall Coast teem with life, and the intertidal zone between high and low tide marks is one of the most productive habitats in the region. The rocks of the shore are covered in a thick coating of seaweed, including the long-stemmed kelp that grows in dense beds in shallow waters and the leathery bladderweed that covers exposed rocks at low tide. The seaweed beds are nursery grounds for a host of marine animals, including the shore crab, the spiny urchin, and many species of mollusc.

The fish of the Stormfall Sea are abundant and diverse, forming the foundation of the coastal economy. Herring runs in vast shoals during the summer months, its passage through the fjords marked by flocks of seabirds that gather to feed upon it, and the cod that preys upon the herring and smaller fish is the most important catch for fishing communities. The cod of the Stormfall Sea are large, often exceeding the length of a human arm, and their flesh is dense and white, salted and dried for export to the markets of the south.

The larger marine animals include the grey seal, which hauls out upon the rocky shores of the outer coast in colonies numbering in the hundreds, and the coastal whale, a species of baleen whale that passes through the Stormfall Sea on its annual migration. Whales are hunted by coastal communities with skill refined over centuries, and the oil, bone, and meat that they provide are essential resources for a region where the land offers little wealth.

## Guillemot, Kittiwake, and the Great Auk

The cliffs of the Stormfall Coast are among the most important seabird nesting sites in the known world, and the bird colonies that occupy the ledges and crevices of the cliff faces are a spectacle that defines the character of the coast. The common guillemot and the black-legged kittiwake nest in dense colonies on narrow ledges, their numbers so great that the sound of their calls can be heard from a distance of miles. The great auk, a flightless bird that nests on the more sheltered islands, is hunted for its meat and feathers, and its populations are carefully managed to ensure continued abundance.

The birds feed upon the fish of offshore waters, and their movements are a reliable guide to the location of shoals that human fishers seek. The relationship between the seabirds and coastal communities is one of mutual observation and shared dependence on the fertility of the sea, and the arrival of the birds in spring signals the beginning of the fishing season, just as their departure in autumn marks its end.

## Stone Villages and the Nordhaven Connection

The [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) settlements of the Stormfall Coast are small and hardy, oriented toward the sea and sustained by the resources of the coast and waters beyond. The villages are built of stone and timber, their roofs weighted against the wind and their walls thick against the cold, clustering at the heads of fjords and on sheltered flats where land offers room enough for a few fields and a harbour. The [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) unites the principal ports and many lesser communities, coordinating the fishing, coastal defence, and trade that sustain the population of the region.

The people of the coast are skilled mariners, and their boats are built to withstand the conditions of the Stormfall Sea. The coastal knarr, a double-ended vessel of clinker construction, is the most common type, used for both fishing and trade, capable of carrying a crew of a dozen [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and a substantial cargo of dried fish, whale oil, and seal skins. The larger longships used by warriors and explorers are among the finest vessels of their kind in the known world.

The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the Morvind interior maintain trade routes down the rivers that descend to the coast, exchanging the metal goods of the mountains for the fish, oil, and salt of the coastal communities. The relationship between the coast and the interior is one of complementarity, for each produces what the other cannot.

## Gateway of Morvind's Wealth

The Stormfall Coast is the gateway through which the wealth of Morvind reaches the wider world, for the ores and crafted goods of the dwarven holds of the interior must pass through the coastal ports on their way to the markets of the south and east. The coast is also a bastion of hardy and self-reliant communities whose traditions of seamanship and resilience are among the oldest in the known world. The storms that give the coast its name remain the defining feature of life in this region, and the peoples who dwell here have shaped a way of life inseparable from the wind, the water, and the rock of their unforgiving but bountiful shore.
