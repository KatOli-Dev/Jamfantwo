## Overview

East of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) and beyond the reach of the rivers that flow west into the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale), the land opens into a broad, sun-baked grassland that the dwellers of the inland valleys call the Sunfire Steppe. The steppe takes its name from the harsh, direct light of the summer sun, and the light is the most immediate impression that a traveller from the wooded valleys receives upon crossing the mountains. The steppe stretches from the eastern foothills of the [Stonespire](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire) to the distant horizon, broken only by the shallow valleys of the rivers that descend from the high interior and by the salt flats that mark the lowest depressions.

The steppe is the home of a sparse population of pastoralists who follow their flocks across the grasslands in a seasonal pattern that has changed little since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The pastoralists live in portable tents of felt and hide, and the pastoralists move with the seasons between the higher ground of the foothills in the summer and the lower ground of the rivers and the salt flats in the winter. The chronicler notes that the steppe is one of the least settled of the landscapes of the three continents, and the steppe is held in particular esteem by the pastoralists who make their living upon it.

## Climate

The steppe enjoys a continental climate that is far harsher than the climate of the coastal regions. The summers are long and hot, and the heat is unbroken by the cooling influence of the sea. The winters are long and bitterly cold, and the cold is unbroken by the warming influence of the ocean. The rainfall is sparse and falls mostly in the spring and the early summer, and the rainfall is sufficient for the grasses that cover the surface of the steppe but not for the trees that the coastal regions support. The chronicler notes that the climate is the foundation of the pastoral economy of the steppe, and the climate is the reason that the pastoralists have not settled in permanent villages.

The steppe is subject to the occasional drought when the spring rains fail, and the droughts are the source of much of the hardship of the pastoralists. The chronicler notes that the droughts are recorded in the chronicles of the pastoralists as exceptional events, and the chronicles describe the loss of the flocks and the migration of the households that the droughts inflict. The chronicler observes that the droughts have been the foundation of the careful management of the flocks that the elders of the pastoralists maintain.

## The Grasslands

The grasslands of the steppe are the foundation of the pastoral economy, and the grasslands support the flocks of sheep and goats that the pastoralists tend. The principal grasses are tough species that the spring rains bring up rapidly and that the summer heat dries into a golden brown that persists through the winter. The grasses regenerate quickly after the flocks have grazed them, and the regeneration is the foundation of the seasonal pattern that the pastoralists follow.

The grasslands also support a population of smaller creatures that is the foundation of the ecology of the steppe. The principal creatures are the burrowing rodents that the birds of prey feed upon, and the birds of prey that the chroniclers of the [Bestiaries and Herbals](/content/belief/science/physical/bestiaries-and-herbals) have catalogued with care. The grasslands also support the wild horses that the pastoralists catch and train for riding, and the horses are the foundation of the mobility that the pastoralists require.

## The Rivers

The rivers that descend from the high interior of the continent cross the steppe in shallow valleys that the pastoralists use as their winter grounds. The rivers are small and unreliable, and the rivers freeze in the winter and dwindle in the summer. The rivers support a narrow strip of trees and shrubs that the pastoralists use for fuel and for building, and the trees are the only source of timber upon the steppe. The chronicler notes that the rivers are the foundation of the winter economy of the pastoralists, and the rivers are the reason that the pastoralists concentrate their flocks along the valleys in the colder months.

The rivers are also the source of the salt that the pastoralists trade with the towns of the inland valleys. The salt is collected from the shallow pools that form in the lowest depressions of the steppe, and the salt is carried by packhorse to the markets of the towns. The chronicler notes that the salt trade has been the foundation of the contact between the pastoralists and the towns, and the contact is the source of the prosperity of both parties.

## The Pastoralists

The pastoralists of the steppe are organised into clans that follow the seasonal pattern of the grasslands, and the clans are governed by the elders of the households in consultation with the headmen of the clans. The elders maintain the flocks, the pastures, the routes, and the public order of the clans. The elders are elected by the households for a term of five years, and the elders hold office at the pleasure of the households. The chronicler notes that the elders are held in high esteem by the clans, and the elders mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies.

The pastoralists trade with the towns of the inland valleys, and the trade has been the foundation of the contact between the steppe and the settled regions. The pastoralists exchange the wool, the cheese, and the salt of the steppe for the grain, the tools, and the cloth that the towns produce. The chronicler notes that the trade has been conducted in peace for several centuries, and the peace is the foundation of the prosperity of both parties.

## The Salt Flats

The salt flats of the steppe mark the lowest depressions of the surface, and the salt flats are the source of the salt that the pastoralists trade with the towns. The salt is collected from the shallow pools that form in the flats after the spring rains, and the salt is dried in the sun and packed into sacks for transport. The salt is of high quality, and the salt has been the foundation of the prosperity of the pastoralists who control the principal flats.

The flats are bounded by the surrounding steppe, and the flats are crossed by the routes that the pastoralists follow in their seasonal migrations. The flats are the home of a sparse population of creatures that are adapted to the salt, and the creatures include the wading birds that the chroniclers of the [Bestiaries and Herbals](/content/belief/science/physical/bestiaries-and-herbals) have catalogued with care. The chronicler notes that the flats are one of the most distinctive of the landscapes of the steppe, and the flats are held in particular esteem by the pastoralists who make their living upon them.

## Significance

The Sunfire Steppe is one of the least settled of the landscapes of the three continents, and the steppe is the foundation of the pastoral economy that the towns of the inland valleys rely upon for wool, cheese, and salt. The grasslands, the rivers, the salt flats, the pastoralists, and the trade routes are the inheritance that the steppe has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the steppe is held in particular memory by the pastoralists who make their living upon it, and the pastoralists mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the steppe, and the ceremonies are attended by the elders and the principal pastoralists of every clan of the grasslands.
