## Overview

Brackenshield Wood lies upon the eastern flank of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands), where the highlands descend in a long ridge toward the headwaters of the rivers that flow south and west into the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale). The wood takes its name from the bracken that covers the forest floor in summer and is one of the smaller but better-tended forests of the cold north. It is bounded on the west by the open ground of the uplands, on the east by the steeper slopes that fall to the rivers, on the north by the higher forest of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood), and on the south by the cleared ground of farms that the vintner-houses have established along the river. The wood occupies a transitional zone between the open uplands and the cultivated lowlands, and its ecology reflects this position at the meeting of two landscapes.

The wood is held under the care of the [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship), which has managed its bounds, the seasons of the chase, and the rules of cutting since the founding of the villages at its edge. The wood is smaller than the Heartwood to the north but more productive, providing much of the timber and game that the towns of the lower vale require. The vintner-houses of the vale have supported the Fellowship in the careful management of the wood for several centuries, recognising that a well-tended wood yields more timber and game over time than one exploited without restraint.

## Climate

The wood enjoys a climate sheltered by the surrounding uplands, milder than the higher forest to the north. Summers are warm and winters cold but rarely severe, and the growing season is sufficient for the timber trees that the Fellowship manages. The wood receives steady rainfall from the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) throughout much of the year, supplemented by snow-melt that descends from the uplands in spring. The combination of shelter and reliable moisture allows the trees to grow to a size that is unusual for woodlands at this latitude.

Occasional storms bring down weaker trees and damage the understorey, requiring labour from the foresters to clear fallen trees and repair damage. These storms have reinforced the careful management of the bounds that the Fellowship maintains, as the gaps created by fallen trees must be managed to prevent erosion on the steeper slopes.

## The Trees

The principal trees of the wood are oak, beech, and ash, which grow to considerable size upon the deeper soils of the lower slopes. These trees are the foundation of the timber trade that the Fellowship supports, providing timber for building and fuel for the towns of the lower vale. The foresters cut mature trees in a rotation established by the Fellowship, maintaining a sustainable timber supply that has contributed to the prosperity of the towns of the vale. Oak is prized for its strength and durability, beech for its straight grain and workability, and ash for its flexibility and resistance to shock.

The forest floor supports the bracken that gives the wood its name, home to smaller game that hunters pursue in season. The trees support nesting birds that breed in the wood in summer, including songbirds that scholars of the [Bestiaries and Herbals](/content/belief/science/physical/bestiaries-and-herbals) have catalogued with care. The understorey includes hazel and hawthorn, whose nuts and berries provide food for wildlife through the autumn and winter months.

## The Wildlife

The wood is home to a rich variety of wildlife that the Fellowship regulates through the chase. The principal game are deer, wild pig, and smaller mammals hunted in season. The chase opens in autumn and closes in spring, the seasons set by the chief warden after consultation with the sworn fellows of the villages at the edge of the wood. The seasons represent a careful compromise between the abundance of the wood and the needs of the villages, ensuring that breeding populations are not depleted.

The forest floor supports insects and small mammals that birds feed upon, and the streams that run through the wood support fish that villagers pursue. The wildlife has been studied extensively by scholars of the academies, who have recorded the patterns of birds and mammals in their catalogues. The presence of certain species, such as the wood warbler and the spotted woodpecker, is taken as an indicator of the health of the woodland.

## The Bounds

The bounds of the wood are marked by ancient stones set by the founders of the Fellowship, running from the ford of the eastern river in the north to the clearing of Greenwell in the south. The bounds enclose the whole of the wood and the fringes reserved to the chase. The chief warden inspects the bounds once each year in the company of the fellows of the villages along the boundary. The stones are a source of local pride and their maintenance is considered a matter of honour among the foresters.

The bounds are protected by regulations of the Fellowship, which has the power to fine any person who cuts timber or sets a snare within the bounds without a licence from the chief warden. The bounds have been the subject of long dispute between the Fellowship and the small lords who hold land within or adjacent to the wood, adjudicated by the magistrates of the principal towns. The Fellowship has generally prevailed, for the bounds are ancient and the accord is clear.

## The Villages

The villages at the edge of the wood are home to small communities of foresters and their families, each serving as the centre of a small district of the bounds. The villages are connected by tracks that run along the edge of the wood, the principal means of communication between the villages and the towns of the vale.

The villages are governed by elders of the households in consultation with the chief warden of the Fellowship. The elders maintain the tracks, bounds, gear, and public order of the villages. They are elected by the households for a term of three years and hold office at the pleasure of the households. The elders mark the anniversaries of the founding with ceremonies attended by the chief warden and the sworn fellows of every village of the bounds.

## Significance

Brackenshield Wood is one of the smaller but better-tended forests of the cold north, forming the foundation of the timber supply and the chase that the towns of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) require. Its bounds, trees, wildlife, villages, and tracks are the inheritance of the present age. The ceremonies marking the founding are among the most important public observances of the vale, attended by the chief warden and the sworn fellows of every village of the bounds.
