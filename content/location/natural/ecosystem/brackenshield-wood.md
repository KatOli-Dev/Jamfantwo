## Overview

Brackenshield Wood lies upon the eastern flank of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/the-morvind-uplands), where the highlands descend in a long ridge toward the headwaters of the rivers that flow south and west into the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale). The wood takes its name from the bracken that covers the forest floor in summer, and the wood is one of the smaller but better-tended of the forests of the cold north. The wood is bounded on the west by the open ground of the uplands, on the east by the steeper slopes that fall to the rivers, on the north by the higher forest of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/the-aethelar-heartwood), and on the south by the cleared ground of the farms that the vintner-houses have established along the river.

The wood is held under the care of the [Forest Fellowship of the Heartwood](/content/government/local/forest-fellowship-of-the-heartwood), and the Fellowship has managed the bounds of the wood, the seasons of the chase, and the rules of the cutting since the founding of the villages at the edge of the wood. The wood is smaller than the Heartwood to the north, but the wood is more productive, and the wood has been the source of much of the timber and the game that the towns of the lower vale require. The chronicler notes that the wood is held in particular esteem by the vintner-houses of the vale, and the vintner-houses have supported the Fellowship in the careful management of the wood for several centuries.

## Climate

The wood enjoys a climate that is sheltered by the surrounding uplands, and the wood is milder than the higher forest to the north. The summers are warm and the winters are cold but rarely severe, and the growing season is sufficient for the timber trees that the Fellowship manages. The wood is subject to the steady rainfall that the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) brings throughout much of the year, and the rainfall is supplemented by the snow-melt that descends from the uplands in the spring.

The wood is subject to the occasional storm that brings down the weaker trees and damages the understorey, and the storms are the source of much of the labour that the foresters must undertake. The chronicler notes that the storms are recorded in the chronicles of the Fellowship as exceptional events, and the chronicles describe the labour that the foresters must undertake to clear the fallen trees and to repair the damage. The chronicler observes that the storms have been the foundation of the careful management of the bounds that the Fellowship maintains.

## The Trees

The principal trees of the wood are oak, beech, and ash, and the trees grow to a considerable size upon the deeper soils of the lower slopes. The trees are the foundation of the timber trade that the Fellowship supports, and the trees are the source of the timber that the towns of the lower vale require for building and for fuel. The trees are managed by the foresters of the Fellowship, and the foresters cut the mature trees in a rotation that the Fellowship has established. The chronicler notes that the rotation has been the foundation of the careful management of the timber supply, and the timber supply has been the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of the vale.

The trees support a population of smaller plants and animals that is the foundation of the ecology of the wood. The forest floor supports the bracken that gives the wood its name, and the bracken is the home of the smaller game that the hunters of the villages pursue in the season. The trees support the nesting birds that breed in the wood in the summer, and the birds include the songbirds that the chroniclers of the [Cataloguing of Flora and Fauna](/content/belief/science/physical/the-cataloguing-of-flora-and-fauna) have catalogued with care.

## The Wildlife

The wood is the home of a rich variety of wildlife, and the wildlife is the foundation of the chase that the Fellowship regulates. The principal game are the deer, the wild pig, and the smaller mammals that the hunters pursue in the season. The chase is opened in the autumn and is closed in the spring, and the seasons are set by the chief warden after consultation with the sworn fellows of the villages at the edge of the wood. The chronicler notes that the seasons are a careful compromise between the abundance of the wood and the needs of the villages, and the seasons have been the subject of long debate in the Fellowship.

The wildlife also includes the smaller creatures that are the foundation of the ecology of the wood. The forest floor supports the insects and the small mammals that the birds feed upon, and the streams that run through the wood support the fish that the fishermen of the villages pursue. The chronicler notes that the wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies, and the scholars have recorded the patterns of the birds and the mammals in their catalogues.

## The Bounds

The bounds of the wood are marked by the ancient stones that the founders of the Fellowship set in the generations of the founding, and the bounds run from the ford of the eastern river in the north to the clearing of Greenwell in the south. The bounds enclose the whole of the wood and the fringes that the Fellowship has reserved to the chase. The chief warden inspects the bounds once in each year, and the inspection is conducted in the company of the fellows of the villages along the bound. The chronicler notes that the bounds have been the foundation of the careful management of the wood, and the bounds are the measure of the public spirit of the Fellowship.

The bounds are protected by the regulations of the Fellowship, and the Fellowship has the power to fine any person who cuts timber or sets a snare within the bounds without the licence of the chief warden. The chronicler notes that the bounds have been the subject of long dispute between the Fellowship and the small lords who hold land within or adjacent to the wood, and the disputes have been adjudicated by the magistrates of the principal towns. The Fellowship has generally prevailed in the disputes, for the bounds are ancient and the accord is clear.

## The Villages

The villages at the edge of the wood are the home of a small community of foresters and their families, and each village is the centre of a small district of the bounds. The villages are connected by the tracks that run along the edge of the wood, and the tracks are the principal means of communication between the villages and the towns of the vale. The chronicler notes that the villages are quiet and functional, and the villages are the foundation of the public order of the bounds.

The villages are governed by the elders of the households in consultation with the chief warden of the Fellowship, and the elders maintain the tracks, the bounds, the gear, and the public order of the villages. The elders are elected by the households for a term of three years, and the elders hold office at the pleasure of the households. The chronicler notes that the elders are held in high esteem by the villages, and the elders mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies.

## Significance

Brackenshield Wood is one of the smaller but better-tended of the forests of the cold north, and the wood is the foundation of the timber supply and the chase that the towns of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) require. The bounds, the trees, the wildlife, the villages, and the tracks are the inheritance that the wood has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the wood is held in particular memory by the foresters and the hunters who make their living upon it, and the foresters and the hunters mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the vale, and the ceremonies are attended by the chief warden and the sworn fellows of every village of the bounds.
