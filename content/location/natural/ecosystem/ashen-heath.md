## Overview

The Ashen Heath stretches along the western coast of the principal continent, between the southern reach of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and the northern mouth of the [Strait of Velden](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden). The heath takes its name from the grey, ash-like soil that covers much of its surface, and the soil is the product of the volcanic activity that shaped the coast in the deep past. The heath is a broad, open landscape of low hills and shallow valleys, covered with a tough vegetation of heather, gorse, and bracken that the soil supports in spite of its thinness. The heath is bounded on the east by the higher ground of the uplands, and on the west by the cliffs that drop to the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean).

The heath has been sparsely populated since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities), and the population is concentrated in a string of small fishing villages along the coast and a smaller number of sheep farms upon the higher ground of the interior. The villages and the farms are connected by a network of unpaved tracks that the magistrates of the towns of the coast maintain, and the tracks are suitable for the small carts that the farmers use but not for the larger vehicles that the paved roads of the trade routes carry. The chronicler notes that the heath is one of the least developed of the regions of the cold north, and the heath is held in particular esteem by the fishermen and the shepherds who make their living upon it.

## Climate

The heath is subject to a harsh maritime climate, with strong winds from the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) throughout much of the year, frequent rain, and a long season of fog that the locals call the grey blanket. The winters are mild but wet, and the summers are cool and often overcast. The growing season is short, and the heath supports only the toughest of the crops. The chronicler notes that the climate is the foundation of the sparse population of the heath, and the climate is the reason that the magistrates of the towns of the coast have not pressed for the development of the interior.

The heath is subject to the occasional storm that drives the spray of the ocean across the cliffs and into the interior, and the storms are the source of much of the hardship of the fishing villages. The chronicler notes that the storms are recorded in the chronicles of the villages as exceptional events, and the chronicles describe the damage that the storms inflict upon the boats and the quays and the labour that the villagers must undertake to repair the damage. The chronicler observes that the storms have been the foundation of the careful management of the boats and the gear that the [Standing Watch of the Northern Roads](/content/government/local/standing-watch-of-the-northern-roads) maintains along the coast.

## The Soil

The soil of the heath is grey and ashen, and the soil is the product of the volcanic activity that shaped the coast in the deep past. The soil is thin and well-drained, and the soil is poor in the nutrients that the crops require. The soil supports the heather, the gorse, and the bracken that cover the surface of the heath, and the soil supports the tough grasses that the sheep feed upon. The soil does not support the cultivation of the grain or the fruits that the inland valleys produce, and the soil has been the foundation of the pastoral economy of the heath since the founding of the villages.

The chronicler notes that the soil has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the [Grammar of Stones and Soils](/content/belief/science/physical/grammar-of-stones-and-soils), and the scholars have concluded that the soil is not capable of improvement by the ordinary methods of cultivation. The scholars have recommended that the heath be reserved to the pastoral economy that the soil supports, and the recommendation has been accepted by the magistrates of the towns of the coast. The chronicler observes that the recommendation is the foundation of the public order of the heath.

## The Vegetation

The vegetation of the heath is dominated by the heather, the gorse, and the bracken that cover the surface of the soil, and the vegetation is the foundation of the ecology of the heath. The heather blooms in the late summer, and the bloom colours the heath in shades of purple that the chroniclers of the academies have celebrated in their verses. The gorse blooms in the spring, and the bloom scents the air with a fragrance that the fishermen carry back to the towns of the coast. The bracken provides cover for the smaller game that the shepherds hunt in the season.

The vegetation supports a population of insects and birds that is unusually rich for a landscape of such poor soil, and the ecologies of the heath have been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the [Cataloguing of Flora and Fauna](/content/belief/science/physical/cataloguing-of-flora-and-fauna). The chronicler notes that the catalogues of the scholars are the most complete of the records of the heath, and the catalogues are consulted by every magistrate who must judge a question of the management of the heath.

## The Villages

The villages of the heath are strung along the cliffs of the coast, and each village is the home of a small community of fishermen and their families. The villages are connected by the tracks that run along the cliff top, and the tracks are the principal means of communication between the villages and the towns of the coast. The chronicler notes that the villages are quiet and functional, and the villages are the foundation of the public order of the coast of the heath.

The villages are governed by the elders of the fishing households in consultation with the magistrates of the nearest town, and the elders maintain the boats, the gear, the quays, and the public order of the villages. The elders are elected by the households for a term of three years, and the elders hold office at the pleasure of the households. The chronicler notes that the elders are held in high esteem by the villages, and the elders mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies.

## The Sheep Farms

The sheep farms of the heath are scattered through the interior, and each farm is the home of a small household of shepherds and their families. The farms are connected by the tracks that run through the interior, and the tracks are the principal means of communication between the farms and the villages of the coast. The chronicler notes that the farms are among the most isolated of the settlements of the cold north, and the farms are the foundation of the wool supply of the towns of the coast.

The farms are governed by the heads of the households in consultation with the magistrates of the nearest town, and the heads maintain the flocks, the pastures, the tracks, and the public order of the farms. The heads are elected by the households for a term of five years, and the heads hold office at the pleasure of the households. The chronicler notes that the heads are held in high esteem by the farms, and the heads mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies.

## Significance

The Ashen Heath is one of the least developed of the regions of the cold north, and the heath is the foundation of the fishing and the pastoral economies of the coast. The villages, the farms, the boats, the gear, and the tracks are the inheritance that the heath has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the heath is held in particular memory by the fishermen and the shepherds who make their living upon it, and the fishermen and the shepherds mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the coast, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every town of the coast.
