## Grey Soil Between the Uplands and the Sea

The Ashen Heath stretches along the western coast of the principal continent, between the southern reach of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and the northern mouth of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden). The heath takes its name from the grey, ash-like soil that covers much of its surface, the product of volcanic activity that shaped the coast in the deep past. It is a broad, open landscape of low hills and shallow valleys, covered with tough vegetation of heather, gorse, and bracken that the thin soil supports in spite of its poor quality. The heath is bounded on the east by the higher ground of the uplands and on the west by the cliffs that drop to the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean).

The heath has been sparsely populated since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The population is concentrated in a string of small fishing villages along the coast and a smaller number of sheep farms upon the higher ground of the interior. The villages and farms are connected by a network of unpaved tracks maintained by the magistrates of the coastal towns, suitable for the small carts that farmers use but not for the larger vehicles of the trade routes. The isolation of these settlements has preserved a way of life that has changed little over the centuries, with families passing down knowledge of the local waters, weather patterns, and grazing grounds from one generation to the next.

## Wind, Fog, and the Grey Blanket

The heath is subject to a harsh maritime climate, with strong winds from the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) throughout much of the year, frequent rain, and a long season of fog that locals call the grey blanket. Winters are mild but wet, and summers are cool and often overcast. The growing season is short, and the heath supports only the toughest crops. The combination of wind and poor soil means that trees are almost entirely absent from the landscape, giving the heath its characteristic open horizon.

The heath experiences occasional storms that drive ocean spray across the cliffs into the interior. These storms are recorded in the chronicles of the villages as exceptional events, and they cause damage to boats and quays that requires extensive labour to repair. The storms have shaped the careful management of boats and gear that the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) maintains along the coast. Fishing vessels are hauled above the high-water mark when storms approach, and the gear is stored in stone sheds built to withstand the strongest winds.

## Volcanic Origin and Pastoral Foundation

The grey, ashen soil of the heath is the product of volcanic activity from the deep past. It is thin and well-drained, poor in the nutrients that crops require. The soil supports heather, gorse, and bracken that cover the surface, along with tough grasses that sheep feed upon. It does not support cultivation of grain or fruits that inland valleys produce, and this limitation has been the foundation of the pastoral economy of the heath since the founding of the villages. The volcanic origin of the soil gives it a distinctive texture that holds moisture poorly, requiring plants to develop deep root systems to survive the dry periods between rains.

Scholars of the Reading the Ground have studied the soil carefully and concluded that it is not capable of improvement by ordinary methods of cultivation. They recommended that the heath be reserved to the pastoral economy that the soil supports, and this recommendation was accepted by the magistrates of the coastal towns. The volcanic minerals in the soil do give the wool of the heath sheep a distinctive quality, making it sought after by weavers in the inland towns.

## Heather, Gorse, and Ground-Nesting Birds

The vegetation is dominated by heather, gorse, and bracken that cover the surface. The heather blooms in late summer, colouring the heath in shades of purple that stretch across the low hills as far as the eye can see. The gorse blooms in spring, scenting the air with a fragrance that fishermen carry back to the coastal towns. The bracken provides cover for smaller game that shepherds hunt in season. The vegetation grows in distinct zones, with heather dominating the better-drained slopes and bracken filling the hollows where moisture collects.

The vegetation supports a population of insects and birds that is unusually rich for a landscape of such poor soil. The ecologies of the heath have been studied extensively by scholars of the Bestiaries and Herbals. Their catalogues are the most complete records of the heath and are consulted by every magistrate who must judge a question of its management. The heath is particularly known for its population of ground-nesting birds, whose camouflage and behaviour have been adapted to the open landscape over many generations.

## Cliff-Top Fishing Communities

The villages of the heath are strung along the cliffs of the coast, each home to a small community of fishermen and their families. The villages are connected by tracks that run along the cliff top, the principal means of communication between the villages and the towns of the coast. Each village has a small harbour cut into the rock, where boats are moored and fish are landed. Smokehouses stand at the edge of every village, their fires burning steadily during the fishing season as the catch is preserved for transport inland.

The villages are governed by elders of the fishing households in consultation with the magistrates of the nearest town. The elders maintain the boats, gear, quays, and public order of the villages. They are elected by the households for a term of three years and hold office at the pleasure of the households. The elders mark the anniversaries of the founding with ceremonies attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every coastal town.

## Hardy Flocks and Interior Sheep Farms

The sheep farms of the heath are scattered through the interior, each home to a small household of shepherds and their families. The farms are connected by tracks through the interior, the principal means of communication between the farms and the coastal villages. The farms are among the most isolated settlements of the cold north and form the foundation of the wool supply for the coastal towns. The sheep of the heath are a hardy breed, their thick fleeces protecting them from the wind and rain that sweep across the open country.

The farms are governed by heads of households in consultation with the magistrates of the nearest town. The heads maintain the flocks, pastures, tracks, and public order of the farms. They are elected by the households for a term of five years and hold office at the pleasure of the households.

## Foundation of Fishing and Wool

The Ashen Heath is one of the least developed regions of the cold north and forms the foundation of the fishing and pastoral economies of the coast. Its villages, farms, boats, gear, and tracks are the inheritance of the present age. The annual ceremonies marking the founding are among the most important public observances of the coast, attended by magistrates and principal citizens of every coastal town.
