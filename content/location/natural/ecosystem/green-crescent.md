## The Fertile Northern Coast of Sahrana

The Green Crescent is the name given by the chroniclers and geographers of the known world to the chain of fertile regions that follows the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), a band of green and cultivated land that curves along the shoreline of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) like a crescent moon lying upon its back. The region is not a single continuous territory but a succession of fertile districts separated by stretches of drier ground, each defined by the reach of a river system that carries water from the interior highlands to the sea. Together, these districts constitute the most densely populated and productive region of Sahrana and the foundation upon which the prosperity of the Sahrani city-states has been built.

The Green Crescent extends from the western approaches of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the west to the eastern districts of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) in the east, a distance that a merchant caravan might cover in several weeks of steady travel. The crescent is narrow, rarely extending more than forty miles inland, for the fertility of the coastal strip is sharply limited by the reach of the moisture-bearing winds from the Auric Ocean and by the presence of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), the great desert that covers the interior of the continent. The boundary between the Green Crescent and the Kharesh is one of the most abrupt ecological transitions in the known world, a line where the cultivated fields and irrigated gardens of the coast give way within a few miles to the sand and rock of the desert.

## Great Rains and the River Systems

The climate of the Green Crescent is warm and humid throughout the year, moderated by the waters of the Auric Ocean and by the steady current that flows along the northern coast of Sahrana from east to west. The rainfall is abundant and reliable, falling in two distinct seasons called the great rains and the little rains, and the rivers that descend from the interior highlands carry sufficient water to sustain intensive agriculture through the drier periods between the rainy seasons.

The river systems that define the districts of the Green Crescent are the principal factor in the geography of the region. The largest of these is the river that flows past [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir), a broad and slow-moving stream that rises in the highlands of the interior and meanders across the coastal plain through a delta of many channels before reaching the sea. The river carries a heavy load of silt that it deposits upon the floodplain during the rainy season, renewing the fertility of the soil with each year's flood and sustaining the cultivation of rice, cotton, and spices that are the principal crops of the region.

The second of the great river systems of the Green Crescent is the river that waters the district of [Taretha](/content/location/settlement/city/taretha), a shorter and more vigorous stream that descends from the escarpment of the interior plateau through a series of rapids and gorges before reaching the coastal plain. The river is less reliable than the Qassir river, its flow varying considerably with the rainfall of the interior, but it supports a dense population of farmers who have learned to manage its variability through the construction of reservoirs and canals.

The smaller rivers and seasonal streams that lie between the great river systems support a patchwork of smaller districts, each with its own character and its own agricultural specialisation. The district of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) is sustained by a network of seasonal streams and groundwater sources that the engineers of the city have developed into one of the most sophisticated irrigation systems in the known world. The district of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) is watered by the easternmost of the great rivers, a stream whose lower reaches have been canalised and embanked to provide both irrigation and navigation for the city's trade.

## Cultivation and the Garden Landscape

The landscape of the Green Crescent is a cultivated garden of extraordinary productivity, shaped over centuries by the labour of generations of farmers who have developed techniques suited to the warm and humid climate of the coast. The principal crops are rice, grown in flooded terraces that cover the lower slopes and valley floors; cotton, grown for the production of the fine cloth that is one of the principal exports of the Sahrani coast; and spices, including saffron, cardamom, and black pepper, grown in gardens and smallholdings surrounding every settlement.

The fields of the Green Crescent are irrigated by an extensive network of canals and channels that distribute water from the rivers to the cultivated areas. The canals are maintained by the communities that depend upon them, and the management of the irrigation system is one of the principal functions of the local authorities in each district. The allocation of water is governed by customary rules that have been refined over centuries, and disputes over water rights are among the most common matters brought before the magistrates of the coastal cities.

Between the fields stand the villages and towns of the Green Crescent, built of local pale limestone that reflects the heat of the sun and keeps the interiors of buildings cool. The settlements are connected by roads and canals that allow the movement of goods and people between the districts. The larger settlements, including Qassir, Khalisar, Taretha, and Amurra, are walled cities that serve as the administrative and commercial centres of their respective districts, while the smaller settlements are agricultural villages whose inhabitants work the surrounding fields.

## The Boundary with the Desert

The southern boundary of the Green Crescent is not a sharp line but a gradual transition from the cultivated lands of the coast to the arid interior of the Kharesh. The transition zone is a belt of savanna and thorn scrub where the rainfall is sufficient to sustain scattered trees and drought-resistant shrubs but not enough for the intensive agriculture of the coastal strip. The soils become sandier and more alkaline as one moves inland, and the irrigation channels that sustain the fields of the coast become fewer and more widely spaced.

The settlements of the transition zone are smaller and more scattered than those of the Green Crescent proper, serving as the interface between the coastal economy and the caravan trade of the desert interior. The outposts of the transition, including [Dunesend](/content/location/settlement/outpost/dunesend) and the villages along the caravan routes, are places where the goods of the coast are exchanged for the spices, minerals, and precious stones of the Kharesh. The peoples of the transition include settled farmers of the coast and nomadic [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) of the desert, and their interactions are governed by agreements of mutual access and shared use of the water sources that sustain both ways of life.

The boundary between the Green Crescent and the Kharesh is also an ecological frontier of considerable significance. The moisture that sustains the coastal strip is carried by the winds from the Auric Ocean, and the rains that fall upon the coast are the product of the same weather systems that bring rain to the interior highlands of Sahrana. The boundary between the green and the arid is therefore not a fixed line but a shifting zone that advances and retreats with the variability of the climate, and the farmers of the coastal strip must adapt their practices to the conditions of each year.

## Commerce and the City-States

The Green Crescent is the economic heart of Sahrana, and the city-states that line its shores are among the most prosperous and cultured in the known world. The trade that flows through the ports of the crescent connects Sahrana to the markets of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and the spices, cloth, and crafted goods of the coastal cities are valued throughout the three continents. The [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) is the most powerful of the coastal polities, its fleet of trading vessels and network of commercial alliances ensuring its dominance over the trade of the northern coast.

The relationship between the Green Crescent and the interior of Sahrana is one of mutual dependence. The coastal cities depend upon the interior for the spices, minerals, and precious stones that are the most valuable components of their trade, and the interior depends upon the coast for the manufactured goods, grain, and cloth that the desert cannot produce. The caravan routes that cross the Kharesh are the arteries of this trade, and the security of the routes is a matter of concern to the rulers of the city-states and the chieftains of the interior alike.

The Green Crescent is the most productive and densely populated region of Sahrana, a cultivated garden of extraordinary fertility that has supported the civilisation of the Sahrani coast for centuries. Its fields and gardens, its canals and cities, and its position at the meeting of the sea and the desert make it one of the most distinctive and significant regions in the known world.
