## Overview

Along the lower course of the Greywash, between the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the sea at [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), the river broadens and slows, and the silt it carries settles in a broad belt of shallow water and saturated ground. This belt is the Reed Marshes, and the marshes cover several days of travel from east to west. The marshes take their name from the dense stands of common reed that grow in the brackish water, and the reeds have been harvested since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities) for thatching, for bedding, and for the small baskets in which the fish of the marshes is carried to the markets of the towns.

The marshes are bounded on the north by the gentle slopes of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale), on the south by the low hills that mark the southern limit of the Greywash basin, on the east by the rapids where the river descends to the plain, and on the west by the estuary that opens to the sea. The marshes are crossed by a network of channels that the river has cut and by the paved road that the [Building of the Stone Roads](/content/history/building-of-the-stone-roads) constructed upon the embankments that the magistrates of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) have raised over the centuries. The chronicler notes that the marshes are one of the most distinctive of the landscapes of the cold north, and the marshes are held in particular esteem by the fishermen and the reed-cutters who make their living upon them.

## Climate

The marshes enjoy a climate that is milder than that of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) to the north but wetter than that of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) to the east. The marshes are subject to the steady rainfall that the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) brings throughout much of the year, and the rainfall is supplemented by the snow-melt that descends from the uplands in the spring. The marshes are often foggy in the cooler months, and the fogs have been the source of much of the hardship of the fishermen who work the channels in the early morning.

The marshes are subject to the occasional flood when the river rises above its banks, and the floods have been the source of much of the damage to the embankments and the roads. The chronicler notes that the floods are recorded in the chronicles of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) as exceptional events, and the chronicles describe the labour that the magistrates of the town have organised to repair the damage. The chronicler observes that the floods have been the foundation of the careful management of the river that the [Treaty of the Greywash](/content/history/treaty-of-the-greywash) established.

## The Water

The water of the marshes is a mixture of fresh water from the river and salt water from the estuary, and the mixture varies with the season and with the tide. The water is brackish in the western reaches and nearly fresh in the eastern reaches, and the variation is the foundation of the ecology of the marshes. The water is shallow throughout the marshes, and the shallowness is the reason that the reed grows so densely and that the fishermen work from flat-bottomed boats rather than from vessels of deeper draft.

The water supports a rich fishery, and the fishery has been the foundation of the prosperity of the villages along the marshes. The principal fish are the eel, the pike, the perch, and the smaller fish that the fishermen take in nets set in the channels. The fishery is regulated by the magistrates of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) in consultation with the elders of the villages, and the regulations are the foundation of the careful management of the fishery.

## The Reeds

The reeds of the marshes grow in dense stands that can reach the height of a tall man, and the reeds are the foundation of the economy of the villages along the marshes. The reeds are cut in the late summer, after the nesting season of the birds that breed in the marshes has ended, and the cut reeds are dried in the sun and bound into bundles for transport. The bundles are taken by boat to the towns of the coast and the inland valleys, where the reeds are used for thatching, for bedding, and for the small baskets that the fishermen and the farmers require.

The cutting of the reeds is regulated by the magistrates of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) in consultation with the elders of the villages, and the regulations set the seasons of the cutting, the methods that the cutters must use, and the price that the cutters may charge. The chronicler notes that the regulations have been the foundation of the careful management of the reed beds, and the reed beds have been the source of much of the prosperity of the villages.

## The Wildlife

The marshes are the home of a rich variety of wildlife, and the wildlife is the foundation of the ecology of the marshes. The water supports the fish that the fishermen pursue and the smaller creatures that the birds feed upon. The reeds support the nesting birds that breed in the marshes in the summer, and the birds include herons, bitterns, and the smaller warblers that the chroniclers of the [Cataloguing of Flora and Fauna](/content/belief/science/physical/cataloguing-of-flora-and-fauna) have catalogued with care. The margins of the marshes support the deer and the wild pig that the hunters of the villages pursue in the season.

The wildlife is the source of much of the food of the villages, and the wildlife is the foundation of the careful management of the chase that the magistrates of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) maintain. The chronicler notes that the wildlife has been the subject of careful study by the scholars of the academies, and the scholars have recorded the patterns of the birds and the fish in their catalogues.

## The Villages

The villages of the marshes are strung along the channels and the embankments, and each village is the home of a small community of fishermen and reed-cutters and their families. The villages are connected by the channels and the road, and the road is the principal means of communication between the villages and the towns of the coast. The chronicler notes that the villages are quiet and functional, and the villages are the foundation of the public order of the marshes.

The villages are governed by the elders of the households in consultation with the magistrates of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), and the elders maintain the boats, the gear, the embankments, and the public order of the villages. The elders are elected by the households for a term of three years, and the elders hold office at the pleasure of the households. The chronicler notes that the elders are held in high esteem by the villages, and the elders mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies.

## Significance

The Reed Marshes of the Greywash are one of the most distinctive and most productive of the landscapes of the cold north, and the marshes are the foundation of the fishing and the reed-cutting economies of the lower Greywash. The channels, the embankments, the road, the villages, and the boats are the inheritance that the marshes have bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the marshes are held in particular memory by the fishermen and the reed-cutters who make their living upon them, and the fishermen and the reed-cutters mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the lower Greywash, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every town of the river.
