## Brackish Belt of the Lower Greywash

Along the lower course of the Greywash, between the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the sea at [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), the river broadens and slows, and the silt it carries settles in a broad belt of shallow water and saturated ground. This belt is the Reed Marshes, covering several days of travel from east to west. The marshes take their name from the dense stands of common reed that grow in the brackish water, harvested since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era) for thatching, bedding, and the small baskets in which fish from the marshes are carried to the markets of the towns.

The marshes are bounded on the north by the gentle slopes of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale), on the south by the low hills that mark the southern limit of the Greywash basin, on the east by the rapids where the river descends to the plain, and on the west by the estuary that opens to the sea. The marshes are crossed by a network of channels cut by the river and by the paved road that the [Laying the Stone Roads](/content/history/laying-the-stone-roads) constructed upon embankments raised by the magistrates of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) over the centuries. The road is raised above the level of the surrounding marsh, providing a dry route through the wetland even during the wettest months.

## Mild, Wet, and Prone to Fog

The marshes enjoy a climate milder than that of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) to the north but wetter than that of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) to the east. The marshes receive steady rainfall from the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) throughout much of the year, supplemented by snow-melt that descends from the uplands in spring. Fog is common in the cooler months, making early morning work on the channels difficult for fishermen. The fog settles thickly over the water while leaving the higher ground clear, a phenomenon that locals use to predict the weather for the day ahead.

Occasional floods when the river rises above its banks damage the embankments and roads. These floods are recorded as exceptional events in the chronicles of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) and have formed the foundation of the careful management of the river that the [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact) established. The most severe floods occur when heavy rain coincides with a spring tide, pushing salt water up the estuary at the same time that fresh water descends from the uplands.

## Fresh and Salt, Shallow and Changing

The water of the marshes is a mixture of fresh water from the river and salt water from the estuary, varying with season and tide. The water is brackish in the western reaches and nearly fresh in the [eastern reach](/content/location/natural/continent/eastern-reach)es, and this variation is the foundation of the marsh ecology. The water is shallow throughout the marshes, allowing reed to grow densely and requiring fishermen to work from flat-bottomed boats rather than vessels of deeper draft. The depth of the water changes with the season, reaching its greatest extent during the spring floods and shrinking to isolated pools during the driest part of summer.

The water supports a rich fishery that has been the foundation of the prosperity of the villages along the marshes. The principal fish are eel, pike, perch, and smaller fish taken in nets set in the channels. The fishery is regulated by the magistrates of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) in consultation with the elders of the villages.

## Thatching, Bedding, and the Small Baskets

The reeds of the marshes grow in dense stands that can reach the height of a tall man. They are cut in late summer, after the nesting season of the birds that breed in the marshes has ended, and the cut reeds are dried in the sun and bound into bundles for transport. The bundles are taken by boat to the towns of the coast and inland valleys, where they are used for thatching, bedding, and small baskets. The quality of the reed depends on the salinity of the water in which it grows, with the brackish-water reed being stronger and more durable than the freshwater variety.

The cutting of the reeds is regulated by the magistrates of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) in consultation with the elders of the villages. The regulations set the seasons of cutting, the methods that cutters must use, and the price that cutters may charge.

## Eel, Pike, Heron, and the Migratory Birds

The marshes are home to a rich variety of wildlife. The water supports fish that fishermen pursue and smaller creatures that birds feed upon. The reeds support nesting birds that breed in the marshes in summer, including herons, bitterns, and smaller warblers catalogued by scholars of the Bestiaries and Herbals. The margins of the marshes support deer and wild pig that hunters pursue in season. The marsh is particularly important as a staging ground for migratory birds, which gather in large numbers during the spring and autumn passages.

## Flat-Bottomed Boats and Wooden Jetties

The villages of the marshes are strung along the channels and embankments, each home to a small community of fishermen and reed-cutters and their families. The villages are connected by the channels and the road, which is the principal means of communication between the villages and the coastal towns. Each village has a small fleet of flat-bottomed boats moored at wooden jetties, and the sound of the boats moving through the channels is a constant presence in the life of the marsh.

The villages are governed by elders of the households in consultation with the magistrates of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara). The elders maintain the boats, gear, embankments, and public order of the villages. They are elected by the households for a term of three years and hold office at the pleasure of the households. The elders mark the anniversaries of the founding with ceremonies attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every town of the river.

## Fishing, Reed-Cutting, and the Greywash Economy

The Greywash Reed Marshes are one of the most distinctive and productive landscapes of the cold north, forming the foundation of the fishing and reed-cutting economies of the lower Greywash. The channels, embankments, road, villages, and boats are the inheritance of the present age. The ceremonies marking the founding are among the most important public observances of the lower Greywash.
