## Overview

The Stormfall Coast is the rugged southern shoreline of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) that faces the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea), a region of high cliffs, deep fjords, and narrow coastal plains that have been shaped by the relentless action of wind and water over geological ages. The coast runs for several hundred miles from the western headlands that jut into the open waters of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) to the eastern approaches of the [Strait of Velden](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), and it presents a face of stone and water that has defied easy settlement while rewarding those who have learned its ways with shelter, food, and passage. The settlements of the coast are small and resilient, oriented toward the sea for their livelihood and their connection to the wider world, and the character of the region is shaped by the storms that give it its name.

## Geology and Landforms

The underlying geology of the Stormfall Coast is ancient, the rock being among the oldest exposed in the known world, and the landforms of the region are the product of glacial carving and marine erosion acting upon a foundation of hard granite and basalt. The cliffs that define the coast rise in many places to a height of several hundred feet, their faces sheer and their summits bare, and they are broken at intervals by the mouths of the fjords that penetrate deep into the interior of Morvind. The fjords themselves are long and narrow, their sides steep and their waters deep, and they extend inland for distances that range from a few miles to more than twenty, their heads marked by the gravel deltas where the mountain streams enter the sea.

The coast is not uniformly steep, for in the lee of the headlands and at the heads of the fjords there occur narrow flats where the land meets the sea in a gentler fashion. These flats are composed of the sediment carried down from the interior by the rivers and the glacial melt, and they support the greater part of the settlement and the agriculture of the region, meagre though these are by the standards of more temperate shores. The beaches are of dark sand and rounded cobbles, and they are narrow, seldom extending more than a few paces before the ground rises into the rocky slopes that mark the beginning of the interior.

The offshore waters of the Stormfall Coast are shallow along the continental shelf, and the sea floor is composed of rock and gravel rather than the fine sediment that accumulates in quieter waters. The shelf is rich in marine life, for the mixing of the cold waters of the Greyring with the warmer currents of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) creates conditions of great fertility, and the waters of the Stormfall Sea are among the most productive fishing grounds in the known world. The seabed is marked by ridges and troughs that are the remains of ancient glacial valleys, and these features channel the currents and concentrate the nutrients that sustain the web of marine life.

## Climate and Weather

The climate of the Stormfall Coast is cold and maritime, moderated by the waters of the Stormfall Sea but subject to the violent weather that gives the region its name. The winters are long and dark, with the sun climbing only a few degrees above the horizon even at midday, and the storms that sweep across the sea during the winter months are the defining feature of the coastal climate. These storms arrive with little warning, bringing winds that can tear the roof from a well-built house and seas that can dash a ship against the cliffs within minutes of the first warning, and the peoples of the coast have learned to read the signs of their approach with a care that borders on reverence.

The summer is brief but intense, with long days and a sun that never fully sets in the northernmost reaches of the coast, and the weather in this season is the most settled of the year. The storms of the winter give way to periods of calm and fog, and the waters of the sea grow calm enough for the boats of the coastal communities to venture out in safety. The fog that rolls in from the sea during the summer is frequent and dense, reducing visibility to a few paces and rendering navigation a matter of sound and memory rather than sight, but the coastal peoples know their waters intimately and move through the fog with a confidence that astounds visitors from the south.

The precipitation of the region is abundant, falling as rain in the summer and as snow in the winter, and the moisture supports a vegetation that is lush despite the harshness of the climate. The snow that falls upon the higher slopes of the coastal mountains accumulates through the winter and feeds the streams that run clear and cold through the summer, and the meltwater that pours down the cliffs and into the fjords is a constant presence in the life of the coast. The air is always damp, carrying the salt of the sea and the scent of the wet rock and the sodden earth, and the inhabitants of the coast live in a world whose every surface is sheathed in moisture for much of the year.

## Coastal Flora

The plant life of the Stormfall Coast is adapted to the twin pressures of salt-laden wind and shallow, rocky soil, and the vegetation that thrives here is low-growing and tough. The coastal heath that covers the cliff tops and the upper slopes of the fjords is composed of dwarf shrubs, mosses, and lichens, with a scattering of hardy grasses that manage to flower in the brief summer. The heath is dominated by the windshrub, a woody plant that grows no higher than a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) knee and spreads by underground runners, forming mats of vegetation that hold the thin soil against the wind and rain. The leaves of the windshrub are small and leathery, covered in a waxy coating that reduces water loss, and its flowers are tiny and inconspicuous, appearing in the early summer and giving way to berries that are a staple food for the birds of the coast.

In the shelter of the fjords, where the cliffs provide protection from the worst of the wind, the vegetation becomes more substantial. Stunted trees of the mountain birch and the wind-bent pine find a foothold in the crevices and on the slopes, their forms shaped by the prevailing wind into permanent postures of submission to the elements. These trees are not tall, seldom exceeding the height of a [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) even in the most sheltered locations, and their wood is dense and slow-grown, prized for its resistance to rot and its suitability for the construction of boats and buildings.

The lower slopes of the coastal mountains, where the influence of the sea begins to wane and the interior climate takes hold, support a denser forest of coniferous trees, including the black spruce and the Morvind fir, the latter being one of the hardiest of all timber trees and capable of surviving conditions that would kill any other species of comparable size. These forests are dark and quiet, their floors carpeted with needles and moss, and they mark the transition between the open coast and the closed interior of the continent.

## Marine Life and the Intertidal Zone

The waters of the Stormfall Coast teem with life, and the intertidal zone between the high and low tide marks is one of the most productive habitats in the region. The rocks of the shore are covered in a thick coating of seaweed, including the long-stemmed kelp that grows in dense beds in the shallow waters and the leathery bladderweed that covers the exposed rocks at low tide. The seaweed beds are the nursery grounds for a host of marine animals, including the shore crab, the spiny urchin, and the many species of mollusc that cling to the rocks and feed upon the algae.

The fish of the Stormfall Sea are abundant and diverse, and they form the foundation of the coastal economy. The herring runs in vast shoals during the summer months, its passage through the fjords marked by the flocks of seabirds that gather to feed upon it, and the cod that preys upon the herring and upon the smaller fish of the shelf is the most important catch for the fishing communities. The cod of the Stormfall Sea are large, often exceeding the length of a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) arm, and their flesh is dense and white, salted and dried for export to the markets of the south.

The larger marine animals of the coast include the grey seal, which hauls out upon the rocky shores of the outer coast in colonies that number in the hundreds, and the coastal whale, a species of baleen whale that passes through the Stormfall Sea on its annual migration between the feeding grounds of the north and the breeding grounds of the south. The whales are hunted by the coastal communities with a skill that has been refined over centuries, and the oil, the bone, and the meat that they provide are essential resources for a region where the land offers little in the way of wealth.

## Seabirds of the Cliffs

The cliffs of the Stormfall Coast are among the most important seabird nesting sites in the known world, and the bird colonies that occupy the ledges and crevices of the cliff faces are a spectacle that defines the character of the coast. The common guillemot and the black-legged kittiwake nest in dense colonies on the narrow ledges, their numbers so great that the sound of their calls can be heard from a distance of miles, and their droppings stain the cliff faces white from the high-water mark to the summit. The great auk, a flightless bird that nests on the more sheltered islands of the coast, is hunted for its meat and its feathers, and its populations are carefully managed by the coastal communities to ensure their continued abundance.

The birds of the cliffs feed upon the fish of the offshore waters, and their movements are a reliable guide to the location of the shoals that the human fishers seek. The relationship between the seabirds and the coastal communities is one of mutual observation and shared dependence on the fertility of the sea, and the arrival of the birds in the spring is the signal for the beginning of the fishing season, just as their departure in the autumn marks its end.

## Human Settlement and the Kingdom of Nordhaven

The [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) settlements of the Stormfall Coast are small and hardy, oriented toward the sea and sustained by the resources of the coast and the waters beyond. The villages are built of stone and timber, their roofs weighted against the wind and their walls thick against the cold, and they cluster at the heads of the fjords and on the sheltered flats where the land offers room enough for a few fields and a harbour. The [Kingdom of Nordhaven](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-nordhaven) unites the principal ports and many of the lesser communities, coordinating the fishing, coastal defence, and trade that sustain the population of the region.

The people of the coast are skilled mariners, and their boats are built to withstand the conditions of the Stormfall Sea. The coastal knarr, a double-ended vessel of clinker construction, is the most common type, and it is used for both fishing and trade, capable of carrying a crew of a dozen [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and a substantial cargo of dried fish, whale oil, and seal skins. The larger longships used by the warriors and the explorers of the coast are among the finest vessels of their kind in the known world, and their design has been copied and adapted by shipwrights across the three continents.

The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the Morvind interior maintain trade routes down the rivers that descend to the coast, exchanging the metal goods of the mountains for the fish, the oil, and the salt of the coastal communities. The relationship between the coast and the interior is one of complementarity, for each produces what the other cannot, and the traffic that passes along the rivers and the coastal routes is the lifeblood of the economy of northern Morvind.

## Significance

The Stormfall Coast is the gateway through which the wealth of Morvind reaches the wider world, for the ores and the crafted goods of the dwarven holds of the interior must pass through the coastal ports on their way to the markets of the south and east. The coast is also a bastion of hardy and self-reliant communities whose traditions of seamanship and resilience are among the oldest in the known world. The storms that give the coast its name remain the defining feature of life in this region, and the peoples who dwell here have shaped a way of life that is inseparable from the wind, the water, and the rock of their unforgiving but bountiful shore.
