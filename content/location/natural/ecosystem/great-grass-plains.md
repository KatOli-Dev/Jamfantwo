## A Rolling Sea of Tall Grasses

The Great Grass Plains of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) constitute the most extensive tract of temperate grassland in the known world, a rolling sea of tall grasses that stretches from the foothills of the mountain spine in the west to the broad river valleys that drain toward the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) in the east. The plains extend northward to the margins of the ancient forests of northern Aethelar and southward to the warmer plains that belong to the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), covering a distance that a fit rider might cross in a month of steady travel. The grasslands have been shaped over centuries by the seasonal movements of great herds of grazing animals and by the presence of the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs), whose nomadic kindreds have ranged across these lands for as long as any record describes.

## Hot Summers, Cold Winters, and the Dramatic Thaw

The climate of the Great Grass Plains is one of marked seasonal contrasts, with hot summers and cold winters separated by brief but dramatic periods of transition. The spring thaw arrives with force, when snow that has lain upon the grass through the winter melts within days and the earth turns to mud before putting forth the first green shoots. Spring rains are reliable, sweeping across the plains from the west and bringing sufficient moisture to sustain the deep-rooted grasses through the summer.

Summer upon the plains is a time of heat and abundance, when the grasses reach their full height and the great herds of grazing animals spread across the open country, feeding and calving under a sky that stretches unbroken from horizon to horizon. The sun beats down from a pale blue dome, heat shimmers above the grass, and storms gather in the afternoons with a regularity that the peoples of the plains have learned to read. Lightning flashes across the darkening sky, and the rain that follows is brief but violent.

Autumn brings gradual cooling and a change in the light as the grasses turn from green to gold and then to pale brown. The herds begin their southward movement along ancient paths that the centaurs call the old ways, and the hunting season reaches its peak. The winter that follows is cold and dry, with winds that sweep across the open country without obstruction, and snow is blown into drifts that gather in the lee of every rise and hollow. The grasses stand dormant through the cold months, their roots deep and their crowns protected by the thatch of dead stems.

## Feathergrass, Fireflower, and the Rootstar

The plant life is dominated by a diverse assemblage of grass species that together form a continuous ground cover over the entire region. The dominant grasses include the tall feathergrass, which grows to the height of a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) in the better-watered eastern portions, and the shorter bristlegrass, which predominates in the drier western reaches. Beneath the grass canopy, the earth is held together by a dense mat of roots and rhizomes that binds the soil against wind and rain.

Between the grass stems grow a variety of flowering forbs, many small and inconspicuous but some erupting into brief displays of colour when conditions favour them. The fireflower, with brilliant orange petals, appears in scattered patches in the weeks following the spring rains, attracting insects that in turn draw birds and small predators. The rootstar, a low-growing plant with a thick taproot that stores water through the dry season, is a staple food for grazing animals and for centaurs who gather it in late summer.

Trees are uncommon upon the plains, for the deep root mat of the grasses leaves little space for taproots of woody plants. Those trees that do establish themselves are found chiefly along watercourses. The riverwood, a hardy species of willow that tolerates both flood and drought, lines the banks of major rivers, its presence marking these waterways from a great distance. Scattered stands of windbreak trees, including the twisted ironbark and the broad-crowned shade elm, occur where the soil is deep enough and the water table high enough to sustain them.

## Plains Cattle, Swift Antelope, and the Praire Digger

The animal life is as abundant as the plant life, and the region supports one of the greatest concentrations of large grazing animals found anywhere in the known world. The plains cattle, a large humped animal that roams in herds of hundreds or thousands, is the most conspicuous, its dark coat and wide horns visible from a great distance. The cattle move in a constant cycle of grazing and migration, following the growth of the grass as it advances northward through spring and retreats southward through autumn.

Alongside the plains cattle graze the swift antelope, a smaller and more graceful animal whose speed is its primary defence, and the shaggy-maned horse, which shares the plains with the centaurs and is regarded with respect bordering on kinship. The [plains thunder boar](/content/species/beasts/plains-thunder-boar), the largest of the wild swine, roots through the grassland and is the quarry of the great mounted hunt of the confederation. The horses of the plains are wild and wary, gathering in bands that roam in patterns that centaurs have studied for generations.

The predators of the plains maintain the balance of the grassland through culling of the herds. The plains lion, a tawny-coated cat that hunts in prides and favours the tall grass of river corridors, is the most formidable of the large predators. The swift hunting cat, smaller and longer-legged, runs down its prey in the open rather than ambushing from cover, and its speed is such that even the antelope cannot outrun it over a short distance. Packs of the grey plains wolf range across the entire region, and their howls are among the most familiar sounds of the grassland night.

The smaller creatures are no less important to the ecosystem. The prairie digger, a stout-bodied rodent that excavates extensive burrow systems beneath the grass, is a keystone species, for its tunnels aerate the soil and its mounds provide elevated sites for germination of plants that cannot compete in the dense sward. The burrows of the digger are used by ground owls, burrowing snakes, and small foxes, and the digger colonies are nodes of activity that draw life from across the grassland.

## The Centaur Kindreds and the Old Ways

The [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) are the dominant sapient inhabitants of the Great Grass Plains, and their presence has shaped the ecology of the region for centuries beyond reckoning. The centaur kindreds move across the plains in a seasonal cycle that mirrors the movements of the herds, following the grass as it greens and fades and moving their encampments according to patterns established over countless generations. The centaurs do not build permanent settlements, for their way of life demands mobility, and their dwellings are tents of hide and woven grass that can be dismantled and packed within the hour, leaving no trace beyond the flattened circle of the camp.

The relationship between the centaurs and the grasslands is one of active stewardship. The centaurs manage the grazing of wild herds through their own movements and through controlled burning of the grass in the dry season, a practice that rejuvenates the sward and prevents the encroachment of woody plants. The centaurs know the plains as a living map, where every rise and watercourse has a name. The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) is the political entity through which the centaur kindreds and the settled [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) communities of the southern plains coordinate their use of the land, and the agreements governing grazing rights, water access, and hunting territories are among the oldest continuous political arrangements in the known world.

## Grazing, Predation, and the Centaur Stewardship

The Great Grass Plains function as a single integrated ecosystem in which the movements of animals, growth of plants, and activities of sapient peoples are bound together in a complex web of mutual dependence. Grazing of the herds stimulates the growth of the grass, for cropping of leaves encourages the plants to put forth new shoots, and animal dung returns nutrients to the soil. Predators cull the weak and sick from the herds, maintaining the health of grazing populations and preventing overgrazing.

The centaurs occupy a central position in this web, for their movements and practices influence the behaviour of both herds and predators, and their stewardship has maintained the health of the grassland through centuries of use. The plains also support smaller populations of other sapient peoples, including [felinids](/content/species/sapient/felinids) who hunt the margins and [halflings](/content/species/sapient/halflings) who dwell in the river valleys.

## The Breadbasket of Central Aethelar

The Great Grass Plains are the breadbasket of central Aethelar. The herds that graze upon them and the fertility of the grassland soils support a population that extends far beyond the plains themselves. The grazing animals are the foundation of the leather and meat trade that supplies the cities of the coast and interior, and the horses that run wild upon the grass are the source of mounts that carry riders and goods across the breadth of the continent. The centaur kindreds who steward the plains are among the oldest continuous cultures in the known world, and their knowledge of the grassland and its rhythms is a resource that the settled peoples of Aethelar have relied upon since the earliest records of trade and contact between the two ways of life.
