## Overview

The Great Grass Plains of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) constitute the most extensive tract of temperate grassland in the known world, a rolling sea of tall grasses that stretches from the foothills of the mountain spine in the west to the broad river valleys that drain toward the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) in the east. The plains extend northward to the margins of the ancient forests of northern Aethelar and southward to the warmer plains that belong to the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), covering a distance that a fit rider might cross in a month of steady travel. The grasslands have been shaped over centuries by the seasonal movements of great herds of grazing animals and by the presence of the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs), whose nomadic kindreds have ranged across these lands for as long as any chronicle records.

## Climate and Seasonality

The climate of the Great Grass Plains is one of marked seasonal contrasts, with hot summers and cold winters separated by brief but dramatic periods of transition. The spring thaw arrives with force upon the plains, when the snow that has lain upon the grass through the winter melts within a span of days and the earth turns to mud before putting forth the first green shoots of the growing season. The spring rains are reliable, sweeping across the plains from the west and bringing sufficient moisture to sustain the deep-rooted grasses through the summer that follows, and the air in this season carries the scent of wet earth and new growth.

Summer upon the plains is a time of heat and of abundance, when the grasses reach their full height and the great herds of grazing animals spread across the open country, feeding and calving under a sky that stretches unbroken from horizon to horizon. The sun beats down from a pale blue dome, and the heat shimmers above the grass, and storms gather in the afternoons with a regularity that the peoples of the plains have learned to read. Lightning flashes across the darkening sky, and the rain that follows is brief but violent, and the grass drinks deeply before the sun returns to bake the land once more.

Autumn brings a gradual cooling and a change in the light, and the grasses turn from green to gold and then to a pale brown that covers the plains like a vast cloth spread across the land. The herds begin their southward movement, following the ancient paths that the centaurs call the old ways, and the hunting season reaches its peak as the animals are fat from the summer grazing and the first chill of the coming winter sharpens the air. The winter that follows is cold and dry, with winds that sweep across the open country without obstruction, and the snow that falls is blown into drifts that gather in the lee of every rise and hollow. The grasses stand dormant through the cold months, their roots deep and their crowns protected by the thatch of dead stems that covers the earth.

## Flora of the Plains

The plant life of the Great Grass Plains is dominated by the grasses themselves, a diverse assemblage of species that together form a continuous ground cover over the entire region. The dominant grasses include the tall feathergrass, which grows to the height of a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) in the better-watered eastern portions of the plains, and the shorter bristlegrass, which predominates in the drier western reaches where the rainfall is less reliable. Beneath the grass canopy, the earth is held together by a dense mat of roots and rhizomes that binds the soil against the wind and the rain, and it is this root mat, more than any above-ground feature, that defines the ecological character of the plains.

Between the grass stems grow a variety of flowering forbs, many of them small and inconspicuous but some erupting into brief displays of colour when the conditions favour them. The fireflower, so named for the brilliant orange of its petals, appears in scattered patches across the plains in the weeks following the spring rains, and its blooms attract a host of insects that in turn draw the birds and the small predators of the grassland. The rootstar, a low-growing plant with a thick taproot that stores water through the dry season, is a staple food for the grazing animals and for the centaurs who gather it in the late summer, when its root is at its most nourishing.

Trees are uncommon upon the plains, for the deep root mat of the grasses leaves little space for the taproots of woody plants, and those trees that do establish themselves are found chiefly along the watercourses that cross the grassland. The riverwood, a hardy species of willow that tolerates both flood and drought, lines the banks of the major rivers, and its presence marks the course of these waterways from a great distance, a ribbon of green against the gold of the grass. Scattered stands of windbreak trees, including the twisted ironbark and the broad-crowned shade elm, occur where the soil is deep enough and the water table high enough to sustain them, and these groves serve as landmarks and gathering places for the peoples of the plains.

## Fauna of the Grasslands

The animal life of the Great Grass Plains is as abundant and as varied as its plant life, and the region supports one of the greatest concentrations of large grazing animals to be found anywhere in the known world. The plains cattle, a large humped animal that roams the grasslands in herds of hundreds or thousands, is the most conspicuous of these, its dark coat and wide horns visible from a great distance across the open country. The cattle move in a constant cycle of grazing and migration, following the growth of the grass as it advances northward through the spring and retreats southward through the autumn, and their hooves and their dung are essential elements of the grassland ecosystem.

Alongside the plains cattle graze the swift antelope, a smaller and more graceful animal whose speed is its primary defence against the predators of the grasslands, and the shaggy-maned horse, which shares the plains with the centaurs and is regarded by them with a respect that borders on kinship. The horses of the plains are wild and wary, and they gather in bands that roam the grasslands in patterns that the centaurs have studied for generations, reading the movements of the horse bands as a sign of the health of the land itself.

The predators of the plains are many, and they maintain the balance of the grassland through their culling of the herds. The plains lion, a tawny-coated cat that hunts in prides and favours the tall grass of the river corridors, is the most formidable of the large predators, and its roar carries across the open country in the still of the night. The swift hunting cat, smaller and longer-legged than the plains lion, runs down its prey in the open rather than ambushing it from cover, and its speed is such that even the antelope cannot outrun it over a short distance. Packs of the grey plains wolf range across the entire region, and their howls are among the most familiar sounds of the grassland night, a call that the centaurs have learned to read for meaning and for warning.

The smaller creatures of the plains are no less important to the functioning of the ecosystem, and the burrowing animals of the grassland are among its most numerous inhabitants. The prairie digger, a stout-bodied rodent that excavates extensive burrow systems beneath the grass, is a keystone species of the plains, for its tunnels aerate the soil and its mounds provide elevated sites for the germination of plants that cannot compete in the dense sward. The burrows of the digger are used by a host of other animals, including the ground owl, the burrowing snake, and the small fox that preys upon both, and the digger colonies are nodes of activity that draw life from across the grassland.

## The Centaur Kindreds

The [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) are the dominant sapient inhabitants of the Great Grass Plains, and their presence has shaped the ecology of the region for centuries beyond reckoning. The centaur kindreds move across the plains in a seasonal cycle that mirrors the movements of the herds, following the grass as it greens and fades and moving their encampments according to patterns that have been established over countless generations. The centaurs do not build permanent settlements upon the plains, for their way of life demands mobility, and their dwellings are tents of hide and woven grass that can be dismantled and packed within the hour, leaving no trace upon the land beyond the flattened circle of the camp.

The relationship between the centaurs and the grasslands is one of active stewardship, for the centaurs manage the grazing of the wild herds through their own movements and through the controlled burning of the grass in the dry season, a practice that rejuvenates the sward and prevents the encroachment of woody plants. The centaurs know the plains as a living map, where every rise and every watercourse has a name, and their knowledge of the land is the foundation of their ability to thrive in a region that other peoples find monotonous and difficult. The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) is the political entity through which the centaur kindreds and the settled [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) communities of the southern plains coordinate their use of the land, and the agreements that govern grazing rights, water access, and hunting territories are among the oldest continuous political arrangements in the known world.

## Ecological Relationships

The Great Grass Plains function as a single integrated ecosystem in which the movements of animals, the growth of plants, and the activities of sapient peoples are bound together in a complex web of mutual dependence. The grazing of the herds stimulates the growth of the grass, for the cropping of the leaves encourages the plants to put forth new shoots, and the dung of the animals returns nutrients to the soil in a form that the grasses can readily absorb. The predators cull the weak and the sick from the herds, and their presence maintains the health of the grazing populations and prevents overgrazing of the sward.

The centaurs occupy a central position in this web, for their movements and their practices influence the behaviour of both the herds and the predators, and their stewardship of the plains has maintained the health of the grassland through centuries of use. The plains also support smaller populations of other sapient peoples, including [felinids](/content/species/sapient/felinids) who hunt the margins of the grassland and [halflings](/content/species/sapient/halflings) who dwell in the river valleys, and these peoples contribute their own patterns of use and management to the ecological whole.

## Significance

The Great Grass Plains are the breadbasket of central Aethelar, for the herds that graze upon them and the fertility of the grassland soils support a population that extends far beyond the plains themselves. The grazing animals of the plains are the foundation of the leather and the meat trade that supplies the cities of the coast and the interior, and the horses that run wild upon the grass are the source of the mounts that carry riders and goods across the breadth of the continent. The centaur kindreds who steward the plains are among the oldest continuous cultures in the known world, and their knowledge of the grassland and its rhythms is a resource that the settled peoples of Aethelar have relied upon since the earliest records of trade and contact between the two ways of life.
