## A Storm-Shaped Continent

Stormfall is a storm-shaped continent that lies far to the west of the established northern routes, beyond waters where fog and heavy weather often conceal the horizon. Its northern shore is broken into dark fjords, its interior contains long mountain chains, and its southern coast opens onto warmer seas where rainforests and grasslands meet. The continent has no gentle side. Even its broad eastern bays are guarded by headlands, reefs, and currents that change sharply when weather moves down from the high country. The [Storm Cape](/content/location/natural/feature/cape-of-storms) marks the westernmost reach of the land and gives the continent's common name its most familiar image.

The name Stormfall is applied both to the continent and to several waters named by sailors who reached its coast before any inland expedition was recorded. The common tongue of its dark fjords and highland valleys is called [Stormic](/content/language/spoken/stormic), or the thunderback speech. The oldest coastal traditions say that storms fall from the mountains as though poured from a bowl, descending through the valleys and striking the sea with little warning. Foreign pilots use the word more broadly for any voyage that passes near the continent. Stormfall peoples distinguish the seasons and regions with greater precision, and their maps show the land to be as varied as any of the warmer continents.

## Thunderback Range and the Fjord Coast

The Stormfall interior is dominated by the [Thunderback Range](/content/location/natural/feature/thunderback-range), a chain of mountains that runs from the northeastern coast to the southwest and divides the continent's watersheds. Its central peaks are high enough to retain snow through the warm season, though the lower ridges are covered in forest and open grass. The mountains are young compared with the worn ranges of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and earthquakes remain common along the southern valleys. Hot springs, sulphurous pools, and new cliffs appear after the strongest shocks, while older valleys are filled with ash-rich soil that produces heavy crops.

The eastern side of the range is steep and wet. Rivers descend through narrow gorges, then spread into fan-shaped plains before reaching the sea. These plains contain the largest agricultural settlements, because the floodwater leaves dark soil and the mountains shield the fields from the strongest ocean winds. The western side is drier. Rain falls upon the outer slopes, but much of the moisture is lost before it crosses the crest, leaving the interior basins covered by grass, thorn scrub, and seasonal streams.

The northern coast has been carved by ice. Fjords reach inland between sheer cliffs, and many end in lakes where glaciers once stood. The southern coast is warmer and lower, with long beaches, mangrove channels, and broad estuaries. Offshore islands form a broken chain along both coasts. Some are volcanic, with fertile black soil and steaming vents, while others are fragments of old mainland rock separated by rising water. Sailors use the islands as stepping stones during the calmer season, but the passages between them are too complex for strangers to attempt without a local pilot.

## Where Three Winds Meet

Stormfall receives weather from three directions. Cold winds descend from the northern ocean, warm air arrives from the southern seas, and mountain valleys funnel both toward the eastern coast. Their meeting produces sudden storms that may begin beneath clear skies. Cloud gathers along the ridge, thunder rolls through the passes, and rain reaches the low country before distant villages have seen the first lightning. The storms are most frequent in late summer and autumn, when the southern sea is warmest.

The northern winter is wet, windy, and dark, though the coastal waters remain partly open because of a warm current that follows the outer shelf. Snow lies deep in the mountains and accumulates upon the high plateaus, feeding the rivers during the spring thaw. The southern winter is drier and mild, with cool nights in the uplands and warm days along the coast. Spring moves northward along the shore, and the first flowering of the southern grasslands may precede the thaw in the high valleys by several weeks.

The people of Stormfall treat weather observation as a common duty. Harbour towers record wind direction, cloud height, and the time between distant thunder and rain. Mountain settlements maintain signal fires and stone drums that carry warnings through the valleys. The accuracy of the records has made Stormfall's pilots valued in distant ports, where captains seek their advice before crossing unfamiliar water.

## Storm Pine, Kelp Forests, and Coral Shelves

The northern forests are composed of storm pine, blue cedar, and red fir, trees with flexible trunks and deep roots that withstand repeated gales. The understory is thick with fern, berry shrubs, and low flowering plants. In sheltered valleys, the trees grow tall enough to form a continuous canopy, while exposed ridges carry only wind-bent woodland. Elk, forest deer, and broad-shouldered boars browse beneath the trees, and the high branches shelter owls, black woodpeckers, and small gliding mammals.

The central basins support tall grasses, thorn scrub, and groves of fruit-bearing trees. Wild horses and striped cattle move between the grasslands and the river margins, while wolves and long-legged hunting cats follow their herds. The southern plain is warmer and wetter, and its natural vegetation includes palms, red-leaved reeds, and hardwood forest. Large river lizards inhabit the estuaries, and the shallow coastal waters support shellfish, sea turtles, and schools of bright fish.

The sea is particularly important to Stormfall life. Kelp forests grow around the northern islands, while coral shelves occupy the warmer southern bays. [Merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) communities live in both regions and exchange knowledge across the continental coast. [Cancrids](/content/species/sapient/cancrids) occupy the wave-cut rocks of the north and maintain tidal gardens where the water is calm. Their settlements are built with living shell, stone, and driftwood, and their understanding of the tides is essential to every coastal harbour.

## River Basins and Highland Assemblies

[Humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) live in the river basins and the larger ports, though their communities are separated by mountains and often develop distinct customs. Northern households preserve fish, smoke meat, and use turf for winter insulation. Central farmers grow grain, beans, and orchard fruit on the alluvial fans. Southern communities cultivate rice, dye plants, and salt-tolerant vegetables in the lower valleys. The same language is not spoken across the continent, but trade signs and measures are shared in every market that handles sea cargo.

[Elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) inhabit the older forests of the eastern slopes, where they tend mixed woodland and protect the headwaters of the rivers. [Orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) are found in the western uplands and the drier interior basins, where many communities follow seasonal routes between pasture and mineral springs. [Goliaths](/content/species/sapient/goliaths) occupy the highest valleys, and their knowledge of snow bridges, cliff paths, and mountain weather makes them indispensable guides. These peoples trade with one another regularly, though they retain different ideas about land, inheritance, and the proper scale of settlement.

Stormfall has no single government. Coastal cities govern their harbours, river councils regulate irrigation, and mountain clans control the passes nearest their homes. Some valleys recognise a highland assembly that meets after the spring thaw, but its decisions depend upon consent rather than force. The common obligations are rescue, the marking of safe roads, and the maintenance of warning stations. A community that fails in one of these duties may lose the trust of its neighbours and find its trade diminished even when its harvest is strong.

## Flexible Masts and Warning Stations

The continent exports timber, resin, preserved fish, metal, wool, dyes, and volcanic glass. Its ships are built from storm pine and fitted with flexible masts that can be lowered rapidly when a squall approaches. Coastal pilots know the channels between the islands and the shelter offered by each headland. Their charts mark not only depth but also the direction from which a storm is likely to arrive, the colour of the water before a current shifts, and the birds that signal land beyond the fog.

River traffic carries mountain ore and forest goods toward the eastern ports, while barges return with grain, salt, cloth, and tools. The mountain roads are open only during part of the year, but the refuge halls allow caravans to cross in stages rather than risk a single long journey. The southern ports trade with [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) and the Auric routes, while northern captains seek fish and metal markets in Morvind. Stormfall's distance makes every voyage costly, yet its resources and skilled mariners ensure that commerce continues.

## Adaptation as an Institution

Stormfall is a land that has made adaptation into an institution. Its inhabitants do not treat the weather as a temporary inconvenience or the mountains as an obstacle to be removed. They build for wind, record the movement of rivers, preserve forested slopes, and maintain roads that may be buried each winter. The continent's natural wealth is inseparable from those practices, because clear rivers, stable soils, and safe harbours depend upon constant care.

The Storm Cape, the Thunderback Range, the northern fjords, and the southern estuaries give the continent several identities, but all are joined by water and weather. Stormfall's history describes a society shaped by warning, shelter, and return. Its people expect the sea to change and the mountains to send rain, yet they have found ways to make those certainties the foundation of settlement and trade.
