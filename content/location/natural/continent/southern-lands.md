## The Far Southern Region of Sahrana

The Southern Lands is the name used by northern navigators and some older charts for the far southern region of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). It lies beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), where the desert gives way to rocky plateaus, temperate highlands, and a basin containing the [Greater Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/greater-inland-sea) and the [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea). Northern mapmakers once treated the country as a separate landmass because few travellers crossed the desert and the first reports described the inland seas without a reliable account of the surrounding coast. The records of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), together with later caravan surveys, establish that the region is joined to Sahrana and forms its remote southern quarter.

The Southern Lands are not a single climate or a country without settlement. The northern approach is dry and broken, the highlands receive seasonal rain, and the basins around the inland seas support fields, orchards, woodland, and permanent towns. The region is cooler than the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent), but its winters are short in the lower country and its summers are warm enough for grain, figs, dates, and citrus. The distinction between the Southern Lands and northern Sahrana is therefore geographical and historical rather than continental.

## The Kharesh Border and the Southern Highlands

The southern routes of the Kharesh cross a changing belt of gravel plains, eroded ridges, and seasonal channels before reaching the first highland settlements. Travellers leave the deep dunes at a series of rocky outcrops where springs rise from the lower slopes. These outcrops provide shade, grazing, and places to repair pack equipment before the final ascent. The route is known to the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), whose guides maintain the wells and recognize the signs of rain in the distant hills.

The southern highlands form the outer wall of the inland basins. Their lower slopes carry open woodland of evergreen oak, wild olive, carob, and scattered thorn trees. Higher ground supports grasses, juniper, and herbs gathered by herders and healers. The valleys retain water after the dry season, and the streams that descend from the ridges feed the inland seas. The highlands are not a continuous barrier. Several passes allow caravans and herds to move between the desert and the basin, though the roads are steep and vulnerable to rockfall after heavy rain.

The communities of the highlands maintain seasonal holdings rather than large towns. Families keep sheep, goats, and pack animals, moving between sheltered winter ground and summer pasture. Stone and turf shelters stand near the principal springs, and customary law requires that the water be kept available to travellers and neighbouring herds. Highland wool, leather, dried herbs, and small quantities of copper are exchanged at the gateway settlement of [Torem](/content/location/settlement/town/torem), where caravans from the north first meet the settled communities of the Southern Reach.

## The Inland Seas and Their Basins

Beyond the highlands the land descends into a broad basin whose rivers collect in two connected inland seas. The [Greater Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/greater-inland-sea) occupies the northern part of the basin and receives the largest rivers from the surrounding hills. Its shores contain reed beds, fishing villages, grain fields, and the city of [Vereth](/content/location/settlement/town/vereth), the seat of the Southern Reach council. The lake is navigable throughout most of the year, and its boats carry passengers and cargo between settlements that would be difficult to reach by road.

The [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea) lies south of the greater water beyond a low saddle of cultivated land. It is smaller and warmer, with broad alluvial fans along its shores. [Ashaar](/content/location/settlement/town/ashaar) is its principal port and the centre of the southern basin's fishing, orchards, and boatbuilding. The lesser sea receives shorter rivers, but those rivers remain dependable in most years, and the surrounding soil supports two harvests of grain where water rights are carefully observed.

The inland seas are fresh, though the channels connecting their wetlands become shallow during the driest months. Fishers use weirs, nets, and shallow boats suited to calm water and frequent mudbanks. Each lakeside community marks breeding grounds and closes them during the spawning season. The rules are administered by local councils and confirmed by the federation, because a failure to protect one reed bed or inlet affects the fishery of the entire basin. The tortolan communities of the southern shore are among the most knowledgeable keepers of these records.

## Woodland, Fields, and Orchards

The lower basin is a mosaic of cultivated fields, open woodland, pasture, and marsh. Wheat and barley grow on the deeper alluvial soils, while millet and sorghum are planted on the drier margins. Figs, dates, citrus, and olives are grown around the settlements, where household wells and channels supplement the seasonal rains. Farmers maintain raised granaries and stone-lined channels, and the villages keep reserve stores against years in which the highland rains fail.

The woodland is managed rather than untouched. Families gather fuelwood from marked districts, leave seed trees beside the streams, and protect the groves that prevent the soil from blowing toward the lake. The [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) councils issue rules for cutting timber above the watercourses, since the loss of the upper woodland increases silt in the rivers and shortens the life of the irrigation channels. The same rules protect the carob and olive trees whose fruit provides food and oil during the dry season.

Wildlife includes deer, antelope, foxes, wild asses, and the smaller cats that hunt among the scrub. Water birds gather in the shallows of both seas, and the reed beds provide nesting places for cranes, herons, and ducks. The upper valleys support eagles and mountain goats. The region's fauna differs from that of the deep Kharesh but is not wholly separate from it; animals follow the seasonal watercourses across the boundary, and the people of the two environments exchange knowledge of their movements.

## The Southern Reach Federation

The Southern Reach is the political federation of the Southern Lands. Its communities retain authority over their internal affairs, while representatives meeting at Vereth coordinate water management, defence, fisheries, and trade with the north. The federation is not a kingdom and has no single hereditary ruler. Each community chooses its representatives according to local custom, and major decisions require ratification by the member settlements. The arrangement is slow when the communities disagree, but it prevents any one lakeside town from controlling the water on which the others depend.

The council meets in spring and autumn, when the roads and lake routes are open. Its records include water allocations, fishing closures, caravan tolls, repairs to roads and bridges, and agreements with the highland herders. Sessions are open to citizens, and the archives at Vereth preserve many generations of decisions. Northern scholars sometimes describe the federation as isolated, but its institutions are connected to the wider Sahrani trade through the Vashari cities and the guides of the Kharesh.

Humans are the principal population of the region, living as farmers, fishers, herders, boatbuilders, and merchants. [Tortolans](/content/species/sapient/tortolans) maintain several shoreline communities and hold recognised fishing rights in exchange for their participation in water planning. [Salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) travellers from the desert regularly reach the southern highlands and carry news between the federation and the Kharesh tribes. The groups have distinct customs, but the economy of the Southern Lands depends upon their cooperation.

## The Southern Trade

Trade with northern Sahrana is limited by the length of the desert crossing, not by a lack of useful goods. The Southern Lands send wool, leather, preserved fish, citrus, figs, dates, glazed pottery, and dried herbs northward. They receive grain in poor harvest years, salt, cloth, iron tools, glass, and manufactured goods from the coastal cities. Most caravans pass through the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), whose inland cities provide staging grounds and arrange guides for the final desert route.

The inland seas also support a regional trade independent of the desert road. Boats carry grain, fruit, fish, and pottery between Vereth, Ashaar, and the smaller settlements, while pack trains connect the lake ports to the highland pastures. The boatyards of Ashaar build broad, shallow vessels with low masts, and the craft is shared between human and tortolan builders. A vessel may serve for decades when its timber is kept dry and its hull is repaired before the first winter rains.

## A Region Once Misdrawn

The name Southern Lands remains in use because it records the history of northern uncertainty. Early charts showed the region as a blank southern coast or as a separate continent reached by dangerous voyages. Later mapmakers copied those outlines, and the name survived even after caravans, lake pilots, and the archives of Vereth demonstrated the connection to Sahrana. The oldest accounts are still valuable for their observations of local plants, animals, and customs, but their continental classification is no longer accepted.

The Southern Lands are therefore a region of Sahrana with a distinctive climate, political tradition, and inland-water economy. Their distance from the northern coast has preserved customs that differ from those of the Green Crescent, while the Kharesh routes and the Southern Reach council connect them to the rest of the continent. The region's geography is best understood through that connection: desert to highland, highland to basin, and basin to the two inland seas that sustain the communities of the far south.
