## Mountains Define Every Feature

Morvind lies to the north of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) across the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea). It is a rugged and mountainous continent, its interior dominated by ranges that hold their snow well into the spring. The coastal lowlands are narrow but support fishing and trading settlements that have endured for centuries, their harbours sheltered by the same cliffs that protect them from the open ocean. Morvind's mineral wealth has long drawn prospectors and traders, and the continent's reputation for fine metalwork is recognised throughout the known world. Though smaller and less varied than Aethelar, Morvind has a character shaped by the mountains that define its every feature.

## Labyrinth of Valleys and High Passes

The interior ranges of Morvind form a labyrinth of valleys and high passes, many of which become impassable during the winter months but reopen with the thaw. Snow lies deep upon the higher slopes for much of the year, and the passes are navigable only during the brief but vivid summer, when the alpine meadows burst into flower and the streams run heavy with meltwater. Between the peaks lie sheltered plateaus where close-knit communities tend flocks of mountain sheep and cultivate cold-resistant crops during the bright growing season. These plateaus are isolated from one another by the ridges that separate them, and each has developed its own distinct customs and dialects.

The lower slopes of the mountains are clothed in coniferous forest, dense and dark, which thins toward the summits and gives way to bare rock and scree. These forests provide timber and game for the upland communities and shelter a variety of wildlife adapted to the cold. The trees are among the hardiest in the known world, capable of surviving the deep freezes and short growing seasons that characterise the Morvind interior, and their wood is prized for its density and straight grain.

The rivers that descend from Morvind's heights are swift and clear, fed by glacial meltwater, and they carve deep gorges through the rock before reaching the sea. These waterways have historically provided the most reliable routes inland, and settlements along their banks remain the most populous on the continent. The largest of these rivers is navigable for much of its length, and its valley forms the principal corridor between the coast and the interior, carrying goods, people, and news between the two.

## Narrow Shores and Deep Inlets

The coastal lowlands of Morvind are narrow, seldom extending more than a few miles inland before the mountains rise. The shore is marked by high cliffs and deep inlets, and the harbours that nestle among them are well sheltered from the storms that sweep across the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) during the autumn and winter. The coastal communities are fishing and trading peoples, and their contact with the sea has shaped every aspect of their lives, from their diet to their architecture to their oral traditions.

The climate of the coast is milder than that of the interior, warmed by the waters of the Stormfall and sheltered from the worst of the mountain weather. Fog is common in the autumn, and the long winter nights are lit at times by the aurora that hangs over the northern seas, a phenomenon regarded with reverence and woven into the oldest traditions. The short but intense summers are a time of feverish activity, as the coastal communities fish, trade, and prepare for the long months when the sea may be impassable. The waters off the southern coast are among the most productive fishing grounds in the known world, with cod, herring, and salmon supporting both coastal villages and the trade that carries preserved fish to the markets of Aethelar.

## Dwarven Halls and Human Harbours

[Dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) are the most numerous of the sapient peoples of Morvind, and the continent holds the greatest concentration of dwarven population in the known world. Their settlements are carved into the mountains of the interior, where vast underground networks offer stable temperatures, refuge from the weather above, and access to the ores and stone that dwarven life requires. The dwarven halls of Morvind are the grandest and most extensive of their kind, and their craftsmanship in metal and stone is the foundation of the continent's wealth and reputation.

[Humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) settle the coastal lowlands, where the climate is milder than the interior uplands. Their communities are oriented toward the sea, and their livelihoods depend upon fishing, trade, and the transport of dwarven goods to the markets of Aethelar. The humans of Morvind are hardy and self-reliant, accustomed to the long winters and the demands of a coast that offers little arable land, and their maritime skills are among the finest in the known world.

Smaller populations of [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) are found in the temperate uplands, where they dwell in the sheltered valleys and lower slopes of the interior ranges. These folk have grown stockier and more tolerant of cold than their Aethelaran kin, and their communities are small and secluded. [Orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) are present in the remote highlands and cold steppes of the far interior, where few other peoples venture. [Lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines) inhabit the cold steppes of the far interior, where they hunt and herd across the open plains, trading furs and meat to the valley settlements for goods that cannot be produced on the steppe.

## Mineral Wealth and the Valdmark

The economy of Morvind is built upon the mineral wealth of the interior and the maritime trade of the coast, connected by the rivers and overland routes that link the mountains to the sea. The metalwork and stone of the dwarven cities are the primary exports of the continent, and the weapons, tools, and decorative objects produced in the dwarven forges are traded to every continent. Grain, cloth, and manufactured goods are imported from the south, for the interior of Morvind cannot produce sufficient food to sustain its population, and the coastal lowlands offer too little arable land to meet the demand.

The [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) is the primary agricultural region of the interior. The grain produced in the river valley of the Valdmark makes a significant contribution to the food supply of the continent. Trade between the Valdmark and the dwarven cities of the interior is conducted through overland routes that cross the mountain passes, with grain and wool exchanged for metalwork and tools. Trade between the interior and the coast flows through the rivers, and the barges that carry the goods of the interior to the coast are a common sight on the waterways during the navigable season.

## Cornerstone of Northern Trade

Morvind's mineral wealth and the skill of its metalworkers have made it a cornerstone of trade in the northern world. The continent's ores and crafted goods flow south across the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) to Aethelar and beyond. The [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), which connects the Stormfall Sea to the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), lies along the southeastern coast of Morvind and has long been a point of strategic and commercial importance, as the wealth of the continent must pass through this narrow channel to reach the markets of the south and east. The relationship between the coastal ports and the dwarven interior is one of mutual dependence that has shaped the political and economic character of the entire continent.
