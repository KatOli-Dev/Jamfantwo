## The Greatest Dwarven Hall of Morvind

Stoneheim is the greatest [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) hall of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the capital of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), the largest dwarven realm in the known world. The city is carved into the living rock of the interior ranges, and its halls and chambers extend for many miles into the mountains, forming a subterranean complex that is the most extensive and magnificent of any dwarven settlement. Stoneheim is the centre of dwarven civilisation in [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and its wealth, craftsmanship, and political influence are unmatched by any other dwarven community.

## The Cavernous Chambers and the Carved Dwellings

The halls of Stoneheim are the grandest of their kind. The great hall is a cavernous space supported by pillars of carved stone, each a masterpiece of dwarven engineering. This hall is the heart of the city, and the forges, council chambers, and oldest dwellings are all situated within it. The chambers and passages that branch from the hall extend in every direction, following the veins of ore and natural cave systems that the dwarves have shaped and enlarged over centuries.

The dwellings of Stoneheim are carved into the walls of the halls and passages, and the deepest and most ancient are those of the oldest families, who have inhabited the city the longest and hold the most prestigious positions in the community. Deeper dwellings are warmer, for the rock of the mountain retains a constant temperature, and the deepest chambers are those closest to the forges and mines, where the heat of molten metal and the labour of the smiths warm the stone.

The arrangement of the halls follows a logic peculiar to dwarven engineering. The chambers are not laid out in a grid but are shaped by the rock of the mountain and the veins of ore that the dwarves have followed. The deepest halls are the oldest, carved in the earliest centuries of the settlement, and the newer chambers are higher in the mountain, where the rock is easier to work and the ventilation is better. The passages connecting the halls are wide and well-lit, for the dwarves use the lanterns of the forges and the glow of molten metal to illuminate the interior, and ventilation shafts driven through the rock to the surface provide a constant flow of fresh air.

## The Ore Veins and the Great Forges

The mining operations of Stoneheim are the most extensive of any dwarven city. The ore extracted from the depths beneath the city includes iron, copper, tin, silver, gold, and the rarer metals found only in the deepest veins of the Morvind ranges. The mines extend for miles below the city, and the tunnels connecting them to the halls above are works of engineering in their own right, wide enough for the passage of ore carts and ventilated by shafts driven through the rock to the surface.

The metalwork produced in the forges of Stoneheim is the finest in the known world, and the smiths of the city are renowned for their skill in working iron, steel, and precious metals. The weapons, tools, and decorative objects produced in Stoneheim are exported to every continent, and the reputation of the city's smiths is the foundation of the wealth and influence of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds).

The forges of Stoneheim are divided into the great forges and the lesser forges, a distinction of function as well as scale. The great forges are situated in the great hall and devoted to smelting ore and producing bulk metals that are the foundation of the city's trade. The lesser forges are situated in side chambers and passages, devoted to the finer work of the smiths: crafting weapons, tools, and decorative objects that are the most valued products of the city. The master smiths of Stoneheim are the most respected figures in the community, and their workshops are the training grounds for apprentices who will carry the craft forward into the next generation.

## The Ancient Carving of the Mountain

The founding of Stoneheim is lost in the depths of dwarven history, for the city was carved from the mountain in an age before human chronicles began. The dwarven records of the founding are preserved in inscriptions cut into the walls of the deepest halls, recording the names of the first kings and master smiths and the dates of the carving of the great hall and the opening of the first mines. The city has been inhabited continuously since its founding, and the dwarven families that hold the deepest dwellings are the descendants of the original settlers who carved the first chambers from the rock.

The history of Stoneheim is a history of expansion. The city has grown steadily over the centuries as the dwarves have carved new halls and opened new mines. The great hall was the first chamber to be carved and remains the heart of the city, but the chambers and passages branching from it have extended the city for many miles into the mountain. The expansion has been guided by the rock of the range, for the dwarves have followed the veins of ore and natural cave systems, and the city is as much a work of the rock as of engineering.

## Capital of the Stoneheim Holds

Stoneheim is the capital of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), the dwarven realm encompassing the interior ranges of Morvind, and the city is the seat of the king and the council that govern the realm. The population of Stoneheim is almost entirely [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), with a small community of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) involved in the trade between the city and the coast, and a few [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) drawn by the metalwork and craftsmanship for which the city is known.

## Metalwork Caravans and the Coastal Markets

The trade of Stoneheim is the most valuable of any dwarven city. The metalwork produced in its forges is prized in every continent, and the demand for its weapons, tools, and decorative objects is constant. The trade is conducted through the mountain passes connecting the interior ranges to the coast, and the caravans carrying Stoneheim's metalwork to the markets of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and beyond are a familiar sight on the highland routes. The trade is managed by the merchant guilds of the city, and the guilds are among the most influential institutions in the community, for the wealth flowing through the trade is the lifeblood of the economy of the Holds.

The humans who reside in Stoneheim are the agents of the trading houses of the coast, and their function is to aid exchange between the dwarven smiths and the human markets. The humans are a small but valued community, and their presence in the city is a sign of the importance of the trade connecting the interior to the coast. The gnomes of Stoneheim are drawn by the craftsmanship of the smiths, and the gnomish artisans are among the most skilled workers of finer metals and decorative objects, a valued part of the city's output.

## The King and the Council of Families

Stoneheim is governed by the king and the council. The council is composed of representatives of the great families, the master smiths, and the merchant guilds. The king is the hereditary ruler of the Stoneheim Holds, and his authority is unquestioned in matters of defence and foreign policy. The council is responsible for governing the city and managing the mines, forges, and trade that are the foundation of the economy of the realm. The council meets in the council chamber situated in the great hall, the most ornate of the public spaces in the city, its walls carved with inscriptions recording the history and laws of the dwarven people.

## The Centre of Dwarven Civilisation

Stoneheim is the greatest dwarven city in the known world, and its mineral wealth, craftsmanship, and political influence are the foundation of the economy and culture of the dwarven people of Morvind. The city is the centre of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), the largest dwarven realm, and its halls are the most extensive and magnificent of any subterranean settlement. The prosperity of Stoneheim is built upon the wealth of the mountains and the skill of its people, and the city is a symbol of the endurance and achievement of the dwarven civilisation.
