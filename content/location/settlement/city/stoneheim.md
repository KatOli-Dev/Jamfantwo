## Overview

Stoneheim is the greatest [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) hall of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the capital of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), the largest dwarven realm in the known world. The city is carved into the living rock of the interior ranges, and its halls and chambers extend for many miles into the mountains, forming a subterranean complex that is the most extensive and the most magnificent of any dwarven settlement. Stoneheim is the centre of dwarven civilisation in Morvind, and its wealth, its craftsmanship, and its political influence are unmatched by any other dwarven community.

## The Halls

The halls of Stoneheim are the grandest of their kind, and the great hall is a cavernous space that is supported by pillars of carved stone, each one a masterpiece of dwarven engineering. The hall is the heart of the city, and the forges, the council chambers, and the oldest dwellings are all situated within it. The chambers and passages that branch from the hall extend in every direction, following the veins of ore and the natural cave systems that the dwarves have shaped and enlarged over centuries.

The dwellings of Stoneheim are carved into the walls of the halls and the passages, and the deepest and the most ancient are those of the oldest families, who have inhabited the city for the longest and who hold the most prestigious positions in the community. The deeper dwellings are warmer, for the rock of the mountain retains a constant temperature, and the deepest chambers are those that are closest to the forges and the mines, where the heat of the molten metal and the labour of the smiths warm the stone.

The arrangement of the halls follows a logic that is peculiar to dwarven engineering, for the chambers are not laid out in a grid but are shaped by the geology of the mountain and the veins of ore that the dwarves have followed. The deepest halls are the oldest, carved in the earliest centuries of the settlement, and the newer chambers are higher in the mountain, where the rock is easier to work and the ventilation is better. The passages that connect the halls are wide and well-lit, for the dwarves use the lanterns of the forges and the glow of the molten metal to illuminate the interior of the mountain, and the ventilation shafts that are driven through the rock to the surface above provide a constant flow of fresh air.

## Mining and Metalwork

The mining operations of Stoneheim are the most extensive of any dwarven city, and the ore that is extracted from the depths beneath the city includes iron, copper, tin, silver, gold, and the rarer metals that are found only in the deepest veins of the Morvind ranges. The mines extend for miles below the city, and the tunnels that connect them to the halls above are works of engineering in their own right, wide enough for the passage of ore carts and ventilated by shafts that are driven through the rock to the surface above.

The metalwork that is produced in the forges of Stoneheim is the finest in the known world, and the smiths of the city are renowned for their skill in the working of iron, steel, and the precious metals. The weapons, the tools, and the decorative objects that are produced in Stoneheim are exported to every continent, and the reputation of the city's smiths is the foundation of the wealth and the influence of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds).

The forges of Stoneheim are divided into the great forges and the lesser forges, and the distinction is one of function as well as scale. The great forges are situated in the great hall, and they are devoted to the smelting of the ore and the production of the bulk metals that are the foundation of the city's trade. The lesser forges are situated in the side chambers and the passages, and they are devoted to the finer work of the smiths, the crafting of the weapons, the tools, and the decorative objects that are the most valued products of the city. The master smiths of Stoneheim are the most respected figures in the community, and their workshops are the training grounds for the apprentices who will carry the craft forward into the next generation.

## History and the Foundation

The founding of Stoneheim is lost in the depths of dwarven history, for the city was carved from the mountain in an age before the human chronicles began, and the dwarven records of the founding are preserved in the inscriptions that are cut into the walls of the deepest halls. The inscriptions record the names of the first kings and the first master smiths, and the dates of the carving of the great hall and the opening of the first mines. The city has been inhabited continuously since its founding, and the dwarven families that hold the deepest dwellings are the descendants of the original settlers who carved the first chambers from the rock.

The history of Stoneheim is a history of expansion, for the city has grown steadily over the centuries as the dwarves have carved new halls and opened new mines. The great hall was the first chamber to be carved, and it remains the heart of the city, but the chambers and passages that branch from it have extended the city for many miles into the mountain. The expansion has been guided by the geology of the range, for the dwarves have followed the veins of ore and the natural cave systems that the mountain provides, and the city is as much a work of geology as of engineering.

## The City and the Realm

Stoneheim is the capital of the Stoneheim Holds, the dwarven realm that encompasses the interior ranges of Morvind, and the city is the seat of the king and the council that govern the realm. The population of Stoneheim is almost entirely [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), with a small community of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) who are involved in the trade between the city and the coast, and a few [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) who are drawn by the metalwork and the craftsmanship for which the city is known.

## Trade and the Exterior

The trade of Stoneheim is the most valuable of any dwarven city, for the metalwork that is produced in its forges is prized in every continent, and the demand for the weapons, the tools, and the decorative objects of the city is constant. The trade is conducted through the mountain passes that connect the interior ranges to the coast, and the caravans that carry the metalwork of Stoneheim to the markets of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and beyond are a familiar sight on the highland routes. The trade is managed by the merchant guilds of the city, and the guilds are among the most influential institutions in the community, for the wealth that flows through the trade is the lifeblood of the economy of the Holds.

The humans who reside in Stoneheim are the agents of the trading houses of the coast, and their function is to facilitate the exchange between the dwarven smiths and the human markets. The humans are a small but valued community, and their presence in the city is a sign of the importance of the trade that connects the interior to the coast. The gnomes of Stoneheim are drawn by the craftsmanship of the smiths, and the gnomish artisans are among the most skilled workers of the finer metals and the decorative objects that are a valued part of the output of the city.

## Governance and the Council

Stoneheim is governed by the king and the council, and the council is composed of the representatives of the great families, the master smiths, and the merchant guilds. The king is the hereditary ruler of the Stoneheim Holds, and his authority is unquestioned in matters of defence and foreign policy. The council is responsible for the governance of the city and the management of the mines, the forges, and the trade that is the foundation of the economy of the realm. The council meets in the council chamber that is situated in the great hall, and the chamber is the most ornate of the public spaces in the city, its walls carved with the inscriptions that record the history and the laws of the dwarven people.

## Significance

Stoneheim is the greatest dwarven city in the known world, and its mineral wealth, its craftsmanship, and its political influence are the foundation of the economy and the culture of the dwarven people of Morvind. The city is the centre of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), the largest dwarven realm, and its halls are the most extensive and the most magnificent of any subterranean settlement. The prosperity of Stoneheim is built upon the wealth of the mountains and the skill of its people, and the city is a symbol of the endurance and the achievement of the dwarven civilisation.
