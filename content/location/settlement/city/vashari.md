## An Inland City at the Desert's Edge

Vashari is an inland city-state of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), situated at the terminus of the great caravan routes that cross the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) from the coastal cities to the interior. The city stands at the edge of the desert, where the caravan routes emerge from the sands and meet the foothills of the interior highlands, and its position at the meeting of desert and highland has made it the primary hub of the inland trade connecting the coast to the interior. Vashari's wealth is built upon the trade flowing through its markets and the caravans passing through its gates.

## The Great Desert Routes and the Caravan Masters

The caravan routes that converge on Vashari are the great trade routes of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and the caravans travelling them are the primary means of commerce between the coastal cities and the interior. The routes cross the desert from the northern coast to the interior, following paths established over centuries of travel, and they are navigated by experienced guides who know the desert and its water sources. The caravans arriving at Vashari carry the spices, dyes, and precious stones of the interior, and they depart with the grain, cloth, and manufactured goods of the coast.

The caravan trade is the primary commerce of the city, and the caravans passing through its gates are the lifeblood of the inland economy. The merchants of Vashari are skilled in managing the caravan trade, organising transport, provisioning, and exchange of goods between the coast and the interior, and the caravan routes connecting Vashari to the coastal cities are among the most valuable trade routes in [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The city is also a centre for breeding and training camels and pack animals used by the caravans, and the stables and corrals of Vashari are among the largest in the known world.

## The Springs, Aqueducts, and Underground Reservoirs

The position of Vashari at the edge of the desert and the foothills of the interior highlands gives the city access to the water that is the most precious resource in the region. The springs emerging from the base of the highlands provide a reliable supply of water that sustains the city and the caravans passing through it, and the water is managed carefully, for it is the foundation of the city's survival and prosperity. The gardens and orchards cultivated around Vashari, using water from the springs, produce fruits and vegetables that supplement the diet of the community and the provisions of the caravans.

The system of water management at Vashari is among the most sophisticated in Sahrana. Water flowing from the springs is channelled through a network of underground aqueducts and reservoirs that distribute it to the gardens, households, and caravan watering stations. The construction of the aqueducts is attributed to the early generations of the city's founders, who recognised that water must be managed if the city were to endure, and the system has been maintained and expanded over the centuries. Water is allocated according to a system of rights governed by the city council, and distribution is overseen by officials who ensure fairness and prevent waste.

## Light-Coloured Stone, Clay Walls, and the Market Square

Vashari is a substantial, well-built city whose architecture reflects the character of the inland region, with buildings of light-coloured stone and sun-baked clay, thick walls, and narrow streets that provide shade and shelter from the heat and wind. The city is walled, for the desert is a source of danger as well as trade, and the gates are guarded and controlled, for the caravans entering and leaving the city are the primary source of its wealth. The market square of Vashari is one of the largest in the inland region, and the market is a daily affair, for the constant traffic of the caravans ensures a steady flow of goods and customers.

The population of Vashari is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a significant community of [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) involved in the caravan trade and the guidance of the desert routes. The relationship between the settled humans and the nomadic salawa is one of mutual dependence, for the salawa provide the knowledge of the desert that the caravans require, and the city provides the goods and market that the salawa need.

## The Council of Merchants and the Vashari Pact

Vashari is governed by a council of merchants and landowners elected from the prominent families of the city. The council meets in a hall near the market square, at the centre of the commercial district, and oversees the regulation of the market, maintenance of the walls and water system, collection of tolls and taxes levied on the caravans, and administration of justice. The chairman of the council is chosen by the members from among their number, and the term of office is fixed at three years, ensuring that governance is shared among the leading families and that no single interest dominates.

The city is the leading member of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the alliance of inland caravan cities at the desert's edge, and the council of Vashari plays a prominent role in its governance. The pact co-ordinates the caravan routes, water rights, and defence of the inland cities, and the council of Vashari is the primary voice in determining the pact's policies. The relationship between the city and the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) is managed through the pact, and co-operation between the settled cities and nomadic peoples is the foundation of the caravan trade that sustains the inland economy.

## The Primary Hub of the Inland Caravan Trade

Vashari is the primary hub of the inland caravan trade of Sahrana, and its position at the terminus of the great caravan routes gives it a commercial importance unmatched by any other inland city. The city is the leading member of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the alliance of inland caravan cities at the desert's edge, and its trade is a vital component of the economy of Sahrana. The significance of Vashari lies in its control of the caravan trade, the primary means of commerce between the coast and the interior, and the city is a symbol of the wealth and endurance of the inland cities. The resilience of Vashari, sustained by its careful management of water and its position on the trade routes, has ensured its survival and prosperity in the demanding environment of the desert edge.
