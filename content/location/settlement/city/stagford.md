## Overview

Stagford is a central city of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), situated at a major crossing of one of the principal rivers of the central grasslands. The city stands where the river is at its narrowest, and the bridge that spans the water at this point is the finest and the most heavily trafficked in the central plains. Stagford is a prosperous, well-positioned city, and its control of the river crossing has made it a significant node in the trade network of the grasslands, for the roads that cross the river at this point connect the northern and southern portions of the plain.

## The River and the Bridge

The river that flows past Stagford is one of the great waterways of the central grasslands, and the crossing at Stagford is the most important point of passage on the river, for the bridge that spans the water is the only stone bridge on the lower reaches, and the ford that preceded it has been a crossing point since the earliest days of human settlement in the region. The bridge is a substantial structure, wide enough for two wagons to pass, and it is supported by piers that are sunk deep into the riverbed, a feat of engineering that was accomplished with the assistance of [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) stonemasons.

The tolls on the bridge are a significant source of the city's revenue, and the traffic that crosses the river at Stagford includes the grain wagons of the agricultural regions, the trading caravans that connect the northern and southern plains, and the livestock that is driven to the markets of the south. The river is also navigable, and the barges that carry goods up and down the waterway call at the wharves of Stagford, where the river trade and the road trade meet and exchange. The wharves are situated downstream of the bridge, where the river broadens and the current slows, and the barges are loaded and unloaded by the stevedores who work the quays from dawn to dusk.

The bridge is maintained by a dedicated corps of masons and labourers who are employed by the city, and the upkeep of the structure is a constant concern, for the river is prone to flooding in the spring, and the piers are subject to the scouring of the current. The bridge has been repaired and strengthened many times over the centuries, and the masons of Stagford are skilled in the techniques of underwater construction that are required for the maintenance of the piers.

## Trade and the City

The trade of Stagford is a mixture of the agricultural products of the surrounding plain and the manufactured goods that are imported from the coast and the interior. The city is a centre for the livestock trade, and the stockyards of Stagford are among the largest in the grasslands, for the cattle and the sheep that are raised on the pasture of the plain are gathered at the city for sale and for transport to the markets of the coast. The name of the city is derived from the stags that were once driven across the ford in great numbers, and the deer of the grasslands are still a valued commodity in the markets of Stagford. The venison and the hides of the deer are prized, and the hunters who supply them are a recognised part of the commercial life of the city.

The granaries of Stagford are a significant feature of the city, for the grain that is produced on the surrounding farms is gathered and stored before it is transported to the markets of the coast. The granaries line the riverbank beside the wharves, and the grain is loaded directly from the stores onto the barges that carry it downstream. The management of the granaries is a skilled business, for the grain must be kept dry and free from the pests that can destroy a stored harvest, and the granary keepers of Stagford are experienced in the techniques of preservation that are required to maintain the quality of the grain.

The city itself is well-built and prosperous, with broad streets and substantial buildings that reflect the wealth of the surrounding agricultural region. The market square is the centre of the city, and the market that is held there is a weekly affair that draws farmers, traders, and craftsmen from the surrounding country. The population of Stagford is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a community of [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) who are regular visitors to the market and the stockyards. The centaur kindreds of the central grasslands are the most frequent visitors to the city, and the trade in livestock and wool is the basis of the relationship between the settled and the nomadic peoples of the plain.

## Significance

Stagford is the primary river crossing of the central grasslands, and its bridge and its roads connect the northern and southern portions of the plain. The city is a significant member of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), and its trade in grain, livestock, and manufactured goods is a valuable component of the economy of the grasslands. The prosperity of Stagford is built upon its position at the crossing, and the city has grown and thrived on the traffic that flows over its bridge and along its roads.

## History

Stagford was founded at the ford that gave the city its name, and the crossing was the original reason for the settlement. The first inhabitants were the herdsmen who drove their stags and their cattle across the river, and the ford was the narrowest and the most reliable crossing on the lower reaches of the waterway. The village grew into a town as the traffic across the river increased, and the construction of the bridge in a later age transformed the crossing from a seasonal passage to a year-round route. The bridge was built with the assistance of dwarven stonemasons from the mountain settlements, and the expertise of the dwarves is evident in the quality of the construction and the durability of the structure.

The growth of the grain trade in the central grasslands drove the expansion of the city, and Stagford became the primary depot for the grain that was produced on the surrounding farms and transported to the markets of the coast. The stockyards and the granaries that line the riverbank are the visible evidence of the prosperity that the trade has brought to the city, and the weekly market is a tradition that dates to the earliest days of the settlement.
