## Overview

Amurra is a wealthy port city-state on the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) and the centre of the spice trade. The city occupies a natural harbour sheltered by a headland near the mouth of a river descending from the interior highlands. Its position has made it the primary port for spices grown in the foothills and upland valleys of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) interior. Amurra's wealth derives entirely from the trade in rare spices found only in Sahrana.

## The Harbour and the Spice Trade

The harbour of Amurra is a deep, sheltered bay protected by a headland extending from the northern end of the city. A lighthouse visible for many miles across the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) marks the approach. The quays line the inner shore of the bay, and the waterfront warehouses hold the spices that are the city's primary export. The spice trade of Amurra is the most valuable single commerce in the known world, as Sahrani interior spices are prized on every continent and demand is constant and growing.

The spices that pass through Amurra include pepper, cinnamon, clove, and other aromatic varieties cultivated in the foothills and upland valleys of the Sahrani interior. Caravans travelling the river valley from the interior bring the spices to the city, where they are loaded onto vessels for transport to the markets of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). Return cargoes of grain, iron, and manufactured goods from the northern continents complete the primary commerce of the city.

The management of the spice trade is the central concern of Amurra's government and merchant community. The guild of spice merchants, whose members are the most respected figures in the commercial community, conducts the grading and sorting of spices in the city's warehouses. Grading is a precise and demanding skill: quality is determined by aroma, colour, and texture, and the highest grades command many times the price of ordinary grades. Amurra's reputation for reliable grading is critical to buyer trust, and the guild enforces its standards rigorously. The guild maintains a public register of approved graders and publishes a yearly schedule of standard prices, a practice that has stabilised the market and reduced disputes between buyers and sellers.

## The River and the Caravans

The river connecting Amurra to the interior is the primary route of the spice trade. Caravans travelling its valley bring spices from upland gardens and foothill plantations, a journey of many days through the foothills and river valley. The merchants of Amurra organise the transport, provisioning, and exchange of goods between coast and interior, and the caravan routes are among the most valuable trade routes in Sahrana. A typical caravan consists of several dozen pack animals and wagons, accompanied by armed guards who protect against the bandits that occasionally raid the river valley during periods of drought.

Waystations at intervals along the river valley provide resting places, water, and supplies for the caravans. Maintained by Amurra's merchants, their upkeep is a significant expense of the caravan trade. Each waystation is staffed by a keeper who manages the stores and reports on the condition of the road and the movement of traffic. The system of waystations has been refined over centuries, with stations spaced at intervals that correspond to a single day's travel for a laden caravan.

## History and the Republic

Amurra's founding dates to the early centuries of Sahrani coastal settlement, when the natural harbour was first recognised as a valuable port for exporting interior spices. The earliest settlement was a trading post, and the first permanent buildings were the warehouses and quays serving the spice trade. The city grew rapidly as the value of the spices passing through its harbour attracted merchants and settlers from every coast, and the population swelled within a few generations. A council of merchants governed the city from its earliest days, and this council evolved into the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), the political system that governs the city to the present.

Amurra has never been conquered or besieged. The community has devoted its energies to expanding trade and refining the culture that trade supports. The great lighthouse marking the harbour entrance was completed in 356 and is the most prominent structure in the city, standing on the headland at the northern end of the harbour. The council hall and the exchange building were constructed in the following century. The public buildings of Amurra are among the most refined in Sahrana, built of limestone brought from quarries along the coast and decorated with carved screens that allow sea breezes to circulate through the interior spaces.

## The City

Amurra is a well-built city whose architecture reflects the wealth of the spice trade. Elegant buildings of light-coloured stone, shaded courtyards, and gardens and fountains characterise the Sahrani architectural tradition. The streets are broad and well-paved, laid out in a grid pattern that extends inland from the harbour. The temples, markets, and council halls form the centre of civic life. The residential districts rise in terraces up the slopes behind the harbour, with the larger houses of the merchant families occupying the higher ground where the sea breeze provides relief from the coastal heat. The population is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a diverse community of traders and merchants from every continent. A small quarter near the harbour is home to the families of foreign merchants who maintain permanent residences in the city.

## Architecture and the Gardens

Amurra's architecture is the most refined on the northern coast of Sahrana. Buildings use light-coloured limestone quarried from the headland, with carved screens and lattice windows characteristic of Sahrani tradition. Courtyards form the centre of domestic life, planted with shade trees, flowering shrubs, and fountains that cool the air and conserve the coast's most precious resource. The roofs are flat and edged with carved parapets, a design that allows residents to sleep outdoors during the hottest months.

The gardens of Amurra are famed throughout the known world. The city's wealth has supported the cultivation of exotic plants and ornamental trees brought from the interior and distant coasts. The public gardens along the waterfront are the most extensive, serving as a gathering place and a display of the republic's wealth and refinement. The private gardens of the merchant houses are smaller but no less elaborate, and competition among the houses for beauty and variety is a valued tradition. Each year the republic awards a garland of dried flowers to the household judged to have maintained the finest garden, an honour that is fiercely contested among the merchant families.

## Governance and the Republic

The [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) governs the city through a council of merchants elected by the trading houses and guilds. The council is drawn from the old merchant families of Amurra, a tradition that has never been formally altered. The council meets in the ornate council hall on the waterfront, a building whose central chamber is open to the sea on one side to allow citizens to observe the proceedings. The council is responsible for regulating trade, maintaining the harbour and lighthouse, and managing relations with other cities of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league). It also adjudicates commercial disputes through a dedicated maritime court whose judges are selected from among the senior merchants.

The republic is noted for its commercial pragmatism. The merchants who govern understand that Amurra's prosperity depends on free trade and the maintenance of relationships with suppliers and buyers. The republic maintains ambassadors in every trading city, responsible for negotiating trade agreements and resolving commercial disputes. The diplomatic service is among the most experienced and respected in the known world. Ambassadors serve fixed terms and file regular reports on market conditions, political developments, and the activities of rival trading powers.

## Culture and the Spice

The spice trade has shaped Amurra's culture. The wealth it has brought supports a refined and cosmopolitan way of life. The city's cuisine is the most spiced and varied in Sahrana, as cooks have access to every spice traded in the harbour. Dishes prepared in merchant-house kitchens are famed for their complexity and subtlety, and cooking is a matter of community pride. Public cookshops near the market offer spiced flatbreads, stewed meats, and sweetened pastries to the workers and sailors who crowd the harbour district.

The festivals of Amurra are oriented toward trade and the sea. The most important is the annual spice fair, held in the market square and waterfront at the height of the trading season. It is the largest commercial gathering on the northern coast of Sahrana, drawing merchants and buyers from every continent. The fair includes feasting, entertainment, processions, music, and performances. A ceremonial tasting opens the fair, during which the head of the spice guild publicly grades the first shipment of the season, a tradition that dates back to the earliest days of the republic.

## Significance

Amurra is the centre of the spice trade and one of the wealthiest cities in Sahrana. It is governed by the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), noted for its merchant-led governance, and is a leading member of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league). The city's significance lies in its control of the spice trade, the most valuable single commerce in the known world, and it stands as a symbol of the wealth and refinement of the Sahrani coast.
