## Overview

Amurra is a wealthy port city-state on the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), renowned throughout the known world as the centre of the spice trade. The city stands on a natural harbour that is sheltered by a headland of rock, and its position on the coast near the mouth of a river that descends from the interior highlands has made it the primary port for the export of the spices that are grown in the foothills and the upland valleys of the Sahrani interior. Amurra is a prosperous, cosmopolitan city, and its wealth is built upon the trade in the rare and the valuable spices that are found only in Sahrana.

## The Harbour and the Spice Trade

The harbour of Amurra is a deep, sheltered bay that is protected by the headland that extends from the northern end of the city, and the approach is marked by a lighthouse that is visible for many miles across the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The quays line the inner shore of the bay, and the warehouses that are situated along the waterfront are filled with the spices that are the city's primary export. The spice trade of Amurra is the most valuable single trade in the known world, for the spices that are grown in the Sahrani interior are prized in every continent, and the demand for them is constant and growing.

The spices that pass through Amurra include the pepper, the cinnamon, the clove, and the other aromatic spices that are cultivated in the foothills and the upland valleys of the Sahrani interior, and the trade in these spices is the foundation of the city's wealth. The spices are brought to the city by the caravans that travel the river valley from the interior, and they are loaded onto vessels at the harbour for transport to the markets of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The return cargo includes the grain, the iron, and the manufactured goods of the northern continents, and the exchange of spices and northern goods is the primary commerce of the city.

The spice trade is the most valuable single commerce in the known world, and the management of the trade is the central concern of the government and the merchant community of Amurra. The spices are graded and sorted in the warehouses of the city, and the grading is conducted by the guild of the spice merchants, whose members are the most respected figures in the commercial community. The grading of the spices is a precise and a demanding skill, for the quality of the spice is determined by the aroma, the colour, and the texture, and the highest grades command prices that are many times those of the ordinary grades. The reputation of Amurra for the reliability of its grading is a critical component of the trust that the buyers place in the trade, and the guild of the spice merchants is rigorous in its enforcement of the standards.

## The River and the Caravans

The river that connects Amurra to the interior is the primary route of the spice trade, and the caravans that travel its valley are the lifeline of the city. The caravans bring the spices from the upland gardens and the foothill plantations where they are grown, and the journey from the interior to the coast is a matter of many days of travel through the foothills and the river valley. The caravan trade is managed by the merchants of Amurra, who organise the transport, the provisioning, and the exchange of goods between the coast and the interior, and the caravan routes that connect Amurra to the interior are among the most valuable trade routes in Sahrana.

The caravan routes are served by the waystations that are situated at intervals along the river valley, and the waystations provide the resting places, the water, and the supplies that the caravans need for the journey. The waystations are maintained by the merchants of Amurra, and the cost of their upkeep is a significant expense of the caravan trade. The journey from the interior to the coast is a matter of many days of travel through the foothills and the river valley, and the caravans are composed of the pack animals and the wagons that are suited to the terrain and the climate of the Sahrani interior.

## History and the Republic

The founding of Amurra is dated to the early centuries of the Sahrani coastal settlement, when the natural harbour was first recognised as a valuable port for the export of the spices of the interior. The early settlement was a trading post, and the first permanent buildings were the warehouses and the quays that served the spice trade. The growth of the city was rapid, for the value of the spices that passed through its harbour attracted merchants and settlers from every coast, and the population of Amurra swelled within a few generations. The city was governed by a council of merchants from its earliest days, and the council evolved into the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), the political system that governs the city to the present day.

The history of Amurra is a history of commercial prosperity, for the city has never been conquered or besieged, and the community has devoted its energies to the expansion of the trade and the refinement of the culture that the trade supports. The construction of the great lighthouse that marks the entrance to the harbour was undertaken in the year 356, and the lighthouse is the most prominent structure in the city, its beam visible for many miles across the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The construction of the council hall and the exchange was undertaken in the following century, and the public buildings of Amurra are among the most refined in Sahrana.

## The City

Amurra is a beautiful, well-built city, and its architecture reflects the wealth that the spice trade has brought, with elegant buildings of light-coloured stone, shaded courtyards, and the gardens and fountains that are the hallmark of the Sahrani architectural tradition. The streets are broad and well-paved, and the public buildings are among the most refined in Sahrana, with the temples, the markets, and the council halls that are the centre of the city's life. The population of Amurra is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a diverse community of traders and merchants from every continent.

## Architecture and the Gardens

The architecture of Amurra is the most refined of any city on the northern coast of Sahrana, and the buildings are noted for their elegance and their suitability to the warm, humid climate of the coast. The walls are constructed of the light-coloured limestone that is quarried from the headland, and the facades are decorated with the carved screens and the lattice windows that are the hallmark of the Sahrani architectural tradition. The courtyards are the centre of the domestic life of the city, and the courtyards are planted with the shade trees, the flowering shrubs, and the fountains that cool the air and provide the water that is the most precious resource of the coast.

The gardens of Amurra are famed throughout the known world, for the wealth of the city has supported the cultivation of the exotic plants and the ornamental trees that are brought from the interior and the distant coasts. The public gardens that are situated along the waterfront are the most extensive in the city, and the gardens are a gathering place for the community and a display of the wealth and the refinement of the republic. The private gardens of the merchant houses are smaller but no less elaborate, and the competition between the houses for the beauty and the variety of their gardens is a valued and a vigorous tradition.

## Governance and the Republic

The governance of Amurra is conducted by the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), and the republic is governed by a council of merchants who are elected by the trading houses and the guilds of the city. The council meets in the council hall that is situated on the waterfront, and the hall is the most ornate public building in the city. The council is responsible for the regulation of the trade, the maintenance of the harbour and the lighthouse, and the management of the relations between Amurra and the other cities of the [League of the Northern Coast](/content/government/national/league-of-the-northern-coast).

The republic is noted for its commercial pragmatism, for the merchants who govern the city understand that the prosperity of Amurra depends upon the free flow of trade and the maintenance of the relationships with the suppliers and the buyers who are the foundation of the spice commerce. The republic maintains ambassadors in every city that trades with Amurra, and the ambassadors are responsible for the negotiation of the trade agreements and the resolution of the disputes that arise from the commerce. The diplomatic service of the republic is among the most experienced and the most respected in the known world, and the ambassadors of Amurra are noted for their skill and their discretion.

## Culture and the Spice

The culture of Amurra is shaped by the spice trade, for the wealth that the trade has brought has supported the development of a refined and a cosmopolitan way of life that is the equal of any city in the known world. The cuisine of Amurra is the most spiced and the most varied in Sahrana, for the cooks of the city have access to every spice that is traded in the harbour, and the dishes that are prepared in the kitchens of the merchant houses are famed for their complexity and their subtlety. The cuisine is a matter of pride for the community, and the cooks of Amurra are among the most respected figures in the households of the city.

The festivals of Amurra are oriented toward the trade and the sea, and the most important is the annual spice fair, which is held in the market square and the waterfront at the height of the trading season. The fair is the largest commercial gathering on the northern coast of Sahrana, and the merchants and the buyers who attend it come from every continent. The fair is an occasion of feasting and entertainment as well as commerce, and the processions, the music, and the performances that accompany the fair are a valued part of the cultural life of the city.

## Significance

Amurra is the centre of the spice trade and one of the wealthiest cities in Sahrana, and its prosperity is built upon the rare and valuable spices that are its primary export. The city is governed by the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), a political system that is noted for its merchant-led governance, and it is a leading member of the [League of the Northern Coast](/content/government/national/league-of-the-northern-coast). The significance of Amurra lies in its control of the spice trade, which is the most valuable single commerce in the known world, and the city is a symbol of the wealth and the refinement of the Sahrani coast.
