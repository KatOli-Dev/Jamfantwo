## Overview

Serren is a city of the inland valleys, founded upon a fertile plain where the rivers that descend from the eastern flank of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) converge before turning west toward the [Greywash](/content/location/natural/feature/mouths-of-the-greywash). The plain is one of the most productive of the inland valleys, and the city has been the principal market for the grain and the cattle of the surrounding farms since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The city takes its name from the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way) deity of the harvest, and the name has been preserved in the city's chronicles since its founding.

Serren is the seat of the magistrates who govern the region of the inland valleys, and the city is the home of the farmers and the merchants who conduct the trade of the plain. The city has grown steadily since its founding, and the city is today among the larger of the towns of the inland valleys. The chronicler notes that Serren is held in particular esteem by the farmers and the merchants of the inland valleys, and the magistrates mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies.

## Founding

The chronicles of the city attribute the founding of Serren to the farmers who settled upon the fertile plain in the generation before the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The settlement was small at first, consisting of a few farmsteads, a small temple, and a modest market. The settlement grew rapidly, for the plain was the most productive of the inland valleys, and the farmers who cultivated the plain found it convenient to bring their goods to the market of the settlement rather than to carry them further.

The growth of the settlement into a city is attributed in the chronicles to the accord that the farmers made with the merchants who traded with the cold north and with the warm coast. The accord established the standards of the grain and the cattle, the rules of the trade, and the procedures by which the disputes were adjudicated. The accord has been revised from time to time but never replaced, and the accord is preserved in the archives of the magistrates of Serren.

## The Plain

The plain of Serren is one of the most productive of the inland valleys. The plain is broad and flat, and the plain is watered by the rivers that descend from the eastern flank of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands). The plain supports the cultivation of the grain that is the staple of the diet of the inland valleys, and the plain supports the cattle that the farmers raise upon the grasslands that border the cultivated fields.

The chronicler notes that the plain has been the foundation of the prosperity of Serren since the founding of the city, and the plain has been carefully cultivated by the farmers of the surrounding villages ever since. The plain is divided into a number of small districts, and each district is governed by an elder who is elected by the households of the district. The chronicler observes that the cultivation has been the foundation of the careful management of the plain, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the farmers of the region.

## The Markets

The markets of Serren are the principal points of contact between the farmers of the plain and the merchants of the inland valleys, and the markets are among the larger of the markets of the cold north. The markets are held in the public square that the magistrates of the city laid out in the early generations of the city's growth, and the markets are attended by the farmers of the surrounding villages and the merchants of the principal towns of the inland valleys. The chronicler notes that the markets have been the foundation of the trade of the plain for several centuries, and the markets are held in particular esteem by the farmers and the merchants of the cold north.

The markets are regulated by the magistrates of the city in consultation with the elders of the surrounding villages, and the regulations set the seasons at which the goods may be brought to the market, the standards of the goods, and the prices at which the goods may be sold. The chronicler notes that the regulations have been the foundation of the careful management of the trade, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the farmers and the merchants of the region.

## The Magistrates

The city is governed by the magistrates of Serren, who are appointed by the council of the city from among the principal farmers and the merchants of the inland valleys. The magistrates hold office for a term of three years, and the magistrates govern the city in concert with the elders of the surrounding villages. The chronicler notes that the magistrates are held in particular esteem by the citizens of the city, and the magistrates mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies.

The magistrates maintain the markets, the roads, the bridges, the irrigation works, and the public order of the city. The magistrates also oversee the collection of the small dues that pay for the maintenance of the markets and the roads, and the magistrates audit the accounts of the dues twice in each year. The chronicler notes that the audit has been the foundation of the careful management of the city's finances, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the farmers and the merchants of the city.

## The Trade

The trade of Serren is the principal business of the city, and the trade has been the foundation of the prosperity of the city since the founding. The merchants of the city trade in the grain and the cattle of the plain, the wool of the cold north, and the salt and the spices of the warm coast. The chronicler notes that the trade has been the foundation of the contact between the city and the towns of the cold north and the warm coast, and the contact is the source of much of the prosperity of the merchants of both parties.

The trade is regulated by the magistrates in consultation with the merchants of the principal towns of the inland valleys. The chronicler notes that the regulation has been the foundation of the careful management of the trade, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the merchants of the city. The trade is conducted through the markets and the roads, and the merchants of the city travel the roads of the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) to conduct the trade.

## Significance

Serren is among the larger of the cities of the inland valleys, and the city is the foundation of the trade in grain and cattle of the region of the plain. The markets, the roads, the bridges, the irrigation works, the magistrates, and the trade are the inheritance that the city has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that Serren is held in particular memory by the farmers and the merchants of the inland valleys, and the farmers and the merchants mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the inland valleys, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every city of the region.
