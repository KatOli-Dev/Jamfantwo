## A City of the Inland Valleys

Serren is a city of the inland valleys, founded upon a fertile plain where the rivers that descend from the eastern flank of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) converge before turning west toward the [Greywash](/content/location/natural/feature/mouths-of-the-greywash). The plain is one of the most productive of the inland valleys, and the city has been the principal market for the grain and cattle of the surrounding farms since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). Serren takes its name from [the Sky Walkers' Way](/content/culture/sky-walkers-way) deity of the harvest. The city is the seat of the magistrates who govern the region of the inland valleys and is home to the farmers and merchants who conduct the trade of the plain. It ranks among the larger towns of the inland valleys.

## The Farmers' Settlement on the Plain

The founding of Serren is attributed to the farmers who settled upon the fertile plain in the generation before the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The settlement began with a few farmsteads, a small temple, and a modest market, but it grew rapidly because the plain was the most productive of the inland valleys, and the farmers who cultivated it found it convenient to bring their goods to the settlement's market rather than carry them further.

The growth of the settlement into a city is attributed to an accord the farmers made with the merchants who traded with the cold north and the warm coast. The accord established the standards for grain and cattle, the rules of trade, and the procedures for adjudicating disputes. It has been revised from time to time but never replaced, and it is preserved in the archives of the magistrates of Serren.

## The Broad Valley and Its Irrigation Channels

The plain of Serren is broad and flat, watered by the rivers that descend from the eastern flank of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands). It supports the cultivation of grain, the staple of the diet of the inland valleys, and the cattle that the farmers raise upon the grasslands bordering the cultivated fields. The plain is divided into a number of small districts, each governed by an elder elected by the households of that district. The careful cultivation and management of the plain has sustained the prosperity of the farmers of the region.

The soil of the plain is deep and fertile, replenished by the seasonal flooding of the rivers that wind across it. The farmers of Serren have developed irrigation channels that draw water from the rivers during the drier months, allowing for reliable harvests even in years of low rainfall. The rotation of grain and pasture maintains the fertility of the soil, and the cattle that graze on the fallow fields provide manure that enriches the land for the following season.

## The Weekly Gatherings and the Public Square

The markets of Serren are the principal points of contact between the farmers of the plain and the merchants of the inland valleys and are among the larger markets of the cold north. They are held in the public square that the magistrates laid out in the early generations of the city's growth and are attended by farmers from the surrounding villages and merchants from the principal towns of the inland valleys. The markets are regulated by the magistrates in consultation with the elders of the surrounding villages. The regulations set the seasons at which goods may be brought to market, the standards of the goods, and the prices at which they may be sold.

The weekly market day is the busiest occasion in the life of the city. Farmers arrive from the surrounding districts with wagons loaded with grain and herds of cattle destined for sale. The merchants who attend the market represent the trading houses of the cold north and the warm coast, and the transactions concluded in the public square set the prices for agricultural goods across the region.

## Governance of the Inland Valleys

Serren is governed by magistrates appointed by the city council from among the principal farmers and merchants of the inland valleys. They hold office for a term of three years and govern in concert with the elders of the surrounding villages. The magistrates are responsible for maintaining the markets, roads, bridges, irrigation works, and public order. They oversee the collection of dues that fund the maintenance of the markets and roads and audit the accounts twice each year.

## Grain, Cattle, and the River Valley Routes

The trade of Serren is the principal business of the city. Merchants trade in the grain and cattle of the plain, the wool of the cold north, and the salt and spices of the warm coast. This trade connects the city to the towns of the cold north and the warm coast, benefiting merchants on both sides. The trade is regulated by the magistrates in consultation with the merchants of the principal towns of the inland valleys. Commerce is conducted through the markets and along the roads, and merchants travel the routes of the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) to conduct their business.

The position of Serren at the meeting point of several river valleys gives it access to a wide hinterland. Grain and cattle from the surrounding farms converge on the city, where they are sorted, graded, and prepared for transport to the markets of the cold north and the warm coast. The merchants of Serren have established networks of agents in the principal towns of both regions, ensuring a steady flow of goods in both directions.

## The Principal Market of the Inland Valleys

Serren is among the larger cities of the inland valleys and is the foundation of the trade in grain and cattle in the region of the plain. The markets, roads, bridges, irrigation works, and governance structures are the inheritance the city has bequeathed to the present age. The anniversary ceremonies of the founding are among the most important public observances of the inland valleys, attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every city in the region. The prosperity of Serren has been sustained through centuries by the fertility of its plain and the skill of its farmers and merchants, and the city remains the principal market for the agricultural produce of the inland valleys.
