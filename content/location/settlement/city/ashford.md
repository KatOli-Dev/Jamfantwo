## Overview

Ashford is a northern city of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), situated at a major crossing of the river that flows through the northern river valleys. The city stands at the point where the river is at its narrowest and most shallow, and the ford that gives the city its name has been a crossing point since the earliest days of human settlement in the region. The bridge that now spans the river is one of the oldest and the most substantial in the north, and the roads that converge on Ashford from all directions make the city a natural hub of communication and trade between the northern valleys and the rest of the continent.

## The Crossing and the Roads

The ford at Ashford was the original reason for the settlement, and the crossing has been the most important point of passage on the northern river for as long as humans have inhabited the region. The river is broad and deep for most of its course, but at Ashford it contracts and shallows, and the bed is firm enough to support the passage of wagons and livestock when the water is low. The bridge that was built at the crossing in a later age is a substantial structure of stone, wide enough for two wagons to pass, and it is one of the finest examples of northern engineering.

The roads that converge on Ashford are the other foundation of the city's prosperity, for the crossing is the meeting point of the routes that connect the northern valleys to each other and to the rest of the continent. The north road runs to [Thornwick](/content/location/settlement/city/thornwick) and the older settlements of the valley, the south road runs to the central grasslands and the southern cities, the east road runs to the coast and the ports of the eastern shore, and the west road climbs into the foothills of the mountain spine. The traffic that passes through Ashford is constant and varied, and the city's inns, taverns, and stables are a significant part of its economy.

The maintenance of the roads is a shared responsibility, for the roads are the arteries of the northern valleys, and the cities and the settlements that depend on them contribute to their upkeep. The road wardens of Ashford are responsible for the sections that lie within the territory of the city, and they patrol the routes, repair the surfaces, and maintain the bridges and the culverts that carry the roads across the streams and the gullies of the valleys. The road wardens are also responsible for the safety of the travellers, and the presence of the patrol is a deterrent to the bandits who have been known to prey on the traffic of the northern roads.

## The City

Ashford is a bustling, well-connected city, and its architecture reflects its function as a crossroads settlement. The city is built on both banks of the river, connected by the bridge and by a second, newer bridge that was built to accommodate the growing traffic of recent centuries. The streets are broad and well-paved, for the wagons and the livestock that pass through the city require room and a firm surface, and the public buildings are situated near the bridge, where the traffic is heaviest and the commerce is greatest. The guildhall of Ashford is the most prominent of the public buildings, and the merchants and the craftsmen who govern the city's affairs meet there to conduct the business of the corporation that administers the crossing and the roads.

The market square of Ashford is one of the busiest in the north, and the market that is held there is a daily affair, for the constant traffic of the roads ensures a steady supply of goods and customers. The population of Ashford is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a small community of [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) who are involved in the trade of metalwork and the maintenance of the bridge and the roads. The dwarven stonemasons of Ashford are the heirs of the engineers who built the bridge, and the maintenance of the structure is a duty that has been passed down through the generations. The inns of Ashford are among the finest in the northern valleys, and the innkeepers are accustomed to the needs of the merchants, the drivers, and the travellers who pass through the city on the roads that converge from every direction.

## Significance

Ashford is the primary hub of communication and trade in the northern river valleys, and its bridge and its roads are the arteries through which the commerce of the north flows. The city is the most important settlement of the region after [Thornwick](/content/location/settlement/city/thornwick), and its position at the crossing of the river gives it a strategic significance that has been recognised since the earliest days of human settlement. The city is a key point in the road network of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches), and its prosperity is a direct consequence of the traffic that passes through its streets and over its bridge.

## History

Ashford was founded at the ford that gave the city its name, and the crossing was the original reason for the settlement. The first inhabitants were the travellers and the herdsmen who used the ford, and the settlement that grew around the crossing was a collection of inns and stables that served the traffic of the valleys. The village grew into a town as the trade of the northern valleys developed, and the construction of the bridge in a later age transformed the crossing from a seasonal passage to a year-round route. The bridge was built by dwarven engineers, and the expertise of the dwarves is evident in the quality of the construction and the durability of the structure.

The growth of the road network that converges on Ashford was the other great factor in the development of the city, for the roads that connect the northern valleys to the rest of the continent all pass through the crossing, and the city has grown and thrived on the traffic that flows along them. The second bridge was built in a later century to accommodate the growing traffic, and the two bridges are the most prominent features of the city, spanning the river in parallel and carrying the commerce of the north between the two halves of the city.
