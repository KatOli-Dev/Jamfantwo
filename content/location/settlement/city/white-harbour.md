## A Major Port on the Northern Coast of Aethelar

White Harbour is a major port on the northern coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), situated where the River Caelin meets the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The city takes its name from the pale limestone cliffs that rise on either side of the estuary, their white faces visible to vessels approaching from the open sea. White Harbour is the principal port of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and the primary point of departure for ships bound for [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim) and the northern isles. The city has been a centre of maritime trade for centuries, and its deep, sheltered harbour accommodates the largest vessels that sail the northern waters.

The estuary of the Caelin forms a natural harbour protected from the prevailing winds by the headlands to east and west. The river has carved a deep channel through the limestone, and the harbour is accessible at all states of the tide, a quality that has made it the preferred port of call for vessels trading between the northern coast and the wider world. The quays and wharves lining both banks of the estuary extend for more than a mile, and the warehouses, counting houses, and merchants halls that back onto them form the commercial heart of the city.

## The White Cliffs and the Deep Channel

The limestone cliffs that give White Harbour its name are the most distinctive feature of the northern coastline. The cliffs rise sheer from the water to a height of some two hundred feet, their white faces streaked with grey and yellow where the stone has weathered. The cliffs are composed of a fine-grained limestone laid down in a distant age when the northern coast lay beneath a shallow sea, and the stone is rich in the fossils of creatures that inhabited those ancient waters. The quarrying of the limestone for building purposes has been a secondary industry of the region for centuries, and the stone of the White Harbour cliffs has been used in the construction of many of the principal buildings of the northern coast.

The channel through which the Caelin reaches the sea is deep and narrow, cut through the limestone by the action of the river over countless millennia. The channel is navigable by vessels of considerable draft, and the harbour masters of White Harbour maintain a system of buoys and beacons marking the deepest water. The approach to the harbour is guarded by a pair of towers built on the headlands at the mouth of the estuary, and the passage of vessels in and out of the harbour is controlled by the harbour authority, which regulates the traffic to prevent congestion and collision.

## From Fishing Village to Commercial Port

The earliest settlement at the mouth of the Caelin was a fishing village established by the first human inhabitants of the northern coast. The sheltered estuary and the abundance of fish in the coastal waters made the site attractive for settlement, and the village grew slowly over the early centuries of the human occupation of Aethelar. The transformation of the village into a port began when the merchants of the southern cities recognised the advantages of the deep, sheltered harbour and established trading posts on the banks of the estuary.

The growth of White Harbour into a major port was accelerated by the development of trade with Nordheim and the northern isles. The merchants of the league saw the value of a northern port that could serve as a terminus for the routes crossing the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea). The city became the primary point of departure for the northern trade, and its merchants grew wealthy on the commerce passing through the harbour.

The founding of the Northern Coast League in the centuries that followed established White Harbour as the principal port of the northern coast and the seat of the league's governing council. The league was formed by a coalition of the coastal cities of the northern coast, united by their common interest in the trade routes and the security of the northern waters. White Harbour was chosen as the seat of the council because of its central position on the coast and the capacity of its harbour to accommodate the vessels of the league.

## The Outer Harbour, the Inner Basin, and the Warehouses

The harbour of White Harbour is divided into an outer harbour and an inner basin, each serving a different function in the commerce of the port. The outer harbour lies beyond the mouth of the estuary, sheltered by the headlands, and it is here that the largest vessels anchor while awaiting loading or unloading. The outer harbour is exposed to the swell of the open sea, but the shelter provided by the headlands makes it a safe anchorage even in the worst weather that the Auric Ocean can produce.

The inner basin lies within the estuary, where the river widens to form a broad pool protected from the sea by the narrowing of the channel. The inner basin is the principal anchorage of the port, and its quays are occupied by the merchant vessels that ply the routes of the northern coast and the Stormfall Sea. The basin is lined with warehouses of stone and timber, their roofs steep and their doors broad, designed for the storage and handling of the goods that pass through the port. The warehouses are divided among the merchants of the city, each company maintaining its own storage and handling facilities.

The goods passing through the harbour include the timber, wool, and grain of the northern coast, the fish and oil of the northern fisheries, and the manufactured goods of the southern cities that arrive by sea for transshipment to the interior. The trade of White Harbour is the foundation of the prosperity of the Northern Coast League, and the revenues generated by the harbour are the principal source of the league's wealth.

## The Navigation School and the Masters of the Coast

White Harbour is the seat of the Navigation School of the Northern Coast League, the principal institution of maritime education on the northern coast. The school was established by the league to train the masters and officers who command the vessels of the northern trade, and its curriculum includes the study of the winds and currents of the Auric Ocean and the Stormfall Sea, the art of navigation by the stars and the sun, the handling of vessels in all weather, and the laws and customs of the sea.

The school is housed in a substantial building overlooking the inner basin, and its halls are furnished with charts, instruments, and models used in the instruction of the students. The masters of the school are among the most experienced mariners of the northern coast, and the graduates of the school are recognised throughout the known world for their skill and knowledge. The school maintains a small fleet of training vessels that put to sea during the warmer months, giving the students practical experience in the handling of ships and the navigation of the coastal waters.

## The Council Hall, the Customs House, and the Streets of the Lower Town

The governing institutions of White Harbour are concentrated in the lower town, the district that lies along the banks of the estuary at the foot of the cliffs. The council hall of the Northern Coast League is the largest and most imposing building in the city, a structure of white limestone built on a rise overlooking the inner basin. The hall is the meeting place of the league's governing council, the body of representatives from the member cities, and the venue for the assemblies that decide the affairs of the league.

The customs house stands adjacent to the council hall, a building of equal size but more functional design, its ground floor occupied by the officials who assess and collect the duties on the goods passing through the port. The customs house is the principal source of revenue for the city and the league, and the officials who serve there are among the most influential figures in the administration of White Harbour.

The streets of the lower town are narrow and winding, lined with the houses of merchants and the offices of traders and ship chandlers. The buildings are constructed of the white limestone of the cliffs, and the lower town has a unified, harmonious character that reflects the prosperity and permanence of the city. The upper town, built on the plateau above the cliffs, is the residential district of the city, its streets broader and its buildings more widely spaced, with gardens and courtyards that give the district a more open and airy character.

## The Timber, Wool, and Grain of the Northern Trade

The trade of White Harbour is the principal business of the city, and the volume of goods passing through the port is exceeded only by the trade of the southern ports. The exports of the northern coast include the timber of the great forests, the wool of the northern sheep, and the grain of the river valleys, all of which are shipped from White Harbour to destinations across the known world. The imports include the manufactured goods of the southern cities, the wines and spices of the warm coasts, and the metalwork of the mountain forges.

The timber trade is one of the oldest and most important components of the city's commerce. The great forests of the northern coast produce timber of exceptional quality, and the logs are floated down the rivers to White Harbour, where they are cut and shaped for export. The wool of the northern sheep is prized for its softness and warmth, and the fleeces are brought to the city for sorting and baling before shipment. The grain of the river valleys is the foundation of the food supply of the northern cities, and the surplus is exported to the northern isles and the coasts of the Stormfall Sea.

## White Harbour and the Northern Seas

White Harbour is the principal port of the northern coast of Aethelar and the seat of the Northern Coast League. The city's deep, sheltered harbour and its position at the mouth of the River Caelin have made it the primary point of departure for the northern trade and the centre of the commercial life of the northern coast. The white limestone cliffs that give the city its name are the symbol of White Harbour, and the city that stands between them is the gateway to the northern seas and the principal port of call for vessels trading between Aethelar and the lands beyond the Stormfall Sea. The prosperity of White Harbour is built upon the commerce of the northern waters, and the city will continue to serve as the principal port of the northern coast for as long as the trade routes connect the shores of the Auric Ocean to the markets of the world.
