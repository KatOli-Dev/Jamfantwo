## Overview

Sahran is a city of the warm coast, founded upon a sheltered bay of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip) at the point where the principal caravan route from the inland valleys reaches the sea. The city commands the principal outlet for the salt, grain, and cloth that the merchants of the inland valleys send to the markets of the warm coast, and it has been the principal port of the coastal strip since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). Sahran takes its name from the coastal strip it commands. The city is the seat of the magistrates who govern the southern coast and home to the salt-makers, farmers, and merchants who conduct the trade of the coast. Its growth has been steady since its founding, and it ranks among the larger towns of the warm coast.

## Founding

The founding of Sahran is attributed to the salt-makers who settled upon the natural harbour in the generation before the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The initial settlement consisted of a few salt-pans, a small temple, and a modest mole, but it grew rapidly because the harbour was the finest at the southern end of the coastal strip, and the salt-makers working the pans preferred to store their goods in the settlement's warehouses rather than carry them further.

The growth of the settlement into a city is attributed to an accord the salt-makers made with the merchants who traded with the inland valleys and with the merchants of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar). This accord established the standards for salt and grain, the rules of trade, and the procedures for adjudicating disputes. It has been revised from time to time but never replaced, and it is preserved in the archives of the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court).

## The Harbour

The harbour of Sahran is one of the better natural harbours of the warm coast. It is sheltered from the prevailing winds by the headland to the north and deep enough for the larger vessels that carry the cargoes of the trade. The harbour is divided into an inner basin for fishing boats and an outer basin for merchant vessels, separated by a stone mole erected by the magistrates in the early generations of the city's growth. The fishing fleet that operates from the inner basin supplies the city and the surrounding region, while the merchant vessels in the outer basin carry goods to the ports of the warm coast and beyond.

The harbour has been fundamental to Sahran's prosperity since its founding. The mole has been repaired several times after storm damage, and the basins have been dredged at intervals to keep the channels navigable. The safety and reliability of the harbour have encouraged the growth of the merchant community and sustained the trade that passes through the city.

## The Salt Pans

The salt pans that surround the city are the source of the salt regulated by the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court). These pans are flooded by small streams that run off the higher ground to the east, and the water is evaporated by the sun in the late summer. The harvest is opened by the master of the Salt Court after consultation with the sworn members and closed in the autumn when the rains begin. The pans are divided into a number of small districts, each governed by an elder elected by the salt-makers of that district. The careful management of the pans has sustained the prosperity of the salt-makers of the region for generations.

The salt produced at Sahran is prized throughout the warm coast for its quality. It is transported from the pans to the warehouses along the harbour, where it is stored before being loaded onto vessels for shipment to the inland valleys and the northern coast. The salt trade is the oldest and most stable component of the city's commerce and has attracted merchants from across the Sahrani coast to the markets of Sahran.

## The Magistrates

Sahran is governed by magistrates appointed by the city council from among the principal salt-makers and merchants of the coast. They hold office for a term of three years and govern in concert with the master of the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court) and the elders of the salt-pan districts. The magistrates are responsible for maintaining the harbour, quays, warehouses, roads, salt pans, and public order. They oversee the collection of dues that fund the maintenance of the harbour and salt pans and audit the accounts twice each year.

## The Trade

The trade of Sahran is the principal business of the city. Merchants trade in the salt of the pans, the grain and cloth of the inland valleys, and the spices and hardwoods of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). This trade connects the city to the towns of the inland valleys and the Kharesh, generating wealth for merchants on both sides. The trade is regulated by the magistrates in consultation with the master of the Salt Court and the merchants of the principal towns of the warm coast. Commerce passes through the harbour and quays, and merchants travel the roads of the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) to conduct their business.

The volume of trade passing through Sahran has grown steadily over the centuries. The city's position at the junction of coastal sea routes and overland caravan routes makes it a natural point of exchange between the maritime and inland economies. Goods arriving by sea are transferred to caravans for the journey inland, while products of the interior are loaded onto vessels for shipment along the coast.

## Significance

Sahran is among the larger cities of the warm coast and is the foundation of the trade in salt and grain in the region of the southern [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip). The harbour, salt pans, quays, warehouses, and governance structures represent the inheritance the city has bequeathed to the present age. The anniversary ceremonies of the founding are among the most important public observances of the warm coast, attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every city in the region. The prosperity of Sahran has endured through centuries of careful management of its salt pans and harbour, and the city continues to serve as the principal port for the exchange of goods between the inland valleys and the warm coast.
