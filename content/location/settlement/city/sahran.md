## Overview

Sahran is a city of the warm coast, founded upon a sheltered bay of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip) at the point where the principal caravan route from the inland valleys reaches the sea. The city commands the principal outlet for the salt, the grain, and the cloth that the merchants of the inland valleys send to the markets of the warm coast, and the city has been the principal port of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip) since the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The city takes its name from the coastal strip that it commands, and the name has been preserved in the city's chronicles since its founding.

Sahran is the seat of the magistrates who govern the region of the southern coast, and the city is the home of the salt-makers, the farmers, and the merchants who conduct the trade of the coast. The city has grown steadily since its founding, and the city is today among the larger of the towns of the warm coast. The chronicler notes that Sahran is held in particular esteem by the salt-makers and the merchants of the warm coast, and the magistrates mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies.

## Founding

The chronicles of the city attribute the founding of Sahran to the salt-makers who settled upon the natural harbour in the generation before the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era). The settlement was small at first, consisting of a few salt-pans, a small temple, and a modest mole. The settlement grew rapidly, for the harbour was the best at the southern end of the coastal strip, and the salt-makers who worked the pans found it convenient to keep their goods in the warehouses of the settlement rather than to carry them further.

The growth of the settlement into a city is attributed in the chronicles to the accord that the salt-makers made with the merchants who traded with the inland valleys and with the merchants of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar). The accord established the standards of the salt and the grain, the rules of the trade, and the procedures by which the disputes were adjudicated. The accord has been revised from time to time but never replaced, and the accord is preserved in the archives of the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court).

## The Harbour

The harbour of Sahran is one of the better natural harbours of the warm coast. The harbour is sheltered from the prevailing winds by the headland that rises to the north, and the harbour is deep enough for the larger vessels that carry the cargoes of the trade. The harbour is divided into an inner basin for the fishing boats and an outer basin for the merchant vessels, and the basins are separated by a stone mole that the magistrates erected in the early generations of the city's growth.

The chronicler notes that the harbour has been the foundation of the prosperity of Sahran since the founding of the city, and the harbour has been carefully maintained by the magistrates ever since. The mole has been repaired several times after the storms that the chroniclers have recorded in the city's chronicles, and the basins have been dredged at intervals to keep the channels navigable. The chronicler observes that the maintenance has been the foundation of the safety of the navigation of the harbour, and the safety is the source of much of the prosperity of the merchants of the city.

## The Salt Pans

The salt pans that surround the city are the source of the salt that the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court) regulates, and the salt pans are the foundation of the prosperity of the salt-makers of the coast. The pans are flooded by the small streams that run off the higher ground to the east, and the pans are evaporated by the sun in the late summer. The harvest is opened by the master of the Salt Court after consultation with the sworn members, and the harvest is closed in the autumn when the rains begin.

The chronicler notes that the salt pans have been the foundation of the prosperity of Sahran since the founding of the city, and the salt pans have been carefully managed by the salt-makers and the magistrates ever since. The pans are divided into a number of small districts, and each district is governed by an elder who is elected by the salt-makers of the district. The chronicler observes that the management has been the foundation of the careful cultivation of the pans, and the cultivation is the source of much of the prosperity of the salt-makers of the region.

## The Magistrates

The city is governed by the magistrates of Sahran, who are appointed by the council of the city from among the principal salt-makers and the merchants of the coast. The magistrates hold office for a term of three years, and the magistrates govern the city in concert with the master of the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court) and the elders of the salt-pan districts. The chronicler notes that the magistrates are held in particular esteem by the citizens of the city, and the magistrates mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies.

The magistrates maintain the harbour, the quays, the warehouses, the roads, the salt pans, and the public order of the city. The magistrates also oversee the collection of the small dues that pay for the maintenance of the harbour and the salt pans, and the magistrates audit the accounts of the dues twice in each year. The chronicler notes that the audit has been the foundation of the careful management of the city's finances, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the salt-makers and the merchants of the city.

## The Trade

The trade of Sahran is the principal business of the city, and the trade has been the foundation of the prosperity of the city since the founding. The merchants of the city trade in the salt of the pans, the grain and the cloth of the inland valleys, and the spices and the hardwoods of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). The chronicler notes that the trade has been the foundation of the contact between the city and the towns of the inland valleys and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and the contact is the source of much of the prosperity of the merchants of both parties.

The trade is regulated by the magistrates in consultation with the master of the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court) and the merchants of the principal towns of the warm coast. The chronicler notes that the regulation has been the foundation of the careful management of the trade, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the merchants of the city. The trade is conducted through the harbour and the quays, and the merchants of the city travel the roads of the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) to conduct the trade.

## Significance

Sahran is among the larger of the cities of the warm coast, and the city is the foundation of the trade in salt and grain of the region of the southern [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip). The harbour, the salt pans, the quays, the warehouses, the magistrates, and the trade are the inheritance that the city has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that Sahran is held in particular memory by the salt-makers and the merchants of the warm coast, and the salt-makers and the merchants mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the warm coast, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every city of the region.
