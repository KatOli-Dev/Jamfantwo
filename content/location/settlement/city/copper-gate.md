## A Mining City at the Foot of the Crown Range

Copper Gate is a city of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) situated at the foot of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range), the great mountain chain that forms the spine of the continent. The city is built where the mountains meet the eastern lowlands, at the mouth of a valley that penetrates deep into the range. Copper Gate takes its name from the gate that once controlled access to the copper mines of the upper valley, and the city has been a centre of mining and metalworking since the earliest period of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar)'s settlement.

The position of Copper Gate at the junction of mountain and lowland has made it a natural centre for the trade in ores and metals. The roads that descend from the passes of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) converge on the city, carrying the copper, tin, and other minerals extracted from the mines of the interior. The city is the principal point of exchange between the mountain and the lowland, and its merchants are among the wealthiest in the eastern districts of Aethelar.

## The Crown Range and the Copper Veins of the Upper Valley

The Crown Range is the principal mountain chain of eastern Aethelar, rising from the lowlands in a series of steep slopes and rocky ridges that culminate in peaks visible from many miles distant. The range is rich in mineral deposits, and the veins of copper, tin, and other metals that run through the rock have been worked since the earliest period of human settlement in the region. The mines of the upper valley above Copper Gate are among the richest in the Crown Range, and the copper extracted from them is of exceptional purity.

The upper valley that gives access to the mines is a narrow, steep-sided gorge that rises from the lowlands into the heart of the Crown Range. The valley is traversed by a road that follows the course of a mountain stream, climbing through a series of switchbacks and terraces to the mining settlements of the interior. The road is maintained by the city of Copper Gate, and the traffic of ore wagons and pack trains that passes along it is the lifeblood of the city's economy.

## From Mining Camp to Fortified Town

The earliest settlement at the site of Copper Gate was a mining camp established by the first prospectors who discovered the copper veins of the upper valley. The camp was a temporary settlement of tents and rough shelters, but it became a permanent village when the richness of the deposits became apparent and the miners began to bring their families to the site. The village grew into a town as the mines expanded, and the settlement acquired a fortified gate that controlled access to the upper valley and the mines beyond.

The gate that gave the city its name was built to protect the mines from raiders and to control the passage of ore from the mines to the lowlands. The gate was a substantial structure of stone and timber, built across the narrowest point of the valley, and it was manned by a garrison responsible for the security of the upper valley. The gate was removed in a later century, when the peace of the region was established and the threat of raiders had passed, but the name of the city preserves the memory of the structure.

The grant of town status followed the growth of the settlement and the increase in its population and prosperity. The town was given the right to hold a weekly market, and the market became the principal commercial event of the eastern foothills. The market of Copper Gate is still held in the market square at the centre of the city, and the square is surrounded by the houses and workshops of the principal merchants and craftsmen.

## The Smelters, the Forges, and the Metalworkers Quarter

The economy of Copper Gate is dominated by the mining and processing of metals, and the industries that transform ore into finished metal are the largest employers in the city. The smelters of Copper Gate are situated at the lower end of the city, where the furnaces are fed by charcoal brought from the forests of the lowlands. The smelters operate day and night during the working season, and the glow of the furnaces is visible from a considerable distance.

The forges of Copper Gate are the principal sites where the smelted metal is worked into finished products. The forges are staffed by skilled smiths who produce a wide range of metal goods, from tools and implements to decorative objects and armour. The metalworkers of Copper Gate are among the most skilled in eastern Aethelar, and the products of their forges are exported to markets across the continent.

The metalworkers quarter of Copper Gate is the district where the smiths and metalworkers have their workshops and forges. The quarter is lined with the buildings of the metal trades, each workshop displaying the products of its craft. The quarter is the busiest and most prosperous district of the city, and the sound of hammer on anvil is a constant presence in its streets.

## The Ore Wagons, the Pack Trains, and the Mountain Road

The traffic of ore wagons and pack trains that passes through Copper Gate is the foundation of the city's economy. The ore extracted from the mines of the upper valley is brought down the mountain road by wagons pulled by teams of oxen, and the wagons are met at the city by the merchants who purchase the ore for processing. The pack trains that carry the finished metal products back up the mountain road to the settlements of the interior are a familiar sight in the streets of the city.

The mountain road is maintained by the city of Copper Gate, and the road wardens employed by the city are responsible for the upkeep of the road and the safety of the travellers who use it. The road is a substantial engineering work, built with stone foundations and retaining walls that prevent erosion and landslides. The road is passable for most of the year, but the winter snows can close the higher passes and interrupt the traffic for several weeks.

## The Council of the Gate and the Wardens of the Mines

The governance of Copper Gate is in the hands of a council elected by the citizens of the city, with the mine owners and master smiths holding the principal positions of authority. The council is responsible for the administration of the city, the regulation of the mining and metalworking trades, and the maintenance of the mountain road. The council meets in the guild hall, a substantial building of stone that stands at one side of the market square.

The wardens of the mines are appointed by the council to oversee the mining operations of the upper valley and to ensure that the mines are worked safely and responsibly. The wardens are experienced miners who understand the rock of the Crown Range and the techniques of extraction and processing that are appropriate to the conditions of the region. The wardens of Copper Gate are among the most respected figures in the city, for the knowledge and experience they bring to the management of the mines are the foundation of the prosperity of the settlement.

## Copper Gate as the Gateway to the Crown Range Mines

Copper Gate is the gateway to the copper mines of the Crown Range and the principal settlement of the eastern foothills. The city's position at the foot of the mountain range and its long history of mining and metalworking have made it the natural centre of the metal trades of eastern Aethelar. The gate that gave the city its name is no longer standing, but the legacy of the mining operations it once protected continues to sustain the prosperity of the settlement. The smiths and miners of Copper Gate are among the most skilled in Aethelar, and the copper and other metals that pass through the city are the foundation of the industries of the eastern lowlands.
