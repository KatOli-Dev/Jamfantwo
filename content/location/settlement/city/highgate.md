## City That Commands the Mountain Pass

Highgate is a city of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) that commands the most important pass through the mountain spine, the great range dividing the continent into eastern and western halves. The city stands at the narrowest point of the pass, where the mountains contract to a defile barely wide enough for a road, and its walls and gates span the full width of the gap, making Highgate the most strategically significant settlement on the overland route between the two coasts. The city has been a point of military and commercial importance for as long as the pass has been used, and its fortifications are among the most extensive and impressive in the known world.

## The Defile, the Gate, and the Tolls

The pass that Highgate commands is the lowest and most direct route across the mountain spine and the only crossing that remains passable throughout the year, though winter snows can close it for weeks at a time. The road running through the pass is the principal overland route between the eastern and western coasts of Aethelar, and the traffic passing through Highgate includes the trade goods, travellers, and armies that have moved between the two halves of the continent for centuries. The pass is narrow, and the walls of the defile rise steeply on either side, funnelling all traffic through the gate that gives the city its name.

The gate itself is a massive structure of stone and iron, built to withstand siege and to control the passage of all who use the road. The gate is the only way through the walls of Highgate, and the city's control of the gate gives it the power to open or close the pass at will, a power that has been exercised many times in the city's long history. The tolls levied on the traffic passing through the gate are the principal source of the city's wealth, and the toll rates are a subject of negotiation between Highgate and the powers that depend on the pass for their trade. The gate mechanism is a complex assembly of gears and counterweights, maintained by a dedicated team of engineers who test the operation weekly to ensure that it can be closed at need.

## Watchtower to the Great Gate

The strategic value of the pass was recognised in the earliest period of recorded history, and the first fortifications at Highgate were raised long before the city itself took shape. The earliest known structure was a stone watchtower built by human settlers of the western lowlands who sought to secure their trade routes to the eastern coast. Over the centuries, the tower was enlarged and reinforced, and the first gate was constructed across the defile to control the passage of travellers and levy tolls upon the goods passing through.

The city grew around the gate as the volume of trade between the two coasts increased, and the population expanded to include soldiers, administrators, merchants, and craftsmen serving the needs of the garrison and travellers. The construction of the present walls and the great gate was undertaken in a period of renewed prosperity, when trade between the eastern and western coasts was at its height, and the fortifications were built to a standard that has not been surpassed. The walls have been reinforced and extended several times in the centuries since, and the dwarven engineers of the city have contributed significantly to the maintenance and improvement of the structures.

Highgate has been besieged on several occasions, for control of the pass has been a prize that the powers on both sides of the mountain spine have sought to claim. The most serious of these sieges was repelled by the garrison without relief from either side, and the endurance of the defenders during this episode is a source of pride for the city and a testament to the strength of the fortifications. The city has maintained its independence throughout these conflicts, and its neutrality has been respected by the powers bordering the mountain spine, for the tolls on the pass are paid by all, and the disruption of the trade flowing through the gate is a cost that no power wishes to bear.

## Compact Fortress, Narrow Streets, and the Grey Stone

Highgate is a city that is also a fortress, and its fortifications are the dominant feature of the settlement. The walls spanning the defile are thick and high, built of the grey stone of the mountains and reinforced with towers and bastions that command the approach from both sides. The city within the walls is compact and densely built, for the space within the defile is limited, and the buildings are tall and close-set, their upper floors projecting over the narrow streets to make the most of the available ground. The garrison of Highgate is among the largest and most professional in Aethelar, and the soldiers of the city are a visible and constant presence in the streets.

The streets of Highgate are arranged in a pattern that reflects the constraints of the defile, with the main road running east to west through the gate and the side streets climbing the slopes on either side. The buildings are constructed of the same grey stone as the walls, and the architecture is plain and functional, for the city is a fortress first and a place of residence second. The market square is situated near the gate and serves as the commercial centre, where goods passing through the pass are exchanged and provisions for travellers and the garrison are sold.

The population of Highgate is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a significant community of [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) involved in the mining and metalwork that the mountains provide and valued for their skill in the maintenance of the fortifications and the gate. The [minotaurs](/content/species/sapient/minotaurs) of the labyrinthine highlands are occasional visitors to the city, and the trade between Highgate and the minotaur communities of the uplands is a small but valued part of the city's commerce.

## Tolls, the Council, and the Pass Neutrality

The trade of Highgate is dominated by the tolls on the traffic passing through the gate. The goods flowing through the city include the products of the eastern coast, the western coast, and the interior. The city is also a centre for the exchange of goods between the lowlands and the mountains, and the metalwork, stone, and mineral products of the mountain spine are traded at Highgate for the grain, cloth, and manufactured goods of the lowlands. The market square near the gate is the site of a daily exchange where the merchants of the city and the traders passing through the gate negotiate the prices and quantities of goods bought and sold.

The tolls levied on traffic are set by the council of Highgate, a body of elected officials and appointed elders that governs the city and oversees the administration of the pass. The toll rates are a subject of negotiation between Highgate and the powers that depend on the pass for their trade, and the rates have been adjusted many times in response to changing conditions of continental trade. The revenue from the tolls funds the maintenance of the fortifications, the payment of the garrison, and the construction of public works that serve the city and the travellers passing through it.

Highgate is the most strategically significant settlement on the overland route between the two coasts of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and its control of the pass gives it a military and commercial importance unmatched by any other city in the mountain spine. The city is an independent polity, allied with but not subject to the powers bordering the mountain spine, and its neutrality is the foundation of its prosperity, for the tolls on the pass are paid by all who use it, regardless of their allegiance. The significance of Highgate lies in its gate, the single most important structure on the overland route between the two coasts, and the city stands as a symbol of the commercial and strategic importance of the mountain pass that it commands.
