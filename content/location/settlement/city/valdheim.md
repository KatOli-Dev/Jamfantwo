## A City in the Principal River Valley of Morvind

Valdheim is a city in the principal river valley of the interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), situated on the banks of the largest river that flows through the interior ranges. The city is the primary settlement of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark), the human realm of the interior, and its position in the most fertile valley of the interior has made it the agricultural and commercial centre of the region. Valdheim is smaller than the coastal cities but is the largest and most prosperous settlement in the Morvind interior, and its trade connects the interior to the coast through the river and the overland routes.

## The Fertile Valley and Its Alluvial Soils

The river valley surrounding Valdheim is the most fertile region in the interior of Morvind, and the alluvial soils lining the banks of the river are among the most productive in the continent. The agriculture of the valley is the foundation of the city's prosperity, and the fields stretching out from the city walls produce grain, vegetables, and the fodder that sustains the livestock of the region. The river is navigable for much of its length, and the barges carrying goods to and from the coast are a common sight on the waterway.

The climate of the valley is milder than the surrounding highlands, for the river moderates the temperature and the surrounding peaks shelter the valley from the worst of the wind. The growing season is longer than on the higher plateaus, and the agriculture of the valley is more varied and productive, for the soil is deeper and the water is more reliable. The valley is the breadbasket of the Morvind interior, and the grain produced here is exported to the coast and other regions of the continent.

The river descends from the interior ranges through a series of rapids and gorges, and the navigable portion begins at Valdheim, where the current slows and the water deepens. Barges are floated downstream to the coast carrying the grain and wool of the valley, and they are towed back upstream by teams of oxen walking the towpaths along the banks, carrying the metalwork and manufactured goods of the coast to the interior.

## Grain Barges, Wharves, and the Weekly Market

The trade of Valdheim is dominated by the agricultural products of the valley and the mineral products of the surrounding mountains, and the city is the primary point of exchange between the interior and the coast. Grain, wool, and leather from the valley are traded for metalwork, cloth, and manufactured goods imported from the coast and the [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) cities of the interior. The river is the primary route of trade, and the wharves and quays lining the riverbank are the centre of the city's commerce. The barges are broad, flat-bottomed vessels suited to the shallow waters of the upper river, and the bargees who work them are a familiar sight on the waterway.

The overland routes connecting Valdheim to the coast and the interior are also a significant part of the trade, for the river is not navigable beyond the city, and goods destined for the higher valleys and mountain settlements are carried by pack trains and wagons. The roads leading from Valdheim into the interior are maintained by the council of the Valdmark, and the road wardens who patrol them are responsible for the upkeep of the surfaces and bridges.

Valdheim is a well-built, prosperous city whose architecture reflects the character of the interior, with sturdy buildings of stone and timber, steep roofs, and streets broad enough for the wagons and livestock passing through on their way to market. The market square is the largest in the interior, and the weekly market draws farmers, traders, and craftsmen from the surrounding country. The population is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a significant community of dwarves involved in the metal trade. The dwarven community of Valdheim is the link between the city and the dwarven settlements of the interior ranges, and the dwarven merchants residing in the city are among the most influential figures in the trade connecting the valley to the mountains.

## Capital of the Valdmark

Valdheim is the capital of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) and the primary settlement of the Morvind interior, and its agricultural products are the foundation of the economy of the interior region. The city is the commercial centre of the interior, and its trade connects the valley to the coast and other regions of the continent through the river and overland routes. The prosperity of Valdheim is built upon the fertility of the valley and the industry of its people, and the city has maintained its position as the principal settlement of the interior through centuries of steady growth.

## From Farming Village to Interior Capital

Valdheim was founded in the early centuries of human settlement in the interior of Morvind, and the site was chosen for the fertility of the valley and the navigability of the river. The earliest settlement was a farming village, and the abundance of the harvest encouraged the growth of the community. The barges that carried the grain to the coast were the foundation of the trade that transformed the village into a town and then a city, and the wharves and warehouses lining the riverbank are the visible evidence of the prosperity that the trade has brought. The growth of Valdheim was steady and uninterrupted, for the interior of Morvind was less exposed to the conflicts that affected the coastal regions, and the city was able to develop its agriculture and trade in conditions of relative peace.

The Valdmark was established with Valdheim as its capital, and the city has been the seat of the realm's governance for centuries. The council of the Valdmark meets in the great hall at the centre of the market square, and the representatives of the valley and surrounding highlands gather there to settle the business of the realm. The relationship between Valdheim and the dwarven settlements of the interior ranges has been a defining feature of the city's history, and the trade in metalwork and minerals has been a source of mutual benefit sustaining the prosperity of both the human and dwarven communities of the interior.
