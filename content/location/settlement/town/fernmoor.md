## Overview

Fernmoor is a northern settlement of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), situated at the boundary where the ancient forests give way to the open grasslands of the central plains. The town stands on a low rise that overlooks the transition from woodland to grass, and its position at the meeting of two kinds of country has made it a natural point of exchange between the forest peoples and the plains peoples. Fernmoor is smaller than the cities of the river valleys, but it is a thriving and well-situated community, and its market is a gathering point for the diverse peoples who inhabit the boundary country.

## The Boundary Country

The land around Fernmoor is a transition zone, where the dense canopy of the ancient forest thins and gives way to the open grass of the central plains. The forest here is not the deep, shadowed woodland of the north but a patchwork of groves and clearings, with the trees growing in clusters that are separated by meadows and grassland. The undergrowth is thick and varied, and the ferns that give the town its name grow in abundance, covering the moister ground in a carpet of green that is among the most distinctive features of the landscape.

The boundary country is rich in game and forage, for the meeting of forest and grassland creates a diversity of habitats that supports a greater variety of plants and animals than either the deep forest or the open plain. The hunting and the gathering that the land provides are a significant part of the economy of Fernmoor, and the game of the boundary country, including deer, boar, and the smaller animals of the woodland edge, is a valued commodity in the markets of the region.

The ferns that give the town its name are the most distinctive feature of the boundary country, for the ferns grow in abundance on the moister ground at the edge of the forest, and the carpet of green that they form is the most striking feature of the landscape in the spring and the summer. The ferns are also a valued resource, for the fronds are used in the production of the dyes and the medicines that are the speciality of the herbalists of the town, and the roots are used in the production of the starch that is a supplement to the diet of the community.

## Trade and the Market

The market of Fernmoor is the primary institution of the town, and it is a meeting point for the peoples of the forest and the plains. The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the town and the surrounding farms bring grain, vegetables, and the manufactured goods that they produce, and they exchange these for the timber, the game, and the forest products of the woodland peoples and the wool, the leather, and the livestock of the plains peoples. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the forest are occasional visitors to the market, and the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the grasslands are regular traders, and the market of Fernmoor is one of the few places where the settled, the forest, and the nomadic peoples meet on equal terms.

The town is also a centre for the processing of the ferns and the herbs that grow in the boundary country, and the herbalists and the dyers of Fernmoor are skilled in the use of the plants that are gathered from the woodland edge. The dyes produced from the plants of the boundary country are a minor but valued export, and the natural colours of the Fernmoor dyes are noted for their subtlety and their durability.

## History and the Boundary

The founding of Fernmoor is dated to the middle centuries of the human settlement of northern Aethelar, when the boundary country was first recognised as a valuable point of exchange between the forest peoples and the plains peoples. The early settlement was a cluster of inns and storehouses that served the traders who travelled between the forest and the plains, and the permanent community grew from these commercial beginnings. The town has never been fortified, for the boundary country has been peaceful for many generations, and the absence of walls is a reflection of the security that the community has enjoyed throughout its history.

The history of Fernmoor is a history of trade and coexistence, for the town has served as the meeting point of the forest and the plains peoples for centuries, and the market that is held there each week is the oldest and the most valued institution of the community. The growth of the town was steady, and the population of Fernmoor is drawn from the settled humans of the boundary country and the visitors who come to the market from the forest and the plains.

## The Town

Fernmoor is a small, open town, and its buildings are a mixture of the timber-frame construction of the forest country and the stone and plaster of the plains. The town is not walled, for the boundary country has been peaceful for many generations, and the streets are broad and open, lined with the gardens and the orchards that the inhabitants tend. The market square is the centre of the town, and it is a broad, open space that can accommodate the stalls, the pens, and the crowds that gather for the weekly market.

## The Peoples and the Market

The market of Fernmoor is the primary institution of the town, and it is the meeting point of the settled, the forest, and the nomadic peoples of the region. The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the town and the surrounding farms bring the grain, the vegetables, and the manufactured goods that they produce, and the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the forest bring the timber, the game, and the forest products that are the resources of the woodland. The [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the grasslands bring the wool, the leather, and the livestock that are the products of the plains, and the exchange of these goods is the commerce that sustains the town.

The elves of the forest are occasional visitors to the market, and their presence in the town is a rare and a notable event, for the elves are a reserved people who do not mingle freely with the other peoples of the region. The elves bring the rare woods, the resins, and the medicines that are the speciality of the forest, and these goods are the most valuable commodities that are traded at the market. The centaurs are more frequent visitors, for the plains are closer to the town than the deep forest, and the trade between the settled humans and the nomadic centaurs is the most regular and the most sustained of the exchanges that take place at the market.

## Culture and the Boundary

The culture of Fernmoor is shaped by its position at the boundary of two kinds of country, and the community is noted for its openness and its respect for the ways of the forest and the plains peoples. The cuisine of Fernmoor is a mixture of the agricultural products of the settled farms and the game and the herbs of the boundary country, and the cooks of the town are skilled in the preparation of the dishes that combine the ingredients of the forest and the plains. The herbalists and the dyers of the town are the most respected craftsmen, for the plants of the boundary country are the raw materials of their trade, and the skill that they possess is a matter of pride for the community.

The festivals of Fernmoor are oriented toward the seasons and the market, and the most important is the autumn fair, which coincides with the gathering of the herbs and the preparation of the town for the winter. The fair is held in the market square, and the elves and the centaurs are the guests of honour, for the products of the forest and the plains are the most valuable commodities of the season. The fair includes the contests of skill that are a tradition of the boundary country, and the archery and the riding are the most fiercely contested events, for the humans, the elves, and the centaurs each bring their particular skills to the competition.

## Significance

Fernmoor is the primary point of exchange between the forest peoples and the plains peoples of northern Aethelar, and its market is a vital link in the trade network of the region. The town is small, but its position at the boundary of two kinds of country gives it a commercial and a social significance that belies its size. The coexistence of the settled, the forest, and the nomadic peoples at Fernmoor is a model of the kind of interchange that has shaped the culture and the economy of the boundary country for centuries.
