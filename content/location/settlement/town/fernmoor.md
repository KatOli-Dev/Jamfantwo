## A Northern Settlement at the Boundary of Forest and Plain

Fernmoor is a northern settlement of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), situated at the boundary where the ancient forests give way to the open grasslands of the central plains. The town stands on a low rise overlooking the transition from woodland to grass, and its position at the meeting of two kinds of country has made it a natural point of exchange between forest peoples and plains peoples. Fernmoor is smaller than the cities of the river valleys but is a thriving and well-situated community whose market is a gathering point for the diverse peoples inhabiting the boundary country.

## The Patchwork of Groves and Clearing

The land around Fernmoor is a transition zone where the dense canopy of the ancient forest thins and gives way to the open grass of the central plains. The forest here is not the deep, shadowed woodland of the north but a patchwork of groves and clearings, with trees growing in clusters separated by meadows and grassland. The undergrowth is thick and varied, and the ferns that give the town its name grow in abundance, covering the moister ground in a carpet of green that is among the most distinctive features of the landscape.

The boundary country is rich in game and forage, for the meeting of forest and grassland creates a diversity of habitats supporting a greater variety of plants and animals than either the deep forest or the open plain. Hunting and gathering are a significant part of Fernmoor's economy. Game of the boundary country, including deer, boar, and smaller animals of the woodland edge, is a valued commodity in the markets of the region.

The ferns that give the town its name are the most distinctive feature of the boundary country. They grow in abundance on the moister ground at the edge of the forest, forming a carpet of green that is the most striking feature of the landscape in spring and summer. The ferns are also a valued resource: the fronds are used in producing dyes and medicines that are the speciality of the town's herbalists, and the roots are used in producing starch that supplements the community's diet.

## The Meeting Point of Forest, Plain, and Town

The market of Fernmoor is the primary institution of the town, a meeting point for the peoples of forest and plains. The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the town and surrounding farms bring grain, vegetables, and manufactured goods, exchanging these for timber, game, and forest products of the woodland peoples and wool, leather, and livestock of the plains peoples. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the forest are occasional visitors to the market, and the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the grasslands are regular traders. The market of Fernmoor is one of the few places where settled, forest, and nomadic peoples meet on equal terms.

The town is also a centre for processing the ferns and herbs that grow in the boundary country. The herbalists and dyers of Fernmoor are skilled in the use of plants gathered from the woodland edge. The dyes produced from the plants of the boundary country are a minor but valued export; the natural colours of Fernmoor dyes are noted for their subtlety and durability.

## The Peaceful Growth of the Boundary Community

Fernmoor was founded in the middle centuries of human settlement of northern Aethelar, when the boundary country was recognised as a valuable point of exchange between forest and plains peoples. The early settlement was a cluster of inns and storehouses serving traders travelling between forest and plains; the permanent community grew from these commercial beginnings. The town has never been fortified, for the boundary country has been peaceful for many generations, and the absence of walls reflects the security the community has enjoyed throughout its history.

The history of Fernmoor is a history of trade and coexistence. The town has served as the meeting point of forest and plains peoples for centuries, and the weekly market is the oldest and most valued institution of the community. The town's population is drawn from the settled humans of the boundary country and the visitors who come to the market from forest and plains.

## Timber-Frame, Stone, and the Open Market Square

Fernmoor is a small, open town whose buildings are a mixture of timber-frame construction of the forest country and stone and plaster of the plains. The town is not walled, for the boundary country has been peaceful for many generations. The streets are broad and open, lined with gardens and orchards the inhabitants tend. The market square is the centre of the town, a broad, open space that can accommodate the stalls, pens, and crowds that gather for the weekly market.

## The Elves, Centaurs, and the Exchange of Goods

The market of Fernmoor is the meeting point of settled, forest, and nomadic peoples of the region. The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the town and surrounding farms bring grain, vegetables, and manufactured goods; the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the forest bring timber, game, and forest products; the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the grasslands bring wool, leather, and livestock. The exchange of these goods is the commerce that sustains the town.

The elves of the forest are occasional visitors to the market, and their presence is a rare and notable event, for the elves are a reserved people who do not mingle freely with other peoples. The elves bring rare woods, resins, and medicines that are the speciality of the forest; these goods are the most valuable commodities traded at the market. The centaurs are more frequent visitors, for the plains are closer to the town than the deep forest, and trade between settled humans and nomadic centaurs is the most regular and sustained of the exchanges at the market.

## Herbalists, Dyers, and the Autumn Fair

Fernmoor's culture is shaped by its position at the boundary of two kinds of country. The community is noted for its openness and respect for the ways of forest and plains peoples. The cuisine of Fernmoor is a mixture of agricultural products of the settled farms and the game and herbs of the boundary country; the cooks of the town are skilled in preparing dishes combining ingredients of forest and plains. The herbalists and dyers of the town are the most respected craftsmen, for the plants of the boundary country are the raw materials of their trade, and their skill is a matter of pride for the community.

The festivals of Fernmoor are oriented toward the seasons and the market. The most important is the autumn fair, coinciding with the gathering of herbs and the preparation of the town for winter. The fair is held in the market square, and the elves and centaurs are the guests of honour, for the products of forest and plains are the most valuable commodities of the season. The fair includes contests of skill traditional to the boundary country; archery and riding are the most fiercely contested events, as humans, elves, and centaurs each bring their particular skills to the competition.

## A Vital Link Between Forest and Plain

Fernmoor is the primary point of exchange between the forest peoples and the plains peoples of northern Aethelar, and its market is a vital link in the trade network of the region. The town is small, but its position at the boundary of two kinds of country gives it a commercial and social significance that belies its size. The coexistence of settled, forest, and nomadic peoples at Fernmoor is a model of the interchange that has shaped the culture and economy of the boundary country for centuries.
