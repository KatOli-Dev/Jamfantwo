## Overview

Misthaven is a western port of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), known throughout the known world for the fog that shrouds its harbour for much of the year. The town stands on a low, rocky shore of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean), where a natural inlet provides a sheltered anchorage seldom seen clearly, for the cold water of the ocean meets the warmer air of the land in a perpetual bank of mist that gives the town its name. Misthaven is smaller than [Greycliff](/content/location/settlement/town/greycliff) but is an important port of the western coast, and its harbour is a vital refuge for vessels caught in the fog and swell of the open ocean.

## The Harbour and the Fog

The harbour of Misthaven is a broad, shallow inlet penetrating the coast for some distance, sheltered by low headlands on either side that are visible only when the fog lifts. The approach to the harbour is marked by a series of bells and horns placed along the shore, for the fog is often so thick that no landmark can be seen. The mariners of Misthaven navigate by sound, following the bells to the entrance of the inlet. Quays are built along the inner shore of the inlet, and the waterfront is occupied by warehouses and offices of the merchants who manage the port's trade.

The fog that gives Misthaven its name is produced by the meeting of the cold [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) current with the warmer air of the land. The fog is most persistent in autumn and winter, when the contrast between water and air is greatest, and it may blanket the harbour for days at a time, reducing the port to a world of grey shadow and muffled sound. The people of Misthaven are accustomed to the fog; they navigate their town and harbour with a confidence that seems uncanny to visitors, for they know the position of every building, every quay, and every channel by heart.

## Trade and Industry

The trade of Misthaven is modest compared to that of the eastern ports, for the western coast is less densely settled and the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) offers fewer trade routes. The primary commerce of the town is the export of fish and whale products to inland markets and the import of grain and manufactured goods from the interior. The fishing grounds off Misthaven are productive, and the fog that shrouds the harbour also shrouds the fishing boats, allowing them to approach shoals undetected and take a larger catch than clearer waters would allow.

Misthaven is also a centre for processing kelp and seaweed gathered from the rocky shores of the inlet. The kelp is dried and burned to produce a substance used in the manufacture of soap and glass, a small but valuable trade. The people of Misthaven are known for their skill in working driftwood, collected from the beaches in great quantities after storms; the furniture and carved objects of Misthaven are prized for their weathered beauty and salt-cured durability.

## The Town

Misthaven is a small, close-built town whose buildings are constructed of dark grey stone of the coast, with low profiles and small windows that minimise exposure to wind and fog. The streets are narrow and winding, paved with cobblestones often slick with moisture, and the town is oriented toward the harbour, with all main streets leading down to the waterfront. The population is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the people are known for their reserve and quiet manner, qualities visitors from the east often mistake for coldness.

The town is approached from the interior by a road winding through the coastal hills, marked by a series of cairns that guide the traveller through the fog that often blankets the high ground as thoroughly as the harbour. The road is narrow and steep in places, seldom travelled by those without business in Misthaven, for the western coast is sparsely settled and the journey is long. The town's isolation has fostered a self-reliant community where people produce much of what they need and depend on trade with the interior only for goods that cannot be made or gathered locally.

## History

Misthaven was founded as a settlement of fishermen and whale-hunters who established a permanent harbour at the inlet because of its sheltered anchorage and proximity to rich fishing grounds. The early settlement was small and seasonal, but the discovery that the fog concealing the inlet also concealed fishing boats from shoals led to a more permanent community, and the town grew steadily over the centuries. The bell system marking the approach to the harbour was established in the early years of the settlement, and the bells are maintained by the harbour guild that has managed the waterfront since the town's founding.

The history of Misthaven has been shaped by the fog that gives the town its name. The most notable events involve the use of the fog as a defence against threats approaching the western coast. On several occasions, fleets seeking to raid the western coast were turned away by the fog that concealed the harbour and the town, and the people of Misthaven have come to regard the fog as a guardian rather than a hindrance. The most famous of these events is the Long Concealment, a period of several days during which a fleet approaching the coast was forced to withdraw because the fog was so thick that no harbour could be found and no landing could be made.

## Significance

Misthaven is a valued member of the [Grey Coast Holdings](/content/government/local/grey-coast-holdings), and its harbour is the primary refuge for vessels navigating the fog-bound western coast. The town is smaller and less wealthy than [Greycliff](/content/location/settlement/town/greycliff), but its position on a stretch of coast where safe harbours are few gives it an importance that belies its size. The fog that shrouds its harbour is both a hindrance and a protection, for it has on many occasions concealed the town from fleets approaching the western coast with hostile intent, and the people of Misthaven regard the fog as a gift rather than a curse.
