## A Western Fishing Settlement at the Southern Edge of the Cliffs

Saltreach is a western fishing settlement of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), situated at the southern edge of the cliff coast where the land begins to slope toward the lowland plains of the south. The town is the smallest of the major settlements of the [Grey Coast Holdings](/content/government/local/grey-coast-holdings), and its harbour is a narrow cove tucked between two headlands that provide shelter from the worst of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) swell. Saltreach is the last port of call on the western coast before the cliffs give way to the open beaches of the south, and its fishermen work some of the most dangerous waters on the coast, for the transition from cliff to beach is marked by reefs and shoals that have wrecked many vessels.

## The Narrow Cove and the Reef Fishing Grounds

The harbour of Saltreach is a small, deep cove accessible only through a narrow entrance between the headlands. The passage requires careful navigation, for the currents running through the entrance are strong and the rocks are close on either side. The harbour is sufficient for the fishing boats of the town but too small for the larger vessels that call at [Greycliff](/content/location/settlement/town/greycliff) or [Misthaven](/content/location/settlement/town/misthaven); Saltreach's trade is limited to the export of its catch and the import of the few goods the town cannot produce for itself.

The fishing grounds off Saltreach are among the most productive and the most dangerous on the western coast. The reefs and shoals marking the transition from cliff to beach are rich in fish but treacherous to navigate. The fishermen of Saltreach are skilled in reading the water and locating fish, and their boats are small and manoeuvrable, designed for the conditions of the reef rather than the open ocean. The catch is landed at the harbour, salted and dried in salting houses lining the cove, and exported overland to inland markets, where the fish of Saltreach is valued for its quality and freshness.

The seasonal patterns of the fishing grounds are well understood by the fishermen. The movement of fish through the reef is governed by currents and water temperature, and the most productive grounds shift from season to season. The spring run of cod is the most valuable catch of the year; the salting houses work through the nights during the spring season to process the volume of fish landed. The autumn catch is dominated by mackerel passing through the reef on their southward migration; dried mackerel of Saltreach is a valued commodity in inland markets during the winter months.

## Grey Stone and the Cliff-Top Salt Pools

Saltreach is a small, rugged town whose buildings are constructed of grey stone of the coast in the manner of other western settlements. The town is clustered around the harbour, and the streets are steep and narrow, climbing the slope from the cove to the cliff top, where a lighthouse marks the entrance to the harbour and the position of the town for vessels approaching from the south. The population is small and entirely [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the people are known for their hardihood and skill in the difficult waters they work.

The salt for which the town is named is gathered from shallow pools forming on the cliff tops above the cove, where the spray of the ocean evaporates in the rare sunshine and leaves a deposit of salt collected and used in the preservation of the catch. The salt of Saltreach is a minor export valued for its quality and whiteness, and salt-gathering is an important secondary activity of the town.

## From Seasonal Camp to Permanent Harbour

The settlement of Saltreach is old, though its founding is not recorded in any chronicle, for the town was never large enough to attract the attention of the historians of the [Grey Coast Holdings](/content/government/local/grey-coast-holdings). The earliest settlers were fishermen who established a seasonal camp at the cove, drawn by the richness of the reef and the shelter the headlands provided. The permanent settlement grew from these seasonal beginnings; the salting houses and the lighthouse were the first substantial buildings constructed. The town has remained small throughout its history, for the harbour is too narrow to support a larger population, and the prosperity of Saltreach has always been modest compared to that of [Greycliff](/content/location/settlement/town/greycliff) or [Misthaven](/content/location/settlement/town/misthaven).

The relationship between Saltreach and the other settlements of the Grey Coast Holdings is one of mutual dependence. The catch of Saltreach is a valued supplement to the fish and whale products of the larger ports, and the goods Saltreach cannot produce for itself are imported from the other settlements. The town is a quiet member of the Holdings, and its people are content with their modest place in the economy of the western coast.

## The Tower on the Cliff Top

The lighthouse of Saltreach stands on the cliff top above the harbour, the most prominent structure in the town. The lighthouse is a tower of grey stone visible for many miles along the coast; its beacon is lit every night to guide the fishing boats home and to warn vessels passing along the coast of the reefs and shoals below the cliffs. The lighthouse is maintained by the town, and the keeper is a respected figure in the community, for the safety of the fishermen and passing vessels depends upon the reliability of the beacon.

The cliffs surrounding Saltreach are among the highest on the western coast, and the grey stone of the cliffs is the same stone used in the construction of the town's buildings. The cliff tops are windswept and barren, but the salt pools forming in hollows of the rock are the source of the salt that gives the town its name; the gathering of salt is a daily activity during the dry season. The cliff path running northward along the coast is the only overland route connecting Saltreach to the other settlements of the Grey Coast Holdings, narrow and treacherous, passable only on foot or by pack animals carrying salt and dried fish to inland markets.

## Songs, Stories, and the Rhythms of the Tide

The culture of Saltreach is shaped by the sea; the town is oriented entirely toward the waters that sustain it, and the rhythms of the community are the rhythms of fishing and tides. The people of Saltreach are known for their hardihood and skill, and the community is bound together by the shared dangers of the reef and the shared labour of fishing. The festivals are modest affairs, for the population is small and the resources limited, but the annual feast of the first catch is a cherished tradition marking the beginning of the spring season and the return of cod to the reef.

The songs and stories of Saltreach are the songs and stories of the sea. The fishermen have a rich tradition of ballads and tales passed down from generation to generation. The stories tell of great storms that have struck the coast, wrecks found on the reef, and the skill of pilots who have guided boats through the narrow entrance to the harbour in the worst weather. The tradition of storytelling is a valued part of the cultural life of the town; evenings in the tavern of Saltreach are spent sharing old tales and singing old songs.

## The Southernmost Harbour of the Grey Coast Holdings

Saltreach is the southernmost of the settlements of the [Grey Coast Holdings](/content/government/local/grey-coast-holdings), and its harbour is the last refuge on the western coast before the open beaches of the south. The town is small and its trade modest, but its fishermen are among the most skilled on the coast, and the catch landed at Saltreach is a valued supplement to the fish and whale products of the larger ports. The town is a quiet and self-sufficient community, oriented toward the sea and the difficult waters that sustain it, and its people are proud of their independence and their ability to thrive in one of the most demanding environments on the coast. The reputation of Saltreach for the quality of its catch and the skill of its fishermen is a source of pride for the community, and the town is a valued member of the [Grey Coast Holdings](/content/government/local/grey-coast-holdings), contributing its fish and salt to the economy of the western coast and maintaining the lighthouse that guides vessels passing along the shore.
