## The Southernmost Settlement of the Southern Reach

Ashaar is the southernmost settlement of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), on the shore of the [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea) in the far south of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). The town is the centre of fishing and agriculture for the southern basin. Its position on the smaller of the two inland seas gives it a distinct economic role from [Vereth](/content/location/settlement/town/vereth), the larger settlement on the [Greater Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/greater-inland-sea) to the north. Ashaar is the most remote of the Southern Reach settlements, and its distance from the caravan routes and the northern world has preserved a way of life more self-sufficient and traditional than that of the other communities.

## The Sheltered Anchorage and the Fishing Fleet

The Lesser Inland Sea is the smaller, warmer, and more sheltered of the two inland seas of the Southern Reach. It is navigable throughout its extent, and the boats that travel its waters connect the lakeside communities that dot its shores. Ashaar's harbour is a natural anchorage sheltered by a curving headland, with quays lining the inner shore where the fishing boats and smaller trading vessels of the southern basin are moored.

The fishing grounds of the Lesser Inland Sea are productive, and the catch is the primary food source of the southern communities. The fishing fleet of Ashaar is the largest on the southern sea; boats venture out daily and return with a catch preserved by salting and drying. The preserved fish are consumed locally and traded to the other settlements of the Southern Reach through the boat routes that connect the two seas.

## The Fertile Fields and the Highland Herds

The land around Ashaar is the most fertile in the Southern Reach, and the town is the centre of southern basin agriculture. The surrounding fields are watered by rivers flowing from the surrounding highlands into the Lesser Inland Sea, and the soil is deep and rich, suited to a great variety of crops. Farmers cultivate grains, vegetables, and fruits; the orchards that line the river valleys produce figs, dates, and citrus fruits that are among the most prized products of the south. Ashaar's agriculture is the foundation of the food supply of the Southern Reach, and surplus produce is traded to other settlements through the inland sea routes.

The highlands south of Ashaar are the southernmost extent of the Southern Reach, their slopes clothed in open woodland and grassland that support the herds of highland farmers. The wool and leather of the highland herds are traded through the caravan routes to the north. The herdsmen of the highlands are a distinct community within the Southern Reach, their way of life shaped by the terrain and climate of the uplands.

## Pale Stone, Broad Streets, and the Tortolan Community

Ashaar is a well-built, prosperous town whose architecture reflects the agricultural wealth of the surrounding land. Substantial buildings of pale stone, broad streets, and a market square at the centre of the town's commercial and social life define the settlement. The buildings follow the southern tradition: thick walls, shaded courtyards, and high windows, with gardens irrigated by rivers flowing through the town. Stone walls in good order surround the town, and the gates are guarded by a modest force funded by the local council.

The population is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), a mixture of farmers, fishermen, and craftsmen whose way of life is oriented toward the land and the sea. A small community of [tortolans](/content/species/sapient/tortolans) lives on the shore of the Lesser Inland Sea; their long-lived and deliberate nature suits the slow pace of agricultural life. The tortolans of Ashaar are the most numerous of any settlement in the Southern Reach, and their knowledge of the waters and the land is valued by the human community.

## Agricultural Exports and the Inland Sea Routes

Ashaar's trade is dominated by the commerce of the inland seas. The town is the primary source of agricultural products and preserved fish traded across the Southern Reach. Boats travelling between the two seas carry Ashaar's goods to [Vereth](/content/location/settlement/town/vereth) and the other settlements of the Greater Inland Sea; the return cargo includes manufactured goods and northern products arriving through the caravan routes. Ashaar is not directly connected to the caravan trade, for the routes from the north terminate at [Torem](/content/location/settlement/town/torem) and Vereth, and goods reaching Ashaar from the north are carried by the inland sea routes.

The boats used on the inland seas are of a distinctive broad, shallow-draught design suited to calm waters and frequent shallows. The boatbuilders of Ashaar are the most skilled in the Southern Reach, and their vessels are valued by the communities of both seas for their stability and capacity. The boatyard on the harbour shore is a visible and valued part of the town's industry, drawing workers and materials from the surrounding region.

## The Council and the Federation of Lakeside Communities

Ashaar is governed by a council drawn from the prominent families of the town. The council meets in a hall on the market square at the centre of the community's commercial and social life. It oversees the regulation of the market, the maintenance of the walls and harbour, the management of water rights and the irrigation system, and the administration of justice. The chairman is chosen by the members from among their number for a fixed term of five years, considered sufficient for implementing policies without encouraging entrenchment of power.

The council also maintains the relationship with the other settlements of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach). Coordination of trade and defence of the inland seas is managed through a federation of lakeside communities that meets at Vereth. The council of Ashaar sends a representative to the federation meetings. The federation is the primary means of cooperation among the Southern Reach settlements, and the trade routes and water rights managed by it are the foundation of the southern basin economy.

## The Festival of the First Harvest and the Pottery Tradition

The people of Ashaar have developed a culture shaped by the isolation and self-sufficiency of the southern basin, distinct from that of the northern world. The most notable tradition is the Festival of the First Harvest, held at the beginning of the growing season when the first fruits of the orchards and the first grain of the fields are gathered for a communal feast on the market square. The festival is the most important event in the social calendar of Ashaar, attended by the entire community from farmers and fishermen to the herdsmen of the highlands and the tortolans of the shore.

Ashaar's traditions include a distinctive style of pottery made from riverbank clay, noted for its durability and simple, elegant decoration. The pottery is traded to other settlements of the Southern Reach, where it is valued for its quality and distinctive style. The weavers of Ashaar produce a cloth noted for its warmth and durability, woven from the wool of the highland herds, and it is among the goods traded across the inland seas.

## The Breadbasket of the Southern Reach

Ashaar is the southernmost settlement of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) and the centre of agriculture and fishing for the southern basin. Its position on the shore of the Lesser Inland Sea and its distance from the caravan routes have preserved a way of life more self-sufficient and traditional than that of the other settlements. Its prosperity is built upon the fertility of the land and the richness of the waters. Ashaar's significance lies in its role as the breadbasket of the Southern Reach and the most remote settled community in the known world.
