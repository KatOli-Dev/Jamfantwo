## Overview

Dustford is a southern river town of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), situated at the edge of the grasslands where the cultivated land of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) gives way to the [Emberlands](/content/location/settlement/region/emberlands). The town stands at a crossing of the river marking the boundary between the fertile plains and the arid margins. Its position at the edge of the grasslands has made it a frontier settlement and a gateway to the warm plains beyond. Dustford is smaller and less prosperous than the cities of the central grasslands but is a thriving and self-sufficient community whose trade connects the grasslands to the regions beyond.

## The River and the Frontier

The river flowing past Dustford is the same that waters the southern plains, but at this point the current is slower, the banks are lower, and the water is warm and turbid with silt from the dry country upstream. The crossing at Dustford is the last reliable ford on the river before it enters the arid margins. The bridge spanning the water is a modest structure maintained by the town for the traffic crossing between the grasslands and the dry country.

The frontier Dustford marks is not a political boundary but a geographical one: the cultivated land of the southern plains ends and the warm, dry country begins at the river crossing. Traffic passing through the town includes grain and livestock of the grasslands exported to the dry country and exotic products of the warm regions imported to the grasslands and northern markets. Caravans crossing the dry country are provisioned at Dustford, the last place where water and supplies may be obtained before the journey into the arid lands, a service that is a significant part of the local economy.

The river at Dustford is also a source of fish. The fishermen maintain a small fleet of boats working the deeper pools and slower reaches of the waterway. The fish are dried and salted in curing houses lining the riverbank, and the dried fish are a minor export finding a market among the caravans and settlements of the dry country, where fresh fish is scarce.

## Trade and the Town

Dustford's trade combines agricultural products of the grasslands with goods imported from the warm regions beyond the frontier. The town is a centre for the livestock trade; the stockyards lining the riverbank are the gathering point for cattle and sheep driven from the grasslands to the markets of the dry country. Wool and leather produced in the surrounding region are also traded at Dustford. The town is known for the quality of its leather, tanned using bark from trees growing along the river. The tanneries of Dustford are a valued industry, producing durable and supple leather prized for saddles, harnesses, and boots made from it by the town's craftsmen.

The inns of Dustford are a significant part of the local economy, for the caravans passing through require lodging, provisions, and the care of their animals. The innkeepers are accustomed to the needs of travellers from the grasslands and the dry country, and the inns are equipped with the stables, storerooms, and kitchens required to serve the frontier traffic. The innkeepers are also sources of news and information, for the caravans bring tidings of the regions they have travelled, making the innkeepers among the best-informed people in the southern plains.

Dustford is a small, rugged town whose buildings are constructed of light-coloured stone and sun-baked clay, the materials of the dry country, with thick walls and small windows providing insulation against the heat. The streets are broad and dusty, for the town sits at the edge of the grasslands and the dust of the dry country is a constant presence. The population is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a small community of [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) involved in the livestock trade. The centaur kindreds of the southern plains are the most frequent visitors to the town, and the stockyards and the market are the meeting point where settled and nomadic peoples conduct their business.

## Significance

Dustford is the primary gateway between the southern grasslands and the warm, dry country beyond, and its trade connects the agricultural economy of the plains to the regions to the south. The town is the southernmost settlement of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), and its position at the edge of the grasslands gives it a commercial significance that belies its modest size. The prosperity of Dustford is built upon the trade that passes through the town, and its people are proud of their role as the frontier community of the southern plains.

## History

Dustford was founded as a crossing point on the river, and the ford that gave the town its name was the original reason for the settlement. The first inhabitants were herdsmen who drove their livestock across the river on the way to the grazing lands of the dry country; the ford was the last reliable crossing before the river entered the arid margins. The bridge was built in a later age, when trade between the grasslands and the dry country had grown sufficiently to justify the expense, transforming the crossing from a seasonal passage to a year-round route.

The town grew slowly, for the edge of the grasslands is a marginal land, and the population has always been small. The people of Dustford have endured the hardships of the frontier with quiet determination, and the town has prospered through the trade passing through its streets. The relationship between the town and the centaur kindreds of the plains has been a defining feature of Dustford's history; the mutual benefit of the livestock trade has fostered cooperation that has sustained the community through lean years and dry seasons that are a part of life at the edge of the grasslands.

The character of Dustford has been shaped by its frontier position. The people are practical and resourceful, accustomed to solving problems with the materials at hand. The buildings are constructed to withstand the heat and the dust, with thick walls of sun-baked clay and light-coloured stone that reflect the sun's rays. The broad streets are designed to accommodate the herds and caravans that pass through the town; the stockyards and tanneries are situated at the edge of the settlement, where the smells and wastes of the animal trade do not inconvenience the residents. The market square is the centre of social life, where the settled farmers and the nomadic centaurs meet to conduct business and exchange news. The inns are the gathering places for travellers, and the stories told by the caravan drivers are the entertainment of the long evenings.
