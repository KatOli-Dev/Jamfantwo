## Overview

Vargvik is a remote coastal town on the northern coast of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), situated on a stretch of shore that is among the most isolated and inhospitable in the known world. The town stands at the head of a narrow inlet cutting deep into the cliffs; its harbour is sheltered but difficult to reach, for the approach requires navigation through a maze of reefs and shoals that have wrecked many vessels. Vargvik is the northernmost permanent settlement in Morvind, and its people are among the most hardy and self-reliant in the known world.

## The Harbour and the Isolation

The harbour of Vargvik is a deep, narrow inlet carved into the cliffs of the coast by a glacier that once occupied the valley. The entrance is so constricted that it is invisible from more than a few hundred yards offshore. The inlet provides excellent shelter from storms, but the reefs and shoals surrounding the entrance make the approach dangerous. The pilots of Vargvik are among the most skilled in Morvind, for they must navigate a passage barely wider than the vessels that use it.

The isolation of Vargvik is the defining feature of the town. The settlement is cut off from the rest of Morvind by the mountains rising behind it and the sea before it. The overland routes connecting Vargvik to other settlements are few and difficult; the town is dependent on the sea for contact with the wider world. Vessels calling at Vargvik are few, and the trade passing through the harbour is modest, but the town is self-sufficient in the essentials of life, and the people have learned to live on what the land and sea provide.

The pilots of Vargvik are the most skilled in Morvind, for the passage through the reefs and shoals guarding the entrance is among the most difficult navigations in the known world. Pilots are trained from childhood; knowledge of the channel is passed down from generation to generation, and the skill is a matter of practice and instinct acquired only through years of experience on the water. The pilots are the most respected figures in the community, for the safety of the fishing boats and the few trading vessels calling at the harbour depends upon their expertise.

## Fishing and Survival

The fishing grounds off Vargvik are productive; the cold offshore waters sustain shoals that are the primary food source of the town. The catch is supplemented by hunting marine mammals inhabiting the coast, including seals and walruses that are a valued source of food, oil, and material. The hunting of these animals is dangerous and demanding, and the hunters of Vargvik are among the most skilled and courageous in Morvind.

The town is also sustained by hunting the [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines) of the cold steppes behind the mountains. Occasional trade between the coastal humans and the steppe lupines is a valued if rare event. The lupines provide fur and meat of the steppe in exchange for fish and oil of the coast; the exchange is conducted at the highland passes connecting the coast to the interior during the brief summer.

The marine mammals hunted off the coast include seals and walruses that provide food, oil, and material. The oil rendered from blubber is a valued commodity used for lamps and heating of the town; the surplus is exported to the few traders calling at the harbour. Hides and bones of the animals are also used: hides are worked into clothing and coverings, and bones are carved into tools and implements used in fishing and hunting.

## History and the Coast

Vargvik was founded in the early centuries of Morvind coastal settlement, when the narrow inlet was recognised as a valuable harbour on a coast where natural harbours are scarce. The early settlement was a fishing community; the first permanent buildings were constructed on the shore of the inlet. The growth of the town was slow, for the isolation and severity of the environment deterred all but the most hardy settlers, and the population has remained small throughout its history. The town was incorporated into the [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) in the year 501, but the distance from the capital means the town is largely self-governing; the authority of the kingdom is exercised through a local council elected by the householders of the community.

## The Town

Vargvik is a small, rugged town whose buildings are constructed of the dark stone of the cliffs, with walls thicker and roofs steeper than those of any other settlement in Morvind, for the weather here is the most severe in the known world. The streets are narrow and winding, and the town is built into the cliff, with buildings climbing the slope from the harbour to the plateau above. The population is small and entirely [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the people are known for their extraordinary hardihood and deep attachment to the remote coast that is their home.

## The Lupines and the Passes

The [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines) of the cold steppes behind the mountains are the nearest neighbours of Vargvik. Occasional trade between the coastal humans and the steppe lupines is a valued if rare event. The lupines are a nomadic people ranging across the cold steppes in patterns governed by the movement of herds and seasons; their visits to the highland passes are unpredictable and infrequent. Trade between the two peoples is conducted at the passes connecting the coast to the interior during the brief summer; the exchanges are made in the open air, for neither people is accustomed to the dwellings of the other.

The relationship between the coastal humans and the steppe lupines is one of cautious respect. The two peoples are separated by the mountains and the differences of their ways of life; contact is limited to brief exchanges at the passes. The lupines are a fierce and independent people, and the humans of Vargvik have learned to respect their ways and value the trade they bring, for the fur and meat of the steppe are the most valuable commodities the town acquires from the interior.

## Culture and Survival

The culture of Vargvik is shaped by the isolation and severity of the environment. The community is noted for its self-reliance and attachment to traditions that have sustained the town through the centuries. The songs and stories of Vargvik are the songs and stories of the sea and the hunt; the tradition of storytelling is a valued part of cultural life, for the long winter evenings are spent sharing old tales and singing old songs. The stories tell of great storms striking the coast, wrecks found on the reefs, and the skill of hunters who braved ice and currents in pursuit of marine mammals that sustain the town.

The festivals of Vargvik are modest affairs, but the annual feast of the midwinter is a cherished tradition marking the turning of the year and the beginning of the long retreat toward spring. The feast is held in the great hall of the town, and the entire community gathers for the occasion, for the midwinter is the darkest and coldest time of the year, and the feast is a celebration of the endurance and solidarity that sustain the community through the months of isolation.

## Significance

Vargvik is the northernmost permanent settlement in Morvind, and its people are the guardians of one of the most remote and inhospitable coastlines in the known world. The town is a member of the [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven), though its distance from the capital means it is largely self-governing. The significance of Vargvik lies in its survival; the town is a testament to the capacity of humans to endure and thrive in the most demanding environments the world can offer.
