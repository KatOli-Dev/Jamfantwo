## Overview

Vargvik is a remote coastal town on the northern coast of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), situated on a stretch of shore that is among the most isolated and most inhospitable in the known world. The town stands at the head of a narrow inlet that cuts deep into the cliffs, and its harbour is sheltered but difficult to reach, for the approach requires navigation through a maze of reefs and shoals that have wrecked many vessels. Vargvik is the northernmost permanent settlement in Morvind, and its people are among the most hardy and the most self-reliant in the known world.

The isolation of Vargvik is the defining feature of the town, for the settlement is cut off from the rest of Morvind by the mountains that rise behind it and the sea that lies before it. The overland routes that connect Vargvik to the other settlements of the coast are few and difficult, and the town is dependent on the sea for its contact with the wider world. The vessels that call at Vargvik are few, and the trade that passes through the harbour is modest, but the town is self-sufficient in the essentials of life, and the people of Vargvik have learned to live on what the land and the sea provide.

The pilots of Vargvik are the most skilled in Morvind, for the passage through the reefs and the shoals that guard the entrance to the inlet is among the most difficult navigations in the known world. The founding of Vargvik is dated to the early centuries of the Morvind coastal settlement, when the narrow inlet was first recognised as a valuable harbour on a coast where natural harbours are scarce. The streets are narrow and winding, and the town is built into the cliff, with the buildings climbing the slope from the harbour to the plateau above. ## Culture and the Survival

The culture of Vargvik is shaped by the isolation and the severity of the environment, and the community is noted for its self-reliance and its attachment to the traditions that have sustained the town through the centuries. The songs and the stories of Vargvik are the songs and the stories of the sea and the hunt, and the tradition of storytelling is a valued part of the cultural life of the community, for the long winter evenings are spent in the sharing of the old tales and the singing of the old songs. The stories tell of the great storms that have struck the coast, the wrecks that have been found on the reefs, and the skill of the hunters who have braved the ice and the currents in pursuit of the marine mammals that sustain the town.

The festivals of Vargvik are modest affairs, for the population is small and the resources are limited, but the annual feast of the midwinter is a cherished tradition that marks the turning of the year and the beginning of the long retreat toward the spring. The feast is held in the great hall of the town, and the entire community gathers for the occasion, for the midwinter is the darkest and the coldest time of the year, and the feast is a celebration of the endurance and the solidarity that sustain the community through the months of isolation.

## Significance

Vargvik is the northernmost permanent settlement in Morvind, and its people are the guardians of one of the most remote and most inhospitable coastlines in the known world. The town is a member of the [Kingdom of Nordhaven](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-nordhaven), though its distance from the capital means that the town is largely self-governing. The significance of Vargvik lies in its survival, for the town is a testament to the capacity of humans to endure and to thrive in the most demanding environments that the world can offer.
