## Overview

Torshavn is a southeastern port of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), situated on the coast near the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) where the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) meets the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea). The town is the southernmost of the Morvind coastal settlements, and its position near the strait gives it a role in the strait trade secondary to [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch) but nonetheless significant. Torshavn is a fishing and trading port, and its harbour is a valued stopping point for vessels approaching or leaving the strait from the south.

## The Harbour and the Fishing

The harbour of Torshavn is a broad, open bay sheltered by a headland extending from the southern end of the town; the approach is straightforward in most weather, though the currents running near the strait can affect the passage. Quays line the inner shore of the bay, and the fishing boats and trading vessels moored there are the primary assets of the town. The fishing grounds off Torshavn are productive, for the mixing of warm waters of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and cold waters of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) near the strait creates a rich and diverse marine environment.

The fishing fleet of Torshavn is the largest on the southeastern coast; boats venture into waters near the strait where fish are most abundant. The catch is landed at the harbour, salted and dried in curing houses lining the waterfront, and exported to inland markets and the ports of the strait. The fish of Torshavn is a valued commodity in the strait towns, where demand for provisions is constant and supply from local fishing grounds is insufficient.

The fishing grounds off Torshavn are shaped by the mixing of warm Auric waters and cold Stormfall waters near the strait. The convergence of the two currents creates a diverse marine environment sustaining a great variety of fish. Seasonal patterns of fishing are governed by the movement of currents and the migration of fish; the fishermen have developed a detailed knowledge of the waters passed down from generation to generation. The spring run of tuna is the most valuable catch of the year, and the autumn catch is dominated by herring passing through the strait on their southward migration.

## Trade and the Strait

Torshavn's trade combines fishing with commerce flowing through the strait. The town is a natural stopping point for vessels approaching the strait from the south or leaving it for the open ocean. The harbour is a staging point for vessels preparing to enter the strait; the inns, taverns, and chandleries serving mariners are a significant part of the local economy. The town is also a centre for repair and supply of fishing boats and smaller vessels working the southeastern coast; the boatbuilders of Torshavn are noted for their skill in constructing sturdy, seaworthy craft. The boatyards at the southern end of the harbour are the most active part of the town, for fishing boats require constant maintenance and vessels passing through the strait are often in need of repair.

## History and the Strait

Torshavn was founded in the early centuries of Morvind coastal settlement, when the broad bay was recognised as a valuable harbour on the southeastern coast. The early settlement was a fishing community; the first permanent buildings were the curing houses and quays serving the fishing trade. The town grew steadily, and the development of the strait trade in the centuries that followed brought additional prosperity. The town was a founding member of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), which has governed the strait trade and the management of the shared waters surrounding the town.

The history of Torshavn is a history of fishing and trade; the town has never been threatened by war or disturbance, and the community has devoted its energies to improving fishing and expanding trade. The construction of stone quays lining the harbour was undertaken in the year 423, marking the transformation of Torshavn from a fishing village into a significant port. The boatyards were established in the following century, and the boatbuilders have been a valued part of the local economy ever since.

The population is chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), a mixture of fishermen and traders, with a small community of [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) involved in the metal trade and the management of overland routes connecting the port to the interior. The [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) of the strait waters are occasionally encountered by the fishermen, and a cautious but peaceful coexistence has developed between the two peoples.

## The Town

Torshavn is a small, well-built town whose architecture reflects the coastal tradition of Morvind: sturdy buildings of dark stone, steep roofs, and narrow streets sheltered from the wind. The town is oriented toward the harbour, and the market square is situated at the head of the main quay where the fishing trade and the strait commerce meet. The town is a member of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), and its role in the strait trade is a valued supplement to its fishing economy.

## The Merfolk and the Waters

The [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) of the strait waters are occasionally encountered by the fishermen of Torshavn; a cautious but peaceful coexistence has developed over the centuries. The merfolk are the inhabitants of the deeper waters, and their knowledge of currents and fish movements is a valuable resource for the fishermen. Encounters between fishermen and merfolk are rare and conducted with wariness reflecting the differences between the two peoples, but the exchanges are mutually beneficial, and the merfolk have been known to warn fishermen of approaching storms and the movement of shoals.

The relationship is governed by a set of unwritten customs developed over the centuries, respected by both peoples. The fishermen of Torshavn do not fish in waters recognised as merfolk territory; the merfolk do not interfere with fishing boats working grounds outside their territory. The customs are observed as scrupulously as any written code, for both peoples recognise that the peace between them is too valuable to disrupt.

## Culture and the Sea

The culture of Torshavn is shaped by the sea and the strait; the town is oriented entirely toward the surrounding waters, and the rhythms of the community are the rhythms of fishing and tides. The people are a mixture of fishermen and traders, bound together by the shared labour of fishing and the shared dangers of the strait. Festivals are modest affairs, but the annual feast of the first catch is a cherished tradition marking the beginning of the spring season and the return of tuna to the strait.

The boatbuilders of Torshavn are the most respected craftsmen in the town, for the fishing boats and smaller vessels built in the yards are the foundation of the local economy; the skill of the builders is a matter of pride for the community. The boats of Torshavn are noted for their sturdy construction and seaworthiness; the reputation of the boatbuilders extends along the southeastern coast and into the strait towns. The boats are built of dark timber imported from the interior, and the construction is a slow, careful process suited to the demands of the waters the boats will work.

## Significance

Torshavn is the southernmost port of Morvind and a valued stopping point for vessels navigating waters near the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden). The town is a member of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), and its fishing and trading economy is a modest but valuable contribution to the commerce of the strait region. The prosperity of Torshavn is built upon the richness of the surrounding waters and the traffic passing through the nearby strait, and the community is a model of the maritime tradition that characterises the Morvind coast.
