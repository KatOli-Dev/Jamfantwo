## A Market Town at a River Crossing

Mill Cross is a market town of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) situated at the crossing of the River Mill, a tributary of the Greywash, at the point where the road from the eastern lowlands meets the road from the southern hills. The town takes its name from the mill that stands at the crossing, a substantial structure of stone and timber that has ground the grain of the surrounding farms for centuries. Mill Cross is a settlement of modest size but considerable importance in the local economy, serving as a market and meeting point for the farming communities of the river valley.

The crossing at Mill Cross is the most reliable ford on the River Mill for many miles in either direction, and the convergence of roads at the crossing has made the town a natural centre for the trade of the region. The roads that meet at Mill Cross carry the traffic of the eastern lowlands and the southern hills, and the market that has grown up at the crossing attracts farmers, traders, and craftsmen from the surrounding country.

## The River Mill and the Stone Ford

The River Mill rises in the southern hills and flows northward through a broad, shallow valley to join the Greywash at a point some miles below Mill Cross. The river is not large by the standards of the region, but it is a significant watercourse in the local landscape, providing water for the farms and villages of the valley and powering the mills that process the grain of the region. The river at Mill Cross is broad and shallow, flowing over a bed of gravel and stone that provides a firm surface for crossing.

The ford at Mill Cross has been improved over the centuries by the addition of stone paving that provides a smooth and stable surface for the passage of wagons and livestock. The ford is wide enough for two wagons to pass abreast, and the approaches on both sides have been graded to provide easy access to the water. The ford is passable for most of the year, but the spring floods can raise the water level above the point of safety, and the town maintains a small ferry boat that can be used to cross the river during the flood season.

## The Mill at the Crossing

The mill that gives the town its name stands on the eastern bank of the river, immediately above the ford. The mill is a substantial structure of stone and timber, built in a design that has been common in the region for centuries. The mill is powered by an undershot water wheel that draws its power from the flow of the river, and the millstones that grind the grain are driven through a system of gears and shafts that transmit the power of the wheel to the millstones.

The mill is the principal industrial establishment of Mill Cross and the source of the town's prosperity. The millers of Mill Cross are among the most skilled in the Greywash valley, and the flour produced by the mill is prized for its quality throughout the region. The mill operates throughout the year, grinding the grain brought by the farmers of the surrounding country and producing the flour that is the staple of the local diet.

## The Weekly Market and the Seasonal Fairs

The weekly market of Mill Cross is held in the market square, an open space at the centre of the town where the roads from the east and south converge. The market square is surrounded by the principal buildings of the town, including the market hall, the tavern, and the houses of the principal merchants. The stalls and booths erected on market day offer the produce of the farms of the valley, the goods of the craftsmen of the town, and the wares of the travelling merchants who pass through Mill Cross on their way to the larger markets of the region.

The seasonal fairs of Mill Cross are the principal events of the commercial calendar, marking the changes of the agricultural year. The spring fair is held at the time of the planting, when the farmers of the valley gather to purchase seed, tools, and livestock for the coming season. The autumn fair is held after the harvest, when the farmers bring their surplus grain and produce to the market for sale and exchange. The autumn fair is the larger of the two, drawing merchants and buyers from as far afield as the eastern cities.

## The Weavers, the Smiths, and the Craftsmen of Mill Cross

The craftsmen of Mill Cross are a valued component of the local economy, producing the goods and services that the farming communities of the valley require. The weavers of Mill Cross produce cloth from the wool of the sheep that graze on the hills surrounding the valley, and the cloth is sold at the market to the farmers and townspeople of the region. The smiths of Mill Cross produce and repair the tools, implements, and hardware that the farms of the valley require, and the smithy at the crossing is one of the busiest establishments in the town.

The craftsmen of Mill Cross are organised into guilds that regulate the standards of the crafts and the conduct of trade. The master craftsmen of Mill Cross are among the most respected figures in the community, for the skills they possess are essential to the functioning of the local economy.

## The Stone Houses and the Streets of Mill Cross

The architecture of Mill Cross reflects the rural character of the settlement and the materials that are available in the region. The buildings of the town are constructed of stone and timber, with stone foundations and timber frames, walls of plaster and wattle, and roofs of thatch or stone tile. The houses are modest in size and design, built to provide comfortable shelter for the families that inhabit them.

The streets of Mill Cross are narrow and winding, following the lines of the roads that converge on the crossing. The main street runs from the ford to the market square, lined with the houses of the merchants and the workshops of the craftsmen. The side streets lead to the residential districts of the town, where the houses of the farmers and the labourers stand in rows along lanes that are unpaved and dusty in summer and muddy in winter.

## The Governance of Mill Cross

Mill Cross is governed by a council of elders elected by the adult inhabitants of the town and the surrounding parish. The council is responsible for the administration of the town, the maintenance of the ford and the mill, and the regulation of the market. The council meets in the market hall, a building of stone and timber that stands at one side of the market square.

The miller holds a position of particular importance in the governance of Mill Cross, for the mill is the most valuable and most important establishment in the town. The miller is a member of the council and holds a voice in all decisions that affect the operation of the mill and the trade in grain and flour. The miller is also responsible for the maintenance of the ford, on which the mill depends for the flow of water that powers the wheel.

## Mill Cross as the Heart of the River Valley

Mill Cross is the heart of the River Mill valley and the principal market of the surrounding farming communities. The town's position at the crossing of the river and at the convergence of the roads from the lowlands and the hills has made it a natural centre for the trade of the region. The mill that gives the town its name is the symbol of Mill Cross, and the flour produced by the mill is the foundation of the town's prosperity. The craftsmen and farmers of Mill Cross are among the most hardworking and reliable in the Greywash valley, and the town they have built at the crossing of the River Mill is a settlement of modest but enduring prosperity.
