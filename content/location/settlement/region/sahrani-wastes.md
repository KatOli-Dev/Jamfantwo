## The Deep and Unmapped Interior of Sahrana

The [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) Wastes are the deep and largely unmapped interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), the great belt of desert and scrub beyond the known caravan routes and beyond the settled territories of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact). The wastes take their name from the continent itself, and the word is used to describe the country at the far edge of the caravan maps, where routes grow uncertain and the names of landmarks are known only to the nomads who traverse them. The Sahrani Wastes are not a single region but the sum of the unmapped places: the deep desert, the salt flats, and the rocky uplands extending toward the inland seas and the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) in the far south.

The wastes are among the most demanding environments in the known world, and the peoples who inhabit them are few and scattered. The [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) of the deep desert and the [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) nomads travelling with them are the primary inhabitants, their knowledge of routes and water sources unmatched by any outsider. The communities dwelling in the wastes have no written records and no formal governance, their way of life shaped by the demands of the desert and the ancient traditions of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes).

## Dunes, Reg, Salt Flats, and Rocky Uplands

The Sahrani Wastes are a desert of many characters, their appearance changing dramatically from one part to another. In some places the land is a true erg, a vast field of shifting dunes whose crests rise hundreds of feet above the interdune corridors. In other places the ground is flat and stony, a reg of pebbles and gravel extending to the horizon without relief. The salt flats lying in deeper depressions are perhaps the most desolate, their surfaces cracking into sharp polygons that cut the feet of the unwary and the hooves of animals straying onto them.

The rocky uplands rising from the sands are the most habitable parts of the wastes. The springs emerging at the base of these uplands are fed by deep aquifers, constant and reliable regardless of seasonal rainfall. The valleys lying between the uplands are sheltered from wind and sand, and gardens cultivated in the most sheltered produce dates and figs valued by passing caravans. The climate is one of extremes, even by the standards of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). The summer sun beats down with a ferocity that can kill the unprepared within hours, and the surface temperature of the dunes can burn unprotected skin. Winter nights are bitterly cold, for sand radiates heat rapidly into the clear sky. Rainfall is rare and unpredictable.

## The Salawa, the Human Nomads, and the Sands' Assembly

The deep desert is the domain of the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa), whose keen senses and endurance are ideally suited to the wastes. They travel in family groups, following routes established over generations of oral tradition and adjusted according to water sources and the movement of game. The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the deep desert are fewer, coexisting with the salawa in mutual respect and cooperation, sharing water sources and grazing grounds according to the conventions of the Kharesh Tribes.

The confederation of the Kharesh Tribes extends its authority through the council of elders convening at the [Sands' Assembly](/content/culture/sands-assembly), a gathering held at a designated oasis at a time determined by the movement of the tribes and the condition of water sources. The decisions of the Assembly are binding on all members. The confederation has no permanent force and no fixed capital, its governance resting entirely upon the consent of the peoples who belong to it and the accumulated wisdom of the elders who lead them. The [orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) of the arid interior are present in the wilder reaches, their nomadic existence similar in form but distinct in tradition. Encounters between the peoples are rare and generally avoided.

## Springs, Wells, and the Few Caravan Routes

The water sources of the wastes are the foundation of life. The springs emerging at the base of rocky uplands are most reliable, constant through the seasons. Wells sunk into the desert floor are less reliable, for the water table lies far below the surface and the work of drawing water is exhausting. The seasonal pools forming in depressions after rains are temporary but attract nomads who know the desert.

The caravan routes passing through the wastes are few and difficult, followed only by the most experienced caravan masters. The routes connect the inland cities of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) with the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), the federation of settled communities on the shores of the inland seas. The caravans carry spices and dyes of the deep south to northern markets and return with grain, cloth, and manufactured goods. The journey takes many weeks, and the margin between sufficiency and disaster is narrow. The caravans are guided by the salawa and human nomads, and the fees paid for their services are a significant source of income for the kindreds of the Kharesh Tribes.

## Heat, Mirages, Sandstorms, and the Salawa Remedies

The summer heat is the most immediate danger; a traveller caught in the open desert without water can be dead within hours. The mirages rising from heated sand are a constant danger to the inexperienced, leading them to walk for hours toward what appears to be water only to find nothing when the vision fades. Sandstorms can strip the flesh from an unprotected face in minutes, and caravans caught in such a storm must halt and cover themselves until the wind passes.

The wildlife is adapted to harshness. Snakes, scorpions, and larger predators are constant hazards. The salawa teach the caravan masters rules observed at every camp: shake out bedding before lying down, check boots before putting them on, never reach into a crevice without looking. The herbs and medicines the salawa carry are the most effective treatment for bites and stings.

## The Final Frontier of the Known World

The Sahrani Wastes are the final frontier of the known world, the deep interior beyond the reach of settled communities and formal governance. The routes crossing the wastes are essential to trade between north and south, and the peoples dwelling in the wastes are the guardians of those routes. The significance lies in the mastery of the desert achieved by the salawa and human nomads, a mastery no outsider has replicated, and in the covenants of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) that keep the routes open and the water sources available. The wastes are both a barrier and a bridge, a region whose harshness is matched only by the resilience of the peoples who call it home.
