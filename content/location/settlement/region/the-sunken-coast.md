## A Low-Lying Coastal Region Prone to Flooding

The Sunken Coast is a low-lying coastal region of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) that lies along the southern shore of the continent, where the land meets the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The region takes its name from its elevation, which is so low that the land appears to have sunk beneath the level of the sea, and the coast is characterised by extensive wetlands, lagoons, and tidal flats that are flooded by the highest tides and the storm surges that sweep in from the ocean. The Sunken Coast is a region of challenging conditions, but it is also a region of abundant resources, and the communities that have established themselves along the coast have developed a distinctive way of life adapted to the water and the marsh.

The Sunken Coast extends for many miles along the southern shore, from the mouth of the [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river) in the west to the approaches of the [Storm Cape](/content/location/natural/feature/cape-of-storms) in the east. The coast is a landscape of broad estuaries, [salt](/content/economy/salt) marshes, and sandy barrier islands, with the open sea separated from the land by a series of lagoons and channels that are navigable by small boats.

## The Subsidence and the Rising Waters

The low elevation of the Sunken Coast is the result of natural processes that have caused the land to sink relative to the sea over a long period. The subsidence of the coast has been gradual, but the effects are visible in the landscape: the drowned valleys that form the estuaries, the salt marshes that have replaced the former farmlands, and the villages that have been abandoned as the water has advanced.

The rising waters of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) have added to the effects of the subsidence, and the coast is now more vulnerable to flooding than it was in earlier centuries. The highest tides and the storm surges that accompany the great storms of the autumn and winter can inundate large areas of the coast, and the communities of the region have developed techniques for living with the water, including the construction of embankments and the raising of houses on stilts.

## The Estuaries and the Lagoons

The estuaries of the Sunken Coast are the drowned valleys of the rivers that flow from the interior to the sea. The estuaries are broad and shallow, with extensive mudflats that are exposed at low tide and channels that are navigable by small boats. The estuaries are the principal routes of transport and communication along the coast, and the communities of the region are connected by the boats that ply the channels.

The lagoons of the Sunken Coast are the bodies of water that lie between the barrier islands and the mainland. The lagoons are sheltered from the open sea, and they provide safe anchorage for boats and a habitat for fish and shellfish. The lagoons are also the sites of the salt pans that produce the salt that is one of the principal products of the region.

## The Communities of the Coast

The communities of the Sunken Coast are small settlements built on the higher ground that rises above the level of the marsh. The settlements are connected by the channels of the estuaries and the lagoons, and the boats that are the principal means of transport are the foundation of the life of the region. The largest settlement of the coast is the town of [Salt Meadows](/content/location/settlement/town/salt-meadows), which serves as a centre of trade and administration for the region.

The other settlements of the coast are fishing villages and farming hamlets, each built on a patch of higher ground that is protected from the highest floods by embankments and drainage works. The villages are small, and the population of the region is sparse, but the communities are self-sufficient and resilient, adapted to the conditions of the coast.

## The Fisheries and the Salt Pans

The fisheries of the Sunken Coast are the principal source of food and income for the communities of the region. The waters of the estuaries and the lagoons are rich in fish and shellfish, and the fishermen of the coast set nets and traps to catch the fish that are the staple of the local diet. The fish of the coast include mullet, bass, and flatfish, and the catch is consumed fresh, smoked, or salted for preservation.

The salt pans of the Sunken Coast are the sites where the salt of the region is produced. The salt pans are shallow basins constructed on the higher ground at the edge of the marsh, where the seawater is admitted and evaporated by the sun to produce the salt that is traded to the interior. The salt of the Sunken Coast is prized for its purity, and the salt trade is an important component of the economy of the region.

## The Embankments and the Drainage Works

The embankments of the Sunken Coast are the works of earth and stone that have been built to protect the settlements and the fields from the flooding of the sea. The embankments are the most important infrastructure of the region, and their maintenance is the principal responsibility of the communities. The embankments are built along the edges of the estuaries and the lagoons, and they are reinforced by the planting of salt-tolerant grasses that bind the soil and absorb the force of the waves.

The drainage works of the Sunken Coast are the channels and ditches that drain the water from the fields and the settlements. The drainage works are essential to the agriculture of the region, for the land must be kept dry enough for the cultivation of crops and the grazing of livestock. The drainage channels are maintained by the labour of the farmers, and the work of cleaning and repairing the channels is a regular obligation of the landholders.

## The Wildlife of the Coast

The Sunken Coast is a region of abundant wildlife, and the estuaries and the lagoons provide habitat for a remarkable diversity of birds, fish, and other animals. The wetlands are the nesting grounds for large numbers of waterfowl, including ducks, geese, and the herons and egrets that are a characteristic sight of the region. The waters of the coast are home to fish, shellfish, and the seals that are the largest marine animals of the region.

The wildlife of the coast is a resource for the communities, providing food and income through hunting and fishing. The birds of the wetlands are hunted for meat and feathers, and the fish and shellfish of the waters are the principal source of protein for the population. The management of the wildlife is regulated by the customs of the communities, and the rights to hunt and fish are allocated among the families of the settlements.

## The Culture of the Sunken Coast

The culture of the Sunken Coast is shaped by the water and the marsh that surround the communities. The people of the coast are skilled in the handling of boats and the navigation of the channels, and the knowledge they possess of the tides and the currents is passed down through the generations. The culture of the region is one of self-reliance and cooperation, for the conditions of the coast demand that the communities work together to maintain the embankments and the drainage works and to share the resources of the wetlands.

The festivals and celebrations of the Sunken Coast are centred on the sea and the marsh, and the most important event of the year is the festival of the first catch, when the fishermen of the coast celebrate the beginning of the fishing season. The festival is held in the spring, when the fish return to the estuaries, and the communities gather to feast and to give thanks for the abundance of the sea.

The estuaries, lagoons, and salt marshes of the coast provide the resources that sustain the communities of the region, and the fisheries and the salt pans are the foundation of the economy. The people of the Sunken Coast have adapted to the challenging conditions of the low-lying shore, and the distinctive culture and way of life they have developed are a testament to the resilience and resourcefulness of the inhabitants of the southern coast.
