## Overview

The Stoneheim Highlands are the upper ranges and the high valleys of the interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), the great belt of mountain country that rises above the river valleys of the continent and is dominated by the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim), the greatest [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) realm in the known world. The highlands take their name from the capital of the dwarven realm, [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim), whose halls are carved into the heart of the range, and the name is used by the chroniclers of both the dwarven and the human world to describe the country that surrounds the great city. The highlands are the workshop of the dwarven people, and the metals and the stone that emerge from their mines are the foundation of the wealth and the reputation of Morvind.

The Stoneheim Highlands are not a single jurisdiction but a mosaic of territories held by the individual holds and clans that make up the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim). The realm is governed from the great hall of Stoneheim, but each hold retains its own affairs and its own traditions, and the highland territory is divided among the great families, the guilds, and the clan communities whose names are recorded in the inscriptions of the halls. The result is a region of strong local loyalties bound together by the common heritage of the dwarven people and the recognition of the king who sits in Stoneheim.

## Geography and Climate

The highlands are a complex of mountain ranges, high plateaus, and deep valleys that extends across the interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) from the approaches to the [Strait of Velden](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) on the southeast to the gates of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) on the far interior. The ranges rise in broken tiers from the river valleys, their slopes clothed in coniferous forest that thins toward the summits and gives way to bare rock and scree. Between the peaks lie sheltered plateaus where the alpine grasses flourish during the brief summer, and the rivers that descend from the heights carve deep gorges through the rock before reaching the lowlands.

The climate of the highlands is among the most demanding in the known world. Winter holds the ranges for most of the year, and the snow lies deep upon the higher slopes long after the valleys have begun to thaw. The summers are short and vivid, releasing torrents of meltwater and covering the sheltered meadows with grass and alpine flowers, and the season is a time of intense activity when the highland passes are open and the trade can move. The growing season on the surface is brief and confined to the lower valleys, where the hardy crops that can tolerate the cold and the thin air are cultivated in terraced plots and sheltered gardens.

The rivers that rise in the highlands are the principal source of the water that sustains the continent, and their flow is constant through the summer months when the snow is melting on the peaks. The rivers descend in white-water torrents through the upper gorges before settling into clearer channels in the lower valleys, and the waterfalls and the rapids of the upper reaches are a distinctive feature of the highland landscape. The valleys that lie below the tree line are forested with pine and spruce, and the timber of these forests is one of the resources that the highland communities manage for their own use and for export to the lowlands.

## Settlements and Population

The settlements of the highlands are of two kinds, corresponding to the two domains of the dwarven people. The underground cities and the mine workings are the principal homes of the dwarven population, and the halls of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) and [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar) are the largest of these. [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) is the capital and the greatest hall, and its chambers and passages extend for many miles into the mountain. [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar) is the second city of the realm, renowned for the quality of its metalwork and the skill of its smiths. The individual holds are smaller communities that are scattered across the highlands, each with its own mine, its own forge, and its own council of elders.

The surface settlements of the highlands are smaller and serve chiefly as the gathering points for the trade and the seasonal activities of the underground communities. [Ironhollow](/content/location/settlement/town/ironhollow) is the principal surface settlement of the eastern ranges, and it is the primary source of the iron that feeds the forges of the realm. The smaller surface villages are scattered across the sheltered valleys and the highland meadows, and they serve as the staging points for the caravans that travel the passes between the interior and the coast. The surface population is small compared to the underground communities, and the people who live above ground are primarily those whose work requires them to be outside, such as the herders, the foresters, and the caravan masters.

The population of the highlands is overwhelmingly [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), with a small number of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) who are involved in the trade and the management of the routes. The human communities are concentrated at the surface settlements, where they serve as the agents of the trading houses of the coast and the points of contact between the dwarven forges and the markets of the lowlands. A few [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) are present in the highland settlements, drawn by the craftsmanship of the dwarven smiths, and their workshops produce the finer instruments and mechanisms that supplement the dwarven work.

## Mining and Metalwork

The mineral wealth of the highlands is the foundation of the prosperity of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim), and the ore that is extracted from the mines of the region includes iron, copper, tin, silver, gold, and the rarer metals that are found only in the deepest veins of the range. The mining operations are the most extensive of any political entity in the known world, and the tunnels that connect the mines to the halls above are works of engineering in their own right, wide enough for the passage of ore carts and ventilated by shafts that are driven through the rock to the surface above. The deepest mines follow the veins of ore as they descend into the mountain, and the miners who work them are among the most skilled in the dwarven world.

The metalwork that is produced in the highland forges is the finest in the known world, and the smiths of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) and [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar) are renowned throughout the continents. The weapons, the tools, and the decorative objects that bear the marks of the highland forges are exported to every continent, and the reputation of the smiths is the foundation of the wealth and the influence of the dwarven realm. The great forges of Stoneheim are devoted to the smelting of the ore and the production of the bulk metals, while the lesser forges are devoted to the finer work of the smiths and the crafting of the most valued products of the realm.

The trade that connects the highlands to the rest of the world is managed through the overland routes that descend from the interior to the coast. The caravans that carry the metalwork of the highlands to the ports of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) are a familiar sight on the highland roads during the open season, and the return cargo includes the grain, the wool, and the cloth that the highland communities cannot produce for themselves. The security of the routes is a constant concern of the dwarven administration, for the wild peoples of the mountains and the highland passes are a recurring threat to the caravans, and the patrols that escort the traffic are a necessary expense of the trade.

## Life in the Highlands

The life of the highland dwarves is shaped by the rhythm of the underground and the brief but vivid summer above. Most of the population lives below ground, where the chambers carved into the rock retain a constant temperature and remain secure during the storms that rage on the surface. The dwellings, the workshops, the forges, and the storehouses are all situated within the mountain, and the surface buildings serve chiefly as the points of entry to the halls and as the shelter for the animals, the timber, and the summer light. The ventilation shafts that are driven through the rock to the surface above provide a constant flow of fresh air, and the lanterns of the forges illuminate the interior with a warm and steady glow.

The opening of the highland passes is the central event of the year, and the communities prepare for the season through the long winter months. The mining crews repair their tools and their lamps, the smiths replenish their stocks of metal and charcoal, and the caravan masters organise their companies for the journey to the coast. When the council declares the passes safe, the activity of the realm intensifies, and the highland roads are filled with the traffic of the trading season. Marriages, apprenticeships, and the settlement of long disputes are arranged in the same brief season, because travel between distant communities is impossible once the snow returns.

The highland festivals are modest but deeply felt, and the gatherings of the clans at the great hall of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) are the most important of the year. The king presides over the assemblies, and the council of hold-elders convenes to hear the reports of the individual holds and to decide the matters that affect the realm as a whole. The feasting and the contests that accompany the assemblies are a reminder of the bonds that unite the highland communities, and the songs that are sung in the great hall preserve the history and the traditions of the dwarven people of Morvind.

## Significance

The Stoneheim Highlands are the heart of the dwarven civilisation in [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and the mineral wealth and the craftsmanship of the region are the foundation of the economy and the reputation of the continent. The highlands are not merely a source of raw materials but a community of skill and tradition, and the smiths and the miners who work the highland forges are among the most respected craftsmen in the known world. The realm's trade connects the interior to the coast and the wider world, and the metalwork that emerges from the highland forges is the foundation of the prosperity of the continent and the envy of every other realm.

The significance of the highlands lies in the scale and the quality of the dwarven achievement. The halls that are carved into the mountains are among the most magnificent works of any age, and the forges that burn within them are the source of the weapons, the tools, and the objects that have shaped the history of the known world. The highland dwarves have endured through centuries of challenge and have maintained their traditions and their skills against every difficulty, and the Stoneheim Highlands remain a symbol of the dwarven capacity to impose order upon the mountain landscape and to extract from it the wealth that sustains their civilisation.
