## The Border Region Between the Heartwood and Settled Lands

The Heartwood March is a region of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) that lies at the boundary between the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood), the great forest that covers the central highlands, and the settled farmlands of the lowlands. The march is a transitional zone of forest and field, a landscape shaped by the centuries-long interaction between the woodland and the communities that have established themselves at its edge. The Heartwood March is not a political unit but a geographical and cultural region, defined by the character of the land and the people who inhabit it.

The Heartwood March extends along the western and southern fringes of the great forest, following the line where the trees give way to the open country. The march varies in width from a few miles to many miles, depending on the topography and the history of settlement. The region is a patchwork of woodland, pasture, and field, with the forest pressing in on the settled areas and the settled areas pushing back into the forest.

## The Border Between Forest and Field

The border between the Heartwood and the settled lands is not a fixed line but a shifting zone of transition. The forest advances into the fields when settlement recedes, and the fields advance into the forest when settlement expands. The history of the Heartwood March is the history of this advance and retreat, and the landscape of the region bears the marks of the struggle between the woodland and the people who seek to clear it.

The border is marked by hedgerows and banks, ditches and fences, the works of generations of farmers and landowners who have sought to define the boundary between the forest and the fields. The hedgerows are thick with hawthorn and blackthorn, providing a barrier that prevents the livestock from straying into the forest and the deer from entering the fields. The banks are built of earth and stone, raised above the level of the surrounding land, and the ditches are dug to drain the fields and to provide a barrier to the advance of the forest.

## The Settlements of the March

The settlements of the Heartwood March are villages and hamlets that have grown at the edge of the forest, their inhabitants living by a combination of farming, woodland management, and the crafts that the forest provides. The settlements are small, for the land that has been cleared from the forest is limited, and the fields that surround them are scattered among the remnants of the woodland.

The largest settlement of the march is the town of [Pinehold](/content/location/settlement/town/pinehold), which serves as a centre of trade and administration for the region. The town is situated at a point where the forest opens out into a broad valley, and its market attracts farmers, timber merchants, and craftsmen from across the march. The other settlements of the march are smaller, each serving the needs of the surrounding farms and woodlands.

## The Economy of the March

The economy of the Heartwood March is based on the products of the forest and the fields. The timber of the Heartwood is the principal resource of the region, and the logging and processing of timber are the most important industries of the march. The timber is harvested in the forest and brought to the settlements for processing, and the planks, beams, and other wood products are exported to the cities of the lowlands.

The fields of the march produce grain, vegetables, and the fodder that sustains the livestock of the farmers. The soils of the march are thinner and more acidic than the soils of the open lowlands, but the land is fertile enough to support the farming that is the foundation of the local economy. The livestock of the march includes cattle, sheep, and pigs, and the animals are raised for meat, milk, wool, and leather.

## The Forest Crafts of the March

The forest crafts of the Heartwood March are the skills that the inhabitants have developed for the working of wood and the other products of the forest. The woodcutters of the march are skilled in the felling and processing of timber, and the carpenters and joiners of the region produce the furniture, fittings, and tools that are used in the settlements and exported to the lowlands.

The charcoal burners of the march produce the charcoal that is used for fuel in the forges and the kitchens of the region. The charcoal is produced by the slow burning of wood in covered pits, and the burners are among the most skilled of the forest craftsmen. The resin collectors of the march gather the resin that is used for the sealing of barrels and the waterproofing of ships, and the resin of the Heartwood is prized for its quality.

## The People of the March

The people of the Heartwood March are descended from the settlers who first cleared the land from the forest and established the farms and villages of the region. The people of the march are known for their hardiness and their independence, and the life they lead has shaped a distinctive character and culture. The inhabitants of the march are skilled in the crafts of the forest and the fields, and the knowledge they possess of the woodland and its resources is passed down through the generations.

The population of the march is predominantly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a smaller number of [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) who inhabit the deeper portions of the forest. The elves are the original inhabitants of the Heartwood, and their relationship with the human settlers of the march is a complex one, shaped by centuries of coexistence and conflict.

## The Governance of the March

The governance of the Heartwood March is divided among the settlements and the landowners of the region, with the council of Pinehold holding the principal authority over the affairs of the march. The council is responsible for the regulation of the timber trade, the maintenance of the roads and bridges, and the resolution of disputes between the settlements. The council meets in Pinehold, and its decisions are binding on the communities of the march.

The laws and customs of the march are based on the traditions of the forest communities, and they reflect the conditions of life at the edge of the woodland. The rights to fell timber, to graze livestock, and to hunt game are regulated by the customs of the march, and the disputes that arise over these rights are resolved by the council and the elders of the settlements.

## The Heartwood March as a Region of Transition

The Heartwood March is a region of transition, a borderland between the forest and the field, the wilderness and the settlement. The landscape of the march is a testament to the centuries-long interaction between the woodland and the people who have made their homes at its edge. The timber, the charcoal, and the other products of the forest are the foundation of the economy of the march, and the skills of the forest craftsmen are the heritage of the people. The march is a region of hard work and modest prosperity, a place where the people have learned to live in balance with the forest that surrounds them.
