## Overview

The Frostwall Marches are the high borderlands of the deep interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), a country of escarpments, glaciated plateaus, and narrow passes held by the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans). The marches take their name from the Frostwall, a long face of dark stone and ice that divides the central uplands from the remote valleys beyond. They do not cross the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) and contain no coastal territory; the word marches refers to the defended mountain approaches between the settled holds and the cold steppes of the far interior.

## Geography and Climate

The Frostwall rises in broken tiers across the interior ranges, in places presenting a sheer cliff several hundred feet high and elsewhere dissolving into ridges, ravines, and steep-sided valleys. Only a small number of passes cross the escarpment, and each is commanded by gates, watchtowers, and refuge halls cut into the rock. Above the wall lie wind-scoured plateaus and high valleys whose rivers descend through narrow gorges toward the more settled ranges of Morvind.

The climate is among the coldest in the known world. Winter holds the marches for most of the year, burying the passes and freezing the rivers into roads that can be used only by travellers who know the hidden cracks and snow-covered falls. Summer is brief and bright, releasing torrents of meltwater and covering the sheltered meadows with grass and alpine flowers. The passes open for only a few months, and the movement of caravans, patrols, and mining crews is concentrated into that narrow season.

## Settlements and Population

[Grimhold](/content/location/settlement/city/grimhold) is the principal fortress-city of the marches and the seat of the clan council. Its gates command the most important passage through the Frostwall, and its garrison maintains the watchtowers and refuge halls of the surrounding ranges. [Ravenscrag](/content/location/settlement/village/ravenscrag) is the most remote permanent settlement, a mining community in a far valley whose approaches are closed by snow for much of the year. Smaller clan holds, seasonal workings, and patrol stations occupy the remaining habitable valleys.

The population is overwhelmingly [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), organised by clan and territory, though a small number of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) serve as routekeepers and traders at Ravenscrag and the outer stations. [Orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) and [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines) range across the cold steppes beyond the defended passes, and contact with the clans varies from guarded trade to open hostility. The hard geography of the marches gives every community a military purpose, whether the defence of a gate, the maintenance of a road, or the protection of a mine.

## Economy

Mining is the foundation of the Frostwall economy. The veins exposed by the escarpment yield iron, copper, silver, and rarer metals, and the clan forges work much of the ore into tools, weapons, and armour before it leaves the region. Grimhold is renowned for heavy plate and defensive ironwork, while Ravenscrag supplies smaller quantities of ore and the practical tools required by the highland communities.

Trade follows the summer roads eastward and westward through the Morvind ranges, linking the clans to the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and, through them, to the coastal markets. Metalwork and stone travel outward in guarded caravans, while grain, cloth, salt, and other goods that the high country cannot provide travel inward. The Frostwall Clans maintain the roads and escort the traffic, for the survival of the marches depends as much upon secure passage as upon the wealth beneath the mountains.

## The Passes and Their Defence

Each major pass is held by a clan whose obligations extend beyond the walls of its own settlement. The gate clan clears rockfalls, marks safe winter crossings, and maintains refuge halls supplied with fuel and preserved food. In return, caravans pay a measured toll in coin or goods, and neighbouring clans provide warriors when a gate faces a threat too large for its household garrison. The council at Grimhold keeps a record of these duties so that a prosperous route cannot evade support for a poorer pass whose defence protects the marches as a whole.

The watchtowers communicate by horn, shuttered lamp, and the deep resonant signals used in dwarven workings. A warning can pass from an outer station to Grimhold before a traveller on foot has descended a single valley. Patrols are trained to distinguish raiding parties from hunters and migrating kindreds, for an unnecessary closure can deprive remote communities of their brief season of trade. When a genuine attack comes, the defenders yield exposed ground and hold the prepared gates, forcing any invader into narrow approaches commanded from the stone above.

Not every danger carries a weapon. Avalanches, sudden thaw, and the collapse of corniced snow kill more travellers in some years than raiders do. The clans map these hazards after every winter and move the road markers when ice or broken rock has changed a slope. Rescue parties are maintained as a public duty, and refusing aid to a traveller in the passes is treated as a grave breach of clan law even when that traveller belongs to a rival hold.

## Life in the Marches

Most Frostwall dwarves live partly below ground, where chambers cut behind the cliff faces retain heat and remain secure during storms. Surface buildings shelter animals, store timber, and collect the brief summer light, while halls, workshops, cisterns, and winter roads extend within the rock. Food comes from hardy roots and greens grown in sheltered plots, mushrooms cultivated in warm galleries, and trade with lower valleys. Herds of shaggy goats graze the alpine meadows during summer and are brought behind the gates before the first lasting snow.

The opening of the passes is the central event of the year. Families repair tools and harness through the winter, then send mining crews, road parties, and caravans outward as soon as the council declares a route safe. Marriages, apprenticeships, and the settlement of long disputes cluster in the same short season because travel between distant clans may be impossible once snow returns. The resulting culture values preparation over haste: stores are counted twice, journeys are planned with several refuges, and every household is expected to contribute labour to the works on which all depend.

The population of the clan settlements is dwarven. Human merchants and routekeepers visit the outer stations during the open season, but they remain guests under clan protection rather than permanent communities of the marches. This distinction preserves the dedicated character of the Frostwall Clans while allowing the roads to serve the wider economy of Morvind.

## Significance

The Frostwall Marches are the defended hinge of Morvind's interior. Their gates keep open the few routes between the eastern and western ranges, while their fortresses shield the settled holds from threats that move out of the far steppes. The endurance of Grimhold, Ravenscrag, and the lesser clan stations is a testament to the dwarven capacity to impose order upon a landscape of cold, distance, and broken stone, and the marches remain both the shield of the Morvind holds and the narrow road by which the deepest interior remains connected to the wider world.
