## Overview

At the southern limit of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/the-sahrani-coastal-strip), where the salt pans give way to the rocky headland that marks the eastern end of the [Glasslight Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/glasslight-coast), the council of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) maintains a small watchpost and a beacon that the chroniclers of the academies have named Saltpan Watch. The watchpost was erected in the early generations of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities), and the watchpost has been maintained by the [Watch of the Inner Sea](/content/government/local/watch-of-the-inner-sea) ever since. The watchpost is the home of a small garrison of the Watch, and the garrison oversees the salt pans and the beacon that marks the coast for the pilots of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean).

The watchpost is one of the smaller of the outposts of the warm coast, and the watchpost is held in particular esteem by the salt-makers and the pilots who rely upon the beacon. The chronicler notes that the watchpost has been the foundation of the safety of the navigation along the coast, and the watchpost is the source of much of the prosperity of the salt-makers and the pilots of the [Salt Court of the Sahrani Coast](/content/government/local/salt-court-of-the-sahrani-coast).

## The Watchpost

The watchpost is a small stone building of two stories, with a stout door and a stout roof, and the watchpost is built upon a rise of ground that commands a wide view of the salt pans and the coast. The watchpost contains a small hearth, a small store of provisions, and the arms of the garrison. The chronicler notes that the watchpost is the smallest of the outposts of the warm coast, and the watchpost is held in particular esteem by the garrison that maintains it.

The garrison of the watchpost consists of a sergeant and four men, and the garrison holds the watchpost for a tour of two months. The sergeant is responsible for the maintenance of the beacon, the inspection of the salt pans, and the register of the vessels that pass along the coast. The chronicler notes that the sergeant has a duty to keep the register carefully, for the register is the evidence by which the magistrates judge the state of the salt pans and the navigation of the coast.

## The Beacon

The beacon at the watchpost was erected by the council of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the early generations of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities), and the beacon has been maintained by the Watch ever since. The beacon stands upon a small tower that rises above the roof of the watchpost, and the beacon is visible far out to sea in clear weather. The keeper of the beacon maintains the fire, trims the wicks at dusk, and signals the state of the tide by the colour of the light that he shows above the doorway of the lantern room.

The chronicler notes that the beacon has been the foundation of the safety of the navigation along the coast, and the beacon has been the refuge of many a pilot who has been caught upon the coast in time of storm. The beacon is one of the most important of the public works of the warm coast, and the beacon is held in high esteem by the pilots and the merchants of every city of the coast.

## The Salt Pans

The salt pans to the north of the watchpost are the source of the salt that the [Salt Court of the Sahrani Coast](/content/government/local/salt-court-of-the-sahrani-coast) regulates, and the salt pans are the foundation of the prosperity of the salt-makers of the coast. The pans are flooded by the small streams that run off the higher ground to the east, and the pans are evaporated by the sun in the late summer. The harvest is opened by the master of the Salt Court after consultation with the sworn members, and the harvest is closed in the autumn when the rains begin.

The garrison of the watchpost inspects the pans at regular intervals, and the sergeant reports the state of the pans to the master of the Salt Court. The chronicler notes that the inspection has been the foundation of the careful management of the pans, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the salt-makers of the coast.

## The Quarters

The quarters of the watchpost were erected by the council of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) at the same time as the watchpost, and the quarters are maintained by the Watch. The quarters are small buildings of stone that provide shelter for the garrison and for the travellers who are caught upon the coast in time of storm. The chronicler notes that the quarters are the foundation of the safety of the coast, and the quarters are held in high esteem by the salt-makers and the pilots who rely upon them.

The quarters are furnished with the supplies that the garrison requires for the tour, and the supplies are delivered by the magistrates of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) at the beginning of every season. The chronicler notes that the delivery has been the foundation of the careful management of the quarters, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of the coast.

## The Customs Post

The watchpost also serves as the customs post at which the small dues that the [Salt Court of the Sahrani Coast](/content/government/local/salt-court-of-the-sahrani-coast) imposes upon the export of salt are collected. The sergeant of the garrison collects the dues from the merchants who load salt upon the smaller vessels at the pans, and the sergeant enters the dues in the register that the master of the Salt Court maintains. The chronicler notes that the customs post has been the foundation of the careful collection of the dues, and the collection is the source of much of the revenue that pays for the maintenance of the pans.

The customs post is inspected by the master of the Salt Court at regular intervals, and the master audits the accounts of the dues at the end of every season. The chronicler notes that the audit has been the foundation of the careful management of the dues, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the salt-makers of the coast.

## Significance

Saltpan Watch is one of the smaller of the outposts of the warm coast, and the watchpost is the foundation of the salt trade and the navigation of the southern coast. The watchpost, the beacon, the quarters, the pans, and the streams are the inheritance that the watchpost has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the watchpost is held in particular memory by the salt-makers and the pilots who rely upon it, and the salt-makers and the pilots mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the warm coast, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every town of the coast.
