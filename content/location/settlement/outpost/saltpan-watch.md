## A Small Watchpost Above the Southern Salt Pans

At the southern limit of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip), where the [salt](/content/economy/salt) pans give way to the rocky headland marking the eastern end of the [Glasslight Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/glasslight-coast), the council of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) maintains a small watchpost and beacon called Saltpan Watch. The watchpost was erected in the early generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace) and has been maintained by the [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch) ever since. A small garrison oversees the salt pans and the beacon that marks the coast for pilots of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean).

The watchpost is one of the smaller outposts of the warm coast, held in particular esteem by the salt-makers and pilots who rely upon the beacon for safe navigation. The coast in this region is low and featureless, offering few landmarks to the mariner approaching from the open ocean, and the beacon that rises above the watchpost is the first sign of land that a vessel may sight after days at sea. The watchpost has been the foundation of safe navigation along this stretch of coast for several centuries and is the source of much of the prosperity of the salt-makers and pilots of the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court). Without the beacon and the garrison that maintains it, the vessels that carry the salt of the pans to the markets of the coast would be at constant risk of grounding on the shoals that lie offshore, and the trade that has made the fortune of the salt-makers would be far more dangerous than it is.

## The Two-Storey Stone Building and the Pale Stone Walls

The watchpost is a small stone building of two stories with a stout door and roof, built upon a rise commanding a wide view of the salt pans and the coast. The walls are constructed of the same pale stone that underlies the region, giving the building a colour that blends with the landscape and makes it difficult to distinguish from a distance. The lower floor contains a small hearth for cooking and warmth, a store of provisions sufficient to sustain the garrison through its tour, and racks for the arms of the watch. The upper floor serves as the sleeping quarters and the workroom where the keeper prepares the beacon for the night. The garrison consists of a sergeant and four men, each tour lasting two months before a fresh company arrives from [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) to take their place. The sergeant is responsible for maintaining the beacon, inspecting the salt pans to ensure that the channels and the evaporation beds are in good order, and keeping the register of vessels that pass along the coast. The register records the name and port of each vessel, the direction of its passage, and the state of the weather at the time of its sighting, and it is the evidence by which the magistrates of Khalisar judge the state of the salt pans and the volume of navigation along the coast.

## The Iron Basket and the Coloured Glass Panels

The beacon was erected by the council of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the early generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace) and has been maintained by the [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch) ever since. It stands upon a small tower that rises above the roof of the watchpost, its iron basket supported by a stone pillar that is visible far out to sea in clear weather. The beacon keeper ascends the tower at dusk to light the fire and to trim the wicks of the oil lamps that supplement the flames in calm weather. The keeper signals the state of the tide by the colour of the light shown, using a system of coloured glass panels that can be shifted into place before the lamps. The beacon has been the refuge of many a pilot caught upon the coast in time of storm, for the light that shines from the tower is the only reliable landmark on a stretch of shore where the low coast and the shifting sands can confuse even the most experienced mariner. It is among the most important [public works](/content/economy/public-works) of the warm coast, and the council of Khalisar allocates a portion of the harbour revenues each year to its maintenance.

## The Evaporation Basins and the Salt Harvest

The salt pans north of the watchpost are the source of the salt regulated by the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court) and the foundation of the prosperity of the salt-makers of the coast. The pans cover a considerable area of flat ground between the watchpost and the coastal strip, their surfaces divided into shallow basins separated by low walls of packed earth. The pans are flooded by small streams running off the higher ground to the east, and the water is admitted to the basins in a controlled sequence that allows the sun to evaporate it in stages, concentrating the brine before the final crystallisation. The harvest is opened by the master of the Salt Court after consultation with the sworn members and closed in the autumn when the rains begin and the evaporation slows. During the harvest season, the pans are a scene of constant activity as the salt-makers rake the crystals from the basins, pile them to drain, and carry them to the storehouses that line the edge of the pans. The garrison inspects the pans at regular intervals, and the sergeant reports their state to the master of the Salt Court, noting any damage to the walls or channels and any sign of pollution that may affect the quality of the salt.

## The Stone Shelters for Garrisons and Travellers

The quarters were erected at the same time as the watchpost and are maintained by the Watch. These small stone buildings provide shelter for the garrison and for travellers caught upon the coast in time of storm. The buildings are simple but sturdy, with thick walls that keep out the heat and roofs that are weighted against the winds that sweep across the coast. Each contains a hearth, sleeping platforms, and a small storeroom. The quarters are furnished with supplies delivered by the magistrates of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) at the beginning of every season, including dried food, lamp oil, candles, and bedding. Travellers who take shelter at the watchpost are expected to contribute to the stores if they are able, but no one in genuine need is turned away.

## The Collection of Salt Export Dues

The watchpost also serves as the customs post where the small dues imposed by the [Sahrani Salt Court](/content/government/local/sahrani-salt-court) upon the export of salt are collected. The sergeant collects the dues from merchants who load salt upon smaller vessels at the pans and enters them in the register maintained by the master of the Salt Court. The rate of the dues is set by the Court and has remained unchanged for several generations, for the salt-makers regard the stability of the levy as essential to the orderly conduct of the trade. The customs post is inspected by the master at regular intervals, and the accounts are audited at the end of every season to ensure that the dues have been collected fairly and that no merchant has evaded the levy.

## The Foundation of the Southern Salt Trade

Saltpan Watch is one of the smaller outposts of the warm coast, serving as the foundation of the salt trade and navigation of the southern coast. The watchpost, beacon, quarters, pans, and streams together form the system that has sustained the salt-makers and the pilots of the region for centuries. The watchpost is held in particular memory by those who rely upon it, and they mark the anniversaries of the founding with ceremonies attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every town of the coast.
