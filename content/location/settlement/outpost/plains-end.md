## Overview

At the eastern limit of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains), where the grasslands give way to the broken ground that marks the foothills of the distant mountains, the magistrates of [Serren](/content/location/settlement/city/serren) maintain a small watchpost and a beacon that the chroniclers of the academies have named Plain's End. The watchpost was erected in the early generations of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities), and the watchpost has been maintained by the garrison of the magistrates ever since. The watchpost is the home of a small community of soldiers and herdsmen, and the community is governed by the sergeant of the garrison in consultation with the elders of the pastoralists who bring their flocks to the plains.

The watchpost is one of the more isolated of the outposts of the inland valleys, and the watchpost is held in particular esteem by the pastoralists and the merchants who rely upon it for the safety of the eastern plains. The chronicler notes that the watchpost has been the foundation of the careful management of the plains for several centuries, and the watchpost is the source of much of the prosperity of the merchants of [Serren](/content/location/settlement/city/serren).

## The Watchpost

The watchpost is a small stone building of two stories, with a stout door and a stout roof, and the watchpost is built upon a rise of ground that commands a wide view of the plains and the approaches from the east. The watchpost contains a small hearth, a small store of provisions, and the arms of the garrison. The chronicler notes that the watchpost is one of the more substantial of the outposts of the inland valleys, and the watchpost is held in particular esteem by the garrison that maintains it.

The garrison of the watchpost consists of a sergeant and four men, and the garrison holds the watchpost for a tour of two months. The sergeant is responsible for the maintenance of the beacon, the inspection of the plains, and the register of the pastoralists and the merchants who pass through the watchpost. The chronicler notes that the sergeant has a duty to keep the register carefully, for the register is the evidence by which the magistrates of [Serren](/content/location/settlement/city/serren) judge the state of the plains and the safety of the road.

## The Beacon

The beacon at the watchpost was erected by the magistrates of [Serren](/content/location/settlement/city/serren) in the early generations of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities), and the beacon has been maintained by the garrison ever since. The beacon stands upon a small tower that rises above the roof of the watchpost, and the beacon is visible far across the plains in clear weather. The keeper of the beacon maintains the fire, trims the wicks at dusk, and signals the approach of any large body of men by the smoke that he builds during the day.

The chronicler notes that the beacon has been the foundation of the safety of the eastern plains, and the beacon has been the refuge of many a merchant who has been caught upon the plains in time of storm. The beacon is one of the more important of the public works of the inland valleys, and the beacon is held in high esteem by the pastoralists and the merchants of every town of the plains.

## The Pastoralists

The pastoralists who bring their flocks to the plains are the principal visitors of the watchpost, and the pastoralists are the source of much of the contact between the watchpost and the towns of the inland valleys. The pastoralists come to the watchpost to purchase the supplies that the watchpost provides, and the pastoralists exchange the wool and the cheese of their flocks for the grain and the tools that the towns of the plains produce. The chronicler notes that the exchange has been the foundation of the contact between the pastoralists and the towns, and the contact is the source of much of the prosperity of both parties.

The pastoralists are governed by the elders of the clans in consultation with the sergeant of the garrison, and the elders maintain the flocks, the pastures, the routes, and the public order of the clans. The chronicler notes that the elders are held in high esteem by the pastoralists, and the elders mark the anniversaries of the founding of the watchpost with appropriate ceremonies.

## The Quarters

The quarters of the watchpost were erected by the magistrates of [Serren](/content/location/settlement/city/serren) at the same time as the watchpost, and the quarters are maintained by the garrison. The quarters are small buildings of stone that provide shelter for the garrison and for the travellers who are caught upon the plains in time of storm. The chronicler notes that the quarters are the foundation of the safety of the plains, and the quarters are held in high esteem by the pastoralists and the merchants who rely upon them.

The quarters are furnished with the supplies that the garrison requires for the tour, and the supplies are delivered by the magistrates of [Serren](/content/location/settlement/city/serren) at the beginning of every season. The chronicler notes that the delivery has been the foundation of the careful management of the watchpost, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of the plains.

## Significance

Plain's End is one of the more isolated of the outposts of the inland valleys, and the watchpost is the foundation of the safety of the eastern plains. The watchpost, the beacon, the quarters, the pastoralists, and the exchange are the inheritance that the watchpost has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the watchpost is held in particular memory by the pastoralists and the merchants who rely upon it, and the pastoralists and the merchants mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the inland valleys, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every town of the plains.
