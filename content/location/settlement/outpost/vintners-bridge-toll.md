## Overview

At the centre of the [Vintner's Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale), where the paved road of the [Building of the Stone Roads](/content/history/building-of-the-stone-roads) crosses the river by the great bridge that the vintner-houses of the towns erected in the early generations of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities), the [Council of Vintners and Cellarers](/content/government/local/council-of-vintners-and-cellarers) maintains a small toll-house and a watchpost that the chroniclers of the academies have named the Vintner's Bridge Toll. The toll-house was erected at the same time as the bridge, and the toll-house has been maintained by the Council ever since. The toll-house is the home of a small garrison of the watchmen of the Council, and the garrison oversees the bridge and the road and collects the small toll that pays for the maintenance of both.

The toll-house is one of the smaller of the outposts of the cold north, and the toll-house is held in particular esteem by the merchants and the travellers who use the road. The chronicler notes that the toll-house has been the foundation of the maintenance of the bridge for several centuries, and the toll-house is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of the vale.

## The Bridge

The bridge that the toll-house serves is one of the more substantial of the bridges of the cold north, and the bridge carries the road across the river in five broad arches of stone. The bridge was erected by the vintner-houses of the towns in the early generations of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities), and the bridge has been maintained by the [Council of Vintners and Cellarers](/content/government/local/council-of-vintners-and-cellarers) ever since. The chronicler notes that the bridge has been the foundation of the land trade of the vale for several centuries, and the bridge is the source of much of the prosperity of the merchants of the vale.

The bridge is maintained by the watchmen of the Council, and the watchmen inspect the arches at regular intervals and repair the stonework as the inspections require. The chronicler notes that the maintenance has been the foundation of the durability of the bridge, and the bridge has endured for several centuries because the maintenance has been careful and continuous.

## The Toll-House

The toll-house is a small stone building of two stories that stands at the northern end of the bridge, and the toll-house contains the office in which the toll is collected and the dwellings in which the watchmen live. The toll-house is built in the same style as the bridge, and the toll-house is one of the more substantial of the smaller buildings of the vale. The chronicler notes that the toll-house is the foundation of the maintenance of the bridge, and the toll-house is held in particular esteem by the merchants and the travellers who use the road.

The toll is set by the master of the Council in consultation with the sworn members, and the toll is paid by every merchant who crosses the bridge with a laden cart. The chronicler notes that the toll has been stable for several generations, and the toll is one of the most carefully audited of the public revenues of the vale. The master of the Council audits the accounts of the toll twice in each year, and the audit is the foundation of the careful management of the toll-house.

## The Watchpost

The watchpost at the toll-house is maintained by the watchmen of the Council, and the watchpost is the principal point of surveillance of the road. The watchmen observe the merchants and the travellers who use the road, and the watchmen report any incident to the master of the Council. The chronicler notes that the watchpost has been the foundation of the safety of the road, and the watchpost is the source of much of the public order of the vale.

The watchpost also serves as the principal point of contact between the Council and the magistrates of the towns of the vale, and the watchmen transmit the orders of the master to the magistrates and the magistrates transmit their reports to the master. The chronicler notes that the contact has been the foundation of the careful management of the vale, and the contact is the measure of the public spirit of the Council and the magistrates.

## The Quarters

The quarters of the toll-house were erected by the vintner-houses of the towns at the same time as the toll-house, and the quarters are maintained by the Council. The quarters are small buildings of stone that provide shelter for the travellers who are caught upon the road in time of storm, and the quarters are the home of the watchmen who maintain the toll-house. The chronicler notes that the quarters are the foundation of the safety of the road, and the quarters are held in high esteem by the merchants and the travellers who use the road.

The quarters are furnished with the supplies that the watchmen require for the tour, and the supplies are delivered by the vintner-houses of the towns at the beginning of every season. The chronicler notes that the delivery has been the foundation of the careful management of the toll-house, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of the vale.

## Significance

The Vintner's Bridge Toll is one of the smaller of the outposts of the cold north, and the toll-house is the foundation of the maintenance of the bridge and the road. The bridge, the toll-house, the watchpost, the quarters, and the toll are the inheritance that the toll-house has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that the toll-house is held in particular memory by the merchants and the travellers who use the road, and the merchants and the travellers mark the anniversaries of the founding with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the vale, and the ceremonies are attended by the master of the Council and the sworn members of the vintner-houses of the vale.
