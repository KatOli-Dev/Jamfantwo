## Overview

[Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) Pick is a small inhabited road station in the highlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), situated at the junction where the [Stoneheim Highland Road](/content/location/route/trade/stoneheim-highland-road) divides toward the central halls, the eastern mines, and the lower coast. The settlement stands beneath a dark ridge whose profile resembles the head of a miner's pick, giving the station its name. Its purpose is to serve the wagons and pack trains that cross the mountain routes, but it also acts as a depot for road materials, a place of exchange for mining families, and a refuge when snow or rockfall closes the higher road. Stoneheim Pick is governed by the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), though its daily affairs are handled by a warden and a council of resident households.

The station is too small for a city wall and too exposed for a loose village. Its buildings form a compact crescent around a paved yard, with the storehouses on the uphill side and the stable galleries below. The permanent population includes wardens, smiths, stable keepers, cooks, masons, and the families who supply them. During the open season the number of inhabitants may double, as merchants, miners, and hired guards wait for roads to clear or gather into a convoy.

## The Ridge and the Junction

The ridge above the station is made of hard black stone veined with pale mineral seams. It rises sharply on one side and falls in broken shelves on the other, creating a natural shelter from the strongest valley winds. The road uses the saddle behind the ridge rather than the lower ravine, where spring floods can carry away loose ground. From the saddle, travellers can see the upper line of the Stoneheim road and the smoke from the forges of distant [Ironhollow](/content/location/settlement/town/ironhollow) when the air is clear.

The three roads meet in the central yard. The main route runs toward Stoneheim, carrying finished metalwork and returning with grain and timber. The eastern road descends toward the iron workings and the lesser holds, while the western track joins the routes leading toward the [Stormfall](/content/location/natural/continent/stormfall) coast and the [Frostwall Pass](/content/location/route/trade/frostwall-pass). The station's warden assigns crews according to the volume and danger of each branch.

The old mine track remains in use even though the nearest working has been exhausted. Its galleries now provide stone for repairs, and the lower tunnels hold dry storage rooms that remain cool throughout the summer. The miners who first occupied the ridge left marks on the rock showing distances to water and safe stopping places, symbols still visible on the walls near the station gate.

## Buildings and Services

The largest building is the Road Hall, a long stone structure with a hearth at either end and sleeping platforms along the walls. Travellers arriving without a private tent can rent a place by the night, while members of an organised convoy receive space according to the warden's list. The hall also serves as a meeting place for merchants and road crews.

The stable galleries are cut partly into the ridge with low roofs, broad doors, and sloped floors carrying waste away from the cistern. The keepers care for mountain ponies, mules, and the heavy draft animals used on ore wagons. Animals unable to continue are not abandoned; the station may purchase them at a reduced price or arrange for their return.

The smithy occupies a separate building. Its forge repairs wheel rims, chain, harness buckles, picks, shovels, and the iron fittings of road gates. The smiths also maintain the station's supply of pitons, bridge clamps, and snow shovels. They have authority to delay a convoy when its equipment is unsafe.

## The People and Governance

Stoneheim Pick is chiefly a dwarven settlement, though human merchants and road workers are present during the trading season. The permanent households are tied to the road rather than to one mine or one forge. The warden is appointed by officials of Stoneheim and confirmed by the households of the station, controlling the gates, assigning sleeping places, ordering emergency repairs, and reporting the condition of the branches to the hold council.

The human visitors contribute knowledge of coastal markets and the changing condition of bridges and slopes. The station keeps a guest ledger in which visitors may record information about markets, weather, and road damage. The entries are read aloud during the evening meal.

## Trade and Stores

The station handles cargo rather than producing much of its own. Wagons from Stoneheim carry tools, weapons, fittings, and worked stone, while returning trains bring grain, salt, cloth, timber, and coastal goods required by the highland holds. Merchants may break a large consignment at Stoneheim Pick, selling part to a passing buyer or storing it until the road ahead reopens.

The storehouses are built with thick walls and raised floors. The station maintains a reserve belonging to the holds rather than to any private merchant, used during closures and replaced when the route reopens. The warden may refuse a sale when stores fall below the level set by Stoneheim. The settlement's market is held on the first clear day after a major convoy arrives.

## Weather and Safety

Snow can close the upper road before it reaches the station, leaving Stoneheim Pick as the last open refuge for travellers descending from the ridge. The inhabitants store fuel for the Road Hall and keep the stable galleries ready for animals brought in from the storm. During thaw, the danger changes to falling stone and swollen streams.

The road is generally secure under the protection of the holds, but isolated theft and raiding occur on the lesser tracks. Stoneheim Pick does not maintain a large garrison, instead calling upon passing guards, nearby mine crews, and patrols assigned to the main route. Travellers are expected to defend the yard if danger reaches the gate.

## History and Significance

Stoneheim Pick began as a quarry camp beside the junction of old mining paths. The first permanent household formed around the workers who repaired the route, and the settlement grew when the holds decided a central highland depot was necessary. The paved yard and Road Hall were built during a period of increased metal exports, when ore trains became too numerous for scattered shelters.

The settlement remains a small but essential part of the Stoneheim road system. It gathers the scattered work of wardens, smiths, miners, and merchants into one dependable point, allowing the highland trade to continue despite distance and weather. The ridge above it is a landmark, but the station itself is the true mark of the road: a place where every load is checked, every traveller can find shelter, and the highland routes are joined into a road worthy of their name.
