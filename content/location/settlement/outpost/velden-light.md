## A Lighthouse Settlement on the Aethelaran Shore

[Velden](/content/location/settlement/city/velden) Light is a small inhabited lighthouse settlement on the Aethelaran shore of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), built upon a headland that marks the outer approach to the channel. The light stands between the larger harbours of [Caerhaven](/content/location/settlement/town/caerhaven) and [Southport](/content/location/settlement/town/southport), guiding vessels away from reefs before they reach the waters governed by the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact). The settlement's population is modest and its buildings are plain, yet ships of many nations recognise its lantern as one of the essential signs of the [Velden Strait Run](/content/location/route/trade/velden-strait-run). Its keepers maintain the lamp, the fog bell, the signal flags, and the stores that support vessels forced to wait outside the strait.

The outpost occupies a narrow shelf above steep water. A stone wall surrounds the lighthouse yard, not as a fortress but as protection against the wind and occasional surge reaching the headland during a storm. The permanent inhabitants include the lighthouse keepers, their families, a pilot clerk, a boat crew, a carpenter, and several workers responsible for the cistern and storehouses.

## The Dark Rock Reef and the Polished Mirror Lamp

The headland is formed of dark rock that continues beneath the channel as a shallow reef, marked by a line of white posts fixed into the stone. Vessels approaching from the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) keep the light on their eastern side before turning toward deeper water, while vessels leaving the strait use it to confirm they have cleared the most dangerous shoals. Currents bend around the headland, and a ship following the apparent direction of the waves can be carried toward rock.

The lighthouse tower is a thick cylinder of pale stone with a gallery near the top. The lamp uses polished metal mirrors and a steady oil flame, with shutters creating the pattern recognised by the pilot guilds. During clear weather the light can be seen from the upper reaches of the strait, but fog reduces its value and makes the bell equally important. The bell sounds at measured intervals when visibility is poor.

The lantern is maintained under league and compact authority. The [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) supplies some of the oil and metal fittings, while the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) pays the keepers and oversees the signal code. Contributions are collected from vessels using the strait and from the towns that benefit from safe passage.

## The Close-Built Houses and the Emergency Stores

The houses of [Velden](/content/location/settlement/city/velden) Light are built close together on the sheltered side of the tower. Thick walls and low roofs reduce the force of the wind, while narrow windows face inland and toward the harbour lights rather than the open sea. The central yard contains a well, a smithy, a covered workbench, and a storehouse for rope, sailcloth, dry timber, and emergency water casks. Ships' crews can purchase basic provisions when weather delays their passage.

A narrow stair descends from the yard to a landing where two boats are kept above the ordinary tide. The boats are used to carry pilots, inspect the reef markers, and assist vessels that lose a mast or drag an anchor near the headland. The storehouse also holds a reserve of dry clothing and blankets for stranded sailors.

## The Night Watches and the Pilot Clerk's Record

The keepers divide the night into watches, with one person always responsible for the lamp and another for the signals. The work requires constant attention. Oil must be filtered, mirrors polished, shutters tested, and the flame protected from drafts. A keeper who notices a change in the lamp reports it immediately, because small faults become dangerous when fog or rain arrives. Children of the lighthouse families learn these tasks early, though succession is not automatic; the compact requires every keeper to demonstrate the necessary skills before receiving a licence.

The pilot clerk maintains the station's main record, entering each passing vessel by name, flag, cargo class, and direction, together with the tide and weather at the time of sighting. The record helps the pilot guilds understand traffic patterns and assists harbour authorities when a ship fails to arrive. It also gives merchants evidence when cargo is lost or delayed, making Velden Light more than a lamp on a rock.

The community receives many visitors but has little room for them. Pilots waiting for a tide sleep in the signal house, while sailors rescued from a damaged vessel occupy the common room. The inhabitants are accustomed to short conversations, foreign accents, and sudden departures. They maintain friendships with families in [Caerhaven](/content/location/settlement/town/caerhaven) and [Southport](/content/location/settlement/town/southport), and marriages between the light keepers and the pilot households of those towns are common. The settlement remains small because most young people leave for the larger harbours, but many return after years at sea, bringing new knowledge of lamps, charts, and rescue methods.

## The Current, the Reef, and the Compact Patrols

The current beneath the headland changes with the tide, and the water can appear calm while moving swiftly toward the reef. The keepers observe floating weed, gulls, and the position of anchored vessels, then pass their observations to the pilots. When storms move through from the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea), the station signals all vessels to seek the deeper shelter of Caerhaven or the inner coves near Southport.

Pirates have used the headland in earlier centuries, hiding among the reefs while merchant ships waited for a favourable tide. The settlement is now protected by compact patrols. Wrecks remain a more common danger than deliberate attack. The strait's narrowness brings ships close together, and a vessel that loses steerage can cross the course of another before its crew can respond. The light's signals help pilots judge their position, but final safety rests upon the trained navigators of [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch) and Southport.

## From Brazier to Stone Tower

The first light on the headland was a simple brazier maintained by fishermen. It was replaced by a timber tower after a series of wrecks, and the present stone structure was raised when traffic through the strait increased beyond the capacity of local boats. The compact later incorporated the station into its formal network.

Velden Light is small enough to be overlooked in accounts of the strait, but no serious chart omits it. Its lantern guides vessels toward the channel, its boats carry aid, and its records connect the movements seen on the water with the decisions made in harbour councils. The settlement embodies the shared obligations that keep the strait open, giving every crew a visible point of safety in waters where a few minutes of uncertainty can decide the fate of an entire cargo.
