## Overview

Dunesend is a caravan stop at the edge of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), situated where the great caravan routes emerge from the desert and meet the foothills of the Sahrani interior. The settlement is the last outpost before the deep desert, and its function is to serve the caravans that prepare to enter the Kharesh or that have just emerged from it. Dunesend is small and purpose-built, and its prosperity is built upon the services it provides to the caravan trade, including provisioning, shelter, and the exchange of goods between the desert routes and the inland cities.

## The Caravan Stop

Dunesend stands at the point where the foothills of the interior highlands give way to the sands of the Kharesh, and the settlement is the last reliable source of water and provisions before the caravans enter the deep desert. The water that sustains the settlement is drawn from a well that is fed by the same aquifers that supply the oases of the interior, and the water is managed carefully, for it must sustain the community and the caravans that pass through. The caravans that stop at Dunesend are preparing for the crossing of the Kharesh, and the provisioning that they receive here is the last they will obtain before they reach the oases of the deep desert.

The caravans that arrive at Dunesend from the desert are weary and in need of rest, and the settlement provides shelter, water, and food for the travellers and their animals before they continue to the inland cities. The inns and the stables of Dunesend are the primary institutions of the settlement, and the services that they provide are the foundation of the local economy. The caravans that stop at Dunesend are the primary contact between the settlement and the wider world, and the trade that flows through the settlement is the lifeline of the community.

## Trade and the Routes

The trade of Dunesend is modest, for the settlement is small and its production is limited, but the services that it provides to the caravans are the foundation of its economy. The tolls and the fees that are levied on the caravans are a significant source of income, and the sale of provisions, water, and shelter to the travellers is the primary commerce of the settlement. The goods that pass through Dunesend include the spices, the dyes, and the precious stones of the interior, which are carried by the caravans from the oases and the upland settlements to the markets of the coast, and the grain, the cloth, and the manufactured goods that are carried in the opposite direction.

The [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) of the desert are the most frequent visitors to Dunesend, and the relationship between the settlement and the nomadic salawa is one of mutual dependence, for the salawa provide the knowledge of the desert that the caravans require, and the settlement provides the water and the provisions that the salawa need. The salawa are welcomed at the settlement, and their presence is a familiar and a valued part of the life of the community.

The caravans that stop at Dunesend are organised and regulated by the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), and the waybills and the seals that are carried by the caravan masters are checked at the settlement before the caravans enter the deep desert. The checking of the waybills is a function that is performed by the agents of the pact who are stationed at Dunesend, and the agents are also responsible for the collection of the tolls and the fees that are levied on the caravans. The agents maintain a register of the caravans that pass through the settlement, and the register is a valuable record of the flow of the trade and the conditions of the routes.

## The Settlement

Dunesend is a small, compact settlement, and its buildings are constructed of the stone of the foothills and the sun-baked clay that is made from the desert earth, with thick walls and small windows that provide insulation against the heat and the wind. The streets are broad and dusty, for the caravans that pass through the settlement require room for their animals and their wagons, and the market square is the centre of the community, where the caravans gather to trade and to rest. The population of Dunesend is small and chiefly [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the people are known for their hospitality and their skill in the management of the caravan trade.

The inns and the stables of Dunesend are the primary institutions of the settlement, and the inns are the gathering places where the caravan masters, the travellers, and the salawa guides meet to exchange news, to negotiate the prices of the provisions, and to plan the crossings of the Kharesh. The inns are large and well-maintained, for the comfort of the travellers is a priority of the community, and the reputation of the inns is a factor in the decision of the caravan masters to stop at Dunesend rather than to bypass the settlement. The stables are likewise well-maintained, and the fodder and the water that are provided for the animals are among the best that are available at the edge of the desert.

## History

The settlement of Dunesend was established by the caravan masters who recognised the need for a provisioning station at the edge of the Kharesh, where the caravans could prepare for the crossing of the deep desert or rest after the emergence from it. The well that sustains the settlement was the initial attraction, and the presence of a reliable water source at the point where the foothills give way to the sands was the foundation upon which the settlement was built. The community grew as the volume of the caravan trade increased, and the inns and the stables that are the primary institutions of the settlement were established and expanded to meet the growing demand.

The admission of Dunesend to the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) was a natural development, for the settlement was a critical waypoint on the caravan routes, and the regulation and the protection of the trade required the inclusion of the settlement in the alliance. The relationship between Dunesend and the pact has been one of mutual benefit, for the pact provides the organisation and the protection that sustain the trade, and Dunesend provides the services and the infrastructure that sustain the caravans. The settlement has endured the challenges of the desert environment and the occasional threat of raiders, and the community has maintained its function as the primary caravan stop at the edge of the Kharesh throughout its history.

## Significance

Dunesend is the primary caravan stop at the edge of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and its services are a vital resource for the caravans that cross the desert. The settlement is a dependency of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the alliance of the inland caravan cities, and its role in the caravan trade is a critical component of the economy of the desert interior. The significance of Dunesend lies in its position at the edge of the desert, where it serves as the last outpost before the deep Kharesh and the first refuge for those who emerge from it, and the settlement is a testament to the endurance and the hospitality of the people who live at the desert's edge.

The community of Dunesend is a symbol of the capacity of the inland peoples to sustain a prosperous and a valued settlement in the demanding environment of the desert's edge, and the services that the settlement provides are a critical component of the caravan trade that connects the coast to the interior. The settlement is a meeting point of the caravans, the salawa, and the inland cities, and the diversity of the people who pass through the settlement is a valued feature of its character.
