## A Village at a Stone Bridge Crossing

Stoneford is a village of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) situated at the crossing of the River Stone, a minor tributary of the Greywash, at the point where a stone bridge of ancient construction carries the road over the river. The village takes its name from the stone ford that preceded the bridge and from the bridge itself, a structure of grey stone that has spanned the river for as many generations as the records cover. Stoneford is a settlement of modest size, serving as a waypoint for travellers on the road and as a market and meeting point for the farming communities of the river valley.

The position of Stoneford at the crossing of the River Stone has determined the character and the economy of the village. The bridge is the only crossing of the river for many miles in either direction, and the traffic that passes over it provides the villagers with a source of income from the services that travellers require. The village also serves as a market for the agricultural products of the surrounding farms, and the weekly market held at the bridge end is the principal commercial event of the region.

## The River Stone and the Ancient Bridge

The River Stone is a minor watercourse that rises in the low hills of the interior and flows northward to join the Greywash at a point some miles below Stoneford. The river is not large, but it is deep enough to be impassable at most points for much of the year, and the crossing at Stoneford is the only reliable point of passage for many miles. The river bed is composed of the grey stone that has given the river and the village their names, and the water flows clear and cold over the stone.

The bridge at Stoneford is a structure of ancient origin, its construction dating from a period so remote that the chronicles do not record the date of its building. The bridge is built of grey stone, with a single arch that spans the full width of the river. The bridge is broad enough for two wagons to pass abreast, and the road that approaches it on both sides has been paved with stone to provide a firm surface for the traffic. The bridge is maintained by the village, and the work of repairing and reinforcing the structure is a regular obligation of the community.

## The Founding of Stoneford

The founding of Stoneford is associated with the construction of the bridge that gives the village its name. The bridge was built to carry the road that connected the interior of Aethelar to the coastal lowlands, and the crossing attracted the first settlers to the site. The earliest settlement consisted of a few houses built at the bridge end, providing shelter and provisions for the travellers who used the crossing.

The village grew slowly over the centuries, as the volume of traffic on the road increased and the demand for the services that the village provided grew with it. The crossing attracted merchants and traders, and the village became a market for the exchange of the goods that passed along the road. The farmers of the surrounding country brought their produce to the market at Stoneford, and the prosperity generated by the market and the traffic supported the growth of the settlement.

## The Bridge and the River Trade

The bridge at Stoneford is the principal infrastructure of the village, and the maintenance of the bridge is the most important responsibility of the community. The bridge is inspected regularly by the elders of the village, and the necessary repairs are carried out by the villagers working under the direction of a master mason. The bridge has been repaired and reinforced many times over the centuries, but the original structure remains the core of the bridge, a testament to the skill of the builders who constructed it.

The river trade of Stoneford is a secondary but significant component of the local economy. The River Stone is navigable by small boats and rafts for a short distance above and below the bridge, and the goods that are carried on the river include the timber and agricultural products of the valley. The villagers maintain a small landing at the bridge end, where the goods that arrive by river are unloaded and stored before being carried onward by road.

## The Weekly Market and the Travellers Trade

The weekly market of Stoneford is held at the bridge end, in the open space where the road from the north meets the road from the south. The market is a modest affair, with stalls and booths offering the agricultural products of the surrounding farms and the goods of the craftsmen of the village. The market is supervised by the elders of the village, who collect the dues that fund the maintenance of the bridge and the roads.

The travellers trade is the other source of income for the village. The tavern at the bridge end provides food, drink, and accommodation for the travellers who pass over the bridge. The smithy at the bridge end provides shoeing and repair services for the horses and wagons of the travellers. The stable at the bridge end provides shelter and fodder for the animals of the travellers. The services that the village provides to the travellers are an important component of the local economy, and the villagers are known for their hospitality and their reliability.

## The Farms and the Fields of the Valley

The farms of the Stoneford valley are the agricultural foundation of the village. The fields that line the valley are fertile, producing grain, vegetables, and the fodder that sustains the livestock of the farmers. The farms are worked by the families that have held the land for generations, and the methods of cultivation that they employ are those that have been developed and refined over centuries of experience.

The principal crops of the Stoneford valley are wheat, barley, and oats, the grain that is the staple of the diet of the region. The farmers also grow beans, peas, and root vegetables, and they raise cattle, sheep, and pigs for meat, milk, and wool. The agricultural products of the valley are sold at the market at Stoneford, and the surplus is carried by road to the larger markets of the Greywash valley.

## The Houses and the Streets of Stoneford

The houses of Stoneford are built in the style that is common in the river valleys of Aethelar, with stone foundations and timber frames, walls of plaster and wattle, and roofs of thatch. The houses are arranged along the road that approaches the bridge from both sides, and the village has a linear plan that reflects its origin as a roadside settlement.

The streets of Stoneford are unpaved, and they are dusty in summer and muddy in winter. The main street is the road that approaches the bridge, and the side streets are the lanes that lead to the fields and the farms of the valley. The village is compact, with the houses and the service buildings concentrated around the bridge end, and the fields and the pastures of the valley extending outward from the built-up area.

## The Elders and the Governance of Stoneford

The governance of Stoneford is in the hands of a council of elders composed of the heads of the principal families of the village. The council is responsible for the administration of the village, the maintenance of the bridge and the roads, and the regulation of the market. The council meets in the tavern at the bridge end, and the decisions of the council are made by the consensus of the members.

The elders of Stoneford are among the most respected figures in the community, and the experience and wisdom they bring to the governance of the village are valued by all the inhabitants. The elders are responsible for the resolution of disputes, the allocation of the common resources, and the representation of the village in its dealings with the wider world.

## Stoneford and the Road Across the River

Stoneford is the settlement at the crossing of the River Stone, a village that has grown around the ancient bridge that spans the river and the road that passes over it. The bridge that gives the village its name is the most recognisable landmark of the region, a structure of grey stone that has connected the two banks of the river for centuries. The village that stands at the bridge end is a waypoint for travellers and a market for the farmers of the valley, and the life of the community is shaped by the traffic that passes over the bridge and the seasons of the agricultural year.
