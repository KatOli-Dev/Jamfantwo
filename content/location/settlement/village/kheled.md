## Overview

Kheled is an upland settlement in the desert massifs of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), the vast desert interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The settlement stands in a sheltered valley of one of the rocky uplands rising from the desert floor, where springs fed by deep aquifers sustain a community that is among the most isolated and self-sufficient in the known world. Kheled is small but enduring, and its people are among the most adapted to the demands of the upland desert environment, where the heat of the day gives way to the cold of the night and the resources are scarce but sufficient for a people who know the land.

## The Massif and the Springs

The massif in which Kheled is situated is one of the rocky uplands rising from the sands of the Kharesh, a remnant of an ancient landscape the desert has otherwise buried. The massif is a complex of bare summits and sheltered valleys; the springs emerging at the base of the upland are the source of the water that sustains the settlement. The springs are fed by deep aquifers beneath the desert; the flow is constant and reliable, unaffected by seasonal variations in rainfall. The water is managed carefully, for it must sustain the settlement and the gardens cultivated in the terraced plots around the springs.

The valley in which Kheled stands is sheltered from wind and sand by the walls of the massif; the climate is more moderate than the open desert, for the rock absorbs the heat of the day and releases it slowly during the night, reducing the extremes of temperature that characterise the desert floor. The gardens and terraced plots cultivated in the valley produce dates, figs, and other fruits that are the primary food source of the settlement; the surplus is traded to caravans passing through the massif.

## The Mines and the Minerals

The massif of Kheled is rich in minerals, including copper, salt, and precious stones that are among the most valued products of Sahrana. The mining of these materials is a significant part of the settlement's economy. The mines are worked on a small scale, for the deposits are not extensive, but the quality of the minerals is high; the copper, salt, and stones extracted from the massif are traded to caravans passing through the Kharesh, where they command high prices in the markets of the coast and the northern continents.

The salt mined from the massif is particularly valued, for the salt of the Kharesh is among the purest in the known world. The salt trade is a significant source of income for the settlement. The salt is extracted from deposits beneath the massif and transported by caravans to the markets of the coast, where it is exchanged for grain, cloth, and manufactured goods the settlement cannot produce for itself. The copper mined from the massif is likewise valued; the copper of Kheled is of a quality prized by metalworkers of the coast, and demand for it is a constant feature of trade between the settlement and the caravans.

The precious stones found in the massif are among the rarest products of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) interior. The stones extracted from the mines of Kheled include carnelians, agates, and the occasional sardonyx sought by jewellers of the coastal cities. The stones are found in the deepest workings of the massif; the mining of them requires patience and skill, for the veins are narrow and extraction must be conducted with care to preserve the integrity of the crystals. The stones recovered are traded to the caravans; the prices they command are a significant supplement to the income of the settlement.

## History

Kheled was established in antiquity. The founders were human prospectors who discovered the springs and mineral deposits of the massif during the early exploration of the Kharesh. The springs were the initial attraction, for water is the most precious resource in the deep desert, and the presence of a reliable water source was the foundation upon which the settlement was built. The mining of minerals followed, and the community grew as the value of the copper, salt, and stones became known to the caravan masters traversing the desert.

The relationship between Kheled and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) was established in the early centuries of the settlement's history. The pact provided the caravans and trade connections that sustained the community in exchange for the minerals and provisions the settlement produced. Kheled's dependency on the pact is a practical arrangement, for the settlement is too small and remote to maintain an independent trade network; the caravans of the pact are the only reliable connection between Kheled and the markets of the coast.

## The Settlement

Kheled is a small, compact settlement whose buildings are constructed of the stone of the massif, with thick walls and small windows providing insulation against heat and cold. The dwellings are built into the sides of the valley; the streets are steep and narrow, connected by paths and stairs cut into the rock. The population is small and entirely [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the people are known for their endurance and deep attachment to the upland that is their home.

The community is organised around the management of the springs and the mines. The affairs of the settlement are governed by a council of elders drawn from the families that have inhabited the massif for generations. The council oversees the distribution of water, the rotation of mining shifts, and the negotiation of trade with caravans passing through the massif. The governance is informal but effective; the council is the primary institution of the community, for the settlement is too small to require a more elaborate administration.

The people of Kheled are distinguished by their knowledge of the upland desert and their skill in the extraction of minerals the massif provides. The miners are among the most experienced in the Kharesh; their techniques for working narrow veins and preserving precious stones are passed from generation to generation. The gardeners are likewise skilled, for the cultivation of terraced plots in the valley requires careful management of water and soil; the dates and figs produced in the gardens are among the finest in the Sahrani interior.

## Significance

Kheled is the most important upland settlement in the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and its minerals and provisions are a valued resource for the caravans crossing the desert. The settlement is a dependency of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the alliance of the inland caravan cities, and its role in the mineral trade is a significant component of the economy of the desert interior. The significance of Kheled lies in its minerals and springs, which are among the most valuable resources in the deep desert; the settlement is a testament to the capacity of humans to extract a living from the most demanding environments in the known world.

The isolation of Kheled is both a challenge and a protection, for the massif that shelters the settlement also separates it from the conflicts and rivalries of the wider world. The community has endured for centuries; the springs and mines that sustained the founders continue to sustain their descendants, and the traditions of the upland desert are preserved in the practices and customs of the people who call Kheled their home.
