## A Fishing Village in the Reed Marshes

Cranemarsh is a fishing village of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) situated in the Reed Marshes, a vast expanse of wetlands that lies along the southern coast of the continent. The village is built on a low island of higher ground that rises above the marsh, accessible only by boat or by the causeway that connects the island to the firmer land of the interior. The village takes its name from the cranes that nest in the marshes and from the reeds that cover the wetlands, and the people of Cranemarsh have lived in the marshes for as many generations as the records cover, their lives shaped by the water and the wildlife of the wetlands.

The Reed Marshes are one of the most extensive wetlands in Aethelar, covering many hundreds of square miles of the southern coastal plain. The marshes are a complex mosaic of open water, reed beds, and islands of higher ground, and they provide habitat for a remarkable abundance of birds, fish, and other wildlife. The people of Cranemarsh are the principal human inhabitants of the marshes, and they have developed a distinctive culture and economy adapted to the conditions of the wetlands.

## The Reed Marshes and the Islands of Higher Ground

The Reed Marshes extend across the southern coastal plain, following the course of several rivers that flow into the sea through the wetlands. The marshes are covered with dense stands of reed, interspersed with areas of open water and channels that connect the various parts of the wetland. The reed beds are tall and dense, forming a barrier that is difficult to penetrate for those unfamiliar with the marshes.

The islands of higher ground that rise above the marsh are the sites of the settlements and the cultivated fields of the marsh people. The islands are formed of alluvial deposits and the accumulated debris of centuries, and they are raised above the level of the highest floods by a combination of natural elevation and the embankments that the inhabitants have built. The islands are small, and the land that is available for cultivation and building is limited, but the islands provide a secure foundation for the life of the community.

The founding of Cranemarsh is recorded in the traditions of the marsh people as having occurred in the early period of the settlement of the southern coast. The first settlers were fishermen and hunters who were drawn to the marshes by the abundance of fish and waterfowl that the wetlands provided. The initial settlement consisted of a few families who built their houses on the island and began to exploit the resources of the marsh.

The village grew slowly over the centuries, as the families of the original settlers multiplied and the techniques for living in the marsh were developed and refined. The inhabitants of Cranemarsh learned to navigate the channels of the marsh in flat-bottomed boats, to harvest the reeds for thatching and weaving, to catch the fish and the eels that inhabited the waters, and to hunt the birds that nested in the reed beds.

## The Fisheries and the Hunting of the Marsh

The fisheries of Cranemarsh are the principal source of food and income for the village. The waters of the marsh are rich in fish and eels, and the fishermen of the village set nets and traps in the channels and the open pools to catch the fish that are the staple of the local diet. The fish of the marsh are varied, including pike, perch, bream, and eels, and the catch is consumed fresh, smoked, or salted for preservation.

The hunting of the marsh is the secondary source of food and income. The marshes are home to a large population of waterfowl, including ducks, geese, and the cranes from which the village takes its name. The hunters of the village pursue the birds for meat and feathers, using a variety of techniques that have been developed over the centuries. The birds are taken with nets, traps, and the longbows that are the traditional weapon of the marsh hunters.

## The Reed Harvest and the Crafts of the Marsh

The reed harvest is the principal seasonal activity of the marsh, and the reeds are the most important raw material that the marsh provides. The reeds are cut in the late summer and early autumn, when the stems are tall, strong, and dry. The reeds are used for the thatching of roofs, the weaving of mats and baskets, and the making of the fences and the screens that are used in the village and the surrounding farms.

The crafts of the marsh are the skills that the inhabitants have developed for the working of the reeds and the other materials that the marsh provides. The thatchers of Cranemarsh are among the most skilled in the southern coast, and the roofs of the houses of the village are covered with reed thatch that is waterproof, durable, and resistant to the wind. The weavers of the village produce mats, baskets, and other objects from the reeds, and the objects are sold at the markets of the interior and the coast.

## The Causeway and the Boats of Cranemarsh

The causeway that connects Cranemarsh to the firmer land of the interior is the principal link between the village and the wider world. The causeway is a low embankment of earth and stone that crosses the marsh, raised above the level of the highest floods and surfaced with gravel and stone. The causeway is wide enough for a single wagon, and the approach to the village is controlled by a gate that can be closed to prevent the passage of unauthorised persons.

The boats of Cranemarsh are the essential means of transport within the marsh. The boats are flat-bottomed craft, shallow of draft and broad of beam, designed for the navigation of the channels and the shallow waters of the wetlands. The boats are used for fishing, hunting, the transport of goods, and the movement of people between the islands and the settlements of the marsh. The boat builders of Cranemarsh are among the most skilled on the coast, and the boats they produce are traded to other settlements of the southern wetlands.

The village green is the common space at the centre of the island, where the inhabitants gather for the meeting of the council and the celebration of the festivals. The governance of Cranemarsh is in the hands of a council of elders composed of the heads of the principal families of the village. The customs of the marsh are the traditions that have been developed over the centuries to govern the life of the community. The customs are respected by all the inhabitants of the village, and the council of elders is the authority that upholds them.
