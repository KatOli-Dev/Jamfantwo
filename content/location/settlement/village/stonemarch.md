## Overview

Stonemarch is a frontier settlement on the eastern marches of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/the-stoneheim-highlands) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), situated where the highland valleys open onto the grasslands that lie between the interior ranges and the cold steppes of the far east. The settlement takes its name from the broad stone causeway that the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) laid across the marches in the early centuries of the dwarven expansion, and the name has been used by the inhabitants and by the chroniclers of the interior to describe both the settlement and the country that surrounds it. Stonemarch is one of the most isolated of the surface communities of the highlands, and its people are among the most familiar with the boundary between the dwarven realm and the open country beyond.

The settlement is a dependency of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim), and its function is the maintenance of the causeway and the watch over the eastern approaches to the dwarven territory. The causeway itself is one of the engineering achievements of the dwarven realm, a paved road of fitted stone that crosses the marches and provides a reliable route between the highland halls and the eastern valleys. The settlement of Stonemarch is the principal waypoint on the causeway, and the services that it provides to the caravans and the patrols are the foundation of the local economy.

## The Marches and the Causeway

The marches in which Stonemarch stands are a transitional country between the highland ranges and the open grasslands of the eastern interior. The land is gently rolling, with broad valleys and low, rounded hills that are clothed in the hardy grasses of the high country and the stunted shrubs that survive the long winters. The soil is thinner than in the sheltered valleys of the highlands, and the agricultural produce of the marches is limited to the hardy crops that can tolerate the cold and the wind. The pastures that cover the hills are grazed by the flocks of the settlement and by the herds of the [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines) who range across the open country to the east.

The causeway is the most distinctive feature of the marches, and its paving stones are a marvel of dwarven engineering. The road is laid in fitted courses of dark stone that has been quarried from the highland ranges and transported across the difficult country to the marches. The surface of the road is smooth and durable, and it remains usable through the winter months when the unpaved tracks of the surrounding country are buried in snow. The causeway is flanked by low walls of the same dark stone, and the walls provide shelter from the wind and a guide to the traveller who is caught on the open road in a storm.

The maintenance of the causeway is the primary responsibility of the settlement, and the masons and the roadkeepers of Stonemarch are the most experienced in the eastern marches. The settlement maintains a store of dressed stone against the need for repairs, and the roadkeepers patrol the causeway at regular intervals to identify the sections that require attention. The work of repair is heaviest in the spring, when the frost has lifted and the damage of the winter can be assessed, and the masons are occupied through the summer with the relaying of the paving and the rebuilding of the walls.

## Settlements and Population

The population of Stonemarch is a mixture of [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) and [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), with the dwarves the larger group. The dwarves are descended from the clans that were assigned to the maintenance of the causeway and the watch over the eastern approaches, and they retain a strong attachment to the halls of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) from which their ancestors came. The humans are the descendants of the muleteers and the traders who settled in the marches to support the caravans, and they have become a permanent community in their own right. The two communities coexist in a relationship of mutual respect and cooperation, and the governance of the settlement is shared between them.

The dwarven families of Stonemarch maintain the traditions of their highland clans, and the forges and the workshops of the settlement are the source of the ironwork and the tools that the roadkeepers and the masons require. The dwarven smiths of Stonemarch are not as celebrated as those of [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar), but their work is well suited to the demands of the marches, and the tools that they produce are valued by the roadkeepers and the farmers of the surrounding country. The dwarven households are centred on the great hall that stands at the eastern end of the settlement, and the hall is the meeting place of the clans and the venue for the festivals that mark the turning of the year.

The human community of Stonemarch is composed of the families that have inhabited the marches for several generations, and their way of life is a blend of the highland traditions of their dwarven neighbours and the practices of the human settlements of the interior. The humans are the farmers and the herders of the settlement, and they manage the flocks and the fields that produce the wool, the leather, and the grain that the community requires. The human households are situated on the western side of the settlement, and the common hall where the elders meet is the centre of the human community.

## The Watch and the Eastern Approaches

The watch over the eastern approaches is one of the principal functions of Stonemarch, and the settlement maintains a garrison of warriors and a network of signal towers that command the views across the marches. The signal towers are built of the same dark stone as the causeway, and they are positioned at intervals of several miles along the eastern horizon. The towers communicate by horn and by the shuttered lamps that are used in dwarven workings, and a warning can pass from the most distant tower to Stonemarch and onward to the highland halls within minutes.

The garrison of Stonemarch is composed of dwarven warriors who serve a tour of duty at the marches before returning to their home holds, and of human rangers who are familiar with the open country beyond the causeway. The garrison is not large, for the marches are not a theatre of constant warfare, but the warriors are well trained and the towers are maintained in good order. The patrols that the garrison mounts across the marches serve both to deter the raiders who might otherwise prey upon the caravans and to gather the intelligence that the highland halls require about the movements of the peoples of the open country.

The [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines) of the cold steppes are the nearest neighbours of Stonemarch, and the relationship between the settlement and the lupine kindreds is one of cautious cooperation and occasional trade. The lupines range across the open country to the east of the causeway, following the herds of the steppe and the game that the marches provide, and their encounters with the settlement are most frequent at the seasonal gatherings that are held at the edge of the highland territory. The lupines trade the fur and the meat of the steppe for the metalwork and the grain of the settlement, and the exchanges are conducted according to the conventions that have been established over many generations.

## Economy and the Caravans

The economy of Stonemarch is built upon the services that the settlement provides to the caravans and the highland halls. The wayhouse of the settlement is the principal stopping point on the causeway, and the provisions and the shelter that it offers are valued by the caravan masters who travel the road. The wool and the leather that are produced in the marches are traded to the caravans in exchange for the grain, the cloth, and the manufactured goods that the settlement cannot produce for itself. The metalwork of the dwarven smiths is exported through the caravans to the markets of the interior, and the tools that they produce are valued by the farmers and the herders of the surrounding country.

The caravans that travel the causeway are a familiar sight at Stonemarch during the open season, and the rhythm of the settlement is set by their arrival and departure. The wayhouse is busiest in the early summer, when the first caravans of the season reach the marches after the long winter, and in the late autumn, when the last caravans are completing their journeys before the snow returns. The roadkeepers and the muleteers of the settlement accompany the caravans along the causeway, and the services that they provide are a significant source of income for the community.

## Significance

Stonemarch is one of the most isolated of the surface settlements of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/the-stoneheim-highlands), and its significance lies in the causeway that it maintains and the watch that it keeps over the eastern approaches. The settlement is not a centre of mining or of great metalwork, and it is not the seat of any high authority, but the services that it provides are a critical component of the infrastructure that connects the highland halls to the eastern interior. The dwarven causeway is one of the engineering marvels of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim), and the settlement that maintains it is a testament to the dwarven capacity to extend their reach across the difficult country of the marches. The significance of Stonemarch lies in the road itself and the watch that it keeps, and the settlement is a symbol of the order that the dwarven realm imposes upon the wild country at the edge of its territory.
