## Overview

Amber Hollow is a small settlement in the wooded foothills of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/the-stoneheim-highlands) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), situated in a sheltered valley where the streams that descend from the interior ranges meet the lower slopes of the western morvind. The settlement takes its name from the golden colour of the birch and rowan that fill the valley in the autumn, when the leaves turn a deep amber before they fall, and the name has been used by the inhabitants for as many generations as any can remember. Amber Hollow is one of the surface communities that serve as the points of contact between the underground halls of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) and the human traders whose caravans travel the highland roads. The settlement is small but enduring, and its people are among the most familiar with the boundary between the world of the dwarven forges and the world of the open valley.

## The Valley and the Woodlands

The valley in which Amber Hollow stands is a long, narrow glen that follows the course of a stream which descends from the higher peaks of the highlands and joins the rivers of the western morvind. The valley is sheltered from the worst of the wind by the surrounding hills, and the climate here is milder than on the exposed slopes above. The woodlands that clothe the valley floor and the lower hillsides are a mixture of birch, rowan, and alder, with occasional stands of pine on the drier ground, and the undergrowth is rich with hazel, willow, and the smaller shrubs that support the wildlife of the valley. The amber colour that gives the settlement its name is most striking in the late autumn, when the birches and the rowans turn together in a single wave of gold and copper that is visible from the highland roads above.

The stream that runs through the valley is the principal water source for the settlement, and its flow is reliable through the summer months when the snow is melting on the higher slopes. The stream is too small for barge traffic, but it provides ample water for the settlement and for the small water mills that grind the grain that the valley can spare. The fish of the stream are a valued supplement to the diet of the community, and the trout and the grayling that are caught in the deeper pools are a familiar feature of the local table. The beavers that inhabit the upper reaches of the stream have built their lodges along the quieter stretches, and the management of the beaver dams is one of the small tasks that the community shares.

The valley opens to the west toward the lower morvind, and the road that follows the stream down to the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) is the principal route of communication with the wider world. The road climbs east from the valley to the surface settlements of the highlands and to the approaches to the gates of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim), and the traffic of the caravans is the foundation of the local economy. The settlement is a waypoint on the route, and the services that it provides to the caravans, including shelter, repair, and the guidance of the local fords, are the reason for its prosperity.

## The Community and the Trade

The population of Amber Hollow is a mixture of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and a small community of [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) who maintain a counting house and a forge in the settlement. The humans are the majority, and they are descended from the families that first settled the valley during the early expansion of the human population into the western morvind. The dwarves are the agents of the trading houses of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim), and their presence in the settlement is a sign of the importance of the trade that flows through the valley. The dwarves of Amber Hollow are respected for their skill in metalwork and their knowledge of the highland routes, and the forge that they maintain is the only source of ironwork in the valley.

The trade of Amber Hollow is built upon the caravans that travel the highland road, and the services that the settlement provides are the foundation of the local economy. The settlement maintains a wayhouse where the caravans can rest and resupply, and the provisions that are produced in the valley, including the grain, the dried fish, and the preserves that are made from the autumn fruits, are valued by the caravan masters for their quality. The mules and the pack horses that are kept in the settlement are available for hire to the smaller caravans that cannot afford the larger companies, and the muleteers of Amber Hollow are familiar figures on the highland road.

The autumn fruits that are gathered in the woodlands are the source of one of the more unusual products of the settlement. The rowan berries, the birch sap, and the honey that the valley bees produce from the autumn flowers are gathered and preserved by the women of the community, and the amber-coloured preserves that are made from them are exported through the caravans to the markets of the coast. The preserves are valued for their colour and their flavour, and they are among the more distinctive products of the western morvind. The amber resin that gathers on the bark of the older birches is also collected, and the small amount that is harvested each year is sold to the jewellers of the coast for use in the making of ornaments and varnishes.

## The Seasons and the Routines

The life of Amber Hollow is shaped by the rhythm of the seasons and the demands of the caravans. The winter is the quietest period, for the highland road is blocked by snow and the settlement is cut off from the wider world for several months of the year. The community prepares for the winter during the autumn, gathering the fruits, the timber, and the fodder that will sustain the settlement through the cold months. The long winter evenings are spent in the repair of tools and the making of the goods that will be sold to the caravans in the spring, and the storytelling and the music that fill the common hall of the wayhouse are a reminder of the bonds that hold the community together.

The spring is the season of preparation, for the caravans begin to move as soon as the highland road is clear, and the settlement must be ready to receive the first companies of the trading season. The mules are shod, the wayhouse is provisioned, and the muleteers are organised into the teams that will accompany the caravans on the highland roads. The summer is the busiest period, for the caravans travel the road in a steady stream from the thaw until the first snow, and the services that the settlement provides are in constant demand. The autumn is the season of harvest and gathering, when the fruits of the valley are collected and the preparations for the next winter begin.

The festivals of Amber Hollow are tied to the seasons and to the trade. The spring opening is marked by a ceremony at the ford where the highland road descends into the valley, and the first caravan of the season is welcomed with bread and salt. The autumn harvest is celebrated with a gathering in the common hall, where the preserves and the honey are shared and the thanks of the community are offered for the produce of the valley. The winter festival is a more solemn occasion, held in the depths of the cold months when the community is most isolated, and the storytelling and the music of the festival are the principal pleasure of the long nights.

## The Settlement

Amber Hollow is a small, compact settlement, and its buildings are constructed of the timber that is gathered from the surrounding woodlands and the stone that is quarried from the hillsides. The dwellings are low and close-set, with thick walls of squared logs and roofs of birch bark weighted with stone to resist the wind and the snow. The wayhouse is the largest building in the settlement, and it serves as the common hall, the storehouse, and the guesthouse for the caravans that pass through. The buildings are clustered around the ford where the highland road crosses the stream, and the settlement extends a short distance up the valley on either side of the crossing.

The governance of Amber Hollow is informal, resting with the elders of the founding families and the senior members of the trading houses that maintain an interest in the settlement. The elders meet in the common hall to discuss the affairs of the community, and their decisions are made by consensus and enforced by the respect in which they are held. The community is too small to require an elaborate administration, and the simplicity of its governance is a reflection of the practical character of the people. The dwarven forge is governed by the master smith who oversees the work, and the trading houses of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) send a representative to the settlement at the opening of each trading season to oversee the season's transactions.

## Significance

Amber Hollow is one of the smaller settlements of the western [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/the-stoneheim-highlands), and its significance lies in its position on the highland road and the services that it provides to the caravans. The settlement is not a centre of mining or metalwork, and it is not the seat of any great authority, but the wayhouse and the muleteers of Amber Hollow are a familiar feature of the highland trade, and the amber-coloured preserves of the valley are a valued product of the regional economy. The community is a reminder that the prosperity of the great cities of the highlands depends upon the labour and the hospitality of the small settlements that line the roads between them, and the existence of Amber Hollow is a testament to the capacity of a small community to make a place for itself at the meeting point of two worlds.
