## A Village in the Aethelar Heartwood Fringe

Elmwood is a village of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) situated at the fringe of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood), the great forest that covers the central highlands of the continent. The village stands where the forest gives way to the open farmlands of the river valleys, and its position at the boundary of woodland and cultivated country has shaped the character and the economy of the settlement. Elmwood takes its name from the elm trees that grow in the hedgerows and the woodlots of the village lands, and the elm is the emblem of the community.

The Heartwood fringe is a transitional zone where the dense forest of the highlands breaks up into a patchwork of woodland, pasture, and field. The elms that give the village its name are a characteristic tree of this zone, growing in the hedgerows that divide the fields and in the small copses that dot the landscape. The village is built at the edge of the forest, where the trees are thinner and the light penetrates to the forest floor, and the inhabitants of Elmwood have lived in close relationship with the woodland for as many generations as the records cover.

## The Heartwood Fringe and the Elm Trees

The Heartwood fringe is the belt of country that lies between the dense forest of the highlands and the open farmlands of the valleys. The fringe is a landscape of small fields, woodlots, and pastures, where the forest has been cleared in places to create land for cultivation but where the woodland remains a significant presence. The soils of the fringe are thinner and more acidic than the soils of the valleys, but the land is fertile enough to support the farming that is the foundation of the local economy.

The elm trees that grow in the hedgerows and the woodlots of the fringe are a characteristic feature of the landscape. The elm is a tall, broad-crowned tree that provides shade for the livestock and timber for the construction of buildings and the making of furniture. The elm wood of Elmwood is prized for its strength and durability, and the timber from the village is sold in the markets of the lowlands.

## The Founding of Elmwood

The founding of Elmwood is recorded in the traditions of the community as having occurred in the early period of the expansion of settlement into the Heartwood fringe. The first settlers were farmers and woodcutters who recognised the potential of the fringe for agriculture and the value of the timber that the woodland provided. The initial settlement consisted of a few families who built their houses on the higher ground at the edge of the forest and cleared the surrounding land for fields and pastures.

The village grew slowly over the centuries, as the families of the original settlers multiplied and the land under cultivation expanded. The woodland was pushed back gradually, the trees felled and the stumps grubbed out to create fields and pastures. The village became a permanent settlement, its inhabitants living by a combination of farming, woodland management, and the crafts that the forest provided.

The fields of Elmwood are scattered among the woodlots and the hedgerows that characterise the landscape of the fringe. The fields are used for the cultivation of grain, vegetables, and the fodder crops that sustain the livestock of the village.

The pastures of Elmwood are the grasslands that lie between the fields and the forest, where the cattle and the sheep of the village graze. The pastures are less productive than the pastures of the open valleys, for the soils of the fringe are thinner and the grazing is coarser, but the animals that are raised on the fringe are hardy and well adapted to the conditions of the region.

The woodlots of Elmwood are the remnants of the forest that have been left standing for the production of timber and the other products that the forest provides. The woodlots are managed by the village, with the rights to fell timber and to gather firewood being regulated by the council of elders. The woodlots are the source of the timber that is used for the construction and repair of the buildings of the village and for the making of the tools, furniture, and other objects that the village requires.

The forest crafts of Elmwood are the skills that the inhabitants have developed for the working of wood and the other products of the forest. The woodcutters of the village are skilled in the felling and processing of timber, and the carpenters and joiners of the village produce the furniture and the fittings that are used in the houses and the buildings of the community. The charcoal burners of the village produce the charcoal that is used for fuel in the forges and the kitchens of the settlement.

The lanes are narrow and unpaved, and they are used by the villagers for the movement of people, livestock, and the goods that are produced in the fields and the workshops of the community.
