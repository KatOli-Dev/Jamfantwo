## Overview

Ravenscrag is a mountain settlement in the far interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), situated in a remote valley of the interior ranges where the mountains are at their most rugged and the approaches are the most difficult. The settlement is the most remote permanent community in Morvind, and its people are among the most hardy and the most self-reliant in the known world. Ravenscrag is a small, isolated settlement, and its primary function is the mining of the mineral deposits that are found in the surrounding peaks and the maintenance of the highland routes that connect the far interior to the rest of the continent.

## The Valley and the Mines

The valley in which Ravenscrag stands is a deep, narrow gorge that is carved into the interior ranges by a river that flows from the highest peaks, and the settlement is built on the floor of the gorge and on the terraces that line its walls. The valley is sheltered from the worst of the wind, and the river provides water and the modest agriculture that sustains the community, but the growing season is short and the crops are limited to the hardiest varieties. The mines that surround Ravenscrag are the primary reason for the settlement, and the ore that is extracted from the peaks includes iron, copper, and the precious metals that are found in the deepest veins of the interior ranges.

The mining operations of Ravenscrag are conducted on a small scale, for the deposits are not as rich as those of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) or [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar), and the remoteness of the settlement makes the export of ore difficult and expensive. The ore that is extracted is processed in the small forges of the settlement and worked into the tools, the weapons, and the implements that the community needs, and the surplus is exported through the highland routes to the markets of the interior. The [corvids](/content/species/sapient/corvids) of the high forests are occasional visitors to the settlement, and the trade between the mountain dwarves and the forest corvids is a small but valued connection to the wider world.

The forges of Ravenscrag are small but capable, and the smiths of the settlement are skilled in the working of the iron and the copper that are the primary products of the mines. The tools and the implements that are produced in the forges are sufficient for the needs of the community, and the surplus is carried over the highland routes to the markets of the interior, where the metalwork of Ravenscrag is valued for its sturdiness and its reliability. The smiths of the settlement are among the most respected figures in the community, for the tools that they produce are the foundation of the mining and the agriculture that sustain the settlement.

## The Routes and the Isolation

The routes that connect Ravenscrag to the rest of Morvind are few and difficult, and the settlement is cut off from the wider world for much of the year, when the snow blocks the passes and the river freezes. The overland routes that lead to the interior are navigable only during the brief summer, and the journey to the nearest settlement is a matter of many days of hard travel through the most demanding terrain in the continent. The isolation of Ravenscrag is the defining feature of the settlement, and the people have learned to live on what the valley and the mines provide, supplementing their resources with the occasional trade that arrives during the summer months.

The highland routes that connect Ravenscrag to the rest of Morvind are the work of the dwarves who founded the settlement, and the routes are engineering achievements in their own right, for the passes that they cross are among the highest and the most difficult in the interior ranges. The routes are marked by the cairns that the dwarves have built at intervals along the way, and the cairns serve as waymarkers for the travellers who venture into the far interior. The routes are passable only during the brief summer, when the snow retreats from the passes and the river that flows through the valley runs free, and the window for travel is short and uncertain.

## The Seasons and the Survival

The seasons of Ravenscrag are the most extreme of any settlement in the known world, for the winter is long and severe, and the snow that falls in the gorge accumulates to a depth that buries the lower buildings and blocks the routes for many months. The community prepares for the winter during the short summer and autumn, stockpiling the food, the fuel, and the supplies that will sustain the settlement through the months of isolation. The preparation for the winter is the most important activity of the community, and every member of the settlement contributes to the stockpiling and the preservation of the resources that will be needed.

The summer is the season of activity, for the passes open and the routes become passable, and the trade that arrives during the summer months is the only contact that the settlement has with the wider world. The summer is also the season of the mining, for the veins of ore that are worked in the peaks are accessible only during the brief months when the snow retreats from the higher slopes. The mining operations are conducted intensively during the summer, for the ore that is extracted must be processed and stockpiled before the winter closes the mines and the forges.

## The Settlement

Ravenscrag is a small, compact settlement, and its buildings are constructed of the dark stone of the gorge, with walls that are thick and roofs that are weighted with stone to resist the wind and the snow. The buildings are built into the terraces that line the walls of the gorge, and the streets are steep and narrow, connected by stairs and paths that are cut into the rock. The population of Ravenscrag is chiefly [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), with a small community of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) who are involved in the management of the routes and the trade with the interior.

## The Corvids and the Forest

The [corvids](/content/species/sapient/corvids) of the high forests are the nearest neighbours of Ravenscrag, and the relationship between the mountain dwarves and the forest corvids is a distinctive and a valued feature of the life of the settlement. The corvids are the inhabitants of the high forests that cover the lower slopes of the interior ranges, and their knowledge of the forests and the routes that pass through them is a valuable resource for the dwarves of Ravenscrag. The trade between the two peoples is small but valued, for the corvids provide the forest products that the settlement cannot produce for itself, including the herbs, the resins, and the meat that are the supplements to the diet of the community.

The trade between the dwarves and the corvids is conducted at the highland passes that connect the gorge to the forests, and the exchanges are made during the summer months when the passes are open. The corvids are cautious and deliberate in their approach to the trade, for they are a careful people who value their independence and their way of life, but the relationship between the two peoples has been peaceful and mutually beneficial for as long as the settlement has existed. The corvids are occasional visitors to the settlement, and their presence in the gorge is a rare and a welcome event, for the contact with the forest people is a reminder that the community is not entirely alone in the far interior.

## Culture and the Isolation

The culture of Ravenscrag is shaped by the isolation and the severity of the environment, and the community is noted for its self-reliance and its attachment to the traditions that have sustained the settlement through the centuries. The songs and the stories of Ravenscrag are the songs and the stories of the mountain and the mine, and the tradition of storytelling is a valued part of the cultural life of the community, for the long winter evenings are spent in the sharing of the old tales and the singing of the old songs. The stories tell of the founding of the settlement, the carving of the first chambers, and the opening of the first mines, and the heroes of the stories are the dwarves who built the settlement and who established the community in the most demanding environment in the known world.

The festivals of Ravenscrag are modest affairs, for the population is small and the resources are limited, but the annual feast of the midwinter is a cherished tradition that marks the turning of the year and the beginning of the long retreat toward the spring. The feast is held in the great chamber of the settlement, and the entire community gathers for the occasion, for the midwinter is the darkest and the coldest time of the year, and the feast is a celebration of the endurance and the solidarity that sustain the community through the months of isolation.

## Significance

Ravenscrag is the most remote permanent settlement in Morvind, and its people are the guardians of the far interior and the highland routes that connect it to the rest of the continent. The settlement is a dependency of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), and its mineral output is a small but valued contribution to the economy of the deep interior. The significance of Ravenscrag lies in its survival and its productivity in an environment that is among the most demanding in the known world, and the settlement is a testament to the endurance and the self-reliance of the people of the Morvind interior.
