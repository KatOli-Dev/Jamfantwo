## Overview

Ravenscrag is a mountain settlement in the far interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), situated in a remote valley of the interior ranges where the mountains are at their most rugged and the approaches are the most difficult. The settlement is the most remote permanent community in Morvind, and its people are among the most hardy and self-reliant in the known world. Ravenscrag is a small, isolated settlement whose primary function is the mining of mineral deposits found in the surrounding peaks and the maintenance of the highland routes connecting the far interior to the rest of the continent.

## The Valley and the Mines

The valley in which Ravenscrag stands is a deep, narrow gorge carved into the interior ranges by a river flowing from the highest peaks. The settlement is built on the floor of the gorge and on terraces lining its walls. The valley is sheltered from the worst of the wind; the river provides water and the modest agriculture that sustains the community, but the growing season is short and crops are limited to the hardiest varieties. The mines surrounding Ravenscrag are the primary reason for the settlement; the ore extracted from the peaks includes iron, copper, and precious metals found in the deepest veins of the interior ranges.

Mining operations are conducted on a small scale, for the deposits are not as rich as those of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) or [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar), and the remoteness of the settlement makes export of ore difficult and expensive. The ore extracted is processed in small forges of the settlement and worked into tools, weapons, and implements the community needs; the surplus is exported through the highland routes to the markets of the interior. The [corvids](/content/species/sapient/corvids) of the high forests are occasional visitors to the settlement; trade between the mountain dwarves and the forest corvids is a small but valued connection to the wider world.

The forges are small but capable; the smiths of the settlement are skilled in working iron and copper that are the primary products of the mines. Tools and implements produced in the forges are sufficient for the needs of the community; the surplus is carried over the highland routes to markets of the interior, where the metalwork of Ravenscrag is valued for its sturdiness and reliability. The smiths are among the most respected figures in the community, for the tools they produce are the foundation of the mining and agriculture that sustain the settlement.

## The Routes and the Isolation

The routes connecting Ravenscrag to the rest of Morvind are few and difficult. The settlement is cut off from the wider world for much of the year, when snow blocks the passes and the river freezes. The overland routes leading to the interior are navigable only during the brief summer; the journey to the nearest settlement is a matter of many days of hard travel through the most demanding terrain in the continent. The isolation of Ravenscrag is the defining feature of the settlement; the people have learned to live on what the valley and the mines provide, supplementing their resources with occasional trade arriving during the summer months.

The highland routes connecting Ravenscrag to the rest of Morvind are the work of the dwarves who founded the settlement. The routes are engineering achievements in their own right, for the passes they cross are among the highest and most difficult in the interior ranges. The routes are marked by cairns built at intervals along the way, serving as waymarkers for travellers venturing into the far interior. The routes are passable only during the brief summer, when snow retreats from the passes and the river flowing through the valley runs free; the window for travel is short and uncertain.

## The Seasons and the Survival

The seasons of Ravenscrag are the most extreme of any settlement in the known world. Winter is long and severe; snow accumulating in the gorge buries the lower buildings and blocks the routes for many months. The community prepares for winter during the short summer and autumn, stockpiling food, fuel, and supplies that will sustain the settlement through the months of isolation. Preparation for winter is the most important activity of the community; every member contributes to the stockpiling and preservation of resources that will be needed.

Summer is the season of activity. The passes open and the routes become passable; the trade arriving during summer is the only contact the settlement has with the wider world. Summer is also the season of mining, for the veins of ore worked in the peaks are accessible only during the brief months when snow retreats from the higher slopes. Mining operations are conducted intensively during summer; the ore extracted must be processed and stockpiled before winter closes the mines and forges.

## The Settlement

Ravenscrag is a small, compact settlement whose buildings are constructed of the dark stone of the gorge, with thick walls and roofs weighted with stone to resist wind and snow. The buildings are built into the terraces lining the walls of the gorge; the streets are steep and narrow, connected by stairs and paths cut into the rock. The population is chiefly [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), with a small community of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) involved in the management of the routes and the trade with the interior.

## The Corvids and the Forest

The [corvids](/content/species/sapient/corvids) of the high forests are the nearest neighbours of Ravenscrag. The relationship between the mountain dwarves and the forest corvids is a distinctive and valued feature of the life of the settlement. The corvids are the inhabitants of the high forests covering the lower slopes of the interior ranges; their knowledge of the forests and the routes passing through them is a valuable resource for the dwarves. Trade between the two peoples is small but valued; the corvids provide forest products the settlement cannot produce for itself, including herbs, resins, and meat that supplement the community's diet.

Trade between the dwarves and the corvids is conducted at the highland passes connecting the gorge to the forests; the exchanges are made during the summer months when the passes are open. The corvids are cautious and deliberate in their approach to trade, for they are a careful people who value their independence, but the relationship has been peaceful and mutually beneficial for as long as the settlement has existed. The corvids are occasional visitors to the settlement; their presence in the gorge is a rare and welcome event, for contact with the forest people is a reminder that the community is not entirely alone in the far interior.

## Culture and the Isolation

The culture of Ravenscrag is shaped by the isolation and severity of the environment. The community is noted for its self-reliance and attachment to traditions that have sustained the settlement through the centuries. The songs and stories of Ravenscrag are the songs and stories of the mountain and the mine; the tradition of storytelling is a valued part of cultural life, for the long winter evenings are spent sharing old tales and singing old songs. The stories tell of the founding of the settlement, the carving of the first chambers, and the opening of the first mines; the heroes of the stories are the dwarves who built the settlement in the most demanding environment in the known world.

The festivals are modest affairs, but the annual feast of the midwinter is a cherished tradition marking the turning of the year and the beginning of the long retreat toward spring. The feast is held in the great chamber of the settlement; the entire community gathers for the occasion, for the midwinter is the darkest and coldest time of the year, and the feast is a celebration of the endurance and solidarity that sustain the community through the months of isolation.

## Significance

Ravenscrag is the most remote permanent settlement in Morvind, and its people are the guardians of the far interior and the highland routes connecting it to the rest of the continent. The settlement is a dependency of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), and its mineral output is a small but valued contribution to the economy of the deep interior. The significance of Ravenscrag lies in its survival and productivity in an environment that is among the most demanding in the known world; the settlement is a testament to the endurance and self-reliance of the people of the Morvind interior.
