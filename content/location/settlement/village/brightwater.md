## Overview

Brightwater is a coastal settlement on the [Velden Coastlands](/content/location/settlement/region/velden-coastlands) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), situated on a sheltered cove to the east of [Caerhaven](/content/location/settlement/town/caerhaven) where a clear spring emerges from the coastal cliffs and flows across a shingle beach into the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The settlement takes its name from the remarkable clarity of the spring water, which is so pure that the chroniclers of the strait have remarked upon it since the first voyages through the channel. Brightwater is one of the smaller settlements of the coastlands, and its people are among the most familiar with the daily rhythms of the strait and the conditions of the coastal waters. The settlement is a dependency of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), and its function is the supply of fresh water and provisions to the vessels that pass through the eastern approaches to the [Strait of Velden](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden).

The settlement is small but enduring, and its position on the cove gives it a shelter from the prevailing winds that the more exposed harbours of the strait cannot match. The vessels that call at Brightwater are chiefly those that are preparing to enter the strait from the east and that require a last provisioning before the difficult passage, or those that have just completed the passage and that need to repair the damage that the treacherous waters have inflicted. The services that the settlement provides are valued by the mariners of the strait, and the reputation of Brightwater water is a byword among the pilots who work the channel.

## The Spring and the Cove

The spring that gives the settlement its name is the most distinctive feature of Brightwater, and its waters are the foundation of the local economy. The spring emerges from a fissure in the coastal cliffs at a point where the underlying rock is a hard and close-grained limestone that filters the water as it rises from the deep aquifers of the interior. The water is cold and clear, with a slight mineral taste that the mariners have come to associate with reliability and safety, and the flow is constant through the seasons, unaffected by the rainfall or the drought. The spring fills a stone trough that has been cut into the rock above the beach, and the trough is the principal source from which the settlement draws its water and from which the visiting vessels are supplied.

The cove that shelters the settlement is a curved bay that is protected from the prevailing winds by the headlands that extend from the coast on either side. The beach is a stretch of fine shingle that slopes gently into the water, and the depth of the bay is sufficient for the smaller vessels that call at the settlement but too shallow for the larger ocean-going ships that require the harbour at [Caerhaven](/content/location/settlement/town/caerhaven). The vessels that anchor in the cove are chiefly the coasting traders, the pilot cutters, and the small craft of the local fishermen, and the beach serves as a natural quay where the boats can be drawn up for repair or for the winter.

The cliffs that surround the cove are home to the seabirds that nest on the ledges in the spring and summer, and the eggs and the feathers of the birds are a small but valued addition to the local economy. The cliffs are also the source of the stone that the settlement uses for its buildings and its quays, and the quarries that have been opened in the cliffs above the cove are worked by the masons of the community. The stone is a pale limestone that weathers to a soft grey, and the buildings of Brightwater are constructed of this stone and roofed with slate that is quarried from the same cliffs.

## The Community and the Mariners

The population of Brightwater is small and entirely [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the people are descended from the families that first settled the cove to take advantage of the spring and the fishing grounds of the coast. The community is a mixture of fishermen, who work the coastal waters, and provisioners, who supply the mariners and the vessels that call at the settlement. The two occupations are not strictly separate, for most households combine the fishing with the provision trade, and the men and the women of the community divide their labour between the boats and the wayhouse according to the season.

The fishermen of Brightwater work the waters of the strait and the coastal reaches of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), and the fish that they catch are a significant addition to the provisions that the settlement provides to the visiting vessels. The catch includes the mackerel and the herring that are found in the coastal waters during the summer, and the cod and the larger fish that are taken from the deeper waters during the winter. The fishing boats are sturdy craft built in the local yards, and the boatbuilders of Brightwater are noted for the quality of their vessels, which are sought by the pilots and the coasting traders of the strait.

The provisioners of Brightwater are the men and the women who manage the wayhouse and the supplies that are sold to the mariners. The wayhouse is the principal building of the settlement, and it serves as the inn, the storehouse, and the meeting place for the community. The provisions that are provided include the fresh water from the spring, the bread that is baked in the community ovens, the salted fish and the dried meat that are the staple of the seafaring diet, and the fruit and the vegetables that are grown in the sheltered gardens behind the wayhouse. The reputation of the Brightwater provisions is such that vessels will put into the cove to refresh their supplies before the passage of the strait, even when the harbour of [Caerhaven](/content/location/settlement/town/caerhaven) is more convenient.

## The Compact and the Pilotage

The relationship between Brightwater and the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) is one of cooperation and mutual support, for the services that the settlement provides to the mariners complement the pilotage and the toll functions of the principal towns of the strait. The compact recognises the role of Brightwater as a provisioning station, and the settlement contributes to the maintenance of the navigation aids that mark the eastern approaches to the strait. The signal tower that stands on the headland above the cove is one of the aids maintained by the compact, and its lamp is visible for many miles across the Auric, guiding vessels toward the strait from the east.

The pilots of the strait are familiar visitors to Brightwater, and the settlement serves as a gathering point for the pilots who are waiting for a favourable tide or for the weather to clear before attempting the passage. The pilots who frequent the settlement appreciate the quality of the water and the provisions, and the wayhouse of Brightwater is a favoured resort for the pilots between voyages. The bond between the settlement and the pilots is reinforced by the marriages that have taken place between the local families and the pilot clans, and several of the pilots who work the strait can trace their ancestry to the founding families of Brightwater.

The [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) of the strait waters are occasional visitors to the cove at Brightwater, and a cautious but peaceful coexistence has developed between the settlement and the merfolk communities of the coastal waters. The merfolk are familiar with the quality of the spring water, and they visit the cove to drink from the outflow of the spring where it enters the sea. The fishermen of Brightwater respect the merfolk as fellow inhabitants of the coast, and the relationship between the two peoples is a feature of the local life that the chroniclers have remarked upon since the earliest days of the settlement.

## The Life of the Settlement

The rhythm of life at Brightwater is set by the tides, the seasons, and the traffic of the strait. The summer months are the busiest, for the weather is favourable and the passage of vessels is at its height, and the wayhouse is filled with the mariners and the merchants who are preparing for or recovering from the crossing of the strait. The winter months are quieter, for the storms that sweep the strait reduce the traffic and the fishermen must work harder to maintain the catch that the community requires. The spring and the autumn are the seasons of preparation and harvest, when the gardens are tended and the stores are laid by for the coming winter.

The festivals of Brightwater are tied to the sea and to the spring. The spring festival is held at the spring itself, and the community gathers to give thanks for the water that sustains them and to welcome the first vessels of the new trading season. The autumn festival is held at the beach, and the fishermen bring their boats ashore for the winter in a ceremony that marks the end of the fishing season. The midwinter festival is held in the wayhouse, and the storytelling and the music that fill the long evenings are a reminder of the bonds that hold the community together through the cold months.

The governance of Brightwater is informal, resting with the elders of the founding families and the senior members of the fishing and provisioning trades. The elders meet in the wayhouse to discuss the affairs of the community, and their decisions are made by consensus and enforced by the respect in which they are held. The community is too small to require an elaborate administration, and the simplicity of its governance is a reflection of the practical character of the people. The relationship with the compact is managed by a representative who is chosen by the elders to attend the meetings of the council, and the representative is responsible for ensuring that the interests of the settlement are represented in the deliberations of the compact.

## Significance

Brightwater is one of the smaller settlements of the [Velden Coastlands](/content/location/settlement/region/velden-coastlands), and its significance lies in the spring that gives it its name and the services that it provides to the mariners of the strait. The settlement is not a centre of trade or of governance, and it is not the seat of any great authority, but the water and the provisions of Brightwater are a familiar feature of the strait traffic, and the pilots of the channel regard the settlement as one of the most reliable points of refreshment on the eastern approaches. The reputation of the spring is a reminder that the prosperity of the great towns of the strait depends upon the labour and the hospitality of the smaller settlements that line the coast, and the existence of Brightwater is a testament to the capacity of a small community to make a place for itself at the edge of the most important waterway in the known world.
