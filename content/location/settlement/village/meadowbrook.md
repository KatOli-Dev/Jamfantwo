## A Farming Village in the Amber Vale

Meadowbrook is a farming village in the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale), the fertile river valley of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) that is the heartland of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael). The village stands on the banks of a small tributary of the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael), hidden in a shallow valley, at the point where the stream flows through a broad meadow that gives the settlement its name. Meadowbrook is a village of modest size, but it is among the oldest and most prosperous settlements of the Amber Vale, its fields and pastures having sustained the community for many generations.

The position of Meadowbrook in the Amber Vale has determined the character and the economy of the village. The soils of the vale are deep and fertile, renewed by the annual flooding of the river, and the fields of Meadowbrook produce bountiful harvests of grain, vegetables, and the fodder that sustains the livestock of the community. The stream that flows through the meadow provides water for the fields and the animals, and the meadow itself is the source of hay that is stored for the winter feeding of the livestock.

## The Amber Vale and the Tributary Stream

The Amber Vale is the most fertile agricultural region in central Aethelar, a broad river valley that follows the course of the River Vael from the foothills of the western mountains to the eastern lowlands. The vale is a landscape of fields and pastures, hedgerows and woodlots, dotted with the villages and market towns that serve the agricultural population. The soils of the vale are dark and rich, the product of centuries of flooding and careful cultivation, and the crops that grow upon them are among the finest in the continent.

The tributary stream that waters Meadowbrook is one of many small watercourses that flow into the River Vael from the surrounding hills. The stream rises in the low hills that bound the vale to the north and flows southward through a shallow valley before joining the Vael at a point some miles below the village. The stream is not large, but its flow is steady throughout the year, providing water for the fields, the livestock, and the domestic needs of the villagers.

The founding of Meadowbrook is recorded in the chronicles of the Amber Vale as having occurred in the early period of the settlement of the region. The village grew slowly over the centuries, as the families of the original settlers multiplied and new families were attracted by the prosperity of the vale. The fields surrounding the village were expanded, and the pastureland on the slopes of the bounding hills was brought into use for the grazing of livestock. ## The Fields and the Crops of Meadowbrook

The fields of Meadowbrook are the foundation of the prosperity of the village. The fields are divided among the farming families of the community, each family working its own holding and contributing to the common welfare through the payment of dues and the performance of the work that maintains the infrastructure of the village. The principal crops of the fields are wheat and barley, the grain that is the staple of the diet of the Amber Vale and the raw material for the bread and ale that sustain the population.

The rotation of crops is practised in the fields of Meadowbrook, with the fields being planted in a sequence that maintains the fertility of the soil and reduces the incidence of pests and diseases. The rotation involves the planting of grain in the first year, followed by a root crop or a legume in the second year, and the fallowing of the field in the third year to allow the soil to recover. The careful management of the fields is the foundation of the agricultural prosperity of the village, and the farmers of Meadowbrook are respected for their knowledge of the soils and the crops of the vale.

## The Pastures and the Livestock

The pastures of Meadowbrook are the meadows that lie along the stream and the grasslands that cover the lower slopes of the bounding hills. The meadows are cut for hay in the summer, and the hay is stored for the winter feeding of the livestock. The hill pastures are grazed by the cattle and sheep of the village, and the animals are brought down to the valley in the winter to be fed on the stored hay.

The livestock of Meadowbrook includes cattle, sheep, pigs, and poultry, each species providing products that are essential to the life of the community. The cattle provide milk, butter, and cheese, as well as meat and leather. The sheep provide wool for the spinning and weaving of cloth, as well as meat and hides. The pigs are raised for meat, and the poultry provides eggs and meat. The management of the livestock is the work of the families of the village, and each family maintains its own animals, grazing them on the common pastures of the community.

## The Mill and the Common Works

The mill of Meadowbrook is the principal industrial establishment of the village, a structure of stone and timber that stands on the stream at the southern end of the settlement. The mill is powered by an undershot water wheel, and the millstones that grind the grain are driven by the power of the stream. The mill is operated by the miller, a member of the community who holds the right to the mill in return for a share of the flour produced.

The common works of Meadowbrook include the maintenance of the roads and lanes that connect the village to the surrounding farms and to the market towns of the Amber Vale. The roads are maintained by the labour of the villagers, each family contributing a certain number of days of work per year to the upkeep of the infrastructure. The stream that runs through the village is also a common work, with the banks and the channel being maintained by the community to prevent flooding and ensure the supply of water.

## The Houses and the Streets of Meadowbrook

The houses of Meadowbrook are built in the traditional style of the Amber Vale, with stone foundations and timber frames, walls of wattle and daub, and roofs of thatch. The houses are arranged along the banks of the stream and along the lane that connects the village to the road that leads to the market towns of the vale. The houses are modest in size, each containing the living quarters of a single family and the storage and work spaces that the family requires.

The streets of Meadowbrook are unpaved lanes that are dusty in summer and muddy in winter. The lanes are narrow and winding, following the line of the stream and the contours of the valley. The lanes are used by the villagers for the movement of people, livestock, and the goods that are produced in the fields and the workshops of the community.

The governance of Meadowbrook is in the hands of a village council composed of the heads of the farming families of the community. The council is responsible for the administration of the village, the regulation of the common lands and the common works, and the resolution of disputes between the inhabitants. The council meets in the mill or in the open air, depending on the season and the weather.

The decisions of the council are made by consensus, and the customary laws that govern the life of the village are passed down through the generations by word of mouth. The council is the principal authority in the village, and its decisions are accepted by the inhabitants as binding on all members of the community.
