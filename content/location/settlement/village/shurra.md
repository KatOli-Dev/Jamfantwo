## Overview

Shurra is an oasis settlement deep in the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), the vast desert interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The settlement stands at the site of one of the largest and most reliable oases in the desert, where a spring fed by deep aquifers emerges at the base of a rocky outcrop and sustains a garden of date palms, figs, and the other crops that are cultivated in the terraced plots around the spring. Shurra is the most remote permanent settlement in Sahrana, and its people are among the most adapted to the demands of the desert environment. The settlement is small but enduring, and its oasis is a vital waypoint on the caravan routes that cross the Kharesh.

## The Oasis and the Water

The oasis of Shurra is the reason for the settlement, and the water that emerges from the spring is the most precious resource in the region. The spring is fed by the deep aquifers that lie beneath the desert, and the flow is constant and reliable, unaffected by the seasonal variations in rainfall that can diminish or destroy the smaller oases. The water is managed carefully, for it must sustain the settlement, the gardens, and the caravans that pass through, and the distribution of the water is governed by a system of rights and obligations that has been maintained over many centuries. The gardens that are cultivated around the spring produce the dates, the figs, and the other fruits that are the primary food source of the settlement and a valued provision for the caravans.

The rocky outcrop that rises above the oasis provides shelter from the wind and the sand, and the settlement is built into the sides of the outcrop, with the dwellings and the storehouses carved into the rock and connected by paths and stairs that are cut into the stone. The outcrop is a landmark that is visible for many miles across the flat desert, and it serves as a navigation point for the caravans that cross the Kharesh, for the oasis of Shurra is one of the few reliable water sources in the deep desert.

## The Caravans and the Trade

The caravan routes that pass through Shurra are the great trade routes of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and the caravans that stop at the oasis are the primary contact between the settlement and the wider world. The caravans bring the goods of the coast and the interior, and they exchange these for the water, the dates, and the other provisions that the oasis provides. The trade of Shurra is modest, for the settlement is small and its production is limited, but the services that it provides to the caravans, including water, food, rest, and shelter, are the foundation of its economy, and the tolls that are levied on the caravans are a significant source of income.

The [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) of the desert are the most frequent visitors to Shurra, and the relationship between the settlement and the nomadic salawa is one of mutual dependence, for the salawa provide the knowledge of the desert that the caravans require, and the settlement provides the water and the provisions that the salawa need. The salawa are welcomed at the oasis, and their presence is a familiar and a valued part of the life of the settlement.

## The Settlement

Shurra is a small, compact settlement, and its buildings are constructed of the stone of the outcrop and the sun-baked clay that is made from the desert earth, with thick walls and small windows that provide insulation against the heat and the wind. The dwellings are built into the sides of the outcrop, and the streets are steep and narrow, connected by stairs and paths that are cut into the rock. The population of Shurra is small and entirely [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the people are known for their endurance and their deep attachment to the oasis that is their home.

The settlement is governed by a council of elders that is drawn from the oldest families of the community, and the council meets in a chamber that is carved into the highest level of the outcrop, where the breeze is strongest and the view across the desert is unobstructed. The council oversees the distribution of the water, the management of the gardens, the collection of the tolls from the caravans, and the maintenance of the storehouses and the shelters that serve the desert traffic. The decisions of the council are made by consensus, and the governance of the settlement is based on the accumulated wisdom of the elders and the deep knowledge of the oasis and the desert that is the heritage of the community.

## Culture and Traditions

The people of Shurra have developed a culture that is shaped by the isolation and the demands of the desert, and their traditions are distinct from those of the coastal cities and the inland caravan towns. The most notable of these traditions is the practice of water stewardship, a code of conduct that governs the use of the water from the spring and the obligations of the community toward the caravans and the travellers who arrive at the oasis. The code is recorded in the oral traditions of the settlement, and it is taught to every child from the earliest age, for the survival of the settlement and the caravans depends upon the careful and the fair management of the water.

The people of Shurra are also noted for their skill in the preservation of food, for the dates and the figs that are produced in the gardens must be preserved for the long periods between the caravans, and the methods that have been developed at Shurra are among the most effective in the desert. The preserved fruits of Shurra are valued by the caravans as provisions for the long crossings of the deep desert, and they are traded to the coastal cities and the inland towns where they are regarded as a delicacy. The people of Shurra also produce a cloth that is woven from the fibres of the desert plants that grow around the oasis, and the cloth is noted for its durability and its resistance to the sun and the wind.

## Significance

Shurra is the most important oasis settlement in the deep [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and its water and its provisions are a vital resource for the caravans that cross the desert. The settlement is a dependency of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the alliance of the inland caravan cities, and its role in the caravan trade is a critical component of the economy of the desert interior. The significance of Shurra lies in its oasis, which is one of the few reliable water sources in the deep desert, and the settlement is a testament to the capacity of humans to endure and to thrive in the most demanding environment in the known world.
