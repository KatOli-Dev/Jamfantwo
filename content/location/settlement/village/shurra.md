## An Oasis Settlement Deep in the Kharesh

Shurra is an oasis settlement deep in the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), the vast desert interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The settlement stands at the site of one of the largest and most reliable oases in the desert, where a spring fed by deep aquifers emerges at the base of a rocky outcrop and sustains a garden of date palms, figs, and other crops cultivated in terraced plots around the spring. Shurra is the most remote permanent settlement in Sahrana, and its people are among the most adapted to the demands of the desert environment. The settlement is small but enduring, and its oasis is a vital waypoint on the caravan routes crossing the Kharesh.

## The Spring, the Gardens, and the Rocky Outcrop

The oasis of Shurra is the reason for the settlement; the water emerging from the spring is the most precious resource in the region. The spring is fed by deep aquifers beneath the desert; the flow is constant and reliable, unaffected by seasonal variations in rainfall that can diminish or destroy smaller oases. The water is managed carefully, for it must sustain the settlement, the gardens, and the caravans passing through; the distribution of water is governed by a system of rights and obligations maintained over many centuries. The gardens cultivated around the spring produce dates, figs, and other fruits that are the primary food source of the settlement and a valued provision for the caravans.

The rocky outcrop rising above the oasis provides shelter from wind and sand. The settlement is built into the sides of the outcrop, with dwellings and storehouses carved into the rock and connected by paths and stairs cut into the stone. The outcrop is a landmark visible for many miles across the flat desert, serving as a navigation point for caravans crossing the Kharesh; the oasis of Shurra is one of the few reliable water sources in the deep desert.

## The Caravan Routes and the Salawa Guides

The caravan routes passing through Shurra are the great trade routes of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). The caravans stopping at the oasis are the primary contact between the settlement and the wider world. The caravans bring goods of the coast and the interior, exchanging them for water, dates, and other provisions the oasis provides. The trade of Shurra is modest, for the settlement is small and production limited, but the services it provides to the caravans, including water, food, rest, and shelter, are the foundation of its economy; the tolls levied on the caravans are a significant source of income.

The [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) of the desert are the most frequent visitors to Shurra. The relationship between the settlement and the nomadic salawa is one of mutual dependence; the salawa provide the knowledge of the desert that the caravans require, and the settlement provides the water and provisions the salawa need. The salawa are welcomed at the oasis; their presence is a familiar and valued part of the life of the settlement.

## Dwellings Carved into the Outcrop

Shurra is a small, compact settlement whose buildings are constructed of the stone of the outcrop and sun-baked clay made from desert earth, with thick walls and small windows providing insulation against heat and wind. The dwellings are built into the sides of the outcrop; the streets are steep and narrow, connected by stairs and paths cut into the rock. The population is small and entirely [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the people are known for their endurance and deep attachment to the oasis that is their home.

The settlement is governed by a council of elders drawn from the oldest families of the community. The council meets in a chamber carved into the highest level of the outcrop, where the breeze is strongest and the view across the desert is unobstructed. The council oversees the distribution of water, the management of the gardens, the collection of tolls from the caravans, and the maintenance of the storehouses and shelters serving the desert traffic. The decisions of the council are made by consensus; the governance of the settlement is based on the accumulated wisdom of the elders and the deep knowledge of the oasis and the desert that is the heritage of the community.

## Water Stewardship and the Preserved Fruits

The people of Shurra have developed a culture shaped by the isolation and demands of the desert; their traditions are distinct from those of the coastal cities and inland caravan towns. The most notable tradition is the practice of water stewardship, a code of conduct governing the use of water from the spring and the obligations of the community toward the caravans and travellers arriving at the oasis. The code is recorded in the oral traditions of the settlement and taught to every child from the earliest age, for the survival of the settlement and the caravans depends upon the careful and fair management of water.

The people of Shurra are noted for their skill in the preservation of food. The dates and figs produced in the gardens must be preserved for the long periods between caravans; the methods developed at Shurra are among the most effective in the desert. The preserved fruits are valued by the caravans as provisions for long crossings of the deep desert and are traded to coastal cities and inland towns where they are regarded as a delicacy. The people also produce a cloth woven from fibres of desert plants growing around the oasis, noted for its durability and resistance to sun and wind.

## The Most Important Oasis in the Deep Kharesh

Shurra is the most important oasis settlement in the deep [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and its water and provisions are a vital resource for the caravans crossing the desert. The settlement is a dependency of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the alliance of the inland caravan cities, and its role in the caravan trade is a critical component of the economy of the desert interior. The significance of Shurra lies in its oasis, which is one of the few reliable water sources in the deep desert; the settlement is a testament to the capacity of humans to endure and thrive in the most demanding environment in the known world.
