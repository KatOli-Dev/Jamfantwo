## Overview

Dustford is a southern river town of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), situated at the edge of the grasslands where the cultivated land gives way to the warm, dry country of the far south. The town stands at a crossing of the river that marks the boundary between the fertile plains and the arid margins, and its position at the edge of the grasslands has made it a frontier settlement and a gateway to the warm plains beyond. Dustford is smaller and less prosperous than the cities of the central grasslands, but it is a thriving and self-sufficient community, and its trade connects the grasslands to the regions that lie beyond.

## The River and the Frontier

The river that flows past Dustford is the same that waters the southern plains, but at this point the current is slower and the banks are lower, and the water is warm and turbid with the silt of the dry country upstream. The crossing at Dustford is the last reliable ford on the river before it enters the arid margins, and the bridge that spans the water is a modest structure that is maintained by the town for the convenience of the traffic that crosses between the grasslands and the dry country.

The frontier that Dustford marks is not a political boundary but a geographical one, for the cultivated land of the southern plains ends and the warm, dry country begins at the river crossing. The traffic that passes through the town includes the grain and the livestock of the grasslands, which are exported to the dry country, and the exotic products of the warm regions, which are imported to the grasslands and the markets of the north. The caravans that cross the dry country are provisioned at Dustford, and the town is the last place where water and supplies may be obtained before the journey into the arid lands, a service that is a significant part of the local economy.

The river at Dustford is also a source of fish, and the fishermen of the town maintain a small fleet of boats that work the deeper pools and the slower reaches of the waterway. The fish are dried and salted in the curing houses that line the riverbank, and the dried fish are a minor export that finds a market among the caravans and the settlements of the dry country, where fresh fish is scarce.

## Trade and the Town

The trade of Dustford is a mixture of the agricultural products of the grasslands and the goods that are imported from the warm regions beyond the frontier. The town is a centre for the livestock trade, and the stockyards that line the riverbank are the gathering point for the cattle and the sheep that are driven from the grasslands to the markets of the dry country. The wool and the leather that are produced in the surrounding region are also traded at Dustford, and the town is known for the quality of its leather, which is tanned using the bark of the trees that grow along the river. The tanneries of Dustford are a valued industry, and the leather that is produced there is durable and supple, prized for the saddles, the harnesses, and the boots that are made from it by the craftsmen of the town.

The inns of Dustford are a significant part of the local economy, for the caravans that pass through the town require lodging, provisions, and the care of their animals. The innkeepers of Dustford are accustomed to the needs of the travellers who come from the grasslands and the dry country, and the inns are equipped with the stables, the storerooms, and the kitchens that are required to serve the traffic of the frontier. The innkeepers are also a source of news and information, for the caravans that pass through bring the tidings of the regions they have travelled, and the innkeepers of Dustford are among the best-informed people in the southern plains.

Dustford is a small, rugged town, and its buildings are constructed of the light-coloured stone and the sun-baked clay that are the materials of the dry country, with thick walls and small windows that provide insulation against the heat. The streets are broad and dusty, for the town is at the edge of the grasslands and the dust of the dry country is a constant presence. The population of Dustford is chiefly [human](/content/humans), with a small community of [centaurs](/content/centaurs) who are involved in the livestock trade. The centaur kindreds of the southern plains are the most frequent visitors to the town, and the stockyards and the market are the meeting point where the settled and the nomadic peoples conduct their business.

## Significance

Dustford is the primary gateway between the southern grasslands and the warm, dry country beyond, and its trade connects the agricultural economy of the plains to the regions that lie to the south. The town is the southernmost settlement of the [Plains Confederation](/content/plains-confederation), and its position at the edge of the grasslands gives it a commercial significance that belies its modest size. The prosperity of Dustford is built upon the trade that passes through the town, and the people of Dustford are proud of their role as the frontier community of the southern plains.

## History

Dustford was founded as a crossing point on the river, and the ford that gave the town its name was the original reason for the settlement. The first inhabitants were the herdsmen who drove their livestock across the river on the way to the grazing lands of the dry country, and the ford was the last reliable crossing before the river entered the arid margins. The bridge was built in a later age, when the trade between the grasslands and the dry country had grown sufficiently to justify the expense, and the bridge transformed the crossing from a seasonal passage to a year-round route.

The town grew slowly, for the edge of the grasslands is a marginal land, and the population has always been small. The people of Dustford have endured the hardships of the frontier with a quiet determination, and the town has prospered through the trade that passes through its streets. The relationship between the town and the centaur kindreds of the plains has been a defining feature of the history of Dustford, and the mutual benefit of the livestock trade has fostered a degree of co-operation that has sustained the community through the lean years and the dry seasons that are a part of life at the edge of the grasslands.
