## Overview

The known world comprises three major landmasses and two great oceans, together with a number of smaller seas and straits that shape the currents of travel and trade. The configuration of these features has influenced the spread of peoples, the rise of kingdoms, and the patterns of climate that define each region. Though much of the world remains uncharted, the broad outlines of its geography are well established.

## Continents

The three continents are separated by deep waters, yet each has served at various times as a bridge or barrier to the movement of civilisations. Their coastlines range from gentle shelving shores to dramatic cliffs that stand above the open sea.

### Aethelar

Aethelar is the largest of the known continents and occupies a central position in the world. It stretches from temperate latitudes in the north to sun-warmed plains in the south, encompassing a greater variety of climates than any other landmass. Its eastern coastline faces the Auric Ocean, while its western shores are washed by the Greyring Ocean. Inland, Aethelar is divided by a mountain range that runs from the northwest to the southeast, forming a natural watershed between the two coasts.

The continent's size and central location have made it the cradle of several major kingdoms and a meeting point of diverse peoples. The northern reaches are dominated by dense forests and fertile river valleys, where early settlements took root and grew into prosperous realms. Further south, the land opens into rolling grasslands and eventually gives way to warm plains, where nomadic peoples have historically grazed their herds and traded with the settled towns. The mountain spine of Aethelar not only divides its watersheds but also serves as a cultural crossroads, where peoples of the eastern and western coasts meet and exchange traditions.

### Morvind

Morvind lies to the north of Aethelar across the Stormfall Sea. It is a rugged and mountainous continent, its interior dominated by ranges that hold their snow well into the spring. The coastal lowlands are narrow but support fishing and trading settlements that have endured for centuries, their harbours sheltered by the same cliffs that protect them from the open ocean. Morvind's mineral wealth has long drawn prospectors and traders, and the continent's reputation for fine metalwork is recognised throughout the known world.

The interior ranges form a labyrinth of valleys and high passes, many of which become quiet during the winter months but reopen with the thaw. Between the peaks lie sheltered plateaus where close-knit communities tend flocks of mountain sheep and cultivate cold-resistant crops during the bright growing season. The rivers that descend from Morvind's heights are swift and clear, fed by glacial meltwater, and they carve scenic gorges through the rock before reaching the sea. These waterways have historically provided the most reliable routes inland, and settlements along their banks remain the most populous in the continent, their people known for hospitality to travellers.

### Sahrana

Sahrana is the southernmost continent, lying across the Auric Ocean from Aethelar. Much of its interior is covered by vast desert, punctuated by rocky uplands and seasonal river systems. The northern coast, washed by warm currents, supports several fertile regions that have given rise to prosperous and cultured city-states. Sahrana's distance from the northern continents has allowed distinct traditions to flourish, and its trade routes for rare spices and dyes are among the most valuable in the known world.

The desert interior, known to its inhabitants as the Anvil, is a place of striking beauty and quiet grandeur. Vast dune fields shift with the seasonal winds, reshaping the landscape and occasionally revealing the remains of old caravan stops beneath the sand. The desert is far from empty. Oases scattered along ancient fault lines sustain small communities, and nomadic peoples traverse the region following routes passed down through generations of oral tradition. The rocky uplands that rise from the sands provide shelter, water, and minerals, and several of these outcrops have been settled for centuries, their terraced gardens a testament to patient cultivation.

## Oceans

Two great oceans cover the majority of the world's surface. Their currents, winds, and seasonal weather govern the patterns of long-distance travel and have shaped the fortunes of seafaring peoples.

### The Auric Ocean

The Auric Ocean lies to the east of Aethelar and separates it from Sahrana. It is the warmer of the two oceans, its equatorial currents giving rise to spirited storms during the summer months. Despite these seasonal challenges, the Auric Ocean carries the heaviest maritime traffic in the known world, as merchant vessels ply the routes between Aethelar and Sahrana. The ocean takes its name from the golden sunsets visible along its western shores, a phenomenon well recorded in the chronicles of coastal peoples.

The prevailing currents of the Auric flow from south to north along the eastern coast of Aethelar, carrying warm water into the temperate latitudes and giving the coastal regions a mild and agreeable climate. These same currents make the return voyage from Sahrana a relatively swift passage, and the calmer months outside the storm season are favoured by merchants and pilgrims alike. Mariners who sail the Auric have developed sophisticated methods for reading the weather, observing the colour of the water and the behaviour of seabirds to anticipate changing conditions.

### The Greyring Ocean

The Greyring Ocean lies to the west of Aethelar and to the south of Morvind. It is the cooler of the two oceans, with steady currents that flow from the polar south toward the equator. Few established trade routes cross the Greyring, and much of its expanse remains unexplored. Sailors who have ventured into its waters speak of long rolling swells and banks of mist that lend the ocean an ethereal quality.

The waters of the Greyring are rich with marine life, and the coastal peoples of western Aethelar have long harvested its cold waters for fish and whales. These fisheries sustain several thriving port towns whose harbours nestle among the cliffs of the western coast. Beyond the continental shelf, the ocean floor drops to extraordinary depths, and the few soundings that have been attempted suggest trenches as vast as the mountains of Morvind are tall. The peoples of the western coast regard the Greyring with a mixture of respect and wonder, and many a legend has been woven around its mists and its depths.

## Notable Seas

Several smaller bodies of water lie between and around the continents, serving as important conduits for trade, travel, and cultural exchange.

### The Stormfall Sea

The Stormfall Sea separates Morvind from Aethelar. It is named for the spirited weather systems that move across it, particularly during the autumn and winter months. Despite its reputation, the Stormfall Sea is a vital passage for the movement of goods and people between the two continents, and its crossing has been mastered by generations of skilled northern mariners.

The sea is relatively shallow compared to the great oceans, and its floor is marked by rocky shoals and submerged ridges that keep pilots on their guard. Experienced sailors from both coasts have charted safe passages and passed this knowledge from one generation to the next. During the calmer months of late spring and early summer, the Stormfall takes on a stillness that belies its name, and the crossing can be made in fair weather under clear skies. These calm seasons are when the majority of trade between the northern and central continents takes place, and the ports on either side fill with merchants and travellers.

### The Strait of Velden

The Strait of Velden is a narrow channel that connects the Auric Ocean to the Stormfall Sea, passing between the southeastern coast of Morvind and the northeastern tip of Aethelar. The strait has long been a point of strategic importance, as any vessel travelling between the two oceans must pass through these waters.

At its narrowest point the strait contracts to less than two miles, where the meeting of two bodies of water creates lively and unpredictable currents. Watchtowers and harbours have been built along both shores over the centuries, and the settlements on either side have grown wealthy from the steady flow of trade through the channel. Treaties governing passage through the strait have been negotiated and renewed many times, and the powers that border it have generally found it more profitable to cooperate than to quarrel. The strait remains one of the most travelled and cosmopolitan stretches of water in the known world, where languages and goods from three continents meet.
