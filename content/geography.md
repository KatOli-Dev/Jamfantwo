## Overview

The known world comprises three major landmasses and two great oceans, together with a number of smaller seas and straits that shape the currents of travel and trade. The configuration of these features has influenced the spread of peoples, the rise of kingdoms, and the patterns of climate that define each region. Though much of the world remains uncharted, the broad outlines of its geography are well established.

## Continents

The three continents are separated by deep waters, yet each has served at various times as a bridge or barrier to the movement of civilisations. Their coastlines range from gentle shelving shores to dramatic cliffs that stand above the open sea.

### Aethelar

[Aethelar](/content/aethelar) is the largest of the known continents and occupies a central position in the world. It stretches from temperate latitudes in the north to sun-warmed plains in the south, encompassing a greater variety of climates than any other landmass. Its eastern coastline faces the [Auric Ocean](/content/auric-ocean), while its western shores are washed by the [Greyring Ocean](/content/greyring-ocean). Inland, Aethelar is divided by a mountain range that runs from the northwest to the southeast, forming a natural watershed between the two coasts. The continent's size and central location have made it the cradle of several major kingdoms and a meeting point of diverse peoples, and its history is the most extensively documented of any land in the known world.

### Morvind

[Morvind](/content/morvind) lies to the north of Aethelar across the [Stormfall Sea](/content/stormfall-sea). It is a rugged and mountainous continent, its interior dominated by ranges that hold their snow well into the spring. The coastal lowlands are narrow but support fishing and trading settlements that have endured for centuries, their harbours sheltered by the same cliffs that protect them from the open ocean. Morvind's mineral wealth has long drawn prospectors and traders, and the continent's reputation for fine metalwork is recognised throughout the known world. The continent holds the greatest concentration of dwarven population in the known world, and its ores and crafted goods are the foundation of its prosperity.

### Sahrana

[Sahrana](/content/sahrana) is the southernmost continent, lying across the [Auric Ocean](/content/auric-ocean) from Aethelar. Much of its interior is covered by vast desert, punctuated by rocky uplands and seasonal river systems. The northern coast, washed by warm currents, supports several fertile regions that have given rise to prosperous and cultured city-states. Sahrana's distance from the northern continents has allowed distinct traditions to flourish, and its trade routes for rare spices and dyes are among the most valuable in the known world. The desert interior, known as the [Kharesh](/content/kharesh), is a place of striking beauty and quiet grandeur, crossed by ancient caravan routes and inhabited by nomadic and settled peoples alike.

## Oceans

Two great oceans cover the majority of the world's surface. Their currents, winds, and seasonal weather govern the patterns of long-distance travel and have shaped the fortunes of seafaring peoples.

### The Auric Ocean

The [Auric Ocean](/content/auric-ocean) lies to the east of Aethelar and separates it from Sahrana. It is the warmer of the two oceans, its equatorial currents giving rise to spirited storms during the summer months. Despite these seasonal challenges, the Auric Ocean carries the heaviest maritime traffic in the known world, as merchant vessels ply the routes between Aethelar and Sahrana. The ocean takes its name from the golden sunsets visible along its western shores, a phenomenon well recorded in the chronicles of coastal peoples, and its trade routes are the lifeblood of commerce between the northern and southern continents.

### The Greyring Ocean

The [Greyring Ocean](/content/greyring-ocean) lies to the west of Aethelar and to the south of Morvind. It is the cooler of the two oceans, with steady currents that flow from the polar south toward the equator. Few established trade routes cross the Greyring, and much of its expanse remains unexplored. Sailors who have ventured into its waters speak of long rolling swells and banks of mist that lend the ocean an ethereal quality. The waters of the Greyring are rich with marine life, sustaining the fishing and whaling communities of the western coast, but its depths are vast and poorly charted, and the peoples who border it regard the ocean with a mixture of respect and wonder.

## Notable Seas

Several smaller bodies of water lie between and around the continents, serving as important conduits for trade, travel, and cultural exchange.

### The Stormfall Sea

The [Stormfall Sea](/content/stormfall-sea) separates Morvind from Aethelar. It is named for the spirited weather systems that move across it, particularly during the autumn and winter months. Despite its reputation, the Stormfall Sea is a vital passage for the movement of goods and people between the two continents, and its crossing has been mastered by generations of skilled northern mariners. The sea is relatively shallow, and its floor is marked by rocky shoals and submerged ridges, but during the calmer months of late spring and early summer it takes on a stillness that belies its name, and the ports on either side fill with merchants and travellers.

### The Strait of Velden

The [Strait of Velden](/content/strait-of-velden) is a narrow channel that connects the [Auric Ocean](/content/auric-ocean) to the [Stormfall Sea](/content/stormfall-sea), passing between the southeastern coast of Morvind and the northeastern tip of Aethelar. The strait has long been a point of strategic importance, as any vessel travelling between the two oceans must pass through these waters. At its narrowest point it contracts to less than two miles, where the meeting of two bodies of water creates lively and unpredictable currents. Watchtowers and harbours have been built along both shores over the centuries, and the settlements on either side have grown wealthy from the steady flow of trade through the channel. Treaties governing passage have been negotiated and renewed many times, and the strait remains one of the most travelled and cosmopolitan stretches of water in the known world.
