## Overview

Halen, Magister of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) is the present chief scholar of the academy of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), and the foremost authority of the present age upon the [Tide-Reading](/content/belief/science/physical/tide-reading) and the [Spherical Cartography](/content/belief/science/theoretical/spherical-cartography). He was elected to the office of magister by the fellows of the academy in the year that the chroniclers of the republic record as the nine hundred and nineteenth year of the republic, succeeding the previous magister, who had held the office for the customary term of seven years. Halen is in the sixty-third year of his life, and his tenure of the office has been marked by a substantial expansion of the academy's programme of observation and by the publication of several treatises that have advanced the understanding of the seas and the heavens.

## Early Life and Education

Halen was born in the city of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), the son of a merchant of the spice trade and a mother who was herself the daughter of a scholar of the academy. The early education of Halen was conducted in the schools of the city, where he showed a precocious aptitude for the mathematical disciplines and the observation of the natural world. The young Halen was admitted to the academy of the republic at the age of seventeen, and he pursued the course of study that leads to the fellowship, including instruction in the [Trade Tongue](/content/language/pseudo/trade-tongue), the law of the republic, and the natural sciences that are the principal concern of the academy.

The young scholar attracted the attention of the magister of the day by the acuity of his observations upon the behaviour of the currents that flow along the coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and he was appointed to the staff of the academy at the age of twenty-five. The first publications of Halen were concerned with the measurement of the position of the sun at the solstice, a matter that had been the subject of considerable debate among the scholars of the academy, and the methods that he proposed were rapidly adopted by the mariners of the republic. The appointment of Halen to the fellowship of the academy followed at the age of thirty-two, and he was thereafter engaged in the work of the academy without interruption until his elevation to the magisterium.

## The Work of the Magister

The work of Halen has been concerned principally with the extension of the academy's programme of observation of the seas and the heavens, and with the publication of the treatises that record the conclusions of the programme. The magister has directed the establishment of new stations of observation upon the coast of Sahrana and in the harbours of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), and the records that are now being maintained at these stations are among the most comprehensive in the known world. The magister has also directed the compilation of the tables of the position of the sun and the principal stars, and the tables that have been published under his direction are the standard reference of the navigators of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean).

The most substantial of the publications of the magister is the treatise upon the currents of the warm waters of the eastern ocean, a work that has been the standard reference of the mariners of the republic for the past decade. The treatise sets forth the principal currents of the Auric and the rules by which the navigator may predict the set of the water at any season of the year, and the work is regarded by the scholars of the academy as the most significant contribution to the study of the waters that has been made in the present century. The magister has also published a commentary upon the [Spherical Cartography](/content/belief/science/theoretical/spherical-cartography), in which he proposes several refinements to the methods of the measurement of position that are commonly employed by the navigators of the great ports.

## The Academy and the Republic

The academy of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) is the foremost institution of learning in the known world, and the magister is the chief officer of the academy, elected by the fellows for a term of seven years. The academy is housed in a complex of buildings that adjoin the harbour of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), and the scholars of the academy have at their disposal a library of considerable extent, a collection of instruments that is unequalled elsewhere in the world, and the records of the observations that have been conducted under the direction of the academy's officers for more than nine centuries. The academy is funded by a grant from the republic, and the scholars are supported in their work by the stipends that the grant provides.

The relation between the academy and the government of the republic is one of considerable independence, for the academy is a self-governing institution and the magister is responsible to the fellows alone. The republic has long recognised that the work of the academy is of value to the commerce of the state, and the grant that funds the academy is among the most generous of the public expenditures of the republic. The magister is consulted upon matters of public policy that touch upon the sciences, and the counsel of the magister is sought by the magistrates of the republic upon the questions of navigation, of measurement, and of the regulation of the maritime trade that are among the principal concerns of the government.

## Reputation and Influence

The reputation of Halen extends throughout the known world, and the scholars of the other academies of the continent have corresponded with him upon the matters that are the subject of his work. The navigator guilds of the harbours of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) and the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) have adopted the tables of the magister as the standard of their practice, and the navigators who are trained in the methods that the magister has published are among the most accomplished of the present age. The reputation of the magister has brought honour to the academy and to the republic, and the city of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) is regarded throughout the world as the foremost seat of the physical sciences.

The influence of the magister is exercised not only through the publications of the academy but also through the scholars who have been trained under his direction. The students who have passed through the academy during the magister's tenure now occupy positions of importance in the academies of the other nations, and the methods of the magister are being disseminated throughout the world by the scholars who have carried them to the courts of the foreign princes. The chronicler notes that the work of the magister is the continuation of a tradition that reaches back to the foundation of the academy, and the contributions that the magister has made to the sciences are among the most substantial of any scholar of the present age.

## Personal Character

Halen is described by the fellows of the academy as a scholar of considerable personal modesty and of great industry. The magister is known for the regularity of his attendance at the academy, and the scholars of the staff are accustomed to find him at his desk in the library at the earliest hours of the morning. The magister is accessible to the students of the academy, and he is known to spend several hours each week in the instruction of the younger scholars who are preparing for the fellowship. The magister is unmarried, and his life is given over to the work of the academy, a circumstance that the fellows regard as a measure of the dedication that the office demands.

The magister is also known for the courtesy with which he receives the scholars who visit the academy from the foreign academies, and the visitors who have been received at [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) speak well of the welcome that they have received. The magister maintains a correspondence with the chief scholars of the other nations, and the letters that are exchanged between the magister and his foreign counterparts are preserved in the library of the academy as a record of the intellectual life of the present age. The chronicler notes that the magister is held in high esteem by his contemporaries, and the reputation that he has acquired is a measure of the value that the world sets upon the work of the scholar.

## Significance

Halen is the present head of the foremost academy of the known world and the leading scholar of the present age in the physical sciences. His tenure of the magisterium has been marked by a substantial extension of the academy's work and by the publication of treatises that have advanced the understanding of the seas and the heavens. The influence of the magister is felt throughout the world, and the navigators of the great ports are guided by the tables that have been compiled under his direction. The chronicler notes that the work of the magister is a continuation of the long tradition of the academy, and the contributions that he has made are a foundation upon which the scholars of the succeeding generations will build.
