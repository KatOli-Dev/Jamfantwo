## Overview

Brona, Guildmaster of [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch) is the present chief of the pilot guild of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), the brotherhood of mariners who guide the larger vessels through the narrow and the treacherous waters of the channel. She was elected to the office of guildmaster by the fellows of the guild in the year that the chroniclers of the strait towns record as the four hundred and seventeenth year of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), succeeding her predecessor, who had held the office for the customary term of five years. Brona is in the forty-ninth year of her life, and her tenure of the office has been marked by a tightening of the standards of the guild and by the acquisition of two new pilot vessels that have extended the reach of the guild's services.

## Background and Training

Brona was born in the town of [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch), the daughter of a pilot of the strait and a midwife who served the town and the neighbouring villages. The early years of Brona were spent upon the water, for her father took her upon the pilot boat from the time that she was old enough to be of use, and she learned the waters of the strait by the direct instruction of an experienced pilot. The young Brona showed a precocious aptitude for the reading of the currents and the winds, and her father was accustomed to say that she knew the strait better than any pilot of her age.

The training of a pilot of the strait is conducted by apprenticeship, and the young Brona was bound to her father's boat at the age of fourteen, the customary age at which the apprentices of the guild begin their service. The apprenticeship lasts for a period of seven years, during which the apprentice learns the channels and the shoals of the strait, the reading of the water and the sky, and the customs of the pilots who serve the foreign vessels that pass through the channel. Brona completed her apprenticeship at the age of twenty-one, and she was admitted to the fellowship of the guild after a probationary year of service upon the pilot boats. The young pilot served for a further period of fifteen years upon the boats before she was elected to the council of the guild, and she served upon the council for a further period of eight years before her elevation to the guildmastership.

## The Office of the Guildmaster

The office of the guildmaster is the chief magistracy of the pilot guild of the strait, and the guildmaster is responsible for the regulation of the pilots, the maintenance of the pilot boats, and the negotiation of the terms of the pilot service with the merchants who use the strait. The guildmaster is elected by the fellows of the guild for a term of five years, and the guildmaster may be re-elected for a further term upon the conclusion of the first. The election is conducted by the council of the guild, which is composed of the senior pilots and the representatives of the towns that are associated with the guild.

The authority of the guildmaster is considerable, for the pilot service is essential to the navigation of the strait, and the guildmaster's decisions upon the conduct of the pilots and the assignment of the boats are binding upon the fellowship. The guildmaster is also the principal officer of the guild in its dealings with the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), the council of the strait towns that governs the affairs of the channel, and the guildmaster represents the guild at the sessions of the compact. The guildmaster is in regular communication with the harbormasters of the principal ports of the strait, and the consultations that the guildmaster holds with these officers are among the most important of the occasions upon which the policy of the guild is determined.

## The Pilot Service

The pilot service of the strait is conducted by the guild from the towns that line the channel, and the principal stations of the guild are at [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch), at [Caerhaven](/content/location/settlement/town/caerhaven), and at the smaller harbours that are situated at the strategic points of the passage. The pilot boats are small vessels of sturdy construction that are designed to operate in the heavy weather of the strait, and each boat carries a pilot of seniority and a crew of three or four oarsmen. The pilots put out from the stations when a vessel is reported approaching the channel, and the pilot boards the vessel at the appropriate point of the strait to guide her through the passage.

The fee for the pilot service is fixed by the guild and is approved by the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), and the fee is the same for all vessels of comparable size, regardless of the flag that the vessel flies. The fee is paid to the guild, and the proceeds are distributed among the pilots and the boats according to a schedule that has been established by long custom. The guildmaster is responsible for the maintenance of the boats and the payment of the crews, and the accounts of the guild are audited annually by the council of the compact.

## Standards and Discipline

The standards of the guild are maintained by the discipline that the guildmaster enforces upon the pilots, and the guildmaster has the authority to suspend a pilot from the service for a breach of the regulations or for a failure of skill. The standards of the guild are high, and the pilots who are admitted to the fellowship are required to demonstrate a thorough knowledge of the channels and the shoals, a reliable judgment of the weather, and a steady nerve in the conduct of the vessel. The guildmaster conducts the examinations of the apprentices and the probationers, and the success rate of the candidates is the measure of the rigour with which the guildmaster maintains the standards.

The most serious of the disciplinary matters that come before the guildmaster are the cases of pilots who have been involved in the loss of a vessel, and the guildmaster is required to investigate such cases and to determine whether the pilot was at fault. The finding of the guildmaster may be appealed to the council of the guild, and the council may confirm or reverse the decision of the guildmaster. The guildmaster is also responsible for the maintenance of the records of the pilots' service, and the records that are kept in the hall of the guild at [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch) are among the most complete in the known world.

## Character and Reputation

Brona is described by the pilots of the strait as a leader of considerable energy and of firm but equitable administration. The guildmaster is known for the close attention that she pays to the conduct of the pilot service, and the pilots are accustomed to see her upon the boats when the weather is at its worst, for the guildmaster insists upon observing the work of the pilots in conditions that would deter a less committed officer. The guildmaster is also known for the courtesy with which she receives the foreign merchants who come to the guild hall to engage the pilots, and the foreign visitors speak well of the welcome that they receive.

The reputation of the guildmaster is not confined to the strait towns, for the guildmaster has corresponded with the harbormasters of the great ports of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea), and the reputation of the guild of [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch) is a credit to the strait and to the compact. The chronicler notes that the guildmaster is held in high esteem by the pilots and by the merchants alike, and the standards that the guildmaster has maintained during her tenure of the office are among the most rigorous that the guild has known.

## Significance

Brona is the present chief of the pilot guild of the strait, and the standards that she maintains are the standards by which the navigation of one of the busiest waterways of the known world is conducted. The pilot service of the strait is essential to the commerce of the three continents, and the guildmaster's stewardship of the service is a contribution to the prosperity of the strait towns and to the trade that flows through the channel. The chronicler notes that the guildmaster is a worthy successor to the long line of pilots who have guided the vessels of the strait, and the office that she holds is one of the most important of the public positions of the strait towns.
