## The Pilot of the River

Lian Greywash is a river pilot of the Greywash, the great watercourse that flows from the mountains of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea). He is one of the most experienced pilots on the river, and his knowledge of the currents, the shallows, and the hazards of the Greywash is unmatched among the pilots of the present age.

Lian was born in a settlement on the bank of the lower Greywash, in the region where the river widens and slows before it meets the sea. The family into which he was born had been pilots of the river for several generations, and the knowledge of the waterway had been passed down through the family since the time of the first settlements on the river.

The education that Lian received was the practical training of a river pilot. He learned to read the currents and the depths, to navigate the channels that changed with the seasons, and to handle the vessels that plied the river. He began his training as a child, accompanying his father on the voyages that carried goods between the ports of the lower river and the towns of the interior.

## The River and Its Challenges

The Greywash is the largest river of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) coast, and its course presents challenges that the pilots of the river must master. The upper reaches of the river are swift and narrow, flowing through the gorges that the water has cut through the rock of the mountains. The middle reaches are broader but are marked by shallows and sandbars that shift with the seasons. The lower reaches are wide and slow, but the estuary is complicated by the tides and the currents of the sea.

The navigation of the river requires knowledge of the conditions at every season of the year. The spring floods bring high water and strong currents, the summer brings low water and the exposure of the sandbars, and the autumn and winter bring the storms that can make the estuary treacherous. The pilot must know the river in all its moods, and the knowledge that Lian has accumulated through decades of experience allows him to navigate the river under any conditions.

The vessels that ply the Greywash are varied in design. The boats of the upper river are small and manoeuvrable, designed to navigate the narrow channels and the swift currents of the gorges. The barges of the middle river are larger and broader, designed to carry the heavy loads of grain and metal that are the principal cargoes of the river trade. The vessels of the lower river are larger still, capable of carrying the cargoes that are transferred to the ocean-going ships that visit the estuary. Lian is qualified to pilot vessels of all types, and he is in demand from the merchants who require his services.

## The Career of the Pilot

The career of Lian Greywash has spanned more than four decades, and he has piloted vessels on every section of the river. He began as an apprentice on the barges of the middle river, learning the channels and the hazards of the most heavily travelled section of the waterway. He advanced to the position of pilot on the barges, and he eventually qualified to pilot the larger vessels that navigate the lower river and the estuary.

The reputation that Lian established was based on his skill in handling vessels in difficult conditions and his knowledge of the river's hazards. He was known for his ability to navigate the river in conditions that other pilots would avoid, and his record of safe voyages was among the best in the guild.

The guild of the river pilots is the organisation that regulates the profession, establishing the standards for the qualification of pilots and the rules that govern the navigation of the river. Lian served on the council of the guild for many years, contributing to the development of the standards that the guild enforces.

The knowledge includes the locations of the channels at different seasons, the behaviour of the currents at different water levels, and the hazards that are hidden beneath the surface of the water.

The channels of the river shift with the seasons and the years, as the currents deposit and remove the sediments that shape the riverbed. The pilot must know the location of the channels at the time of each voyage, and the knowledge must be updated constantly through direct observation. Lian has maintained a personal chart of the river, marked with the observations he has made on each voyage, and the chart is the most detailed record of the river that exists.

The currents of the river are complicated by the shape of the riverbed, the volume of water flowing at different seasons, and the influence of the tides in the lower reaches. Lian's knowledge of the currents allows him to predict the behaviour of the vessel in different conditions and to choose the courses that make the best use of the flow of the water.

The hazards of the river include rocks that are exposed at low water, sandbars that shift with the currents, and the wrecks of vessels that have sunk in the channel. Lian's knowledge of the hazards is based on direct observation and the reports of other pilots, and he updates his knowledge constantly through the exchange of information within the guild.
