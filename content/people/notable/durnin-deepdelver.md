## The Explorer of the Depths

Durnin Deepdelver is a dwarven explorer of the deep caves that lie beneath the mountains of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), a figure whose systematic exploration of the subterranean world has contributed more to the knowledge of the deep places than any other living explorer. He is a master of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), and his expeditions have pushed the boundaries of the known underground world further than any previous generation of dwarven explorers dared to venture.

Durnin was born in the city of [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar), the metalworking capital of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), into a family of miners who had worked the deep veins for many generations. The family had a tradition of venturing further into the earth than the cautious practices of the mining guilds advised, and the stories of the deep caves that Durnin heard in childhood kindled in him a fascination with the unknown passages that lay beyond the worked mines.

The education that Durnin received was the standard training of a dwarven miner, including instruction in the properties of different types of rock, the management of underground water, and the techniques of excavation and support that were necessary for safe mining. He excelled in the study of the rock and the behaviour of the earth, and his instructors noted that his understanding of the structure of the underground world was exceptional.

## The First Expeditions

The first expeditions that Durnin led were into the deeper levels of mines that had been abandoned by the mining companies of [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar). The mines had been worked to the limit of the technology available at the time, and the passages that extended beyond the worked areas had been sealed to prevent accidents. Durnin, who had obtained permission from the mining guild to explore the sealed passages, led small parties of volunteers into the abandoned workings.

The conditions in the abandoned mines were hazardous. The passages were unstable, the air was stagnant, and the darkness was absolute. Durnin and his party were equipped with the lamps, ropes, and tools that were standard for underground work, but the exploration of passages that had not been maintained for decades required a careful and methodical approach.

The discoveries that Durnin made in the abandoned mines were significant. He found deposits of minerals that the earlier miners had not recognised, including veins of rare metals that were valuable for the smiths of Khazgar. He also found evidence of the fauna that inhabited the deep caves, including species that had not been described by any previous explorer. The reports that he submitted to the mining guild described the passages he had explored, the stone of the deeper levels, and the potential for the extension of mining operations.

## The Deep Caves of the Eastern Ranges

The most ambitious of Durnin's expeditions was the exploration of the deep caves of the eastern ranges of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), a region that had not been systematically explored by any previous expedition. The caves of the eastern ranges were known to the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), who avoided them because of the dangers they contained, but the stories that the clans told of vast underground passages and strange creatures of the depths intrigued Durnin.

The expedition to the eastern caves was organised over a period of two years and involved a party of twenty explorers, including miners, surveyors, and guards who were equipped to defend against the creatures of the deep caves. The expedition carried supplies for several months, including provisions, water, and the materials required for the construction of camps and the marking of passages.

The caves that the expedition explored were among the largest ever discovered. The passages extended for miles through the rock, connecting chambers that were large enough to contain entire dwarven settlements. The caves were inhabited by creatures that had not been seen by surface dwellers, including blind fish that swam in the underground rivers and pale insects that fed on the fungi that grew in the damp passages. The expedition also found evidence of previous inhabitants, including the remains of structures that had been built by an unknown people in the distant past.

The exploration of the eastern caves required more than a year to complete, and the expedition returned to Khazgar with a wealth of information about the underground world. The maps that Durnin produced of the caves were the most detailed ever made of any subterranean system, and they were studied by the students of the rock and the miners of the [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) Holds for the knowledge they contained.

## The Techniques of Deep Exploration

Durnin has developed techniques for deep exploration that have been adopted by explorers across the dwarven world. The methods for the navigation of underground passages, the management of supplies and equipment in the depths, and the documentation of underground features were developed through the experience of his expeditions.

The navigation of underground passages is the most challenging aspect of deep exploration. The absolute darkness of the caves makes orientation difficult, and the absence of landmarks that can be recognised from different directions creates the risk of becoming lost. Durnin developed a system of markers that his parties used to mark the passages they had explored, and he established procedures for the mapping of the caves through the measurement of distances and directions.

The management of supplies and equipment in the depths requires careful planning. The weight of the provisions, water, and tools that an expedition requires limits the distance it can travel from the surface, and the establishment of supply depots at strategic points is essential for extended exploration. Durnin developed a system of depots that his expeditions established at intervals along the passages they explored, stocked with provisions and equipment that could be used by the explorers and by any subsequent parties.

The documentation of underground features is essential for the scientific value of an expedition. Durnin established a standard format for the recording of observations, including the measurement of the dimensions of chambers and passages, the description of the rock and the mineral deposits, the identification of the fauna and flora of the caves, and the mapping of the underground system. The reports that his expeditions have produced are the most comprehensive records of the subterranean world that exist.

## The Discoveries of the Later Expeditions

The later expeditions that Durnin has led have taken him to the most remote regions of the subterranean world. He has explored the caves beneath the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea), where the passages extend below the seabed and the water seeps through the rock, and he has descended into the deep shafts of the [Whitewall Range](/content/location/natural/feature/whitewall-range), where the pressure of the rock above creates conditions that test the limits of dwarven engineering.

The discoveries that Durnin has made are among the most significant in the history of subterranean exploration. He has found deposits of minerals that were previously unknown, including a metal that he named deepsilver for its colour and its resistance to corrosion. He has identified species of underground fauna that have expanded the understanding of the ecology of the deep caves. He has also found evidence of the civilisations that inhabited the underground world in the distant past, including the remains of cities that were built by a people who are not recorded in any surface chronicle.

The most controversial of Durnin's discoveries is the evidence he has found of passages that extend beyond the known boundaries of the subterranean world, leading to regions that cannot be explained by the rock of the surface. The passages, which are located in the deepest levels of the eastern caves, appear to extend into spaces that are not connected to the mountains above, and the conditions in these passages, including the temperature and the composition of the air, are different from those of the caves that have been explored previously. The interpretation of Durnin's findings is a subject of debate among scholars, and some have proposed that the passages may lead to regions that are completely separate from the surface world.

## The Reputation among the Holds

Durnin Deepdelver is held in high esteem by the dwarves of the Stoneheim Holds and by the scholars of the subterranean world across the known continents. The knowledge that he has accumulated through his expeditions has contributed to the understanding of the rock and the living things of the deep caves, and the techniques that he has developed have been adopted by explorers who seek to extend the boundaries of the known underground world.

The mining guild of Khazgar has recognised Durnin's contributions through the award of its highest honours, and the reports of his expeditions are studied by the students of the mining colleges as examples of the systematic approach to exploration that the guild promotes. The maps that he has produced are kept in the guild's archives, and they are consulted by the mining companies that plan to extend their operations into the regions that he has explored.

Durnin continues to lead expeditions in the present age, despite the advanced age that the records of his career indicate. The passion for the exploration of the depths that he developed in childhood has not diminished, and the expeditions that he leads are still producing discoveries of significance. He is regarded as the pre-eminent explorer of the subterranean world, and the knowledge that he has accumulated through his work is the foundation of the understanding of the deep places in the present age.
