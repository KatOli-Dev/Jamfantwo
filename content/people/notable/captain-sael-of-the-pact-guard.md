## Overview

Captain Sael of the Pact Guard is the present commander of the collective force of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the alliance of the inland caravan cities at the edge of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). He was appointed to the command by the council of the pact in the year that the chroniclers of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) record as the three hundred and forty-first year of the pact, succeeding the previous captain, who had held the command for the customary term of six years. Captain Sael is in the forty-second year of his life, and his command of the guard has been marked by a substantial improvement in the discipline of the force and by the construction of two new watchtowers upon the caravan routes that pass through the deep desert.

## Background and Early Career

Sael was born in the city of [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari), the principal member of the pact and the seat of the council, and he is the son of a merchant of the caravan trade and a mother who was herself the daughter of a soldier of the pact guard. The early years of Sael were spent in the markets of the city, where he learned the customs of the caravan trade and the names of the merchants who operated the routes that crossed the desert. The young Sael showed an early aptitude for the management of the caravans and for the conduct of the guards who accompanied them, and he was enlisted in the pact guard at the age of seventeen, the customary age at which the young men of the inland cities enter the service.

The early service of Sael was spent upon the caravan routes, and the young soldier accompanied many caravans upon the crossing of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) and the journey to the inland oases. The soldier showed a precocious talent for the command of the small parties that are detached from the main body of the guard for the protection of the caravans, and he was promoted to the rank of sergeant at the age of twenty-four and to the rank of lieutenant at the age of thirty. The lieutenant served for a further period of seven years upon the routes before he was appointed to the staff of the guard at [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari), and he served upon the staff for a further period of three years before his elevation to the command of the force.

## The Pact Guard

The pact guard is the collective force of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), and the force is composed of the soldiers and the guards who are contributed by the member cities of the pact. The force is stationed at the principal points of the caravan routes, and the patrols that are conducted by the force are the principal means by which the routes are kept secure from the raiders and the wild peoples of the deep desert. The force is commanded by a captain, who is appointed by the council of the pact for a term of six years, and the captain is responsible for the discipline of the force, the conduct of the patrols, and the defence of the member cities in the event of an attack.

The captain of the guard is the principal military officer of the pact, and the captain's authority extends over the soldiers of all the member cities who are detached for the service of the collective force. The captain is responsible for the appointment of the officers of the force, and the lieutenants and the sergeants of the guard are selected by the captain from among the soldiers who have been recommended by the commanders of the individual cities. The captain is also responsible for the maintenance of the watchtowers and the shelters that the guard maintains upon the caravan routes, and the inspections that the captain conducts are a regular feature of the management of the force.

## The Caravan Routes

The principal concern of the captain is the security of the caravan routes that cross the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) and connect the inland cities to the coast. The routes are the lifeline of the pact, and the security of the routes is the foundation of the prosperity of the member cities. The captain is responsible for the disposition of the patrols that are conducted along the routes, and the lieutenants of the guard are stationed at the principal watchtowers and the shelters that the guard maintains at intervals along the routes. The patrols are conducted both by day and by night, and the soldiers of the guard are accustomed to the demands of the desert service.

The routes under the present captain have been the subject of substantial attention, and the captain has directed the construction of two new watchtowers upon the routes that pass through the deep desert. The watchtowers are situated at the points where the routes are most exposed to the threat of raiding, and the construction of the watchtowers has been a significant undertaking that has required the labour of the soldiers of the guard and the craftsmen of the member cities. The watchtowers are now in operation, and the patrols that are conducted from the watchtowers have substantially reduced the incidence of raiding upon the routes that they command.

## Defence of the Member Cities

The captain is also responsible for the defence of the member cities of the pact in the event of an attack, and the captain maintains a reserve of the guard at [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) that may be dispatched to the relief of any of the cities that may be threatened. The reserve is composed of the most experienced soldiers of the guard, and the reserve is equipped with the arms and the provisions that would be required for a campaign in the deep desert. The captain is also responsible for the coordination of the militias of the individual cities, and the lieutenants of the guard are in regular communication with the commanders of the militias to ensure that the defences of the cities are in a state of readiness.

The captain maintains a careful record of the strength and the disposition of the forces that are available for the defence of the cities, and the reports that the captain submits to the council of the pact are among the most important of the documents that come before the sessions. The reports include an assessment of the threats that the cities face from the raiders of the deep desert and from the wild peoples of the interior, and the recommendations that the captain makes are taken into consideration by the council in the determination of the contributions that the member cities are required to make to the support of the collective force.

## Character and Reputation

Captain Sael is described by the soldiers of the guard as a commander of considerable courage and of firm but equitable administration. The captain is known for the close attention that he pays to the welfare of the soldiers under his command, and the soldiers are accustomed to see him at the watchtowers and the shelters that the guard maintains upon the routes. The captain is also known for the courtesy with which he receives the deputations of the merchants who come to the guard hall at [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari), and the merchants speak well of the welcome that they receive.

The reputation of the captain is not confined to the inland cities, for the captains of the caravans that operate the routes have corresponded with Sael upon the matters that concern the security of the passages, and the reputation of the guard is a credit to the pact and to the council. The chronicler notes that the captain is held in high esteem by the soldiers and by the merchants alike, and the discipline that the captain has maintained during his tenure of the command is among the most rigorous that the long history of the force records.

## Significance

Captain Sael is the present commander of the pact guard and the principal military officer of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact). His command of the force has been marked by a substantial improvement in the discipline of the soldiers and by the construction of two new watchtowers upon the caravan routes. The chronicler notes that the office of the captain is one of the most important of the public positions of the pact, and the security of the caravan routes that the captain provides is a foundation upon which the commerce of the inland cities depends.
