## Overview

High Chronicler Marisel of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) is the present chief chronicler of the confederation of the island communities of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), and the principal keeper of the archives and the records of the isles. She was elected to the office of high chronicler by the assembly of the isles in the year that the chroniclers of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) record as the seven hundred and eighteenth year of the confederation, succeeding her predecessor, who had held the office for the customary term of nine years. High Chronicler Marisel is in the fifty-seventh year of her life, and her tenure of the office has been marked by a substantial extension of the archives and by the publication of a comprehensive chronicle of the isles that has become the standard reference of the students of the maritime history of the known world.

## Background and Early Life

Marisel was born on the largest of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), the island that serves as the seat of the assembly and the home of the principal archives of the confederation. Her father was a fisherman of the coastal village where she was born, and her mother was the daughter of a chronicler who had served the assembly for several decades. The early years of Marisel were spent upon the water and in the household of her maternal grandfather, who introduced her to the lore of the isles and to the records that the chroniclers maintained. The young Marisel showed a precocious aptitude for the reading of the chronicles and for the copying of the records, and her grandfather was accustomed to say that she would one day serve the assembly in his stead.

The education of a chronicler of the isles is conducted through a long apprenticeship to the senior members of the college of chroniclers, and the young Marisel was bound at the age of sixteen to the high chronicler who then held the office. The apprenticeship lasted for a period of nine years, during which Marisel learned the traditions of the college, the methods of the compilation of the chronicles, and the conduct of the relations with the foreign princes whose representatives visit the isles. The apprentice accompanied the high chronicler upon the circuits of the islands and upon the voyages to the principal ports of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), and the experience that she gained during these years has served her well in her subsequent tenure of the office.

## The College of Chroniclers

The college of chroniclers is the body that maintains the archives and the records of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), and the high chronicler is the chief magistrate of the college. The college is composed of the chroniclers who serve the individual islands of the confederation, and the chroniclers are appointed by the assemblies of the islands for their knowledge of the traditions and their skill in the compilation of the records. The high chronicler is elected by the assembly of the confederation for a term of nine years, and the high chronicler is responsible for the maintenance of the central archives, the publication of the chronicles of the confederation, and the conduct of the relations with the chroniclers of the other nations.

The archives of the college are housed in a building that adjoins the hall of the assembly on the principal island, and the archives contain the records of the confederation from its foundation to the present day. The records include the treaties that the confederation has negotiated with the foreign princes, the accounts of the voyages that the islanders have conducted upon the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), and the chronicles that the chroniclers have compiled of the events of the confederation. The high chronicler is responsible for the maintenance of the archives and for the publication of the chronicles that record the events of the confederation, and the work of the high chronicler is the foundation of the historical record of the isles.

## The Chronicle of the Isles

The most substantial of the publications of the present high chronicler is the comprehensive chronicle of the isles that has been published under her direction. The chronicle is a work of many volumes that sets forth the history of the confederation from its foundation to the present day, and the chronicle has been compiled from the records that are preserved in the archives and from the oral traditions that are maintained by the elders of the islands. The chronicle is the standard reference of the students of the maritime history of the known world, and the work has been received with acclaim by the scholars of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the other academies of the continent.

The chronicle has been published in several instalments, and the instalments are distributed to the chroniclers of the individual islands and to the scholars who visit the isles from the foreign academies. The work is being continued under the direction of the high chronicler, and the remaining volumes of the chronicle are expected to be published over the course of the next several years. The chronicler notes that the publication of the chronicle is one of the most significant events in the intellectual life of the isles, and the work is a credit to the college of chroniclers and to the confederation that the college serves.

The assembly of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) is the governing body of the confederation, and the high chronicler is the principal officer of the assembly in the conduct of the relations with the foreign princes whose representatives visit the isles. The ambassadors of the foreign princes are received by the high chronicler upon their arrival at the principal island, and the high chronicler is responsible for the conduct of the negotiations that take place during the visit. The high chronicler maintains a regular correspondence with the chroniclers and the archivists of the foreign princes, and the letters that are exchanged are preserved in the archives as a record of the diplomatic relations of the confederation. The chronicler notes that the high chronicler is the principal channel of communication between the assembly and the foreign princes in matters that touch upon the records and the chronicles, and the influence of the high chronicler in the conduct of these relations is considerable.

## Character and Reputation

High Chronicler Marisel is described by the chroniclers of the college as a scholar of considerable industry and of firm but patient administration. The high chronicler is known for the close attention that she pays to the work of the college, and the chroniclers of the staff are accustomed to find her at her desk in the archives at the earliest hours of the morning. The high chronicler is also known for the courtesy with which she receives the foreign scholars who visit the archives, and the foreign visitors speak well of the welcome that they receive at the hall of the college.

The reputation of the high chronicler extends throughout the known world, and the scholars of the foreign academies have corresponded with Marisel upon the matters that concern the history and the records of the isles. The chronicler notes that the high chronicler is held in high esteem by the chroniclers of the college and by the foreign scholars who have visited the archives, and the work that she has directed during her tenure of the office is among the most significant that the long history of the college records.
