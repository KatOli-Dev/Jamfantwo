## The Elder Who Founded the Confederation

Darian of the Plains is the centaur elder to whom the founding of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) is attributed, a figure of such central importance to the history of the central grasslands that his name is invoked at every council meeting and every gathering of the confederation's members. The Confederation was not the work of a single individual, but Darian's vision of co-operation between the settled cities and the nomadic kindreds provided the framework within which the alliance was established, and his mediation between the conflicting interests of the two ways of life was essential to the success of the founding council.

Darian was born into the kindred that ranged across the central grasslands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) in the centuries before the founding of the Confederation. The centaur kindreds of the period were nomadic, following the herds and the seasons across the vast expanse of grass that stretched from the foothills of the mountain spine to the river valleys of the east. The kindreds were self-governing units, each led by an elder who commanded the loyalty of the members and represented the kindred in dealings with other kindreds and with the settled peoples of the grasslands.

The period of Darian's youth was one of increasing tension between the nomadic centaurs and the settled agricultural communities of the plains. The expansion of farming into traditional grazing lands, the competition for water sources during dry seasons, and the disputes over the ownership of livestock that wandered across boundaries created a cycle of conflict that threatened the stability of the entire region. The settled communities regarded the centaurs as a threat to their fields and herds, and the centaurs regarded the farmers as encroachers on the lands that had sustained their way of life for generations.

## The Vision of Coexistence

The story of Darian's vision is preserved in the oral traditions of the centaur kindreds and in the written records of the settled cities. According to the most widely accepted account, Darian experienced a revelation during a journey across the plains in the dry season, when the grass was parched and the streams had dwindled to trickles. The scarcity of water had brought the kindreds and the settled communities into conflict more acute than usual, and Darian had witnessed the suffering that resulted from the cycle of raids and reprisals that characterised the relations between the two peoples.

In the course of this journey, Darian encountered a farmer whose fields had been trampled by a passing herd of centaurs, and whose family was threatened by the violence that had followed. The farmer, a man of the settlement that would later become [Avaros](/content/location/settlement/city/avaros), spoke to Darian not with the anger that he might have expected but with a weariness that reflected the exhaustion of both communities. The farmer expressed a desire for peace and a willingness to share the resources of the plains if a way could be found to ensure that the sharing was fair.

The encounter was, according to the tradition, the moment at which Darian's vision of a confederation of the two peoples took shape. He recognised that the conflict between the nomadic and settled peoples was not inevitable, but was the result of the absence of institutions through which the two ways of life could be accommodated. The solution was not to attempt to convert the centaurs to a settled existence or to drive the farmers from the plains, but to create a framework within which the two peoples could co-exist, each pursuing its own way of life while respecting the rights and needs of the other.

## The Years of Persuasion

The years that followed were devoted to the work of persuasion. Darian travelled across the plains, visiting the encampments of the centaur kindreds and the settlements of the agricultural communities, discussing the idea of a confederation with elders and farmers, merchants and herders. The reception was mixed. Many centaur elders were suspicious of any arrangement that would bind them to promises made with the settled peoples, and many farmers were unwilling to share the resources that they regarded as their own.

Darian's method of persuasion was patient and cumulative. He did not attempt to convince his listeners of the rightness of his vision through argument alone but invited them to consider the costs of the existing cycle of conflict and the benefits that co-operation might bring. He pointed to the scarcity of water in dry seasons and the waste of resources that resulted from raids and reprisals, and he asked his listeners to imagine a system in which the allocation of grazing and water was determined by agreement rather than by conflict.

The work of persuasion continued for more than a decade. Darian established relationships with the elders of the principal kindreds and the leaders of the principal settlements, and he gradually built a network of support that crossed the boundary between the two peoples. The network was informal, based on personal trust and shared experience, but it provided the foundation for the more formal institutions that Darian intended to establish.

## The Founding Council

The founding council of the Plains Confederation met in the year 340, at a site on the central plains that had been chosen by Darian for its proximity to both centaur grazing lands and settled farmlands. The council was attended by representatives of the principal cities and the elders of the principal kindreds, and it met over the course of several weeks in the summer, when the grass was high and the weather was favourable for travel and deliberation.

Darian presided over the council in his capacity as the elder who had convened it, and he delivered the opening address that set the terms for the negotiations that followed. The address is preserved in the records of the Confederation and is still recited at the opening of each spring council. Darian spoke of the interdependence of the nomadic and settled peoples, the waste and suffering that resulted from their conflicts, and the possibility of a future in which the two ways of life could be accommodated within a single framework of governance that respected the autonomy of each community while providing for co-operation on matters of common concern.

The negotiations that followed were difficult and prolonged. The settled cities demanded guarantees that the centaur kindreds would respect the boundaries of agricultural land and refrain from raiding. The centaur elders demanded recognition of their right to range across the plains following the seasons and their access to water sources and grazing lands. Darian mediated between the two groups, proposing compromises that addressed the concerns of each side while preserving the essential character of the other's way of life.

The agreement that emerged from the council was the founding charter of the Plains Confederation. The charter established a council of representatives from each member city and each centaur kindred, with authority to co-ordinate trade, agriculture, and defence. The charter also established the Rights of the Plains, the set of principles that no council could set aside, including the right of every free adult to speak at the Open Grass Assembly and the right of every community to choose its own representative. The charter was the first document of its kind in the history of the known world, a formal agreement between nomadic and settled peoples that established a framework for co-operation without requiring either to abandon its way of life.

## The Role of Elder and Mediator

Darian served as the first presiding elder of the Confederation council, a role that he held for the remainder of his life. The position was not one of formal authority, for the charter had established that the council would reach decisions by consensus and that no single member could impose its will on the others. The presiding elder was responsible for convening the council, aiding its deliberations, and representing the Confederation in dealings with external powers. The authority that Darian exercised derived from the respect he commanded among both the settled and nomadic members, and from the trust that had been established during the years of persuasion that preceded the founding.

The decisions of the council in its early years were shaped by Darian's guidance. The allocation of grazing lands and water sources was resolved through agreements that balanced the needs of the settled and nomadic peoples. The disputes that arose between communities were mediated by Darian, who applied the principles of the Rights of the Plains to resolve conflicts in a manner that the parties accepted. The military forces of the Confederation were organised in response to threats from beyond the plains, and Darian's advice on the deployment of forces was followed by the council.

The later years of Darian's life were devoted to the consolidation of the Confederation and the training of a successor. The council had become an established institution, and the annual meetings were attended by representatives from communities across the plains. The Rights of the Plains had become part of the political culture of the region, and the principles of co-operation and mutual respect that Darian had articulated were accepted by both the settled and nomadic peoples.

## The Death and the Mourning of the Plains

Darian died in the forty-fifth year after the founding of the Confederation, at an age that the traditions record as very great but do not specify with precision. The manner of his death is described in the oral traditions of the centaur kindreds: he died while travelling across the plains in the company of his kindred, at a place where the grass was high and the sky was wide, and his passing was as peaceful as the traditions of the centaurs could wish.

The mourning that followed was unprecedented in the history of the plains. The council of the Confederation declared a period of mourning that lasted for a full season, and representatives from every member city and every centaur kindred gathered at the site of his death to pay their respects. Settled farmers and nomadic centaurs, who had been enemies a generation before, stood together in grief for the elder who had brought them together.

The stone circle that marks the grave of Darian of the Plains stands at the site where the founding council met, and it is the most sacred site of the Confederation. The circle is maintained by the descendants of the kindred that Darian led, and the stones of the circle are renewed when they weather or crack. The council meets at the site on anniversaries of the founding, and the opening of each council session is marked by the recitation of Darian's address and the reaffirmation of the Rights of the Plains.

## The Assessment of the Historians

Darian of the Plains is recognised by historians of all nations as one of the most significant figures in the political history of Aethelar. His achievement in establishing a framework for co-operation between nomadic and settled peoples was unprecedented, and the institutions that he created have endured through the centuries that followed. The Plains Confederation that he founded remains the most inclusive political entity in the known world, and the Rights of the Plains that he articulated remain the foundation of its governance.

Scholars of the Khalisar Academy have studied Darian's methods of mediation and persuasion, noting that his approach to conflict resolution anticipated the principles that were later codified in the diplomatic traditions of the Sahrani city-states. The patience and persistence with which he pursued his vision, the respect he showed for the interests and concerns of both parties, and the creativity with which he proposed solutions to seemingly intractable problems have been cited as models for negotiators and mediators in the present age.

Chroniclers of the centaur kindreds have preserved the memory of Darian through the oral traditions of the plains, and his name is invoked by centaur elders at gatherings of the kindreds as an example of leadership and wisdom. The traditions emphasise not only his political achievements but also his personal qualities: his patience, his fairness, his courage in the face of opposition, and his unwavering commitment to the vision of co-operation that he had conceived.

The name of Darian of the Plains is spoken with respect by the settled peoples of the confederation as well. The farmers and merchants of the plains recognise that the institutions he established have brought peace and prosperity to the region, and that the framework of co-operation that he created has benefited both the settled communities and the nomadic kindreds. The stone circle that marks his resting place is a place of pilgrimage for those who wish to honour the memory of the elder who founded the Confederation.
