## Overview

Lien, of the House of Winds is recorded in the chronicles of the academies of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) as the scholar who compiled the first complete atlas of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the coasts that bound it. Lien was born in [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the middle generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace), and Lien was educated in the academy of Khalisar before being appointed to the position of chief cartographer of the council. Lien served the council for more than forty years, and Lien's atlas has been the foundation of every subsequent chart of the warm coast and the western islands.

Lien is held in particular esteem by the pilots and the merchants of the warm coast, and Lien's atlas is consulted by every pilot who must set a course across the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The chronicler notes that Lien is one of the most useful of the scholars of the present age, and Lien's atlas is the measure of the public spirit of the academies of Khalisar.

## Early Life

Lien was born into a household of pilots who had served the [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch) for several generations, and Lien's father was a senior pilot of the Watch at the time of Lien's birth. Lien grew up among the charts and the instruments of the Watch, and Lien learned the names and the bearings of the coasts and the islands of the warm coast before Lien learned to read. Lien was sent to the academy of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) at the customary age, and Lien excelled in the studies of geography and of the stars. Lien graduated from the academy with the highest honours of Lien's year, and Lien was appointed to the position of junior cartographer in the office of the chief cartographer of the council.

Lien served the office of the chief cartographer for several years, and Lien compiled the charts of the harbours of the warm coast with care. Lien's charts were more accurate than any that the office had previously produced, and Lien's charts were adopted by the [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch) for the use of the pilots of the fleet. The chronicler notes that the adoption was the foundation of Lien's reputation, and the reputation is the measure of Lien's public spirit.

## The Atlas

The atlas that Lien compiled was the work of more than twenty years, and the atlas was published by the academy of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the fortieth year of Lien's service to the council. The atlas contained charts of every coast and every island of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), and the atlas contained tables of the tides and the currents that Lien had compiled from the records of the pilots and the merchants. The chronicler notes that the atlas was the most comprehensive of the productions of the academy, and the atlas was the foundation of the navigation of the warm coast for several generations.

The atlas was received with great public approval by the magistrates and the merchants of the warm coast, and the approval was expressed in the public ceremonies that the council conducted to mark the publication. The ceremonies included a procession through the streets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), a service in the temple of [the Sea Mother's Court](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sea-mothers-court), and a public reading of the dedication of the atlas. The dedication honoured the pilots who had furnished the records that Lien had used, and the dedication expressed Lien's gratitude to the magistrates who had supported the work.

## Later Life

Lien continued to serve the council for several years after the publication of the atlas, and Lien revised the atlas in the light of the new information that the pilots brought back from their voyages. Lien also compiled a smaller atlas of the harbours of the warm coast for the use of the merchants, and the smaller atlas was published by the academy and adopted by the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) for the use of the magistrates of the towns of the coast.

Lien retired from the service of the council in the seventieth year of Lien's life, and Lien spent the remaining years in the academy of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the company of the other scholars who had been Lien's colleagues. Lien died in the eightieth year of Lien's life, and Lien was buried in the cemetery of the academy with the honours that the council had decreed. The chronicler notes that Lien was mourned by the magistrates and the merchants of every city of the warm coast, and the mourning was the foundation of the public memory of Lien's life.

## Legacy

Lien's atlas has been the foundation of every subsequent chart of the warm coast and the western islands, and the atlas is consulted by every pilot who must set a course across the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The atlas has been revised from time to time by the scholars of the academy, and the revisions have been entered in the copies that the academy keeps. The chronicler notes that the atlas is the most useful of the productions of the academy, and the atlas is the measure of the public spirit of the magistrates and the scholars of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar).

The public memory of Lien is preserved in the chronicles of the academy and in the public observances that the council conducts each year on the anniversary of the publication of the atlas. The chronicler notes that the observances are among the most important of the public observances of the warm coast, and the observances are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every city of the coast.

## Significance

Lien, of the House of Winds is one of the most useful of the scholars of the present age, and Lien's atlas is the foundation of the navigation of the warm coast for several centuries. The atlas, the smaller atlas of the harbours, and the legacy of Lien's service to the council are the inheritance that Lien has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that Lien is held in particular memory by the pilots and the merchants of the warm coast, and Lien is the measure by which the magistrates and the councils of the coast judge the public acts of their own day.
