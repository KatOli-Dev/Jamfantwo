## The Engineer of the Stonespire Road

Bron Stoneheim was the engineer who designed and oversaw the construction of the great road through the [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass). Born in the upper reaches of the Stonespire in the early generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace), Bron trained as an engineer in the workshops of the senior holds before being appointed chief engineer of the road-building programme, serving for more than thirty years. The road that Bron built became the foundation of land trade between the cold north and the warm coast.

The Stonespire Pass had been a formidable obstacle for merchants travelling between the northern highlands and the southern coastal plains. Before Bron's road, the only routes across the pass were treacherous tracks used by shepherds and hunters, impassable for laden wagons and dangerous even for pack animals during the winter months. The road that Bron designed changed this entirely, establishing a permanent route that could accommodate the heaviest traffic through every season of the year.

Bron is held in particular esteem by the dwarves of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and by the merchants of the inland valleys. The road across the Stonespire Pass is consulted by every merchant who must cross the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands). Bron is regarded as one of the most accomplished engineers of the age, and the road is considered a measure of the public spirit of the dwarven holds.

## Upbringing in the Dwarven Workshops

Bron was born into a household of miners and engineers who had served the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) for several generations. Bron's father was a master engineer of the senior hold at the time of Bron's birth. Bron grew up among the workshops and forges of the hold, learning the principles of stone-cutting and road-building before learning to read. Sent to the engineering school of the senior hold at the customary age, Bron excelled in geometry and the strength of materials, graduating with the highest honours of the year and being appointed junior engineer in the office of the chief engineer of the holds.

Bron served the office of the chief engineer for several years, designing retaining walls and culverts for the principal roads. Bron's designs proved more durable than any the office had previously produced. The retaining walls incorporated drainage channels that prevented the accumulation of groundwater behind the stonework, an innovation that reduced the damage caused by frost heave during the winter months. The culverts were designed with a wider cross-section than the customary pattern, allowing them to accommodate the increased runoff of the spring thaw without being overwhelmed. Both innovations were adopted by the holds for use by the engineers of the road-building programme, establishing Bron's reputation throughout the Stoneheim region.

## Design, Construction, and Opening

The road that Bron designed and oversaw required more than twenty years of work and opened to traffic in the middle of Bron's service to the programme. It crossed the [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass) in a series of cuttings cut into the rock, descending to the foothills of the warm coast in a long, winding course laid out with skill. The road was surfaced with layers of compacted stone that provided a firm footing for wagons even in wet weather, and its gradient was carefully calculated to allow laden vehicles to ascend without excessive strain on the draft animals. The road was the most remarkable work of the road-building programme and became the foundation of land trade across the three continents for several generations.

The road was received with great public approval by the magistrates and merchants of the three continents. The council of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) conducted public ceremonies to mark the opening, including a procession across the pass, a service in the temple of the holds, and a public reading of the inscription that Bron had composed for the principal cutting. The inscription honoured the engineers who had worked upon the road and expressed Bron's gratitude to the magistrates who had supported the work. The route was divided into sections, each marked by stone pillars that indicated distances and provided travellers with a means of estimating the remaining journey to the nearest settlement or way station.

## Retirement and Teaching the Next Generation

Bron continued to serve the programme for several years after the opening of the road, supervising its maintenance and the construction of watchtowers and quarters required by the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch). These watchtowers were placed at intervals along the pass, providing shelter for travellers caught by sudden storms and serving as relay points for signals carried along the route. Bron also advised the magistrates of the holds on the construction of new roads that the programme required, and Bron's advice was followed in every case. This advice was the foundation of the careful management of the road, which contributed much to the prosperity of the towns of the three continents by ensuring that trade could pass through the mountains without interruption.

Bron retired from the service of the programme in the seventieth year of life and spent the remaining years in the senior hold in the company of fellow engineers. The time was devoted to teaching younger engineers the principles that had guided Bron's own work, passing on knowledge of stone-cutting, drainage, and route selection that might otherwise have been lost. Bron died in the eightieth year and was buried in the cemetery of the holds with the honours decreed by the council. Bron was mourned by the dwarves of the holds and by the merchants of every town of the three continents, who recognised that the road through the Stonespire Pass had transformed the economic life of the entire region.

## Memory and Annual Observances

Bron's road has been the foundation of land trade between the cold north and the warm coast for several centuries. It is consulted by every merchant who must cross the Stoneheim Highlands and has been maintained by the watchmen of the [Northern Roads Watch](/content/government/local/northern-roads-watch) since its opening. The road is the most useful work of the programme and a measure of the public spirit of the dwarves of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds). The principles that Bron established in the design of the road have been applied to other routes across the mountains, extending the benefits of reliable land communication to regions that had previously been isolated by the terrain.

Public memory of Bron is preserved in the chronicles of the holds and in annual observances conducted by the council on the anniversary of the opening of the road. These observances are among the most important public observances of the three continents, attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every town of both regions.

## Bron's Measure as an Engineer

Bron Stoneheim is remembered as one of the most accomplished engineers of the age. The road, the watchtowers, the quarters, and the legacy of Bron's service constitute the inheritance bequeathed to the present age. Bron is held in particular memory by the dwarves of the holds and by the merchants of the three continents, and Bron is the measure by which the magistrates and councils of the three continents judge the public acts of their own day.
