## The Cartographer of the Warm Coast

Lien Wind was the scholar who compiled the first complete atlas of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the coasts that bound it. Born in [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the middle generations of the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace), Lien was educated in the academy of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) before being appointed chief cartographer of the council. Lien served the council for more than forty years, and the atlas became the foundation of every subsequent chart of the warm coast and the western islands.

Before Lien's work, the charts available to pilots were fragmentary and inconsistent. Each port maintained its own set of coastal descriptions, often copied and recopied with accumulating errors. Distances between harbours were recorded differently depending on the source, and the positions of reefs and shoals were frequently omitted or mislocated. The council of Khalisar had long recognised the need for a comprehensive and consistent compilation, but the scale of the task had deterred earlier cartographers. Lien undertook the work with a systematic approach that had no precedent in the region.

Lien is held in particular esteem by the pilots and merchants of the warm coast. The atlas is consulted by every pilot who must set a course across the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). Lien is regarded as one of the most useful scholars of the present age, and the atlas is considered a measure of the public spirit of the academies of Khalisar.

## Born to a Household of Pilots

Lien was born into a household of pilots who had served the [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch) for several generations. Lien's father was a senior pilot of the Watch at the time of Lien's birth. Lien grew up among the charts and instruments of the Watch, learning the names and bearings of the coasts and islands of the warm coast before learning to read. The family's collection of pilot logs and coastal descriptions, accumulated over generations, gave Lien early access to records that other cartographers would have had to travel to multiple ports to consult.

Sent to the academy of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) at the customary age, Lien excelled in geography and astronomy, graduating with the highest honours of the year and being appointed junior cartographer in the office of the chief cartographer of the council. The training at Khalisar included instruction in the use of the astrolabe and the cross-staff, instruments that Lien would later employ in verifying the positions recorded in the pilot logs. Lien also studied the methods of the astronomers who had charted the movements of the stars, adapting their techniques of angular measurement to the problem of determining positions at sea.

Lien served the office of the chief cartographer for several years, compiling charts of the harbours of the warm coast that were more accurate than any the office had previously produced. These charts were adopted by the [Inner Sea Watch](/content/government/local/inner-sea-watch) for use by the pilots of the fleet. This adoption established Lien's reputation.

## Twenty Years of Charts and Tables

The atlas that Lien compiled was the work of more than twenty years, published by the academy of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the fortieth year of Lien's service to the council. It contained charts of every coast and every island of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), together with tables of tides and currents compiled from the records of pilots and merchants. The charts were drawn to a consistent scale, a feature that had been absent from earlier collections and that allowed pilots to compare distances between different sections of the coast with confidence. The atlas also included diagrams of the principal harbours, showing the depths of the approaches and the locations of safe anchorage.

The atlas was received with great public approval by the magistrates and merchants of the warm coast. The council conducted ceremonies to mark the publication, including a procession through the streets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), a service in the [temple of the Sea Mother](/content/religion/temple-of-the-sea-mother)'s Court, and a public reading of the dedication. The dedication honoured the pilots who had furnished records that Lien had used and expressed Lien's gratitude to the magistrates who had supported the work. Copies of the atlas were distributed to every major port of the warm coast, where they were kept in the pilot guilds for reference by ships setting out on voyages.

## Revisions and the Smaller Harbour Atlas

Lien continued to serve the council for several years after the publication of the atlas, revising it in the light of new information brought back by pilots from their voyages. Each revision incorporated corrections submitted by mariners who had found discrepancies between the charts and the conditions they encountered at sea. Lien also compiled a smaller atlas of the harbours of the warm coast for use by merchants, published by the academy and adopted by the [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) for use by the magistrates of the coastal towns. This smaller volume omitted the detailed coastal charts of the full atlas and focused instead on the approaches to ports, the facilities available at each harbour, and the local customs and dues that merchants needed to know.

Lien retired from the service of the council in the seventieth year of life and spent the remaining years in the academy of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the company of other scholars. Lien died in the eightieth year and was buried in the cemetery of the academy with the honours decreed by the council. Lien was mourned by the magistrates and merchants of every city of the warm coast.

## Annual Observances and Enduring Use

Lien's atlas has been the foundation of every subsequent chart of the warm coast and the western islands. It is consulted by every pilot who must set a course across the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and has been revised from time to time by scholars of the academy, with revisions entered in the copies the academy keeps. The atlas is the most useful production of the academy and a measure of the public spirit of the magistrates and scholars of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar). The consistency of the charts allowed, for the first time, the compilation of reliable route descriptions that pilots could follow from port to port without needing local knowledge of each section of the coast.

Public memory of Lien is preserved in the chronicles of the academy and in annual observances conducted by the council on the anniversary of the publication of the atlas. These observances are among the most important of the warm coast, attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every city.

## Lien's Measure as a Scholar

Lien Wind is remembered as one of the most useful scholars of the present age. The atlas, the smaller atlas of the harbours, and the legacy of Lien's service to the council constitute the inheritance bequeathed to the present age. Lien is held in particular memory by the pilots and merchants of the warm coast, and Lien is the measure by which the magistrates and councils of the coast judge the public acts of their own day.
