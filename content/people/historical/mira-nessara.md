## The Healer Who Faced the Stone Plague

Mira Nessara was the healer who maintained the practice of medicine in the lower Greywash through the worst years of the [Stone Plague](/content/history/stone-plague). Born into a household of healers in [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) in the generations before the plague, Mira trained in the practice of the Plague and Remedy before being appointed chief healer of the magistrates. Mira served the magistrates through the years of the plague, and the records of cases attended became the foundation of the present understanding of the disease.

The Stone Plague was the most devastating epidemic in the recorded history of the northern continent. It appeared without warning in the coastal cities of the east and spread along the trade routes into the interior, killing a significant portion of the population in the regions it affected. The disease was characterised by a progressive hardening of the flesh, beginning at the extremities and spreading inward, accompanied by fever and delirium. No reliable remedy was known at the time of its first appearance, and the healers of the cold north were forced to develop methods of treatment through trial and error as the epidemic ran its course.

Mira is held in particular esteem by the healers and magistrates of the cold north. The records are consulted by every healer who must judge a question of public health. Mira is regarded as one of the most useful healers of the present age, and the records are considered a measure of the public spirit of the magistrates of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara).

## Raised in the Public Hospital

Mira was born into a household of healers who had served the magistrates of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) for several generations. Mira's mother was a senior healer of the public hospital at the time of Mira's birth. Mira grew up among the wards and dispensaries of the hospital, learning the names and virtues of the plants of the Plant-Lore before learning to read. The hospital of Nessara was one of the oldest institutions of its kind in the cold north, maintaining a garden of medicinal plants and a library of remedy texts that had been accumulated over centuries.

Sent to the academy of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) at the customary age, Mira excelled in the study of remedies and the management of disease, graduating with the highest honours of the year and being appointed junior healer in the public hospital. The academy of Vassholm was the principal centre of medical learning in the northern region, and its faculty included healers who had studied the practices of the southern continents as well as the traditions of the cold north.

Mira served the public hospital for several years, compiling a catalogue of the remedies the hospital used that was more complete than any previously produced. The catalogue included not only the names and preparations of each remedy but also observations on which conditions responded best to each treatment. The catalogue was adopted by the magistrates for use by the healers of the region, establishing Mira's reputation as a healer of unusual diligence.

## Chief Healer in the Time of Pestilence

The plague reached [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) in the second year of its spread. The magistrates appointed Mira chief healer of the public response. Mira worked in concert with the commissioners of public health appointed by the magistrates, overseeing the separation of the sick from the well, the purification of the air and water, and the careful disposal of the dead. The measures that Mira carried out were based on the observation that the disease appeared to spread through contact with the afflicted, a theory that had been proposed by earlier healers but had not been systematically applied. Mira worked tirelessly through the years of the plague, and this work became the foundation of the practice of disease management in the present age.

Mira kept careful records of cases attended, entered in the public register maintained by the magistrates. Each record included the name and condition of the patient, the remedy used, and the outcome. Mira noted not only which treatments were effective but also the stage of the disease at which they were administered, recognising that remedies applied early in the progression of the illness had a greater chance of success. These records are the most complete surviving records of the plague and the foundation of the present understanding of the disease. Mira's records were consulted by healers of every city of the three continents.

## Reopening and the New Catalogue

Mira continued to serve the magistrates through the years of recovery following the plague, overseeing the reopening of the public hospital and the resumption of healing practices that the plague had interrupted. The hospital had been closed during the worst months of the epidemic, its wards converted to isolation chambers for the afflicted. Its reopening required the training of new healers to replace those who had died, the replenishment of remedies that had been exhausted, and the restoration of public confidence in the institution. Mira advised the magistrates on measures required for recovery, and Mira's advice was followed in every case.

Mira also compiled a new catalogue of remedies for the hospital, incorporating lessons the plague had taught. The new catalogue included remedies that had proved effective against the plague alongside the traditional treatments of the cold north, and it noted which remedies had been found to be useless or harmful. This new catalogue was the most complete of the cold north and was adopted by the magistrates of every town of the region.

## Teaching at the Academy of Vassholm

Mira retired from the service of the magistrates in the fiftieth year of life and spent the remaining years in the academy of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) in the company of other healers. During these years, Mira assisted in the training of a new generation of healers, teaching the lessons learned during the plague and demonstrating the methods of record-keeping that had proved so valuable. Mira died in the seventieth year and was buried in the cemetery of the academy with the honours decreed by the magistrates. Mira was mourned by the healers and magistrates of every city of the cold north.

## The Records That Saved Future Generations

Mira's records of cases attended have been the foundation of the present understanding of the [Stone Plague](/content/history/stone-plague). They are consulted by every healer who must judge a question of public health and have been preserved in the archives of the academy of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm), which has maintained them with care. The records are the most useful production of the academy and a measure of the public spirit of the healers and magistrates of the cold north. The methods of isolation and record-keeping that Mira developed during the plague have been applied to the management of subsequent outbreaks, reducing the mortality of later epidemics.

Public memory of Mira is preserved in the chronicles of the academy and in annual observances conducted by the magistrates on the anniversary of the end of the plague. These observances are among the most important of the cold north, attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every city of the region.

## Mira's Enduring Contribution

Mira Nessara is remembered as one of the most useful healers of the present age. The records, the catalogue of remedies, and the legacy of service to the magistrates constitute the inheritance bequeathed to the present age. Mira is held in particular memory by the healers and magistrates of the cold north, and Mira is the measure by which the magistrates and councils of the region judge the public acts of their own day.
