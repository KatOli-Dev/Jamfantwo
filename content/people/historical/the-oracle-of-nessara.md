## Overview

The Oracle of Nessara is the most enigmatic figure in the history of the Sahrani coast, a prophet of obscure origin whose utterances shaped the destiny of the city-states of the northern littoral for more than a century. The Oracle was not a single individual but a succession of seers who spoke from the sanctuary of the temple that stood upon the headland overlooking the harbour of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara), and the prophecies that were delivered from the sanctuary influenced the decisions of rulers, the movements of armies, and the beliefs of peoples across the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The chronicler must note that the nature of the Oracle's power is a matter of enduring debate, for the sceptical scholars of the present age have proposed explanations that range from political manipulation to the systematic exploitation of maritime intelligence, but the influence of the prophecies upon the history of the region is beyond dispute.

The first Oracle appeared in the year that the chroniclers of the [League of the Northern Coast](/content/government/national/league-of-the-northern-coast) record as the two hundred and sixth year of the league, a period when the Sahrani coast was divided among a number of competing city-states that were engaged in a cycle of warfare and alliance that threatened the stability of the entire region. The city of Nessara at that time was a modest port, less wealthy than [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) to the east and less fortified than the southern cities of the coast, and its position at the edge of the fertile strip made it vulnerable to the incursions of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) from the interior and the raids of the corsairs who operated from the harbours of the western coast.

## The Prophecies

The first prophecy of the Oracle was delivered in the marketplace of Nessara on a day that the chronicles record with unusual precision, for the event marked the beginning of the Oracle's influence over the affairs of the coast. A figure appeared in the marketplace at the height of the trading day, a woman of indeterminate age and origin who was dressed in the white robes that would become the characteristic garment of the Oracle's successors. She climbed onto the steps of the fountain that stood in the centre of the square, and she spoke in a voice that carried across the noise of the market, declaring that the city of Nessara would be attacked within the month by a fleet from the western coast, and that the attack would come from the direction of the setting sun on the night of the new moon.

The prophecy was met with scepticism by the merchants and the officials of the city, for Nessara had no reason to expect an attack from the western coast, and the relations between the city-states of the northern coast were governed by treaties that had been negotiated the previous year. The scepticism of the officials did not prevent the Oracle from being taken into custody by the guards of the city, and she was brought before the council that governed Nessara, where she repeated the prophecy with the same calm certainty that she had displayed in the marketplace.

The council of Nessara debated the fate of the Oracle, and the decision was made to keep her in custody until the prophecy could be tested by the passage of time. The month that followed was a period of tension in the city, for the prophecy had been spread by the merchants and the workers who had heard it, and the population of Nessara was divided between those who believed that the Oracle spoke the truth and those who dismissed her as a fraud or a madwoman. On the night of the new moon, the lookouts on the headland reported the lights of vessels approaching from the west, and the fleet that had been predicted by the Oracle was sighted approaching the harbour.

The defence of Nessara was improvised, for the council had taken no steps to prepare for an attack that the majority of the members had believed to be impossible, and the fleet successfully landed a raiding party that plundered the warehouses of the waterfront before withdrawing into the darkness. The attack was less destructive than it might have been, for the warning had allowed the city to move the most valuable goods from the waterfront to the interior of the city, but the fact of the attack itself was sufficient to transform the standing of the Oracle from a curiosity to a figure of immense importance.

The Oracle was released from custody and brought before the council, which now received her with the deference that was due to a seer whose prophecy had been fulfilled with such precision. The council offered her any reward that the city could provide, and the Oracle asked only for the use of the temple that stood upon the headland overlooking the harbour, a small sanctuary that had been dedicated to the old gods of the Sahrani coast and that had fallen into disuse in the previous century. The request was granted, and the Oracle took up residence in the temple, where she would remain for the rest of her life.

The subsequent prophecies of the Oracle were recorded by the scribes of the temple, and the collection of the oracles that was compiled in the following decades became the most consulted text in the libraries of the Sahrani coast. The prophecies were delivered in a distinctive style, a measured and ambiguous language that permitted multiple interpretations, and the interpretation of the oracles became a specialised discipline that was studied by the scholars and the rulers of the coastal city-states.

The second Oracle of note delivered a prophecy that warned the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) of a conspiracy among the merchants of the spice trade, a warning that allowed the republic to uncover a plot to adulterate the spice shipments that were the foundation of the city's commerce. The third Oracle predicted a drought that affected the interior of Sahrana three years before the drought began, and the warning allowed the city-states of the coast to stockpile the grain that sustained the population through the period of scarcity. The fourth Oracle was consulted by the emissaries of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), who had travelled to Nessara to seek guidance on the matter of the caravan routes that crossed the desert, and the prophecy that they received led to the discovery of a new water source in the interior that opened a previously impassable route to the southern coast.

## Influence

The influence of the Oracle of Nessara extended far beyond the immediate predictions that were delivered from the temple on the headland. The sanctuary that the Oracle established became a centre of pilgrimage for the peoples of the Sahrani coast, and the offerings that were brought by the pilgrims provided the resources that supported the maintenance of the temple and the training of the successors who would continue the Oracle's work. The temple developed into an institution of considerable wealth and influence, and the oracles that were delivered from the sanctuary were treated with the same respect that the rulers of the coast accorded to the pronouncements of the most powerful kingdoms.

The political influence of the Oracle was exercised through the advice that was given to the rulers and the councils of the city-states, and the prophecies that were delivered from the temple frequently contained guidance on matters of diplomacy, of commerce, and of war. The Oracle of Nessara was credited with preventing several conflicts between the city-states of the coast, for the prophecies that warned of the consequences of war were heeded by the rulers who had been influenced by the accuracy of earlier predictions, and the mediation that the Oracle provided was a factor in the stability of the region during a period when the competition between the city-states might otherwise have led to a destructive cycle of conflict.

The relationship between the Oracle and the government of Nessara was a matter of mutual benefit, for the prestige that the Oracle brought to the city attracted pilgrims and traders who contributed to the prosperity of the port, and the protection that the city provided to the temple ensured the security of the institution that was the source of the Oracle's authority. The council of Nessara consulted the Oracle on matters of policy, and the oracles that were delivered from the temple were taken into account in the decisions that shaped the development of the city.

## Legacy

The legacy of the Oracle of Nessara is the tradition of prophecy that has persisted on the Sahrani coast for more than four centuries, a tradition that has shaped the political and the commercial development of the region and that has contributed to the stability that has allowed the city-states of the coast to prosper. The temple that the first Oracle established on the headland of Nessara is still maintained by the successors of the original seer, and the oracles that are delivered from the sanctuary are still consulted by the rulers and the merchants who seek guidance on the matters that affect the fortunes of the coast.

The collection of prophecies that was compiled in the first century of the Oracle's activity is preserved in the library of Nessara, and the text is studied by the scholars who seek to understand the methods and the sources of the prophetic tradition. The sceptical scholars of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) have proposed that the accuracy of the prophecies was based on the systematic collection of intelligence from the pilgrims and the traders who visited the temple, and the scholars of the [League of the Northern Coast](/content/government/national/league-of-the-northern-coast) have noted that the ambiguity of the prophetic language allowed the oracles to be interpreted in a manner that was consistent with events after they had occurred. The defenders of the tradition argue that the precision of certain predictions, particularly the first prophecy of the fleet from the western coast, cannot be explained by the theories of the sceptics, and the debate between the schools of interpretation continues in the academies of the present age.

The Oracle of Nessara is the most enduring and the most mysterious figure in the history of the Sahrani coast, and the influence of the prophecies upon the development of the region is a subject that the chroniclers of every nation of the known world have addressed. The figure of the Oracle, whether understood as a genuine seer, a skilled political operator, or a fortunate fraud, has left a mark upon the history of Sahrana that is as deep as the mark of any conqueror or any king, and the temple that stands upon the headland of Nessara is the monument of a tradition that has endured through the rise and the fall of dynasties, the shifting of trade routes, and the passage of centuries.
