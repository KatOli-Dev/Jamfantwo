## Overview

King Halric of the [Kingdom of Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael) is the thirty-sixth monarch of the Vaelic dynasty, the ruler whose long reign of thirty-eight years preceded the accession of his son [King Aldric of Vael](/content/people/notable/king-aldric-of-vael) and the ruler who consolidated the institutions of the kingdom that his predecessors had established. Halric was born in the royal palace that overlooks the confluence of the two rivers at [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth), and he ascended to the throne of his ancestors in the year that the chroniclers of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) record as the eight hundred and seventy-third year of the republic, succeeding his father King Dorian, who died after a reign of twenty-seven years. Halric died in the seventy-fourth year of his life and the thirty-eighth year of his reign, and his body was interred in the royal tomb on the hill that overlooks the city of Vaelmouth. The chronicler of the Vaelic dynasty records that Halric's reign was one of the most stable of the long succession, and the institutions that Halric maintained have endured through the present age.

## Birth and Lineage

Halric was born in the royal palace at [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) in the year that the chroniclers of the [Kingdom of Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael) record as the year of his grandfather's reign, and he was the second son of King Dorian by his queen, a lady of the great house of the western marches. The lineage of Halric is unbroken through the long succession of the Vaelic dynasty, and the chroniclers of the court have taken care to record the descent of the king from the founder of the line through a chain of marriages and of births that is unbroken by any irregularity of succession. The early years of Halric were spent in the palace at Vaelmouth, and he received the customary education of the princes of the realm, including instruction in the law and the governance of the kingdom, in the history of the dynasty and the continent, and in the arts of the sword and the bow.

The firstborn son of King Dorian died in childhood, and the death left Halric as the heir to the throne at an age when most princes of the line were still under the supervision of their tutors. The chronicler of the dynasty records that the early elevation of Halric to the position of heir was conducted with a care that the council of the kingdom observed, and the care included the appointment of senior lords of the valley to serve as the prince's advisors. The advisors instructed the prince in the affairs of the kingdom, and the instruction gave the prince a knowledge of the governance that would serve him well in the reign that was to follow. The chronicler notes that the early preparation of Halric for the throne is one of the reasons that his reign was so stable, and the preparation is a model that the council of the kingdom has recommended to the successors of Halric.

## Accession

Halric ascended to the throne of the [Kingdom of Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael) upon the death of his father King Dorian, and the accession was marked by a ceremony that the chronicler of the court has recorded at length. The coronation was conducted in the hall of the palace at [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) in the presence of the lords of the valley, the chief judges of the realm, and the representatives of the guilds of the capital. The crown was placed upon the head of the new king by the lord high steward, and the lord high steward spoke the customary words of office. The chronicler records that the new king received the oaths of the lords and the judges with a gravity that impressed even the most sceptical of the council, and the new king spoke the customary response with a clarity that the chronicler has described as one of the most striking features of the ceremony.

The accession of Halric was followed by the customary confirmation of the governors of the provinces, and the confirmation was conducted in the manner that [Queen Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale) had established and that her successors had observed. The governors were confirmed in their offices for the customary term of years, and the confirmation was accompanied by the customary exchange of letters between the king and the governors. The chronicler notes that the confirmation of the governors is one of the foundations of the stability of the kingdom, and the confirmation has been observed by every monarch of the Vaelic dynasty since the founding of the line.

## The Long Reign

The reign of King Halric extended over a period of thirty-eight years, and the reign is one of the longest in the history of the [Kingdom of Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael). The reign was marked by a careful stewardship of the kingdom's affairs and by a close attention to the counsel of the lords of the valley and the guilds of the capital. The king maintained the institutions of the kingdom that had been established by [Queen Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale), and the code of law that the queen had instituted remained the foundation of the legal system of the realm. The chronicler records that the king's attention to the law was one of the most distinctive features of his reign, and the attention is the foundation of the reputation of the king as a ruler of measured and considered temperament.

The foreign policy of the reign was marked by a close attention to the relations with the neighbouring realms, and the treaties that had been negotiated by Halric's predecessors were renewed and extended during his reign. The king maintained the customary relations with the [Kingdom of Aldreth](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-aldreth) and the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), and the embassies that were exchanged between [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) and the courts of these neighbours were among the most regular of the diplomatic correspondences of the continent. The king did not engage in any war of conquest during his reign, and the peace that prevailed throughout his years upon the throne is in keeping with the traditions of the dynasty. The chronicler notes that the peace was not the result of a neglect of the kingdom's defences, for the king maintained the standing army and the fortifications in good order, and the peace was the result of a prudent management of the kingdom's affairs.

The economic policies that Halric pursued were designed to encourage the flow of trade across the boundaries that the kingdom's predecessors had established, and the king invested in the maintenance of the roads and the bridges that connect the provinces of the kingdom. The grain of the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale) continued to flow through the harbour of [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) to the markets of the eastern coast, and the iron of the mountain kingdoms and the cloth of the eastern cities continued to reach the towns of the interior. The chronicler records that the prosperity of the kingdom during the reign of Halric was one of the most distinctive features of the period, and the prosperity is the foundation of the reputation of the king as a ruler of considerable accomplishment.

## The Court at Vaelmouth

The court of King Halric was maintained at [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) in the palace that [Queen Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale) had built above the confluence of the rivers. The king was attended by the great officers of the household, including the lord high steward, the lord chamberlain, and the master of the horse, and the council of the king was composed of the lords of the valley, the chief judges of the realm, and the representatives of the guilds of the capital. The chronicler records that the court of Halric was among the most refined of the long succession, and the refinement is the foundation of the reputation of the king as a patron of the arts and the learning.

The queen of Halric, a lady of the great house of the eastern marches, was a patron of the religious houses of the kingdom, and the patronage was the foundation of the reputation of the queen as a woman of considerable piety. The chronicler records that the queen attended the chapel of the royal palace daily, and the queen's example was followed by the ladies of the court. The queen bore the king three children, the eldest of whom is [King Aldric of Vael](/content/people/notable/king-aldric-of-vael), the present ruler of the kingdom. The chronicler notes that the queen's influence upon the court was one of the most distinctive features of the reign, and the influence is the foundation of the reputation of the queen as a woman of considerable accomplishment.

## Family and Succession

The king was married to his queen for forty-one years, and the marriage was marked by a fidelity that the chronicler of the dynasty has recorded as one of the most distinctive features of the reign. The queen bore the king three children, two sons and a daughter, and the children were raised in the palace at [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) with the customary care of the princes of the line. The eldest son, Aldric, was invested with the governance of the western marches at the age of twenty-four, and the investment was the customary preparation of the heir for the responsibilities of the crown. The younger son was invested with the governance of a province of the kingdom, and the daughter was married into a great house of a neighbouring realm. The chronicler records that the family of the king was one of the most harmonious of the long succession, and the harmony is the foundation of the reputation of the king as a father and a husband.

The succession of the king was the subject of careful preparation in the closing years of the reign, and the preparation included the appointment of senior lords of the valley to serve as the advisors of the heir. The advisors instructed the heir in the affairs of the kingdom, and the instruction gave the heir a knowledge of the governance that would serve him well in the reign that was to follow. The chronicler records that the preparation of the succession is one of the foundations of the stability of the kingdom, and the preparation is a model that the council of the kingdom has recommended to the successors of Halric.

## The Last Years

The last years of King Halric were marked by a gradual decline in the king's health, and the decline was the subject of concern to the council of the kingdom. The king continued to attend the council and the ceremonies of the court, but the king's attendance was conducted with a care that the chronicler has described as one of the most moving features of the period. The king appointed [King Aldric of Vael](/content/people/notable/king-aldric-of-vael) to act as his representative in the affairs of the kingdom, and the appointment was the customary preparation of the heir for the responsibilities of the crown. The chronicler records that the king's care for the succession was one of the most distinctive features of the last years, and the care is the foundation of the reputation of the king as a ruler of measured and considered temperament.

King Halric died in the seventy-fourth year of his life and the thirty-eighth year of his reign, and the death was marked by a mourning that the chronicler has described as the most extensive of the long succession. The body of the king was interred in the royal tomb on the hill that overlooks the city of [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth), and the tomb is a place of pilgrimage for the people of the kingdom. The chronicler notes that the tomb is among the most distinguished of the royal tombs of the continent, and the tomb is the centre of the ceremonial observances of the kingdom upon the anniversary of the king's death.

## Significance

King Halric is the thirty-sixth monarch of the Vaelic dynasty, and the king's long reign of thirty-eight years was one of the most stable of the long succession. The institutions that Halric maintained have endured through the present age, and the institutions are the foundation of the prosperity of the [Kingdom of Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael) in the present day. The reputation of the king abroad was favourable, and the embassies that were exchanged during his reign were conducted with a smoothness that is in keeping with the traditions of the dynasty. The chronicler finds in King Halric one of the most patient and least displaced of the monarchs of the long succession, a ruler whose careful stewardship of the kingdom's affairs has been the model for the successors of Halric through the present age. The king endures, in the royal tomb on the hill above [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth), as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the kingdom that the Vaelic dynasty has sustained through the centuries.
