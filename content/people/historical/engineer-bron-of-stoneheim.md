## Overview

Engineer Bron of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) is recorded in the chronicles of the dwarven holds as the engineer who designed and oversaw the construction of the great road through the [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass). Bron was born in the upper reaches of the Stonespire in the early generations of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities), and Bron was trained as an engineer in the workshops of the senior holds before being appointed to the position of chief engineer of the road-building programme. Bron served the programme for more than thirty years, and Bron's road is the foundation of the land trade between the cold north and the warm coast.

Bron is held in particular esteem by the dwarves of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) and by the merchants of the inland valleys, and Bron's road is consulted by every merchant who must cross the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands). The chronicler notes that Bron is one of the most useful of the engineers of the present age, and Bron's road is the measure of the public spirit of the dwarven holds.

## Early Life

Bron was born into a household of miners and engineers who had served the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) for several generations, and Bron's father was a master engineer of the senior hold at the time of Bron's birth. Bron grew up among the workshops and the forges of the hold, and Bron learned the principles of stone-cutting and of road-building before Bron learned to read. Bron was sent to the engineering school of the senior hold at the customary age, and Bron excelled in the studies of geometry and of the strength of materials. Bron graduated from the school with the highest honours of Bron's year, and Bron was appointed to the position of junior engineer in the office of the chief engineer of the holds.

Bron served the office of the chief engineer for several years, and Bron designed the retaining walls and the culverts of the principal roads of the holds with care. Bron's designs were more durable than any that the office had previously produced, and Bron's designs were adopted by the holds for the use of the engineers of the road-building programme. The chronicler notes that the adoption was the foundation of Bron's reputation, and the reputation is the measure of Bron's public spirit.

## The Road

The road that Bron designed and oversaw was the work of more than twenty years, and the road was opened to traffic in the middle of Bron's service to the programme. The road crossed the [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass) in a series of cuttings that Bron's engineers had cut into the rock with care, and the road descended to the foothills of the warm coast in a long, winding course that Bron's engineers had laid out with skill. The chronicler notes that the road was the most remarkable of the works of the road-building programme, and the road was the foundation of the land trade of the three continents for several generations.

The road was received with great public approval by the magistrates and the merchants of the three continents, and the approval was expressed in the public ceremonies that the council of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) conducted to mark the opening. The ceremonies included a procession across the pass, a service in the temple of the holds, and a public reading of the inscription that Bron had composed for the principal cutting. The inscription honoured the engineers who had worked upon the road, and the inscription expressed Bron's gratitude to the magistrates who had supported the work.

## Later Life

Bron continued to serve the programme for several years after the opening of the road, and Bron supervised the maintenance of the road and the construction of the watchtowers and the quarters that the [Standing Watch of the Northern Roads](/content/government/local/standing-watch-of-the-northern-roads) required. Bron also advised the magistrates of the holds upon the construction of the new roads that the programme required, and Bron's advice was followed by the magistrates in every case. The chronicler notes that Bron's advice was the foundation of the careful management of the road, and the management is the source of much of the prosperity of the towns of the three continents.

Bron retired from the service of the programme in the seventieth year of Bron's life, and Bron spent the remaining years in the senior hold in the company of the other engineers who had been Bron's colleagues. Bron died in the eightieth year of Bron's life, and Bron was buried in the cemetery of the holds with the honours that the council had decreed. The chronicler notes that Bron was mourned by the dwarves of the holds and by the merchants of every town of the three continents, and the mourning was the foundation of the public memory of Bron's life.

## Legacy

Bron's road has been the foundation of the land trade between the cold north and the warm coast for several centuries, and the road is consulted by every merchant who must cross the Stoneheim Highlands. The road has been maintained by the watchmen of the [Standing Watch of the Northern Roads](/content/government/local/standing-watch-of-the-northern-roads) ever since, and the maintenance has been the foundation of the durability of the road. The chronicler notes that the road is the most useful of the works of the programme, and the road is the measure of the public spirit of the dwarves of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim).

The public memory of Bron is preserved in the chronicles of the holds and in the public observances that the council conducts each year on the anniversary of the opening of the road. The chronicler notes that the observances are among the most important of the public observances of the three continents, and the observances are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every town of both regions.

## Significance

Engineer Bron of Stoneheim is one of the most useful of the engineers of the present age, and Bron's road is the foundation of the land trade of the three continents for several centuries. The road, the watchtowers, the quarters, and the legacy of Bron's service to the programme are the inheritance that Bron has bequeathed to the present age. The chronicler notes that Bron is held in particular memory by the dwarves of the holds and by the merchants of the three continents, and Bron is the measure by which the magistrates and the councils of the three continents judge the public acts of their own day.
