## The Magistrate of the Greywash Compact

Oren Greywash was the magistrate who negotiated the [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact). Born into a household of magistrates in a small town of the upper river in the generations before the treaty, Oren trained in the law of the river before being appointed magistrate of the principal district. Oren served the council of the river towns for more than thirty years, and the treaty became the foundation of the public order of the river for several centuries.

Before the Compact, the Greywash was a source of constant dispute among the towns along its course. Each town levied its own customs dues on vessels passing through its stretch of the river, and the multiplicity of charges made the transport of goods both expensive and unpredictable. Merchants could not calculate the cost of a journey in advance because the dues varied from town to town and were subject to change without notice. The resulting uncertainty discouraged trade and kept the towns of the upper river poorer than their location on a navigable waterway should have allowed.

Oren is held in particular esteem by the magistrates and merchants of the cold north. The treaty is consulted by every magistrate who must judge a dispute upon the river. Oren is regarded as one of the most useful magistrates of the present age, and the treaty is considered a measure of the public spirit of the magistrates of the river towns.

## Brought Up among River Law

Oren was born into a household of magistrates who had served the river towns for several generations. Oren's father was a magistrate of the upper district at the time of Oren's birth. Oren grew up among the courts and records of the river towns, learning the customs and practices of the river before learning to read. The family's collection of river agreements and dispute records, accumulated over generations, gave Oren a detailed knowledge of the precedents that had shaped the legal traditions of the Greywash.

Sent to the academy of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) at the customary age, Oren excelled in the study of law and river agreements, graduating with the highest honours of the year and being appointed junior magistrate in the court of the principal district. The academy of Vassholm was known for its rigorous training in the interpretation of customary law, and its faculty included scholars who had studied the legal systems of the southern continents as well as the traditions of the cold north.

Oren served the court for several years, compiling records of disputes adjudicated that were more complete than any the court had previously produced. These records were adopted by the [Vintners and Cellarers](/content/government/local/vintners-and-cellarers) for use by the magistrates of the region, establishing Oren's reputation as a magistrate of unusual thoroughness.

## Reconciling the Upper and Lower Towns

The negotiations that produced the [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact) were the most important undertaking of Oren's career, occupying the middle years of service to the council. The negotiations were opened by the magistrates of the principal towns along the river, and Oren was appointed to represent them in discussions with the mediators of the academies. The mediators were scholars of the academies of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) who brought to the negotiations knowledge of the traditions the parties had observed for several centuries.

The central difficulty of the negotiations was the need to reconcile the interests of the upper towns, which depended on the river for access to the markets of the coast, with those of the lower towns, which had grown accustomed to the revenue from customs dues. Oren proposed a compromise in which all customs dues along the river would be abolished and replaced by a single small transit fee collected at the river's mouth. The revenue from this fee would be distributed among all the towns in proportion to their length of riverbank, providing each with a predictable income while removing the burden of multiple charges from the merchants.

Oren worked in concert with the mediators, helping to draft the compromise that abolished the customs dues and replaced them with the small transit fee that the treaty established. Oren's contribution to the compromise was the foundation of the success of the negotiations.

## The Public Ceremony at the Confluence

The treaty was signed in a public ceremony at the confluence of the Greywash, with Oren signing for the magistrates of the upper river. The ceremony was attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every town of the river and included a procession upon the river and a service in the temples. The ceremony was a measure of the public spirit of the magistrates and merchants of both parties.

Oren delivered the principal address at the ceremony, recorded in the chronicles of the academies as one of the finest public speeches of the present age. The address honoured the mediators who had helped draft the compromise and expressed Oren's gratitude to the magistrates who had supported the negotiations.

## Supervising the Treaty's Implementation

Oren continued to serve the council for several years after the signing of the treaty, supervising its operation and the appointment of the river warden that the treaty had established. The river warden was given authority to enforce the terms of the treaty, to adjudicate disputes that arose under its provisions, and to collect the transit fee and distribute its revenue. Oren also advised the magistrates of the upper river on measures required for its operation, and Oren's advice was followed in every case. This advice was the foundation of the careful management of the treaty, which contributed much to the prosperity of the towns of the river by creating a predictable and stable environment for trade.

Oren retired from the service of the council in the seventieth year of life and spent the remaining years in the academy of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) in the company of other magistrates. Oren died in the eightieth year and was buried in the cemetery of the academy with the honours decreed by the council. Oren was mourned by the magistrates and merchants of every town of the river.

## A Model for Other Waterways

Oren's treaty has been the foundation of the public order of the river for several centuries and is consulted by every magistrate who must judge a dispute upon the river. The treaty has been revised from time to time by the magistrates of the towns, with revisions entered in the copies kept by the magistrates. The treaty is the most useful public instrument of the cold north and a measure of the public spirit of the magistrates of the river. The model that the Compact established has been studied by magistrates of other regions and has influenced the negotiation of similar agreements on other waterways.

Public memory of Oren is preserved in the chronicles of the academy and in annual observances conducted by the magistrates on the anniversary of the signing of the treaty. These observances are among the most important of the cold north, attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every town of the river.

## Oren's Place among Magistrates

Oren Greywash is remembered as one of the most useful magistrates of the present age. The treaty, the records of disputes, and the legacy of service to the council constitute the inheritance bequeathed to the present age. Oren is held in particular memory by the magistrates and merchants of the cold north, and Oren is the measure by which the magistrates and councils of the river towns judge the public acts of their own day.
