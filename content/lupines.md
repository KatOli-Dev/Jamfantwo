## Origins

The origins of the lupines are bound up with the cold northern steppes of the world, for the lupine body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by open, cold country, by the wind, and by the demands of a nomadic, predatory life in a harsh environment. Lupines are a wolf-headed people, found in the cold steppes of [Morvind](/content/morvind) and the northern reaches of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), and their form combines a humanoid body with the head, the fur, and the senses of a wolf. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between lupines and [salawa](/content/salawa), citing the canine-headed form shared by both peoples and their preference for open, marginal terrain, though the lupines are larger, more cold-adapted, and more social than the solitary salawa. Others have argued for a connection to [ursines](/content/ursines), pointing to the shared northern habitat and the partly animal form, but the lupine body is too lean and too different in build for the comparison to be sustained. The lupines themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their having emerged from the wind and the grass of the first steppe, and they regard the cold northern plains as their rightful home.

What is certain is that lupines have inhabited the cold northern steppes of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands is attested in the oldest records of the peoples who border the steppe. The lupines and the [humans](/content/humans) of the Morvind coast have coexisted for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the overlap in their range and the difference in their preferred terrain.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Lupines are a medium to large people, standing comparable in height to [humans](/content/humans) or slightly taller, with a lean and wiry frame that is built for endurance, speed, and the sustained pursuit of prey across open ground. The head is that of a wolf, with a long, broad muzzle, a large nose, and sharp, forward-facing eyes that are adapted to the low light of the northern latitudes. The ears are large, pointed, and mobile, capable of swivelling toward the source of a sound, and they are covered in short fur that ranges from grey to black, white, and combinations of these. The body is humanoid from the neck down, with lean musculature and long limbs, and the whole form is built for the sustained, steady effort that the steppe environment demands.

The lupine body is superbly adapted to the cold, open environment of the northern steppe. Their fur is thick and double-layered, with a dense undercoat that provides insulation against the cold and a longer outer coat that sheds snow and wind, and the fur ranges from grey to black, white, and mottled combinations of these. Their skin is dark and cold-tolerant, and their metabolism is efficient, capable of sustaining long periods of exertion in conditions where the temperature is far below freezing. Lupine hands are strong and capable, with thick, calloused fingers and blunt nails that are used for the manipulation of tools and the working of materials.

The lupine sense of smell is among the keenest of any sapient people, rivalled only by that of the [ursines](/content/ursines), and the lupine can track by scent across the open steppe with a reliability that no other people can match, following a trail that is hours or even days old. Their hearing is acute, and their large, mobile ears can detect the faintest sound in the still air of the steppe, from the movement of prey at a great distance to the approach of weather that no eye can see. Their vision is keen, particularly in low light, and their eyes are adapted to the long twights and the short days of the northern latitudes, performing well in the dim light of the winter afternoon.

## Life Cycle

Lupines are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of sixty to ninety years under favourable conditions. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late teens. Lupine women carry their young for approximately seven months before giving birth, and births are frequently multiple, with litters of two to four being common. The young are small and helpless at birth, and they require several years of care before they are capable of keeping pace with the community, but they grow quickly and reach adolescence within a decade.

Lupine populations grow steadily when prey is abundant and the steppe is at peace, and the frequency of multiple births ensures that populations can recover quickly from losses. The community is closely knit and co-operative, and the young are raised by the entire group, with all adults sharing in their care, protection, and training. The elders of a lupine community are those who have maintained their strength and their senses into later life, and their knowledge of the steppe, its prey, and its seasonal changes is the foundation of the community's survival.

## Habitat and Distribution

Lupines are found chiefly in the cold steppes of [Morvind](/content/morvind), where the open, grassy plains of the interior provide the terrain and the space that their nomadic way of life requires. They are the dominant people of the cold steppe, and their communities range across the full extent of the interior plains, from the foothills of the coastal ranges to the edges of the deep interior. The [geography](/content/geography) of the steppe, with its open terrain and its harsh climate, is well suited to the lupine form, and the lupines have shaped the steppe through their movements and their management of the grazing herds.

Smaller populations of lupines are found in the northern reaches of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), where the open moorlands and the edges of the great forests provide a similar if less extreme environment, and in the cold uplands of the Morvind interior, where the terrain is higher and the climate is harsher. Lupines are absent from the warm regions, the forests, and the coasts, for their bodies are adapted to cold and open ground, and they are uncomfortable in the heat and the close air of the woodland. The lupine range is the most northerly of any people save the [goliaths](/content/goliaths) and the [giants](/content/giants), and the lupines are the most widely distributed of the cold-steppe peoples.

## Diet

The lupine diet is dominated by meat, and lupines are formidable hunters of the steppe environment they inhabit. They prey upon the large grazing mammals of the plains, including the wild horses, the elk, and the reindeer that share their range, and their hunting is characterised by the sustained pursuit that is the hallmark of the canine predator. Lupines are endurance hunters, capable of following their quarry for many miles at a steady pace until the prey is exhausted, and the co-ordinated efforts of the community allow them to bring down animals that are far larger and stronger than any individual lupine.

Plant foods form a minor part of the diet, though lupines will eat berries, roots, and the occasional fruits that grow along the watercourses of the steppe. Lupines do not practise agriculture, and their relationship with food is entirely that of the predator, for their digestion is specialised for a diet of meat and poorly suited to the processing of large quantities of plant material. Water is obtained from the streams and pools of the steppe, and from the melting of snow during the winter months, when the liquid water is scarce and the lupines obtain moisture from the flesh of their prey.

## Ecology

Lupines occupy the niche of a medium-bodied, social predator in the cold steppe ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on the ecology of the plains is considerable, for the lupines are the primary predators of the large grazing mammals, and their hunting regulates the populations of the herds that share the steppe. The lupine management of the grazing herds is more deliberate than that of other predators, for the lupines are aware of the relationship between the health of the herds and the survival of their own communities, and their hunting is governed by a practical understanding of the balance between predator and prey.

Lupines share the cold steppe with [orcs](/content/orcs), and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by competition for the same game. The orcs, as ambush predators, and the lupines, as endurance hunters, occupy overlapping but distinct niches, and their paths cross with occasional tension but rarely with open conflict. [Humans](/content/humans) encounter lupines at the margins of the settled country, where the steppe meets the coast, and the relationship is generally wary, for the lupine predator and the human settler have little in common and much to be cautious of.
