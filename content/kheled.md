## Overview

Kheled is an upland settlement in the desert massifs of the [Kharesh](/content/kharesh), the vast desert interior of [Sahrana](/content/sahrana). The settlement stands in a sheltered valley of one of the rocky uplands that rise from the desert floor, where springs fed by deep aquifers sustain a community that is among the most isolated and the most self-sufficient in the known world. Kheled is small but enduring, and its people are among the most adapted to the demands of the upland desert environment, where the heat of the day gives way to the cold of the night and the resources are scarce but sufficient for a people who know the land.

## The Massif and the Springs

The massif in which Kheled is situated is one of the rocky uplands that rise from the sands of the Kharesh, a remnant of an ancient landscape that the desert has otherwise buried. The massif is a complex of bare summits and sheltered valleys, and the springs that emerge at the base of the upland are the source of the water that sustains the settlement. The springs are fed by the deep aquifers that lie beneath the desert, and the flow is constant and reliable, unaffected by the seasonal variations in rainfall. The water is managed carefully, for it must sustain the settlement and the gardens that are cultivated in the terraced plots around the springs.

The valley in which Kheled stands is sheltered from the wind and the sand by the walls of the massif, and the climate here is more moderate than the open desert, for the rock of the upland absorbs the heat of the day and releases it slowly during the night, reducing the extremes of temperature that characterise the desert floor. The gardens and the terraced plots that are cultivated in the valley produce the dates, the figs, and the other fruits that are the primary food source of the settlement, and the surplus is traded to the caravans that pass through the massif.

## The Mines and the Minerals

The massif of Kheled is rich in minerals, including copper, salt, and the precious stones that are among the most valued products of Sahrana, and the mining of these materials is a significant part of the settlement's economy. The mines are worked on a small scale, for the deposits are not extensive, but the quality of the minerals is high, and the copper, the salt, and the stones that are extracted from the massif are traded to the caravans that pass through the Kharesh, where they command high prices in the markets of the coast and the northern continents.

The salt that is mined from the massif is particularly valued, for the salt of the Kharesh is among the purest in the known world, and the salt trade is a significant source of income for the settlement. The salt is extracted from the deposits that lie beneath the massif, and it is transported by the caravans to the markets of the coast, where it is exchanged for the grain, the cloth, and the manufactured goods that the settlement cannot produce for itself. The copper that is mined from the massif is likewise valued, for the copper of Kheled is of a quality that is prized by the metalworkers of the coast, and the demand for the copper is a constant feature of the trade between the settlement and the caravans.

The precious stones that are found in the massif are among the rarest products of the Sahrani interior, and the stones that are extracted from the mines of Kheled include the carnelians, the agates, and the occasional sardonyx that are sought by the jewellers of the coastal cities. The stones are found in the deepest workings of the massif, and the mining of them is a labour that requires patience and skill, for the veins are narrow and the extraction must be conducted with care to preserve the integrity of the crystals. The stones that are recovered are traded to the caravans, and the prices that they command are a significant supplement to the income of the settlement.

## History

The settlement of Kheled was established in antiquity, and the founders of the community were the human prospectors who discovered the springs and the mineral deposits of the massif during the early exploration of the Kharesh. The springs were the initial attraction, for water is the most precious resource in the deep desert, and the presence of a reliable water source in the massif was the foundation upon which the settlement was built. The mining of the minerals followed, and the community grew as the value of the copper, the salt, and the stones became known to the caravan masters who traversed the desert.

The relationship between Kheled and the [Vashari Pact](/content/vashari-pact) was established in the early centuries of the settlement's history, and the pact provided the caravans and the trade connections that sustained the community in exchange for the minerals and the provisions that the settlement produced. The dependency of Kheled on the pact is a practical arrangement, for the settlement is too small and too remote to maintain an independent trade network, and the caravans of the pact are the only reliable connection between Kheled and the markets of the coast.

## The Settlement

Kheled is a small, compact settlement, and its buildings are constructed of the stone of the massif, with thick walls and small windows that provide insulation against the heat and the cold. The dwellings are built into the sides of the valley, and the streets are steep and narrow, connected by paths and stairs that are cut into the rock. The population of Kheled is small and entirely [human](/content/humans), and the people are known for their endurance and their deep attachment to the upland that is their home.

The community of Kheled is organised around the management of the springs and the mines, and the affairs of the settlement are governed by a council of elders who are drawn from the families that have inhabited the massif for generations. The council oversees the distribution of the water, the rotation of the mining shifts, and the negotiation of the trade with the caravans that pass through the massif. The governance of Kheled is informal but effective, and the council is the primary institution of the community, for the settlement is too small to require a more elaborate administration.

The people of Kheled are distinguished by their knowledge of the upland desert and their skill in the extraction of the minerals that the massif provides. The miners of Kheled are among the most experienced in the Kharesh, and their techniques for the working of the narrow veins and the preservation of the precious stones are passed from generation to generation. The gardeners of the settlement are likewise skilled, for the cultivation of the terraced plots in the valley requires a careful management of the water and the soil, and the dates and the figs that are produced in the gardens are among the finest in the Sahrani interior.

## Significance

Kheled is the most important upland settlement in the [Kharesh](/content/kharesh), and its minerals and its provisions are a valued resource for the caravans that cross the desert. The settlement is a dependency of the [Vashari Pact](/content/vashari-pact), the alliance of the inland caravan cities, and its role in the mineral trade is a significant component of the economy of the desert interior. The significance of Kheled lies in its minerals and its springs, which are among the most valuable resources in the deep desert, and the settlement is a testament to the capacity of humans to extract a living from the most demanding environments in the known world.

The isolation of Kheled is both a challenge and a protection, for the massif that shelters the settlement also separates it from the conflicts and the rivalries of the wider world. The community has endured for centuries, and the springs and the mines that sustained the founders of the settlement continue to sustain their descendants, and the traditions of the upland desert are preserved in the practices and the customs of the people who call Kheled their home.
