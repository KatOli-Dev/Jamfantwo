## Overview

Kharoshi is the common tongue of [Kharos](/content/location/natural/continent/kharos), the broad western landmass whose coasts and interior are divided by a long belt of red mesas, dry valleys, and the mineral-bright woodland of the famous glass groves. The language is most closely associated with the human population of the eastern harbours and the highland valleys, but the chronicler records that Kharoshi is spoken as a first language by the [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) communities of the mineral-rich foothills and as a second language by the [gnome](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) communities of the glass groves and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the western maritime trade. The name is taken from the continent itself, and the language is sometimes called the red-stone speech by the coastal communities of the east, who treat it as the medium of the highland commerce rather than as a learned accomplishment.

The origins of Kharoshi lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the eastern harbours, and the language has grown over the centuries through contact with the dwarven speech of the highland mines, the gnomish speech of the glass groves, and the trade tongues that arrive by sea from the ports of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) across the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean). The chronicler has traced two distinct waves of borrowing, each of which has left a layer of vocabulary and a small set of grammatical habits that the eastern-harbour speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable strength, suited to the description of the red stone, the minerals, the glass, and the patient business of the long western trade, and capable of being adapted to the specialised vocabularies of the miner, the glass-blower, and the counting house of the eastern harbours.

## Sounds

Kharoshi is spoken in a register that is more clipped and more deliberate than the common speech of the heartland of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and the chronicler notes that eastern visitors often remark upon the brevity of the harbour sentences and the willingness of the harbour speakers to leave unsaid what the eastern custom would require to be set out at length. The consonants of Kharoshi are sharply distinguished, the vowels are few and clear, and the rhythm of the sentence is carried by the regular accent that falls upon the first syllable of most words of more than one syllable. The chronicler records that the harbour singers use the consonantal range to produce the striking percussive effects that mark the songs of the mining festivals, and the songs are one of the most distinctive of the cultural expressions of the highland communities. The pitch of the voice is treated by the harbour speakers as a matter of custom rather than of meaning, and the chronicler has observed that the harbour speakers tend to speak with a flatter pitch than the highland speakers, in unconscious imitation of the working register of the mine and the forge.

The script of Kharoshi is alphabetic, with twenty-nine marks that represent the consonants and vowels of the language. The script is read from left to right in horizontal lines, and the lines are arranged from the top of the page to the bottom. The script is descended from the angular signs that the dwarven stonemasons cut into the lintels of the highland mines, and the chronicler has identified a small number of borrowings from the marks used by the earliest human chroniclers of the eastern harbours in the signs used for the sounds that the older dwarven script did not distinguish. The marks are incised into stone or metal for the records of the highland mines, and painted or printed upon parchment for the correspondence of the eastern harbours, and the writing is taught in the schools of the larger highland settlements to children of every species who require it for the business of the highland trade. The chronicler has noted that the script of the [Ember Ridge](/content/location/natural/feature/ember-ridge) is distinguished by a set of decorative marks that the ridge scribes add to the headings of the mineral tallies, and the marks are treated by the highland speakers as a sign of the antiquity of the mines.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Kharoshi is rich in words for the red stone, the minerals, the glass, the ores, the tools, and the patient business of the highland trade, and the chronicler records that the language has more words for the grades of stone, the kinds of glass, the kinds of ore, and the stages of the miner's art than any of the languages of the eastern harbours. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the workshop, the clan, and the customary relations of the highland communities, for the highland populations place great weight upon the distinctions of the mining clans and the long memory of the [Ember Ridge](/content/location/natural/feature/ember-ridge). The chronicler notes that the highland compounds are formed by the joining of two or more of the simple words, and the compounds are used in the formal speech of the clan councils and the songs of the mineral festivals.

Kharoshi has borrowed from the dwarven speech of the highland mines a set of words for the metals, the stones, the ores, and the tools of the workshop that the harbour communities have required for the conduct of the highland trade. The borrowings are recognised by the harbour speakers as belonging to the trade of the miner rather than to the craft of the merchant, and the chronicler records that the harbour speakers are careful to use the dwarven word in the dwarven context and the harbour word in the harbour context. The language has also borrowed a small number of words from the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) trade tongue through the ships that cross the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean), and the borrowings are chiefly the names of the eastern goods, the eastern customs, and the eastern offices that the harbour merchants have required for the conduct of the long-distance trade.

## Speech

Kharoshi is the language of the great mines of the [Ember Ridge](/content/location/natural/feature/ember-ridge), and the mines are worked in a register that is precise and patient, suited to the long observation of the veins and the careful timing of the breaking seasons. The mine register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a shift, the inspection of a face, and the closing of a haul, and the phrases are known to every miner of the principal mines. The chronicler has observed that the phrases are intelligible to children of eleven or twelve years who have been brought up in the mining families, and the intelligibility is a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from parent to child in the highland communities.

Kharoshi is also the language of the harbour councils of the eastern communities, and the councils are conducted in a more formal register that is preserved by the elders of the larger harbours and the principal mining clans of the highland valleys. The formal register is used for the public announcements of the harbour magistrates, the reading of the customary law, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and the register is intelligible to any speaker of Kharoshi who has had the benefit of a customary education in the mine or the council. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the highland interior, and the borrowings are treated by the highland speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The chronicler has noted that the formal register is used with care in the chambers of the principal harbours, and the care is a measure of the importance that the highland communities attach to the conduct of public business in a language that the miners and the merchants understand and trust.

## Relations

Kharoshi stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions that surround [Kharos](/content/location/natural/continent/kharos), and the relations are a measure of the long history of contact and exchange that has shaped the highland communities. The chronicler records that Kharoshi is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the gnomish speech of the glass groves, and the intelligibility is greater in the highland towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Kharoshi and the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) of the heartland of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) is the subject of scholarly dispute, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another through the long ocean routes, and the chronicler has been told by the highland scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the harbour and the eastern merchants at the far ports.

The relation of Kharoshi to the dwarven speech of the highland mines is the subject of some scholarly interest, for the two languages share a long history of contact across the mineral-rich foothills, and the chronicler has been told by the highland scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the human and the dwarven communities. The chronicler records that the highland speakers tend to treat the dwarven speech as a learned accomplishment rather than as a working language, and the dwarven communities of the mines tend to treat Kharoshi as the necessary medium of the highland trade. The relation of Kharoshi to the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) trade tongue is closer in vocabulary than in grammar, and the chronicler has been told by the harbour merchants that the eastern speech lacks the precision of the highland tongue in the description of the metals and the stones.

## Significance

Kharoshi is the common tongue of [Kharos](/content/location/natural/continent/kharos), the language of the great mines and the harbour councils, the medium of the long western trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to dwell or to trade among the red mesas and the eastern harbours of the broad western landmass. The language is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary of its own, and it has been refined over the centuries by the long practice of the highland miners and the patient correction of the harbour councils. The chronicler finds in Kharoshi one of the most precise of the languages of the known world, and the language is a measure of the central place that the highland mines have held in the mineral trade and the harbour customs of the western continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the eastern coast. The language is the foundation of the highland identity, and the highland identity is the foundation of the mining community, and the two together are the measure of the long attention that the highland communities have given to the conduct of the mine in a tongue that the miners and the merchants understand and trust.
