## Overview

Quessan is the spoken language of the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the smaller elven communities of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the western forests, used for the ordinary commerce of life and for the preservation of elven traditions and learning. The name is the elves' own, older than any elven settlement catalogued. Quessan is distinct from the [Sylvan](/content/language/pseudo/sylvan) signals, a code of limited scope used only for the hunt, the walk, and the stealthy approach through the forest. Quessan is a true language with grammar and vocabulary refined over many generations, the language of the hearth, the song of the festival, and the long conversation of the elven councils.

Quessan is treated by the elven communities as a trust rather than a possession, transmitted from parent to child with unusual care. The elven elders maintain the forms of the language in oral tradition, and the forms are revised only when a generation has had time to weigh the change against the older usage. The result is a language of great stability. The forms of Quessan recorded in the earliest elven chronicles are recognisable in the speech of the present day, though elven scholars note the differences in pronunciation and vocabulary that centuries have introduced.

## The Sounds and the Script

Quessan is spoken in a register quieter than the common speech of surrounding human communities. Elves are noted for their evenness of tone and deliberation of articulation. The language has a wide range of vowel sounds, and the vowels carry the rhythm of the sentence in a manner the consonants of common human speech do not. The consonants are few in number, and the elves rely upon the vowels to distinguish words that would otherwise be confused in rapid speech. Elven singers use the vowel range to produce the long sustained notes of elven song, one of the most distinctive cultural expressions of the elven people.

The script of Quessan is a set of curved marks incised into the soft bark of certain trees of the deep forest, read in long horizontal lines running from the base of the trunk to the first branch. The marks are not letters in the human sense, for each mark represents a syllable rather than a single sound. The order of the marks in the line is read in a particular direction determined by the orientation of the tree; no rule for orientation holds across all inscriptions, and elven scholars treat it as a matter of custom rather than rule. The marks are cut with a small blade of bronze or stone, shallow enough that the bark grows over it in a generation, leaving the inscription to be renewed by the keeper of the tree.

## The Vocabulary and the Compounds

The vocabulary of Quessan is rich in words for the things of the forest and the long observations of elven scholars. The language has more words for the kinds of tree, the kinds of leaf, the kinds of bird song, and the kinds of shadow than any of the human languages of the known world, a measure of the long attention the elven people have given to the life of the woodland. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the relations of kinship, for the elven communities place great weight upon the distinctions of descent and marriage, preserved in a set of compound terms that human scholars find difficult to translate. Elven compounds are formed by joining two or more simple words, used in the formal speech of councils and festivals.

The vocabulary of Quessan is also notable for what it lacks. The language has few words for the instruments of war, and elven scholars remark upon the absence as a feature of the language rather than a defect. The elven people do not require the words because they do not require the things, though elven communities have on occasion taken up arms in defence of their forests, and elven warriors use borrowed words of human speech for the instruments they employ.

## The Speech of the Councils and the Festivals

Quessan is the language of the elven councils, conducted in a formal register preserved by the elders of the community. The formal register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening and closing of proceedings, inherited from earlier generations. The formal register is also used for public announcements, spoken from customary platforms in the market squares of elven settlements. The formal register is intelligible to elven children of seven or eight years, a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted.

The festivals of the elven communities are also conducted in Quessan, with songs sung in the formal register. A festival song may last for the better part of a day, with verses taken up in turn by the singers of the community. The songs are not narrative in the manner of human songs — they are concerned with the recurrence of the seasons and the long memory of the community — and the verses are repeated with small variations from year to year. The songs of the festivals are the most complete of the oral records of the elven communities, preserving the forms of Quessan through the centuries.

## The Relation to the Spoken Tongues of the Other Peoples

Quessan is distinct from the [Sylvan](/content/language/pseudo/sylvan) signals of the forest, but the language has borrowed a small number of words from the human speech of surrounding communities. The borrowed words are chiefly the names of new objects and practices the elven communities have taken up through contact with human settlements, adapted to the phonology of Quessan by the addition of vowels the elven speech requires. The elven communities do not borrow from the human speech in the opposite direction; elven scholars treat the absence of borrowing as a point of pride.

The relation of Quessan to the elven speech of the southern forests and the elven speech beyond the mountains is the subject of scholarly dispute. Elven scholars maintain that the differences are matters of dialect rather than language. A speaker of Quessan from the forests of Aethelar can make himself understood to a speaker from the southern forests after a short period of adjustment, chiefly a matter of vocabulary rather than grammar. The extent of elven speech beyond the known world has not been determined; elven scholars remark that the question is not one the elven people are inclined to answer.

## Significance

Quessan is the language of the elven people, the medium of elven councils, the songs of elven festivals, and the long conversation of elven hearths. It is a true language with grammar and vocabulary of its own, transmitted with exemplary care. The elven people have maintained Quessan through the centuries by the patient practice of the elders and the listening of the young, and the language is the foundation of elven identity and elven memory. It is one of the most stable and carefully preserved of the languages of the known world, a measure of the long attention the elven people have given to the life of the forest and to relations among elven communities.
