## Overview

Frostic is the speech of the interior of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim), used in the Frostwall Tundra, the broad plateaus, and the sheltered valleys of the [Whitewall Range](/content/location/natural/feature/whitewall-range) that divide the northern continent. The language is most closely associated with the human communities of the tundra and the highland valleys. Frostic is spoken as a first language by the [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) communities of the high passes and the mountain refuges and as a second language by the [lupine](/content/species/sapient/lupines) communities of the cold steppes and by every traveller who requires a working knowledge of the tundra routes. The name is taken from the long walls of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) that guard the northern edge of the habitable country, and the language is sometimes called the tundra speech by the coastal communities of the south.

The origins of Frostic lie in the speech of the earliest human communities of the tundra and the highland valleys. The language has grown over the centuries through contact with the dwarven speech of the mountain refuges, the speech of the lupine communities of the cold steppes, and the trade tongues that arrive overland from the southern coast. Two distinct waves of borrowing have left layers of vocabulary and grammatical habits that tundra speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable gravity, suited to the description of snow, ice, the long winter, and the patient business of the refuge, and capable of adaptation to the specialised vocabularies of the guide, the trapper, and the council chamber of the highland communities.

## Sounds

Frostic is spoken in a register that is deeper and more deliberate than the common speech of the southern coast. Coastal visitors often remark upon the slowness of the tundra speech and the weight speakers place upon the closing consonants of every word. The consonants are numerous, and the language distinguishes several sounds that the coastal speech has merged, particularly in the cluster of voiced stops occurring at the end of many tundra words. The vowels are fewer than in the coastal speech. Tundra singers use the consonantal range to produce the long sustained notes of the winter songs, one of the most distinctive cultural expressions of the tundra communities. Pitch is a matter of custom rather than meaning; tundra speakers tend to speak with a lower pitch than coastal speakers, in unconscious imitation of the resonance of the long winters.

The script of Frostic is runic, with twenty-six marks representing not single sounds but the consonant clusters and vowel groups that are the basic units of the spoken language. The script is read from left to right in horizontal lines. It is descended from the marks the earliest tundra chroniclers cut into the frozen wood of the highland caches, with borrowings from the dwarven script of the mountain refuges in the marks used for sounds the older tundra script did not distinguish. The marks are incised into wood, bone, or stone for records of the tundra communities, and painted or printed upon parchment for correspondence of the highland valleys. Writing is taught in schools of the larger highland settlements to children of every species who require it for the business of the tundra trade. The script of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) is distinguished by decorative marks added to the headings of genealogies, treated by tundra speakers as a sign of the antiquity of the refuge halls.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Frostic is rich in words for snow, ice, the long winter, the tundra, the highland pastures, and the patient business of the refuge. The language has more words for the kinds of snow, the kinds of ice, the kinds of pasture, and the stages of the trapper's art than any of the languages of the southern coast. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the clan, the lineage, and the customary relations of kinship that govern the tundra communities, preserved in a set of compound terms that coastal scholars find difficult to translate. Tundra compounds are formed by joining two or more simple words, used in the formal speech of clan councils and the songs of winter festivals.

Frostic has borrowed from the dwarven speech of the mountain refuges a set of words for metals, stones, ores, and tools of the workshop required for the construction of refuge halls and the repair of highland equipment. These borrowings are recognised as belonging to the trade of the smith rather than the craft of the trapper, and tundra speakers are careful to use the dwarven word in the dwarven context and the tundra word in the tundra context. A small number of words have also been borrowed from the [Nordhal](/content/language/spoken/nordhal) of the southern coast through overland trade, chiefly the names of southern goods, customs, and offices.

## Speech

Frostic is the language of the great refuges of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches), conducted in a register that is formal and measured, suited to the long deliberations of clan councils and patient negotiation of matters brought before them during the long winters. The refuge register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a council, the addressing of an elder, and the closing of a deliberation, known to every member of the principal clans. These phrases are intelligible to children of twelve or thirteen years brought up in the refuges.

Frostic is also the language of the long-winter councils of the tundra communities, conducted in a still more formal register preserved by the elders of the principal clans and refuge halls of the [Whitewall Range](/content/location/natural/feature/whitewall-range). The formal register is used for public announcements of clan magistrates, the reading of customary law, and the debates of larger assemblies, intelligible to any speaker of Frostic who has had a customary education in the refuge or workshop. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the highland valleys, treated as marks of dignity and authority.

## Relations

Frostic stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions surrounding the interior of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim). It is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the coastal [Nordhal](/content/language/spoken/nordhal) of the southern belt, and intelligibility is greater in foothill towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Frostic and the speech of the [lupine](/content/species/sapient/lupines) communities of the cold steppes is the subject of scholarly dispute; the two languages share a small vocabulary generally understood to be evidence of an old coexistence, and tundra scholars describe the relationship as one of long neighbours rather than kinship.

The relation of Frostic to the dwarven speech of the mountain refuges draws scholarly interest, for the two languages have borrowed vocabulary from one another over the centuries, a measure of long coexistence. Tundra speakers tend to treat dwarven speech as a learned accomplishment rather than a working language, and dwarven communities of the refuges treat Frostic as the necessary medium of the long-winter trade. The relation of Frostic to the trade tongues arriving from the south is closer in vocabulary than in grammar; tundra merchants note that the southern speech lacks the precision of the tundra tongue in describing the long winters and highland pastures.

## Significance

Frostic is the speech of the interior of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim), the language of the great refuges and the long-winter councils, the medium of the tundra trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who dwells or travels among the Frostwall Tundra and the highland valleys of the [Whitewall Range](/content/location/natural/feature/whitewall-range). It is a true language with grammar and vocabulary of its own, refined over centuries by the long practice of tundra clansmen and the patient correction of the workshops. It is one of the most resonant of the languages of the known world, a measure of the central place the highland refuges have held in the metalwork and governance of the northern continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the sheltered valleys of the interior.
