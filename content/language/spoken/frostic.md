## Overview

Frostic is the speech of the interior of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim), used in the Frostwall Tundra, the broad plateaus, and the sheltered valleys of the [Whitewall Range](/content/location/natural/feature/whitewall-range) that divide the northern continent. The language is most closely associated with the human communities of the tundra and the highland valleys, but the chronicler records that Frostic is spoken as a first language by the [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) communities of the high passes and the mountain refuges and as a second language by the [lupine](/content/species/sapient/lupines) communities of the cold steppes and by every traveller who requires a working knowledge of the tundra routes. The name is taken from the long walls of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) that guard the northern edge of the habitable country, and the language is sometimes called the tundra speech by the coastal communities of the south, who treat it as the medium of the inland commerce rather than as a learned accomplishment.

The origins of Frostic lie in the speech of the earliest human communities of the tundra and the highland valleys, and the language has grown over the centuries through contact with the dwarven speech of the mountain refuges, the speech of the lupine communities of the cold steppes, and the trade tongues that arrive overland from the southern coast. The chronicler has traced two distinct waves of borrowing, each of which has left a layer of vocabulary and a small set of grammatical habits that the tundra speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable gravity, suited to the description of the snow, the ice, the long winter, and the patient business of the refuge, and capable of being adapted to the specialised vocabularies of the guide, the trapper, and the council chamber of the highland communities.

## Sounds

Frostic is spoken in a register that is deeper and more deliberate than the common speech of the southern coast, and the chronicler notes that coastal visitors often remark upon the slowness of the tundra speech and the weight that the tundra speakers place upon the closing consonants of every word. The consonants of Frostic are numerous, and the language distinguishes a number of sounds that the coastal speech has merged, particularly in the cluster of voiced stops that occur at the end of many tundra words. The vowels are fewer than in the coastal speech, and the chronicler records that the tundra singers use the consonantal range to produce the long sustained notes of the winter songs, and the songs are one of the most distinctive of the cultural expressions of the tundra communities. The pitch of the voice is treated by the tundra speakers as a matter of custom rather than of meaning, and the chronicler has observed that the tundra speakers tend to speak with a lower pitch than the coastal speakers, in unconscious imitation of the resonance of the long winters.

The script of Frostic is runic, with twenty-six marks that represent not single sounds but the consonant clusters and vowel groups that the chronicler has identified as the basic units of the spoken language. The script is read from left to right in horizontal lines, and the lines are arranged from the top of the page to the bottom. The script is descended from the marks that the earliest tundra chroniclers cut into the frozen wood of the highland caches, and the chronicler has identified a small number of borrowings from the dwarven script of the mountain refuges in the marks used for the sounds that the older tundra script did not distinguish. The marks are incised into wood, bone, or stone for the records of the tundra communities, and painted or printed upon parchment for the correspondence of the highland valleys, and the writing is taught in the schools of the larger highland settlements to children of every species who require it for the business of the tundra trade. The chronicler has noted that the script of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) is distinguished by a set of decorative marks that the refuge scribes add to the headings of the genealogies, and the marks are treated by the tundra speakers as a sign of the antiquity of the refuge halls.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Frostic is rich in words for the snow, the ice, the long winter, the tundra, the highland pastures, and the patient business of the refuge, and the chronicler records that the language has more words for the kinds of snow, the kinds of ice, the kinds of pasture, and the stages of the trapper's art than any of the languages of the southern coast. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the clan, the lineage, and the customary relations of kinship that govern the tundra communities, for the highland populations place great weight upon the distinctions of descent and of marriage, and the distinctions are preserved in a set of compound terms that the coastal scholars find difficult to translate. The chronicler notes that the tundra compounds are formed by the joining of two or more of the simple words, and the compounds are used in the formal speech of the clan councils and the songs of the winter festivals.

Frostic has borrowed from the dwarven speech of the mountain refuges a set of words for the metals, the stones, the ores, and the tools of the workshop that the tundra communities have required for the construction of the refuge halls and the repair of the highland equipment. The borrowings are recognised by the tundra speakers as belonging to the trade of the smith rather than to the craft of the trapper, and the chronicler records that the tundra speakers are careful to use the dwarven word in the dwarven context and the tundra word in the tundra context. The language has also borrowed a small number of words from the [Nordhal](/content/language/spoken/nordhal) of the southern coast through the overland trade, and the borrowings are chiefly the names of the southern goods, the southern customs, and the southern offices that the tundra communities have required for the conduct of the long-distance trade.

## Speech

Frostic is the language of the great refuges of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches), and the refuges are conducted in a register that is formal and measured, suited to the long deliberations of the clan councils and the patient negotiation of the matters that come before them during the long winters. The refuge register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a council, the addressing of an elder, and the closing of a deliberation, and the phrases are known to every member of the principal clans. The chronicler has observed that the phrases are intelligible to children of twelve or thirteen years who have been brought up in the refuges, and the intelligibility is a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from parent to child in the clan families of the high country.

Frostic is also the language of the long-winter councils of the tundra communities, and the councils are conducted in a still more formal register that is preserved by the elders of the principal clans and the refuge halls of the [Whitewall Range](/content/location/natural/feature/whitewall-range). The formal register is used for the public announcements of the clan magistrates, the reading of the customary law, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and the register is intelligible to any speaker of Frostic who has had the benefit of a customary education in the refuge or the workshop. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the highland valleys, and the borrowings are treated by the tundra speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The chronicler has noted that the formal register is used with care in the chambers of the principal clans, and the care is a measure of the importance that the tundra communities attach to the conduct of public business in a language that the clansmen understand and trust.

## Relations

Frostic stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions that surround the interior of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim), and the relations are a measure of the long history of contact and exchange that has shaped the tundra communities. The chronicler records that Frostic is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the coastal [Nordhal](/content/language/spoken/nordhal) of the southern belt, and the intelligibility is greater in the foothill towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Frostic and the speech of the [lupine](/content/species/sapient/lupines) communities of the cold steppes is the subject of scholarly dispute, for the two languages share a small vocabulary that is generally understood to be evidence of an old coexistence, and the chronicler has been told by the tundra scholars that the relationship is one of long neighbours rather than of kinship.

The relation of Frostic to the dwarven speech of the mountain refuges is the subject of some scholarly interest, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another over the centuries, and the chronicler has been told by the tundra scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the tundra and the dwarven communities. The chronicler records that the tundra speakers tend to treat the dwarven speech as a learned accomplishment rather than as a working language, and the dwarven communities of the refuges tend to treat Frostic as the necessary medium of the long-winter trade. The relation of Frostic to the trade tongues that arrive overland from the south is closer in vocabulary than in grammar, and the chronicler has been told by the tundra merchants that the southern speech lacks the precision of the tundra tongue in the description of the long winters and the highland pastures.

## Significance

Frostic is the speech of the interior of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim), the language of the great refuges and the long-winter councils, the medium of the tundra trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to dwell or to travel among the Frostwall Tundra and the highland valleys of the [Whitewall Range](/content/location/natural/feature/whitewall-range). The language is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary of its own, and it has been refined over the centuries by the long practice of the tundra clansmen and the patient correction of the workshops. The chronicler finds in Frostic one of the most resonant of the languages of the known world, and the language is a measure of the central place that the highland refuges have held in the metalwork and the governance of the northern continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the sheltered valleys of the interior. The language is the foundation of the tundra identity, and the tundra identity is the foundation of the highland clan, and the two together are the measure of the long attention that the tundra communities have given to the conduct of the council in a tongue that the clansmen understand and trust.
