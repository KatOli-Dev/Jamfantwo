## Overview

Khari is the speech of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), the great desert that covers the interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), used by the caravan tribes, the oasis communities, and the scattered settlements that mark the long routes between the northern coast and the deep south. The language is most closely associated with the nomadic peoples of the desert, but the chronicler records that Khari is spoken as a first language by the human populations of the oases and as a second language by the [orc](/content/species/sapient/orcs) communities of the desert fringes and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the caravan trade. The name is taken from the desert itself, and the language is sometimes called the desert speech by the coastal communities of the north, who treat it as the medium of the inland commerce rather than as a learned accomplishment.

The origins of Khari lie in the speech of the earliest nomadic communities of the desert interior, and the language has grown over the centuries through contact with the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) of the northern coast, the [Vashari](/content/language/spoken/vashari) of the deep south, and the trade tongues that pass through the desert on the long caravan routes. The chronicler has traced two distinct waves of borrowing, each of which has left a layer of vocabulary and a small set of grammatical habits that the desert speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable economy, suited to the description of the sand, the wind, the stars, and the patient business of the caravan, and capable of being adapted to the specialised vocabularies of the guide, the water-finder, and the council of the oasis elders.

## Sounds

Khari is spoken in a register that is more clipped and more deliberate than the common speech of the northern coast, and the chronicler notes that coastal visitors often remark upon the brevity of the desert sentences and the willingness of the desert speakers to leave unsaid what the coastal custom would require to be set out at length. The consonants of Khari are sharply distinguished, the vowels are few and clear, and the rhythm of the sentence is carried by the regular accent that falls upon the first syllable of most words of more than one syllable. The chronicler records that the desert singers use the consonantal range to produce the striking percussive effects that mark the songs of the caravan, and the songs are one of the most distinctive of the cultural expressions of the desert communities. The pitch of the voice is treated by the desert speakers as a matter of custom rather than of meaning, and the chronicler has observed that the desert speakers tend to speak with a flatter pitch than the coastal speakers, in unconscious imitation of the open spaces of the sand.

The script of Khari is alphabetic, with twenty-two marks that represent the consonants and vowels of the language. The script is read from right to left in horizontal lines, and the lines are arranged from the top of the page to the bottom. The script is descended from the marks used by the earliest nomadic chroniclers of the desert interior, and the chronicler has identified a small number of borrowings from the coastal [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) script in the marks used for the sounds that the older desert script did not distinguish. The marks are incised, painted, or printed upon parchment, leather, or fine cloth, and the writing is taught in the schools of the larger oases to children of every species who require it for the ordinary business of the caravan. The chronicler has noted that the script of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) is distinguished by a set of contraction marks that the caravan scribes use in the records of the routes, and the marks are treated by the desert speakers as a sign of the practicality of the trade.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Khari is rich in words for the sand, the wind, the stars, the oases, the camels, and the patient business of the caravan, and the chronicler records that the language has more words for the kinds of sand, the kinds of wind, the kinds of star, and the stages of the guide's art than any of the languages of the coastal regions. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the tribe, the lineage, and the customary relations of kinship that govern the desert communities, for the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) place great weight upon the distinctions of descent and of marriage, and the distinctions are preserved in a set of compound terms that the coastal scholars find difficult to translate. The chronicler notes that the desert compounds are formed by the joining of two or more of the simple words, and the compounds are used in the formal speech of the tribal councils and the songs of the night encampments.

Khari has borrowed from the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) of the northern coast a set of words for the spices, the woven cloths, the precious metals, and the patient business of the long trade that the desert communities have required for the conduct of the caravan commerce. The borrowings are recognised by the desert speakers as belonging to the trade of the merchant rather than to the craft of the guide, and the chronicler records that the desert speakers are careful to use the coastal word in the coastal context and the desert word in the desert context. The language has also borrowed a small number of words from the [Vashari](/content/language/spoken/vashari) of the deep south through the long caravan routes, and the borrowings are chiefly the names of the southern goods, the southern customs, and the southern offices that the desert merchants have required for the conduct of the trade that flows from the southern reaches of the continent.

## Speech

Khari is the language of the caravans of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and the caravans are conducted in a register that is brisk and practical, suited to the rapid movement of the long strings and the patient negotiation of the matters that arise upon the road. The caravan register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a march, the calling of a halt, and the closing of an encampment, and the phrases are known to every guide of the principal routes. The chronicler has observed that the phrases are intelligible to children of nine or ten years who have been brought up in the caravan families, and the intelligibility is a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from parent to child in the desert communities.

Khari is also the language of the tribal councils of the desert communities, and the councils are conducted in a more formal register that is preserved by the elders of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the principal oasis settlements. The formal register is used for the public announcements of the tribal elders, the reading of the customary law, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and the register is intelligible to any speaker of Khari who has had the benefit of a customary education in the caravan or the council. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the desert interior, and the borrowings are treated by the desert speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The chronicler has noted that the formal register is used with care in the councils of the principal tribes, and the care is a measure of the importance that the desert communities attach to the conduct of public business in a language that the tribesmen understand and trust.

## Relations

Khari stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions that surround the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and the relations are a measure of the long history of contact and exchange that has shaped the desert communities. The chronicler records that Khari is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the coastal [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) of the northern coast, and the intelligibility is greater in the caravan towns of the desert's edge than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Khari and the southern [Vashari](/content/language/spoken/vashari) of the deep south is closer still, for the two languages share a long border along the southern oases, and the speakers of the one are commonly able to follow the speech of the other after a short period of adjustment, though the chronicler has been told by the southern merchants that the northern speech lacks the precision of the southern tongue in the description of the inland seas and the southern highlands.

The relation of Khari to the trade tongues that pass through the desert is the subject of some scholarly interest, for the desert has long been a place of meeting between the languages of the north and the languages of the south, and the chronicler has been told by the desert scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the caravan communities. The chronicler records that the desert speakers tend to treat the trade tongues as learned accomplishments rather than as working languages, and the foreign merchants who visit the oases tend to treat Khari as the necessary medium of the caravan trade. The relation of Khari to the speech of the [orc](/content/species/sapient/orcs) communities of the desert fringes is the subject of dispute among the scholars of the coastal academies, and the chronicler has been unable to determine the extent of the contact between the two, for the desert scholars are said to remark that the question is not one that the desert people are inclined to answer.

## Significance

Khari is the speech of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), the great desert of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), the language of the caravans and the tribal councils, the medium of the long inland trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to dwell or to trade among the caravan routes and the oasis settlements of the desert interior. The language is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary of its own, and it has been refined over the centuries by the long practice of the desert guides and the patient correction of the councils. The chronicler finds in Khari one of the most economical of the languages of the known world, and the language is a measure of the central place that the desert has held in the caravan trade and the tribal customs of the southern continent since the earliest nomadic communities were established along the long routes of the interior. The language is the foundation of the desert identity, and the desert identity is the foundation of the tribal community, and the two together are the measure of the long attention that the desert communities have given to the conduct of the caravan in a tongue that the tribesmen understand and trust.
