## Thessari Outlined

Thessari is the common tongue of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), the long eastern continent of high interior country, sheltered valleys, and the chain of warm seas that line its eastern coast. The language is most closely associated with the human population of the central valleys and the eastern ports. Thessari is spoken as a first language by the [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) communities of the highland mines and as a second language by the [halfling](/content/species/sapient/halflings) communities of the lower pastures and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the eastern maritime trade. The name is taken from the continent itself, and the language is sometimes called the highland speech by the coastal communities of the east.

The origins of Thessari lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the central valleys. The language has grown over the centuries through contact with the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the highland meadows, the dwarven speech of the interior mines, and the [trade tongue](/content/language/pseudo/trade-tongue)s that arrive by sea from the ports of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) on the eastern coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). At least three distinct waves of borrowing have left layers of vocabulary and grammatical habits that highland speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable precision, suited to the description of the valleys, the highland pastures, the mines, and the patient business of the long eastern trade, and capable of adaptation to the specialised vocabularies of the shepherd, the miner, and the counting house of the eastern ports.

## Sound and Writing

Thessari is spoken in a register that is more clipped and precise than the common speech of the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) heartland. Western visitors often remark upon the brevity of highland sentences and the willingness of highland speakers to leave unsaid what the western custom would require to be set out at length. The consonants are sharply distinguished, the vowels are few and clear, and the rhythm of the sentence is carried by the regular accent falling upon the first syllable of most longer words. Highland singers use the consonantal range to produce the striking rhythmic effects that mark the songs of the shepherding festivals, one of the most distinctive cultural expressions of the highland communities. Pitch is a matter of custom rather than meaning; highland speakers tend to speak with a flatter pitch than lowland speakers, in unconscious imitation of the open spaces of the high meadows.

The script of Thessari is alphabetic, with twenty-seven marks representing the consonants and vowels of the language. It is read from left to right in horizontal lines. The script is descended from the marks used by the earliest human chroniclers of the central valleys, with borrowings from the dwarven script of the interior mines in the marks for sounds the older highland script did not distinguish. The marks are painted or printed upon parchment, paper, or cloth. Writing is taught in schools of the larger highland towns to children of every species who require it for the ordinary business of life. The script of the [Thessaran Basin](/content/location/settlement/region/thessaran-basin) is distinguished by contraction marks used by basin scribes in the records of the shepherding families, treated by highland speakers as a sign of the practicality of the trade.

## Pasture Words and Borrowings

The vocabulary of Thessari is rich in words for the highland pastures, the sheep, the wool, the mines, and the patient business of the long eastern trade. The language has more words for the kinds of pasture, the kinds of sheep, the kinds of wool, and the stages of the shepherd's art than any of the languages of the western continents. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the valley, the council, and the customary relations of the highland cantons, for these communities place great weight upon the distinctions of the valley clans and the long memory of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league). Highland compounds are formed by joining two or more simple words, used in the formal speech of valley councils and the songs of summer festivals.

Thessari has borrowed from the dwarven speech of the interior mines a set of words for metals, stones, ores, and tools of the workshop required for the mining trade. These borrowings are recognised as belonging to the trade of the miner rather than the craft of the shepherd, and highland speakers are careful to use the dwarven word in the dwarven context and the highland word in the highland context. A small number of words have also been borrowed from the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) trade tongue through the eastern ports of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) that share the old maritime connection, chiefly the names of western goods, customs, and offices.

## Registers of the Language

Thessari is the language of the great valleys of the central highlands, tended in a register that is patient and precise, suited to the long observation of the pastures and the careful timing of the shepherding seasons. The valley register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of the spring move, the inspection of the flocks, and the closing of the autumn return, known to every shepherd of the principal valleys. These phrases are intelligible to children of nine or ten years brought up in the shepherding families.

Thessari is also the language of the league councils of the highland communities, conducted in a more formal register preserved by the elders of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) and the principal cantons of the central valleys. The formal register is used for public announcements of cantonal magistrates, the reading of customary law, and the debates of larger assemblies, intelligible to any speaker of Thessari who has had a customary education in the valley or the council. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the central highlands, treated as marks of dignity and authority.

## Thessari and Neighbouring Tongues

Thessari stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions surrounding [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara). It is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the dwarven speech of the interior mines, and intelligibility is greater in highland towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Thessari and the western [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) of the heartland of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) is the subject of scholarly dispute, for the two languages share a small vocabulary generally understood to be evidence of an old maritime connection, and highland scholars describe the relationship as one of long neighbours rather than kinship.

The relation of Thessari to the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the highland meadows is of scholarly interest, for the two languages have borrowed vocabulary from one another over the centuries. Highland speakers tend to treat [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) as a learned accomplishment rather than a working language, and elven communities of the highland meadows treat Thessari as the necessary medium of the valley trade. The relation of Thessari to the halfling speech of the lower pastures is closer in vocabulary than in grammar; halfling communities maintain their speech is a separate language, though speakers of Thessari are commonly able to follow it after a short period of adjustment.

## Highland Speech of the East

Thessari is the common tongue of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), the language of the great valleys and the league councils, the medium of the long eastern trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who dwells or trades among the highland pastures and the eastern ports of the long continent. It is a true language with grammar and vocabulary of its own, refined over centuries by the long practice of highland shepherds and the patient correction of the league. It is one of the most precise of the languages of the known world, a measure of the central place the highland valleys have held in the shepherding trade and league customs of the eastern continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the sheltered valleys of the interior.
