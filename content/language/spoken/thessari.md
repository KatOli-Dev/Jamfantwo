## Overview

Thessari is the common tongue of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), the long eastern continent of high interior country, sheltered valleys, and the chain of warm seas that line its eastern coast. The language is most closely associated with the human population of the central valleys and the eastern ports, but the chronicler records that Thessari is spoken as a first language by the [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) communities of the highland mines and as a second language by the [halfling](/content/species/sapient/halflings) communities of the lower pastures and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the eastern maritime trade. The name is taken from the continent itself, and the language is sometimes called the highland speech by the coastal communities of the east, who treat it as the medium of the mountain commerce rather than as a learned accomplishment.

The origins of Thessari lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the central valleys, and the language has grown over the centuries through contact with the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the highland meadows, the dwarven speech of the interior mines, and the trade tongues that arrive by sea from the ports of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) on the eastern coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The chronicler has traced at least three distinct waves of borrowing, each of which has left a layer of vocabulary and a small set of grammatical habits that the highland speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable precision, suited to the description of the valleys, the highland pastures, the mines, and the patient business of the long eastern trade, and capable of being adapted to the specialised vocabularies of the shepherd, the miner, and the counting house of the eastern ports.

## Sounds

Thessari is spoken in a register that is more clipped and more precise than the common speech of the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) heartland, and the chronicler notes that western visitors often remark upon the brevity of the highland sentences and the willingness of the highland speakers to leave unsaid what the western custom would require to be set out at length. The consonants of Thessari are sharply distinguished, the vowels are few and clear, and the rhythm of the sentence is carried by the regular accent that falls upon the first syllable of most words of more than one syllable. The chronicler records that the highland singers use the consonantal range to produce the striking rhythmic effects that mark the songs of the shepherding festivals, and the songs are one of the most distinctive of the cultural expressions of the highland communities. The pitch of the voice is treated by the highland speakers as a matter of custom rather than of meaning, and the chronicler has observed that the highland speakers tend to speak with a flatter pitch than the lowland speakers, in unconscious imitation of the open spaces of the high meadows.

The script of Thessari is alphabetic, with twenty-seven marks that represent the consonants and vowels of the language. The script is read from left to right in horizontal lines, and the lines are arranged from the top of the page to the bottom. The script is descended from the marks used by the earliest human chroniclers of the central valleys, and the chronicler has identified a small number of borrowings from the dwarven script of the interior mines in the marks used for the sounds that the older highland script did not distinguish. The marks are painted or printed upon parchment, paper, or cloth, and the writing is taught in the schools of the larger highland towns to children of every species who require it for the ordinary business of life. The chronicler has noted that the script of the [Thessaran Basin](/content/location/settlement/region/thessaran-basin) is distinguished by a set of contraction marks that the basin scribes use in the records of the shepherding families, and the marks are treated by the highland speakers as a sign of the practicality of the trade.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Thessari is rich in words for the highland pastures, the sheep, the wool, the mines, and the patient business of the long eastern trade, and the chronicler records that the language has more words for the kinds of pasture, the kinds of sheep, the kinds of wool, and the stages of the shepherd's art than any of the languages of the western continents. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the valley, the council, and the customary relations of the highland cantons, for the highland communities place great weight upon the distinctions of the valley clans and the long memory of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league). The chronicler notes that the highland compounds are formed by the joining of two or more of the simple words, and the compounds are used in the formal speech of the valley councils and the songs of the summer festivals.

Thessari has borrowed from the dwarven speech of the interior mines a set of words for the metals, the stones, the ores, and the tools of the workshop that the highland communities have required for the conduct of the mining trade. The borrowings are recognised by the highland speakers as belonging to the trade of the miner rather than to the craft of the shepherd, and the chronicler records that the highland speakers are careful to use the dwarven word in the dwarven context and the highland word in the highland context. The language has also borrowed a small number of words from the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) trade tongue through the eastern ports of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) that share the old maritime connection, and the borrowings are chiefly the names of the western goods, the western customs, and the western offices that the highland merchants have required for the conduct of the long-distance trade.

## Speech

Thessari is the language of the great valleys of the central highlands, and the valleys are tended in a register that is patient and precise, suited to the long observation of the pastures and the careful timing of the shepherding seasons. The valley register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of the spring move, the inspection of the flocks, and the closing of the autumn return, and the phrases are known to every shepherd of the principal valleys. The chronicler has observed that the phrases are intelligible to children of nine or ten years who have been brought up in the shepherding families, and the intelligibility is a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from parent to child in the valley communities of the highlands.

Thessari is also the language of the league councils of the highland communities, and the councils are conducted in a more formal register that is preserved by the elders of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) and the principal cantons of the central valleys. The formal register is used for the public announcements of the cantonal magistrates, the reading of the customary law, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and the register is intelligible to any speaker of Thessari who has had the benefit of a customary education in the valley or the council. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the central highlands, and the borrowings are treated by the highland speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The chronicler has noted that the formal register is used with care in the chambers of the league, and the care is a measure of the importance that the highland communities attach to the conduct of public business in a language that the cantonsmen understand and trust.

## Relations

Thessari stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions that surround [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), and the relations are a measure of the long history of contact and exchange that has shaped the highland communities. The chronicler records that Thessari is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the dwarven speech of the interior mines, and the intelligibility is greater in the highland towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Thessari and the western [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) of the heartland of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) is the subject of scholarly dispute, for the two languages share a small vocabulary that is generally understood to be evidence of an old maritime connection, and the chronicler has been told by the highland scholars that the relationship is one of long neighbours rather than of kinship.

The relation of Thessari to the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the highland meadows is the subject of some scholarly interest, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another over the centuries, and the chronicler has been told by the highland scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the highland and the elven communities. The chronicler records that the highland speakers tend to treat Quessan as a learned accomplishment rather than as a working language, and the elven communities of the highland meadows tend to treat Thessari as the necessary medium of the valley trade. The relation of Thessari to the halfling speech of the lower pastures is closer in vocabulary than in grammar, and the chronicler has been told by the halfling communities that their speech is a separate language, though the speakers of Thessari are commonly able to follow it after a short period of adjustment.

## Significance

Thessari is the common tongue of [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara), the language of the great valleys and the league councils, the medium of the long eastern trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to dwell or to trade among the highland pastures and the eastern ports of the long continent. The language is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary of its own, and it has been refined over the centuries by the long practice of the highland shepherds and the patient correction of the league. The chronicler finds in Thessari one of the most precise of the languages of the known world, and the language is a measure of the central place that the highland valleys have held in the shepherding trade and the league customs of the eastern continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the sheltered valleys of the interior. The language is the foundation of the highland identity, and the highland identity is the foundation of the league, and the two together are the measure of the long attention that the highland communities have given to the conduct of the council in a tongue that the cantonsmen understand and trust.
