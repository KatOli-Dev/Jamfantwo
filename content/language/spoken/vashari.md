## Vashari in Brief

[Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) is the speech of the deep south of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), used in the temperate uplands, the inland seas, and the settled communities that lie beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert. The language is most closely associated with the human population of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) and the communities that have grown up around the long shores of the inland waters. Vashari is spoken as a first language by the [elf](/content/species/sapient/elves) communities of the wooded southern valleys and as a second language by the [orc](/content/species/sapient/orcs) communities of the wilder interior and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the long caravan routes that connect the deep south to the northern coast. The name is taken from the pact that unites the southern communities, and the language is sometimes called the southern speech by the desert communities of the interior.

The origins of Vashari lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the southern uplands. The language has grown over the centuries through contact with the [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the desert, the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded valleys, and the [trade tongue](/content/language/pseudo/trade-tongue)s that pass through the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) on the long caravan routes. Two distinct waves of borrowing have left layers of vocabulary and grammatical habits that southern speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable richness, suited to the description of the lakes, the rivers, the highlands, and the patient business of the southern communities, and capable of adaptation to the specialised vocabularies of the fisher, the potter, and the council chamber of the southern settlements.

## Sound and Script

Vashari is spoken in a register that is softer and more flowing than the common speech of the desert. Desert visitors often remark upon the music of the southern vowels and the ease with which southern speakers link one syllable to the next. The consonants are softly distinguished, the vowels are full and open, and the rhythm of the sentence is carried by the regular accent falling upon the second or third syllable of most longer words. Southern singers use the vowel range to produce the long sustained notes of the lake songs, one of the most distinctive cultural expressions of the southern communities. Pitch is a matter of custom rather than meaning; southern speakers tend to speak with a higher pitch than desert speakers, in unconscious imitation of the singing register of the lake shores and the festivals.

The script of Vashari is alphabetic, with twenty-seven marks representing the consonants and vowels of the language. It is read from right to left in horizontal lines. The script is descended from the marks used by the earliest human chroniclers of the southern uplands, with borrowings from the elven tree script in the marks for sounds the older southern script did not distinguish. The marks are painted or printed upon parchment, paper, or fine cloth. Writing is taught in schools of the larger southern towns to children of every species who require it for the ordinary business of life. The script of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) is distinguished by ornamental marks added to the headings of official documents, treated by southern speakers as a sign of the dignity of the federation.

## Lake Words and Loanwords

The vocabulary of Vashari is rich in words for the lakes, the rivers, the fish, the highlands, and the patient business of the southern communities. The language has more words for the kinds of fish, the kinds of waterfowl, the kinds of highland pasture, and the stages of the fisher's art than any of the languages of the desert. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the council, the office, and the customary relations of the federation, for the southern communities place great weight upon the distinctions of the southern cantons and the long memory of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach). Southern compounds are formed by joining two or more simple words, used in the formal speech of councils and the songs of lake festivals.

Vashari has borrowed from the [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the desert a set of words for camels, oases, caravan routes, and the patient business of the long crossing required for the trade flowing from the northern coast. These borrowings are recognised as belonging to the trade of the caravan rather than the craft of the fisher, and southern speakers are careful to use the desert word in the desert context and the southern word in the southern context. A small number of words have also been borrowed from the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded valleys, chiefly the names of trees, birds, and small creatures of the southern forests required for describing materials of their craft.

## Registers of Vashari

Vashari is the language of the great lakes of the deep south, tended in a register that is patient and precise, suited to the long observation of the waters and the careful timing of the fishing seasons. The lake register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of the season, the inspection of the catch, and the closing of the harvest, known to every fisher of the principal lakes. These phrases are intelligible to children of eight or nine years brought up among the lakes.

Vashari is also the language of the council chambers of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), conducted in a more formal register preserved by the elders of the federation and the principal cantons of the southern uplands. The formal register is used for public announcements of cantonal magistrates, the reading of customary law, and the debates of larger assemblies, intelligible to any speaker of Vashari who has had a customary education in the council or the lake. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the southern uplands, treated as marks of dignity and authority.

## Vashari and Other Languages

Vashari stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions surrounding the deep south. It is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the desert [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and intelligibility is greater in caravan towns of the southern oases than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Vashari and the coastal [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) of the northern coast is more distant, for the two languages have had less contact over the centuries, and speakers of one are not commonly able to follow the speech of the other without a period of study. Merchants of the principal caravan towns are commonly able to make themselves understood after a few weeks upon the road.

The relation of Vashari to the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded valleys is of scholarly interest, for the two languages have borrowed vocabulary from one another over the centuries. Southern speakers tend to treat [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) as a learned accomplishment rather than a working language, and elven communities of the wooded valleys treat Vashari as the necessary medium of the lake trade. The relation of Vashari to the speech of the [orc](/content/species/sapient/orcs) communities of the wilder interior is disputed among scholars of the southern cantons, who remark that the question is not one the southern people are inclined to answer.

## Southern Speech of the Deep South

Vashari is the speech of the deep south of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), the language of the great lakes and the council chambers, the medium of the long southern trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who dwells or trades among the temperate uplands and the inland seas of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach). It is a true language with grammar and vocabulary of its own, refined over centuries by the long practice of southern fishers and the patient correction of the councils. It is one of the most musical of the languages of the known world, a measure of the central place the deep south has held in the lake trade and federation customs of the southern continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the shores of the inland seas.
