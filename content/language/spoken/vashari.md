## Overview

Vashari is the speech of the deep south of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), used in the temperate uplands, the inland seas, and the settled communities that lie beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert. The language is most closely associated with the human population of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) and the communities that have grown up around the long shores of the inland waters, but the chronicler records that Vashari is spoken as a first language by the [elf](/content/species/sapient/elves) communities of the wooded southern valleys and as a second language by the [orc](/content/species/sapient/orcs) communities of the wilder interior and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the long caravan routes that connect the deep south to the northern coast. The name is taken from the pact that unites the southern communities, and the language is sometimes called the southern speech by the desert communities of the interior, who treat it as the medium of the long trade rather than as a learned accomplishment.

The origins of Vashari lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the southern uplands, and the language has grown over the centuries through contact with the [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the desert, the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded valleys, and the trade tongues that pass through the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) on the long caravan routes. The chronicler has traced two distinct waves of borrowing, each of which has left a layer of vocabulary and a small set of grammatical habits that the southern speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable richness, suited to the description of the lakes, the rivers, the highlands, and the patient business of the southern communities, and capable of being adapted to the specialised vocabularies of the fisher, the potter, and the council chamber of the southern settlements.

## Sounds

Vashari is spoken in a register that is softer and more flowing than the common speech of the desert, and the chronicler notes that desert visitors often remark upon the music of the southern vowels and the ease with which the southern speakers link one syllable to the next. The consonants of Vashari are softly distinguished, the vowels are full and open, and the rhythm of the sentence is carried by the regular accent that falls upon the second or third syllable of most words of more than one syllable. The chronicler records that the southern singers use the vowel range to produce the long sustained notes of the lake songs, and the songs are one of the most distinctive of the cultural expressions of the southern communities. The pitch of the voice is treated by the southern speakers as a matter of custom rather than of meaning, and the chronicler has observed that the southern speakers tend to speak with a higher pitch than the desert speakers, in unconscious imitation of the singing register of the lake shores and the festivals.

The script of Vashari is alphabetic, with twenty-seven marks that represent the consonants and vowels of the language. The script is read from right to left in horizontal lines, and the lines are arranged from the top of the page to the bottom. The script is descended from the marks used by the earliest human chroniclers of the southern uplands, and the chronicler has identified a small number of borrowings from the elven tree script in the marks used for the sounds that the older southern script did not distinguish. The marks are painted or printed upon parchment, paper, or fine cloth, and the writing is taught in the schools of the larger southern towns to children of every species who require it for the ordinary business of life. The chronicler has noted that the script of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) is distinguished by a set of ornamental marks that the council scribes add to the headings of the official documents, and the marks are treated by the southern speakers as a sign of the dignity of the federation.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Vashari is rich in words for the lakes, the rivers, the fish, the highlands, and the patient business of the southern communities, and the chronicler records that the language has more words for the kinds of fish, the kinds of waterfowl, the kinds of highland pasture, and the stages of the fisher's art than any of the languages of the desert. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the council, the office, and the customary relations of the federation, for the southern communities place great weight upon the distinctions of the southern cantons and the long memory of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach). The chronicler notes that the southern compounds are formed by the joining of two or more of the simple words, and the compounds are used in the formal speech of the councils and the songs of the lake festivals.

Vashari has borrowed from the [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the desert a set of words for the camels, the oases, the caravan routes, and the patient business of the long crossing that the southern merchants have required for the conduct of the trade that flows from the northern coast. The borrowings are recognised by the southern speakers as belonging to the trade of the caravan rather than to the craft of the fisher, and the chronicler records that the southern speakers are careful to use the desert word in the desert context and the southern word in the southern context. The language has also borrowed a small number of words from the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded valleys, and the borrowings are chiefly the names of the trees, the birds, and the small creatures of the southern forests that the southern communities have required for the description of the materials of their craft.

## Speech

Vashari is the language of the great lakes of the deep south, and the lakes are tended in a register that is patient and precise, suited to the long observation of the waters and the careful timing of the fishing seasons. The lake register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of the season, the inspection of the catch, and the closing of the harvest, and the phrases are known to every fisher of the principal lakes. The chronicler has observed that the phrases are intelligible to children of eight or nine years who have been brought up among the lakes, and the intelligibility is a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from parent to child in the fisher families of the southern communities.

Vashari is also the language of the council chambers of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), and the councils are conducted in a more formal register that is preserved by the elders of the federation and the principal cantons of the southern uplands. The formal register is used for the public announcements of the cantonal magistrates, the reading of the customary law, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and the register is intelligible to any speaker of Vashari who has had the benefit of a customary education in the council or the lake. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the southern uplands, and the borrowings are treated by the southern speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The chronicler has noted that the formal register is used with care in the chambers of the federation, and the care is a measure of the importance that the southern communities attach to the conduct of public business in a language that the cantonsmen understand and trust.

## Relations

Vashari stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions that surround the deep south, and the relations are a measure of the long history of contact and exchange that has shaped the southern communities. The chronicler records that Vashari is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the desert [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and the intelligibility is greater in the caravan towns of the southern oases than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Vashari and the coastal [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) of the northern coast is more distant, for the two languages have had less contact over the centuries, and the speakers of the one are not commonly able to follow the speech of the other without a period of study, though the chronicler has noted that the merchants of the principal caravan towns are commonly able to make themselves understood after a few weeks upon the road.

The relation of Vashari to the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded valleys is the subject of some scholarly interest, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another over the centuries, and the chronicler has been told by the southern scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the southern and the elven communities. The chronicler records that the southern speakers tend to treat Quessan as a learned accomplishment rather than as a working language, and the elven communities of the wooded valleys tend to treat Vashari as the necessary medium of the lake trade. The relation of Vashari to the speech of the [orc](/content/species/sapient/orcs) communities of the wilder interior is the subject of dispute among the scholars of the southern cantons, and the chronicler has been unable to determine the extent of the contact between the two, for the southern scholars are said to remark that the question is not one that the southern people are inclined to answer.

## Significance

Vashari is the speech of the deep south of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), the language of the great lakes and the council chambers, the medium of the long southern trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to dwell or to trade among the temperate uplands and the inland seas of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach). The language is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary of its own, and it has been refined over the centuries by the long practice of the southern fishers and the patient correction of the councils. The chronicler finds in Vashari one of the most musical of the languages of the known world, and the language is a measure of the central place that the deep south has held in the lake trade and the federation customs of the southern continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the shores of the inland seas. The language is the foundation of the southern identity, and the southern identity is the foundation of the federation, and the two together are the measure of the long attention that the southern communities have given to the conduct of the council in a tongue that the cantonsmen understand and trust.
