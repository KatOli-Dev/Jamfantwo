## Sahrani Introduced

Sahrani is the common tongue of the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), spoken in the great port-cities, the inland termini, and the fertile valleys that lie between the warm sea and the rising foothills of the interior. The language is most closely associated with the human population of the coast. Sahrani is spoken as a first language by the [halfling](/content/species/sapient/halflings) communities of the river valleys and as a second language by the [elf](/content/species/sapient/elves) communities of the wooded coastal districts and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the great Auric trade. The name is taken from the continent itself, and the language is sometimes called the coast speech by the desert communities of the interior.

The origins of Sahrani lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the northern river valleys. The language has grown over the centuries through contact with the [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert, the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded coastal districts, and the trade tongues that arrive by sea from [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) across the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). At least three distinct waves of borrowing have left layers of vocabulary and grammatical habits that coastal speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable elegance, suited to the description of the cultivated land, the spices, the woven cloths, and the patient business of the bazaar, and capable of adaptation to the specialised vocabularies of the counting house, the library, and the courts of the city-states.

## Sound and Script

Sahrani is spoken in a register that is more deliberate and musical than the common speech of the northern continents. Northern visitors often remark upon the slowness of the coastal speech and the care coastal speakers take to distinguish the vowels at the close of every word. The consonants are softly distinguished, the vowels are full and open, and the rhythm of the sentence is carried by the regular accent falling upon the second or third syllable of most longer words. Coastal singers use the vowel range to produce the long sustained notes of the festival songs, one of the most distinctive cultural expressions of the coastal communities. Pitch is a matter of custom rather than meaning; coastal speakers tend to speak with a higher pitch than desert speakers, in unconscious imitation of the singing register of the bazaar and the festival.

The script of Sahrani is alphabetic, with twenty-eight marks representing the consonants and vowels of the language. It is read from right to left in horizontal lines. The script is descended from the marks used by the earliest human chroniclers of the northern coast, with borrowings from the elven tree script in the marks used for sounds the older coastal script did not distinguish. The marks are painted or printed upon parchment, paper, or fine cloth. Writing is taught in schools of the larger port-cities to children of every species who require it for the ordinary business of life. The script of the principal courts is distinguished by ornamental marks added to the headings of official documents, treated by coastal speakers as a sign of the dignity of the state.

## Market Words and Borrowings

The vocabulary of Sahrani is rich in words for spices, dyes, woven cloths, precious metals, and the patient business of the long-distance trade. The language has more words for the varieties of spice, the kinds of dye, the kinds of weave, and the stages of the merchant's art than any of the languages of the northern continents. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the law, the office, and the customary relations of the city-states, for the coastal communities place great weight upon the distinctions of rank and the long memory of the courts. Coastal compounds are formed by joining two or more simple words, used in the formal speech of the courts and the songs of public festivals.

Sahrani has borrowed from the [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the desert a set of words for camels, oases, caravan routes, and the patient business of the long crossing required for the inland trade. These borrowings are recognised as belonging to the trade of the caravan rather than the craft of the merchant, and coastal speakers are careful to use the desert word in the desert context and the coastal word in the coastal context. A small number of words have also been borrowed from the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) trade tongue through ships crossing the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), chiefly the names of northern goods, customs, and offices.

## Registers of the Coast

Sahrani is the language of the great bazaars of the northern coast, conducted in a register that is brisk and elegant, suited to the rapid exchange of goods and the negotiation of the prices of the long trade. The bazaar register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a bargain, the testing of a cargo, and the closing of a sale, known to every merchant of the principal port-cities. These phrases are intelligible to children of ten or eleven years brought up in the merchant families.

Sahrani is also the language of the courts of the city-states, conducted in a more formal register preserved by the elders of the principal city-states and the learned academies of the coast. The formal register is used for public announcements of city magistrates, the reading of customary law, and the debates of larger assemblies, intelligible to any speaker of Sahrani who has had a customary education in the bazaar or the library. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the northern coast, treated as marks of dignity and authority. The formal register is used with care in the chambers of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact).

## Sahrani and Other Languages

Sahrani stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions surrounding the northern coast. It is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the desert [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and intelligibility is greater in caravan towns of the desert's edge than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Sahrani and the southern [Vashari](/content/language/spoken/vashari) of the deep south is closer still, for the two languages share a long border along the southern river valleys, and speakers of one are commonly able to follow the speech of the other after a short period of adjustment.

The relation of Sahrani to the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded coastal districts is of scholarly interest, for the two languages have borrowed vocabulary from one another over the centuries. Coastal speakers tend to treat Quessan as a learned accomplishment rather than a working language, and elven communities of the wooded districts treat Sahrani as the necessary medium of the bazaar trade. The relation of Sahrani to the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) trade tongue is the subject of dispute among scholars of the coastal academies, who maintain that the two languages are of separate origin and share only the relation of long-distance trade. The northern coast also employs the [Northern Coast Bell Codes](/content/language/pseudo/northern-coast-bell-codes), a system of bell signals used by fishing and trading communities to communicate between boats and shore.

## Northern Coast Tongue

Sahrani is the common tongue of the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), the language of the bazaars and the courts, the medium of the great Auric trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who dwells or trades among the great port-cities and inland termini of the northern coast. It is a true language with grammar and vocabulary of its own, refined over centuries by the long practice of coastal merchants and the patient correction of the academies. It is one of the most elegant of the languages of the known world, a measure of the central place the northern coast has held in the spice trade and scholarship of the southern continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the warm sea.
