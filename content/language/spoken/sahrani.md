## Overview

Sahrani is the common tongue of the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), spoken in the great port-cities, the inland termini, and the fertile valleys that lie between the warm sea and the rising foothills of the interior. The language is most closely associated with the human population of the coast, but the chronicler records that Sahrani is spoken as a first language by the [halfling](/content/species/sapient/halflings) communities of the river valleys and as a second language by the [elf](/content/species/sapient/elves) communities of the wooded coastal districts and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the great Auric trade. The name is taken from the continent itself, and the language is sometimes called the coast speech by the desert communities of the interior, who treat it as the medium of the caravan commerce rather than as a learned accomplishment.

The origins of Sahrani lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the northern river valleys, and the language has grown over the centuries through contact with the [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert, the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded coastal districts, and the trade tongues that arrive by sea from [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) across the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The chronicler has traced at least three distinct waves of borrowing, each of which has left a layer of vocabulary and a small set of grammatical habits that the coastal speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable elegance, suited to the description of the cultivated land, the spices, the woven cloths, and the patient business of the bazaar, and capable of being adapted to the specialised vocabularies of the counting house, the library, and the courts of the city-states.

## Sounds

Sahrani is spoken in a register that is more deliberate and more musical than the common speech of the northern continents, and the chronicler notes that northern visitors often remark upon the slowness of the coastal speech and the care that the coastal speakers take to distinguish the vowels at the close of every word. The consonants of Sahrani are softly distinguished, the vowels are full and open, and the rhythm of the sentence is carried by the regular accent that falls upon the second or third syllable of most words of more than one syllable. The chronicler records that the coastal singers use the vowel range to produce the long sustained notes of the festival songs, and the songs are one of the most distinctive of the cultural expressions of the coastal communities. The pitch of the voice is treated by the coastal speakers as a matter of custom rather than of meaning, and the chronicler has observed that the coastal speakers tend to speak with a higher pitch than the desert speakers, in unconscious imitation of the singing register of the bazaar and the festival.

The script of Sahrani is alphabetic, with twenty-eight marks that represent the consonants and vowels of the language. The script is read from right to left in horizontal lines, and the lines are arranged from the top of the page to the bottom. The script is descended from the marks used by the earliest human chroniclers of the northern coast, and the chronicler has identified a small number of borrowings from the elven tree script in the marks used for the sounds that the older coastal script did not distinguish. The marks are painted or printed upon parchment, paper, or fine cloth, and the writing is taught in the schools of the larger port-cities to children of every species who require it for the ordinary business of life. The chronicler has noted that the script of the principal courts is distinguished by a set of ornamental marks that the court scribes add to the headings of the official documents, and the marks are treated by the coastal speakers as a sign of the dignity of the state.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Sahrani is rich in words for the spices, the dyes, the woven cloths, the precious metals, and the patient business of the long-distance trade, and the chronicler records that the language has more words for the varieties of spice, the kinds of dye, the kinds of weave, and the stages of the merchant's art than any of the languages of the northern continents. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the law, the office, and the customary relations of the city-states, for the coastal communities place great weight upon the distinctions of rank and the long memory of the courts. The chronicler notes that the coastal compounds are formed by the joining of two or more of the simple words, and the compounds are used in the formal speech of the courts and the songs of the public festivals.

Sahrani has borrowed from the [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the desert a set of words for the camels, the oases, the caravan routes, and the patient business of the long crossing that the coastal merchants have required for the conduct of the inland trade. The borrowings are recognised by the coastal speakers as belonging to the trade of the caravan rather than to the craft of the merchant, and the chronicler records that the coastal speakers are careful to use the desert word in the desert context and the coastal word in the coastal context. The language has also borrowed a small number of words from the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) trade tongue through the ships that cross the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), and the borrowings are chiefly the names of the northern goods, the northern customs, and the northern offices that the coastal merchants have required for the conduct of the long-distance trade.

## Speech

Sahrani is the language of the great bazaars of the northern coast, and the bazaars are conducted in a register that is brisk and elegant, suited to the rapid exchange of goods and the negotiation of the prices of the long trade. The bazaar register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a bargain, the testing of a cargo, and the closing of a sale, and the phrases are known to every merchant of the principal port-cities. The chronicler has observed that the phrases are intelligible to children of ten or eleven years who have been brought up in the merchant families, and the intelligibility is a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from parent to child in the trading houses of the coast.

Sahrani is also the language of the courts of the city-states, and the courts are conducted in a more formal register that is preserved by the elders of the principal city-states and the learned academies of the coast. The formal register is used for the public announcements of the city magistrates, the reading of the customary law, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and the register is intelligible to any speaker of Sahrani who has had the benefit of a customary education in the bazaar or the library. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the northern coast, and the borrowings are treated by the coastal speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The chronicler has noted that the formal register is used with care in the chambers of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), and the care is a measure of the importance that the coastal communities attach to the conduct of public business in a language that the merchants and the magistrates understand and trust.

## Relations

Sahrani stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions that surround the northern coast, and the relations are a measure of the long history of contact and exchange that has shaped the coastal communities. The chronicler records that Sahrani is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the desert [Khari](/content/language/spoken/khari) of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and the intelligibility is greater in the caravan towns of the desert's edge than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Sahrani and the southern [Vashari](/content/language/spoken/vashari) of the deep south is closer still, for the two languages share a long border along the southern river valleys, and the speakers of the one are commonly able to follow the speech of the other after a short period of adjustment.

The relation of Sahrani to the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded coastal districts is the subject of some scholarly interest, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another over the centuries, and the chronicler has been told by the coastal scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the coastal and the elven communities. The chronicler records that the coastal speakers tend to treat Quessan as a learned accomplishment rather than as a working language, and the elven communities of the wooded districts tend to treat Sahrani as the necessary medium of the bazaar trade. The relation of Sahrani to the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) trade tongue is the subject of dispute among the scholars of the coastal academies, and the chronicler has been told by the learned academies that the two languages are of separate origin and have only the relation of long-distance trade.

## Significance

Sahrani is the common tongue of the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), the language of the bazaars and the courts, the medium of the great Auric trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to dwell or to trade among the great port-cities and the inland termini of the northern coast. The language is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary of its own, and it has been refined over the centuries by the long practice of the coastal merchants and the patient correction of the academies. The chronicler finds in Sahrani one of the most elegant of the languages of the known world, and the language is a measure of the central place that the northern coast has held in the spice trade and the scholarship of the southern continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the warm sea. The language is the foundation of the coastal identity, and the coastal identity is the foundation of the city-states, and the two together are the measure of the long attention that the coastal communities have given to the conduct of the bazaar in a tongue that the merchants and the magistrates understand and trust.
