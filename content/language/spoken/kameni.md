## Overview

Kameni is the speech of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), used in the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) and the trade towns that lie at the foot of the passes. The language is most closely associated with the dwarven halls that occupy the high country, but the chronicler records that Kameni is spoken as a first language by the human populations of the highland valleys and as a second language by the elven communities of the wooded foothills and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the mountain trade. The name is taken from the old word for stone in the speech of the highland smiths, and the language is sometimes called the stonemason's tongue by the lowland traders who employ it for the negotiation of contracts and the settlement of weights and prices.

The origins of Kameni lie in the speech of the earliest dwarf communities of the mountain spine, and the language has grown over the centuries through contact with the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) of the heartland and with the [Morvindi](/content/language/spoken/morvindi) traders who cross the [Strait of Velden](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) to reach the southern markets. The chronicler has traced two distinct waves of borrowing, each of which has left a layer of vocabulary and a small set of grammatical habits that the highland speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable precision, suited to the description of metals and stones and the patient business of the workshop, and capable of being adapted to the specialised vocabularies of the mine, the forge, and the counting house of the highland guilds.

## Sounds

Kameni is spoken in a register that is sharper and more clipped than the common speech of the heartland, and the chronicler notes that lowland visitors often remark upon the distinctness of every syllable and the absence of the soft linking sounds that mark the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) of the central plains. The consonants of Kameni are numerous, and the language distinguishes a number of sounds that the lowland speech has merged, particularly in the cluster of unvoiced stops that occur at the beginning of many highland words. The vowels are fewer than in the lowland speech, and the chronicler records that the highland singers use the consonantal range to produce the striking percussive effects that mark the music of the mountain halls. The pitch of the voice is treated by the highland speakers as a matter of custom rather than of meaning, and the chronicler has observed that the highland speakers tend to speak with a flatter pitch than the lowland speakers, in unconscious imitation of the working register of the smith and the mason.

The script of Kameni is alphabetic, with thirty-one marks that represent the consonants and vowels of the language. The script is read from left to right in horizontal lines, and the lines are arranged from the top of the page to the bottom. The script is descended from the angular signs that the dwarven stonemasons cut into the lintels of the highland halls, and the chronicler has identified a small number of borrowings from the elven tree script in the marks used for the sounds that the older dwarven script did not distinguish. The marks are incised into stone or metal for the records of the highland guilds, and painted or printed upon parchment for the correspondence of the trade towns, and the writing is taught in the schools of the larger highland settlements to children of every species who require it for the business of the mountain trade.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Kameni is rich in words for the metals, the stones, the ores, the tools, and the procedures of the workshop, and the chronicler records that the language has more words for the grades of iron, the kinds of stone, the kinds of joint, and the stages of the smith's work than any of the languages of the lowlands. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the relations of the workshop, the ranks of the guild, and the customary obligations of apprentice, journeyman, and master, for the highland halls have long been governed by a customary law of the craft that requires a precise language for the conduct of guild business. The chronicler notes that the highland compounds are formed by the joining of two or more of the simple words, and the compounds are used in the formal speech of the guilds and the councils of the highland halls.

Kameni has borrowed from the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) of the lowland heartland a set of words for the cultivated land, the seasons, and the relations of neighbourhood that the highland communities have required for the conduct of trade with the lowland towns. The borrowings are recognised by the highland speakers as belonging to the trade of the merchant rather than to the craft of the smith, and the chronicler records that the highland speakers are careful to use the lowland word in the lowland context and the highland word in the highland context. The language has also borrowed a small number of words from the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded foothills, and the borrowings are chiefly the names of the trees, the birds, and the small creatures of the lower slopes that the highland smiths have required for the description of the materials of their craft.

## Speech

Kameni is the language of the highland guilds, and the guilds are conducted in a register that is precise and technical, suited to the careful description of the work and the patient negotiation of the contracts that govern the highland trade. The guild register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a contract, the testing of a piece of work, and the closing of a bargain, and the phrases are known to every master of the highland halls. The chronicler has observed that the phrases are intelligible to apprentices of fourteen or fifteen years who have served the customary term, and the intelligibility is a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from master to apprentice in the workshops of the highland halls.

Kameni is also the language of the highland councils, and the councils are conducted in a more formal register that is preserved by the elders of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) and the principal trade towns of the mountain spine. The formal register is used for the public announcements of the highland magistrates, the reading of the customary law, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and the register is intelligible to any speaker of Kameni who has had the benefit of a customary education in the workshop. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the highland halls, and the borrowings are treated by the highland speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The chronicler has noted that the formal register is used with care in the chambers of the principal guilds, and the care is a measure of the importance that the highland communities attach to the conduct of public business in a language that the craftsmen understand and trust.

## Relations

Kameni stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions that surround the mountain spine, and the relations are a measure of the long history of contact and exchange that has shaped the highland communities. The chronicler records that Kameni is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the lowland [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) of the heartland, and the intelligibility is greater in the border towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Kameni and the [Karathi](/content/language/spoken/karathi) of the Morvind interior is closer still, for the two languages share a long history of contact across the [Strait of Velden](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), and the speakers of the one are commonly able to follow the speech of the other after a short period of adjustment, though the chronicler has been told by the highland smiths that the Morvindi speech lacks the precision of the highland tongue in the description of the metals and the stones.

The relation of Kameni to the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) is the subject of some scholarly dispute, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another over the centuries, and the chronicler has been told by the highland scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the highland and the foothill communities. The chronicler records that the highland speakers tend to treat Quessan as a learned accomplishment rather than as a working language, and the elven communities of the foothills tend to treat Kameni as the necessary medium of the mountain trade. The relation of Kameni to the dwarven speech of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) is the subject of dispute among the scholars of the highland halls, and the chronicler has been unable to determine the extent of the kinship between the two, for the highland scholars are said to remark that the question is not one that the highland people are inclined to answer.

## Significance

Kameni is the speech of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), the language of the highland guilds and the highland councils, the medium of the mountain trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to dwell or to trade among the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands). The language is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary of its own, and it has been refined over the centuries by the long practice of the highland craftsmen and the patient correction of the workshops. The chronicler finds in Kameni one of the most precise of the languages of the known world, and the language is a measure of the central place that the highland halls have held in the metalwork and the stonework of the continent since the earliest dwarven settlements were established along the mountain spine. The language is the foundation of the highland identity, and the highland identity is the foundation of the highland guild, and the two together are the measure of the long attention that the highland communities have given to the conduct of the craft in a tongue that the craftsmen understand and trust.
