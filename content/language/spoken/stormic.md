## Overview

Stormic is the common tongue of [Stormfall](/content/location/natural/continent/stormfall), the storm-shaped continent that lies far to the west of the established northern routes, spoken in the dark fjords of the northern shore, the highland valleys of the [Thunderback Range](/content/location/natural/feature/thunderback-range), and the warmer grasslands of the southern coast. The language is most closely associated with the human population of the coastal settlements and the highland communities, but the chronicler records that Stormic is spoken as a first language by the [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) communities of the highland mines and as a second language by the [halfling](/content/species/sapient/halflings) communities of the southern lowlands and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the western maritime trade. The name is taken from the storms that fall from the mountains of the interior, and the language is sometimes called the thunderback speech by the coastal communities of the south, who treat it as the medium of the highland commerce rather than as a learned accomplishment.

The origins of Stormic lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the dark fjords, and the language has grown over the centuries through contact with the dwarven speech of the highland mines, the halfling speech of the southern lowlands, and the trade tongues that arrive by sea from the ports of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) across the long western waters. The chronicler has traced two distinct waves of borrowing, each of which has left a layer of vocabulary and a small set of grammatical habits that the fjord speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable strength, suited to the description of the storms, the mountains, the fish, the timber, and the patient business of the long western trade, and capable of being adapted to the specialised vocabularies of the pilot, the miner, and the counting house of the larger fjords.

## Sounds

Stormic is spoken in a register that is sharper and more clipped than the common speech of the central continents, and the chronicler notes that eastern visitors often remark upon the brevity of the fjord sentences and the willingness of the fjord speakers to leave unsaid what the eastern custom would require to be set out at length. The consonants of Stormic are numerous, and the language distinguishes a number of sounds that the eastern speech has merged, particularly in the cluster of unvoiced stops that occur at the beginning of many fjord words. The vowels are fewer than in the eastern speech, and the chronicler records that the fjord singers use the consonantal range to produce the striking percussive effects that mark the songs of the storm festivals, and the songs are one of the most distinctive of the cultural expressions of the highland communities. The pitch of the voice is treated by the fjord speakers as a matter of custom rather than of meaning, and the chronicler has observed that the fjord speakers tend to speak with a flatter pitch than the southern speakers, in unconscious imitation of the working register of the boat and the storm.

The script of Stormic is runic, with twenty-eight marks that represent not single sounds but the consonant clusters and vowel groups that the chronicler has identified as the basic units of the spoken language. The script is read from left to right in horizontal lines, and the lines are arranged from the top of the page to the bottom. The script is descended from the marks that the earliest fjord chroniclers cut into the dark stone of the headlands, and the chronicler has identified a small number of borrowings from the dwarven script of the highland mines in the marks used for the sounds that the older fjord script did not distinguish. The marks are incised into stone or metal for the records of the highland communities, and painted or printed upon parchment for the correspondence of the larger fjords, and the writing is taught in the schools of the principal highland settlements to children of every species who require it for the business of the fjord trade. The chronicler has noted that the script of the [Thunderback Range](/content/location/natural/feature/thunderback-range) is distinguished by a set of decorative marks that the range scribes add to the headings of the storm tallies, and the marks are treated by the highland speakers as a sign of the antiquity of the mines.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Stormic is rich in words for the storms, the mountains, the fish, the timber, the ores, and the patient business of the highland trade, and the chronicler records that the language has more words for the kinds of storm, the kinds of mountain, the kinds of fish, and the stages of the miner's art than any of the languages of the eastern continents. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the clan, the council, and the customary relations of the highland communities, for the highland populations place great weight upon the distinctions of the mining clans and the long memory of the [Thunderback Range](/content/location/natural/feature/thunderback-range). The chronicler notes that the highland compounds are formed by the joining of two or more of the simple words, and the compounds are used in the formal speech of the clan councils and the songs of the storm festivals.

Stormic has borrowed from the dwarven speech of the highland mines a set of words for the metals, the stones, the ores, and the tools of the workshop that the fjord communities have required for the conduct of the highland trade. The borrowings are recognised by the fjord speakers as belonging to the trade of the miner rather than to the craft of the pilot, and the chronicler records that the fjord speakers are careful to use the dwarven word in the dwarven context and the fjord word in the fjord context. The language has also borrowed a small number of words from the [Morvindi](/content/language/spoken/morvindi) trade tongue through the ships that cross the long western waters, and the borrowings are chiefly the names of the eastern goods, the eastern customs, and the eastern offices that the fjord merchants have required for the conduct of the long-distance trade.

## Speech

Stormic is the language of the great fjords of the northern shore, and the fjords are worked in a register that is patient and precise, suited to the long observation of the weather and the careful timing of the sailing seasons. The fjord register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a voyage, the inspection of a cargo, and the closing of a landing, and the phrases are known to every pilot of the principal fjords. The chronicler has observed that the phrases are intelligible to children of ten or eleven years who have been brought up in the pilot families, and the intelligibility is a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from parent to child in the highland communities of the long coast.

Stormic is also the language of the storm councils of the highland communities, and the councils are conducted in a more formal register that is preserved by the elders of the larger fjords and the principal mining clans of the [Thunderback Range](/content/location/natural/feature/thunderback-range). The formal register is used for the public announcements of the fjord magistrates, the reading of the customary law, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and the register is intelligible to any speaker of Stormic who has had the benefit of a customary education in the boat or the mine. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the highland interior, and the borrowings are treated by the highland speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The chronicler has noted that the formal register is used with care in the councils of the principal fjords, and the care is a measure of the importance that the highland communities attach to the conduct of public business in a language that the pilots and the miners understand and trust.

## Relations

Stormic stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions that surround [Stormfall](/content/location/natural/continent/stormfall), and the relations are a measure of the long history of contact and exchange that has shaped the highland communities. The chronicler records that Stormic is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the dwarven speech of the highland mines, and the intelligibility is greater in the highland towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Stormic and the [Morvindi](/content/language/spoken/morvindi) of the coast of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) is the subject of scholarly dispute, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another through the long western routes, and the chronicler has been told by the highland scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the fjord and the eastern merchants at the far ports.

The relation of Stormic to the halfling speech of the southern lowlands is the subject of some scholarly interest, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another over the centuries, and the chronicler has been told by the highland scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the highland and the lowland communities. The chronicler records that the highland speakers tend to treat the halfling speech as a learned accomplishment rather than as a working language, and the halfling communities of the southern lowlands tend to treat Stormic as the necessary medium of the highland trade. The relation of Stormic to the trade tongues that arrive from the eastern continents is closer in vocabulary than in grammar, and the chronicler has been told by the fjord merchants that the eastern speech lacks the precision of the highland tongue in the description of the storms and the mountains.

## Significance

Stormic is the common tongue of [Stormfall](/content/location/natural/continent/stormfall), the language of the great fjords and the storm councils, the medium of the long western trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to dwell or to trade among the dark fjords and the highland valleys of the storm-shaped continent. The language is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary of its own, and it has been refined over the centuries by the long practice of the highland pilots and the patient correction of the councils. The chronicler finds in Stormic one of the most precise of the languages of the known world, and the language is a measure of the central place that the highland fjords have held in the maritime trade and the storm customs of the western continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the dark shore. The language is the foundation of the highland identity, and the highland identity is the foundation of the fjord community, and the two together are the measure of the long attention that the highland communities have given to the conduct of the storm in a tongue that the people understand and trust.
