## Stormic Described

Stormic is the common tongue of [Stormfall](/content/location/natural/continent/stormfall), the storm-shaped continent that lies far to the west of the established northern routes, spoken in the dark fjords of the northern shore, the highland valleys of the [Thunderback Range](/content/location/natural/feature/thunderback-range), and the warmer grasslands of the southern coast. The language is most closely associated with the human population of the coastal settlements and the highland communities. Stormic is spoken as a first language by the [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) communities of the highland mines and as a second language by the [halfling](/content/species/sapient/halflings) communities of the southern lowlands and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the western maritime trade. The name is taken from the storms that fall from the mountains of the interior, and the language is sometimes called the thunderback speech by the coastal communities of the south.

The origins of Stormic lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the dark fjords. The language has grown over the centuries through contact with the dwarven speech of the highland mines, the halfling speech of the southern lowlands, and the trade tongues that arrive by sea from the ports of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) across the long western waters. Two distinct waves of borrowing have left layers of vocabulary and grammatical habits that fjord speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable strength, suited to the description of the storms, the mountains, the fish, the timber, and the patient business of the long western trade, and capable of adaptation to the specialised vocabularies of the pilot, the miner, and the counting house of the larger fjords.

## Pronunciation and Script

Stormic is spoken in a register that is sharper and more clipped than the common speech of the central continents. Eastern visitors often remark upon the brevity of fjord sentences and the willingness of fjord speakers to leave unsaid what the eastern custom would require to be set out at length. The consonants are numerous, and the language distinguishes several sounds the eastern speech has merged, particularly in the cluster of unvoiced stops occurring at the beginning of many fjord words. The vowels are fewer than in the eastern speech. Fjord singers use the consonantal range to produce the striking percussive effects that mark the songs of the storm festivals, one of the most distinctive cultural expressions of the highland communities. Pitch is a matter of custom rather than meaning; fjord speakers tend to speak with a flatter pitch than southern speakers, in unconscious imitation of the working register of the boat and the storm.

The script of Stormic is runic, with twenty-eight marks representing not single sounds but the consonant clusters and vowel groups that are the basic units of the spoken language. The script is read from left to right in horizontal lines. It is descended from the marks the earliest fjord chroniclers cut into the dark stone of the headlands, with borrowings from the dwarven script of the highland mines in the marks for sounds the older fjord script did not distinguish. The marks are incised into stone or metal for records of the highland communities, and painted or printed upon parchment for correspondence of the larger fjords. Writing is taught in schools of the principal highland settlements to children of every species who require it for the business of the fjord trade. The script of the [Thunderback Range](/content/location/natural/feature/thunderback-range) is distinguished by decorative marks added to the headings of storm tallies, treated by highland speakers as a sign of the antiquity of the mines.

## Storm Words and Loans

The vocabulary of Stormic is rich in words for storms, mountains, fish, timber, ores, and the patient business of the highland trade. The language has more words for the kinds of storm, the kinds of mountain, the kinds of fish, and the stages of the miner's art than any of the languages of the eastern continents. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the clan, the council, and the customary relations of the highland communities, for these populations place great weight upon the distinctions of the mining clans and the long memory of the [Thunderback Range](/content/location/natural/feature/thunderback-range). Highland compounds are formed by joining two or more simple words, used in the formal speech of clan councils and the songs of storm festivals.

Stormic has borrowed from the dwarven speech of the highland mines a set of words for metals, stones, ores, and tools of the workshop required for the conduct of the highland trade. These borrowings are recognised as belonging to the trade of the miner rather than the craft of the pilot, and fjord speakers are careful to use the dwarven word in the dwarven context and the fjord word in the fjord context. A small number of words have also been borrowed from the [Morvindi](/content/language/spoken/morvindi) trade tongue through ships crossing the long western waters, chiefly the names of eastern goods, customs, and offices.

## Registers of Stormic

Stormic is the language of the great fjords of the northern shore, worked in a register that is patient and precise, suited to the long observation of the weather and the careful timing of the sailing seasons. The fjord register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a voyage, the inspection of a cargo, and the closing of a landing, known to every pilot of the principal fjords. These phrases are intelligible to children of ten or eleven years brought up in the pilot families.

Stormic is also the language of the storm councils of the highland communities, conducted in a more formal register preserved by the elders of the larger fjords and the principal mining clans of the [Thunderback Range](/content/location/natural/feature/thunderback-range). The formal register is used for public announcements of fjord magistrates, the reading of customary law, and the debates of larger assemblies, intelligible to any speaker of Stormic who has had a customary education in the boat or the mine. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the highland interior, treated as marks of dignity and authority.

## Stormic and Other Tongues

Stormic stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions surrounding [Stormfall](/content/location/natural/continent/stormfall). It is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the dwarven speech of the highland mines, and intelligibility is greater in highland towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Stormic and the [Morvindi](/content/language/spoken/morvindi) of the coast of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) is the subject of scholarly dispute, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another through the long western routes, a measure of the long coexistence of fjord and eastern merchants at the far ports.

The relation of Stormic to the halfling speech of the southern lowlands is of scholarly interest, for the two languages have borrowed vocabulary from one another over the centuries. Highland speakers tend to treat the halfling speech as a learned accomplishment rather than a working language, and halfling communities of the southern lowlands treat Stormic as the necessary medium of the highland trade. The relation of Stormic to the trade tongues arriving from the eastern continents is closer in vocabulary than in grammar; fjord merchants note that the eastern speech lacks the precision of the highland tongue in describing storms and mountains.

## Fjord Speech of the West

Stormic is the common tongue of [Stormfall](/content/location/natural/continent/stormfall), the language of the great fjords and the storm councils, the medium of the long western trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who dwells or trades among the dark fjords and the highland valleys of the storm-shaped continent. It is a true language with grammar and vocabulary of its own, refined over centuries by the long practice of highland pilots and the patient correction of the councils. It is one of the most precise of the languages of the known world, a measure of the central place the highland fjords have held in the maritime trade and storm customs of the western continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the dark shore.
