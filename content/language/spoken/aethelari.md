## Aethelari and Its Domain

Aethelari is the common tongue of the heartland of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), spoken across the river valleys, the eastern coastlands, and the broad grasslands that lie between them. It is the language of the markets and the harbours, the council chambers and the country roads, and the medium through which most of the commerce of the central continent is conducted. The name is taken from the continent itself, and the language is sometimes called the common speech by those who treat it as a default, though no people of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) use the term for their own language without a measure of regional pride. Aethelari is the first language of the human population of the heartland and the second language of nearly every [elf](/content/species/sapient/elves), [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), and [gnome](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) who trades or serves among the towns of the central plains.

The origins of Aethelari lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the river valleys. The language has grown over the centuries through contact with the elven communities of the northern forests, the dwarven halls of the mountain spine, and the [trade tongue](/content/language/pseudo/trade-tongue)s that arrive by sea from [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) and [Thessara](/content/location/natural/region/thessara). At least three distinct waves of borrowing have left layers of vocabulary and grammatical habits that speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable flexibility, suited to the description of farms, laws, and the slow business of governance, and capable of adaptation to the specialised vocabularies of the harbour, the counting house, and the workshop.

## Pronunciation and Script

Aethelari is spoken in a measured and unhurried register. Visitors from the mountain spine often remark upon the evenness of the central speech. The vowels are full and open, the consonants are few and clearly distinguished, and the rhythm of the sentence is carried by the regular accent that falls upon the second syllable of most words of more than one syllable. The language has no tone; pitch is a matter of custom rather than meaning. Elven communities of the northern forests tend to speak Aethelari with a flatter pitch than human speakers of the central plains, in unconscious imitation of elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan).

The script of Aethelari is alphabetic, with twenty-six marks representing the consonants and vowels of the language with little ambiguity. The script is read from left to right in horizontal lines. It is descended from the marks used by the earliest human chroniclers of the river valleys, with borrowings from the elven tree script and the angular signs of the dwarven stonemasons in certain marks. The marks are incised, painted, or printed upon parchment, paper, or cloth. Writing is taught in schools of the larger towns to children of every species who require it for the ordinary business of life.

## Word Stock and Loanwords

The vocabulary of Aethelari is rich in words for the cultivated land, the seasons, the tools of agriculture, and the relations of neighbourhood that have shaped the long history of the heartland. The language has more words for the kinds of grain, kinds of fence, kinds of soil, and stages of the agricultural year than any of the trade tongues of the known world; the richness reflects the attention the central populations have given to the cultivation of the land. The vocabulary is also notable for its careful distinctions of rank, office, and courtesy, for the central continent has long been governed by councils and courts requiring precise language for the conduct of public business.

Aethelari has borrowed freely from the languages of its neighbours, and the borrowings are a record of the relations of the heartland with the wider world. From [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) the language has taken words for kinds of tree, kinds of bird, and small creatures of the forest, for the elven communities of the northern woodlands were the first to name them in terms that satisfied human curiosity. From the dwarven speech of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) the language has taken words for metals, stones, and tools of the workshop, recognised by central speakers as belonging to the trade of the smith and the mason. From the trade tongue of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) ports the language has taken words for the spices, dyes, woven cloths, and precious metals that arrive by the long sea routes, reflecting the importance of the eastern trade to the prosperity of the heartland. The [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) also maintains [Old Vaelic](/content/language/pseudo/old-vaelic), a system of banner signals used by the garrisons of the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael) valley to communicate over distances too great for the voice.

## Registers of the Language

Aethelari is the language of the great markets of the heartland, conducted in a register that is brisk and practical, suited to the rapid exchange of goods and negotiation of prices. The market register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a bargain, the testing of an offer, and the closing of a sale, known to every merchant of the central plains. These phrases are intelligible to children of nine or ten years brought up in the market towns, a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from parent to child in the trading families.

Aethelari is also the language of the councils of the heartland, conducted in a more formal register preserved by the elders of the communities. The formal register is used for public announcements of the magistrates, the reading of the laws, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and is intelligible to any speaker of Aethelari who has had a customary education. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the river valleys, treated by central speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The formal register is used with care in the chambers of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth).

## Aethelari and Neighbouring Tongues

Aethelari stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions surrounding the heartland. It is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the highland [Kameni](/content/language/spoken/kameni) of the mountain spine, and the intelligibility is greater in border towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Aethelari and the southern [Vintic](/content/language/spoken/vintic) is closer still, for the two languages share a long border along the river valleys, and speakers of one are commonly able to follow the speech of the other after a short period of adjustment.

The relation of Aethelari to the speech of the elven communities is the subject of some scholarly dispute. [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) is the first language of the elven population of the northern forests, and Aethelari is the second. Elven scholars maintain that the two languages are of separate origin and share only the relation of long neighbours. Central speakers tend to treat the question as one of little practical concern, for the business of the central markets and councils is conducted in Aethelari by speakers of every species who require a common medium. The relation of Aethelari to the dwarven [Kameni](/content/language/spoken/kameni) is closer in vocabulary than in grammar; central speakers remark upon the dwarven preference for compound terms where the central speech uses a single word.

## Common Tongue of the Heartland

Aethelari is the common tongue of the heartland of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), the language of the markets and the councils, the medium of central commerce, and the second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to trade or serve among the towns of the central plains. It is a true language with grammar and vocabulary of its own, refined over centuries by the long practice of speakers and the patient correction of the schools. It is one of the most widely understood languages of the known world, a measure of the central place the heartland of Aethelar has held in the commerce and governance of the continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the river valleys. The language is the foundation of the central identity, and that identity is the foundation of the central polity.
