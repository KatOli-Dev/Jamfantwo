## Overview

Aethelari is the common tongue of the heartland of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), spoken across the river valleys, the eastern coastlands, and the broad grasslands that lie between them. It is the language of the markets and the harbours, the council chambers and the country roads, and the medium through which most of the commerce of the central continent is conducted. The name is taken from the continent itself, and the language is sometimes called the common speech by those who treat it as a default, though the chronicler records that no people of Aethelar use the term for their own language without a measure of regional pride. Aethelari is the first language of the human population of the heartland and the second language of nearly every [elf](/content/species/sapient/elves), [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), and [gnome](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) who trades or serves among the towns of the central plains.

The origins of Aethelari lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the river valleys, and the language has grown over the centuries through contact with the elven communities of the northern forests, the dwarven halls of the mountain spine, and the trade tongues that arrive by sea from [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) and [Thessara](/content/location/natural/continent/thessara). The chronicler has traced at least three distinct waves of borrowing, each of which has left a layer of vocabulary and a small set of grammatical habits that the speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable flexibility, suited to the description of farms and laws and the slow business of governance, and capable of being adapted to the specialised vocabularies of the harbour, the counting house, and the workshop.

## Sounds

Aethelari is spoken in a measured and unhurried register, and the chronicler notes that visitors from the mountain spine often remark upon the evenness of the central speech. The vowels are full and open, the consonants are few and clearly distinguished, and the rhythm of the sentence is carried by the regular accent that falls upon the second syllable of most words of more than one syllable. The language has no tone, and the pitch of the voice is treated by the speakers as a matter of custom rather than of meaning, though the chronicler has observed that the elven communities of the northern forests tend to speak Aethelari with a flatter pitch than the human speakers of the central plains, in unconscious imitation of the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan).

The script of Aethelari is alphabetic, with twenty-six marks that represent the consonants and vowels of the language with little ambiguity. The script is read from left to right in horizontal lines, and the lines are arranged from the top of the page to the bottom. The script is descended from the marks used by the earliest human chroniclers of the river valleys, and the chronicler has identified borrowings in the form of certain marks from the elven tree script and from the angular signs of the dwarven stonemasons. The marks are incised, painted, or printed upon parchment, paper, or cloth, and the writing is taught in the schools of the larger towns to children of every species who require it for the ordinary business of life.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Aethelari is rich in words for the cultivated land, the seasons, the tools of agriculture, and the relations of neighbourhood that have shaped the long history of the heartland. The chronicler records that the language has more words for the kinds of grain, the kinds of fence, the kinds of soil, and the stages of the agricultural year than any of the trade tongues of the known world, and the richness is a measure of the attention that the central populations have given to the cultivation of the land. The vocabulary is also notable for its careful distinctions of rank, of office, and of courtesy, for the central continent has long been governed by councils and courts that have required a precise language for the conduct of public business.

Aethelari has borrowed freely from the languages of its neighbours, and the borrowings are a record of the relations of the heartland with the wider world. From [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) the language has taken a set of words for the kinds of tree, the kinds of bird, and the small creatures of the forest, for the elven communities of the northern woodlands were the first to name them in terms that satisfied the human curiosity. From the dwarven speech of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) the language has taken words for the metals, the stones, and the tools of the workshop, and the borrowings are recognised by the central speakers as belonging to the trade of the smith and the mason. From the trade tongue of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) ports the language has taken words for the spices, the dyes, the woven cloths, and the precious metals that arrive by the long sea routes, and the borrowings are a measure of the importance of the eastern trade to the prosperity of the heartland.

## Speech

Aethelari is the language of the great markets of the heartland, and the markets are conducted in a register that is brisk and practical, suited to the rapid exchange of goods and the negotiation of prices. The market register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a bargain, the testing of an offer, and the closing of a sale, and the phrases are known to every merchant of the central plains. The chronicler has observed that the phrases are intelligible to children of nine or ten years who have been brought up in the market towns, and the intelligibility is a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from parent to child in the trading families.

Aethelari is also the language of the councils of the heartland, and the councils are conducted in a more formal register that is preserved by the elders of the communities. The formal register is used for the public announcements of the magistrates, the reading of the laws, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and the register is intelligible to any speaker of Aethelari who has had the benefit of a customary education. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the river valleys, and the borrowings are treated by the central speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The chronicler has noted that the formal register is used with care in the chambers of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth), and the care is a measure of the importance that the central polities attach to the conduct of public business in a language that the people understand.

## Relations

Aethelari stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions that surround the heartland, and the relations are a measure of the long history of contact and exchange that has shaped the central continent. The chronicler records that Aethelari is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the highland [Kameni](/content/language/spoken/kameni) of the mountain spine, and the intelligibility is greater in the border towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Aethelari and the southern [Vintic](/content/language/spoken/vintic) is closer still, for the two languages share a long border along the river valleys, and the speakers of the one are commonly able to follow the speech of the other after a short period of adjustment.

The relation of Aethelari to the speech of the elven communities is the subject of some scholarly dispute, for [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) is the first language of the elven population of the northern forests, and Aethelari is the second. The chronicler has been told by the elven scholars that the two languages are of separate origin and have only the relation of long neighbours, and the chronicler records that the central speakers tend to treat the question as one of little practical concern, for the business of the central markets and the central councils is conducted in Aethelari by speakers of every species who require a common medium. The relation of Aethelari to the dwarven [Kameni](/content/language/spoken/kameni) is closer in vocabulary than in grammar, and the central speakers are said to remark upon the dwarven preference for compound terms where the central speech uses a single word.

## Significance

Aethelari is the common tongue of the heartland of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), the language of the markets and the councils, the medium of the central commerce, and the second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to trade or to serve among the towns of the central plains. The language is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary of its own, and it has been refined over the centuries by the long practice of the speakers and the patient correction of the schools. The chronicler finds in Aethelari one of the most widely understood of the languages of the known world, and the language is a measure of the central place that the heartland of Aethelar has held in the commerce and the governance of the continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the river valleys. The language is the foundation of the central identity, and the central identity is the foundation of the central polity, and the two together are the measure of the long attention that the heartland has given to the conduct of public business in a tongue that the people understand and trust.
