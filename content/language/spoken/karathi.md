## Overview

Karathi is the speech of the interior highlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), used in the sheltered plateaus, the mountain valleys, and the great dwarven halls that lie beneath the central ranges. The language is most closely associated with the dwarven population of the high country, but the chronicler records that Karathi is spoken as a first language by the human communities of the upper valleys and as a second language by the [elf](/content/species/sapient/elves) communities of the lower slopes and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the highland trade. The name is taken from an old highland word for the central range, and the language is sometimes called the hall speech by the coastal communities of the north, who treat it as the medium of the dwarven commerce rather than as a learned accomplishment.

The origins of Karathi lie in the speech of the earliest dwarven settlements of the central ranges, and the language has grown over the centuries through contact with the [Morvindi](/content/language/spoken/morvindi) of the coastal lowlands, the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the sheltered valleys, and the trade tongues that arrive by sea from [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) across the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea). The chronicler has traced two distinct waves of borrowing, each of which has left a layer of vocabulary and a small set of grammatical habits that the highland speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable gravity, suited to the description of stone, of metal, of the long winters, and the patient business of the halls, and capable of being adapted to the specialised vocabularies of the mine, the forge, and the council chamber of the highland clans.

## Sounds

Karathi is spoken in a register that is deeper and more resonant than the common speech of the coastal lowlands, and the chronicler notes that lowland visitors often remark upon the slowness of the highland speech and the weight that the highland speakers place upon the closing consonants of every word. The consonants of Karathi are numerous, and the language distinguishes a number of sounds that the lowland speech has merged, particularly in the cluster of voiced stops that occur at the end of many highland words. The vowels are fewer than in the lowland speech, and the chronicler records that the highland singers use the consonantal range to produce the long sustained notes of the clan songs, and the songs are one of the most distinctive of the cultural expressions of the highland communities. The pitch of the voice is treated by the highland speakers as a matter of custom rather than of meaning, and the chronicler has observed that the highland speakers tend to speak with a lower pitch than the lowland speakers, in unconscious imitation of the resonance of the great halls.

The script of Karathi is alphabetic, with thirty-two marks that represent the consonants and vowels of the language. The script is read from left to right in horizontal lines, and the lines are arranged from the top of the page to the bottom. The script is descended from the angular signs that the dwarven stonemasons cut into the lintels of the highland halls, and the chronicler has identified a small number of borrowings from the elven tree script in the marks used for the sounds that the older dwarven script did not distinguish. The marks are incised into stone or metal for the records of the highland clans, and painted or printed upon parchment for the correspondence of the upper valleys, and the writing is taught in the schools of the larger highland settlements to children of every species who require it for the business of the mountain trade. The chronicler has noted that the script of the [Deep Realm of Karhold](/content/government/national/deep-realm-of-karhold) is distinguished by a set of decorative marks that the clan scribes add to the headings of the genealogies, and the marks are treated by the highland speakers as a sign of the antiquity of the clans.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Karathi is rich in words for stone, for metal, for the ores, for the tools, and the patient business of the mine and the forge, and the chronicler records that the language has more words for the grades of ore, the kinds of stone, the kinds of vein, and the stages of the miner's work than any of the languages of the lowlands. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the clan, the lineage, and the customary relations of kinship that govern the highland halls, for the dwarven communities place great weight upon the distinctions of descent and of marriage, and the distinctions are preserved in a set of compound terms that the lowland scholars find difficult to translate. The chronicler notes that the highland compounds are formed by the joining of two or more of the simple words, and the compounds are used in the formal speech of the clan councils and the songs of the winter festivals.

Karathi has borrowed from the [Morvindi](/content/language/spoken/morvindi) of the coastal lowlands a set of words for the sea, the boats, and the fish that the highland communities have required for the conduct of trade with the lowland harbours. The borrowings are recognised by the highland speakers as belonging to the trade of the merchant rather than to the craft of the miner, and the chronicler records that the highland speakers are careful to use the lowland word in the lowland context and the highland word in the highland context. The language has also borrowed a small number of words from the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the sheltered valleys, and the borrowings are chiefly the names of the trees, the birds, and the small creatures of the lower slopes that the highland clans have required for the description of the materials of their craft.

## Speech

Karathi is the language of the great halls of the highland clans, and the halls are conducted in a register that is formal and measured, suited to the long deliberations of the clan councils and the patient negotiation of the matters that come before them. The hall register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a council, the addressing of an elder, and the closing of a deliberation, and the phrases are known to every member of the principal clans. The chronicler has observed that the phrases are intelligible to children of twelve or thirteen years who have been brought up in the halls, and the intelligibility is a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from parent to child in the clan families of the high country.

Karathi is also the language of the clan councils of the highland communities, and the councils are conducted in a still more formal register that is preserved by the elders of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) and the principal halls of the central ranges. The formal register is used for the public announcements of the clan magistrates, the reading of the customary law, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and the register is intelligible to any speaker of Karathi who has had the benefit of a customary education in the hall or the workshop. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the highland halls, and the borrowings are treated by the highland speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The chronicler has noted that the formal register is used with care in the chambers of the principal clans, and the care is a measure of the importance that the highland communities attach to the conduct of public business in a language that the clansmen understand and trust.

## Relations

Karathi stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions that surround the interior highlands, and the relations are a measure of the long history of contact and exchange that has shaped the highland communities. The chronicler records that Karathi is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the coastal [Morvindi](/content/language/spoken/morvindi) of the lowlands, and the intelligibility is greater in the foothill towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Karathi and the highland [Kameni](/content/language/spoken/kameni) of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) is closer still, for the two languages share a long history of contact across the [Strait of Velden](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), and the speakers of the one are commonly able to follow the speech of the other after a short period of adjustment, though the chronicler has been told by the highland smiths that the southern speech lacks the precision of the highland tongue in the description of the metals and the stones.

The relation of Karathi to the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) is the subject of some scholarly dispute, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another over the centuries, and the chronicler has been told by the highland scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the highland and the valley communities. The chronicler records that the highland speakers tend to treat Quessan as a learned accomplishment rather than as a working language, and the elven communities of the valleys tend to treat Karathi as the necessary medium of the mountain trade. The relation of Karathi to the dwarven speech of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) is the subject of dispute among the scholars of the highland halls, and the chronicler has been unable to determine the extent of the kinship between the two, for the highland scholars are said to remark that the question is not one that the highland people are inclined to answer.

## Significance

Karathi is the speech of the interior highlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), the language of the great halls and the clan councils, the medium of the highland trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to dwell or to trade among the sheltered plateaus and the mountain valleys of the central ranges. The language is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary of its own, and it has been refined over the centuries by the long practice of the highland clansmen and the patient correction of the workshops. The chronicler finds in Karathi one of the most resonant of the languages of the known world, and the language is a measure of the central place that the highland halls have held in the metalwork and the governance of the continent since the earliest dwarven settlements were established along the central ranges. The language is the foundation of the highland identity, and the highland identity is the foundation of the highland clan, and the two together are the measure of the long attention that the highland communities have given to the conduct of the council in a tongue that the clansmen understand and trust.
