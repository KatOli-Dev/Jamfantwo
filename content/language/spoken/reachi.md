## Overview

Reachi is the common tongue of the [Eastern Reach](/content/location/natural/continent/eastern-reach), the long and irregular continent that extends beyond the far side of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The language is most closely associated with the human population of the river plains and the western ports, but the chronicler records that Reachi is spoken as a first language by the [halfling](/content/species/sapient/halflings) communities of the river margins and as a second language by the [elf](/content/species/sapient/elves) communities of the wooded lowlands and by every merchant who requires a working knowledge of the long eastern maritime trade. The name is taken from the continent itself, and the language is sometimes called the reach speech by the inland communities of the central plains, who treat it as the medium of the coast commerce rather than as a learned accomplishment.

The origins of Reachi lie in the speech of the earliest human settlements of the western coast, and the language has grown over the centuries through contact with the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded lowlands, the halfling speech of the river margins, and the trade tongues that arrive by sea from the ports of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) across the long ocean routes. The chronicler has traced two distinct waves of borrowing, each of which has left a layer of vocabulary and a small set of grammatical habits that the coastal speakers no longer recognise as foreign. The result is a language of considerable openness, suited to the description of the rivers, the plains, the salt grass, and the patient business of the long maritime trade, and capable of being adapted to the specialised vocabularies of the pilot, the navigator, and the counting house of the western ports.

## Sounds

Reachi is spoken in a register that is more clipped and more direct than the common speech of the heartland of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and the chronicler notes that western visitors often remark upon the brevity of the coastal sentences and the willingness of the coastal speakers to leave unsaid what the western custom would require to be set out at length. The consonants of Reachi are sharply distinguished, the vowels are few and clear, and the rhythm of the sentence is carried by the regular accent that falls upon the first syllable of most words of more than one syllable. The chronicler records that the coastal singers use the consonantal range to produce the striking rhythmic effects that mark the songs of the river pilots, and the songs are one of the most distinctive of the cultural expressions of the coastal communities. The pitch of the voice is treated by the coastal speakers as a matter of custom rather than of meaning, and the chronicler has observed that the coastal speakers tend to speak with a flatter pitch than the inland speakers, in unconscious imitation of the working register of the boats and the long shore.

The script of Reachi is alphabetic, with twenty-six marks that represent the consonants and vowels of the language. The script is read from left to right in horizontal lines, and the lines are arranged from the top of the page to the bottom. The script is descended from the marks used by the earliest human chroniclers of the western coast, and the chronicler has identified a small number of borrowings from the elven tree script in the marks used for the sounds that the older coastal script did not distinguish. The marks are painted or printed upon parchment, paper, or cloth, and the writing is taught in the schools of the larger coastal towns to children of every species who require it for the ordinary business of life. The chronicler has noted that the script of the principal estuaries is distinguished by a set of contraction marks that the estuary scribes use in the records of the river pilots, and the marks are treated by the coastal speakers as a sign of the practicality of the trade.

## Vocabulary and Borrowing

The vocabulary of Reachi is rich in words for the rivers, the estuaries, the boats, the salt grass, and the patient business of the long maritime trade, and the chronicler records that the language has more words for the kinds of river, the kinds of estuary, the kinds of boat, and the stages of the pilot's art than any of the languages of the western continents. The vocabulary is also rich in words for the village, the council, and the customary relations of the coastal communities, for the coastal populations place great weight upon the distinctions of the river clans and the long memory of the long shore. The chronicler notes that the coastal compounds are formed by the joining of two or more of the simple words, and the compounds are used in the formal speech of the village councils and the songs of the spring festivals.

Reachi has borrowed from the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded lowlands a set of words for the trees, the birds, and the small creatures of the forests that the coastal communities have required for the conduct of the inland trade. The borrowings are recognised by the coastal speakers as belonging to the trade of the forester rather than to the craft of the pilot, and the chronicler records that the coastal speakers are careful to use the elven word in the elven context and the coastal word in the coastal context. The language has also borrowed a small number of words from the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) trade tongue through the long ocean routes, and the borrowings are chiefly the names of the western goods, the western customs, and the western offices that the coastal merchants have required for the conduct of the long-distance trade.

## Speech

Reachi is the language of the great estuaries of the [Eastern Reach](/content/location/natural/continent/eastern-reach), and the estuaries are worked in a register that is patient and precise, suited to the long observation of the tides and the careful timing of the sailing seasons. The estuary register uses a set of fixed phrases for the opening of a voyage, the inspection of a cargo, and the closing of a landing, and the phrases are known to every pilot of the principal estuaries. The chronicler has observed that the phrases are intelligible to children of nine or ten years who have been brought up in the pilot families, and the intelligibility is a measure of the care with which the forms are transmitted from parent to child in the coastal communities of the long shore.

Reachi is also the language of the long-shore councils of the coastal communities, and the councils are conducted in a more formal register that is preserved by the elders of the larger estuaries and the principal ports of the western coast. The formal register is used for the public announcements of the village magistrates, the reading of the customary law, and the debates of the larger assemblies, and the register is intelligible to any speaker of Reachi who has had the benefit of a customary education in the boat or the council. The formal register borrows a small number of phrases from the older speech of the western coast, and the borrowings are treated by the coastal speakers as marks of dignity and authority. The chronicler has noted that the formal register is used with care in the councils of the principal estuaries, and the care is a measure of the importance that the coastal communities attach to the conduct of public business in a language that the villagers understand and trust.

## Relations

Reachi stands in a complex relation to the languages of the regions that surround the [Eastern Reach](/content/location/natural/continent/eastern-reach), and the relations are a measure of the long history of contact and exchange that has shaped the coastal communities. The chronicler records that Reachi is intelligible, with some adjustment, to speakers of the halfling speech of the river margins, and the intelligibility is greater in the river towns than in the deeper interior of either tongue. The relationship between Reachi and the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) of the heartland of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) is the subject of scholarly dispute, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another through the long ocean routes, and the chronicler has been told by the coastal scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the coastal and the western merchants at the far ports.

The relation of Reachi to the elven [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan) of the wooded lowlands is the subject of some scholarly interest, for the two languages have borrowed a small vocabulary from one another over the centuries, and the chronicler has been told by the coastal scholars that the borrowings are a measure of the long coexistence of the coastal and the elven communities. The chronicler records that the coastal speakers tend to treat Quessan as a learned accomplishment rather than as a working language, and the elven communities of the wooded lowlands tend to treat Reachi as the necessary medium of the long-shore trade. The relation of Reachi to the inland speech of the central plains is closer in vocabulary than in grammar, and the chronicler has been told by the inland communities that their speech is a separate language, though the speakers of Reachi are commonly able to follow it after a short period of adjustment.

## Significance

Reachi is the common tongue of the [Eastern Reach](/content/location/natural/continent/eastern-reach), the language of the great estuaries and the long-shore councils, the medium of the long maritime trade, and the first or second language of nearly every speaker of every species who has occasion to dwell or to trade among the river plains and the western ports of the long continent. The language is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary of its own, and it has been refined over the centuries by the long practice of the coastal pilots and the patient correction of the village councils. The chronicler finds in Reachi one of the most direct of the languages of the known world, and the language is a measure of the central place that the western coast has held in the boat trade and the long-shore customs of the eastern continent since the earliest human settlements were established along the long estuaries of the interior. The language is the foundation of the coastal identity, and the coastal identity is the foundation of the long-shore community, and the two together are the measure of the long attention that the coastal communities have given to the conduct of the estuary in a tongue that the villagers understand and trust.
