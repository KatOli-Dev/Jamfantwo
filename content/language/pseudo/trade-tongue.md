## Overview

The Trade Tongue is the name given to the system of hand signals used by the merchants and the factors of the great trading routes to communicate across the noise and the distance of the crowded marketplaces and the busy harbours of the known world. It is not a language in the ordinary sense, for it has no grammar and no vocabulary of the kind that is written or spoken. It is instead a code of gestures, finger positions, and hand movements that conveys the essential information of commerce across the crowded spaces of the ports and the bazaars. The system is used by the traders of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), the caravan masters of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), the factors of the [League of the Northern Coast](/content/government/national/league-of-the-northern-coast), and the agents of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), and its signals are understood by every merchant who deals in the long-distance trade, whatever the language of his native speech.

## The Nature of the Signals

The signals of the Trade Tongue are produced by the hands and the fingers, and they are distinguished one from another by the position of the hand, the number of the fingers extended, and the movement that is made. The closed fist, raised to the level of the chest, is a signal of inquiry, asking whether the other party wishes to deal. The open hand, with the palm turned outward, is a signal of refusal, indicating that the merchant is not interested in the offer that has been made. The hand held at the side, with a single finger extended, indicates a price of one unit of the local currency, and the number of the fingers extended indicates the number of the units that are proposed. The hand moved in a circular motion, with the palm turned downward, is a signal of negotiation, indicating that the merchant is willing to adjust his price.

The signals are exchanged in a rapid and a practised sequence, and a negotiation conducted in the Trade Tongue can move from offer to acceptance or refusal in a matter of moments. The system allows the merchants to discuss the prices and the terms of the trade without the use of the spoken voice, which is often drowned by the noise of the market, and without the use of the written word, which would require the presence of a scribe and the delay of the writing. The signals are also useful in the circumstances where the merchants do not share a common spoken language, for the hand codes of the Trade Tongue are understood by traders from all three continents, whatever the speech of their homeland.

## Centres of Use

The use of the Trade Tongue is most extensive in the great marketplaces of the trading cities, where the merchants of many peoples gather to exchange the goods of the known world. The markets of Amurra are the principal centres of the system, for the Amurran traders are among the most active users of the hand signals, and the marketplace of the city is the place where the conventions of the Trade Tongue are most fully developed. The harbours of the [Strait of Velden](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) are another centre of the system, for the strait is the meeting point of the eastern and the western trade, and the ships that pass through the narrows bring merchants from all three continents who must communicate across the barriers of language and custom.

The caravan stops of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) are also places where the Trade Tongue is used, for the caravans that cross the desert bring traders from the coastal cities and the interior settlements who must negotiate the terms of the passage and the exchange of goods. The signal system is well suited to the conditions of the caravan stop, where the noise of the animals and the bustle of the camp make the spoken voice difficult to hear, and where the traders who meet may not share a common spoken language.

## Relation to the Spoken Languages

The Trade Tongue is distinct from the spoken languages of the trading peoples, for the spoken tongues are true languages with grammar and vocabulary, used for the ordinary commerce of life and for the preservation of the traditions and the histories of the peoples. The hand signals, by contrast, are a code of limited scope, capable of conveying only the information that is relevant to the negotiation of a trade, and they are used only for the specific purposes of the bargaining and the exchange. The merchants who use the Trade Tongue also speak the spoken languages of their own peoples, and they use the spoken word for the conversations that go beyond the terms of the trade.

Because the code is limited, prudent merchants use it to establish quantities and immediate intent rather than to settle every obligation of a bargain. Quality, delivery, credit, penalties, and guarantees are transferred to speech or writing once the initial terms have been understood. Each major market appoints recognised factors who teach the standard forms and resolve disputes over an ambiguous gesture. Local variations exist, especially in signs for measures and coinage, but a trader is expected to state the unit visibly before naming a number, preventing a familiar sign from concealing an unfamiliar value.

## Significance

The Trade Tongue is the hand of the merchant in the crowded marketplaces of the known world, the means by which the traders of many peoples conduct the business of commerce across the barriers of language and the noise of the market. Without the hand signals, the negotiations of the great trading routes would be a far slower and a far more difficult undertaking, dependent upon the uncertain translation of the interpreters and the delays of the written word. The system is a practical invention, born of the necessities of the marketplace, and it is a measure of the ingenuity by which the trading peoples of the world have overcome the barriers that separate them. In the noise and the bustle of the harbour where the traveller hears only the confusion of many tongues, the merchant sees the passing of the Trade Tongue, the silent signs that move the goods of the known world from one shore to the next.
