## Overview

Sylvan is the name given by the scholars of the surface to the system of forest signals used by the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the smaller communities of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to communicate through the deep woodland without the use of the spoken voice. It is not a language in the ordinary sense, for it has no grammar and no vocabulary of the kind that the other peoples recognise as speech. It is instead a code of bird calls, wood knocks, and leaf rustles that carries information through the forest canopy and the undergrowth. The system is used by the elven hunters, the forest wardens, and the messengers who travel the woodland paths, and its signals are understood by every elf who walks the forest, whatever the dialect of his spoken tongue.

## The Nature of the Signals

The signals of Sylvan are produced by a variety of means, each suited to a particular distance and a particular condition of the forest. The most common signals are bird calls, which the elves have learned to imitate with such accuracy that even the birds themselves are deceived, and which carry through the canopy for distances much greater than the human voice can manage. A particular call, that of the forest thrush repeated three times at intervals, is a signal of greeting, informing the hearer that a friend approaches. A rapid series of woodpecker knocks, struck upon a hollow branch, is a signal of danger, warning the hearer of the presence of strangers or of predators. The sound of a dry leaf crushed beneath the foot, produced deliberately in a particular rhythm, is a signal of passage, informing the hearer that a messenger travels the woodland path and that the way is to be kept clear.

The range of the signals is determined by the density of the forest and the nature of the sounds that are used, and a bird call from a skilled elven caller can be heard at a distance of half a mile or more in the quiet of the forest morning. The rustle of leaves, by contrast, carries only a short distance, but it is well suited to the close quarters of the undergrowth, where the louder calls would alert the quarry or the stranger. The elves have thus developed a layered system of signals, in which the bird calls are used for the long distances and the treetops, the wood knocks for the middle distances, and the leaf rustles for the close work of the hunt and the stealthy approach.

## Centres of Use

The use of Sylvan is most extensive in the deep forests of Aethelar, where the elven communities maintain their ancient ways and where the woodland signals are as much a part of the forest as the birds and the streams. The hunters and the forest wardens are the most frequent users of the system, for their work requires them to move through the forest in silence and to communicate their observations and their intentions to one another without alarming the game. The messengers who travel between the scattered elven settlements also make use of Sylvan, sending signals ahead to announce their approach and to request the hospitality of the settlement.

The human peoples who live near the elven forests are generally aware of the existence of Sylvan, but few have learned to read its signals. The calls of the elven system are easily mistaken for the ordinary sounds of the forest by an ear that is not trained to distinguish them, and the deliberate rhythm of the knocks and the rustles is not easily perceived by one who does not know what to listen for. The human hunters who venture into the elven forests are sometimes taught a limited set of signals for practical purposes, such as the call that warns of danger and the call that summons aid, but the deeper codes of the Sylvan system are kept within the elven community.

## Relation to the Spoken Tongue

Sylvan is distinct from the spoken language of the elves, which is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary, used for the ordinary commerce of life and for the preservation of the elven traditions and the elven learning. The forest signals, by contrast, are a code of limited scope, capable of conveying only a fixed set of meanings, and they are used only for the specific purposes of the hunt and the walk and the stealthy approach. The spoken tongue of the elves, which the elves call Quessan, is a language of words and sentences, used for the conversation of the hearth and the song of the festival. The Sylvan signals, by contrast, are the voice of the forest itself, the means by which the elven people move through the woodland in harmony with the birds and the beasts that share their home.

## Significance

Sylvan is the voice of the deep forest, the means by which the elven people maintain their connection with the woodland and with one another across the distances of the canopy. Without the forest signals, the hunters and the wardens of the elven communities would be forced to rely upon the spoken voice, which would alarm the game and reveal their presence to the strangers and the enemies who move through the forest. The system is a practical invention, born of the necessities of the woodland life, and it is a measure of the elven harmony with the natural world. In the quiet of the forest where the traveller hears only the birds, the elf hears the call of Sylvan, carrying the news and the warnings of the woodland from one tree to the next.
