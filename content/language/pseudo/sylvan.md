## Forest Signals

Sylvan is the name given by scholars of the surface to the system of forest signals used by the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the smaller communities of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to communicate through the deep woodland without the use of the spoken voice. It is a code of bird calls, wood knocks, and leaf rustles that carries information through the forest canopy and undergrowth. Elven hunters, forest wardens, and messengers travelling the woodland paths use the system, and its signals are understood by every elf who walks the forest, whatever the dialect of his spoken tongue. The elves have developed a layered system of signals, with bird calls used for long distances and the treetops, wood knocks for middle distances, and leaf rustles for the close work of the hunt and stealthy approach.

## Bird Calls, Knocks, and Rustles

The signals of Sylvan are produced by a variety of means, each suited to a particular distance and condition of the forest. The most common signals are bird calls, which elves imitate with such accuracy that even the birds themselves are deceived, carrying through the canopy for distances much greater than the human voice can manage. A particular call — that of the forest thrush repeated three times at intervals — is a signal of greeting, informing the hearer that a friend approaches. A rapid series of woodpecker knocks struck upon a hollow branch is a signal of danger, warning of the presence of strangers or predators. The sound of a dry leaf crushed beneath the foot, produced deliberately in a particular rhythm, is a signal of passage, informing the hearer that a messenger travels the woodland path and the way is to be kept clear.

The range of the signals is determined by the density of the forest and the nature of the sounds used. A bird call from a skilled elven caller can be heard at a distance of half a mile or more in the quiet of the forest morning. The rustle of leaves carries only a short distance but is well suited to the close quarters of the undergrowth, where louder calls would alert quarry or strangers. The system is thus layered by necessity. A warden working the forest canopy may use bird calls to coordinate with a partner working the forest floor, each adjusting the signal to the depth of the cover between them. The same signal may be produced by different means at different distances: a greeting that sounds as a thrush call at long range becomes a pair of soft wood knocks at close quarters and a specific rustle of leaves within sight of the other party.

A skilled warden learns not merely to reproduce each sound but to place it within the natural rhythm of the wood. A call made too often, at the wrong hour, or in a season when the imitated bird is absent would reveal the caller as surely as a shouted word. For this reason the common meanings have several forms, and the caller chooses among them according to the birds, weather, and cover present in that part of the forest. The code changes slowly as migrations and habitats alter, with wardens meeting at seasonal gatherings to agree upon replacements for signals that no longer pass unnoticed.

## Forests of Aethelar

Sylvan is most extensively used in the deep forests of Aethelar, where elven communities maintain their ancient ways and the woodland signals are as much a part of the forest as the birds and streams. Hunters and forest wardens are the most frequent users, for their work requires them to move through the forest in silence and communicate observations and intentions without alarming the game. Messengers travelling between scattered elven settlements also make use of Sylvan, sending signals ahead to announce their approach and request hospitality.

Human peoples living near the elven forests are generally aware of Sylvan, but few have learned to read its signals. The calls of the elven system are easily mistaken for ordinary sounds of the forest by an untrained ear, and the deliberate rhythm of knocks and rustles is not easily perceived by one who does not know what to listen for. Human hunters venturing into the elven forests are sometimes taught a limited set of signals for practical purposes — the call that warns of danger and the call that summons aid — but the deeper codes of the Sylvan system are kept within the elven community.

## Sylvan and Quessan

Sylvan is distinct from the spoken language of the elves, which is a true language with grammar and vocabulary, used for the ordinary commerce of life and for the preservation of elven traditions and learning. Quessan has changed slowly over centuries, its forms carefully maintained by oral tradition, while Sylvan evolves more rapidly as habitats and bird populations shift. The forest signals, by contrast, are a code of limited scope, capable of conveying only a fixed set of meanings, used only for the specific purposes of the hunt, the walk, and the stealthy approach. The spoken tongue of the elves, called [Quessan](/content/language/spoken/quessan), is a language of words and sentences used for the conversation of the hearth and the song of the festival. The Sylvan signals are the voice of the forest itself, the means by which the elven people move through the woodland in harmony with the birds and beasts that share their home.

## Voice of the Deep Forest

Sylvan is the voice of the deep forest, the means by which the elven people maintain their connection with the woodland and with one another across the distances of the canopy. Without the forest signals, the hunters and wardens of the elven communities would be forced to rely upon the spoken voice, which would alarm the game and reveal their presence to strangers and enemies moving through the forest. The system is a practical invention born of the necessities of the woodland life, a measure of the elven harmony with the natural world. In the quiet of the forest where the traveller hears only the birds, the elf hears the call of Sylvan, carrying news and warnings of the woodland from one tree to the next.
