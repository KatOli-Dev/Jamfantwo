## Banner Signals of the Vael

Old Vaelic is the name given by the scholars of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) to the system of signal banners and standing flags by which the court and the garrisons of the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael) valley communicate over distances too great for the voice to carry. It is a code of colours, patterns, and sequences displayed upon poles and towers that conveys information across the fields and fortifications of the kingdom. Castle watchmen, garrison signalmen, and royal messengers use the system to send warnings, relay commands, and coordinate the movements of troops and goods across the length of the valley. The signalmen of the valley maintain a chain of relay stations, each within sight of the next, allowing a message flown at one end of the valley to reach the other in a matter of hours.

## Banners, Colours, and Sequences

The signals of Old Vaelic are displayed upon tall poles standing upon the towers of castles and high points of the valley, distinguished by the colour and arrangement of the banners flown. A single red banner is a signal of assembly, summoning the garrison or the local militia to a gathering point. A red banner above a white banner is a signal of alarm, warning settlements of the approach of an armed force. A blue banner flown alone is a signal of passage, informing the watch that a messenger or convoy travels upon the road and the way is to be kept clear. A blue banner above a yellow banner is a signal of the king's business, commanding that the road be cleared for a royal messenger or a company of the king's own riders.

The range of the signals is determined by the height of the pole and the clarity of the air. A banner flown from the high tower of the royal castle at Vael can be seen from the watchtowers of the outer garrisons at a distance of several miles in good weather. The system is thus a rapid and reliable means of communication, well suited to a kingdom whose settlements are scattered along a river valley and whose garrisons must coordinate their response to threats and the movements of the royal household. Messages that would take a rider half a day to deliver can be relayed from tower to tower in under an hour.

Old Vaelic is least reliable during heavy fog, snowfall, or still nights when no wind opens the banners. The towers therefore keep horns, lamps, and mounted couriers as secondary means of communication, though none combines the range and precision of the flag code in clear weather. A signalman is judged not only by speed but by the restraint to suspend a doubtful message rather than relay a colour or sequence that has not been clearly seen.

## Towers of the Valley

The use of Old Vaelic is most extensive in the valley of the River Vael, where the castles and watchtowers of the kingdom maintain banner poles and skilled signalmen. The garrisons of the outer fortifications, guarding the approaches to the valley from the plains and hills, are the principal users, for their watchmen must signal the approach of travellers and armed forces to the inner settlements. The royal castle at Vael is the centre of the system, and its signalmen are among the most skilled in the kingdom, trained to read banners at the limits of visibility and distinguish colours in the fading light of evening.

The settled peoples of the valley who live within sight of the towers are generally familiar with the common signals, for the red banner that summons the militia and the blue banner that clears the road are matters concerning every household. The deeper codes carrying the orders of the king and the intelligence of the garrisons are known only to the signalmen and officers of the garrisons. This restriction prevents the enemy from reading the banners should they capture a station.

## Banners and Spoken Vaelic

Old Vaelic is distinct from the spoken language of the kingdom, the tongue of the Vaelic people and the medium of the court and the market. The spoken language is a true language with grammar and vocabulary, used for the ordinary purposes of life and for the preservation of the laws and histories of the realm. The banner signals, by contrast, are a code of limited scope, capable of conveying only a fixed set of meanings, used only for communication across distance. Many of the common people of the valley cannot read the signals at all but rely upon the public criers and town heralds whose signal-boxes relay the important messages that fly from the towers.

The training of a Vaelic signalman begins with the recognition of colours under poor light, for mist, rain, and sunset can make one banner resemble another at a distance. Apprentices next learn the fixed order in which a message is opened, repeated, acknowledged, and closed, allowing each station to detect an incomplete relay before passing it onward. Watches keep written logs of every signal received and sent, and discrepancies are carried to the captain of the tower at the end of the day. These disciplines prevent the speed of the system from becoming a source of confusion and allow the royal court to trace an error to the station at which it arose.

## Voice of the Watchtowers

Old Vaelic is the voice of the watchtowers in the valley of the Vael, the means by which the garrisons and settlements of the kingdom maintain awareness of the movements of the valley. Without the signal banners, the outer garrisons would have no rapid means of warning the inner settlements of the approach of travellers or armed forces, and the coordination of the defences of the valley would be a far slower and more uncertain undertaking. The system is a practical invention born of the necessities of the defence of the realm, a measure of the ingenuity by which the kingdom of Vael has maintained its security across the centuries.
