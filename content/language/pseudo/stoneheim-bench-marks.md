## Cut Signs of the Deep Galleries

The [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) Bench Marks are the system of chisel-cut signs by which the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) of the eastern ranges of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) record distance, direction, and warning upon the walls of the galleries and tunnels of the underground world. It is a code of carved signs, struck into the dressed stone of the gallery walls at customary intervals, that conveys information to every dwarf who walks the tunnels of the hold. Miners, tunnel wardens, smiths, and messengers travelling between the workings use the system, and its signs are understood by every dwarf who works the stone of the mountain, whatever the dialect of his spoken tongue. The signs are cut to a standard depth, allowing them to be read by touch as well as by sight, for a dwarf walking the gallery in darkness may pass his hand over the wall and feel the sign the chisel has cut.

## Chisel Cuts and Shapes

The signs are produced by chisel and hammer, cut into the dressed surface of the gallery walls at customary places. They are distinguished by the shape of the cut, the depth of the cut, and the arrangement of the cuts composing a single sign. A single horizontal line, cut cleanly across the wall at the level of a dwarf's shoulder, is a sign of distance, marking the hundredth pace from the last junction. A pair of horizontal lines, set one above the other with a hand's breadth between them, is a sign of a junction, indicating that a side gallery opens at the point where the sign is cut. A vertical line from shoulder to knee is a sign of descent, indicating the gallery descends at a steep grade. A vertical line from knee to shoulder is a sign of ascent, indicating the gallery rises. A circle cut cleanly into the wall at shoulder level is a sign of warning, indicating the gallery ahead is hazardous and the dwarf reading the sign must proceed with caution.

## Navigating by the Marks

The principal use of the bench marks is recording distance and direction. Distance signs are cut at every hundredth pace from the last junction, accompanied by numerical marks in dwarven numerals. The dwarves count by twenties rather than tens, and the numerical marks are cut in the dwarven numerals the spoken tongue employs, readable by any dwarf instructed in the customary reckoning of the holds.

Direction signs are cut at every junction, indicating the principal workings reachable by way of the gallery opening at the junction. A sign cut at the right-hand wall pointing toward the gallery opening to the right is accompanied by the mark of the working the gallery serves. A sign cut at the left-hand wall is accompanied in the same fashion. The marks accompanying the direction signs are the customary abbreviations established for the principal workings, understood by every dwarf who walks the galleries. The system of distance and direction signs is among the most reliable of dwarven inventions, the foundation upon which orderly conduct of the work of the hold depends. A dwarf who reads them correctly can navigate the longest galleries without a guide.

## Hazard Signs

Warnings and cautions are a second principal use of the system. The circle is the most common warning sign, cut at the entrance to galleries the tunnel wardens have judged hazardous. The circle may be accompanied by additional signs specifying the nature of the hazard, cut beneath the circle in the customary arrangement: a small triangle with apex pointing downward for falling stone; a pair of wavy lines, one above the other, for foul air not fit to breathe without precautions; and a series of short vertical lines side by side for water that may gather in dangerous quantities. The signs are cut at a sufficient distance from the hazard to allow the dwarf reading them to withdraw or take customary precautions.

## Ownership Marks

The marking of property is a third principal use of the bench marks. Boundaries of the principal workings are marked by signs established by the customs of the holds. A sign cut in the form of a hammer, with the head pointing toward the working it claims, is the mark of the smithing claim, indicating the working in that direction is the property of the smiths of the hold. A sign cut in the form of a chisel, with the blade pointing toward the working it claims, is the mark of the mining claim, indicating the working is the property of the miners. The signs of property are cut at junctions and principal points along boundaries, renewed by wardens at customary intervals to ensure legibility. The marking of property is a matter of considerable importance, for the wealth of the mountain is the foundation of prosperity and boundaries must be clearly indicated to avoid disputes. The signs are also the means by which the council determines the extent of workings each family or craft may claim, and the records kept in the hall of the council are based upon the signs cut in the walls.

## Warden Inspections

The maintenance of the signs is the responsibility of the tunnel wardens of the hold. They conduct customary inspections of the galleries in pairs, each accompanied by an apprentice being instructed in reading and cutting the signs. The wardens carry the customary tools — chisel and hammer for cutting and renewing signs — and are required to renew any sign damaged by water or falling stone. The inspection is also the occasion for wardens to record observations of the condition of the galleries in a customary log maintained in the hall of the council. The log is a record of the state of the workings from one inspection to the next, consulted when questions arise concerning the safety or productivity of the galleries.

## Bench Marks and Deep Speech

Stoneheim Bench Marks are distinct from the [Deep Speech](/content/language/pseudo/deep-speech) the dwarves employ for long-distance communication of the galleries, though the two systems are used in combination at principal junctions. Deep Speech carries messages that must travel beyond the range of the voice; the Bench Marks record information the dwarf walking the gallery may read upon the wall. The two systems are complementary, and a dwarf skilled in both may walk the length of the longest gallery without a guide. The Bench Marks are also distinct from the spoken language of the dwarves, Khazdul, a true language with grammar and vocabulary, while the bench marks are a code of limited scope conveying only a fixed set of meanings.

## Galleries of the Holds

The bench marks are most extensively used in the great workings of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), where galleries extend for hundreds of miles beneath the eastern ranges of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) and the orderly conduct of work requires every passage and junction be marked with customary signs. The bench marks are maintained in every hold of the federation, from the great central halls of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) to the most remote workings in the high country, and the system is one of the principal assurances of the unity of the federation. Surface peoples who visit the dwarven halls are sometimes permitted to view the bench marks in the galleries adjoining guest areas, and visitors marvel at the care with which the signs are cut and renewed. The deeper marks recording boundaries and warnings are not shared with outsiders.

## Voice of the Gallery

Stoneheim Bench Marks are the voice of the gallery in the underground world, the means by which the dwarven people maintain the orderly conduct of the work of the mountain. Without the bench marks, the galleries of the holds would be a maze of unmarked passages, and the dwarf walking the tunnels would be dependent upon memory and guidance for every journey. The system is a practical invention born of the necessities of the underground life, a measure of the dwarven genius for adapting the materials of the world to the purposes of their civilisation. In the deep places where the surface dweller sees only darkness and stone, the dwarf reads the signs the chisel has cut, and the signs carry news of distance, direction, and warning from one end of the hold to the other.
