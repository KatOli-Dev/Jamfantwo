## Overview

Deep Speech is the name given by surface scholars to the system of percussive signals by which the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) and the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) communicate across the stone galleries and long tunnels of the underground world. It is a code of rhythms and intervals, struck upon the living rock by hammer or pick, that carries information across distances far greater than a voice can reach. Dwarven miners and tunnel wardens use the system to send warnings, summon aid, and coordinate the work of the deep galleries, and its signals are understood by every dwarf who works the stone, whatever the dialect of his spoken tongue. Surface scholars have studied the system for centuries, but no outsider has mastered the full range of its signals, for the ability to read the stone requires a lifetime of experience in the deep galleries.

## The Nature of the Signals

The signals of Deep Speech are produced by striking the stone with a hammer or similar implement and are distinguished by the number of blows, the interval between them, and the force with which they are delivered. A single sharp blow signals attention, calling the hearer to listen for what follows. A double blow struck in quick succession is an acknowledgment, confirming that a signal has been heard. A rapid sequence of blows increasing in strength signals danger, understood by every dwarf who hears it as a call to silence and caution. A slow, measured rhythm repeated at regular intervals signals safety, indicating that the way is clear and work may proceed. A pattern of three blows separated by even pauses signals the end of a shift, while a prolonged ringing strike signals that a messenger approaches and the way must be cleared.

The range of the signals is determined by the density of the stone and the nature of the galleries through which they pass. A signal struck in the deep granite of the Stoneheim galleries can be heard at a distance of several miles, carried through the rock by vibrations. Dwarves read the quality of the transmitted signal: sound stone carries a clean, ringing tone, while fractured or weakened stone returns a dull, muffled sound. An experienced tunnel warden can judge the condition of the galleries by the manner in which his signal is answered. The system is thus not only a means of communication but also a method of surveying; the signals that pass through the deep places carry information about the health of the tunnels as surely as they carry messages between workers. A tunnel that returns a weaker signal than expected may be developing cracks, while a gallery that fails to echo at all may have collapsed.

## Centres of Use

Deep Speech is most extensively used in the great dwarven settlements of the underground world, where distances between galleries and chambers are measured in miles rather than paces, and where even the strongest dwarf's voice cannot carry from one working to the next. In the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), where tunnels extend for hundreds of miles beneath the central ranges, the signal system is the principal means of communication between outlying workings and the central halls of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim). The wardens who maintain the signals are among the most valued members of the community, serving as the ears of the hold across its entire extent. The [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm), across the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) on the Aethelaran mountain spine, maintains a related system whose rhythms are recognisably similar to those of the Stoneheim galleries, the product of a shared dwarven tradition adapted to the geology of each range. The Stoneheim system uses deeper, slower rhythms suited to the dense granite of the eastern ranges, while the Karhold system employs more rapid patterns that travel better through the fractured stone of the Aethelaran spine.

Surface peoples who deal with the dwarves are generally aware of Deep Speech, but few have learned to read its signals, for the rhythms of the stone are not easily distinguished by ears accustomed to the open air. Human traders who visit the dwarven halls are sometimes permitted to signal their arrival with a prescribed rhythm, a courtesy that allows gate wardens to recognise their approach and prepare hospitality. The deeper signals of the system — the warnings, alarms, and coordination of mining work — are kept within the dwarven community and are not shared with outsiders. A trader who attempted to learn these restricted signals would be regarded with deep suspicion.

## Relation to Dwarven Speech

Deep Speech is distinct from the spoken language of the dwarves. The spoken tongue is a true language with grammar and vocabulary, while the signal system is a code of limited scope capable of conveying only a fixed set of meanings. The two systems are used in different contexts and for different purposes. A dwarf who converses in the spoken language at the hearth will use Deep Speech in the galleries and workings. The spoken tongue, which the dwarves call Khazdul, is a language of words and sentences used for the ordinary commerce of life and for the preservation of dwarven traditions and learning. Deep Speech, by contrast, is a language of the stone and the hammer, used for the work of the underground world and the communication that work requires. The two systems are complementary, and dwarves who practise both regard them as belonging to different domains of life, as different as the forge and the hearth, the gallery and the hall.

## Significance

Deep Speech is the voice of the stone in the underground world, the means by which the dwarven people maintain the coherence and safety of their vast subterranean settlements. Without the signal system, the galleries of Karhold and the workings of Stoneheim would be silent and isolated places, each community cut off from the others by distances the voice cannot bridge and darkness the eye cannot penetrate. The system is a practical invention born of the necessities of the deep earth, a measure of the dwarven genius for adapting the materials of the world to the purposes of their civilisation. In the deep places where the surface dweller hears only silence, the dwarf hears the rhythm of the stone, the pulse of Deep Speech carrying news, warnings, and orders from one end of the hold to the other.
