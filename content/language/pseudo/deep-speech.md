## Overview

Deep Speech is the name given by surface scholars to the system of percussive signals by which the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Deep Realm of Karhold](/content/government/national/deep-realm-of-karhold) and the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) communicate across the stone galleries and the long tunnels of the underground world. It is not a language in the ordinary sense, for it has no grammar and no vocabulary of the kind that surface peoples recognise as speech. It is instead a code of rhythms and intervals, struck upon the living rock by hammer or pick, that carries information across distances far greater than a voice can reach. The system is used by the dwarven miners and the tunnel wardens to send warnings, to summon aid, and to coordinate the work of the deep galleries, and its signals are understood by every dwarf who works the stone, whatever the dialect of his spoken tongue.

## The Nature of the Signals

The signals of Deep Speech are produced by striking the stone with a hammer or a similar implement, and they are distinguished one from another by the number of blows, the interval between them, and the strength with which they are delivered. A single sharp blow is a signal of attention, calling the hearer to listen for what follows. A double blow, struck in quick succession, is an acknowledgment, confirming that a signal has been heard. A rapid sequence of blows, increasing in strength, is a signal of danger, and it is understood by every dwarf who hears it as a call to silence and to caution. A slow and measured rhythm, repeated at regular intervals, is a signal of safety, indicating that the way is clear and that the work may proceed.

The range of the signals is determined by the density of the stone and the nature of the galleries through which they pass, and a signal struck in the deep granite of the Karhold galleries can be heard at a distance of several miles, carried through the rock by the vibrations that the blow produces. The dwarves have learned to read the quality of the transmitted signal, for the stone that is sound carries a clean and a ringing tone, while the stone that is fractured or weakened returns a dull and a muffled sound, and the experienced tunnel warden can judge the condition of the galleries by the manner in which his signal is answered. The system is thus not only a means of communication but also a method of surveying, and the signals that pass through the deep places carry information about the health of the tunnels as surely as they carry messages between the workers.

## Centres of Use

The use of Deep Speech is most extensive in the great dwarven settlements of the underground world, where the distances between the galleries and the chambers are measured in miles rather than in paces, and where the voice of even the strongest dwarf cannot carry from one working to the next. In the Deep Realm of Karhold, where the tunnels extend for hundreds of miles beneath the mountains of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), the signal system is the principal means of communication between the outlying workings and the central halls, and the wardens who maintain the signals are among the most valued members of the community. The Holds of Stoneheim, though smaller, maintain their own system of signals, and the rhythm of the Deep Speech in the Stoneheim galleries is recognisably related to that of Karhold, though the two have diverged across the centuries of their separation.

The surface peoples who have dealings with the dwarves are generally aware of the existence of Deep Speech, but few have learned to read its signals, for the rhythms of the stone are not easily distinguished by ears that are accustomed to the open air. The human traders who visit the dwarven halls are sometimes permitted to signal their arrival with a prescribed rhythm, a courtesy that allows the gate wardens to recognise their approach and to prepare the hospitality of the hold, but the deeper signals of the system, the warnings and the alarms and the coordination of the mining work, are kept within the dwarven community and are not shared with the outsiders.

## Relation to Dwarven Speech

Deep Speech is distinct from the spoken language of the dwarves, for the spoken tongue is a true language with a grammar and a vocabulary, while the signal system is a code of limited scope, capable of conveying only a fixed set of meanings. The two systems are used in different contexts and for different purposes, and a dwarf who converses in the spoken language at the hearth will use the Deep Speech in the galleries and the workings. The spoken tongue, which the dwarves call Khazdul, is a language of words and sentences, used for the ordinary commerce of life and for the preservation of the dwarven traditions and the dwarven learning. Deep Speech, by contrast, is a language of the stone and the hammer, used for the work of the underground world and for the communication that the work requires. The two systems are complementary, and the dwarves who practise both regard them as belonging to different domains of life, as different as the forge and the hearth, the gallery and the hall.

## Significance

Deep Speech is the voice of the stone in the underground world, the means by which the dwarven people maintain the coherence and the safety of their vast subterranean settlements. Without the signal system, the galleries of Karhold and the workings of Stoneheim would be silent and isolated places, each community cut off from the others by the distances that the voice cannot bridge and the darkness that the eye cannot penetrate. The system is a practical invention, born of the necessities of the deep earth, and it is a measure of the dwarven genius for adapting the materials of the world to the purposes of their civilisation. In the deep places where the surface dweller hears only silence, the dwarf hears the rhythm of the stone, the pulse of the Deep Speech that carries the news and the warnings and the orders of the underground world from one end of the hold to the other.
