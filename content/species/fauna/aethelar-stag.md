## The Great Deer of Aethelar

The [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) stag is the largest deer in the known world. A mature bull stands taller at the shoulder than a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) hunter, and its antlers span a width sufficient to turn aside any but the stoutest spear. The stag is confined to the ancient woodlands of the central continent; its range has retreated with the cutting of the forest and the spread of agriculture. The remaining population is concentrated in the deeper woods and in the protected hunting grounds of the highland valleys, where the nobility of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) maintain seasonal hunts governed by formal codes.

## Antlers, Coat, and Conformation

The Aethelar stag carries the conformation of the deep-wood deer in its most pronounced form. The body is long and deep through the chest, set on sturdy legs, and the shoulders and hindquarters are heavily muscled. The summer coat is a warm reddish brown shading to a paler buff along the flanks and underside; the winter coat thickens to a dense grey-brown. The rump patch is a pale cream bordered by a darker line, conspicuous when the stag flees through underbrush.

The antlers rise from heavy burrs in a broad forward sweep, curving upward and inward in a series of tines; a typical mature bull carries ten or more points. New antlers are grown each year and shed in late winter. The velvet that covers them during the growing season is traded among foresters for medicinal preparations. Pairs of antlers recovered from a stag that has died of old age are displayed in forest lodges. A mature bull's skull mount weighs between fifteen and twenty pounds.

## Woodland Home and Shrinking Range

The stag inhabits mature deciduous and mixed forests of central and northern Aethelar, with the greatest concentration in the deep woodlands bordering the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) and the river valleys of the interior. It prefers forest edges and clearings where new grass springs up after burnings, and it ranges widely in spring and summer in search of the best grazing. In autumn the mature bulls hold small territories in the deeper wood, lying up in thickets during the day and moving to water only at dusk. Does and young remain in larger herds through autumn, gathering on ridge tops where the wind keeps flies at bay.

The historical range once extended across the whole of the temperate forest of Aethelar, from the foothills of the western mountain spine to the eastern coastal woodlands, but clearing of land for the plough has reduced it to the great forests that remain, principally in the northern valleys and along the slopes of the highland interior. The noble houses of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) maintain large hunting forests in which the stag is protected and hunted according to strict seasons and quotas; these forests are the last stronghold of the great deer in the heart of the continent. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the deep forest tolerate the stag and do not hunt it except in famine, for their traditions consider it an elder of the woodland whose death requires ceremonial observance.

## Grazing, Rutting, and Social Order

The stag is a grazer and browser, feeding upon grasses and herbs of the woodland floor in spring and summer and upon bark, buds, and fallen mast in autumn and winter. It is fond of acorns and beechnuts; the autumn feeding upon mast is the period when the bull lays down the fat that carries it through the lean weeks of late winter. Does nurse their fawns upon the rich milk of early summer, and the fawns are weaned by autumn and capable of independent foraging before the first snow. The stag is a creature of early morning and late afternoon, resting during the heat of the day in the cool of the deeper wood, a habit the hunter uses to advantage when the wind is right and the forest is still.

The social order of the stag is most visible in the autumn rutting season, when mature bulls gather herds of does into their territories and challenge one another for the choicest grounds. The roar of the bull at the height of the rut is a deep resonant call audible for miles. Fights between bulls involve locking antlers and shoving, lasting from several minutes to over an hour. The victor assumes the herd and drives the younger bulls to the periphery.

## The Ceremonial Chase

The hunt is governed by detailed codes. The king of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) maintains a court of hunt-masters to manage the royal forests and organise the seasonal hunt. The quarry is the mature bull of not less than ten tines; young bulls, does, and fawns are protected and may not be taken except by commoners for food.

Methods vary with season and terrain. In early autumn the stalk is the customary approach, the hunter and beaters moving through the wood to find the bull lying up in its thicket, the shot taken at close range with the heavy bow. In later autumn, when leaves have fallen, the hunter may call the bull by imitating its roar. The kill is followed by the ritual breaking of the quarry, in which the hunter cuts the throat of the fallen stag, and the meat is distributed to the company and to the foresters on whose ground the hunt took place.

## Predators and Forest Neighbours

The stag shares its range with the great predators of the forest in numbers that vary with region and season. The grey wolf of the central woodlands will take a fawn or a sick adult when opportunity offers, but the mature bull is too formidable a quarry for any but the largest pack, and the wolves are not numerous enough to threaten the stag population as a whole. The lynx of the highland forests takes fawns in the spring, and the bear of the deep wood will contest the carcass of a stag that has fallen to a hunter's arrow, though the bear does not ordinarily hunt the healthy stag. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) tolerate the stag as a fellow dweller of the wood and do not hunt it except in the strictest necessity; the elves of the central forest are known to drive the stag from the deeper wood at the time of the autumn cull, lest the hunters of the human kingdoms take more than the laws of the chase permit.

## Emblem of the Wild Wood

The Aethelar stag's range has contracted by approximately sixty percent since the third century, following the expansion of ploughland across central Aethelar. The remaining population is concentrated in the hunting forests maintained by the noble houses of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth), where seasonal quotas and protected breeding grounds have stabilised numbers over the past century. The current population is estimated at between six and eight thousand mature bulls across all hunting forests and wild deep-wood ranges. Stag antlers are a common motif in the heraldry and architecture of the central kingdoms, appearing on the great seals of three royal houses and on the entrance hall of the Vaelic palace at [Sunmere](/content/location/settlement/city/sunmere).
