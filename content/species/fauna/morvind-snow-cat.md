## The White Feline of the High Peaks

The [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) snow cat is the apex predator of the alpine zone of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands), a great feline of pure white coat and heavy build whose range extends across the full extent of the high mountains of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The cat is solitary and elusive, seldom seen by the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) who inhabit the valleys and lower slopes; its presence is known chiefly through the tracks it leaves in the snow and the occasional loss of livestock from valley farms. The snow cat is held in peculiar reverence by the mountain peoples, who regard it as the spirit of the high peaks made flesh; the killing of a snow cat is forbidden by the customary law of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) and the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) alike.

## Winter Coat and Alpine Build

The snow cat is a powerfully built feline, larger than any of the lowland cats of the known world, with a body that is broad through the chest and set on legs of moderate length capable of leaps covering fifteen feet among the rocks of the high country. The head is broad and rounded, the ears short and tufted, the eyes a pale blue-grey adapted to the brilliant light of the snowfields. The tail is long and thick, nearly as long as the body itself, used as a counterweight in the descent of steep slopes.

The coat changes with the seasons in close match to the background of the high country. In winter the coat is dense and pure white with a slight silvering along the flanks and back that breaks the outline against the snow. In summer the coat is shorter and a pale grey with faint darker markings along the shoulders and haunches matching the colour of exposed rock of the alpine meadows. The winter coat is among the most valuable of mountain furs; pelts of the snow cat are prized possessions of the noble houses of the mountain kingdoms, who pay handsomely for rare specimens taken by trappers of the high valleys. Trapping is strictly limited by customary law; the sale of pelts is permitted only in the case of cats killed in defence of livestock or in self-defence.

## Above the Treeline

The snow cat inhabits the alpine zone of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands), the region above the treeline where mountain grasses and flowering herbs of the high meadows give way to rock and permanent snow. The cat ranges from the high meadows of summer to the lower slopes of winter, following the prey that migrate between elevations with the seasons; a single cat may hold a territory extending across many miles of broken country. The territory is marked by scratches of great claws upon rocks and by scent left upon prominent boulders; boundaries are respected by other cats of the same sex, who avoid marked ground except in the mating season.

The cat prefers the broken country of ridges and cirques, where rock provides cover for the stalk and the open meadows beyond provide grazing for prey. Deep snow of the high valleys is avoided in winter, for the bulk of the cat sinks deeply into soft snow; the cat moves to wind-scoured slopes where snow is thin and prey is more easily overtaken. Summer range extends higher into meadows where mountain goats graze; the cat lies upon a rocky outcrop above a game trail and waits for hours for the opportunity to take a kid or yearling.

## Stalking the Mountain Goat

The principal prey is the mountain goat of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) Uplands, a sure-footed herbivore whose populations fluctuate with the severity of winter and the availability of summer grazing. The cat stalks the goat by approaching from above, where its white coat blends with snow and broken rock, and takes the prey by a sudden rush from a short distance; the great forepaw breaks the neck of the goat with a single blow. The cat can carry a young goat up the steepest slope to a sheltered ledge where the carcass may be eaten at leisure; an adult male is capable of taking a goat of considerable size, though it will not attempt a full-grown billy unless the snow favours the stalk.

When the goat is scarce, the cat takes smaller mammals of the high country, including the alpine hare and the marmot, and also takes ptarmigan and grouse of the high meadows. The cat occasionally descends into forests of the lower slopes in winter, where it preys upon red deer and wild boar; the occasional loss of a calf from highland farms is laid to the snow cat forced down from the mountains by the depth of snow. The cat does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), though there are records of attacks upon solitary travellers in high passes; the mountain peoples regard an unprovoked attack by a snow cat as an omen of ill fortune.

## Cubs, Dens, and Solitary Maturity

The snow cat is a long-lived animal for a creature of its size, with a typical lifespan of fifteen to twenty years in the wild. The cubs are born in late spring, usually in a den among the rocks lined with fur the mother has shed from her coat. The litter is small, typically two or three cubs; the cubs remain with the mother through summer and autumn, learning the hunting grounds and techniques of the stalk. The young cats disperse in late winter of their second year; males range widely in search of a territory they may claim against established adults.

Adults are solitary outside the brief mating season, which falls in late winter; males and females come together for a few days before separating again to solitary ranges. Fights between males for possession of a female in season are conducted with great violence; the scars upon the face and forequarters of an old male record many such contests. The successful male remains with the female for the period of her receptivity and then departs, taking no further part in the rearing of the cubs.

## Reverence and Protection

The mountain peoples of Morvind regard the snow cat with a mixture of reverence and wary respect; customary law forbids the killing of a snow cat except in the case of an unprovoked attack upon livestock or a person. The prohibition is enforced by the councils of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) and the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans); fines levied upon a transgressor are among the heaviest of the customary penalties. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) do not hunt the cat; their traditions hold that the snow cat is a creature of the high places the dwarves do not claim, and dwarven mountain halls are designed with avoidance of the cat in mind, the lower entrances kept clear of scent that would attract the great predator.

Trappers of the highland valleys take a small number of snow cats each year under licence from the councils; the pelts recovered are among the most valuable of mountain furs. A licence is granted only in the case of a cat killed in defence of livestock; the trapper is required to present the carcass to the council before the pelt may be sold. The meat is not eaten, for the mountain peoples hold that the flesh of such a creature is not to be consumed; the carcass is burned upon a pyre of mountain wood in accordance with ancient custom.

## Spirit of the High Places

The snow cat's presence in the mountains of the northern continent is an indicator of the health of the high country. It is the predator that keeps the populations of mountain goat in balance; the loss of the cat from a district is followed by overgrazing of the meadows. Population surveys conducted by the Valdmark councils between 830 and 850 AR estimated approximately three hundred individuals across the entirety of the Morvind Uplands, with territories averaging forty square miles per adult. The protection of the cat by customary law dates from the First Gathering of the Frostwall Clans in 612 AR and is among the oldest conservation regulations in the known world.
