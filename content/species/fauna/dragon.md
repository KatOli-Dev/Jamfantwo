## The Greatest of Reptiles

The dragon is the largest reptile of the known world. Encounters between humans and mature dragons are infrequent, and such meetings are recorded in the annals of the kingdoms; chroniclers of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and the courts of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) have each preserved accounts of meetings between dragons and the kings of their lands. The dragon is not numerous. Naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) have estimated that fewer than a hundred dragons of mature age survive in all the world. The creature is found only in the most remote and inaccessible high places: the mountain ranges of the interior and the cliffs of distant coasts where the foot of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) does not ordinarily fall.

## Scales, Wings, and Fiery Breath

The dragon is a great reptilian beast, four-legged and winged, with a body covered in a thick hide of overlapping scales and a head set upon a long muscular neck. Measurements taken from the few carcasses recovered by naturalists suggest that a great male may measure forty feet from the tip of the snout to the root of the tail; the wingspan is greater than the length of the body. The scales are of considerable thickness; a breastplate of dragon scale is a possession dwarven smiths have fashioned for the kings of the highland realms, though the cost is reckoned in the labour of years and in the favour of princes.

The colour varies with the individual and range, but the predominant colours are deep red, black of considerable sheen, or pale silver-grey. The head bears a pair of horns that curve backward over the skull; the jaws are armed with teeth of considerable length that are renewed throughout the animal's life. The eyes are deep amber or pale gold, a colour not found in any other creature. The breath of the dragon is a blast of fire; accounts preserved by the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) and dwarven chroniclers describe it as sufficient to consume a barn or strip the flesh from the bones of a horse.

## High Mountains and Remote Lairs

The dragon inhabits the highest mountains of the three continents, with the largest populations in the interior ranges of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the southern highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and a smaller population in the western highlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) found in the cliffs above the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). The dragon requires high cliffs for the lair and wide valleys for the hunt; it is not found in the lowlands or in the country of gentle hills. The lair is typically situated in a cave that opens upon a sheltered ledge commanding a view of the country below; the cave is the residence for the greater part of the dragon's life, ranging out to hunt and returning to the same cave season after season.

A mature male holds a territory of considerable extent, ranging many miles from the lair in search of prey. The territory is marked by scratches of great claws upon the rocks and by scent left upon prominent outcrops; territories of mature dragons do not ordinarily overlap, the dragons spacing themselves across the high country at intervals estimated by naturalists to be many tens of miles. Encounters between two mature dragons are consequently rare; the few such meetings recorded have been conducted with displays of size and fire that ended, in accounts that have been preserved, in the withdrawal of one animal.

## Predator of the High Country

The dragon takes a wide range of prey according to opportunity. The principal prey is the larger mammals of the high country, including mountain goats and deer; a mature dragon will take several such animals in the course of a week. It also takes cattle of highland pastures when they are grazing in the open; shepherds of the mountain valleys must keep their herds within the protection of dogs and watchers, for a dragon that has found a herd will take several beasts in a single night. The dragon does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but accounts of the mountain communities include incidents in which a dragon has taken a shepherd or traveller who unwarily approached the lair.

The dragon is a creature of considerable intelligence. Accounts of the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the mountain holds include descriptions of dragons that have answered the speech of dwarven chroniclers with sounds interpreted as a form of language. The dragon is not social; encounters outside the brief mating season are conducted with wariness. Mating is rare; the female lays a small number of eggs in a chamber excavated in the deepest part of the lair. The young remain with the mother for several years before dispersing to find their own territories. Mortality among the young is very high, and only a fraction of dragons that hatch in any century survive to maturity.

## Legend and Captivity

The dragon appears in the traditions of the three continents. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the deep forests hold that the dragon is the eldest of the reptiles, predating the first elven settlements. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the mountain holds do not hunt the dragon; their traditions hold that the dragon rules the upper world and the dwarves the lower, and they seal the upper passages of their halls against intrusion.

The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the lowland kingdoms preserve a substantial body of romance and cautionary tale concerning the dragon. The kings of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) have at times maintained dragons in captivity, held in great enclosures built for the purpose; chroniclers of the royal courts have preserved accounts of dragons brought to the courts in the early years of their lives and tended by keepers of the royal menageries. The custom has fallen out of practice in the present age; the cost and danger are considerable, and the last of the royal dragons died in the reign of the seventh king of the Vaelic line.

## The Wildness of the Upper World

The dragon is the largest reptile of the world, with mature males measuring up to 40 feet from snout to tail root. Naturalists of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) estimate that fewer than 100 mature dragons survive across all three continents, concentrated in the interior ranges of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the southern highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). A mature male's territory may extend for 30 to 40 miles, and territories of mature dragons do not overlap. Females lay clutches of 2 to 4 eggs, and mortality among hatchlings is high; the few accounts of dragons raised in captivity indicate a maturation period of approximately 50 years. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the mountain holds have, on rare occasions, fashioned breastplates from dragon scale for the kings of the highland realms, a craft requiring years of labour. The last dragon held in royal captivity died during the reign of the seventh king of the Vaelic line, and the custom has not been revived since.
