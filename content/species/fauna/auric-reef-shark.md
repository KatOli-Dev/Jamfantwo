## Predator of the Warm Reefs

The Auric reef shark is the most numerous predatory shark of the warm waters of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), a sleek and powerful fish whose range extends across the reef country of the eastern Aethelaran coast and the waters of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden). The shark takes its name from the golden cast of the warm waters it inhabits; the colour gives the shark's back a metallic golden-bronze sheen. The reef shark is not a creature of the open ocean, for it prefers the broken country of the reefs and the straits, and it is rarely encountered by vessels that ply the deep waters of the Auric.

## Sleek Body and Keen Senses

The Auric reef shark is a fish of moderate size, with a body that may attain twice the height of a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) of average stature, though the great majority encountered by fishermen are of more modest size. The body is streamlined and fusiform, adapted to the rapid and agile motion that is its principal mode of progression, and the tail is a powerful and asymmetrical fin that provides thrust. The skin is covered in small dermal denticles characteristic of sharks. The colour is a metallic grey-bronze upon the back and flanks, with a paler underside that renders the shark less visible from below.

The head is blunt and powerful, with a jaw set with several rows of sharp serrated teeth that are constantly replaced as the outer teeth are worn or broken. The eyes are small and black; the shark is reputed to detect the movement of a fish at considerable distance in the clear water of the reef. The nostrils are set upon the underside of the snout and are sensitive to the scent of blood carried in the water from a wounded creature. The shark possesses the ampullae of Lorenzini characteristic of sharks, a network of sensory organs that detect the electrical fields of prey, enabling it to detect a creature hidden beneath sand or in the crevices of the reef.

## Reef Country and Coastal Waters

The Auric reef shark inhabits the warm waters of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), with the greatest concentration in the vicinity of the reefs and submerged ridges of the eastern coast. The shark prefers the broken country of the reef, where prey is concentrated by the currents and where it can find cover among rocks and weed for ambush. It is not found in the colder waters of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) and is rarely encountered outside the strait and adjacent coast.

The shark is most active in the warm months of late spring and summer, when the waters of the Auric are at their warmest and prey is most abundant. It ranges widely through the reef country during the day, moving with the currents and tides in search of prey, and returns to the deeper water beyond the reefs at night to rest upon the bottom. The shark is solitary outside the brief mating season, and mature animals hold ranges marked by the scent left upon prominent rocks and by bites inflicted upon the reef.

## Opportunistic Predator of the Reef

The reef shark is an opportunistic predator, feeding upon fish of the reef, squid and octopus that haunt the rocky bottom, and shelled creatures found among the crevices. It hunts by a combination of stealth and speed, approaching prey from the cover of the reef and accelerating with a sudden burst that takes the prey before it can escape. The shark is also a scavenger, visiting the carcasses of larger animals that have died upon the reef and driving off smaller scavengers.

The shark does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), though fishermen of the coastal towns report occasional encounters with sharks that have been provoked or attracted by the blood of the catch. The pearl divers of the strait are the most exposed of the maritime communities to the danger; the traditions of the diving guilds include a number of practices designed to reduce risk. The divers are careful to work in pairs and refrain from diving when the water is warm and the sharks are unusually active. The guilds maintain a careful record of shark sightings near the pearl beds.

## Birth and Early Life in the Shallows

The female gives birth to a small number of well-developed young, typically two or three, the eggs being retained within the body until they hatch; the young sharks are capable of independent life from the moment of birth. The young remain in the shallows of the reef for the first months of their lives, where they are less exposed to larger predators of the open water, and they disperse to the deeper water of the reef as they grow.

Mortality among the young is high, for juvenile sharks are preyed upon by larger fish of the reef, and only a small fraction of the brood survives to maturity. Sharks that survive to adulthood are long-lived; a reef shark that escapes the hazards of the fishery and predation by larger sharks may live for several decades. Sharks reach maturity at several years of age, and mature animals return to the same reef year after year to breed; the females gather in the shallows to give birth while males patrol the deeper water in search of receptive females.

## Fishermen's Lore and Practical Management

The communities of the strait have a long acquaintance with the reef shark, and the lore of the fishermen includes a substantial body of knowledge of the creature's habits. The fishermen read signs of the shark's presence in the behaviour of other fish; they know that a sudden cessation of feeding among smaller fish or a scattering of birds from a particular spot may indicate a shark in the vicinity. Fishing boats are careful to discard offal from the catch at a distance from the nets, and crews carry sharp hooks with which to fend off a shark that approaches too closely.

The shark is regarded by the coastal communities as a hazard to be managed. It is occasionally taken by fishermen for the oil rendered from its liver, yielding approximately 3 to 5 gallons per mature animal, which is used in the lamps of coastal towns and in the preparation of caulking applied to the hulls of fishing boats.

## Keystone of the Reef

The Auric reef shark is the most numerous predatory shark of the warm waters of the eastern coast, particularly concentrated in the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden). It regulates populations of reef fish, and its presence in the reef country serves as an indicator of reef health. The shark attains maturity at 6 to 8 years and may live for several decades. Females give birth to two or three live young in the shallows, where juveniles remain for their first months before dispersing to deeper water. The pearl diving guilds of the strait maintain written records of shark sightings near the pearl beds, and divers refrain from entering the water during the warmest months when shark activity is highest. Fishermen occasionally take the shark for liver oil, yielding approximately 3 to 5 gallons per mature animal, used in lamps and hull caulking.
