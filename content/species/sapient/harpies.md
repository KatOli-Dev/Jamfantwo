## Ancient People of the High Cliffs

The origins of the harpies are debated among naturalists, as the harpy body admits no easy comparison with other species. From the waist up, their form is broadly humanoid with arms that also serve as wings, adapted entirely for flight. Some naturalists propose a distant kinship with the [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk), citing the radical adaptation of both peoples to non-terrestrial environments, but most scholars dismiss this as speculative. Harpies have inhabited cliffs and high peaks longer than any recorded history, and their presence in these remote places is attested in the oldest records of the mountain peoples. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) have long been aware of the harpies, and the relationship between the two peoples numbers among the oldest interspecies contacts in the known world.

## Wings of Arm and Hollow Bones

Harpies are lighter and more lightly built than [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), as flight demands a body that is strong but not heavy. Their bones are hollow and thin-walled, like those of birds, reducing weight while retaining structural strength. Musculature concentrates in the chest and shoulders, where the flight muscles are anchored, making the upper body correspondingly broad and powerful. The arms serve as wings, combining flight and manipulation in a single limb, though the hands at the wingtips are less dexterous than those of fully humanoid peoples.

The wings span twice the harpy's height when fully extended, covered in feathers ranging from dark brown to grey, white, and russet, often patterned in ways that carry significance among harpy kindreds. Harpies devote considerable time to grooming, as the condition of plumage is both a practical necessity and a source of personal pride. The lower body bears shorter feathers blending into scaled raptor legs and taloned feet, used for perching, hunting, and defence.

Harpy faces are humanoid in outline but sharp and angular, with large forward-facing eyes granting excellent binocular vision. Their eyesight is the keenest of any sapient people, capable of detecting prey at distances that would leave a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) or an [elf](/content/species/sapient/elves) staring in vain. Their hearing is acute, and their sense of smell is moderate. Harpy voices produce a remarkable range of sounds including speech, song, and piercing cries that carry for miles across the mountains.

## Eggs, Chicks, and Aerie Uphringing

Harpies live sixty to ninety years under favourable conditions, though the demands of flight and habitat harshness prevent many from reaching old age. They mature quickly, reaching full stature and flight capacity by their early teens, and their powers begin to decline after the fifth decade as feathers thin and flight muscles weaken. Harpy women carry their young for approximately six months before laying a single egg, which incubates for a further two months before hatching. The chick is helpless at hatching and requires many months of care before it can fly, during which the mother and community share the burden of protection and feeding.

Harpy populations grow slowly, as the harsh conditions of the high peaks and the demands of flight limit the number of young that can be raised successfully. The loss of an adult is a serious matter, for the community is small and each member plays a vital role in defending and provisioning the aerie. Elders are those who have maintained their strength and plumage into later life; their authority rests upon knowledge of the mountains and weather as much as upon their years.

## Cliffs and High Peaks of All Continents

Harpies inhabit cliffs and high peaks of all three continents wherever the terrain rises high enough to provide suitable nesting sites and the updrafts that flight requires. In [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), they occupy the crags and summits of the mountain spine, nesting in aeries inaccessible to any earthbound people. In [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), they are present throughout the interior ranges, where the high peaks and deep valleys provide an ideal habitat; the harpy kindreds of Morvind are the most numerous and widely known. In [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), harpies are found in the rocky massifs of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) interior, where upland cliffs rise from the desert floor.

Aeries are typically situated on high ledges or in shallow caves on cliff faces, chosen for their inaccessibility and exposure to prevailing winds. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region determines the distribution of harpy populations; they are absent from lowlands and forests entirely, as their wings require open air and their feet require stone. A harpy grounded by injury or illness is regarded as having suffered the greatest misfortune that can befall one of their kind.

## Aerial Hunter of Mountain Prey

The harpy diet is dominated by meat. Harpies are formidable hunters of mountain and cliff environments, preying upon birds, mammals, and reptiles of the high country. They take quarry from the air or from elevated perches, and their hunting displays speed, precision, and aerial mastery that no other people can equal. Mountain goats, hares, and larger upland birds are staple prey; a harpy can carry a substantial kill back to the aerie in its talons.

Plant foods are a minor part of the diet, though harpies eat berries and fruits when available, gathering them from lower slopes during seasonal movements. Harpies do not cook, as fire is impractical in their aeries, and they consume food raw, tearing it with talons and beak-like teeth. Water is obtained from mountain streams and springs, and from melting snow and ice on the higher peaks. Each harpy hunts for itself and its dependants, though surplus is shared within the kindred in times of need.

## Dominant Hunters of the Upper Reaches

Harpies occupy the niche of aerial predator in the high mountain and cliff environments they inhabit. They are the dominant hunters of the upper reaches, and their presence regulates the populations of birds and mammals that share their habitat. The relationship between harpies and mountain ecosystems is one of balance, governed by traditions and restraint that shape the other aspects of their culture.

Harpies have little contact with other sapient peoples owing to the height and remoteness of their aeries. [Dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) and harpies share the mountain ranges of Morvind and Aethelar, maintaining a distant but respectful coexistence, each recognising the other as a people of the mountains. [Centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) and harpies occasionally encounter one another where grasslands meet foothills; the relationship is wary, for the centaur and the harpy are predators of very different kinds.
