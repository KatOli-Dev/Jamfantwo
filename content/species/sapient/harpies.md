## Origins

The origins of the harpies are among the most debated questions in the natural history of the thinking peoples, for the harpy body is unlike that of any other species and admits no easy comparison. From the waist up, the harpy form is broadly humanoid, with a head, shoulders, and arms, but the arms are also wings, and the whole upper body is adapted for flight. Some naturalists have proposed that harpies share a distant kinship with the [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk), citing the radical adaptation of both peoples to a non-terrestrial environment, but this view has been dismissed by most scholars as speculative. The harpies themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their having been born from the wind and the stone of the high peaks, and they hold these traditions with a conviction that brooks no argument.

What is certain is that harpies have inhabited the cliffs and high peaks of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these remote and inaccessible places is attested in the oldest records of the mountain peoples. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) have long been aware of the harpies, and the relationship between the two peoples, though distant, is among the oldest interspecies contacts in the known world.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Harpies are a medium-sized people, lighter and more lightly built than [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), for the demands of flight require a body that is strong but not heavy. Their bones are hollow and thin-walled, like those of birds, reducing weight while retaining structural strength. Their musculature is concentrated in the chest and shoulders, where the great flight muscles are anchored, and the upper body is correspondingly broad and powerful. The arms of a harpy are also its wings, and the two functions are combined in a single limb that is as capable of flight as it is of manipulation, though the hands at the wingtips are less dexterous than those of a fully humanoid people.

The wings are large, spanning twice the height of the harpy when fully extended, and they are covered in feathers that range from dark brown to grey, white, and russet, often patterned in ways that hold significance among harpy kindreds. The feathers are strong and well maintained, for the harpies devote considerable time to their grooming, and the condition of a harpy's plumage is a matter of both practical necessity and personal pride. The lower body is covered in shorter feathers that blend into the scaled legs and taloned feet of a raptor, and the legs are strong and the talons sharp, used for perching, hunting, and defence.

Harpy faces are humanoid in general outline, but they are sharpened and angular, with large, forward-facing eyes that grant excellent binocular vision. Their eyesight is the keenest of any sapient people, capable of detecting prey at distances that would leave a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) or an [elf](/content/species/sapient/elves) staring in vain. Their hearing is acute, and their sense of smell is moderate, less developed than that of the ground-dwelling peoples but sufficient for their needs. Harpy voices are remarkable, for they can produce a range of sounds that includes speech, song, and piercing cries that carry for miles across the mountains.

## Life Cycle

Harpies are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of sixty to ninety years under favourable conditions, though the demands of flight and the harshness of their habitats mean that many do not reach old age. They mature quickly, reaching full stature and the capacity for flight by their early teens, and their powers begin to decline after the fifth decade as the feathers thin and the flight muscles weaken. Harpy women carry their young for approximately six months before laying a single egg, which is incubated for a further two months before hatching. The chick is helpless at hatching and requires many months of care before it is capable of flight, during which time the mother and the community share the burden of protection and feeding.

Harpy populations grow slowly, for the harsh conditions of the high peaks and the demanding nature of flight limit the number of young that can be raised successfully. The loss of an adult is a serious matter for a harpy kindred, for the community is small and each member plays a vital role in the defence and provisioning of the aerie. The elders of a harpy kindred are those who have maintained their strength and their plumage into later life, and their authority rests upon their knowledge of the mountains and the weather as much as upon their years.

## Habitat and Distribution

Harpies are found in the cliffs and high peaks of all three continents, wherever the terrain rises high enough to provide suitable nesting sites and the updrafts that flight requires. In [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), they inhabit the crags and summits of the mountain spine, nesting in aeries that are inaccessible to any earthbound people. In [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), they are present throughout the interior ranges, where the high peaks and deep valleys provide an ideal habitat, and the harpy kindreds of Morvind are the most numerous and the most widely known. In [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), harpies are found in the rocky massifs of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) interior, where the upland cliffs rise from the desert floor.

Harpy aeries are typically situated on high ledges or in shallow caves on cliff faces, chosen for their inaccessibility and their exposure to the prevailing winds. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region determines the distribution of harpy populations, and the harpies are absent from the lowlands and the forests entirely, for their wings require open air and their feet require stone. A harpy grounded by injury or illness is a pitiful sight, and the harpies regard the loss of flight as the greatest misfortune that can befall one of their kind.

## Diet

The harpy diet is dominated by meat, and harpies are formidable hunters of the mountain and cliff environments they inhabit. They prey upon the birds, mammals, and reptiles of the high country, taking their quarry from the air or from elevated perches, and their hunting is a display of speed, precision, and aerial mastery that no other people can equal. Mountain goats, hares, and the larger birds of the uplands are the staple prey, and a harpy can carry a substantial kill back to the aerie in its talons.

Plant foods are a minor part of the harpy diet, though harpies will eat berries and fruits when they are available, gathering them from the lower slopes during their seasonal movements. Harpies do not cook, for fire is impractical in their aeries, and they consume their food raw, tearing it with their talons and beak-like teeth. Water is obtained from mountain streams and springs, and from the melting of snow and ice on the higher peaks. The harpy approach to food is practical and individual, for each harpy hunts for itself and its dependants, though surplus is shared within the kindred in times of need.

## Ecology

Harpies occupy the niche of an aerial predator in the high mountain and cliff environments they inhabit. Their impact on the ecology of the high country is significant, for the harpies are the dominant hunters of the upper reaches, and their presence regulates the populations of the birds and mammals that share their habitat. The relationship between harpies and the mountain ecosystems is one of balance, for the harpies take what they need and no more, and their hunting is governed by the same traditions and restraint that shape the other aspects of their culture.

Harpies have little contact with other sapient peoples, owing to the height and remoteness of their aeries. [Dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) and harpies share the mountain ranges of Morvind and Aethelar, and the two peoples maintain a distant but respectful coexistence, each recognising the other as a people of the mountains. [Centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) and harpies occasionally encounter one another where the grasslands meet the foothills, and the relationship is wary, for the centaur and the harpy are predators of very different kinds.
