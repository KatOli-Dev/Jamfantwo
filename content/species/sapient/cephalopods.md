## Mysteries of the Abyssal Depths

Cephalopods are a tentacled people inhabiting the deep ocean floor of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean). Their bodies are adapted to the extreme conditions of the abyssal depths, and their habitat is the most remote and inaccessible of any sapient people. Naturalists have proposed a distant kinship with [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) on the basis of shared aquatic adaptation, but the differences are too great for the comparison to be sustained: cephalopods are creatures of the deep sea rather than the shallows, and their bodies are entirely unlike the humanoid form of the merfolk. The cephalopods themselves are almost wholly unknown to surface peoples, and what little is known of their origins comes from rare encounters between cephalopods and the merfolk of the deep waters.

Cephalopods have inhabited the deep ocean floor for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in the abyssal depths is recorded in the oldest oral traditions of the merfolk. The two peoples share the ocean but inhabit different worlds: the merfolk are creatures of light and the shallows, while the cephalopods are creatures of darkness and the deep.

## Tentacles and Colour-Shifting Skin

Cephalopods are a large people whose form is immediately recognisable as cephalopod. The body is soft and muscular, divided into a large, domed head and a ring of eight tentacled arms surrounding the mouth. The head houses the brain, the eyes, and the organs of sense, and it is covered in smooth, moist skin capable of extraordinary changes of colour and texture. The arms are long, powerful, and flexible, equipped with rows of suckers capable of gripping, tasting, and fine manipulation. The body is supported not by bone but by a combination of muscular hydrostats and a rigid internal pen, and the cephalopod can alter the shape and stiffness of its body to suit the demands of the moment.

The cephalopod body is superbly adapted to the deep ocean. Their skin can change colour in milliseconds through arrays of pigment cells, used for both communication and camouflage, their bodies rippling with patterns that convey information or blend seamlessly with the surrounding seafloor. Their eyes are the largest of any sapient people, extraordinarily sensitive and adapted to the near-total darkness of the abyssal depths, capable of detecting the faintest traces of light, including the natural glow of deep-sea creatures.

Cephalopods breathe through gills, and their bodies are adapted to the enormous pressures of the deep sea, with flexible tissues and gas-filled spaces that compress without damage. They move by a combination of tentacled crawling across the seafloor and jet propulsion through the water, expelling water from a muscular funnel to drive themselves forward with remarkable speed. Their sense of touch is extraordinary, as each sucker on their arms can taste and feel independently, allowing the cephalopod to explore and manipulate eight objects simultaneously with precision no other people can match.

## Eggs, Hatchlings, and Deep-Sea Growth

Cephalopods are short to moderately long-lived, with a lifespan estimated at forty to sixty years based on merfolk accounts, as no naturalist has been able to study a cephalopod community in its natural environment. They mature quickly, reaching full size and adulthood within five to eight years. Cephalopod women lay clutches of several thousand eggs in protected crevices on the deep seafloor, and the eggs are guarded and tended by the mother until they hatch, a period that may last many months. The hatchlings are small but fully formed, miniature versions of the adult, capable of swimming and feeding from the moment of hatching.

Cephalopod populations are difficult to assess, as the deep ocean floor is vast and the cephalopods are widely dispersed. Merfolk who have encountered them report that their communities are small and widely separated, each occupying a territory spanning many miles of the abyssal plain. The mortality rate among the young is high, as the deep sea is a harsh environment and the hatchlings are vulnerable to predation by larger creatures, but the enormous number of eggs ensures that enough survive to sustain the population.

## Ocean Floor of Auric and Greyring

Cephalopods are found on the deep ocean floor of the Auric Ocean and the Greyring Ocean, at depths no other sapient people can reach. Their habitat is the most remote and inaccessible of any in the known world. They favour rocky outcrops, canyon walls, and thermal vents of the abyssal floor, where hard surfaces provide shelter and deep-sea creatures provide food. Their settlements are built in crevices and caves of the deep seafloor, constructed from materials the abyssal environment provides, situated where currents bring food and terrain offers protection.

The geography of the deep ocean floor is almost entirely unknown to surface peoples. The distribution of cephalopod populations can only be inferred from rare encounters reported by merfolk and the occasional discovery of remains in the nets of deep-water fishermen. Cephalopods are absent from shallows, coasts, and the surface, as their bodies are adapted to the enormous pressures and total darkness of the deep and cannot survive in low-pressure, sunlit waters near the surface.

## Predator of the Deep-Sea Floor

The cephalopod diet is dominated by the creatures of the deep sea. Cephalopods are formidable predators in the abyssal environment, preying upon the fish, shelled creatures, and other deep-sea creatures that share their habitat. Their tentacled arms and suckers are well suited to capturing and consuming prey in the darkness of the deep. The hunt is conducted by ambush and active pursuit, and the cephalopod's ability to alter its colour and texture allows it to approach prey undetected before launching a swift attack.

The cephalopod digestive system is robust and well adapted to a diet of raw marine foods. Cephalopods consume their prey raw, as fire is impossible in the deep sea. Their beak-like jaws are strong enough to crack the shells of deep-sea shelled creatures and tear the flesh of larger fish, and their digestion is efficient, extracting nourishment from the sparse food the deep sea provides. The cephalopod approach to food is that of a patient and opportunistic predator, as the deep ocean is a place of scarcity and the cephalopod must take what it can find when it can find it.

## The Abyss's Keystone Hunter

Cephalopods occupy the niche of a large-bodied, intelligent predator on the deep ocean floor. Their impact on the ecology of the abyssal depths is significant: they are among the largest and most capable creatures of the deep sea, and their hunting regulates the populations of deep-sea creatures that share their habitat. Their presence on the seafloor is a stabilising force in an ecosystem governed by scarcity and the slow descent of organic matter from the sunlit waters above.

Cephalopods have almost no contact with other sapient peoples, as their habitat is separated from the surface world by miles of water and enormous pressure. [Merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) are the only other sapient people who inhabit the ocean, and the merfolk of the deep waters occasionally encounter cephalopods at the margins of their respective ranges, where the shallows meet the deep. These encounters are rare and generally cautious, as the two peoples are too different in form and habitat for either to benefit from sustained contact, and each regards the other with a mixture of curiosity and wariness.
