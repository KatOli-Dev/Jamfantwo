## Origins

The origins of the anurans are bound up with the marshes and still waters of the world, for the anuran body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by wet ground, still water, and the dense vegetation of the margins. Anurans are an amphibious people, found in the marshes and still waters of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the warm wetlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and their bodies are adapted to a life that moves freely between water and land. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between anurans and [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa), citing the amphibious nature shared by both peoples and their preference for fresh water and quiet country, though the anurans are smaller, more broadly built, and lack the shell and the dish-crown of the kappa. Others have argued for a connection to [lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians), pointing to the shared wetland habitat and the reptilian or amphibian form, but the anuran body is too distinct in its proportions and its life cycle for the comparison to be sustained. The anurans themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their having emerged from the mud of the first marsh, and they regard the still waters as their rightful home.

What is certain is that anurans have inhabited the marshes and still waters of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands is attested in the oldest records of the peoples who border the wetlands.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Anurans are a small people, standing no taller than two thirds the height of an average [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a compact and broadly built frame that is well suited to both swimming and the patient stillness that their way of life demands. Their skin is smooth and moist, ranging from green to brown and mottled combinations of these, and it is thinner and more permeable than that of land-dwelling peoples, requiring regular immersion in water to remain healthy. The skin serves as a secondary respiratory organ, absorbing oxygen from the water, and the anuran can remain submerged for extended periods without breathing air.

The anuran head is broad and flat, with a wide mouth and large, bulging eyes that are set high on the head, granting a near-complete field of vision while the body remains submerged. Their eyes are adapted to both above-water and underwater vision, and they are capable of detecting movement in conditions of very low light, for the marshes and still waters they inhabit are often shadowed by dense vegetation. Their hearing is acute, and their large, disc-like ears are sensitive to a wide range of sounds, particularly the low frequencies that carry well through water and dense vegetation.

Anuran limbs are strong and muscular, with the hind legs being notably longer and more powerful than the forelimbs, built for the sudden, explosive leaps that are the anuran's primary means of escape and ambush. An anuran can cover many times its own body length in a single jump, and the speed and distance of these leaps are remarkable for a people of their size. Their hands and feet are webbed, aiding in swimming, and their fingers are long and strong, capable of fine work and the patient manipulation of materials. The anuran body is not built for sustained effort or long-distance travel, for their musculature is specialised for the sudden burst rather than the steady pace, and they are most comfortable when they can remain in or near the water.

## Life Cycle

Anurans are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of fifty to seventy years under favourable conditions. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their mid-teens. Anuran women lay clutches of fifty to one hundred eggs in the water, where the eggs are left to develop without further care, hatching within a few weeks into aquatic larvae that are wholly different in form from the adult. The larvae are small, legless, and gilled, spending their first months entirely in the water before undergoing a gradual transformation that develops the limbs, the lungs, and the adult form.

The anuran life cycle is the most variable of any sapient people, for the larval stage is wholly aquatic and bears no resemblance to the adult, and the transformation from larva to adult is a dramatic and sustained process that may take several months. The mortality rate among the larvae is high, for the eggs and the young are vulnerable to predation by the fish, birds, and other creatures of the marsh, but the extraordinary number of eggs ensures that enough survive to sustain the population. Anuran populations grow quickly when the wetlands are healthy and the water is clean, and the species is among the most sensitive to changes in water quality, for their permeable skin absorbs pollutants as readily as it absorbs oxygen.

## Habitat and Distribution

Anurans are found in the marshes and still waters of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the warm wetlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), wherever the water is fresh, still, and abundant in vegetation. They favour the quiet margins of lakes, the still reaches of slow rivers, and the flooded meadows and marshes where the water spreads wide and shallow, and their settlements are typically built on the banks and the low islands within the wetland, combining structures above and below the waterline. The [geography](/content/geography) of each wetland shapes the character of the anuran communities, and the anurans of the northern marshes differ in form and habit from those of the southern swamps.

In Aethelar, anurans are found in the marshes of the central river valleys and the wetlands of the northern forests, where the water is still and the vegetation is thick. In Sahrana, they inhabit the warm wetlands of the coastal lowlands and the river deltas, sharing the swamp environment with the [lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians) and the [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga). Anurans are absent from the fast-flowing rivers, the open sea, and the dry regions, for their bodies require still water and a moist environment, and they cannot tolerate the salt or the aridity.

## Diet

The anuran diet is dominated by insects, small fish, and the other small creatures of the marsh and still water, and anurans are sit-and-wait predators of extraordinary patience. They remain motionless at the water's edge or among the vegetation, watching for prey to come within range, and their strike is a single, explosive movement of the tongue or the body that captures the prey before it can react. The anuran tongue is long, sticky, and extraordinarily fast, capable of extending to a distance of several times the length of the head and securing prey with a precision that is unmatched by any other people.

Plant foods form a minor part of the diet, though anurans will eat soft fruits, algae, and the tender shoots of aquatic plants when they are available. The anuran digestive system is simple and adapted to a diet of small, soft-bodied prey, and they consume their food whole, for their teeth are small and suited only for gripping rather than chewing. The anuran approach to food is patient and opportunistic, for they eat what comes within reach and fast when nothing does, and their metabolism is slow enough to sustain long periods without food between successful hunts.

## Ecology

Anurans occupy the niche of a small-bodied amphibious predator in the marsh and still-water ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is significant where they are present in numbers, for the anurans are among the primary consumers of insects in the wetland, and their presence regulates the populations of the smaller creatures that would otherwise overwhelm the ecosystem. The anurans are also a food source for the larger predators of the wetland, and their position in the food web is central to the ecology of the marsh.

Anurans share the wetlands with [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa) in portions of their range, and the two peoples maintain a peaceful coexistence, for their needs are similar but their sizes are different, and they prey upon different portions of the wetland fauna. [Lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians) and anurans share the warm swamps of Sahrana, and the relationship between the two is shaped by the difference in size, with the larger lacertians preying upon the fish and the larger creatures and the smaller anurans taking the insects and the smaller prey.
