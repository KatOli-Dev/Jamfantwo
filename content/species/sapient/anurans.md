## Emergence from the First Marsh

Anurans are an amphibious people inhabiting the marshes and still waters of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the warm wetlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). Their bodies are adapted for a life that moves freely between water and land, with smooth, permeable skin, webbed limbs, and the ability to remain submerged for extended periods. Naturalists have proposed kinship with [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa) on the basis of shared amphibious habits and preference for fresh water, though anurans are smaller and lack the shell and dish-crown of the kappa. A connection to [lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians) has also been suggested, citing the shared wetland habitat, but the anuran body is distinct in its proportions and its life cycle. Anuran oral traditions speak of their having emerged from the mud of the first marsh, and they regard the still waters as their rightful home. Their presence in the marshes of the known world is attested in the oldest records of the peoples who border the wetlands.

## Smooth Skin and Leaping Limbs

Anurans stand no taller than two-thirds the height of an average [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a compact, broadly built frame suited to both swimming and the patient stillness their way of life demands. Their skin is smooth and moist, ranging from green to brown and mottled combinations of these, and it is thinner and more permeable than that of land-dwelling peoples, requiring regular immersion in water to remain healthy. The skin serves as a secondary respiratory organ, absorbing oxygen from the water, and anurans can remain submerged for extended periods without surfacing to breathe air.

The anuran head is broad and flat, with a wide mouth and large, bulging eyes set high on the head, granting near-complete field of vision while the body remains submerged. Their eyes are adapted to both above-water and underwater vision and can detect movement in very low light, as the marshes they inhabit are often shadowed by dense vegetation. Their hearing is acute, and their large, disc-like ears are sensitive to a wide range of sounds, particularly low frequencies that carry well through water and dense vegetation.

Anuran limbs are strong and muscular, with hind legs notably longer and more powerful than the forelimbs. These are built for the sudden, explosive leaps that serve as the anuran's primary means of escape and ambush. An anuran can cover many times its own body length in a single jump. Their hands and feet are webbed, aiding in swimming, and their fingers are long and strong, capable of fine work and patient manipulation of materials. The anuran body is not built for sustained effort or long-distance travel, as their musculature is specialised for the sudden burst rather than the steady pace, and they are most comfortable when they can remain in or near the water.

## Eggs, Larvae, and Transformation

Anurans are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of fifty to seventy years under favourable conditions. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature by their mid-teens. Anuran women lay clutches of fifty to one hundred eggs in the water, where the eggs develop without further care, hatching within weeks into aquatic larvae wholly different in form from the adult. The larvae are small, legless, and gilled, spending their first months entirely in the water before undergoing a gradual transformation that develops the limbs, the lungs, and the adult form over the course of several months.

The anuran life cycle is the most variable of any sapient people. The larval stage is wholly aquatic and bears no resemblance to the adult, and the transformation from larva to adult completes over a period of three to five months. The mortality rate among the larvae is high, as the eggs and young are vulnerable to predation by fish, birds, and other marsh creatures; of fifty to one hundred eggs in a clutch, an average of eight to twelve larvae survive to adulthood, sufficient to sustain the population. Anuran populations grow quickly when the wetlands are healthy and the water is clean. The species is among the most sensitive to changes in water quality, as their permeable skin absorbs pollutants as readily as it absorbs oxygen.

## Marshes and Still Waters

Anurans are found in the marshes and still waters of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the warm wetlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), wherever the water is fresh, still, and abundant in vegetation. They favour the quiet margins of lakes, the still reaches of slow rivers, and the flooded meadows and marshes where water spreads wide and shallow. Their settlements are typically built on banks and low islands within the wetland, combining structures above and below the waterline. The character of anuran communities varies with the geography of each wetland, and the anurans of the northern marshes differ in form and habit from those of the southern swamps.

In Aethelar, anurans are found in the marshes of the central river valleys and the wetlands of the northern forests. In Sahrana, they inhabit the warm wetlands of the coastal lowlands and the river deltas, sharing the swamp environment with [lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians) and [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga). Anurans are absent from fast-flowing rivers, the open sea, and dry regions, as their bodies require still water and a moist environment and cannot tolerate salt or aridity.

## Patient Sit-and-Wait Feeding

The anuran diet is dominated by insects, small fish, and other small creatures of the marsh and still water. Anurans are sit-and-wait predators capable of remaining motionless at the water's edge or among the vegetation for up to two hours, watching for prey to come within range. Their strike is a single, explosive movement of the tongue or body that captures the prey before it can react. The anuran tongue is long, sticky, and fast, capable of extending to four times the length of the head and striking in under forty milliseconds, securing prey with a success rate of approximately eighty percent on insects and small fish.

Plant foods form a minor part of the diet, though anurans will eat soft fruits, algae, and the tender shoots of the [Water Lily](/content/species/flora/water-lily) when available. The anuran digestive system is simple and adapted to a diet of small, soft-bodied prey. They consume their food whole, as their teeth are small and suited only for gripping rather than chewing. The anuran approach to food is patient and opportunistic: they eat what comes within reach and fast when nothing does, and their slow constitution sustains long periods without food between successful hunts.

## Amphibious Niche in Wetland Ecosystems

Anurans occupy the niche of a small-bodied amphibious predator in marsh and still-water ecosystems. Their impact on their surroundings is significant where they are present in numbers, as they are among the primary consumers of insects in the wetland, regulating the populations of smaller creatures that would otherwise overwhelm the ecosystem. Anurans are also a food source for larger wetland predators, and their position in the food web is central to marsh ecology.

Anurans share the wetlands with [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa) in portions of their range, maintaining a peaceful coexistence grounded in similar needs but different sizes, each preying upon different portions of the wetland fauna. [Lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians) and anurans share the warm swamps of Sahrana, with the larger lacertians taking fish and larger creatures while the smaller anurans take insects and smaller prey. The relationship between anurans and the other wetland peoples is shaped by these differences in scale and in the niches each occupies within the shared environment.
