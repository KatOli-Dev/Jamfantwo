## Origins

The origins of the murids are poorly documented, for the murid peoples have left no written records and their oral traditions are known to other peoples only through fragmentary and often unreliable accounts. Murids are a small, rat-like people, found in the underground and the margins of settled communities across the known world, and their form combines a humanoid body with the head, ears, and teeth of a rodent. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between murids and [kobolds](/content/species/sapient/kobolds), citing the small stature, the subterranean habits, and the preference for tunnels and enclosed spaces shared by both peoples, though the differences in form are considerable. Others have argued for a connection to [goblins](/content/species/sapient/goblins), pointing to the marginal habitat and the opportunistic way of life that both share, but the murid body is too distinct in form for the comparison to be sustained. The murids themselves have contributed little to the debate, for they are a people of the present moment, more concerned with survival than with questions of origin.

What is known is that murids have been present in the known world for as long as records have been kept, and their populations have proved remarkably resilient in the face of pressure from the larger and more powerful peoples among whom they dwell. Murid communities have persisted in the margins of settled territory, and their ability to maintain themselves in the smallest and most overlooked spaces is a defining characteristic of the species.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Murids are the smallest of the sapient peoples alongside [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) and [halflings](/content/species/sapient/halflings), standing no taller than half the height of an average [human](/content/species/sapient/humans). Their frames are thin and wiry, built for agility and the navigation of narrow spaces rather than strength. The head is that of a rat, with a long, pointed muzzle, large rounded ears, and sharp, forward-facing eyes that are adapted to low light. The teeth are large and ever-growing, with prominent incisors that are used for gnawing, cutting, and the working of materials, and murids must wear their teeth down through constant use, for they will otherwise grow too long and cause injury.

The murid body is superbly adapted to the underground and the marginal spaces it inhabits. Their movements are quick and fluid, and they are capable of fitting through openings that would defeat any other people, for their skeletons are flexible and their bodies are lean. Their hands are small and quick, with long, slender fingers that are capable of fine and precise work, and their feet are similarly dexterous, capable of gripping and climbing with a facility that recalls the [kobolds](/content/species/sapient/kobolds). Murid movements are characterised by a restless energy, and they are seldom still, for their metabolism is fast and their bodies demand constant nourishment.

Murid senses are sharp and specialised for their environment. Their large ears are mobile and extraordinarily sensitive, capable of detecting the faintest sound in the quiet of the underground, and their hearing is the keenest of any people in the high registers. Their vision is adapted to very low light, performing well in the dim conditions of tunnels and cellars, though they are poorly suited to bright light and tend to avoid the open sun. Their sense of smell is acute, and murids can detect food, water, and the approach of other creatures by scent alone, a gift that serves them well in the darkness where they often dwell. The murid sense of touch is highly developed, and their long, hairless tails are sensitive organs of balance and sensation, used to navigate tight spaces and to detect vibrations.

## Life Cycle

Murids are the shortest-lived of the sapient peoples, with a typical lifespan of twenty to thirty-five years under favourable conditions, and many do not reach old age owing to the harshness of their circumstances. They mature with extraordinary speed, reaching physical adulthood within three or four years of birth, and their decline in later years is rapid. Murid women carry their young for approximately four months before giving birth to litters of four to eight, the largest and most frequent litters of any sapient people, and a single murid community may produce several generations within a decade.

Murid populations can increase with extraordinary rapidity when food is abundant and conditions are favourable, and a small community may grow to many times its original size within a few years. This capacity for growth is balanced by a high mortality rate at all ages, for the marginal habitats that murids occupy are dangerous and the food supply is uncertain, and the murid population is subject to sharp fluctuations in response to changes in the availability of resources. The rapid turnover of generations has produced a people who are wholly oriented toward the present, for the individual murid has little time for long-term planning and even less for the accumulation of knowledge beyond what is immediately useful.

## Habitat and Distribution

Murids are found in the underground and the margins of settled communities across all three continents, and they are the most widely distributed of the smaller peoples, owing to their ability to live in the spaces that other peoples overlook or abandon. In the towns and cities of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), murids inhabit the cellars, sewers, and abandoned buildings of the human settlements, where they find shelter and food in the waste and surplus of the larger peoples. In the underground of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), murids share the shallow tunnels with the [kobolds](/content/species/sapient/kobolds), occupying the margins of the cave systems and the spaces between the [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) workings. In [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), murids are found in the ruins and the refuse heaps of the coastal cities, and in the walls and foundations of the older buildings.

Murid settlements are small and impermanent, established in whatever sheltered space is available, and a community may relocate at short notice in response to pressure from other peoples or changes in the availability of food. The [geography](/content//geography) of the murid habitat is defined not by the natural landscape but by the structures and activities of the larger peoples, and the murids are a people of the margins, living in the spaces between and beneath the settlements of others.

## Diet

The murid diet is the most varied and least selective of any sapient people, and murids will eat almost anything that is organic and can be digested. Grain, vegetables, meat, fish, insects, fungi, and the waste of other peoples all contribute to the murid diet, and their digestion is hardy and tolerant of spoilage and contamination. This willingness to eat anything is the foundation of the murid capacity for survival in the margins of settled territory, where the food supply is the surplus and the refuse of the larger peoples.

Murids do not practise agriculture or husbandry of any kind, and they obtain their food entirely through foraging, scavenging, and the occasional hunting of small animals. Their gnawing teeth allow them to access food that other peoples cannot reach, for they can chew through wood, cloth, and thin metal to reach the stores within, and their small size allows them to enter spaces where food has been left or discarded. The murid approach to food is entirely opportunistic, driven by the constant demand of a fast metabolism and a short life, and a murid will eat whatever is available whenever it is hungry, which is nearly always.

## Ecology

Murids occupy the niche of a small-bodied opportunist and scavenger in the margins of the settlements and underground spaces they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is modest in scale but pervasive, for the murid presence in an area is felt through the consumption of stored food, the gnawing of structures, and the spreading of materials from one place to another. Murids are both a nuisance and an incidental benefit to the communities among whom they dwell, for they consume waste that would otherwise accumulate and they are a food source for the predators and hunters of the underground.

Murids share their marginal habitat with [goblins](/content/species/sapient/goblins) in portions of their range, and the two peoples maintain a wary coexistence, for their needs and habits overlap and they compete for the same resources. [Kobolds](/content/species/sapient/kobolds) and murids occasionally share the same tunnel systems, and the relationship between the two is one of mutual avoidance, with each people occupying a different portion of the underground.
