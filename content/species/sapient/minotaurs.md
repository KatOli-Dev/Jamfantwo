## Origins

The origins of the minotaurs are bound up with the labyrinthine highlands of the known world, for the minotaur body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by rocky, broken terrain and the demands of a large, powerful frame. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between minotaurs and [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs), citing the partly animal form of both peoples and their shared preference for open country. Others have argued that minotaurs are more closely related to [ogres](/content/species/sapient/ogres), pointing to the great size and strength that both share. The minotaurs themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their having emerged from the heart of the labyrinthine highlands, born from the stone and the earth, and they hold these traditions with a quiet but unshakeable conviction.

What is certain is that minotaurs have inhabited the labyrinthine highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands is the oldest and most enduring feature of the region's cultural landscape. The highlands bear the mark of minotaur culture in their ancient trackways, their carved stone markers, and the careful management of the upland pastures that has sustained the minotaur people for centuries.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Minotaurs are a large and powerfully built people, standing taller than [orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) and broader than [ogres](/content/species/sapient/ogres), with a form that combines humanoid and bovine features. The head is that of a bull, large and heavy, with a broad muzzle, a pair of forward-curving horns, and large, dark eyes. The body is humanoid from the neck down, but it is massive in proportion, with thick bones, heavy musculature, and a broad chest that houses large lungs and a powerful heart. The hands are large and strong, with thick, calloused fingers that are nonetheless capable of considerable dexterity, and the feet are broad and cloven, like those of a bovine, providing sure footing on rocky and uneven ground.

The minotaur body is built for strength and endurance, and the combination of humanoid hands and bovine lower limbs grants them a unique combination of power and surefootedness. A minotaur can sustain heavy labour for many hours, carry loads that would defeat a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) or a [dwarf](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), and traverse the broken terrain of the highlands with a confidence that no fully humanoid people can match. Their horns are a formidable natural weapon, and a charging minotaur is a sight that few who have witnessed it care to see again.

Minotaur skin is thick and tough, ranging from dark brown to reddish-tan, and it is covered in a short, coarse fur that is darker on the lower body and lighter on the upper. Their eyes are adapted to the variable light of the highlands, performing well in both the bright sun of the open heights and the dim light of the narrow valleys and gorges. Their hearing is acute, and their sense of smell is well developed, particularly for the detection of water and the reading of the wind, a gift that serves them in the navigation of their labyrinthine homeland.

## Life Cycle

Minotaurs are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of eighty to one hundred and twenty years under favourable conditions. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late teens, though they continue to gain strength and mass well into their twenties. Minotaur women carry their young for approximately nine months before giving birth, and births are usually single. The infant is large and well-developed, capable of walking within hours, and it grows quickly, reaching adolescence within a decade.

Minotaur populations grow steadily when the highlands are at peace and the pastures are sufficient, and the young are raised collectively by the community, with all adults sharing in their care and training. The education of a minotaur is long and thorough, encompassing the navigation of the highlands, the care of livestock, the working of stone and metal, and the history and traditions of the people. A minotaur is not considered fully adult until they have demonstrated competence in all of these, and the rites of passage that mark the transition to adulthood are the most significant events in minotaur culture.

## Habitat and Distribution

Minotaurs are found chiefly in the labyrinthine highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the terrain is a complex network of ridges, valleys, gorges, and plateaus that forms a natural labyrinth. They are the dominant people of the highlands, and their communities are scattered across the full extent of the upland country, from the foothills of the mountain spine to the edges of the northern forests and the central grasslands. The [geography](/content//geography) of the highlands, with its broken terrain and its mixture of open pasture and sheltered valley, is ideally suited to the minotaur form, and the minotaurs have shaped the landscape over centuries through the construction of trackways, walls, and stone markers.

Smaller populations of minotaurs are found in the highlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), where the interior ranges provide a similar mix of rocky terrain and upland pasture, and in the rocky massifs of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where the upland settlements of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) interior offer a comparable environment. Minotaurs are absent from the lowlands, the forests, and the coasts, for their large frames and cloven feet are ill suited to soft ground, dense vegetation, or the confined spaces of a ship.

## Diet

The minotaur diet is a mixture of cultivated and gathered foods, reflecting the pastoral and highland character of their way of life. The staple is grain, grown in terraced fields on the valley floors and the lower slopes of the highlands, supplemented by hardy vegetables and the produce of orchards tended in the sheltered glens. Minotaurs are also pastoral, keeping herds of hardy cattle and goats on the upland pastures, and the products of these herds, including milk, cheese, and meat, form a significant part of the diet.

Meat is eaten regularly, and the minotaurs are skilled in the preservation of meat through salting, smoking, and drying, techniques that allow them to store provisions for the winter months when the highlands are snowbound. The minotaur approach to food is communal and generous, for the sharing of meals is a central feature of their culture, and the hospitality of a minotaur household is extended freely to guests, whether of their own kind or of other peoples.

## Ecology

Minotaurs occupy the niche of a large-bodied cultivator and pastoralist in the highland ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is considerable, for the minotaur way of life reshapes the highlands through terracing, walling, and the management of pasture. A valley long inhabited by minotaurs bears the mark of their care in every terrace and wall, and the upland pastures are maintained through a tradition of rotational grazing that sustains both the herds and the grass.

Minotaurs share the highlands with [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), and the three peoples coexist in a relationship that is generally peaceful and mutually beneficial. The humans settle the lowland valleys and trade routes, the dwarves work the deeper mountains, and the minotaurs tend the highland pastures, and each benefits from the produce and the craft of the others. [Centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) occasionally range into the lower highlands, and the two peoples maintain a respectful distance, for their ways of life are different but their values are similar.
