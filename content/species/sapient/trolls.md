## Origins

The origins of the trolls are shrouded in uncertainty, though their affinity for stone and mountain suggests a lineage bound to the deep places of the world. Some naturalists have proposed that trolls share a distant kinship with [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), citing their thick hides, their preference for subterranean and upland habitats, and their remarkable capacity for endurance. Others reject this connection, noting that trolls lack the fine dexterity and the instinct for craft that define dwarvenkind, and that their bodies are altogether more primitive in form. The trolls themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of emerging from the roots of the mountains when the world was young, but they take little interest in the questions of naturalists and have offered no detailed account of their history.

What is clear from the archaeological record is that trolls have inhabited the mountains and deep forests of the known world for as long as any people can account for. Their remains are found in the deepest layers of cave systems in both [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), often beneath the oldest dwarven workings, and the evidence suggests that trolls occupied these places before any other sapient people. Whether this indicates a truly ancient lineage or merely a species that has always favoured remote and sheltered ground is a question that no scholar has settled conclusively.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Trolls are the largest of the sapient peoples save for the [giants](/content/species/sapient/giants), standing a full head and shoulders above an [orc](/content/species/sapient/orcs) and more than twice the height of a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans). Their frames are massive and broadly built, with thick bones and heavy musculature that grant them enormous physical strength. Their movements are deliberate and slow, lacking the agility of smaller peoples, but the force they can bring to bear is formidable, and a troll can uproot a tree or shatter a stone wall with little apparent effort.

The skin of a troll is its most remarkable feature, for it is thick, tough, and remarkably resistant to injury. The hide ranges in colour from greyish-green to dark brown, often mottled with patterns that blend into the rocky and forested environments the trolls inhabit. This toughness provides a natural armour that turns aside blows that would wound any other people, and it contributes to the trollish reputation for being nearly impossible to harm. Trollish hair is coarse and sparse, confined chiefly to the head and the ridge of the spine, and it is dark and wiry where it grows.

Trollish senses are adequate but undistinguished. Their vision is keen in darkness, adapted to the caves and deep forests they prefer, though they are uncomfortable in bright sunlight and tend to avoid open ground during the day. Their hearing is competent but not especially acute, and their sense of smell is moderate. Trolls rely more upon their great strength and resilience than upon any sharpness of sense, and their awareness of their surroundings is sufficient rather than exceptional. One curious feature of trollish physiology is their slow metabolism, for trolls may go for long periods without food and show little sign of distress, a quality that suits them well in the sparse environments they favour.

## Life Cycle

Trolls are long-lived, with a typical lifespan of two hundred to three hundred years under favourable conditions, though reliable data are difficult to obtain, as trolls do not keep written records and are suspicious of those who ask them questions about their age. They grow to maturity very slowly, not reaching their full size and strength until their fortieth or fiftieth year, and they remain vigorous well into their second century. Trollish women carry their young for approximately two years before giving birth, the longest gestation among the sapient peoples, and births are invariably single. The infant is large and well-developed at birth, capable of walking within hours, though it requires many years of care before it is fully independent.

Trollish births are very infrequent, and populations grow at a glacial pace even when conditions are favourable. This slow increase is balanced by the extraordinary longevity of the species and by the low rate of death among adults, for a fully grown troll has few natural predators and is remarkably resistant to disease. Trollish communities are small and widely dispersed, and the slow pace of their lives is reflected in every aspect of their culture, from the patient working of their dwellings to the unhurried exchange of news between far-flung kindreds. A trollish community may go for decades without contact from others of its kind, and such reunions are marked by long and deliberate gatherings in which years of accumulated experience are shared.

## Habitat and Distribution

Trolls are found in the mountains and deep forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the more remote highlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), with smaller populations reported in the rocky uplands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). They favour terrain that is rugged, secluded, and far from the settlements of other peoples, where their great size is an advantage rather than a hindrance and where the sparse resources are sufficient for a people of slow metabolism and small numbers. Trollish dwellings are typically caves or shallow excavations, enlarged and shaped over many years, and they are invariably situated in places that are difficult to reach.

In Aethelar, trolls are found chiefly in the higher reaches of the mountain spine, above the [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) settlements and below the snowline, and in the deepest parts of the northern forests, where the canopy is so thick that the ground is in perpetual shade. In Morvind, they inhabit the most remote valleys and highest plateaus of the interior, in places that even the dwarves find too exposed or too barren to claim. The [geography](/content//geography) of each region determines the distribution of trollish populations, and trolls are absent from the lowlands and coasts entirely, for they are uncomfortable in warm, open, or densely settled country.

## Diet

The trollish diet is dominated by what the mountains and forests can provide, and trolls are opportunistic omnivores with a strong preference for meat. They hunt the large game animals of the uplands and forests, including elk, boar, and mountain goats, and a single troll can consume an extraordinary quantity of food at one sitting before fasting for many days. Plant foods are gathered rather than cultivated, and trolls have no tradition of agriculture, though they possess a practical knowledge of the edible roots, fungi, and berries of their homelands.

Trollish digestion is robust and unhurried, like the trolls themselves, and they are able to extract nourishment from coarse and unappealing foods that other peoples would reject. The bark of certain trees, the roots of mountain shrubs, and the larger fungi of the cave systems all contribute to the trollish diet when game is scarce. Trolls do not cook with any regularity, preferring their food raw, though they will roast meat when a fire is already burning and the occasion warrants it. Their relationship with food is one of patient sufficiency rather than pleasure or refinement, and they eat what is available when they are hungry and think little of it otherwise.

## Ecology

Trolls occupy the niche of a large-bodied, slow-breeding omnivore in the remote and rugged environments they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is considerable where they are present, for a troll consumes a great deal and alters the landscape through the excavation of dwellings and the clearing of paths through difficult terrain. Yet their numbers are so few and their movements so deliberate that their overall effect on the ecology of a region is modest compared to that of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) or orcs. Trollish communities and the wildlife of their territories tend to reach an equilibrium that persists for centuries without significant change.

Trolls have little contact with other sapient peoples, owing to the remoteness of their habitats and their preference for solitude. Where trolls and [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) share the same mountain ranges, they generally separate by elevation, with dwarves below and trolls above, and the two peoples avoid one another with a mutual lack of interest. Trolls and [orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) occasionally encounter one another in the wilder uplands, and such meetings are wary but rarely hostile, for neither people has much that the other desires.
