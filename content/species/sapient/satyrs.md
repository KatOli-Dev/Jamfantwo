## Origins

The origins of the satyrs are bound up with the woodland fringes of the world, for the satyr body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by the borderlands between forest and open country. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between satyrs and [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs), citing the partly animal form of both peoples and their shared preference for the edges of the wild. Others have argued that satyrs are more closely related to [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes), pointing to their small stature, their woodland habits, and their gift for going unnoticed. The question remains unresolved, and the satyrs themselves have contributed little to the debate, for they are a people more given to song and story than to natural philosophy.

What is known is that satyrs have inhabited the woodland fringes of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands is attested in the oldest elven records. The satyrs and the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) have coexisted in the borderlands of the forests for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is among the oldest and most complex in the known world.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Satyrs are a small to moderate people, standing shorter than [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) but taller than [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes), with a form that combines humanoid and caprine features. From the waist up, they present a humanoid torso, lean and wiry, with a head, shoulders, and arms. From the waist down, the legs are those of a goat, jointed in the caprine manner and ending in cloven hooves. The lower body is covered in a short, coarse fur that ranges from brown to black, and a small pair of horns grows from the brow, curving back and slightly outward. The whole form is compact and agile, built for climbing, leaping, and the swift navigation of broken ground.

The satyr body is superbly adapted to the woodland fringe, where the terrain is a mixture of trees, scrub, and open glade. Their hooves are sure on rock and root, and they can traverse steep and uneven ground with a speed and confidence that no fully humanoid people can match. Their arms are strong and their hands are quick, used for climbing, the playing of instruments, and the manipulation of tools and food. Satyr movements are fluid and expressive, and they are noted among other peoples for their grace and their seemingly inexhaustible capacity for leaping and capering.

Satyr senses are acute, particularly their hearing and their sense of balance. Their ears are long and pointed, mobile and sensitive, capable of detecting the faintest sound in the woodland around them. Their vision is keen in the dappled light of the forest fringe, performing well in both shade and open air, though they are less capable in true darkness than the cave-dwelling peoples. Their sense of smell is moderate, adequate for the detection of food and the reading of the woodland, but not remarkable. Satyr skin ranges from ruddy to brown, and the hair of the head is dark and curly, often worn long and untamed.

## Life Cycle

Satyrs are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of eighty to one hundred and twenty years under favourable conditions. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late teens. Satyr women carry their young for approximately seven months before giving birth, and births are usually single, though twins are not uncommon. Infant mortality is low in settled communities, for the woodland fringe is a relatively sheltered environment, and satyr communities are attentive to the care of their young.

Satyr populations grow steadily when conditions are favourable, and the woodland fringes of Aethelar have supported stable satyr communities for many centuries. The satyr way of life is oriented around the kindred and the glade, and the bonds of community are strong and enduring. The elders of a satyr kindred are valued for their memory of songs, stories, and the history of the woodland, and the transmission of this oral tradition from one generation to the next is the most important function of satyr culture.

## Habitat and Distribution

Satyrs are found chiefly in the woodland fringes of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the forest gives way to glade, meadow, and scrub. They favour the edges of the great northern forests and the wooded margins of the central grasslands, where the terrain offers both the shelter of trees and the openness of clear ground. Satyr settlements are small and inconspicuous, typically a loose cluster of shelters built among the trees at the forest's edge, woven from branches and thatched with leaves and reed.

Smaller populations of satyrs are found in the wooded uplands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), where the lower slopes of the interior ranges provide a similar mix of tree cover and open ground. In [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), a few satyr communities are reported in the wooded foothills of the northern coast, where the vegetation is thick enough to provide cover but open enough to suit the satyr temperament. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region determines the character of satyr life, but in all cases the satyrs seek the edge, the boundary, the place where two kinds of country meet.

## Diet

The satyr diet is a mixture of plant and animal foods, reflecting the varied resources of the woodland fringe. Fruits, nuts, berries, and tender shoots form the staple, gathered from the trees and shrubs of the forest edge and supplemented by cultivated herbs and vegetables grown in small garden plots near the settlement. Mushrooms and fungi are gathered from the woodland floor, and the satyr knowledge of edible and useful fungi is extensive, rivaling that of the [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes).

Meat is eaten in moderation, chiefly small game such as rabbits, birds, and fish from woodland streams. Satyrs are capable hunters but not dedicated ones, and the hunt is regarded as a pleasant diversion rather than a central activity. The satyr approach to food is communal and celebratory, for meals are occasions for music, storytelling, and the strengthening of kindred bonds. Fermentation is practised for the making of wine from wild grapes and other fruits, and the satyr gift for winemaking is recognised throughout the regions where they are known.

## Ecology

Satyrs occupy the niche of a small-bodied forager and browser in the woodland fringe, and their impact on their surroundings is modest and localised. They alter the forest edge through their gathering and their small-scale cultivation, encouraging the growth of useful plants and discouraging the spread of thorny and unproductive growth. The effect is subtle, and a satyr-inhabited woodland fringe is often indistinguishable from wild country to the untrained eye, though the careful observer will note the abundance of fruit-bearing shrubs and the neatly maintained paths.

Satyrs share the woodland fringe with [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) and [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes), and the three peoples coexist in a relationship that is generally peaceful and mutually beneficial. The elves tend the deep forest, the gnomes the hills and hollows, and the satyrs the edges between, and each people respects the domain of the others. Where satyrs encounter [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), the relationship is friendly but guarded, for satyrs are wary of the human tendency to clear and settle the land that the satyrs prefer.
