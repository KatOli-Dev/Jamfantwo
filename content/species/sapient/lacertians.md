## Origins

The origins of the lacertians are bound up with the swamps and warm wetlands of the world, for the lacertian body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by warm water, mud, and the dense vegetation of the marshes. Lacertians are a reptilian people, found in the swamps and warm wetlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) and the southern reaches of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and their scaled bodies and aquatic habits suggest a lineage adapted to warmth and water. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between lacertians and [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga), citing the reptilian form and the warm, watery habitat shared by both peoples, though the lacertians are smaller, more broadly built, and lack the serpentine lower body of the naga. Others have argued for a connection to [kobolds](/content/species/sapient/kobolds), pointing to the reptilian form shared by both, but the differences in size, habitat, and habit are too great for the comparison to be sustained. The lacertians themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their having emerged from the mud of the first swamp, and they regard the warm wetlands as their rightful home.

What is certain is that lacertians have inhabited the swamps and warm wetlands of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands is attested in the oldest records of the peoples who border the marshes. The lacertians and the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the southern coasts have coexisted for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the difference in their preferred terrain.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Lacertians are a medium-sized people, standing shorter than [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) but taller than [kobolds](/content/species/sapient/kobolds), with a compact and muscular frame that is well suited to both swimming and the navigation of dense vegetation. Their bodies are covered in smooth, overlapping scales that range from dark green to olive, brown, and mottled combinations of these, providing camouflage in the shadowed waters and the vegetation of the swamp. The scales are water-resistant and provide a measure of natural protection against abrasion and the bites of the aquatic creatures that share their habitat.

The lacertian head is broad and flat, with a long, rounded snout and rows of sharp teeth that are well suited to a carnivorous diet. The eyes are set high on the head, allowing a lacertian to see above the surface while the rest of the body remains submerged, and their vision is keen both above and below the water. A nictitating membrane protects the eyes during submersion, and their colour vision is good, adapted to the variable light of the swamp. Their hearing is competent, though less acute than that of the surface-dwelling peoples, and their sense of smell is well developed, particularly in water.

Lacertian limbs are strong and proportioned for a life that alternates between water and land, with webbed fingers and toes that aid in swimming and powerful legs that are capable of sustained movement through mud and dense vegetation. Their tails are long and muscular, used for swimming and balance, and the whole body is built for the kind of sustained, patient effort that the swamp environment demands. Lacertians are warm-blooded, despite their reptilian appearance, and their bodies are well suited to the warm climates they prefer, though they are uncomfortable in cold and become sluggish when the temperature drops.

## Life Cycle

Lacertians are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of sixty to ninety years under favourable conditions. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late teens. Lacertian women lay clutches of six to twelve eggs in a nest dug into the muddy bank of a swamp or waterway, and the eggs are incubated by the warmth of the decomposing vegetation that surrounds them for a period of two to three months before hatching. The hatchlings are small and numerous, and they are capable of swimming and feeding within hours of emerging from the egg.

Lacertian populations can grow quickly when conditions are favourable, for the size of the clutches ensures that each generation is larger than the last, and the warm, sheltered environment of the swamp provides a relatively safe setting for the young. The mortality rate among hatchlings is moderate, for the swamp contains predators that take their toll, but the survivors grow quickly and reach maturity within a decade. The rapid growth and the high reproductive rate give lacertian communities a resilience that allows them to recover quickly from losses, and their populations tend to be stable or growing when the wetlands are healthy.

## Habitat and Distribution

Lacertians are found chiefly in the swamps and warm wetlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where the climate is hot and humid and the water is warm, and the vegetation is dense and abundant. They favour the slow-moving rivers, the still lagoons, and the flooded forests of the coastal lowlands, where the water provides both food and shelter and the land is too wet for other peoples to settle. Their settlements are typically built on raised platforms and mounds within the swamp, constructed from the materials that the wetland provides, and they are situated where the water is shallow and the fishing is good.

Smaller populations of lacertians are found in the warm wetlands of southern [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the rivers of the southern plains spread into marshes and lagoons, and in the river deltas of the Sahrani coast. Lacertians are absent from the cold regions, the mountains, and the dry interior, for their bodies are adapted to warmth and water and fare poorly without both. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region determines the distribution of lacertian populations, and the lacertian range is restricted to the warm, wet lowlands where the conditions suit them.

## Diet

The lacertian diet is dominated by aquatic and semi-aquatic prey, reflecting the swamp environment they inhabit. Fish, crustaceans, amphibians, and water birds form the staple, hunted in the water and along the margins of the swamp. Lacertians are patient and methodical hunters, capable of lying submerged for long periods while waiting for prey to come within range, and their speed and power in the water make them formidable predators in their element. They are also skilled foragers, collecting the edible plants, roots, and fruits that grow in and around the wetlands, and their knowledge of the swamp's resources is extensive.

Lacertians do not practise agriculture, for the swamp provides abundantly without the need for cultivation, and their relationship with food is that of a predator and a gatherer operating within a rich and complex ecosystem. They consume their food raw or lightly cooked over driftwood fires, and their digestion is robust and well adapted to a diet of raw aquatic foods. The lacertian tolerance for spoiled and partially decomposed food is notable, for their digestive system can process materials that would make a human ill, a gift of the warm, organic environment in which they live.

## Ecology

Lacertians occupy the niche of a medium-bodied aquatic predator in the swamp and wetland ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is considerable, for the lacertians are the dominant predators of the warm wetlands, and their hunting regulates the populations of fish, amphibians, and water birds throughout their range. The lacertian management of the swamp is subtle but pervasive, for their movements through the water and their construction of platforms and mounds alter the flow and the vegetation of the wetland in ways that support a diverse and abundant ecosystem.

Lacertians share the warm wetlands with [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga) in portions of their range, and the two peoples maintain a wary coexistence, for both are aquatic predators and their needs overlap. The naga, being larger and more mobile, tend to favour the deeper waters, while the lacertians prefer the shallows and the vegetation, and the two peoples separate by depth and terrain. [Anurans](/content/species/sapient/anurans) share the swamp environment with lacertians, and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the difference in size and habit, with the smaller anurans occupying the margins and the lacertians the deeper waters.
