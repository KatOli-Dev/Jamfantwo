## Origins

Ogres are a large and powerfully built people, larger than [orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) but smaller than [trolls](/content/species/sapient/trolls), found chiefly in the wild and marginal lands of the world where the presence of other peoples is thin. Their origins are a matter of speculation, for the ogreish peoples have left no written records and their oral traditions are known to other peoples only through fragmentary accounts. Some naturalists propose a kinship between ogres and orcs, citing the broadly similar build, preference for harsh terrain, and social organisation of the two peoples. Others argue for a connection to trolls, pointing to the great size and resilience that both share. Neither view has been demonstrated, and the ogreish peoples themselves have shown no interest in the question. The archaeological record offers little clarity, as ogreish remains are seldom found in conditions allowing confident identification. Ogres have inhabited the wild margins of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence predates the expansion of settled peoples. Ogres may thus be counted among the older inhabitants of the world, though their history is the least documented of any sapient species.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Ogres stand taller than [orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) and approach the height of [trolls](/content/species/sapient/trolls), though they lack the enormous bulk of the latter. Their bones are thick and musculature is massive, built for brute strength rather than agility or endurance. An ogre can match a troll in sheer force for a brief period, though trolls surpass them in sustained effort. Their movements are heavy and deliberate, and they are incapable of the swift sprints that orcs can manage, relying instead on patient ambush and overwhelming force when they hunt.

Ogreish skin is thick and leathery, ranging from dull yellowish-brown to greyish-green, providing natural protection against injury and the elements. Their hair is coarse, dark, and sparse, worn short or in matted tangles. Ogreish faces are broad and heavy-featured, with prominent brows and large jaws, and their teeth are large and strong, with lower canines often protruding as blunt tusks. Their eyes are small but keen, adapted to the low light of dawn and dusk, and their hearing and sense of smell are adequate though not remarkable.

One notable feature of ogreish physiology is their remarkable tolerance for hardship. Ogres endure cold, hunger, and injury to a degree that would fell most other peoples, and they recover from wounds that would be mortal to a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans). This resilience is the defining characteristic of the ogreish body, allowing survival in conditions that would defeat any people less able to absorb punishment.

## Life Cycle

Ogres live eighty to one hundred and twenty years under favourable conditions, though many do not reach old age owing to the harshness of their lives. They mature faster than trolls but slower than humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their early twenties. Ogreish women carry their young for approximately ten months before giving birth, and births are usually single, though twins are not uncommon. Infant mortality is moderate by the standards of wild peoples, lower than among orcs or [goblins](/content/species/sapient/goblins) owing to the fierce protectiveness of ogreish parents, but higher than among the settled peoples.

Ogreish populations grow slowly, as births are infrequent and the conditions in which ogres live are demanding. The slow increase is balanced by the longevity and resilience of adults, and ogreish communities tend to remain stable over long periods, their numbers neither growing rapidly nor declining sharply. The elders of an ogreish community are those who have survived the longest, and their authority rests upon proven strength and the accumulated knowledge of a hard life.

## Habitat and Distribution

Ogres are found in the wild margins of all three continents, in places that other peoples find too remote, too rugged, or too poor to claim. In [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), they inhabit untamed moorlands, edges of great forests, and foothills of the mountain spine where the terrain is too rough for settled agriculture. In [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), they are present in the cold steppes of the interior and the more desolate valleys of the far north. In [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), ogres are found in the arid margins beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), in rocky wastelands where no caravan route passes.

Ogreish settlements are small and semi-permanent, typically a cluster of rough shelters built from stone, timber, or earth depending on what the locality provides. A community may remain in one place for many years if the hunting is good and the ground is defensible, but ogres are capable of relocating when conditions change, carrying their few possessions with them. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region determines the character of ogreish life, and the ogres of moorlands differ in habit from those of steppes or desert margins, though all share a common resilience and preference for solitude.

## Diet

The ogreish diet is dominated by meat. Ogres are formidable hunters of large game, favouring ambush and brute strength over pursuit and endurance tactics. A single ogre can bring down a large animal through sheer force, and the carcass is consumed in its entirety, as ogreish digestion is robust and wasteful only when food is abundant. Plant foods are gathered opportunistically but form a minor part of the diet, and ogres have no tradition of cultivation.

Ogreish communities supplement hunting with whatever the land offers: fish from mountain streams, birds and their eggs from moorlands, and the occasional domestic animal taken from the fringes of settled territory. This last habit has brought ogres into conflict with [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and other settled peoples, though ogres are more likely to scavenge what has been left unguarded than to raid actively. Ogreish digestion processes spoiled and coarse foods that other peoples would refuse, and their tolerance for poor fare is among the qualities that allow them to persist in lands where little is to be had.

## Ecology

Ogres occupy the niche of a large-bodied predator and opportunist in the wild margins of the world. Their impact on surroundings is significant where they are present, as a single ogre consumes a great deal and a small community can affect the population of large game in its territory. Ogreish hunting tends to focus on the largest available prey, and their presence can alter the balance of species in an area, favouring smaller swifter animals over the large and slow that ogres prefer.

Where ogreish ranges overlap with those of other peoples, the relationship is shaped by competition and mutual wariness. [Orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) and ogres are sometimes found in the same regions, maintaining an uneasy coexistence, competing for the same game but avoiding direct conflict when possible. [Goblins](/content/species/sapient/goblins) occasionally attach themselves to ogreish communities, scavenging from ogreish kills in exchange for minor services, much as they do with orcs. Ogres and [trolls](/content/species/sapient/trolls) rarely encounter one another, as trolls favour higher and more remote ground, but when they do, each gives the other a wide berth.
