## Origins

The origins of the ogres are a matter of speculation, for the ogreish peoples have left no written records and their oral traditions are known to other peoples only through fragmentary and often unflattering accounts. What can be observed is that ogres are a large and powerfully built people, larger than [orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) but smaller than [trolls](/content/species/sapient/trolls), found chiefly in the wild and marginal lands of the world where the presence of other peoples is thin. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between ogres and orcs, citing the broadly similar build, the preference for harsh terrain, and the social organisation of the two peoples. Others have argued for a connection to trolls, pointing to the great size and resilience that both share. Neither view has been demonstrated, and the ogreish peoples themselves have shown no interest in the question.

The archaeological record offers little clarity, for ogreish remains are seldom found in conditions that allow for confident identification. What is known is that ogres have inhabited the wild margins of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands predates the expansion of the settled peoples. Ogres may thus be counted among the older inhabitants of the world, though their history is the least documented of any sapient species.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Ogres are a large and heavily built people, standing taller than [orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) and approaching the height of [trolls](/content/species/sapient/trolls), though they lack the enormous bulk of the latter. Their bones are thick and their musculature is massive, built for brute strength rather than agility or endurance. An ogre can match a troll in sheer force for a brief period, though trolls surpass them in sustained effort. Their movements are heavy and deliberate, and they are incapable of the swift sprints that orcs can manage, relying instead on patient ambush and overwhelming force when they hunt.

Ogreish skin is thick and leathery, ranging from dull yellowish-brown to greyish-green, and it provides a measure of natural protection against injury and the elements. Their hair is coarse, dark, and sparse, worn short or in matted tangles. Ogreish faces are broad and heavy-featured, with prominent brows and large jaws, and their teeth are large and strong, with the lower canines often protruding as blunt tusks. Their eyes are small but keen, adapted to the low light of dawn and dusk, and their hearing and sense of smell are adequate though not remarkable.

One notable feature of ogreish physiology is their remarkable tolerance for hardship. Ogres can endure cold, hunger, and injury to a degree that would fell most other peoples, and they recover from wounds that would be mortal to a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans). This resilience is the defining characteristic of the ogreish body, for it allows them to survive in conditions that would defeat any people less able to absorb punishment.

## Life Cycle

Ogres are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of eighty to one hundred and twenty years under favourable conditions, though many do not reach old age owing to the harshness of their lives. They mature faster than trolls but slower than humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their early twenties. Ogreish women carry their young for approximately ten months before giving birth, and births are usually single, though twins are not uncommon. Infant mortality is moderate by the standards of the wild peoples, lower than among orcs or [goblins](/content/species/sapient/goblins) owing to the fierce protectiveness of ogreish parents, but higher than among the settled peoples.

Ogreish populations grow slowly, for births are infrequent and the conditions in which ogres live are demanding. The slow increase is balanced by the longevity and resilience of adults, and ogreish communities tend to remain stable over long periods, their numbers neither growing rapidly nor declining sharply. The elders of an ogreish community are those who have survived the longest, and their authority rests upon proven strength and the accumulated knowledge of a hard life.

## Habitat and Distribution

Ogres are found in the wild margins of all three continents, in places that other peoples find too remote, too rugged, or too poor to claim. In [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), they inhabit the untamed moorlands, the edges of the great forests, and the foothills of the mountain spine where the terrain is too rough for settled agriculture. In [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), they are present in the cold steppes of the interior and the more desolate valleys of the far north, where the land offers little to any people. In [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), ogres are found in the arid margins beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), in the rocky wastelands where no caravan route passes.

Ogreish settlements are small and semi-permanent, typically a cluster of rough shelters built from stone, timber, or earth, depending on what the locality provides. A community may remain in one place for many years if the hunting is good and the ground is defensible, but ogres are capable of relocating when conditions change, and they carry their few possessions with them when they move. The [geography](/content//geography) of each region determines the character of ogreish life, and the ogres of the moorlands differ in habit from those of the steppes or the desert margins, though all share a common resilience and a preference for solitude.

## Diet

The ogreish diet is dominated by meat, and ogres are formidable hunters of large game. They favour ambush and brute strength over the pursuit and endurance tactics of orcs, and a single ogre can bring down a large animal through sheer force. The carcass is consumed in its entirety, for ogreish digestion is robust and wasteful only when food is abundant. Plant foods are gathered opportunistically but form a minor part of the diet, and ogres have no tradition of cultivation.

Ogreish communities supplement their hunting with whatever the land offers, including fish from mountain streams, birds and their eggs from the moorlands, and the occasional domestic animal taken from the fringes of settled territory. This last habit has brought ogres into conflict with [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and other settled peoples, though ogres are more likely to scavenge what has been left unguarded than to raid actively. Ogreish digestion can process spoiled and coarse foods that other peoples would refuse, and their tolerance for poor fare is among the qualities that allow them to persist in lands where little is to be had.

## Ecology

Ogres occupy the niche of a large-bodied predator and opportunist in the wild margins of the world. Their impact on their surroundings is significant where they are present, for a single ogre consumes a great deal, and a small community can affect the population of large game in its territory. Ogreish hunting tends to focus on the largest available prey, and their presence can alter the balance of species in an area, favouring smaller and swifter animals over the large and slow that ogres prefer.

Where ogreish ranges overlap with those of other peoples, the relationship is shaped by competition and mutual wariness. [Orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) and ogres are sometimes found in the same regions, and the two peoples maintain an uneasy coexistence, competing for the same game but avoiding direct conflict when possible. [Goblins](/content/species/sapient/goblins) occasionally attach themselves to ogreish communities, scavenging from ogreish kills in exchange for minor services, much as they do with orcs. Ogres and [trolls](/content/species/sapient/trolls) rarely encounter one another, for trolls favour higher and more remote ground, but when they do, each gives the other a wide berth.
