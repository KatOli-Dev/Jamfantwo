## Origins

The origins of the corvids are a subject of particular interest to naturalists, for the corvid body combines a humanoid form with the wings, the beak, and the covering of a large crow, and the corvid mind is among the sharpest of any sapient people. Corvids are found in the high forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the upper woodlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and their bodies are adapted to a life that moves between the forest floor and the high canopy. Some naturalists have proposed a distant kinship between corvids and [harpies](/content/species/sapient/harpies), citing the capacity for flight shared by both peoples and the partly avian form, but the corvids are far smaller, more terrestrial, and their intelligence is of a different order. Others have argued for a connection to [lepidians](/content/species/sapient/lepidians), pointing to the shared forest habitat and the capacity for flight, but the differences in form, life cycle, and habit are too great for the comparison to be sustained. The corvids themselves preserve oral traditions that are conveyed through complex vocalisations and gestures, and they have shown a greater willingness than most peoples to share these traditions with outsiders, though their accounts are often deliberately misleading.

What is certain is that corvids have inhabited the high forests of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these woodlands is attested in the oldest elven records. The corvids and the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) have coexisted in the forests for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the overlap in their range and the difference in their habits.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Corvids are a small to medium people, standing shorter than [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) but taller than [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes), with a light and angular frame that is built for both flight and terrestrial movement. Their bones are hollow and thin-walled, reducing weight for flight while retaining structural strength. Two large wings extend from the back, covered in feathers that range from glossy black to dark grey, with occasional individuals displaying patches of white or a deep iridescent sheen. The wings are broad and capable, granting sustained flight over long distances, and the corvid is also capable of short, precise flights within the forest canopy, navigating between branches and trunks with a precision that is remarkable for a creature of its size.

The corvid head is humanoid in general outline, but it bears the beak, the eyes, and the feathered crown of a large crow. The beak is strong and sharp, used for the manipulation of objects, the preparation of food, and defence, and the corvid hands, at the wingtips, are small but capable, with long, fine fingers that are used for the precise work that the corvids are known for. The corvid body is covered in feathers from the head to the shoulders, with the rest of the body covered in a fine, dark down that provides insulation and water resistance. Their legs are strong and scaled, and the feet are capable of gripping branches and perches with a sureness that suits a people who spend much of their time above the ground.

Corvid senses are sharp and well balanced. Their vision is the keenest of any people, for their large, forward-facing eyes are capable of extraordinary resolution and colour perception, and they can detect details at distances that would leave a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) or an [elf](/content/species/sapient/elves) squinting. Their hearing is acute, and they are capable of distinguishing between a great variety of sounds, a gift that serves them in the complex vocal communication that is characteristic of corvid society. Their sense of smell is moderate, less developed than that of the canine or ursine peoples, but adequate for their needs. The corvid mind is their most remarkable feature, for the corvids are possessed of an intelligence and a capacity for problem-solving that is unmatched by any other people of comparable size, and their ability to use tools, to plan, and to remember is well documented by the naturalists who have studied them.

## Life Cycle

Corvids are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of fifty to seventy years under favourable conditions. They mature quickly, reaching full stature and the capacity for flight within four or five years of hatching. Corvid women lay clutches of two to four eggs in nests built high in the canopy of the forest, and the eggs are incubated for approximately six weeks before hatching. The hatchlings are small and helpless, and they require several months of constant care before they are capable of flight, during which time the mother and the community share the burden of feeding and protection.

Corvid populations grow steadily when the forest is healthy and the food supply is reliable, and the young are raised communally, with all adults sharing in their care. The education of a corvid is long and demanding, for the corvid mind requires constant stimulation, and the young are taught not only the practical skills of flight, foraging, and tool use but also the complex system of vocalisations and gestures that constitutes the corvid language. The elders of a corvid community are the repositories of the community's accumulated knowledge, and their memories of the forest, its resources, and its history are the most valuable resource the community possesses.

## Habitat and Distribution

Corvids are found chiefly in the high forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the trees are tall and the canopy is dense, and the opportunities for flight and foraging are abundant. They favour the mature woodland with a mix of large trees and a diverse understorey, where the nesting sites are plentiful and the food supply is varied. Corvid settlements are built in the upper canopy, suspended among the strongest branches and the forks of the largest trees, and they are organised in clusters that reflect the communal nature of corvid society.

Smaller populations of corvids are found in the upper woodlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), where the coniferous forests of the lower slopes provide a suitable if harsher environment, and in the wooded foothills of the [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) northern coast. Corvids are absent from the open plains, the mountains above the treeline, and the deep caves, for their bodies require the forest and the air. The [geography](/content//geography) of each region determines the distribution of corvid populations, and the corvid range is restricted to the forested regions where the conditions suit them.

## Diet

The corvid diet is the most varied of any flying people, and corvids are opportunistic omnivores who make use of whatever the forest provides. Fruits, nuts, seeds, and insects form the staple, gathered from the trees and the understorey by foraging parties that range across the canopy and the forest floor. Eggs and the young of other birds are taken when they are found, and the corvids are known for their willingness to eat almost anything that is edible, a flexibility that serves them well in the seasonal environment of the temperate forest.

Meat from larger prey is a minor part of the diet, taken from the hunting of small mammals and the scavenging of carcasses that are found in the forest. The corvids are capable of co-operative hunting, and a group of corvids can overwhelm prey that would be too large for any individual, but they prefer the easier and less dangerous foods that foraging provides. The corvid approach to food is practical and opportunistic, and they are known to cache surplus food in hidden locations throughout their territory, returning to these stores when food is scarce. Their memory for the location of these caches is extraordinary, and a corvid can recall the position of hundreds of individual caches months after they were made.

## Ecology

Corvids occupy the niche of a small-bodied, intelligent, flying omnivore in the forest ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on the ecology of the forest is modest in scale but complex in effect, for the corvids influence the distribution of seeds through their caching, the populations of insects through their foraging, and the behaviour of other birds through their predation on eggs and young. The corvids are an important component of the forest ecosystem, and their presence is generally associated with a healthy and diverse woodland.

Corvids share the high forests with [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves), [lepidians](/content/species/sapient/lepidians), and [cervines](/content/species/sapient/cervines), and the four peoples coexist in a relationship that is shaped by the overlap in their range and the difference in their habits. The elves favour the deeper woodland, the lepidians the night, the cervines the forest floor, and the corvids the high canopy, and each people occupies a different niche within the same forest. Where corvids encounter [ursines](/content/species/sapient/ursines), the relationship is one of mutual caution, for the corvids are aware of the ursine's great strength and the ursines are aware of the corvid's sharp eyes and quicker wits.
