## Origins

The origins of the cancrids are bound up with the rocky intertidal coasts of the world, for the cancrid body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by the meeting of land and sea, by the tides, and by the hard surfaces of the shore. Cancrids are a crustacean people, found on the rocky intertidal coasts of all three continents, and their bodies combine a humanoid upper form with the lower body and shell of a large crustacean. Some naturalists have proposed a distant kinship between cancrids and [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa), citing the amphibious nature shared by both peoples and their preference for the coastal margin, but the cancrids are more thoroughly adapted to the salt water and the hard shore than the fresh-water kappa. Others have argued for a connection to [arachne](/content/species/sapient/arachne), pointing to the segmented form and the hard exoskeleton shared by both, but the differences in habitat and habit are too great for the comparison to be sustained. The cancrids themselves preserve oral traditions that are conveyed through gesture and the drumming of their claws upon the rock, and these traditions are unintelligible to other peoples.

What is certain is that cancrids have inhabited the rocky intertidal coasts of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence on these shores is attested in the oldest maritime records. The cancrids and the [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) share the coastal waters, and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the overlap in their range and the difference in their preferred depth.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Cancrids are a medium-sized people, standing comparable in height to [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), with a broad, heavily built frame that is protected by a thick, hard shell of chitin. The upper body is humanoid in outline, with a head, shoulders, and arms, and the arms end in powerful claws that are used for gripping, cutting, and defence. The lower body is that of a large crustacean, with a broad, flat abdomen and six sturdy legs that are jointed and covered in the same hard shell as the rest of the body. The whole form is low and wide, and the cancrid stands closer to the ground than any other people of comparable size, for the body is built for stability rather than height.

The cancrid shell is the most remarkable feature of the cancrid body, for it is extraordinarily hard and tough, providing protection that is unmatched by the skin or hide of any other people. The shell ranges in colour from dark red to orange, brown, and mottled green, and it is shed and regrown periodically as the cancrid grows, a process that leaves the cancrid vulnerable for a short period while the new shell hardens. The claws are the largest and strongest of the cancrid's natural weapons, and a cancrid can exert enough force to crush stone or sever a rope with a single pinch.

Cancrid senses are adapted to the intertidal environment. Their eyes are set on short stalks that can be raised above the shell to survey the surroundings, and their vision is keen in both air and water, adapted to the variable light of the tidal zone where the surface of the sea rises and falls with the tide. Their sense of touch is highly developed, particularly in the claws, and the cancrid can detect the texture, weight, and shape of an object through the claws alone. Their hearing is rudimentary, for sound behaves differently in water and air and the cancrid straddles both, and they rely more upon vibration, detected through the legs and the shell, for awareness of their surroundings.

## Life Cycle

Cancrids are long-lived, with a typical lifespan of one hundred to one hundred and fifty years under favourable conditions, for the hard shell and the stable environment of the intertidal zone provide effective protection against the threats that shorten the lives of other peoples. They mature slowly, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late twenties, and they continue to grow throughout their lives, though at a decreasing rate. Cancrid women carry their eggs internally before releasing them into the water in clutches of several hundred, and the eggs are left to develop in the intertidal pools where they are sheltered by the rocks and the seaweed.

The eggs hatch into larvae that are wholly different in form from the adult, small, transparent, and planktonic, drifting in the coastal waters for several weeks before settling onto the rocks and developing the adult form. The mortality rate among the larvae is extremely high, for they are vulnerable to predation by the fish and the filter-feeders of the coastal waters, but the number of eggs ensures that enough survive to sustain the population. The cancrids who reach adulthood are extraordinarily resilient, for the hard shell and the stable environment provide effective protection, and the adult population tends to remain stable over long periods.

## Habitat and Distribution

Cancrids are found on the rocky intertidal coasts of all three continents, wherever the shore is hard and the tides are strong, and the rocks provide the surfaces and the shelter that their way of life requires. They favour the lower intertidal zone, where the rocks are covered and uncovered by the tide twice each day, and the pools and crevices of the shore provide food and shelter. Their settlements are typically built in the crevices and the caves of the rocky shore, combining natural shelter with the construction of walls and barriers from the stones of the beach.

In [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), cancrids are found on the rocky coasts of the western shore, where the cliffs of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) provide an ideal habitat. In [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), they inhabit the rugged coasts of the southern shore, where the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) pounds the rocks and the tides are strong. In [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), cancrids are found on the rocky stretches of the northern coast, where the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) meets the hard stone of the shoreline. The [geography](/content/geography) of each coast determines the distribution of cancrid populations, and they are absent from the sandy shores, the mudflats, and the open sea, for their bodies require hard surfaces and the tidal zone.

## Diet

The cancrid diet is dominated by the organisms of the intertidal zone, and cancrids are opportunistic foragers who make use of the abundant and varied food that the rocky shore provides. The staple is the shellfish, barnacles, and small crustaceans that encrust the rocks of the intertidal zone, and the cancrid claws are well suited to the breaking of shells and the prising of attached organisms from the hard surfaces. Algae and seaweed are also consumed, scraped from the rocks with the smaller claws, and the cancrid diet is a mixture of animal and plant foods that reflects the richness of the intertidal ecosystem.

The cancrids are methodical and patient foragers, working systematically across the rocks as the tide retreats and taking what the shore provides. They consume their food raw, for fire is impractical on the wet rocks of the intertidal zone, and their digestion is robust and well adapted to a diet of raw shellfish and algae. The cancrid approach to food is entirely that of the tidal forager, governed by the rhythm of the tide and the availability of the intertidal resources, and a cancrid eats when the tide is out and rests when the tide is in.

## Ecology

Cancrids occupy the niche of a medium-bodied crustacean forager in the intertidal ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on the ecology of the rocky shore is considerable, for the cancrids are among the primary consumers of the attached organisms that encrust the rocks, and their foraging regulates the populations of the barnacles, mussels, and algae that compete for space on the hard surfaces. The cancrid presence on a shore is generally associated with a diverse and well-maintained intertidal community, and the cancrids are an important component of the coastal ecosystem.

Cancrids share the intertidal zone with [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) in portions of their range, and the two peoples maintain a cautious but peaceful coexistence, for their needs are different and their habitats overlap only at the margin. The merfolk favour the deeper water and the open coast, while the cancrids prefer the rocks and the tidal zone, and the two peoples encounter one another only at the edges of their respective ranges.
