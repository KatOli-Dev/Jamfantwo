## Crustacean People of the Intertidal Shore

Cancrids are a shelled people found on the rocky intertidal coasts of all three continents. Their bodies combine a humanoid upper form with the lower body and shell of a large crab or lobster, shaped by the meeting of land and sea, by the tides, and by the hard surfaces of the shore. Naturalists have proposed a distant kinship with [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa) on the basis of shared amphibious nature and preference for the coastal margin, though cancrids are more thoroughly adapted to salt water and the hard shore than the freshwater kappa. A connection to [arachne](/content/species/sapient/arachne) has also been suggested, citing the segmented form and hard outer shell shared by both, but differences in habitat and habit are too great for the comparison to be sustained. Cancrid oral traditions are conveyed through gesture and the drumming of claws upon rock, and they are unintelligible to other peoples.

The cancrids have inhabited the rocky intertidal coasts of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence on these shores is attested in the oldest maritime records. They share the coastal waters with [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk), and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the overlap in their range and the difference in their preferred depth.

## Hard Shell and Powerful Claws

Cancrids are a medium-sized people, standing comparable in height to [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), with a broad, heavily built frame protected by a thick, hard shell. The upper body is humanoid in outline, with a head, shoulders, and arms ending in powerful claws used for gripping, cutting, and defence. The lower body is that of a large hard-shelled shore creature, with a broad, flat abdomen and six sturdy legs covered in the same hard shell. The whole form is low and wide, built for stability rather than height, and the cancrid stands closer to the ground than any other people of comparable size.

The cancrid shell is the most remarkable feature of the cancrid body, extraordinarily hard and tough, providing protection unmatched by the skin or hide of any other people. The shell ranges in colour from dark red to orange, brown, and mottled green, and it is shed and regrown periodically as the cancrid grows, a process that leaves the cancrid vulnerable for a short period while the new shell hardens. The claws are the largest and strongest of the cancrid's natural weapons, capable of exerting enough force to crush stone or sever a rope with a single pinch.

Cancrid senses are adapted to the intertidal environment. Their eyes are set on short stalks that can be raised above the shell to survey the surroundings, and their vision is keen in both air and water, adapted to the variable light of the tidal zone. Their sense of touch is highly developed, particularly in the claws, allowing the cancrid to detect the texture, weight, and shape of an object through the claws alone. Their hearing is rudimentary, as sound behaves differently in water and air and the cancrid straddles both, and they rely more upon vibration, detected through the legs and the shell, for awareness of their surroundings.

## Larvae and the Tidal Rhythm

Cancrids are long-lived, with a typical lifespan of one hundred to one hundred and fifty years under favourable conditions. The hard shell and stable environment of the intertidal zone provide effective protection against the threats that shorten the lives of other peoples. They mature slowly, reaching full stature by their late twenties, and they continue to grow throughout their lives, though at a decreasing rate. Cancrid women carry their eggs internally before releasing them into the water in clutches of several hundred, and the eggs develop in intertidal pools sheltered by rocks and seaweed.

The eggs hatch into larvae wholly different in form from the adult, small, transparent, and planktonic, drifting in coastal waters for several weeks before settling onto rocks and developing the adult form. The mortality rate among the larvae is extremely high, as they are vulnerable to predation by fish and filter-feeders of coastal waters, but the number of eggs ensures that enough survive to sustain the population. Cancrids who reach adulthood are extraordinarily resilient, and the adult population tends to remain stable over long periods.

## Rocky Shores of All Three Continents

Cancrids are found on the rocky intertidal coasts of all three continents, wherever the shore is hard and the tides are strong. They favour the lower intertidal zone, where rocks are covered and uncovered by the tide twice each day, and the pools and crevices of the shore provide food and shelter. Their settlements are built in the crevices and caves of the rocky shore, combining natural shelter with walls and barriers constructed from beach stones.

In [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), cancrids are found on the rocky coasts of the western shore, where the cliffs of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) provide an ideal habitat. In [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), they inhabit the rugged coasts of the southern shore, where the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) pounds the rocks. In [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), cancrids are found on the rocky stretches of the northern coast, where the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) meets the hard stone of the shoreline. The geography of each coast determines the distribution of cancrid populations, and they are absent from sandy shores, mudflats, and the open sea, as their bodies require hard surfaces and the tidal zone.

## Shellfish and Algae of the Tidal Zone

The cancrid diet is dominated by the creatures of the intertidal zone. Cancrids are opportunistic foragers who make use of the abundant and varied food the rocky shore provides. The staple includes shellfish, barnacles, and small hard-shelled creatures that encrust the rocks of the intertidal zone, and the cancrid claws are well suited to breaking shells and prising attached living things from hard surfaces. Algae and seaweed are also consumed, scraped from rocks with the smaller claws, and the cancrid diet is a mixture of animal and plant foods reflecting the richness of the intertidal ecosystem.

Cancrids are methodical and patient foragers, working systematically across the rocks as the tide retreats and taking what the shore provides. They consume their food raw, as fire is impractical on the wet rocks of the intertidal zone, and their digestion is robust and well adapted to a diet of raw shellfish and algae. The cancrid approach to food is governed entirely by the rhythm of the tide and the availability of intertidal resources: a cancrid eats when the tide is out and rests when the tide is in.

## Foragers of the Intertidal Ecosystem

Cancrids occupy the niche of a medium-bodied shelled forager in intertidal ecosystems. Their impact on the ecology of the rocky shore is considerable, as they are among the primary consumers of the attached living things that encrust the rocks, regulating the populations of barnacles, mussels, and algae that compete for space on hard surfaces. The cancrid presence on a shore is generally associated with a diverse and well-maintained intertidal community, and they are an important component of the coastal ecosystem.

Cancrids share the intertidal zone with [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) in portions of their range, maintaining a cautious but peaceful coexistence grounded in different needs and habitats that overlap only at the margin. The merfolk favour deeper water and the open coast, while the cancrids prefer the rocks and the tidal zone, and the two peoples encounter one another only at the edges of their respective ranges.
