## Origins

The origins of the ursines are bound up with the northern forests of the world, for the ursine body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by cold, by dense woodland, and by the demands of a large, powerful frame in a seasonal environment. Ursines are a bear-like people, found in the northern forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the coniferous woodlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and their form combines a humanoid body with the fur, the head, and the physical power of a large bear. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between ursines and [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines), citing the northern habitat, the cold-weather adaptation, and the partly animal form shared by both peoples, though the ursines are far larger and more broadly built than the lean, swift lupines. Others have argued for a connection to [minotaurs](/content/species/sapient/minotaurs), pointing to the great size and strength shared by both peoples, but the differences in form and habitat are too great for the comparison to be sustained. The ursines themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their having emerged from the roots of the oldest pines, and they regard the northern forests as their rightful home.

What is certain is that ursines have inhabited the northern forests of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these woodlands is attested in the oldest elven and human records. The ursines and the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) have coexisted in the northern forests for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the overlap in their range and the difference in their size and habits.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Ursines are a large and powerfully built people, standing taller than [orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) and comparable in height to [ogres](/content/species/sapient/ogres), with a massive and broadly built frame. Their bones are thick and heavy, and their musculature is enormous, built for raw strength and sustained effort rather than speed or agility. An ursine can match an ogre in brute force, and the ursine body is more heavily insulated and more resistant to cold than that of any other people save the [giants](/content/species/sapient/giants). Their movements are heavy and deliberate, but they are capable of sudden, explosive bursts of speed that are startling in a creature of their size.

The ursine head is that of a bear, with a broad muzzle, a large nose, and small, sharp eyes. The jaw is powerful and the teeth are large and strong, with prominent canines that are used for tearing food and for defence. The body is humanoid from the neck down, but it is covered in a thick coat of fur that ranges from dark brown to black, with occasional individuals displaying a reddish or a pale pelage. The fur is dense and water-resistant, providing excellent insulation against the cold and the wet of the northern forest, and it is renewed each spring in a thorough moult that leaves the ursine temporarily shabby but soon restored to full coat.

Ursine hands are large and strong, with thick, calloused fingers and heavy claws that are used for digging, climbing, and fighting. The claws are non-retractable, unlike those of the [felinids](/content/species/sapient/felinids), and they are blunt and strong rather than sharp and fine. Ursine feet are similarly large and broad, with thick soles that are well suited to the soft ground and the tangled undergrowth of the forest, and the ursine is a capable climber, able to ascend the largest trees with a slow and deliberate confidence.

The ursine sense of smell is the keenest of any sapient people, surpassing even that of the [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines), and the ursine can detect food, other creatures, and changes in the weather by scent alone at distances that seem extraordinary. Their vision is adequate but not exceptional, for the ursine relies more upon smell than sight, and their eyes are small and their colour vision is limited. Their hearing is competent, and their sense of touch is moderate, for the thick fur and the calloused skin reduce the sensitivity of the extremities.

## Life Cycle

Ursines are long-lived, with a typical lifespan of one hundred to one hundred and fifty years under favourable conditions. They mature slowly, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late twenties. Ursine women carry their young for approximately eight months before giving birth, and births are usually single, though twins are not uncommon. The infant is small relative to the size of the adult, and it is helpless at birth, requiring many months of constant care before it is capable of walking. The young grow slowly, not reaching their full size until their second decade, and they remain with the mother for many years before becoming independent.

Ursine populations grow slowly, for births are infrequent and the young require long periods of care, but the longevity and the resilience of the adults ensure that the population remains stable over long periods. The ursine community is small and widely dispersed, for the northern forest can support only a limited number of such large individuals, and the territories of ursine families may span many square miles of woodland. The bonds between mother and young are the strongest in ursine life, and they persist for many years after the young has reached independence, maintained through shared territory and occasional contact.

## Habitat and Distribution

Ursines are found chiefly in the northern forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the trees are large and the winters are long and the undergrowth is thick. They favour the mature woodland with a mix of coniferous and deciduous trees, where the canopy is dense and the ground is soft, and the streams and rivers provide water and the fishing that supplements their diet. Their settlements are typically small and solitary, established in sheltered locations within the forest, and an ursine family may range across a wide territory rather than remaining in a single location.

In [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), ursines are found in the coniferous forests of the lower slopes, where the trees are hardy and the cold is intense, and the ursines of Morvind are the most heavily built and the most cold-tolerant of their kind. Smaller populations are reported in the wooded highlands of the mountain spine, where the forests are thinner but the terrain is remote. The [geography](/content//geography) of each region determines the distribution of ursine populations, and they are absent from the open plains, the mountains above the treeline, and the warm regions, for their bodies are adapted to the cold and the forest.

## Diet

The ursine diet is the most varied of any of the larger peoples, and ursines are opportunistic omnivores who make use of a wide range of foods that the northern forest provides. Fish is the staple, caught from the streams and rivers of the forest by a patient and powerful technique that relies on the ursine's great strength and its sharp eyes for the movement of fish in the water, and the salmon runs of the northern rivers are the most important seasonal event in the ursine year. Berries, nuts, roots, and honey are gathered from the forest floor and the understorey, and the ursine sense of smell is well suited to the finding of these foods.

Meat from land animals is a secondary part of the diet, taken from the hunting of the large mammals of the forest, including elk, boar, and the smaller game that shares the ursine habitat. Ursines are capable predators when the opportunity arises, but they are not dedicated hunters, and they prefer the patient fishing and the leisurely foraging that suit the deliberate pace of ursine life. Their digestion is robust and well adapted to a mixed diet, and the ursine metabolism is capable of building the large reserves of fat that sustain the ursine through the lean months of winter, when food is scarce and the forest is deep in snow.

## Ecology

Ursines occupy the niche of a large-bodied omnivore in the northern forest ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is considerable where they are present, for the ursine consumes a great deal and alters the vegetation and the wildlife of its territory through foraging, fishing, and the occasional hunting of large game. The ursine presence in a forest is generally associated with a healthy and diverse ecosystem, for the ursines take from the forest what they need and the forest sustains them in return, and the balance between the two has been maintained over many centuries.

Ursines share the northern forests with [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) and [corvids](/content/species/sapient/corvids), and the three peoples coexist in a relationship that is generally peaceful, for their needs are different and their habitats overlap only at the margins. The elves favour the deeper woodland, the corvids the high canopy, and the ursines the forest floor and the streams, and each people occupies a different niche within the same woodland. Where ursines encounter [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), the relationship is wary but not hostile, for the ursines are too large and too powerful to be threatened by human hunters, and the humans are too few in the deep forest to compete with the ursines for food.
