## Bound to the Rivers and Lakes

The origins of the kappa are bound up with the rivers and lakes of the interior, for the kappa body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by fresh water and the damp margins of the land. Kappa are an amphibious people found in the rivers, lakes, and wetlands of the interior of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and their bodies are adapted to a life that moves freely between water and land. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between kappa and [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) citing the aquatic adaptation of both peoples, but the differences are considerable: kappa are creatures of fresh water rather than salt, and their bodies retain a far greater capacity for terrestrial life. Kappa have inhabited the rivers and lakes of the Aethelaran interior for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these waters is attested in the oldest human and elven records. The kappa and the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the river valleys have coexisted for centuries in one of the oldest and most interdependent interspecies relationships in the known world.

## Shell, Dish-Crown, and Webbed Limbs

Kappa are a small to medium people, standing shorter than [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) but taller than [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes), with a compact sturdy frame suited to both swimming and walking. Their skin is smooth and moist, ranging from greenish to olive and brown, thinner and more permeable than that of land-dwelling peoples, requiring regular immersion in water to remain healthy. Their limbs are proportioned for a life that alternates between water and land, with webbed fingers and toes that aid in swimming and strong short legs capable of sustained walking.

The most distinctive feature of the kappa is the shell, a tough domed carapace covering the back and shoulders that provides protection and structural support. The shell is a natural part of the kappa body, grown from the same tissues as the scales and skin, and ranges in colour from dark green to brown and grey. It is strong enough to turn aside a blow that would injure an unarmoured person, contributing to the kappa reputation for resilience. The head is broad and flat, with a dish-like depression on the crown that holds a pool of water; if the water empties, the kappa grows weak and disoriented. This singular feature is the most remarkable aspect of kappa physiology and its function is not fully understood by naturalists.

Kappa senses are well adapted to both aquatic and terrestrial environments. Vision is keen both above and below the water, and their large forward-set eyes grant good depth perception. Hearing is competent in air and excellent in water, where sound travels more efficiently, and they communicate over considerable distances through submerged vocalisations. Their sense of smell is acute, particularly in water, and kappa detect the chemical signatures of different water sources, a gift that serves them in navigation and the assessment of water quality.

## Eggs in Muddy Banks and Watery Youth

Kappa live sixty to ninety years under favourable conditions, maturing at a pace comparable to humans and reaching full stature by their late teens. Kappa women carry their young for approximately seven months before laying a clutch of three to six eggs in a shallow nest dug into the muddy bank of a river or lake. The eggs incubate for a further two months before hatching, and the hatchlings are small and aquatic, spending their first years entirely in the water before developing the legs and lungs that allow them to venture onto land.

Kappa populations grow steadily when the rivers and lakes are clean and water is plentiful. The young are raised communally, with adults sharing in their care and protection. Education is long and practical, encompassing swimming, fishing, knowledge of rivers and currents, and the traditions of the people. A kappa is not considered fully adult until they have mastered all of these; the rites of passage include a journey from the headwaters to the mouth of their river, a test of endurance and knowledge that every kappa must complete.

## Freshwater Systems of Aethelar

Kappa are found chiefly in the rivers and lakes of the interior of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the water is fresh and the land is well watered. They favour the slower reaches of great rivers, the still waters of lakes, and the marshes and wetlands where water spreads wide and shallow. Their settlements are typically built on the banks, combining structures above and below the waterline. The [geography](/content/geography) of each river system shapes the character of kappa communities, and the kappa of mountain streams differ in habit from those of lowland marshes.

Smaller populations are found in the rivers and lakes of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) coast, where the water is cold but clean, and in the river systems of the [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) interior, where seasonal rivers provide a temporary habitat shared with the nomadic peoples of the desert. Kappa are absent from the sea, as their bodies are adapted to fresh water and they find salt water harmful, and they do not occur in arid regions where permanent water is scarce.

## Fish, Crustaceans, and Watery Cultivation

The kappa diet mixes aquatic and terrestrial foods, reflecting their amphibious nature. Fish, shelled creatures, and aquatic plants form the staple, gathered from rivers and lakes by techniques including nets, traps, and the patient art of hand-fishing for which kappa are noted. Cucumbers and other watery vegetables are a particular favourite, cultivated in gardens along riverbanks, and the kappa reputation for fine cucumber cultivation is recognised throughout the regions where they are known.

Meat is eaten in moderation, chiefly fish and waterfowl, and meals are prepared and shared by the community. Kappa are skilled in preserving food through drying and salting, storing provisions for winter months when rivers freeze and fishing is poor. Kappa do not brew strong drink, as their constitution is sensitive to alcohol, but they are known for a light fragrant tea made from water herbs, consumed in quantity and offered to guests as a gesture of hospitality.

## Stewards of Freshwater Health

Kappa occupy the niche of an amphibious forager and predator in freshwater ecosystems. Their impact on their surroundings is considerable, as their management of rivers and banks sustains both aquatic and terrestrial life. Kappa are careful stewards of water, maintaining quality and flow through management of vegetation, clearing of obstructions, and prevention of pollution; their presence in a river system is generally associated with clean water and abundant life.

Kappa share their habitats with [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) across much of their range, and the two peoples coexist closely, as their needs are complementary. Humans tend the land and towns while kappa tend the water and rivers, and each benefits from the produce and skills of the other. [Merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) and kappa occasionally encounter one another where rivers meet the sea, maintaining a cautious but respectful distance, each recognising the other as a people of the water.
