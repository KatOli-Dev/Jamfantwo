## Origins

The origins of the noctules are bound up with the deep caves of the world, for the noctule body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by darkness, silence, and the air of the subterranean void. Noctules are a bat-like people, found in the deep caves of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the larger cave systems of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and their bodies are adapted to a life of flight in total darkness. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between noctules and [harpies](/content/species/sapient/harpies), citing the capacity for flight shared by both peoples and the occupation of elevated, inaccessible habitats, but the differences are too great for the comparison to be sustained, for the noctules are far smaller, nocturnal, and adapted to the underground rather than the open air. Others have argued for a connection to [kryn](/content/species/sapient/kryn), pointing to the shared subterranean habitat and the use of non-visual senses, but the noctule body is too distinct in form for the comparison to be sustained. The noctules themselves preserve oral traditions that are conveyed through high-frequency vocalisation rather than speech, and these traditions are unintelligible to other peoples.

What is certain is that noctules have inhabited the deep caves of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lightless places is attested in the oldest subterranean records. The noctules and the [arachne](/content/species/sapient/arachne) share the deep underground, and the two peoples have coexisted for centuries, each occupying a different niche in the subterranean world.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Noctules are a small people, standing no taller than half the height of an average [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a light and angular frame that is built for flight. Their bones are hollow and thin-walled, reducing weight while retaining structural strength, and their musculature is concentrated in the chest and shoulders, where the great flight muscles are anchored. The arms of a noctule are also its wings, and the two functions are combined in a single limb, with a thin, tough membrane stretching from the elongated fingers to the sides of the body and the legs. The wings are large relative to the body, granting manoeuvrability in the confined spaces of the caves, and the noctule is capable of flight that is both swift and precise, navigating the narrowest tunnels and the largest caverns with equal facility.

The noctule head is small and angular, with a short muzzle, large ears, and small eyes that are functionally useless in the total darkness of the deep caves. Their vision is rudimentary, capable of distinguishing only light from dark, and the noctules rely upon a far more remarkable sense: echolocation. The noctule produces high-frequency vocalisations, far above the range of human hearing, and the echoes that return from the walls, the ceiling, and the objects of the cave environment are processed by the large, sensitive ears to construct a detailed auditory image of the surroundings. This sense is extraordinarily precise, allowing the noctule to detect objects as fine as a strand of silk in total darkness, and it is the primary means by which the noctules navigate, hunt, and communicate.

Noctule skin is thin and dark, ranging from deep brown to near-black, and it is covered in a short, fine fur that provides insulation in the cold air of the deep caves. Their hands, at the wingtips, are small but capable, with long, slender fingers that are used for climbing, gripping, and the manipulation of small objects. The noctule body is built for the air, and a noctule grounded by injury is severely handicapped, for the legs are short and weak and the body is poorly suited to walking or crawling.

## Life Cycle

Noctules are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of forty to sixty years under favourable conditions. They mature quickly, reaching full stature and the capacity for flight within five or six years of birth. Noctule women carry their young for approximately four months before giving birth to a single infant, though twins are not uncommon. The infant is small and helpless, clinging to the mother's fur for the first weeks of life, and it is carried in flight until it is old enough to fly on its own, a milestone that is typically reached within two years.

Noctule populations grow slowly, for births are infrequent and the conditions of the deep caves are demanding, and the mortality rate among the young is moderate, for the underground environment is unforgiving of error. The noctule community is closely knit, and the individuals of a colony roost together in a shared shelter, hanging from the ceiling of a cavern in a cluster that provides warmth and protection. The elders of a noctule colony are those who have maintained their flight and their echolocation into later life, and their knowledge of the cave system and its passages is the foundation of the colony's survival.

## Habitat and Distribution

Noctules are found chiefly in the deep caves of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), where the vast underground networks of the interior ranges provide the space and the darkness that their way of life requires. They are the primary flying people of the subterranean world, and their colonies are found throughout the larger cave systems, from the middle levels to the deepest reaches that are accessible by air. Smaller populations are found in the cave systems of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the limestone formations provide the kind of enclosed, dark spaces that the noctules prefer.

Noctules are entirely absent from the surface world, for their eyes are useless in bright light and their bodies are adapted to the stable temperature and the still air of the underground. Exposure to sunlight is painful and disorienting, and the noctules venture to the surface only in the darkest hours of the night, when the sky is overcast and the moon is new. The [geography](/content/geography) of the underground determines the distribution of noctule colonies, and each colony is situated in a location that provides access to flying space, roosting sites, and the food sources of the cave system.

## Diet

The noctule diet is dominated by the insects and small creatures of the deep caves, and noctules are aerial hunters of extraordinary precision. They capture their prey in flight, using echolocation to detect and track the small, flying insects that share the subterranean air, and their speed and agility in the confined spaces of the caves allow them to pursue and catch prey that no ground-dwelling predator could reach. The hunt is conducted in the dark, guided entirely by sound, and the noctule's echolocation is precise enough to distinguish between edible and inedible insects by the shape and density of their echo.

Larger prey is taken when it is available, including the small birds and mammals that occasionally venture into the cave systems from the surface, and the noctules are capable of overwhelming creatures larger than themselves through co-ordinated hunting in which several individuals work together to drive and capture their quarry. The noctule digestive system is simple and fast, suited to a diet of small, soft-bodied prey, and the noctules eat frequently, for their high metabolism demands a constant intake of food to sustain the energy costs of flight and echolocation.

## Ecology

Noctules occupy the niche of a small-bodied aerial predator in the subterranean ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on the ecology of the caves is considerable, for the noctules are the primary predators of the flying insects of the underground, and their presence regulates the populations of the species that would otherwise multiply unchecked in the stable, predator-free environment of the deep caves. The noctules and the cave ecosystem exist in a balance that has been maintained over centuries, the noctules taking what they need and the cave sustaining both them and the organisms upon which they prey.

Noctules share the deep underground with [arachne](/content/species/sapient/arachne) and [kryn](/content/species/sapient/kryn), and the three peoples occupy different niches in the subterranean world. The arachne build their webs on the floors and walls, the kryn cultivate their gardens in the deepest caverns, and the noctules hunt in the air, and the three peoples coexist without significant competition, for their needs and their methods are too different to bring them into conflict.
