## Bat-Like People of the Deep Caves

Noctules are a bat-like people found in the deep caves of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the larger cave systems of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), their bodies adapted to a life of flight in total darkness. Some naturalists propose a kinship between noctules and [harpies](/content/species/sapient/harpies), citing the capacity for flight shared by both peoples and the occupation of elevated inaccessible habitats, but the differences are too great for the comparison to be sustained: noctules are far smaller, nocturnal, and adapted to the underground rather than the open air. Others argue for a connection to [kryn](/content/species/sapient/kryn), pointing to the shared subterranean habitat and use of non-visual senses, but the noctule body is too distinct in form for the comparison to be sustained. Noctules convey their oral traditions through high-frequency vocalisation rather than speech, unintelligible to other peoples. They have inhabited the deep caves for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lightless places is attested in the oldest subterranean records. The noctules and the [arachne](/content/species/sapient/arachne) share the deep underground, having coexisted for centuries, each occupying a different niche in the subterranean world.

## Echolocation and Lightless Flight

Noctules stand no taller than half the height of an average [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a light angular frame built for flight. Their bones are hollow and thin-walled, reducing weight while retaining structural strength, and musculature concentrates in the chest and shoulders where the flight muscles are anchored. The arms are also wings, combining both functions in a single limb, with a thin tough membrane stretching from elongated fingers to the sides of the body and legs. The wings are large relative to the body, granting manoeuvrability in confined spaces, and noctule flight is both swift and precise, navigating narrowest tunnels and largest caverns with equal facility.

The noctule head is small and angular, with a short muzzle, large ears, and small eyes that are functionally useless in the total darkness of deep caves. Vision is rudimentary, distinguishing only light from dark, and noctules rely upon echolocation. They produce high-frequency vocalisations far above the range of human hearing, and the echoes returning from walls, ceiling, and objects are processed by the large sensitive ears to construct a detailed auditory image of surroundings. This sense is extraordinarily precise, allowing detection of objects as fine as a strand of silk in total darkness, and it is the primary means by which noctules navigate, hunt, and communicate.

Noctule skin is thin and dark, ranging from deep brown to near-black, covered in short fine fur that provides insulation in the cold air of deep caves. Their hands at the wingtips are small but capable, with long slender fingers used for climbing, gripping, and manipulation of small objects. The noctule body is built for the air; a noctule grounded by injury is severely handicapped, as the legs are short and weak and the body poorly suited to walking or crawling.

## Clinging Young and Colony Roosts

Noctules live forty to sixty years under favourable conditions. They mature quickly, reaching full stature and flight capacity within five or six years of birth. Noctule women carry their young for approximately four months before giving birth to a single infant, though twins are not uncommon. The infant is small and helpless, clinging to the mother's fur for the first weeks of life, and carried in flight until old enough to fly on its own, a milestone typically reached within two years.

Noctule populations grow slowly, as births are infrequent and conditions of the deep caves are demanding. Mortality among the young is moderate, as the underground environment is unforgiving of error. The noctule community is closely knit, and individuals of a colony roost together in a shared shelter, hanging from the ceiling of a cavern in a cluster that provides warmth and protection. The elders of a noctule colony are those who have maintained their flight and echolocation into later life, and their knowledge of the cave system and its passages is the foundation of the colony's survival.

## Deep Caves of Morvind and Aethelar

Noctules are found chiefly in the deep caves of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), where vast underground networks of the interior ranges provide the space and darkness their way of life requires. They are the primary flying people of the subterranean world, their colonies found throughout the larger cave systems, from the middle levels to the deepest reaches accessible by air. Smaller populations are found in the cave systems of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where limestone formations provide the kind of enclosed dark spaces noctules prefer.

Noctules are entirely absent from the surface world, as their eyes are useless in bright light and their bodies are adapted to the stable temperature and still air of the underground. Exposure to sunlight is painful and disorienting, and they venture to the surface only in the darkest hours of night when the sky is overcast and the moon is new. The [geography](/content/geography) of the underground determines the distribution of noctule colonies, each situated in a location providing access to flying space, roosting sites, and food sources of the cave system.

## Aerial Insect Hunting in Darkness

The noctule diet is dominated by insects and small creatures of the deep caves. Noctules are aerial hunters of extraordinary precision, capturing prey in flight using echolocation to detect and track small flying insects that share the subterranean air. Their speed and agility in confined spaces allow them to pursue and catch prey that no ground-dwelling predator could reach. The hunt is conducted in the dark, guided entirely by sound, and their echolocation is precise enough to distinguish between edible and inedible insects by the shape and density of their echo.

Larger prey is taken when available, including small birds and mammals that occasionally venture into cave systems from the surface. Noctules are capable of overwhelming creatures larger than themselves through co-ordinated hunting, with several individuals working together to drive and capture their quarry. Their digestive system is simple and fast, suited to small soft-bodied prey, and they eat frequently, as their high metabolism demands constant food intake to sustain the energy costs of flight and echolocation.

## Primary Hunters of Subterranean Insects

Noctules occupy the niche of a small-bodied aerial predator in subterranean ecosystems. They are the primary predators of flying insects of the underground, and their presence regulates populations of species that would otherwise multiply unchecked in the stable predator-free environment of deep caves. The noctules and the cave ecosystem exist in a balance maintained over centuries, the noctules taking what they need and the cave sustaining both them and the organisms upon which they prey.

Noctules share the deep underground with [arachne](/content/species/sapient/arachne) and [kryn](/content/species/sapient/kryn), and the three peoples occupy different niches in the subterranean world. The arachne build their webs on floors and walls, the kryn cultivate their gardens in the deepest caverns, and the noctules hunt in the air, coexisting without significant competition as their needs and methods are too different to bring them into conflict.
