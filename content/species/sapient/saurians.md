## Origins

The origins of the saurians are a subject of learned debate, for the saurian body is large, reptilian, and adapted to the warm rivers of the south in a manner that suggests a lineage of great age. Saurians are found in the warm southern rivers of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) and the hot regions beyond, and their bodies combine a humanoid upper form with the lower body and tail of a large reptile. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between saurians and [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga), citing the reptilian form, the warm climate, and the aquatic habits shared by both peoples, though the saurians are larger, more broadly built, and possess legs rather than a serpentine trunk. Others have argued for a connection to [lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians), pointing to the shared reptilian form and the warm, wet habitat, but the saurians are too different in scale and in habit for the comparison to be sustained. The saurians themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their having descended from the great reptiles of the ancient rivers, and they regard the warm waters as their rightful home.

What is certain is that saurians have inhabited the warm southern rivers of Sahrana for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these waters is attested in the oldest records of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) city-states. The saurians and the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the northern coast have coexisted for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the difference in their preferred terrain and the distance between their settlements.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Saurians are a large people, standing taller than [orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) and comparable in height to [trolls](/content/species/sapient/trolls), with a powerful and heavily built frame. The upper body is humanoid in outline, broad and muscular, with a head, shoulders, and arms. The head is reptilian, with a long, powerful snout, rows of sharp teeth, and eyes that are set high and forward, granting excellent vision both above and below the water. The lower body is supported by two thick, muscular legs and a long, heavy tail that provides balance and propulsion in the water. The whole form is covered in thick scales that range from dark green to grey, brown, and mottled combinations of these, and the scales are thicker and more robust than those of the smaller reptilian peoples, providing substantial natural protection.

The saurian body is superbly adapted to the warm river environments it inhabits. Their legs are strong and capable of sustained movement on land, and their webbed feet and powerful tail make them excellent swimmers, able to move through the water with a speed and force that no other people can match. Their lungs are large and efficient, allowing them to remain submerged for extended periods, and their metabolism is adapted to the warm climate, functioning efficiently in the heat but becoming sluggish when the temperature drops. Saurians are warm-blooded, despite their reptilian appearance, and their bodies are well insulated by the thick scales and the layer of fat beneath.

Saurian senses are well developed and specialised for the river environment. Their eyes are adapted to both above-water and underwater vision, and a nictitating membrane protects them during submersion. Their vision is keen in low light, for the rivers they inhabit are often turbid and shadowed by overhanging vegetation. Their hearing is competent in air and excellent in water, and their sense of smell is acute, particularly in the water, where they can detect the chemical signatures of prey and the seasonal changes in the river.

## Life Cycle

Saurians are long-lived, with a typical lifespan of one hundred to one hundred and fifty years under favourable conditions. They mature slowly, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late twenties. Saurian women lay clutches of six to ten eggs in nests dug into the sandy banks of the rivers they inhabit, and the eggs are incubated by the warmth of the sun and the decomposing vegetation that the mother piles above them for a period of three to four months before hatching. The hatchlings are large and well-developed, capable of swimming and walking within hours of emerging from the egg, and they grow steadily over many years.

Saurian populations grow slowly, for births are infrequent and the young require many years of care before they are fully independent. The mortality rate among the young is moderate, for the rivers contain predators that take their toll, but the size and strength of the saurian adults provide effective protection, and the community is attentive to the defence of the nesting sites. The saurian community is small and closely knit, and the bonds between kindred are strong, maintained through shared territory and the collective defence of the nesting grounds.

## Habitat and Distribution

Saurians are found chiefly in the warm southern rivers of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where the water is warm and the banks are thick with vegetation. They favour the broad, slow-moving reaches of the great rivers, the lagoons and backwaters that lie beside the main channel, and the flooded forests that line the riverbanks during the seasonal floods. Their settlements are typically built on the banks, combining structures above and below the waterline, and they are situated where the water is deep enough for swimming and the banks are stable enough for construction.

Smaller populations of saurians are found in the warm rivers of southern [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the climate is mild enough to support their way of life, and in the river systems of the Sahrani interior, where the seasonal rivers provide a habitat that the saurians share with the [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga) and the [lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians). Saurians are absent from the cold regions, the mountains, and the sea, for their bodies are adapted to warm fresh water and fare poorly in the cold or the salt. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region determines the distribution of saurian populations, and the saurian range is restricted to the warm, wet lowlands where the conditions suit them.

## Diet

The saurian diet is dominated by fish and aquatic creatures, and saurians are formidable predators in the rivers they inhabit. They hunt the large fish of the warm rivers, including the species that are too large or too powerful for smaller predators to take, and their strength and speed in the water make them the dominant hunters of the riverine environment. The hunt is conducted by ambush and pursuit, and the saurian can overtake the swiftest fish in the water with a burst of speed from its powerful tail.

Meat from land animals is a secondary part of the diet, taken from the creatures that come to the river to drink, and the saurians are capable of overwhelming large prey through sheer strength. Plant foods are a minor part of the diet, though saurians will eat the fruits and tender shoots that grow along the riverbanks. Saurians consume their food raw, for fire is impractical in their semi-aquatic way of life, and their digestion is robust and well adapted to a diet of raw fish and meat. The saurian metabolism is efficient and capable of sustaining long periods between meals, for the saurian can gorge after a successful hunt and then fast for many days while the food is slowly digested.

## Ecology

Saurians occupy the niche of a large-bodied aquatic predator in the warm river ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on the ecology of the rivers is considerable, for the saurians are the apex predators of the warm waters, and their hunting regulates the populations of the large fish and the aquatic mammals throughout their range. The saurian presence in a river system is generally associated with a healthy and balanced ecosystem, for the saurians take what they need and no more, and their hunting is governed by the slow metabolism and the infrequent needs of a large, long-lived predator.

Saurians share the warm rivers with [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga) and [lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians) in portions of their range, and the three peoples maintain a wary coexistence, for all are aquatic predators and their needs overlap. The saurians, being the largest and the most powerful, favour the deepest waters and the largest prey, while the naga prefer the middle reaches and the lacertians the shallows and the vegetation, and the three peoples separate by depth and by the size of the prey they pursue.
