## Origins

The origins of the orcs are a matter of conjecture, as the orcish peoples have left few written records and their oral traditions are known to other peoples only in fragmentary form. What can be observed is that orcs are a large and powerful people, found chiefly in the wilder and less settled regions of the world, and their physiology suggests a constitution well suited to harsh conditions and violent exertion. Some naturalists have proposed that orcs share a distant kinship with [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), citing the generalised structure of the orcish body and its capacity to thrive in a range of environments. Others have argued for a closer connection to [goblins](/content/species/sapient/goblins), pointing to the overlap in habitat and the broadly similar social organisation of the two peoples. Neither view has been demonstrated conclusively, and the orcish peoples themselves have shown little interest in the question.

What is clear from the archaeological record is that orcs have inhabited the wilder regions of the world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands predates the expansion of the common peoples into the regions they now occupy. The orcish peoples may thus be counted among the older inhabitants of the known world, though their history is poorly documented compared to that of the more settled peoples.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Orcs are the largest and most powerfully built of the sapient peoples, standing taller than [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) and broader than [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves). Their bones are thick and heavy, and their musculature is massive, built for sudden and forceful exertion rather than sustained effort. This physique gives orcs great strength in the short term, though they tire more quickly than humans or dwarves when exertion is prolonged. Their frames are well suited to the harsh and demanding environments they inhabit, where the ability to endure cold, hunger, and injury is of greater value than endurance over distance.

Orcish senses are keen, though they differ in emphasis from those of other peoples. Their vision is excellent in low light, adapted to the dawn and dusk activity patterns common among orcish communities, and their sense of smell is well developed, aiding them in tracking and in the detection of both prey and danger. Their hearing is competent but less acute than that of elves or gnomes, and they rely more upon sight and scent than upon sound.

Orcish skin is thick and tough, ranging from grey-green to dark brown in colour, and it provides a measure of natural protection against the elements and against injury. Their hair is coarse and dark, worn short by both sexes, and their teeth are large and strong, with the lower canines often protruding as tusks that are used for tearing food and, in some communities, as tools.

## Life Cycle

Orcs are short-lived by comparison with the common peoples, with a typical lifespan of fifty to seventy years under favourable conditions. They mature rapidly, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their early to mid-teens, and their powers begin to decline after the fourth decade. Orcish women carry their young for approximately eight months, and births are frequently multiple. Infant mortality is high by the standards of the common peoples, owing in part to the harsh conditions in which orcish communities live, but the high rate of birth ensures that populations can recover quickly from losses.

The brevity of the orcish lifespan and the rapid succession of generations have shaped a people who live intensely and act decisively. The elders of an orcish community are those who have survived the longest, and their authority rests upon proven experience rather than upon longevity alone. The orcish peoples do not fear death in the manner of longer-lived peoples, and their traditions place great value on deeds performed within the span of a short life.

## Habitat and Distribution

Orcs are found chiefly in the wilder and less settled regions of the world, including the untamed forests and moorlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), the remote highlands and cold steppes of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and the arid interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). They favour lands that other peoples find difficult to inhabit, where the terrain is rugged, the climate harsh, and the resources scarce but sufficient for a people adapted to hardship. Orcish settlements are typically temporary or semi-permanent, as many orcish communities move with the seasons or in response to the availability of game and forage.

The orcish preference for marginal lands has kept them largely separate from the common peoples, and contact between orcs and the settled populations is infrequent and often tense. Where the expansion of human or elven settlements has encroached upon orcish ranges, conflict has sometimes followed, as the orcish peoples do not readily yield territory that they have long occupied.

## Diet

The orcish diet is dominated by meat, reflecting the demands of a large and powerful body and the hunting traditions of most orcish communities. Game animals, fish, and gathered fungi and roots form the greater part of what orcs eat, and the hunting of large beasts is a central activity of orcish life, both as a source of food and as a test of skill and courage. Orcish digestion is robust and capable of processing a wide range of animal foods, including raw flesh and organs, which are consumed when cooking is impractical. Plant foods are gathered rather than cultivated, and the orcish peoples have no tradition of agriculture, though they are knowledgeable about the edible plants of their homelands.

## Ecology

Orcs occupy the niche of a large-bodied predator and opportunist in the wilder regions of the world. Their impact on their surroundings is considerable where they are present in numbers, as their hunting can significantly affect the populations of large game animals. Orcish communities move through their territories in patterns that follow the movement of prey, and their relationship with the land is one of pursuit and adaptation rather than settlement and modification.

Where orcish ranges overlap with those of other peoples, the relationship is shaped by competition for resources. [Humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and orcs encounter one another at the margins of settled land, where farmers and hunters come into contact with orcish hunting parties. [Goblins](/content/species/sapient/goblins) and orcs are often found in the same regions, and the two peoples sometimes coexist in a wary symbiosis, though their relations are not always peaceful.
