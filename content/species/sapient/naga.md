## Serpent People of the Warm Waters

Naga combine a humanoid upper body with the trunk and tail of a large serpent, adapted to a life in warm watery environments. From the waist up, the naga form is broadly humanoid; from the waist down, the body continues as a muscular scaled serpent's tail. Some naturalists propose a kinship between naga and [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk), citing the radical adaptation of both peoples to a non-terrestrial mode of life, and the serpent form of the naga has been compared to the aquatic form of the merfolk as a parallel adaptation to a different medium. Naga have inhabited the warm southern coasts and rivers of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands is attested in the oldest records of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) city-states. The naga and the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the northern coast have coexisted for centuries in one of the oldest interspecies contacts in the southern world.

## Humanoid Torso, Serpent Lower Body

Naga are medium to large, longer in the body than any other sapient species, as the serpent trunk extends a full six to eight feet beyond the waist. The upper body is lean and flexible, with a humanoid head, shoulders, and arms; the hands are strong and capable, used for manipulation of tools, weapons, and goods. The lower body is a powerful serpent's tail covered in smooth scales ranging from deep green to gold, brown, and patterned combinations, serving as both means of locomotion and a formidable weapon. A naga can constrict with enough force to overpower large prey, and the tail is also used for swimming, climbing, and stabilisation of the body when the upper torso is engaged in fine work.

The naga body is superbly adapted to warm watery environments. Naga are excellent swimmers, moving through water with speed and grace that rivals the [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk). Their scales provide natural protection against injury and the elements, and they are water-resistant, allowing naga to remain submerged for extended periods. Naga are warm-blooded despite reptilian appearance, well suited to warm climates, though uncomfortable in cold and faring poorly in temperate and subarctic regions.

Naga senses are well developed, particularly vision and sense of smell. Their eyes are large and golden, with vertical pupils that narrow to slits in bright light and open wide in dim conditions, granting excellent vision in both the bright sun of southern coasts and the dim light of forest and river. Their sense of smell is acute, and naga detect chemical signatures of water from different sources, a gift that serves them in navigation and identification of suitable habitat. Hearing is competent, comparable to that of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), though less acute than that of forest-dwelling peoples.

## Live Birth and Slow Maturation

Naga live one hundred to one hundred and fifty years under favourable conditions. They mature slowly, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late twenties, and retain their vigour well into later years. Naga women carry their young for approximately ten months before giving birth to live young, and births are usually single, though twins are not uncommon. The infant is capable of swimming and crawling within hours of birth, and naga young grow steadily over many years, developing the strength and size of their adult form gradually.

Naga populations grow slowly, as births are infrequent and the young require many years of care before becoming fully independent. This slow growth is balanced by the longevity of the species and the low rate of death among adults, as naga are formidable in their element and have few natural predators. Naga communities are small and closely knit, and the bonds between kindred are strong and enduring, maintained through shared territory and the collective care of the young.

## Sahrani Coasts and Rivers

Naga are found chiefly in the warm southern coasts and rivers of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where the climate is hot and humid and the water is warm. They favour the mouths of rivers, lagoons and swamps of the coastal strip, and the lower reaches of river systems descending from the interior highlands. Naga settlements are typically built on platforms and structures raised above the water, combining elements of land and aquatic architecture, situated where the water is shallow and fishing is good.

Smaller populations are found on the warm coasts of southern [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the climate is mild enough to support their way of life, and in the river systems of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) interior, where water is warm and banks are thick with vegetation. Naga are absent from cold coasts and temperate rivers, as their bodies are adapted to warmth and fare poorly in chill northern waters. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region determines the distribution of naga populations, and the naga range is the most restricted of any people to the warm latitudes.

## Fish, Cultivation, and Shared Meals

The naga diet is dominated by fish and aquatic creatures. Naga are skilled hunters in the water, pursuing and catching fish with speed and precision that no land-dwelling people can match, and their catches include larger species of rivers and coasts, as well as shelled creatures, shellfish, and waterfowl. The hunt is conducted both individually and collectively, and naga drive fish into shallows and nets in a manner that recalls the techniques of the merfolk.

Plant foods form a significant part of the diet. Naga cultivate rice, fruits, and vegetables in the fertile lands along rivers and coasts, supplementing the catch with produce of careful cultivation. Meals are occasions for gathering of the kindred and sharing of news, and the naga palate is discerning and varied. Spices and herbs are used freely, and the naga reputation for cookery is recognised throughout the regions where they are known.

## Aquaculturists of River and Coast

Naga occupy the niche of a large-bodied aquatic and semi-terrestrial predator in warm coastal and riverine environments. Their impact on the ecology of these regions is considerable, as they manage waters and banks with a care that sustains both fish populations and vegetation. Naga practice a form of aquaculture, maintaining beds of fish and shellfish near their settlements, and they cultivate riverbanks with a mixture of food plants and trees that provide shade and materials.

Naga share their habitats with [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) across much of the Sahrani coast, and the two peoples coexist closely, as their needs and skills are complementary. Humans tend the land and cities while naga tend the water and rivers, each benefiting from the produce of the other. [Merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) and naga occasionally encounter one another where rivers meet the sea, maintaining a cautious but peaceful coexistence, each respecting the other's domain.
