## Origins

The origins of the naga are a subject of learned speculation, for the naga body is unlike that of any other sapient people in the warm regions they inhabit. From the waist up, the naga form is broadly humanoid, with a head, shoulders, and arms. From the waist down, the body continues as the trunk and tail of a large serpent, muscular and scaled, and the whole form is adapted to a life in warm, watery environments. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between naga and [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk), citing the radical adaptation of both peoples to a non-terrestrial mode of life, and the serpent form of the naga has been compared to the aquatic form of the merfolk as a parallel adaptation to a different medium. The naga themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their emergence from the warm rivers of the south, and they regard the water as their first home.

What is certain is that naga have inhabited the warm southern coasts and rivers of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands is attested in the oldest records of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) city-states. The naga and the humans of the northern coast have coexisted for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is among the oldest interspecies contacts in the southern world.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Naga are a medium to large people, longer in the body than any other sapient species, for the serpent trunk extends a full six to eight feet beyond the waist. The upper body is lean and flexible, with a humanoid head, shoulders, and arms, and the hands are strong and capable, used for the manipulation of tools, weapons, and goods. The lower body is a powerful serpent's tail, covered in smooth scales that range in colour from deep green to gold, brown, and patterned combinations of these, and the tail is both the means of locomotion and a formidable weapon in its own right. A naga can constrict with enough force to overpower large prey, and the tail is also used for swimming, climbing, and the stabilisation of the body when the upper torso is engaged in fine work.

The naga body is superbly adapted to warm, watery environments, and naga are excellent swimmers, capable of moving through water with a speed and grace that rivals the [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk). Their scales provide a measure of natural protection against injury and the elements, and they are water-resistant, allowing naga to remain submerged for extended periods. Naga are warm-blooded, despite their reptilian appearance, and their bodies are well suited to the warm climates they prefer, though they are uncomfortable in cold and fare poorly in the temperate and subarctic regions.

Naga senses are well developed, particularly their vision and their sense of smell. Their eyes are large and golden, with vertical pupils that can narrow to slits in bright light and open wide in dim conditions, granting them excellent vision in both the bright sun of the southern coasts and the dim light of forest and river. Their sense of smell is acute, and naga can detect the chemical signatures of water from different sources, a gift that serves them in navigation and in the identification of suitable habitat. Their hearing is competent, comparable to that of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), though less acute than that of the forest-dwelling peoples.

## Life Cycle

Naga are long-lived, with a typical lifespan of one hundred to one hundred and fifty years under favourable conditions. They mature slowly, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late twenties, and they retain their vigour well into their later years. Naga women carry their young for approximately ten months before giving birth to live young, and births are usually single, though twins are not uncommon. The infant is capable of swimming and crawling within hours of birth, and the naga young grow steadily over many years, developing the strength and size of their adult form gradually.

Naga populations grow slowly, for births are infrequent and the young require many years of care before they are fully independent. This slow growth is balanced by the longevity of the species and by the low rate of death among adults, for naga are formidable in their element and have few natural predators. Naga communities are small and closely knit, and the bonds between kindred are strong and enduring, maintained through shared territory and the collective care of the young.

## Habitat and Distribution

Naga are found chiefly in the warm southern coasts and rivers of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where the climate is hot and humid and the water is warm. They favour the mouths of rivers, the lagoons and swamps of the coastal strip, and the lower reaches of the river systems that descend from the interior highlands. Naga settlements are typically built on platforms and structures raised above the water, combining elements of land and aquatic architecture, and they are situated where the water is shallow and the fishing is good.

Smaller populations of naga are found on the warm coasts of southern [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the climate is mild enough to support their way of life, and in the river systems of the Sahrani interior, where the water is warm and the banks are thick with vegetation. Naga are absent from the cold coasts and the temperate rivers, for their bodies are adapted to warmth and fare poorly in the chill waters of the north. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region determines the distribution of naga populations, and the naga range is the most restricted of any people to the warm latitudes.

## Diet

The naga diet is dominated by fish and aquatic creatures, reflecting the watery environments they inhabit. Naga are skilled hunters in the water, capable of pursuing and catching fish with a speed and precision that no land-dwelling people can match, and their catches include the larger species of the rivers and coasts, as well as crustaceans, shellfish, and waterfowl. The hunt is conducted both individually and collectively, and the naga are known to drive fish into shallows and nets in a manner that recalls the techniques of the merfolk.

Plant foods form a significant part of the diet, and naga cultivate rice, fruits, and vegetables in the fertile lands along the rivers and coasts, supplementing the catch of the hunt with the produce of careful cultivation. The naga approach to food is communal and refined, for meals are occasions for the gathering of the kindred and the sharing of news, and the naga palate is discerning and varied. Spices and herbs are used freely, and the naga reputation for cookery is recognised throughout the regions where they are known.

## Ecology

Naga occupy the niche of a large-bodied aquatic and semi-terrestrial predator in the warm coastal and riverine environments they inhabit. Their impact on the ecology of these regions is considerable, for the naga manage the waters and the banks they inhabit with a care that sustains both the fish populations and the vegetation. The naga practice a form of aquaculture, maintaining beds of fish and shellfish near their settlements, and they cultivate the riverbanks with a mixture of food plants and trees that provide shade and materials.

Naga share their habitats with [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) across much of the Sahrani coast, and the two peoples coexist closely, for their needs and skills are complementary. The humans tend the land and the cities, while the naga tend the water and the rivers, and each benefits from the produce of the other. [Merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) and naga occasionally encounter one another where the rivers meet the sea, and the two peoples maintain a cautious but peaceful coexistence, each respecting the other's domain.
