## Origins

The origins of the arachne are among the most mysterious of any thinking people, for the arachne body is unlike that of any other species and admits no easy comparison. From the waist up, the arachne form is broadly humanoid, with a head, shoulders, and arms. From the waist down, the body continues as the trunk and legs of a large spider, with eight powerful limbs and a segmented abdomen. Some naturalists have proposed a distant kinship between arachne and [kobolds](/content/species/sapient/kobolds), citing the subterranean habitat shared by both peoples, but the differences in form and habit are too great for the comparison to be sustained. The arachne themselves preserve no oral traditions of their origins, or if they do, they do not share them with outsiders, and their silence on the subject has only deepened the mystery.

What is known is that arachne have inhabited the deep caverns of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the deeper reaches of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) for as long as any people can account for. Their presence in these remote and lightless places is attested in the oldest dwarven records, where they are mentioned with a mixture of respect and unease that suggests the relationship between the two subterranean peoples is ancient and complex.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Arachne are a large and striking people, and their form is immediately recognisable. The upper body is humanoid in outline, lean and angular, with a head, shoulders, and two arms ending in hands that are long-fingered and strong. The lower body is that of a large spider, with a broad, segmented abdomen and eight powerful legs that are jointed and covered in a short, dark chitin. The whole form is larger than a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the span of the legs gives the arachne a low, wide silhouette that is well suited to the irregular surfaces of the deep caves.

The arachne body is superbly adapted to the subterranean environment. Their legs are strong and flexible, capable of carrying them across ceilings and walls as easily as floors, and the chitin that covers the lower body is tough and resistant to abrasion. The upper body is softer, protected by a smooth, dark skin that ranges from deep grey to black, and the hands are capable of fine and precise work. Arachne are capable of producing silk from glands in the abdomen, and this silk is extraordinarily strong and versatile, used for the construction of webs, the reinforcement of tunnel walls, the wrapping of prey, and the making of ropes and fabrics that are among the finest and most durable in the known world.

Arachne senses are specialised for the deep underground. Their eyes are large and multiple, arranged in two rows across the brow, and they are adapted to the complete darkness of the deep caves, capable of detecting the faintest trace of light and the body heat of other creatures. Their hearing is less developed, for sound behaves differently in the enclosed spaces of the deep, and they rely more upon vibration, detected through the sensitive hairs on their legs, for awareness of their surroundings. Their sense of touch is extraordinary, and an arachne can detect the presence of other creatures through the vibrations of the stone beneath their feet.

## Life Cycle

Arachne are long-lived, with a typical lifespan of one hundred to one hundred and fifty years under favourable conditions, though reliable data are difficult to obtain, as the arachne inhabit the deepest and most inaccessible places and do not welcome visitors. They mature slowly, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late twenties. Arachne women lay clutches of fifty to one hundred eggs, which are encased in silk and guarded in a communal nesting area for a period of several months before hatching. The hatchlings are small and numerous, and many do not survive to adulthood, but those that do grow steadily over many years, developing the strength and the skills of their adult form.

Arachne populations grow slowly, for the mortality rate among the young is high and the conditions of the deep underground are demanding. The community is tightly organised, with each member playing a defined role in the maintenance of the web, the provisioning of the group, and the defence of the territory. The elders of an arachne community are respected for their knowledge of the deep tunnels and their skill in the working of silk, and their authority is exercised through the collective agreement of the community rather than through any individual dominance.

## Habitat and Distribution

Arachne are found chiefly in the deep caverns of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), where the vast underground networks of the interior ranges provide the space and the seclusion that their way of life requires. They are the dominant people of the deepest caves, and their webs and settlements are found at depths that no other sapient people can reach. Smaller populations are reported in the deeper reaches of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the cave systems descend to levels that the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) have not explored. The [geography](/content/geography) of the underground determines the distribution of arachne populations, and they are entirely absent from the surface world.

Arachne settlements are built within the largest caverns and tunnel intersections, and the silk of the arachne is used to construct structures that span the open spaces, creating platforms, bridges, and dwellings that are suspended from the cavern walls and ceilings. These settlements are extraordinary in their complexity and beauty, and the few outsiders who have seen them describe a world of silk and shadow that is unlike anything on the surface.

## Diet

The arachne diet is dominated by the creatures of the deep underground, and arachne are formidable hunters in their lightless domain. They prey upon the large subterranean organisms that inhabit the deep caves, including pale, eyeless creatures that are unknown on the surface, and the various fungi and lichens that grow in the damp, warm air of the deep. The hunt is conducted using webs, which are strung across the tunnels and caverns to trap passing prey, and the arachne are patient hunters, capable of waiting for days or weeks for a suitable catch.

Fungi form a secondary part of the diet, and the arachne cultivate certain species in the damp areas of their settlements, tending them with a care that recalls the agriculture of the surface peoples. The arachne do not cook, for fire is impractical in the deep caves, and they consume their food raw, digesting it with a robust system that can process a wide range of organic materials. Water is obtained from the underground streams and pools that flow through the deep caverns, and the arachne are careful to maintain the quality of these sources, for they are the only water available.

## Ecology

Arachne occupy the niche of a large-bodied predator and web-builder in the deep subterranean ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on the ecology of the deep caves is considerable, for their webs alter the movement of air and the distribution of organisms, and their hunting regulates the populations of the subterranean fauna. The arachne management of their environment is deliberate and careful, and the deep caves where they dwell are among the most stable and well-ordered ecosystems in the known world, maintained by the patient and methodical attention of their arachne stewards.

Arachne have little contact with other sapient peoples, owing to the depth and inaccessibility of their habitats. [Dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) and arachne share the underground world, and the two peoples maintain a distant and wary coexistence, each avoiding the territory of the other. The dwarves know of the arachne and respect their skill, and occasional trade occurs at the margins of their respective ranges, with the arachne offering silk and the dwarves offering metalwork, but the contact is rare and conducted with great caution.
