## Enigmatic Denizens of the Deep

The arachne are among the most enigmatic of the sapient peoples. Their bodies combine a humanoid upper form with the trunk and eight legs of a large spider, a morphology unlike that of any other known species. Naturalists have proposed a distant kinship with [kobolds](/content/species/sapient/kobolds) on the basis of the subterranean habitat both peoples share, but the differences in form and habit are too great for the comparison to be sustained. The arachne themselves preserve no oral traditions of their origins, or if they do, they do not share them with outsiders, and their silence on the subject has deepened the mystery surrounding their past.

The arachne have inhabited the deep caverns of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the deeper reaches of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) for as long as any people can account for. Their presence in these remote and lightless places is recorded in the oldest dwarven records, where they are mentioned with a mixture of respect and unease that suggests an ancient and complex relationship between the two subterranean peoples.

## Humanoid Upper Body, Spider Lower

Arachne are a large people whose form is immediately recognisable. The upper body is humanoid in outline, lean and angular, with a head, shoulders, and two arms ending in hands that are long-fingered and strong. The lower body is that of a large spider, with a broad, segmented abdomen and eight powerful legs that are jointed and covered in short, dark hardened shell. The whole form is larger than a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the leg span gives the arachne a low, wide silhouette well suited to the irregular surfaces of deep caves.

The arachne body is superbly adapted to the subterranean environment. Their legs are strong and flexible, capable of carrying them across ceilings and walls as easily as floors, and the hard shell covering the lower body is tough and resistant to abrasion. The upper body is softer, protected by smooth, dark skin ranging from deep grey to black, and the hands are capable of fine and precise work. Arachne produce silk from glands in the abdomen, and this silk is extraordinarily strong and versatile, used for constructing webs, reinforcing tunnel walls, wrapping prey, and making ropes and fabrics that are among the finest and most durable in the known world.

Arachne senses are specialised for the deep underground. Their eyes are large and multiple, arranged in two rows across the brow, adapted to complete darkness and capable of detecting the faintest trace of light and the body heat of other creatures. Their hearing is less developed, as sound behaves differently in the enclosed spaces of the deep, and they rely more upon vibration, detected through sensitive hairs on their legs, for awareness of their surroundings. Their sense of touch is extraordinary, and an arachne can detect the presence of other creatures through vibrations in the stone beneath their feet.

## Eggs, Silk, and Communal Care

Arachne are long-lived, with a typical lifespan of one hundred to one hundred and fifty years under favourable conditions, though reliable data are difficult to obtain as they inhabit the deepest and most inaccessible places and do not welcome visitors. They mature slowly, reaching full stature and adulthood by their late twenties. Arachne women lay clutches of fifty to one hundred eggs, which are encased in silk and guarded in a communal nesting area for several months before hatching. The hatchlings are small and numerous, and many do not survive to adulthood, but those that do grow steadily over many years, developing the strength and skills of the adult form.

Arachne populations grow slowly due to high mortality among the young and the demanding conditions of the deep underground. The community is tightly organised, with each member playing a defined role in maintaining the web, provisioning the group, and defending the territory. The elders of an arachne community are respected for their knowledge of the deep tunnels and their skill in working silk, and authority is exercised through collective agreement rather than individual dominance.

## Deep Caverns of Morvind and Aethelar

Arachne are found chiefly in the deep caverns of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), where the vast underground networks of the interior ranges provide the space and seclusion their way of life requires. They are the dominant people of the deepest caves, and their webs and settlements are found at depths no other sapient people can reach. Smaller populations are reported in the deeper reaches of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where cave systems descend to levels the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) have not explored. The distribution of arachne populations is determined by the geography of the underground, and they are entirely absent from the surface world.

Arachne settlements are built within the largest caverns and tunnel intersections. Their silk is used to construct structures that span open spaces, creating platforms, bridges, and dwellings suspended from cavern walls and ceilings. These settlements are extraordinary in their complexity and beauty, and the few outsiders who have seen them describe a world of silk and shadow unlike anything on the surface.

## Web-Hunting in the Lightless Deep

The arachne diet is dominated by the creatures of the deep underground. Arachne are formidable hunters in their lightless domain, preying upon large subterranean creatures, including pale, eyeless things unknown on the surface, as well as the various fungi and lichens that grow in the damp, warm air of the deep. The hunt is conducted using webs strung across tunnels and caverns to trap passing prey, and arachne are patient hunters capable of waiting for days or weeks for a suitable catch.

Fungi form a secondary part of the diet. Arachne cultivate certain species in the damp areas of their settlements, tending them with a care that recalls the agriculture of surface peoples. Arachne do not cook, as fire is impractical in the deep caves, and they consume their food raw, digesting it with a robust system that processes a wide range of organic materials. Water is obtained from underground streams and pools that flow through the deep caverns, and arachne are careful to maintain the quality of these sources, as they are the only water available.

## Web-Builders of the Subterranean World

Arachne occupy the niche of a large-bodied predator and web-builder in deep subterranean ecosystems. Their impact on the ecology of the deep caves is considerable: their webs alter the movement of air and the distribution of living things, and their hunting regulates the populations of subterranean fauna. The arachne management of their environment is deliberate and careful, and the deep caves where they dwell are among the most stable and well-ordered ecosystems in the known world, maintained by their patient attention.

Arachne have little contact with other sapient peoples owing to the depth and inaccessibility of their habitats. [Dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) and arachne share the underground world and maintain a distant and wary coexistence, each avoiding the other's territory. The dwarves respect arachne skill, and occasional trade occurs at the margins of their respective ranges, with arachne offering silk and dwarves offering metalwork, but contact is rare and conducted with great caution.
