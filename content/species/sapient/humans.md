## Generalist Lineage of Great Adaptability

The origins of humankind are a subject of learned debate. Unlike [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves), whose lineage is plainly of great age, or [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), whose bodies are shaped by the mountains, humans present a puzzling case: they show no strong mark of any single environment. Their generalised bodies suit no one habitat in particular, leading some scholars to propose that humans arose in a place of great environmental variety where adaptability was more useful than specialisation. The earliest human remains have been found in the temperate regions of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and from there the species spread to [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) through migration, exploration, and coastal settlement.

Humans appear to share a closer kinship with [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) than with [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves), as comparison of their forms suggests that the human and dwarven lines parted from a common stock at a time more recent than the divergence of the elven line. The separation likely occurred when one portion of the ancestral population took to the mountains, developing the stout specialised form of the dwarf, while the other remained in the lowlands, retaining the versatile unspecialised form that characterises humanity.

## Unspecialised Form, Wide Variation

Humans stand between the taller elves and the shorter dwarves, and their form is notably free of the specialisations that mark the other common peoples. Their bones are of ordinary density, neither the heavy frame of the dwarf nor the light frame of the elf. Their musculature is balanced between strength and endurance, excelling at neither but adequate to both. This generality is the defining characteristic of the human body: it carries no adaptation to any single environment and yet restricts humans to none.

Human senses are adequate but unremarkable. Vision is keen in daylight but poor in dim conditions, lacking the reflective membrane found in dwarven eyes. Hearing is competent across a moderate range but does not match elven acuity. Smell is the weakest of the three common peoples, a deficiency some attribute to reliance on learning and toolcraft rather than scent.

Physical variation among humans is considerable and follows climate. Those native to cold regions tend to be shorter and more heavily built, as a compact form conserves heat, while those of warm climates are typically taller and more slender. Skin pigmentation varies with the strength of the sun, darker in southern peoples and lighter in northern ones. Hair and eye colour vary widely, reflecting the broad range of climates the species inhabits and the long ages over which local conditions have shaped each population.

## Short Life, Rapid Reproduction

Humans live seventy to ninety years under favourable conditions, the shortest lifespan of the common peoples. They mature quickly, reaching full stature and physical adulthood in the late teens, and their powers begin to decline after the fifth decade. Human women carry their young for approximately nine months, the shortest term among the three common peoples, and multiple births are not uncommon. Infant mortality is higher than among dwarves or elves, but the high birth rate more than compensates, and human populations can increase rapidly when food and shelter are sufficient.

This combination of short life and frequent childbearing is the foundation of human demographic success. Populations recover quickly from famine, war, or plague, and they can spread into new territories within a few generations. The rapid succession of generations means each generation faces its conditions anew, contributing to the great physical diversity of the species as each population has been shaped by the particular demands of its own land.

## Every Continent, Nearly Every Climate

Humans are the most widely distributed of the sapient species, found on every continent and in nearly every climate. Their generalised physiology allows them to inhabit environments ranging from cold northern coasts to warm southern plains, from humid forests to arid deserts. This range is achieved not through bodily specialisation but through ingenuity: humans build shelters to ward off cold, kindle fires for warmth, and cultivate crops suited to whatever land they find themselves in.

In Aethelar, humans are the dominant population across the temperate river valleys and coastal plains. In Morvind, they settle the coastal lowlands, where the climate is milder than the interior uplands preferred by dwarves. In Sahrana, they are concentrated along the fertile northern coast, with smaller numbers in the desert interior. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region sets the outer bounds of human settlement, but within those bounds humans make use of a broader range of habitats than either of the other common peoples.

## True Omnivore Across All Lands

The human diet is the most varied of the common peoples, reflecting the breadth of climates the species inhabits and the adaptability of the human constitution. Humans are true omnivores, digesting both plant and animal foods with equal facility, and their diet shifts with whatever the local land provides. The cultivation of grain is central to most human populations, supplemented by legumes, fruits, vegetables, and varying proportions of meat, fish, and dairy. This digestive flexibility is among the chief advantages of the species, allowing humans to establish themselves in new lands without requiring the specialised foods of elves or dwarves.

## Transformative Force in Every Habitat

Humans occupy a broad flexible niche as generalist omnivores and habitat generalists. Their capacity to alter surroundings through farming, building, and the use of fire makes them a dominant force in whatever land they settle, and human activity has reshaped the vegetation, waters, and wildlife of large portions of all three continents. The scale of this impact exceeds that of elves or dwarves, owing to the greater size and faster growth of human populations.

Humans share habitats with both [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) and [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) across much of their range. In Aethelar, human and elven populations meet at the forest's edge, with humans favouring cleared and cultivated ground and elves the deeper woodland. In Morvind, human and dwarven ranges overlap by elevation, with humans on the coast and lowlands and dwarves in the upland interior. The generalised nature of human ecology allows coexistence with both peoples, though competition for resources is not unknown where ranges press closely together.
