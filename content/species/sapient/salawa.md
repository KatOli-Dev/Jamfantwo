## Origins

Salawa are a jackal-headed people found in the arid [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) interior, combining a humanoid body with the head and senses of a desert canine. Their body bears the mark of a people shaped by the desert and the heat. Some naturalists propose a kinship between Salawa and [felinids](/content/species/sapient/felinids), citing the animal-headed form and preference for warmer regions shared by both peoples. Others argue that Salawa are more closely related to [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines), pointing to the canine features shared by both, though the differences in habitat and habit are considerable. The Salawa have inhabited the arid Sahrani interior for as long as any people can account for, and their presence is attested in the oldest records of the Sahrani city-states. The Salawa and the [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) nomads of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) have coexisted for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is among the oldest and most complex in the southern world.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Salawa are comparable in height to [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a lean and wiry frame well suited to the heat and demands of desert life. The head is that of a jackal, with a long narrow muzzle, large pointed ears, and sharp golden eyes adapted to both the bright sun of day and the dim light of the desert night. The body is humanoid from the neck down, with lean musculature built for endurance rather than strength, and the limbs are long and slender, proportioned for sustained travel over long distances in the heat.

The Salawa body is superbly adapted to the arid environment. Their skin is dark and sun-tolerant, covered in short fine fur ranging from tawny gold to sandy brown, providing protection against the sun and abrasive sand. Their large ears are their most remarkable feature: they are mobile and highly sensitive, capable of detecting the faintest sound in the still air of the desert, and they also serve to dissipate heat, as the thin skin of the ears is richly supplied with blood vessels that radiate warmth into the air. Their sense of smell is the keenest of any sapient people save the [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines), and they track by scent across the open desert with a reliability no other people can match.

Salawa vision is excellent, particularly in low light, and their eyes are adapted to the desert environment with a nictitating membrane that protects against sand and glare. Their hands are humanoid, with long strong fingers and hard nails used for digging and working materials. Their feet are broad and calloused, well suited to hot sand and rocky desert ground, and they are capable of sustained travel across terrain that would cripple a shod people.

## Life Cycle

Salawa live sixty to ninety years under favourable conditions, though the demands of desert life mean many do not reach old age. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature by their mid-teens. Salawa women carry their young for approximately seven months before giving birth, and births are frequently multiple, with twins and triplets being common. Infant mortality is moderate, lower than among orcish peoples owing to the strong communal bonds of Salawa society, but higher than among settled peoples.

Salawa populations grow steadily when the desert is at peace and water sources are reliable. The young are raised by the extended family group, with all adults sharing in their care. Education is long and practical, encompassing tracking of game, finding of water, navigation of the desert by star and wind, and the traditions of the people. The elders of a Salawa community are respected for their knowledge of the desert and its ways, and their counsel guides the movements and decisions of the group.

## Habitat and Distribution

Salawa are found chiefly in the arid interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) and its surrounding uplands provide the environment their way of life requires. They are a nomadic people, moving across the desert in family groups, following seasonal water sources and the movement of game. Their range extends from the desert fringes of the northern coast to the poorly charted reaches of the deep south, and they are the most widely distributed of the desert-dwelling peoples.

Smaller populations are found in the rocky uplands of the Sahrani interior, where massifs provide shelter and water, and in the desert margins of southern [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where warm plains give way to arid country. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region shapes the character of Salawa communities, but in all cases they seek the same qualities of openness, warmth, and freedom of movement.

## Diet

The Salawa diet mixes hunted and gathered foods, reflecting the resources of the desert and the nomadic character of their way of life. Meat is the staple, provided by hunting of gazelles, hares, and other desert game, and Salawa are formidable trackers and patient hunters, capable of pursuing quarry across the open desert for many miles. The hunt is a central activity of Salawa life, both as a source of food and as a test of skill and endurance, and their hunting traditions are among the most developed of any people.

Plant foods are gathered from the desert: fruits and seeds of hardy shrubs and grasses, and roots dug from the sand after rain. Salawa knowledge of edible desert plants is extensive, encompassing not only what may be eaten but also what may be used for medicine and material. Water is the most precious resource, and Salawa are expert in finding it, knowing hidden springs and seasonal watercourses invisible to those who do not know the desert. They carry water in skins and clay vessels, and their arid-adapted physiology allows them to sustain themselves on far less water than any other people.

## Ecology

Salawa occupy the niche of a medium-bodied nomadic predator and forager in desert ecosystems. Their impact on surroundings is modest, as their numbers are small and their movements are spread across a wide territory, and they take from the desert only what they need. The Salawa regard the desert as their charge, and their traditions of stewardship are directed toward maintenance of water sources and careful management of game, ensuring the desert can sustain both the Salawa and the other creatures that depend upon it.

Salawa share the desert with [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the relationship between the two peoples is one of cautious respect and occasional trade. The human nomads of the Kharesh and the Salawa travel different routes and pursue different game, and their paths cross without significant conflict. [Orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) are present in the wilder reaches of the desert, and the Salawa regard them with wariness, as the orcs are competitors for the same scarce resources.
