## Origins

The origins of the Salawa are bound up with the arid interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), for the Salawa body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by the desert and the heat. The Salawa are a jackal-headed people, found in the arid [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) interior, and their form combines a humanoid body with the head and senses of a desert canine. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between Salawa and [felinids](/content/species/sapient/felinids), citing the animal-headed form shared by both peoples and their preference for the warmer regions of the world. Others have argued that the Salawa are more closely related to [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines), pointing to the canine features shared by both, though the differences in habitat and habit are considerable. The Salawa themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their having been born from the sand and the wind, and they regard the desert as their rightful home.

What is certain is that the Salawa have inhabited the arid Sahrani interior for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands is attested in the oldest records of the Sahrani city-states. The Salawa and the [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) nomads of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) have coexisted for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is among the oldest and most complex in the southern world.

## Anatomy and Physiology

The Salawa are a medium-sized people, standing comparable in height to [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a lean and wiry frame that is well suited to the heat and the demands of desert life. The head is that of a jackal, with a long, narrow muzzle, large pointed ears, and sharp, golden eyes that are adapted to both the bright sun of the day and the dim light of the desert night. The body is humanoid from the neck down, with lean musculature built for endurance rather than strength, and the limbs are long and slender, proportioned for sustained travel over long distances in the heat.

The Salawa body is superbly adapted to the arid environment. Their skin is dark and sun-tolerant, covered in a short, fine fur that ranges from tawny gold to sandy brown, providing protection against the sun and the abrasive sand. Their large ears are their most remarkable feature, for they are mobile and highly sensitive, capable of detecting the faintest sound in the still air of the desert, and they also serve to dissipate heat, for the thin skin of the ears is richly supplied with blood vessels that radiate warmth into the air. Their sense of smell is the keenest of any sapient people save the [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines), and the Salawa can track by scent across the open desert with a reliability that no other people can match.

Salawa vision is excellent, particularly in low light, and their eyes are adapted to the desert environment, with a nictitating membrane that protects against sand and glare. Their hands are humanoid, with long, strong fingers and hard nails that are used for digging and the working of materials. Their feet are broad and calloused, well suited to the hot sand and the rocky ground of the desert, and the Salawa are capable of sustained travel across terrain that would cripple a shod people.

## Life Cycle

Salawa are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of sixty to ninety years under favourable conditions, though the demands of desert life mean that many do not reach old age. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their mid-teens. Salawa women carry their young for approximately seven months before giving birth, and births are frequently multiple, with twins and triplets being common. Infant mortality is moderate, lower than among the orcish peoples owing to the strong communal bonds of Salawa society, but higher than among the settled peoples.

Salawa populations grow steadily when the desert is at peace and the water sources are reliable, and the young are raised by the extended family group, with all adults sharing in their care. The education of a Salawa is long and practical, encompassing the tracking of game, the finding of water, the navigation of the desert by star and wind, and the traditions of the people. The elders of a Salawa community are respected for their knowledge of the desert and its ways, and their counsel guides the movements and decisions of the group.

## Habitat and Distribution

Salawa are found chiefly in the arid interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) and its surrounding uplands provide the environment that their way of life requires. They are a nomadic people, moving across the desert in family groups, following the seasonal water sources and the movement of game. Their range extends from the desert fringes of the northern coast to the poorly charted reaches of the deep south, and they are the most widely distributed of the desert-dwelling peoples.

Smaller populations of Salawa are found in the rocky uplands of the Sahrani interior, where the massifs provide shelter and water, and in the desert margins of southern [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the warm plains give way to arid country. The [geography](/content/geography) of each region shapes the character of the Salawa communities, but in all cases the Salawa seek the same qualities of openness, warmth, and the freedom of movement that their nomadic way of life demands.

## Diet

The Salawa diet is a mixture of hunted and gathered foods, reflecting the resources of the desert and the nomadic character of their way of life. Meat is the staple, provided by the hunting of the gazelles, hares, and other desert game that share their habitat, and the Salawa are formidable trackers and patient hunters, capable of pursuing their quarry across the open desert for many miles. The hunt is a central activity of Salawa life, both as a source of food and as a test of skill and endurance, and the Salawa hunting traditions are among the most developed of any people.

Plant foods are gathered from the desert, including the fruits and seeds of the hardy shrubs and grasses, and the roots that can be dug from the sand after rain. The Salawa knowledge of the edible plants of the desert is extensive, encompassing not only what may be eaten but also what may be used for medicine and material. Water is the most precious resource, and the Salawa are expert in the finding of it, knowing the hidden springs and the seasonal watercourses that are invisible to those who do not know the desert. They carry water in skins and clay vessels, and they are able to sustain themselves on far less water than any other people, a gift of their arid-adapted physiology.

## Ecology

Salawa occupy the niche of a medium-bodied nomadic predator and forager in the desert ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is modest, for their numbers are small and their movements are spread across a wide territory, and they take from the desert only what they need. The Salawa regard the desert as their charge, and their traditions of stewardship are directed toward the maintenance of the water sources and the careful management of game, ensuring that the desert can sustain both the Salawa and the other creatures that depend upon it.

Salawa share the desert with [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the relationship between the two peoples is one of cautious respect and occasional trade. The human nomads of the Kharesh and the Salawa travel different routes and pursue different game, and their paths cross without significant conflict. [Orcs](/content/species/sapient/orcs) are present in the wilder reaches of the desert, and the Salawa regard them with wariness, for the orcs are competitors for the same scarce resources.
