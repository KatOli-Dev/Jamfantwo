## Shaped by the Mountains

The origins of dwarvenkind are a matter of learned dispute, though the dwarves themselves care little for the question. Dwarves are plainly a people fashioned by the mountains: their bodies are suited to the deep places of the earth in a way that no other people's are. Whether the first dwarves were shaped by the stone itself, as their oldest songs maintain, or came from some other stock and were changed over long ages by the mountains they chose to inhabit, no naturalist can say with certainty. The deep caves of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) have yielded bones of a taller, more slender folk beneath the oldest dwarven workings, and these may represent an ancestral form, though the evidence is scant.

Scholars of other species have noted that dwarven bodies are consistent across widely separated communities, whether in Morvind or [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), as though the mountains themselves impose the same form upon all who dwell within them. [Human](/content/species/sapient/humans) and [elven](/content/species/sapient/elves) naturalists have speculated that the same cause, whatever it may be, has produced the dwarven form independently in separate ranges, but this too remains unproven.

## Dense Bones and Subterranean Senses

Dwarves are a short and broadly built people, standing several inches below the average human but comparable in weight owing to their compact and muscular frames. Their bones are remarkably thick and dense, heavier by far than those of a human of similar size, and their ribcages are broad and deep, housing lungs and a heart well suited to the thin air of high altitudes. This density of bone and muscle gives them great physical strength but also makes them poor swimmers, for they are heavy in water and quick to sink, a fact that has shaped their preference for stone over sea.

Dwarven eyes are unusually large for the size of the skull and are furnished with a reflective membrane, such that they gleam in dim light like the eyes of a cat or a wolf. This grants them excellent vision in darkness and in the low light of caves and mine workings, though they are pained by bright sunlight and tend to squint or shade their eyes when abroad on the surface. Their hearing is keen, particularly in the lower tones that carry through stone, and a dwarf can often detect the ringing of a pick or the footsteps of a companion through many fathoms of rock. Their sense of smell is likewise well developed, and experienced dwarves can detect by scent the presence of ore veins, dangerous air, and the approach of other creatures through tunnels.

Dwarven hands are broad and strong, with short, thick fingers that are nonetheless capable of great precision. The skin is thick and rough, and dwarven hair, where it grows, is coarse and dark, most commonly black, brown, or deep red. Dwarves of both sexes grow beards, and the fullness of a beard is considered a sure sign of physical maturity.

## Slow Maturation and Long Vigour

Dwarves are long-lived, though not so long-lived as [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves), with a typical lifespan of one hundred and fifty to two hundred years. They grow to maturity slowly, not reaching their full strength and stature until their late twenties, and they remain hale and vigorous well into their second century. Dwarven women carry their young for some fourteen months before giving birth, longer than is the case among humans, and births are nearly always single. Infant mortality is low in settled communities, owing in part to the sheltered nature of dwarven dwellings, where the dangers of weather and predator are largely absent.

Dwarven births are infrequent, and populations grow only slowly even when food is plentiful and conditions are favourable. This slow increase is balanced by the low rate of death among adults, and dwarven populations tend to remain steady over long periods. A typical dwarven community is weighted toward older individuals, for the long-lived and the slow-breeding produce a people in which elders outnumber the young.

## Mountain Ranges of Morvind and Aethelar

Dwarves are found chiefly in the mountain ranges of Morvind and the interior highlands of Aethelar, where the geography provides the subterranean and high-altitude environments to which they are best suited. Their settlements are carved into the living rock, making use of natural cave systems and the veins of mineral that run through the mountains. The greatest concentration of dwarven population is in Morvind, where vast underground networks offer stable temperatures, refuge from the weather above, and access to the ores and stone that dwarven life requires.

Dwarves tolerate cold, dry air and dim light well, but they fare poorly in hot, humid, or low-lying places. They are rarely encountered in the lowland forests of Aethelar or the warm coasts of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), except as temporary visitors. In the temperate uplands of Morvind, smaller numbers have taken to surface life in sheltered valleys, though even these maintain dwellings beneath the ground for shelter and for the extraction of stone and ore.

## Fungi, Grains, and Highland Fare

The dwarven diet draws heavily on what the underground and the highlands can provide. Fungi, cultivated in dark chambers, form a staple, supplemented by hardy root vegetables, grains grown in terraced upland fields, and the flesh of mountain goats and sheep. Dwarven digestion is robust and well suited to a hearty, calorie-rich diet, reflecting the demands of life in cold environments and the heavy labour of mining and smithing. Fermentation is widely practised, both for the preservation of food and for the brewing of strong drink, for which dwarves are known across every continent.

## Excavators and Stone-Shapers

In their mountain and subterranean habitats, dwarves occupy a singular niche, for no other people alters the stone of the earth so thoroughly. Their excavations create new passages and chambers that serve as habitat for other living things, both the fungi they cultivate and the various creatures of the dark that find shelter in dwarven workings. The movement of air and water through a dwarven settlement can change the conditions of surrounding caves in ways that support or disturb the life within them.

Dwarves share their upland habitats with a range of other creatures. In the highlands of Aethelar and the interior of Morvind, their ranges overlap with those of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), with humans generally occupying the lowlands and dwarves the higher ground. Where dwarves and [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) encounter one another, the two peoples tend to separate by elevation, with dwarves favouring the rocky heights and elves the wooded valleys below. For an account of dwarven attitudes toward other peoples, see [Prejudices](/content/culture/prejudices). For a broader overview of the world's peoples, see [Population](/content/population).
