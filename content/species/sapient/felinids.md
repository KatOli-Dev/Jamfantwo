## Cat-Headed Hunters of the Grasslands

Felinids are a cat-headed people found in the grasslands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the savannas of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). Their form combines a humanoid body with the head and senses of a large feline, shaped by open country and the pursuit of prey. Naturalists have proposed a kinship with [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) on the basis of shared animal-headed form and preference for warm, open terrain, though the differences in build and habit are considerable. Others have argued for a connection to [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs), pointing to the shared grassland habitat and predatory nature of both peoples, but the comparison of form does not support it. Felinid oral traditions speak of their having emerged from the tall grass of the first savanna, and they regard the open plains as their rightful home.

Felinids have inhabited the grasslands and savannas of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands is recorded in the oldest records of the settled peoples. They have coexisted with the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the central grasslands for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the overlap in their range and the difference in their needs.

## Feline Senses and Ambush Build

Felinids are a medium-sized people, standing comparable in height to [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), with a lean and agile frame built for speed, balance, and the sudden explosive effort of a predator. The head is that of a large cat, with a broad muzzle, sharp teeth, and forward-facing eyes that grant excellent binocular vision. The ears are large and mobile, capable of swivelling independently toward the source of a sound, and they are covered in short fur ranging from tawny to spotted and striped patterns. The body is humanoid from the neck down, with lean musculature and long limbs, and the hands are strong and capable, with sharp, retractable claws used for climbing, fighting, and manipulating prey.

The felinid body is superbly adapted to grassland and savanna environments. Their vision is the keenest of any people in bright light, and their eyes are adapted to detect movement at great distance across the open plains, serving them in both hunting and the avoidance of danger. Their hearing is acute, capable of detecting the faintest rustle in the tall grass, and their sense of smell is well developed, though less so than that of the canine-headed peoples. The felinid sense of balance is extraordinary, and they are capable of feats of agility and coordination that no other people can match, landing on their feet from falls that would injure a human and moving through broken ground with fluid ease.

Felinid skin is covered in short, fine fur ranging from tawny gold to reddish-brown, often marked with spots or stripes that provide camouflage in the dappled light of the grassland. Their fur provides protection against the sun and the elements and is well suited to the warm climates they prefer. Felinids are uncomfortable in cold and fare poorly in temperate and subarctic regions, as their bodies are adapted to warmth and lack the insulation of northern peoples.

## Litters and Rapid Maturation

Felinids are short to moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of fifty to eighty years under favourable conditions. They mature quickly, reaching full stature by their mid-teens, and their powers begin to decline after the fourth decade. Felinid women carry their young for approximately seven months before giving birth, and births are frequently multiple, with two or three young being common. The young are small and helpless at birth and require several years of care before they are capable of fending for themselves, though they grow quickly and reach adolescence within a decade.

Felinid populations grow steadily when prey is abundant and the grasslands are at peace. The frequency of multiple births ensures that populations can recover quickly from losses. The high rate of reproduction is balanced by a relatively high mortality rate among the young, as the grassland environment is demanding and the predators that share the felinid range are a constant threat to the inexperienced. The adults of a felinid community are those who have survived the dangers of youth, and their numbers tend to remain stable over long periods once maturity is reached.

## Warm Grasslands and Savannas

Felinids are found chiefly in the grasslands of Aethelar and the savannas of Sahrana, where the open terrain and warm climate suit their way of life. They favour the tall grass and scattered woodlands of the plains, where cover is available for ambush and prey is abundant. Their settlements are typically small and semi-permanent, established in sheltered locations that offer good views of the surrounding country, and a community may relocate in response to the movement of game or the changing of the seasons.

In Aethelar, felinids share the central grasslands with [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs), with the centaurs favouring the open grass and the felinids the edges and scattered woodlands. In Sahrana, felinids are found in the savannas of the interior and the warm plains of the southern coast. They are absent from forests, mountains, and cold regions, as their bodies are adapted to warmth and open ground. The geography of each region determines the character of felinid communities, but in all cases they seek the same qualities of openness, warmth, and the availability of prey.

## Ambush Predation of the Plains

The felinid diet is dominated by meat. Felinids are formidable hunters of the grassland and savanna environments, preying upon the large and small mammals of the plains, including antelope, hares, and the young of larger grazing animals. Their hunting is characterised by stealth, patience, and the sudden burst of speed that is the hallmark of the feline predator. Felinids are ambush hunters, using the cover of tall grass to approach their quarry before launching a swift and decisive attack, and their claws and teeth are well suited to killing and consuming prey.

Plant foods form a minor part of the felinid diet, though felinids will eat grasses and herbs opportunistically. Felinids do not practise agriculture, and their relationship with food is entirely that of the predator, as their digestion is specialised for a diet of meat and poorly suited to processing large quantities of plant material. Water is obtained from streams and pools of the grassland, and felinids can sustain long periods without drinking, obtaining much of their moisture from the flesh of their prey.

## Regulators of Grassland Prey

Felinids occupy the niche of a medium-bodied ambush predator in grassland and savanna ecosystems. Their impact on their surroundings is significant where they are present in numbers, as their hunting regulates the populations of grazing animals and smaller mammals of the plains. The felinids and the grassland ecosystem exist in a balance maintained over centuries, the felinids taking what they need and the plains sustaining both them and the herds upon which they prey.

Felinids share the grasslands with [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs), and the relationship between the two peoples is one of cautious respect and occasional competition for game. The centaurs, as nomadic pastoralists and hunters, and the felinids, as ambush predators, occupy overlapping but distinct niches, and their paths cross without significant conflict. [Humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) encounter felinids at the margins of the settled grasslands, and the relationship is generally wary, as the felinid predator and the human settler have little in common and much to be cautious of.
