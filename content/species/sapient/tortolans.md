## Origins

The origins of the tortolans are a subject of considerable interest to naturalists, for the tortolan body is unlike that of any other sapient people in its most distinctive feature: the possession of a great shell that covers the back and sides, a natural armour that is the thickest and most robust of any people. Tortolans are a tortoise-like people, found on the warm coasts and islands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) and the southern reaches of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and their bodies are adapted to a slow, deliberate life in a warm climate. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between tortolans and [cancrids](/content/species/sapient/cancrids), citing the hard shell shared by both peoples and their preference for the coastal margin, but the tortolan shell is an internal structure grown from bone, while the cancrid shell is an external exoskeleton of chitin, and the differences in form and habitat are too great for the comparison to be sustained. Others have argued for a connection to [lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians), pointing to the reptilian form and the warm climate shared by both, but the tortolan body is too different in build and in pace of life for the comparison to be sustained. The tortolans themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their having emerged from the sand of the first beach, and they regard the warm coasts as their rightful home.

What is certain is that tortolans have inhabited the warm coasts and islands of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these lands is attested in the oldest records of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) city-states. The tortolans and the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the Sahrani coast have coexisted for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the overlap in their range and the difference in their pace of life.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Tortolans are a medium-sized people, standing shorter than [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) but taller than [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), with a broad, heavy frame that is dominated by the great shell that covers the back and sides. The shell is a dome of bone, covered in horny plates called scutes, and it ranges in colour from dark brown to olive, green, and patterns of yellow and black. The shell is the thickest and most robust natural armour of any sapient people, and it is capable of turning aside blows that would wound any other people, for the bone is dense and the scutes are hard and resistant to both impact and penetration. The shell is an integral part of the tortolan skeleton, grown from the ribs and the spine, and it cannot be removed without killing the individual.

The tortolan head is reptilian, with a broad, rounded snout, a wide mouth, and large, dark eyes that are set forward and adapted to both bright light and the dim conditions of dawn and dusk. Their skin is thick and leathery, ranging from dark green to brown and grey, and it is covered in a pattern of scales that provides additional protection against abrasion and the elements. Their limbs are thick and strong, built for bearing the great weight of the shell and the body, and their hands and feet are broad and calloused, with thick, blunt nails that are used for digging and the working of materials. The tortolan body is not built for speed, and their movements are the slowest and most deliberate of any people, for the great weight of the shell and the construction of the limbs favour stability over agility.

Tortolan senses are adequate and well suited to their way of life. Their vision is keen in bright light and adapted to the warm, sunlit environments they prefer, though they are less capable in low light than the nocturnal peoples. Their hearing is competent, and their sense of smell is moderate, adequate for the detection of food and the reading of the environment. The tortolan body is not specialised by sense but by resilience, for the capacity to endure hardship, to resist injury, and to persist through difficult conditions is the quality that sets the tortolans apart from all other peoples.

## Life Cycle

Tortolans are the longest-lived of the sapient peoples, with a lifespan that is estimated at two hundred to four hundred years, though reliable data are difficult to obtain, for the tortolans outlive the naturalists who study them, and their own accounts of their age are not always consistent. They grow to maturity very slowly, not reaching their full size and strength until their fortieth or fiftieth year, and they remain vigorous for centuries, their powers declining only gradually and late in life. Tortolan women carry their young for approximately fourteen months before laying a clutch of three to six eggs in a nest dug into the warm sand of the coast, and the eggs are incubated by the heat of the sun for a period of four to five months before hatching.

The hatchlings are small and soft-shelled, and they are vulnerable to predation during the first years of life, before the shell has fully hardened and the young tortolan has grown large enough to defend itself. The mortality rate among the young is high, but those who survive to adulthood are extraordinarily long-lived and resilient, and the tortolan population is weighted heavily toward older individuals, for the long-lived and the slow-breeding produce a people in which the elders far outnumber the young. The slow pace of tortolan life is reflected in every aspect of their biology, from the patient growth of the young to the gradual accumulation of size and strength over centuries, and a tortolan community is among the most stable and unchanging of any people.

## Habitat and Distribution

Tortolans are found chiefly on the warm coasts and islands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where the climate is hot and the sun is strong, and the sandy shores provide the nesting sites that their reproductive cycle requires. They favour the sheltered bays, the quiet coves, and the warm beaches where the sand is soft and the water is shallow, and their settlements are typically built near the shore, combining structures of stone and earth with the natural shelter of the coastal terrain. The [geography](/content/geography) of each coast determines the distribution of tortolan populations, and the tortolans are found only where the warmth and the sun are sufficient to sustain their cold-blooded metabolism.

Smaller populations of tortolans are found on the warm coasts of southern [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the climate is mild enough to support their way of life, and on the islands of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), where the volcanic heat and the warm currents provide a suitable environment. Tortolans are absent from the cold regions, the mountains, and the interior, for their bodies are adapted to warmth and they become sluggish and vulnerable when the temperature drops. The tortolan range is the most restricted of any people to the warm coastal regions, and the tortolans are the most thermally dependent of all the sapient peoples.

## Diet

The tortolan diet is dominated by plant foods, and tortolans are browsing and grazing herbivores who make use of the varied vegetation of the warm coast. Seagrasses, algae, and the tender shoots of the coastal shrubs form the staple, gathered from the shore and the shallow water by the patient and methodical foraging that suits the deliberate pace of tortolan life. Fruits, berries, and the pads of the coastal cacti are eaten when they are available, and the tortolan diet is supplemented by the occasional small animal or fish that is caught and consumed without particular effort.

Tortolans do not practise agriculture, for their slow metabolism and their long lifespan make the rapid growth of crops unnecessary, and their relationship with food is that of the patient browser, taking what the coast provides at the pace that the coast provides it. Their digestion is slow and efficient, capable of extracting nourishment from the tough and fibrous plants that other peoples cannot use, and the tortolan metabolism is so efficient that a tortolan can sustain long periods without food, drawing on the reserves of a body that is built to endure scarcity as well as plenty. The tortolan approach to food is unhurried and unwasteful, for the tortolans eat what they need and no more, and the slow pace of their digestion matches the slow pace of their lives.

## Ecology

Tortolans occupy the niche of a medium-bodied, slow-moving herbivore in the warm coastal ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on the vegetation of the coast is modest, for their numbers are small and their consumption is slow, but their grazing maintains the coastal plant communities in a state of equilibrium that is beneficial to the overall health of the shore. The tortolan presence on a coast is generally associated with a well-maintained and diverse plant community, and the tortolans are a stable and enduring component of the coastal ecosystem.

Tortolans share the warm coasts with [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga) in portions of their range, and the three peoples coexist in a relationship that is generally peaceful, for their needs are different and their habitats overlap only at the margins. The humans tend the land and the cities, the naga the water and the rivers, and the tortolans the quiet shore, and each people occupies a different niche within the same coastal environment. Where tortolans encounter [cancrids](/content/species/sapient/cancrids) on the rocky portions of the coast, the relationship is one of mutual indifference, for the two peoples are too different in pace and in habit to interact in any meaningful way.
