## The Question of Aquatic Origins

The origins of the merfolk are among the most debated questions in the natural history of the known world. The merfolk are an aquatic people inhabiting the coastal waters and shallow seas around all three continents, their bodies adapted to a life in water in a manner that admits no comparison with any land-dwelling people. Whether the merfolk descended from a land-dwelling ancestor that returned to the sea, or whether they originated in the waters and never dwelt upon the land at all, is a question no naturalist has answered with certainty. The merfolk preserve oral traditions that speak of their having always been creatures of the sea, but these traditions are known to other peoples only through the accounts of the few merfolk who have engaged in sustained contact with land-dwellers.

The merfolk are an ancient people, well established in the coastal waters of the world long before the earliest records of the land-dwelling peoples. Their populations are widely distributed and show considerable variation between regions, suggesting a long history of separation and local adaptation comparable in its way to the variation observed among the elven peoples of different continents.

## Tail, Fins, and Aquatic Senses

Merfolk are adapted to an aquatic life in every feature of their bodies. From the waist up, they resemble a human or elven form in general outline, with a head, shoulders, and arms, though the skin is smooth and faintly scaled, ranging from pale blue-green to deep slate. From the waist down, the body tapers into a powerful tail, flattened and finned, that propels them through the water with a grace and speed no land-dwelling creature can match. The transition between upper and lower body is seamless, and the whole form is hydrodynamic, offering little resistance to the flow of water.

Merfolk eyes are large and dark, adapted to the shifting light of shallows and the dimmer conditions of deeper water. Vision is acute both above and below the surface, and they see in conditions that would leave a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) effectively blind. Their hearing is specialised for the underwater environment, where sound travels far more efficiently than in air, and they communicate over considerable distances using a system of vocalisations that carry through water. Their sense of smell is likewise adapted to the aquatic environment, and merfolk detect the chemical signatures of water from different regions, an ability that serves them in navigation and in the identification of suitable habitats.

Merfolk are warm-blooded despite their aquatic life, and their bodies are insulated by a layer of fat beneath the skin that retains heat in cold water. They breathe air for short periods and may be seen at the surface in calm weather, but they cannot survive out of water for more than a few hours, and their bodies are poorly suited to movement on land.

## Slow Birth and Community Care

Merfolk live an estimated one hundred to one hundred and fifty years, though reliable data are difficult to obtain, as merfolk do not keep written records and their accounts of their own longevity are not easily verified by other peoples. They mature slowly, reaching physical adulthood in the second or third decade of life, and retain their vigour well into their later years. Merfolk women carry their young for approximately one year before giving birth, and births are nearly always single. The infant is able to swim from the moment of birth and is cared for by the mother and the community jointly.

Merfolk populations are small relative to the extent of their habitat and grow slowly, as births are infrequent and the young require many years of care before becoming fully independent. This slow growth makes merfolk communities vulnerable to decline when deaths among adults rise through disease, predation, or conflict, and the recovery of a diminished population may take many decades.

## Coastal Waters of All Three Continents

Merfolk are found in coastal waters and shallow seas around all three continents, from the temperate shores of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) to the cold coastlines of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the warm waters of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). They favour waters rich in marine life, where the seabed is varied and offers both food and shelter, and their settlements are typically located in reefs, shallows, and submerged rock formations that provide protection from predators and the open ocean. Merfolk are rarely found in the deep waters beyond the continental shelf, as their bodies and communities are adapted to the shallows where light penetrates and life is abundant.

The merfolk of different regions are distinct in their appearance and customs, shaped by the particular conditions of the waters they inhabit. The merfolk of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) are more colourful and numerous, reflecting the warmth and richness of their waters. Those of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) are larger and more heavily built, adapted to the cold and rough conditions of the western coast. The merfolk of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) are the most accustomed to contact with land-dwellers, as the busy traffic of that sea brings them into frequent proximity with human mariners.

## Fish, Shellfish, and Seaweed Cultivation

The merfolk diet consists chiefly of fish, shellfish, and other marine creatures, gathered and hunted from the waters around their settlements. Merfolk are skilled hunters, working together to drive fish into shallows or nets, and they are accomplished gatherers, collecting shellfish, shelled creatures, and edible seaweeds from the seabed. Their digestion is adapted to a diet of raw marine foods, and they do not cook, as fire is unavailable in their environment. Some merfolk communities cultivate beds of edible seaweed and shellfish near their settlements, a form of aquaculture that provides a reliable supplement to the catch.

## Stewards of the Reef and Shallows

Merfolk occupy the niche of a sapient predator and steward in coastal ecosystems. Their presence in an area is generally associated with healthy and abundant marine life, as they tend the waters they inhabit with a care that recalls the stewardship of the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) over their forests. They cull overabundant species, protect breeding grounds, and maintain the balance of the reef and shallows communities upon which they depend.

The relationship between merfolk and land-dwelling peoples is limited by the fundamental separation of their habitats. Where contact occurs, it is generally at the shoreline or through the medium of fishing and sailing. [Humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and merfolk encounter one another most frequently, as human fishermen and sailors work the same coastal waters that the merfolk inhabit, and a cautious but generally peaceful coexistence has developed in many regions, with each people maintaining a respectful distance from the other.
