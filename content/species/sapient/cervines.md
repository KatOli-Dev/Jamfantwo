## Origins

The origins of the cervines are bound up with the deep forests of the world, for the cervine body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by woodland, by the need for swift flight, and by the demands of a large, alert body in a complex environment. Cervines are a deer-headed people, found in the deep forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the wooded valleys of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and their form combines a humanoid body with the head, the ears, and the antlers of a deer. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between cervines and [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs), citing the partly animal form and the shared preference for open movement, but the cervines are smaller, solitary, and adapted to the forest rather than the plain. Others have argued for a connection to [satyrs](/content/species/sapient/satyrs), pointing to the woodland habitat and the partly caprine or cervine form shared by both, but the cervine body is too different in build and in habit for the comparison to be sustained. The cervines themselves are a reclusive people, and they have offered no account of their origins to outsiders, preferring the quiet of the deep forest to the company of other peoples.

What is certain is that cervines have inhabited the deep forests of the known world for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these woodlands is attested in the oldest elven records. The cervines and the [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) have coexisted in the forests for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is shaped by the overlap in their range and the mutual preference for solitude.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Cervines are a medium to large people, standing comparable in height to [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) or slightly taller, with a lean and graceful frame that is built for speed, agility, and the swift navigation of dense woodland. The head is that of a deer, with a long, narrow muzzle, large ears that are capable of swivelling independently, and large, dark eyes that are set on the sides of the head, granting a near-complete field of vision that is the hallmark of a prey species. The body is humanoid from the neck down, with lean musculature and long limbs, and the whole form is built for the sudden, explosive sprint that is the cervine's primary means of escape.

The most distinctive feature of the cervine is the antlers, which are borne by both sexes and which are shed and regrown each year. The antlers are branched and complex, growing from two points on the brow, and their size and complexity increase with the age of the individual. The antlers are used for defence, for the display of maturity, and for the clearing of branches and undergrowth during movement through the forest, and the annual shedding and regrowth of the antlers is a significant event in the cervine life cycle, marking the passage of the seasons and the age of the individual.

Cervine senses are sharp and specialised for the forest environment. Their vision is keen, with a wide field of view that grants awareness of the surroundings in nearly all directions, and their eyes are adapted to the dappled light of the forest floor, performing well in both the shade of the canopy and the brightness of the clearings. Their hearing is acute, and their large, mobile ears can detect the faintest sound in the stillness of the deep forest, from the movement of a predator at a distance to the breaking of a twig underfoot. Their sense of smell is well developed, and the cervine can detect the approach of other creatures by scent alone, a gift that serves them well in the dense vegetation where sight and sound are often obscured.

Cervine skin is covered in a short, fine coat of fur that ranges from reddish-brown to grey and dark brown, changing with the seasons to a thicker, darker pelage in the winter. Their legs are long and their feet are narrow, and they are capable of moving through the forest with a speed and a silence that is remarkable for a people of their size, their steps placed with a care that avoids the breaking of twigs and the rustling of leaves.

## Life Cycle

Cervines are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of sixty to ninety years under favourable conditions. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late teens, though the antlers continue to grow in size and complexity throughout life. Cervine women carry their young for approximately seven months before giving birth, and births are usually single, though twins are not uncommon. The infant is well-developed, capable of standing and walking within hours of birth, and it grows quickly, reaching adolescence within a decade.

Cervine populations grow slowly, for births are infrequent and the deep forest can support only a limited number of such large, solitary individuals. The cervine is the most solitary of the woodland peoples, and a typical cervine may go for long periods without encountering another of its kind, save during the seasonal gatherings that bring the cervines together for the brief periods of mating and the exchange of information. The slow pace of cervine life and the low rate of reproduction are balanced by the longevity of adults and by the effectiveness of their flight response, for a healthy adult cervine has few natural predators and is extraordinarily difficult to catch.

## Habitat and Distribution

Cervines are found chiefly in the deep forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), where the trees are ancient and the undergrowth is thick, and the forest provides the cover and the isolation that their way of life demands. They favour the oldest and most remote portions of the woodland, where the canopy is dense and the forest floor is shadowed, and the human and elven settlements are far away. Their settlements are typically solitary dwellings, built in secluded clearings or beneath the largest trees, and a cervine may range across a wide territory rather than remaining in a single location.

Smaller populations of cervines are found in the wooded valleys of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), where the sheltered lower slopes of the interior ranges provide a suitable if harsher environment, and in the wooded foothills of the [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) northern coast, where the warm, humid forests support a different variety of cervine. Cervines are absent from the open plains, the mountains, and the cold steppes, for their bodies are adapted to the forest and the cover that the trees provide. The [geography](/content//geography) of each region determines the distribution of cervine populations, and the cervine range is restricted to the deep forest where the conditions suit them.

## Diet

The cervine diet is dominated by plant foods, and cervines are browsing herbivores who make use of the varied vegetation of the forest. Leaves, twigs, bark, and the tender shoots of shrubs and trees form the staple, gathered from the understorey and the lower canopy by the cervine's long neck and its ability to reach high branches. Grasses, herbs, and forbs are grazed from the forest floor in clearings and along the edges of streams, and the cervine diet shifts with the seasons, from the fresh growth of spring to the bark and the evergreen needles of winter.

Fungi, fruits, and berries are gathered when they are available, and the cervine knowledge of the edible plants of the forest is extensive, encompassing not only what may be eaten but also what may be used for medicine and material. Cervines do not practise agriculture, and their relationship with food is that of the browser and the grazer, for their digestion is specialised for a diet of plant material and their teeth are adapted for the grinding of coarse vegetable matter. The cervine metabolism is efficient, capable of extracting nourishment from the tough and fibrous plants of the winter forest, when the more tender foods of the warmer months are unavailable.

## Ecology

Cervines occupy the niche of a medium-bodied browsing herbivore in the forest ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on the vegetation of the forest is considerable where they are present in numbers, for the cervines are among the primary consumers of the understorey and the lower canopy, and their browsing shapes the composition and the structure of the vegetation. The cervine presence in a forest is generally associated with a diverse and well-maintained understorey, for the cervines encourage the growth of some species through their browsing and discourage the spread of others.

Cervines share the deep forests with [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) and [corvids](/content/species/sapient/corvids), and the three peoples coexist in a relationship that is generally peaceful, for their needs are different and their habitats overlap only at the margins. The elves tend the canopy, the corvids the high branches, and the cervines the understorey and the floor, and each people occupies a different vertical niche within the same woodland. Where cervines encounter [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines) at the edge of the forest and the steppe, the relationship is one of predator and prey, for the lupines are among the few peoples who can match the cervine's speed and endurance, and the cervines are wary of the open ground where the lupines hunt.
