The gorse is the spiny and invasive shrub of the heathlands of the Ashen Heath and the Stormfall Coast, its dense and impenetrable thickets controlled by the regulated burning of the dry season under the supervision of the fire managers of the Northern Marches.

## The Gorse of the Heathlands

The gorse that grows in the heathlands of the [Ashen Heath](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/ashen-heath) and the uplands of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) is a shrub that is invasive in the regions where the land has been cleared of the forest and the grazing of the animals is not sufficient to control the growth. The gorse is a spiny and dense shrub that forms the thickets that are impenetrable to the animals and the people, and the gorse is a serious problem in the regions where the land has been abandoned and the gorse has taken over.

The control of the gorse is achieved by the burning of the thickets in the dry season, when the gorse is dry and the fire removes the above-ground growth and kills the seeds that are lying in the soil. The burning of the gorse is a dangerous operation that must be conducted under the supervision of the experienced fire managers, and the burning of the heathlands is regulated by the authorities of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) and the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael).

## Physical Characteristics

The gorse is a perennial shrub that grows to a height of six to ten feet, the stems being green and angular and armed with the sharp spines that make the shrub impenetrable to the animals and the people. The leaves of the gorse are reduced to the small scales and the spines that cover the stems, the reduction of the leaves being an adaptation to the dry and the exposed conditions of the heathlands where the moisture is scarce and the wind is constant. The flowers of the gorse are bright yellow and pea-like, the flowers appearing in the early spring and the late autumn and giving the gorse a cheerful and golden appearance that belies the hostile nature of the shrub.

The root system of the gorse is deep and extensive, the roots penetrating the soil to the depth that allows the shrub to survive the droughts that kill the shallow-rooted plants. The gorse is also a plant that is adapted to the poor and the acid soils of the heathlands, the roots being associated with the bacteria that fix the nitrogen from the air and that enable the gorse to thrive in the soils that are too poor for the other plants. The combination of the deep roots and the nitrogen fixation makes the gorse a formidable competitor in the heathland environment, and the shrub is able to colonise and dominate the land that has been disturbed or abandoned.

## Habitat and the Heathland Ecology

The gorse is a plant of the heathlands and the uplands, the shrub being found in the regions where the soil is acid and the rainfall is moderate and the exposure to the wind is severe. The gorse is a characteristic species of the [Ashen Heath](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/ashen-heath) and the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast), the shrub being one of the plants that define the heathland ecosystem and that give the heathlands their distinctive and the austere character. The gorse is also found in the upland regions of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands), where the shrub colonises the land that has been cleared of the forest by the grazing and the burning that the farmers have practiced over the centuries.

The gorse is a plant that is adapted to the fire, the seeds of the gorse being hardened and resistant to the heat, and the fire that destroys the above-ground growth of the shrub also stimulates the germination of the seeds that lie dormant in the soil. The gorse is therefore a plant that benefits from the burning that is intended to control it, the shrub returning after the fire with the renewed vigour that the fire has stimulated. The fire managers of the Northern Marches understand the relationship between the gorse and the fire, and the burning is conducted in the manner that minimises the regeneration of the gorse and that promotes the growth of the grasses and the heather that are the desired species of the heathland.

## Control and the Regulated Burning

The control of the gorse is a perennial problem for the farmers and the landowners of the heathland regions, the shrub being too aggressive and too resilient to be eliminated by the ordinary methods of the weed control. The cutting of the gorse is difficult and dangerous, the spines of the shrub inflicting the painful wounds that fester in the damp conditions, and the cut stumps of the gorse resprouting from the roots with the vigour that defeats the repeated cutting. The grazing of the [sheep](/content/species/fauna/sheep) and the [goats](/content/species/fauna/goats) can control the gorse if the stocking is heavy and the grazing is sustained, but the gorse that is left ungrazed for a single season will regrow and reclaim the land that has been cleared.

The regulated burning of the heathlands is the method that has been developed by the fire managers of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) to control the gorse and to maintain the open character of the heathlands. The burning is conducted in the late summer or the early autumn, when the gorse is dry and the weather is settled, the fire being set in the strips that are controlled by the firebreaks that the fire managers have prepared. The burning is regulated by the authorities of the Northern Marches and the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), and the farmers who wish to burn the gorse on their land must obtain the permission of the fire managers and must conduct the burning in the manner that the regulations prescribe.

## Uses of the Gorse

The gorse is not without its uses, the shrub being valued in the rural economy of the heathland regions for the several purposes that the plant can serve. The gorse is a plant that burns hot and bright, the dry wood and the spines being a useful fuel for the heating of the cottages and the firing of the kilns in the regions where the other fuel is scarce. The gorse that is cut in the summer and dried in the sun is a fuel that is used by the poorer people of the heathlands, and the cutting of the gorse for the fuel is a task that is performed by the landless labourers who have the access to the common land where the gorse grows.

The gorse is also used as a hedge plant in the regions where the shrub is native, the gorse being planted in the hedges that mark the boundaries of the fields and the gardens. The spiny and the dense growth of the gorse makes the shrub an effective barrier that the livestock do not penetrate, and the gorse hedge is a characteristic feature of the field boundaries of the [Ashen Heath](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/ashen-heath) and the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast). The gorse hedge requires the regular trimming to prevent the shrub from spreading into the fields, and the trimming of the gorse hedges is a winter task that the farm labourers perform with the heavy gloves and the long-handled shears that the spines of the gorse demand.

The flowers of the gorse are used in the preparation of the dye that produces a yellow colour on the wool and the linen, the flowers being gathered in the spring and the autumn and boiled in the water that extracts the pigment. The yellow of the gorse is not as bright or as permanent as the yellow of the [Spices and Dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) or the other dye plants, but the gorse yellow is a common colour in the heathland regions where the other dye plants are not available, and the cloth that is dyed with the gorse is the cloth of the rural people of the northern heathlands.

## Cultural Significance and the Heathland Landscape

The gorse is a plant that is woven into the culture and the folklore of the heathland regions, the bright yellow flowers and the spiny stems being the features of the landscape that the people of the heathlands recognise as the signs of their home. The gorse is mentioned in the songs and the poems of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) as a symbol of the resilience and the stubbornness of the heathland people, who have learned to live with the shrub that the other regions regard as a weed and a nuisance. The saying that the gorse is always in flower is a reference to the habit of the shrub of producing the flowers in the seasons when the other plants are dormant, and the saying is used by the people of the heathlands to express the continuity and the persistence of their way of life.

The gorse is also associated with the folklore of the heathlands, the thickets of the gorse being the places where the spirits and the creatures of the heath are said to dwell. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the heathland margins regard the gorse as a plant that is under the protection of the old powers of the land, and the cutting of the gorse in the wrong season or the burning of the gorse without the proper rituals is believed to bring the misfortune that the old powers inflict on those who violate the customs. The fire managers of the Northern Marches are careful to observe the traditional rituals that accompany the burning of the heathlands, and the burning is conducted with the respect for the customs that the heathland people have maintained for the generations.
