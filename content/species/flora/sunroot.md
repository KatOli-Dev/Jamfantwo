The sunroot is the rarest and the most expensive of the Sahrani spices, harvested from the deepest parts of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert by the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides who know the locations of the hidden groves where the sunroot plant grows.

## Sunroot and the Hidden Groves of the Kharesh

The sunroot is a tuber that grows beneath the sand, sending up a single leaf to the surface that is visible only to those who know what to look for. The sunroot is harvested in the spring, when the plant has stored its energy for the coming season, and the tuber is dried and ground into a powder that has a flavour unlike any other spice in the known world. The sunroot is used in the preparation of the most elaborate dishes of the Sahrani cuisine, and the sunroot is also valued for the medicinal properties that the healers of the interior attribute to it.

The harvest of the sunroot is a ritual that is conducted by the salawa guides in accordance with the traditions of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), and the locations of the groves are passed from generation to generation within the families of the guides. The sunroot is never cultivated, for the plant does not grow in the gardens and the plantations, and the supply of the spice is limited to what the desert yields naturally.

## Physical Characteristics

The sunroot plant is a low and unremarkable species that produces a single leaf above the surface of the sand, the leaf being a narrow blade that is the colour of the desert sand and that is virtually indistinguishable from the surrounding ground. The leaf of the sunroot grows to a length of four to six inches and persists for only a few weeks in the early spring before the heat of the advancing season causes the leaf to wither and the plant to retreat into the dormancy of the underground tuber. The root system of the sunroot is a single tuber that is buried at a depth of eight to twelve inches below the surface, the tuber being a rounded and firm structure that measures two to three inches in the diameter in the mature specimens.

The tuber of the sunroot is covered with a thin and papery skin that is the colour of the dark sand, and the flesh of the tuber is a deep amber that deepens to a rich brown when the tuber is dried. The aroma of the fresh tuber is subtle and earthy, the scent being released only when the skin is broken, and the dried and ground powder has a fragrance that is complex and persistent, the notes of the flavour being described by the Sahrani cooks as a combination of the warmth of the [black pepper](/content/species/flora/black-pepper), the depth of the [saffron](/content/species/flora/saffron), and a quality that the cooks describe as distinct from any other spice traded on the Sahrani coast.

## The Salawa and the Harvest

The [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides are the custodians of the knowledge of the sunroot groves, and the locations of the groves are among the most closely guarded secrets of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) interior. The knowledge of the groves is passed from the parent to the child within the families of the guides, and the transmission of the knowledge is accompanied by the rituals and the obligations that bind the guide to the desert and to the traditions of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes). The guide who knows the location of a grove is responsible for the harvest of the grove and for the protection of the grove from the exploitation that would deplete the population of the plants.

The harvest of the sunroot is conducted in the early spring when the leaf of the plant is visible above the sand, the guide travelling to the grove in the cool of the morning and digging the tubers with the careful and the selective method that the tradition prescribes. Only the mature tubers are harvested, the younger tubers being left in the ground to grow and to produce the plants of the future years, and the harvest of each grove is limited to the quantity that the tradition permits. The harvested tubers are carried in the leather pouches that the guides use for the transport, the pouches being lined with the cloth that prevents the damage to the skin of the tubers and the loss of the aroma that the broken skin would release.

## Culinary Uses

The sunroot is used in the cuisine of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast and the interior in the dishes that are prepared for the most significant occasions and the most discerning palates, the flavour of the sunroot being the element that distinguishes the dishes that are served at the feasts of the wealthy and the powerful. The powder of the dried sunroot is added to the stews and the sauces that are the foundation of the Sahrani cuisine, the flavour of the sunroot being released slowly in the cooking and pervading the dish with the warmth and the complexity that the spice imparts.

The [sahrani caravan feast](/content/culture/sahrani-caravan-feast) is the occasion on which the sunroot is most prominently featured, the spice being used in the preparation of the central dish that is the highlight of the feast. The caravan leaders who commission the feasts provide the sunroot from the supplies that the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides deliver, and the presence of the sunroot at the feast is the mark of the status and the wealth of the host. The sunroot is also used in the preparation of the bread that is baked for the feasts, the powder being kneaded into the dough to produce a bread that is fragrant and golden.

## Medicinal Properties

The sunroot is valued by the healers of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) interior for the medicinal properties that the tuber possesses, the powder of the dried sunroot being administered as a remedy for the ailments of the digestion and the disorders of the spirit. The healers of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) prepare the sunroot as an infusion that is drunk for the relief of the stomach pains and the intestinal disorders that the desert diet and the hardships of the travel produce, and the infusion is also used as a tonic that is believed to strengthen the body and to restore the energy that the desert environment depletes.

The sunroot is also used in the [healing and restoration](/content/magic/healing-and-restoration) practices of the mages who study the properties of the Sahrani plants, the spice being incorporated into the preparations that the healers use in the treatment of the conditions that the conventional remedies cannot address. The mages of the [Southern Reach Academy](/content/government/local/southern-reach-academy-council) have conducted the investigations of the medicinal properties of the sunroot, and the findings of the investigations suggest that the sunroot contains the compounds that are active against the inflammation and the infections that the desert environment produces.

## Trade and Value

The trade in the sunroot is the most exclusive of the [spices and dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) trade of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) interior, and the price of the sunroot in the markets of the coastal cities is measured in the gold pieces per ounce that only the wealthiest of the merchants and the households can afford. The sunroot that reaches the markets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and the other coastal cities is sold through the agents of the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides, and the trade is conducted under the regulations that the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) have established for the commerce in the rare products of the desert.

The [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) is the route by which the sunroot is transported from the interior to the markets of the coast, and the caravans that carry the sunroot are among the most heavily guarded of the desert trade. The value of the sunroot that a single caravan carries may exceed the value of all the other goods that the caravan transports, and the protection of the sunroot is the responsibility that the caravan leaders take with the greatest seriousness. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the spice merchants in the coastal cities are the principal buyers of the sunroot, and the guilds distribute the sunroot to the cooks and the healers who are the end users of the spice.
