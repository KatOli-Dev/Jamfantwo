## The Date Palm of the Kharesh Oases

The date palm is the most important of the oasis plants of the Kharesh desert, providing food, shade, and building material for the people of the oasis. The date palm requires abundant water and intense heat, and the palms that grow in the oases are among the most productive of the fruit trees in the known world. The oases that punctuate the Kharesh are the sites of the most intensive plant cultivation in the desert, and the date palm is the foundation of the settled life of the desert communities.

The fruit of the date palm is the staple food of the oasis dwellers, and the dates are eaten fresh or dried for storage and transport. The trade in dates along the caravan routes of the Kharesh sustains the connections between the desert communities and the markets of the coast, and the date palm is valued not only for its fruit but also for the shade that it provides beneath its spreading fronds and for the wood of its trunk that is used in the construction of the buildings of the oasis settlements.

## Physical Characteristics

The date palm is a tall and stately tree that reaches a height of sixty to eighty feet in the mature specimens, the trunk being a single unbranched column that rises from the ground and is marked with the diamond-shaped scars of the frond bases that have fallen from the tree over the years of the growth. The trunk of the date palm is rough and fibrous in the texture, the surface being composed of the persistent bases of the old fronds that the tree retains for the protection of the inner tissue against the extreme temperatures of the desert. The diameter of the trunk is narrow for the height of the tree, rarely exceeding two feet even in the largest of the specimens, and the trunk tapers gently from the base to the crown.

The crown of the date palm is a massive cluster of the fronds that spread outward and downward from the apex of the trunk, the fronds being the pinnate leaves that measure up to twenty feet in the length and that are composed of the numerous narrow leaflets arranged along the central rachis. The fronds are a grey-green in the colour, and the surface of the fronds is covered with a waxy coating that reduces the loss of the water through the transpiration, an adaptation that allows the date palm to survive in the intense heat of the desert environment. The fronds of the date palm are evergreen and persist for the several years before they wither and drop, and the crown of the mature palm contains the fronds of the several years of the growth.

The date palm is a dioecious species that produces the separate male and the female individuals, and the flowers of the date palm are produced in the large and the branching clusters that emerge from the axils of the fronds in the spring. The flowers of the male tree produce the abundant pollen that is carried by the wind and by the hand of the cultivator to the flowers of the female trees, and the pollination that is assisted by the cultivators is the essential step in the production of the fruit. The fruit of the date palm is the [date](/content/species/flora/date), a drupe that develops from the pollinated flower and that matures over the course of the summer months, the fruit reaching the full size and the sugar content in the late summer and the early autumn.

## Habitat and Distribution

The date palm is a tree of the arid and the hot regions of the known world, and the date palm is found in the oases of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert and in the irrigated gardens of the river valleys of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The date palm requires the combination of the abundant water for the roots and the intense heat and the dry air for the crown, conditions that are met in the oases where the underground water rises to the surface and in the irrigated lands where the canals bring the water from the rivers to the plantations. The date palm will not produce the fruit in the regions where the humidity is high or where the summer temperatures are insufficient for the ripening of the fruit.

The distribution of the date palm is a map of the settled life of the desert, and the oases that support the date palm groves are the sites of the permanent settlements that punctuate the routes of the caravans across the Kharesh. The date palm is not found in the deep desert where the water is too far below the surface for the roots to reach, and the date palm is absent from the coastal regions where the humidity and the sea breezes interfere with the ripening of the fruit. The date palm is cultivated in the oases of the interior and in the gardens of the settlements that lie along the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way), and the cultivation of the date palm at the oases is the activity that makes the travel across the desert possible.

## Cultivation and Propagation

The cultivation of the date palm in the oases of the Kharesh is a practice that has been refined over the centuries by the oasis communities, and the methods of the cultivation are passed from the one generation to the next in the traditions of the desert peoples. The date palm is propagated by the offshoots that the mature trees produce at the base of the trunk, and the offshoots are removed from the parent tree and planted in the prepared pits that are dug to the depth of the water table. The young palms require the abundant water during the first years of the growth, and the oasis cultivators irrigate the young trees with the water that is drawn from the wells and the channels that distribute the water through the gardens.

The pollination of the date palms is the task that the cultivators perform by the hand, and the cultivators collect the pollen from the male trees and dust the flowers of the female trees with the pollen that they have gathered. The cultivators who possess the skill in the pollination are valued members of the oasis communities, and the knowledge of the timing and the technique of the pollination is a body of the expertise that distinguishes the skilled cultivator from the amateur. The date palms that are not pollinated by the hand produce the fruit that is smaller and less sweet than the fruit of the pollinated trees, and the hand pollination is the practice that ensures the quality and the quantity of the harvest.

The date palm requires the regular irrigation throughout the growing season, and the water that the date palm consumes is drawn from the underground sources that feed the oases. The cultivators maintain the systems of the channels and the basins that distribute the water to the individual trees, and the management of the water is the constant concern of the oasis communities, for the supply of the water is limited and the demand of the date palms is high. The date palm is a tree that thrives in the heat and the sun, and the cultivation of the date palm is the activity that defines the rhythm of the life in the oasis, the cycle of the cultivation being the framework that organises the activities of the community through the year.

## The Harvest and Preparation of the Dates

The harvest of the dates is the principal event of the agricultural year in the oases of the Kharesh, and the harvest begins in the late summer when the fruit has ripened on the tree and the sugar content has reached the peak. The dates are harvested in the several stages of the maturity, and the harvesters climb the trunks of the palms with the aid of the ropes and the harnesses that the oasis peoples have developed for the purpose. The bunches of the dates are cut from the crown with the knives that the harvesters carry, and the bunches are lowered to the ground where the dates are separated from the stems and sorted by the quality and the ripeness.

The dates are consumed fresh during the harvest season, and the fresh dates are a food that provides the energy and the nutrition that sustain the oasis communities through the period of the harvest. The dates that are not consumed fresh are dried for the storage and the transport, and the drying of the dates is performed by the exposure of the fruit to the sun and the dry air of the desert. The dried dates are packed into the sacks and the baskets that are stored in the cool and the dry chambers of the oasis buildings, and the dried dates provide the food that sustains the communities through the months when the fresh fruit is not available.

The dates are also processed into the products that extend the use of the fruit beyond the simple consumption, and the oasis communities produce the date syrup and the date paste that are used in the cooking and the baking of the desert cuisine. The date syrup is produced by the boiling of the dates in the water and the reduction of the liquid to a thick and a sweet concentrate, and the syrup is used as a sweetener in the dishes and the drinks of the desert peoples. The date paste is produced by the grinding of the dried dates into a dense and a nutritious mass that can be stored for the long periods and that is carried by the travellers on the journeys across the desert.

## The Date Palm as a Source of Materials

The date palm provides the materials that the oasis communities use for the construction, the craft, and the daily life in addition to the fruit that is the primary product of the tree. The wood of the trunk of the date palm is used in the construction of the buildings of the oasis settlements, and the trunks of the old palms that have ceased to produce the fruit are cut and milled into the beams and the planks that the builders use. The wood of the date palm is fibrous and the grain is coarse, but the wood is durable in the dry climate of the desert and the supply of the wood from the old palms is sufficient for the modest requirements of the oasis construction.

The fronds of the date palm are used in the weaving of the mats, the baskets, and the screens that the oasis households employ, and the fronds are also used in the construction of the roofs and the walls of the simpler buildings of the oasis settlements. The fibres of the fronds are stripped and twisted into the ropes and the cords that the oasis communities use for the many purposes of the binding and the tying, and the leaflets of the fronds are used in the production of the brushes and the brooms that the daily life of the oasis requires.

The midribs of the date palm fronds are used as the fuel for the fires of the oasis households, and the midribs burn with a hot and a steady flame that is suitable for the cooking and the heating. The fruit stalks and the other parts of the inflorescence that are not consumed in the harvest are also used as the fuel, and the efficient use of every part of the date palm is the characteristic of the oasis economy that reflects the scarcity of the resources in the desert environment.

## Cultural and Religious Significance

The date palm occupies a place of the central importance in the culture and the religion of the desert peoples, and the date palm is the symbol of the life and the sustenance in the environment that is otherwise barren and hostile to the human settlement. The [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) regard the date palm as a gift from the spirits of the water and the earth, and the planting of a date palm is an act that is accompanied by the rituals and the prayers that invoke the blessing of the spirits on the tree and on the community that it will sustain.

The date palm is celebrated in the poetry and the song of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) tradition, and the poets of the desert describe the date palm as the queen of the oasis and the mother of the people, the tree that gives the food and the shelter and the materials that make the life in the desert possible. The date palm is also a symbol of the hospitality and the generosity in the culture of the desert, for the shade of the palm is the place where the stranger is welcomed and the traveller is refreshed, and the gift of the dates is the gesture of the welcome that the host extends to the guest.

The date palm is associated with the cycles of the fertility and the renewal in the religion of the desert peoples, and the festivals that mark the seasons of the agricultural year are centred on the events of the date palm cultivation. The pollination of the palms in the spring, the ripening of the fruit in the summer, and the harvest of the dates in the autumn are the occasions for the celebrations and the rituals that express the gratitude of the communities for the bounty of the trees. The date palm is also a symbol of the endurance and the permanence in the culture of the desert, for the palm that is planted by a person will outlive the person and will provide for the generations that follow, and the planting of a date palm is an act of the hope that extends beyond the lifetime of the planter.

## Relation to Other Desert Species

The date palm is the keystone species of the oasis ecosystem, and the presence of the date palm creates the conditions that support the community of the plants and the animals that inhabit the oases. The shade of the date palm fronds reduces the temperature and the evaporation in the garden that the palm dominates, and the shade allows the cultivation of the more delicate plants such as the [citrus](/content/species/flora/citrus) trees and the vegetables that would not survive in the full exposure of the desert sun. The gardens of the oases are planted in the several layers beneath the canopy of the date palms, and the arrangement of the plants in the vertical space is the adaptation that maximises the productivity of the limited area of the oasis.

The [acacia](/content/species/flora/acacia) and the date palm are the two tree species that define the oasis communities of the Kharesh, and the two trees are often found growing together in the oases where the water is close to the surface. The acacia provides the hard wood and the [Acacia](/content/species/flora/acacia) gum that the scribes of the [Reading Cloisters](/content/religion/cloisters-of-reading) use, and the date palm provides the fruit and the shade and the materials, and the combination of the two trees is the foundation of the settled life of the oasis communities.

The date palm also supports the animals of the oasis ecosystem, and the fruit of the date palm is eaten by the birds and the small mammals that inhabit the gardens, and the fronds of the palm provide the shelter and the nesting sites for the birds and the insects. The [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa), the lizard-folk who serve as the guides for the caravans of the Kharesh, are among the principal consumers and the traders of the dates, and the salawa communities of the desert have their own traditions of the date palm cultivation and the date preparation that are similar to the traditions of the human oasis communities.

## The Date Palm in the Economy of the Desert

The trade in the dates and the date products is the principal economic activity that connects the oases to the markets of the coast and to the other regions of the known world. The dates are packed into the sacks of the camel-hair cloth and transported along the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) to the markets of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the coastal cities of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The trade in the dates is the source of the income that the oasis communities use to purchase the goods that cannot be produced in the desert, including the metal tools, the cloth, and the luxury items that the merchants of the coast offer in exchange for the dates.

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the date merchants regulate the trade in the dates and maintain the standards of the quality that govern the commerce in the fruit, and the guilds also arrange the transport of the dates through the caravans that ply the trade routes of the desert. The prices of the dates fluctuate with the season and with the abundance of the harvest, and the years of the abundant harvest produce the low prices that allow the dates to be exported to the distant markets of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), where the dates are a luxury that is consumed by the wealthy households and the institutions.

The date palm is also the source of the employment and the livelihood for the many people who work in the cultivation, the harvest, the processing, and the trade of the dates, and the economy of the oases is built upon the foundation that the date palm provides. The [prices and livelihood](/content/economy/prices-and-livelihood) of the oasis communities are tied to the success of the date harvest, and the failure of the harvest through the drought or the disease is a calamity that threatens the survival of the communities that depend on the date palm for their living. The date palm is not merely a tree but the basis of a civilisation that has adapted to the harsh conditions of the desert and that has built a way of life around the cultivation and the use of the date.
