## The Snow Fungus of the Morvind Highlands

The snow fungus is the most characteristic fungus of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) highlands, appearing at the edge of the melting snow in the weeks after the spring thaw. The snow fungus is a small and white fungus that grows in clusters on the ground, and the flesh of the fungus is crisp and watery. The snow fungus is edible, and the human and the dwarven communities of the region gather the fungus in the spring and cook it in the soups and the stews that sustain the households through the last weeks of winter.

## Physical Characteristics

The snow fungus is a member of the jelly fungi, a group of the species that produce the fruiting bodies of the gelatinous and the translucent texture that the snow fungus exhibits. The fruiting body of the snow fungus is a cluster of the irregular and the lobed structures that emerge from the ground or from the decaying wood that lies on the surface of the soil, and the individual lobes of the fungus are the small and the white or the pale cream in the colour and the texture is the firm and the jelly-like that trembles when the fungus is touched. The size of the cluster varies with the age and the conditions of the growth, and the clusters that are gathered by the highland communities range from the size of a thumb to the size of a fist in the mature specimens that have developed over the several weeks of the moist conditions that the melting snow provides.

The flesh of the snow fungus is crisp and the texture is the watery and the delicate, and the flavour of the raw fungus is mild and the slightly the earthy and the taste is the subtle that requires the cooking to bring out the qualities that the fungus possesses. The flesh of the snow fungus is translucent and the light passes through the flesh when the fungus is held up to the sun, and the translucence is the feature that distinguishes the snow fungus from the other species of the fungi that grow in the same habitats at the time of the spring thaw. The snow fungus does not have the distinct cap and the stem that the typical mushrooms possess, and the form of the fungus is the amorphous and the irregular that the jelly fungi exhibit.

The mycelium of the snow fungus is the network of the fine white threads that permeates the soil and the decaying wood of the highland forests, and the mycelium is the persistent form of the fungus that survives through the winter beneath the snow and the frozen ground. The fruiting bodies of the snow fungus appear only when the snow has melted and the soil has warmed to the temperature that triggers the formation of the fruiting bodies from the mycelium, and the fruiting bodies emerge at the very edge of the retreating snow where the soil is the wettest and the coolest at the same time. The association of the snow fungus with the melting snow is so strong that the fungus is seldom found at the distances of more than a few feet from the edge of the snow, and the fungus disappears from the ground when the snow has melted completely and the soil has dried from the saturation.

## Habitat and Distribution

The snow fungus is a species of the high elevations and the cold climates of the northern [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) continent, and the snow fungus is found throughout the highland forests and the alpine meadows of the regions that experience the prolonged snow cover through the winter months. The snow fungus is most abundant in the coniferous forests that are dominated by the [spruce](/content/species/flora/spruce) and the [pine](/content/species/flora/pine) trees, the fungus being the decomposer of the woody debris that accumulates on the forest floor and the fungus deriving the nutrition from the breakdown of the wood and the leaf litter that the forest produces. The snow fungus is also found in the birch and the aspen woodlands that occur at the margins of the coniferous forests, and the fungus is found in the areas of the open ground that are the sites of the former forest that has been cleared by the wind or the fire.

The distribution of the snow fungus extends from the lower slopes of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) mountains to the highest elevations that support the woody vegetation, and the snow fungus is one of the first of the organisms to appear in the spring at the elevations where the snow persists into the late spring and the early summer. The fungus is not found in the regions of the mild winters and the early springs that characterise the lowlands of Morvind, and the snow fungus is a species that is restricted to the environments where the winter snow cover is a feature of the climate and where the spring thaw provides the period of the cold moisture that the fungus requires for the fruiting.

The snow fungus is also found in the vicinity of the [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar) holds that are located in the high elevations of Morvind, and the dwarves gather the snow fungus from the forests that surround the holds in the spring. The dwarven communities have developed the knowledge of the locations and the timing of the fruiting of the snow fungus, and the dwarves incorporate the collection of the fungus into the seasonal round of the activities that the dwarven communities conduct in the mountains surrounding the holds.

## Seasonal Pattern and Life Cycle

The life cycle of the snow fungus is adapted to the seasonal pattern of the snow cover and the thaw that dominates the environment of the Morvind highlands, and the fungus has evolved the timing and the physiology that allow the species to exploit the brief period of the cold moisture that the melting snow provides. The mycelium of the snow fungus remains dormant through the winter months, the mycelium being the network of the dehydrated and the frozen threads that survive the temperatures that are well below the freezing point of the water. The mycelium is revived by the moisture that the melting snow releases into the soil, and the revival of the mycelium is the first stage in the production of the fruiting bodies that the fungus will send above the surface of the soil in the weeks that follow the thaw.

The fruiting of the snow fungus is triggered by the combination of the temperature and the moisture that occurs at the edge of the melting snow, the soil being at the temperature that is just above the freezing point and the soil being saturated with the meltwater that the fungus requires for the formation of the gelatinous fruiting bodies. The fruiting bodies appear in the clusters at the surface of the soil or on the woody debris that the mycelium is decomposing, and the fruiting bodies grow rapidly in the days that follow the emergence, the growth being the result of the rapid uptake of the water that the melting snow provides and the expansion of the cells of the fungal tissue to the size that the fruiting body achieves in the mature state.

The fruiting season of the snow fungus is brief, the fruiting bodies lasting only for the weeks between the appearance of the thaw and the drying of the soil that follows the complete melting of the snow. The fungus cannot fruit when the soil is dry, and the fruiting bodies that are produced at the edge of the snow wither and disappear within the days of the disappearance of the snow, the gelatinous tissue of the fungus being the structure that requires the constant moisture for the maintenance of the form and the function. The spores of the snow fungus are released from the fruiting bodies during the period of the fruiting, and the spores are dispersed by the wind and the water to the new sites where the mycelium may become established if the conditions of the moisture and the temperature and the substrate are favourable.

## Gathering and Harvesting

The gathering of the snow fungus is the activity that the human and the dwarven communities of the Morvind highlands conduct in the spring when the snow is melting and the fungus is appearing at the edge of the retreating snow. The gatherers walk the margins of the snow fields and the snow patches that remain in the forest after the general thaw has begun, and the gatherers search for the white clusters of the snow fungus that contrast with the brown of the forest floor and the white of the remaining snow. The fungus is difficult to see against the snow, and the experienced gatherers know the habit of the fungus to grow at the precise edge where the snow meets the bare ground, the fungus appearing as the fringe of the white against the dark and the dark against the white that the gatherers learn to recognise.

The gathering of the snow fungus is conducted with the care that the fragile nature of the fungus requires, and the clusters are lifted from the ground by the gentle pressure of the fingers that separates the fungus from the mycelium at the base without the damage to the fruiting body. The gathered fungus is placed in the basket or the sack that the gatherer carries, and the fungus is handled with the care that the delicate and the gelatinous texture demands. The snow fungus is best gathered in the morning when the texture is firm and the fungus has not been warmed by the sun of the day, and the fungus that is gathered in the afternoon when the temperature has risen is more likely to be damaged by the handling.

The knowledge of the locations and the conditions that favour the fruiting of the snow fungus is the knowledge that is passed from the generation to the generation in the communities of the Morvind highlands, and the children of the highland communities learn the identification and the gathering of the snow fungus from the parents and the grandparents who take the children on the gathering expeditions in the spring. The gathering of the snow fungus is the occasion for the family outings that mark the end of the winter and the beginning of the spring, and the gathering is the activity that the children remember as the first evidence of the return of the warmth and the life to the highlands after the long months of the snow.

## Culinary Uses and Preparation

The snow fungus is prepared by the methods that the communities of the Morvind highlands have developed for the cooking of the fungus, and the fungus is most commonly used in the soups and the stews that are the mainstay of the highland diet in the spring when the fresh foods are scarce and the stores of the winter are running low. The flavour of the snow fungus is mild and the fungus absorbs the flavours of the broth and the other ingredients that are cooked with the fungus, and the snow fungus is valued more for the texture that the fungus adds to the dish than for the flavour of the fungus itself.

The snow fungus is also prepared by the sauteing in the butter or the fat of the mountain sheep and the goats that the highland communities herd, and the sauteed fungus is served as the dish that accompanies the meat or the bread of the meal. The sauteing produces the concentration of the flavour of the fungus that the raw fungus lacks, and the sauteed snow fungus has the rich and the savoury flavour that complements the heavier foods of the highland diet. The snow fungus is also eaten raw in the salads of the spring greens that the highland communities gather from the meadows, and the raw snow fungus adds the crisp and the watery texture to the salad that is the refreshment after the months of the cooked and the preserved foods of the winter.

The snow fungus is not preserved for the storage because the gelatinous texture of the fungus does not survive the drying or the salting that the highland communities use for the preservation of the other foods, and the snow fungus is a food that is consumed fresh in the season of the appearance and that is not available at the other times of the year. The limitation of the snow fungus to the fresh consumption is one of the features that makes the fungus a special and the anticipated food of the spring, and the return of the snow fungus is one of the signs of the change of the season that the highland communities welcome each year as the evidence of the end of the winter and the beginning of the time of the growth and the plenty.

The snow fungus provides the nutrition that is the valuable addition to the diet of the highland communities in the spring when the fresh foods are scarce and the diet has been limited to the preserved and the stored foods through the months of the winter. The snow fungus contains the protein and the carbohydrates and the minerals that are the components of the balanced diet, and the snow fungus is a food that is easily digested and that provides the nutrients that the body has been lacking through the winter when the fresh vegetables and the greens have not been available. The highland communities regard the snow fungus as a food that cleanses the body of the heaviness of the winter diet and that prepares the body for the work of the spring, and the consumption of the snow fungus is the tradition that is associated with the renewal and the purification of the spring.

The snow fungus is also a source of the water that the highland communities value in the spring when the water of the landscape is still cold and the consumption of the cold water is a discomfort, and the fungus provides the hydration that the body requires in a form that is more pleasant than the drinking of the cold meltwater. The snow fungus has been noted by the healers of the highland communities and the apothecaries and the herbalists for the property of the fungus to soothe the stomach and the digestive ailments that the change of the diet from the winter stores to the spring foods can cause. The snow fungus is recommended by the healers as a food that is gentle on the stomach and that eases the transition from the rich and the heavy foods of the winter to the lighter and the fresher foods of the spring and the summer.

## Cultural and Social Significance

The snow fungus occupies the place of the particular affection in the culture of the Morvind highland communities, and the fungus is the symbol of the return of the spring and the end of the winter that the communities have endured through the months of the snow and the cold. The sight of the snow fungus at the edge of the melting snow is the event that the children of the highland communities anticipate and that the children race to be the first to report to the families, and the first gathering of the snow fungus of the season is the occasion for the small celebration that marks the turning of the season.

The snow fungus is associated with the [folk omens and superstitions](/content/mythology/folk-omens-and-superstitions) of the Morvind highlands, and the abundance or the scarcity of the snow fungus in the spring is read as the omen for the weather and the prosperity of the year to come. The season of the abundant snow fungus is the omen of the good summer and the abundant harvest, and the season of the scarce snow fungus is the warning of the difficult summer and the poor harvest. The [dwarven ancestor worship](/content/religion/dwarven-ancestor-worship) includes the offering of the first gathering of the snow fungus at the shrines of the ancestors, the offering being the gesture of the gratitude for the return of the spring and the petition for the continued favour of the ancestors through the year of the work.

The snow fungus is also a food that is shared in the traditions of the mutual support that the highland communities practise, and the families that have gathered the more of the fungus than they need give the surplus to the families that have gathered the less, the giving being the expression of the community and the solidarity that the highland life requires. The snow fungus is a food that is free for the gathering and that does not require the cultivation or the purchase, and the snow fungus is one of the foods that the poorest of the families can obtain for the sustenance, the fungus being the gift of the forest and the season that is available to all who know the places and the times of the gathering.
