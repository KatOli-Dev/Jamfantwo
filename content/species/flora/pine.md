The pine is the dominant conifer of Morvind and the Northern Marches, yielding the highest volume of timber in the known world for the houses, the barns, and the ship masts of the northern lands.

## Pine and the Softwood Timber of the North

The pine is the dominant timber tree of the northern regions, growing in the extensive forests of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) of Aethelar. The pine is a coniferous tree that grows more quickly than the hardwoods of the southern forests, reaching timber size in thirty to forty years, and the volume of pine timber that is harvested each year exceeds the volume of all the hardwood timbers combined.

The wood of the pine is light and strong, easy to work with hand tools or with the saws of the timber mills, and it is used in the construction of the houses, the barns, and the workshops of the northern settlements. The pine is also the principal source of the timber that is used for the masts and the spars of ships, for the lightness of the wood and the straightness of the grain make it ideal for these purposes.

The pine forests of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) are managed by the dwarven communities who have developed techniques for harvesting the timber without damaging the fragile soils of the mountain slopes. The pine of the uplands is slower-growing than the pine of the lowlands, but the wood is denser and more resinous, and the timber that it produces is more resistant to decay. The dwarven foresters mark the trees that are to be felled and supervise the harvest to ensure that the forest regenerates successfully.

## Physical Characteristics of the Pine

The pine is a coniferous evergreen, the leaves being needle-shaped and arranged in bundles that are called fascicles, each fascicle containing two, three, or five needles according to the species. The needles of the pine are coated with a waxy resin that protects them from the desiccation of the winter winds and from the damage of the freezing temperatures, and the needles persist on the tree for several years before they are shed, the shedding being a gradual process that is not noticeable to the casual observer.

The pine produces its seeds in the cones, the woody structures that are the reproductive organs of the tree, and the cones of the pine are the most conspicuous of the features of the tree in the autumn, when the cones open to release the seeds that are dispersed by the wind. The seeds of the pine are called the pine nuts, and the nuts of the larger species are edible, being gathered by the forest dwellers and used in the preparation of the dishes that are a feature of the winter cuisine of the northern regions.

The bark of the pine is thick and furrowed, the outer layers being shed in the plates that protect the tree from the fire and the insects, and the bark of the older trees is a deep reddish brown that is the source of the tannins that are used in the tanning of the leather. The root system of the pine is shallow and spreading, the roots extending far from the trunk to anchor the tree in the thin soils of the mountain slopes, and the shallow roots make the pine susceptible to the windthrow in the exposed sites.

## The Pine Forests of the Northern Regions

The pine forests of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) are the most extensive of the coniferous forests in the known world, the trees covering the slopes of the mountains from the valley floors to the tree line at the upper elevations. The pine is the dominant tree of these forests, the other conifers being the [spruce](/content/species/flora/spruce) and the fir, and the pine is the first of the trees to colonise the areas that have been cleared by the fire or the harvest, the seeds of the pine being dispersed by the wind to the open ground where the seedlings establish themselves in the full sun.

The pine forests of the lowlands are different in character from the pine forests of the uplands, the lowland pine growing more quickly and reaching a greater size in the deeper soils and the milder climate of the valleys. The lowland pine is harvested for the construction timber that is used in the building of the houses and the barns of the settlements, and the lowland pine is also the source of the pulp that is used in the production of the paper that is manufactured in the mills of the northern cities.

## The Resin and the Tar of the Pine

The pine is a source of the resin that is extracted from the trunk by the tapping of the tree, the resin being collected in the cups that are fixed to the trunk beneath the cuts that are made in the bark. The resin of the pine is called the turpentine, and the turpentine is used in the preparation of the varnishes and the paints that are used by the woodworkers and the artists of the three continents. The resin is also distilled to produce the pitch that is used in the caulking of the ships, the pitch being applied to the seams of the hull to make them watertight.

The tar of the pine is produced by the burning of the pine wood in the kilns that are built in the forests, the kilns being sealed to exclude the air and the wood being heated to the temperature at which the tar is driven from the wood. The tar of the pine is used in the preservation of the wood that is exposed to the moisture and the decay, and the fence posts and the pilings that are treated with the tar last for decades longer than the untreated wood. The tar is also used in the preparation of the medicinal ointments that are applied to the skin diseases and the wounds of the animals.

## The Dwarven Foresters of the Uplands

The dwarven communities of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) have developed the techniques for the management of the pine forests that are the most sophisticated in the known world, the dwarven foresters having studied the growth of the pine over the centuries and having developed the practices that ensure the continuous production of the timber. The dwarven foresters mark the trees that are to be felled and supervise the harvest to ensure that the forest regenerates successfully, and the foresters also control the burning of the underbrush that reduces the risk of the fires that would destroy the standing timber.

The pine of the uplands is slower-growing than the pine of the lowlands, but the wood is denser and more resinous, and the timber that it produces is more resistant to decay. The upland pine is used in the construction of the structures that must endure for generations, the beams and the posts of the dwarven holds being cut from the upland pine that has been aged for decades in the drying sheds. The dwarven foresters also select the trees that produce the straightest and the clearest timber, the selection being based on the shape of the trunk and the grain that is revealed in the test cuts.

## Trade and the Pine Timber Market

The trade in pine timber is the largest of the timber trades in the known world, and the pine that is harvested in the forests of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) is transported to every region of the three continents. The pine is floated down the rivers in the rafts that are assembled in the forests, the rafts being guided by the raftsmen who navigate the currents and the rapids of the rivers to the sawmills that are located at the falls and the narrows. The sawn timber is then loaded onto the carts and the ships that carry it to the markets of the south, where the pine is traded for the goods that the north cannot produce.

The pine timber is also exported in the form of the masts and the spars that are used in the construction of the ships, the tall and straight pines of the uplands being the source of the masts that are required by the large vessels that carry the trade of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) and the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea). The trade in the masts is a specialised business that is controlled by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the mast merchants, who select the trees in the forests and supervise the cutting and the transport of the masts to the shipyards.
