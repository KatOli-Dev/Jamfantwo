## Weld

Weld is a plant that grows wild in the meadows and the waste places of Aethelar, and its leaves and its flowers produce a yellow dye that is used in the dyeing of the cloth of the northern settlements.

The weld is harvested in the summer when the plant is in flower, and the harvested plant material is dried and stored for the use of the dyers. The yellow that the weld produces is a clear and bright colour that is used in the dyeing of the cloth that is worn by the young women of the northern settlements and in the production of the tapestries and the embroideries that decorate the houses of the wealthy. The weld is also used in combination with the woad and the madder to produce the green and the orange colours that the dyers require for the patterns that they create.

## Physical Characteristics

Weld is a biennial plant that in the first year produces a rosette of the leaves that lie close to the ground, the leaves being wrinkled and dark green and being divided into the lobes that give the leaf the distinctive shape that the botanists use to identify the species. In the second year the plant sends up the flowering stem that reaches a height of two to four feet, the stem being branched and bearing the numerous small flowers that are arranged in the loose spikes that extend from the upper portions of the stem. The flowers of the weld are a pale yellow and are inconspicuous, the flower being no more than a quarter of an inch in the diameter and being visited by the [honey bees](/content/species/fauna/honey-bee) and the other insects that pollinate the plant.

The leaves and the stems of the weld are the source of the dye that the plant produces, the tissue containing the pigment that is extracted by the boiling of the plant material in the water. The concentration of the pigment is highest in the plant that is harvested at the time of the flowering, the dye being the product that the dyers obtain from the plant that is gathered at the peak of the growth. The seeds of the weld are small and numerous and are produced in the pods that follow the flowers, the seeds being the means by which the plant reproduces and the colonies of the weld are maintained in the meadows and the waste places where the plant grows.

## Habitat and Distribution

Weld grows in the meadows and the waste places of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), the plant being found in the regions where the soil is dry and the lime is present in the substrate that the plant prefers. The weld is most abundant in the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) and the other northern regions of Aethelar where the conditions are suitable for the growth of the species, the plant being a common sight in the meadows and the roadsides of the northern districts. The weld is also found in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) and the other central regions of the continent where the soil and the climate are favourable.

The weld is a plant that is gathered from the wild rather than cultivated in the fields, the plant being sufficiently abundant in the regions where it grows that the cultivation is not necessary for the supply that the dyers require. The gathering of the weld is the activity that the rural communities conduct in the summer when the plant is in flower, the gatherers cutting the stems and the flowers and bundling the material for the drying that the preservation of the dye requires. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the dyers regulate the gathering of the weld and the trade in the dried material, the regulation being the means of maintaining the supply and the quality that the dyeing industry demands.

## Dye Production

The dye that is produced from the weld is the yellow that is the brightest and the clearest of the natural yellows that the dyers of the known world use, the colour being the shade that is produced by the extraction of the pigment from the plant material and the application of the dye to the cloth that is treated with the mordant. The mordant that is used with the weld is the alum that the dyers obtain from the [mining and metals](/content/economy/mining-and-metals) trade, the alum being the agent that fixes the dye to the fibre and that determines the fastness and the brightness of the colour that the dye produces.

The dye bath is prepared by the boiling of the dried weld in the water, the liquid that results being the yellow solution that the cloth is immersed in for the dyeing. The cloth that is dyed with the weld takes on a yellow that ranges from the pale lemon of the diluted bath to the deep and the golden yellow of the concentrated and the repeated dyeing, the depth of the colour being the result that the dyer achieves by the control of the concentration and the duration of the dyeing. The [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) industry of the northern settlements uses the weld yellow as the colour that is worn by the young women of the communities, the yellow being the colour that is associated with the youth and the freshness that the colour represents.

## Combination with Other Dyes

The weld is used in combination with the [woad](/content/species/flora/woad) and the [madder](/content/species/flora/madder) to produce the green and the orange colours that the dyers require for the patterns and the designs that they create. The green is produced by the dyeing of the cloth with the woad blue followed by the dyeing with the weld yellow, the blue and the yellow combining in the fibre to produce the green that is the colour of the [clover](/content/species/flora/clover) meadows and the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) forests that the designers seek to reproduce. The orange is produced by the dyeing of the cloth with the madder red followed by the dyeing with the weld yellow, the red and the yellow combining to produce the orange that is the colour of the [saffron](/content/species/flora/saffron) and the [sunroot](/content/species/flora/sunroot) that the Sahrani trade provides.

The combinations of the weld with the other dyes are the techniques that the dyers of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) and the other regions of Aethelar have developed over the centuries of the practice, the techniques being the knowledge that the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the dyers maintain and transmit to the apprentices who learn the craft. The combinations are the methods that the dyers use to extend the range of the colours that the natural dyes provide, the combinations being the means by which the palette of the natural dyeing is expanded beyond the primary colours that the individual dyes produce.

## Uses in Tapestry and Embroidery

The weld yellow is the colour that is used in the production of the tapestries and the embroideries that decorate the houses of the wealthy of the Aethelari and the [Morvindi](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) regions, the yellow being the colour that the designers use for the representation of the light and the gold that the tapestries depict. The [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) products that the weavers and the embroiderers produce are the goods that the [markets and fairs](/content/economy/markets-and-fairs) of the cities trade, the tapestries being among the most valuable of the textile products that the Aethelari craft produces.

The embroideries that use the weld yellow are the products of the [halfling](/content/species/sapient/halflings) and the [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) communities that specialise in the needlework that the decoration of the cloth requires, the embroiderers using the weld yellow thread for the flowers and the details that the designs include. The [halfling harvest tales](/content/mythology/halfling-harvest-tales) and the other cultural traditions of the Aethelari peoples include the imagery that the embroiderers reproduce in the yellow and the other colours that the natural dyes provide, the embroidery being the medium through which the stories and the symbols of the culture are expressed.

## Trade and Economics

The trade in the weld is the component of the [spices and dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) economy of the Aethelari regions, the dried weld being the commodity that the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the dyers and the [cartage and transport](/content/economy/cartage-and-transport) networks distribute from the regions where the plant is gathered to the centres where the dyeing is done. The [prices and livelihood](/content/economy/prices-and-livelihood) of the dyers and the gatherers are partly dependent on the trade in the weld, the plant being one of the resources that the dyeing industry requires.

The weld is also traded to the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast where the dyers use the weld in the combinations with the [indigo](/content/species/flora/indigo) and the [madder](/content/species/flora/madder) that the Sahrani dyeing traditions employ, the weld being the Aethelari dye that the Sahrani dyers incorporate into the palette of the colours that the Sahrani textile industry produces. The trade in the weld between the continents is the commerce that the [Sahrani Desert Trail](/content/location/route/trade/sahrani-desert-trail) and the sea routes carry, the weld being among the Aethelari products that the Sahrani markets demand.
