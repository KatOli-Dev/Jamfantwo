## The Wood Ear of the Heartwood

The second of the Heartwood fungi is the wood ear, a fungus that grows on the dead wood of the fallen trees and the rotting stumps of the forest. The wood ear is a jelly fungus that swells when it absorbs water, and the texture of the cooked fungus is crisp and chewy. The wood ear is used in the soups and the stews of the elven and the human communities that live near the forest, and the fungus is valued for the texture that it adds to the dishes of the autumn.

## Physical Characteristics

The wood ear is a jelly fungus that produces the fruiting body that is the ear-shaped structure from which the fungus takes its name, the body being a cupped and the convoluted form that attaches to the substrate by the narrow base and that spreads into the broad and the undulating surface that the growth produces. The fruiting body of the wood ear is a reddish-brown to the dark brown in the colour when the fungus is fresh and moist, the surface being the smooth and the slightly wrinkled texture that the jelly fungi display. The body of the wood ear is a gelatinous and the translucent tissue that is flexible when the moisture is present and that shrinks and becomes the hard and the brittle material when the fungus dries.

The wood ear is a fungus that grows on the dead wood of the broadleaf trees, the species being found on the fallen logs and the stumps of the [oak](/content/species/flora/oak) and the [beech](/content/species/flora/beech) and the other trees that the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) comprises. The fungus does not grow on the living trees and does not damage the wood that the living forest produces, the wood ear being the decomposer that breaks down the dead wood and returns the nutrients to the soil that the forest depends on.

## Habitat and Distribution

The wood ear is found in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) and the other forests of the Aethelari continent where the dead wood of the broadleaf trees is present, the fungus being the species that the woodland ecosystem produces in the conditions that the decomposition of the dead wood provides. The wood ear is most abundant in the mature and the undisturbed forests where the dead wood is allowed to remain on the forest floor and the decomposition that the fungus performs is the process that the ecology of the forest depends on. The [Brackenshield Wood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/brackenshield-wood) and the other old forests of Aethelar are the regions where the wood ear is most commonly gathered, the old forests being the habitats that the dead wood and the fungus produce.

The wood ear is also found in the forests of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the other northern continents where the conditions for the growth of the fungus are present, the wood ear being the species that the temperate forests of the known world produce. The [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and the other forested regions of the north are the habitats where the wood ear is gathered by the communities that live near the forests and that use the fungus in the cuisine that the northern traditions produce.

## Gathering and Harvest

The gathering of the wood ear is the activity that the [elven](/content/species/sapient/elves) and the [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) communities that live near the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) conduct in the autumn and the winter when the fungus is producing the fruiting bodies that the gatherers harvest. The wood ear is collected from the dead wood on the forest floor, the gatherers searching for the fungus on the fallen logs and the stumps where the wood ear grows, the gathering being the activity that the forest communities pursue in the season when the other forest products are not available. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the Heartwood are the most skilled of the gatherers of the wood ear, the elven knowledge of the forest including the locations where the fungus is most abundant and the conditions that the fruiting of the fungus requires.

The wood ear is dried for the storage and the transport, the drying being the process that reduces the moisture content of the fungus to the level that the preservation requires. The dried wood ear is the form in which the fungus is traded and stored, the dried fungus being the product that the [markets and fairs](/content/economy/markets-and-fairs) of the Aethelari regions sell. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the food merchants regulate the trade in the wood ear and the other fungi that the forest produces, the guilds maintaining the standards of the quality and the pricing that the commerce in the forest products requires.

## Culinary Uses

The wood ear is the fungus that the cuisine of the [elven](/content/species/sapient/elves) and the [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) communities of the Aethelari forests uses for the soups and the stews that the autumn and the winter diet includes, the fungus being the ingredient that the texture of the dishes is valued for. The dried wood ear is reconstituted by the soaking in the water before the use, the soaking being the process that restores the gelatinous and the flexible texture that the fresh fungus displays. The reconstituted wood ear is sliced and added to the soups and the stews that the forest communities prepare, the wood ear contributing the crisp and the chewy texture that the dishes are known for.

The wood ear is also used in the stir-fried dishes that the Aethelari and the Sahrani cuisines produce, the wood ear being the ingredient that the cooks add to the dishes for the texture and the colour that the fungus provides. The [feasting and dining](/content/culture/feasting-and-dining) traditions of the elven communities include the wood ear as the ingredient that is featured in the dishes that are served at the autumn festivals that celebrate the gathering of the forest products. The [sahrani caravan feast](/content/culture/sahrani-caravan-feast) and the other culinary traditions of the known world also include the wood ear in the dishes that the trade in the dried fungus has made available to the regions beyond the forests where the fungus is gathered.

## Ecological Role

The wood ear is a decomposer fungus that plays the role in the ecology of the forest that the breakdown of the dead wood and the recycling of the nutrients that the forest depends on requires, the fungus being the organism that the forest ecosystem uses to return the nutrients from the dead wood to the soil that the living trees depend on. The wood ear is the species that colonises the dead wood after the other decomposers have begun the breakdown, the wood ear being the fungus that completes the decomposition that the [crimson cap](/content/species/flora/crimson-cap) and the other fungi initiate.

The wood ear is also the food for the [Honey Bee](/content/species/fauna/honey-bee) and the other organisms that the dead wood supports, the fungus being the substrate that the larvae of the beetles and the other insects that inhabit the dead wood consume. The [Seabird](/content/species/fauna/seabird) and the other birds that feed on the insects of the dead wood are the predators that the wood ear supports through the chain of the relationships that the decomposition ecology comprises. The [elven nature veneration](/content/religion/elven-nature-veneration) includes the wood ear as the element of the forest that the elven reverence for the natural processes recognises, the fungus being the organism that the elven understanding of the forest values.
