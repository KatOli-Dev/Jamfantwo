## The Rice of Sahrana

Rice is the staple grain of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where it is cultivated in flooded paddies that cover the lower slopes and valley floors of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent). The cultivation of rice is the most labour-intensive of all grain farming, requiring the construction of terraces and irrigation channels, the transplanting of seedlings by hand, and the careful management of water levels throughout the growing season.

## The Terraced Paddies of the Green Crescent

The terraced paddies of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) are constructed on slopes that would otherwise be unsuitable for cultivation, the level terraces held in place by stone walls that have been built and maintained over generations. Water from the rivers of the interior is diverted into channels that feed the uppermost terraces, and the water flows from one terrace to the next through controlled outlets, depositing silt that replenishes the fertility of the soil. The rice that grows in these paddies is a long-grain variety that cooks into separate, fluffy grains that accompany the spiced dishes of [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) cuisine.

Two harvests of rice are possible in the warmer districts of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip), the first in early summer and the second in late autumn. The labour of planting and harvesting the rice is shared among the members of the farming communities, and the rice that is not consumed locally is exported to the markets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), where it is traded for the goods of Aethelar and Morvind.

## Physical Characteristics of the Rice Plant

The rice is an annual grass that grows to a height of two to four feet, the stems being erect and jointed, and the leaves being long and flat with the sheaths that wrap the stem at the nodes. The rice produces the flowers in the panicles that are borne at the top of the stem, the panicles being the branching clusters that carry the spikelets, and each spikelet containing the single grain that is the edible product of the plant. The grain of the rice is enclosed in the husk that is removed in the milling, the husk being the protective covering that surrounds the grain during the growth and the ripening.

The root of the rice is a fibrous system that spreads through the mud of the paddy, the roots being adapted to the conditions of the saturated soil and able to extract the oxygen from the air that is present in the water. The rice is a plant that is adapted to the conditions of the flooded field, the stems being able to grow through the water and the leaves being able to float on the surface, and the rice is one of the few grain crops that can be grown in the conditions of the standing water.

## Cultivation and the Management of the Water

The cultivation of the rice begins with the preparation of the seedbed, the seedbed being the small plot that is flooded and planted with the seed that is broadcast by the hand. The seedlings are grown in the seedbed for three or four weeks, the seedlings being transplanted to the paddies when they have reached the height of six to eight inches. The transplanting is the most labour-intensive of the operations of the rice cultivation, the seedlings being planted by the hand in the rows that are spaced to allow the plants to tiller and to spread, and the transplanting is the work that is shared by the whole community.

The management of the water in the paddies is the continuous task of the rice farmer, the water being maintained at the depth that is appropriate for the stage of the growth, the depth being shallow during the transplanting and deeper during the tillering and the flowering. The water is drained from the paddies before the harvest, the draining being the operation that allows the ground to dry and the rice to ripen, and the harvest is begun when the grain has turned golden and the panicles have begun to droop under the weight of the grain.

## The Harvest and the Milling

The harvest of the rice is accomplished by the sickle, the stalks being cut at the base and gathered into the sheaves that are carried to the threshing floor. The threshing is the process that separates the grain from the stalk, the threshing being done by the flailing or by the trampling of the oxen, and the threshed grain is winnowed in the wind that blows away the chaff and leaves the clean grain. The grain is then dried in the sun and stored in the granaries that are the structures that protect the rice from the moisture and the pests.

The milling of the rice is the process that removes the husk and the bran from the grain, the milling being done by the stone mills that are turned by the hand or by the water. The white rice that is the product of the milling is the staple food of the Sahrani coast, and the bran that is removed in the milling is used as a feed for the animals and as a fertiliser for the fields. The rice that is milled for the export is graded by the length of the grain and the quality of the milling, and the rice that is exported to the northern markets is the long-grain variety that is the characteristic product of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent).

## The Rice in the Sahrani Cuisine

The rice is the staple food of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast, the grain being cooked in the water and the spices that are the ingredients of the dishes that are the hallmark of the Sahrani cuisine. The rice is served with the sauces and the stews that are prepared from the vegetables and the meats of the region, and the rice is also used in the preparation of the sweet dishes that are the desserts of the Sahrani table. The rice flour is used in the baking of the flatbreads and the cakes that are the products of the Sahrani kitchens, and the rice flour is also used in the preparation of the noodles that are the accompaniment to the soups and the dishes of the coast.

The rice is also used in the production of the rice wine that is the ceremonial beverage of the Sahrani coast, the wine being fermented from the rice that is steeped in the water and the yeast that converts the starches to the sugars and the sugars to the alcohol. The rice wine is served at the festivals and the ceremonies that mark the occasions of the Sahrani life, and the rice wine is also used in the cooking as a flavouring and a marinade for the meats and the vegetables.

## Trade and the Export of the Rice

The trade in rice is one of the most important components of the Sahrani economy, and the rice that is produced in the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) is exported to every region of the known world. The rice is carried in the sacks that are loaded onto the boats that travel the rivers to the coast, and the rice is transferred to the ships that carry it to the markets of the north. The rice that is exported to [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) is a luxury of the tables of the wealthy, for the climate of the north does not permit the cultivation of the rice, and the rice that is consumed in the northern settlements is the product of the import from the south.

The rice trade is regulated by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the rice merchants, who control the quality and the prices of the rice that is sold in the markets. The guilds maintain the warehouses where the rice is stored and the mills where the rice is milled, and the guilds also organise the transport of the rice from the interior to the coast. The rice that is exported is graded by the quality and the variety, and the seals of the guilds that are stamped on the sacks are the guarantee of the quality that the buyers in the northern markets rely upon.
