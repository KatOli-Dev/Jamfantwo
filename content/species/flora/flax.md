## Flax

Flax is the most important fibre plant of the temperate regions of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and the linen that is produced from the flax is the most common of the textiles that are worn by the people of the northern settlements.

The flax is an annual plant that is sown in the spring and harvested in the late summer, when the stems have reached the full height and the seeds have begun to ripen. The process of the extraction of the fibre from the flax is called the retting, and the retting involves the soaking of the flax stems in the water for a period of several weeks, during which the natural processes of decay break down the substances that bind the fibres to the woody core of the stem. The retted stems are dried and then broken and scutched to separate the fibre from the woody waste, and the cleaned fibre is combed and spun into the yarn that is woven into the linen cloth.

The linen of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) is recognised as the finest in the known world, and the flax that is grown in the river valleys of these regions produces a fibre that is long and strong and fine, the ideal material for the production of the cloth that is used in the garments of the wealthy and the sailcloth of the ships.

## Physical Characteristics

The flax is a slender annual plant that grows to a height of two to four feet, the stems being thin and unbranched and the leaves being narrow and lance-shaped and arranged alternately along the stem. The flowers of the flax are small and delicate, the petals being a pale blue or a white colour that gives the flax fields a distinctive and beautiful appearance when the plants are in bloom in the early summer. The seed capsules of the flax are small and round, each capsule containing several seeds that are rich in the oil that is pressed from them and used in the preparation of the paints and the finishes that protect the wood and the metal.

The fibre of the flax is a bast fibre that is found in the inner bark of the stem, the fibre being arranged in the bundles that run the length of the stem and that give the plant its structural strength. The individual flax fibres are among the longest of the natural fibres, and the length of the fibre is the quality that makes the flax so valuable for the production of the fine linen cloth. The fibre is strong and smooth and has a natural lustre that gives the linen its characteristic sheen, and the fibre becomes softer and more supple with the repeated washing that the linen cloth receives in the domestic use.

## Cultivation and the Growing Season

The cultivation of the flax is a demanding process that requires careful preparation of the soil and the precise timing of the sowing and the harvesting. The flax is sown in the early spring, the seed being broadcast by hand or sown in the rows that are marked by the plough, and the seed is covered with a light layer of the soil that is drawn over it by the harrow. The flax is sown more densely than the other crops, for the close spacing of the plants encourages the growth of the tall and unbranched stems that produce the best fibre, and the farmer who sows the flax too thinly will harvest the short and branchy plants that yield the coarse fibre of the inferior quality.

The flax requires a cool and moist climate for the best growth, and the plant is sown in the regions where the spring rains are reliable and the summer temperatures are moderate. The flax is not grown in the hot and dry regions of the south, for the heat and the drought cause the plants to mature too quickly and the fibre that is produced in the hot conditions is short and coarse. The flax-growing regions of Aethelar are concentrated in the river valleys of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth), where the soil is deep and the rainfall is reliable, and the flax that is produced in these regions is the standard against which the flax of the other regions is measured.

## Harvesting and Retting

The harvesting of the flax is a critical operation that must be timed to coincide with the stage of the growth at which the fibre is at its best, the stems being pulled from the ground rather than cut, for the pulling preserves the full length of the fibre. The flax is harvested when the stems have begun to turn yellow and the seed capsules have begun to brown, the stage at which the fibre has reached its maximum strength and the quality of the fibre is at its peak. The harvested flax is laid in the field to dry for a few days, and the dried stems are then tied in the bundles that are called the beets and carried to the retting ponds.

The retting of the flax is the process that separates the fibre from the woody core of the stem, the process being achieved by the soaking of the flax in the water that encourages the growth of the microorganisms that break down the pectins that bind the fibre to the wood. The retting ponds are shallow pools that are dug in the ground and filled with the water from the streams and the rivers, and the beets of flax are submerged in the ponds and weighted with the stones to keep them below the surface. The retting takes from one to three weeks depending on the temperature of the water and the stage of the growth of the flax, and the experienced retter judges the completion of the process by the ease with which the fibre separates from the woody core when the stem is broken.

The retted flax is removed from the ponds and spread in the field to dry, and the dried flax is then broken and scutched to separate the fibre from the woody waste that is called the shives. The breaking is done by the wooden breaks that crush the stems and release the fibre, and the scutching is done by the wooden knives that scrape the remaining shives from the fibre. The cleaned fibre is hackled by drawing it through the combs that separate the long fibre from the short tow and that align the fibres for the spinning. The hackled fibre is then spun into the yarn on the spinning wheel or the distaff, and the yarn is woven into the linen cloth on the looms of the weavers.

## The Linen Trade and the Guilds

The production of the linen is a major industry in the northern regions of Aethelar, and the [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) that are produced from the flax are traded to every region of the known world. The linen that is produced in the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) is recognised as the finest in the known world, and the weavers of these regions are organised into the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) that regulate the quality of the cloth and the terms of the trade. The guild marks that are stamped on the bolts of the linen are the guarantee of the quality and the origin of the cloth, and the linen that bears the guild mark of the Vael or the Aldreth commands the highest prices in the markets of the known world.

The linen is used in the production of the garments that are worn by the people of the northern settlements, the cloth being valued for its coolness and its durability and its ability to withstand the repeated washing that the daily use requires. The finest linen is used in the garments of the wealthy and the ceremonial robes of the priests and the officials, and the coarser linen is used in the household linens and the work clothes of the common people. The linen is also used in the production of the sailcloth that is essential to the maritime trade, and the sailcloth that is woven from the flax of the Vael is the standard material for the sails of the ships that trade between the ports of Aethelar and the other continents.

## The Seed and the Oil

The seeds of the flax are a valuable by-product of the fibre cultivation, the seeds being rich in the oil that is pressed from them and used in a variety of the industrial and the domestic applications. The flaxseed oil is used in the preparation of the paints and the varnishes that protect the wood and the metal from the weather and the corrosion, and the oil is also used in the preparation of the linoleum that is laid on the floors of the houses and the public buildings. The flaxseed oil is valued for its ability to dry quickly and to form a hard and durable surface, and the painters and the varnishers of the northern cities are among the principal consumers of the oil.

The flaxseed is also used as a food, the seeds being ground into a meal that is mixed with the flour and baked into the bread that is eaten in the northern regions. The flaxseed meal is rich in the protein and the fat that supplement the diet of the people who consume it, and the flaxseed bread is a traditional food of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) that is eaten in the winter months when the other foods are scarce. The flaxseed is also fed to the [cattle](/content/species/fauna/cattle) and the other livestock in the winter, the seeds providing the fat and the protein that the animals need to maintain the condition through the cold months.
