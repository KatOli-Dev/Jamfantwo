## Hemp

Hemp is the second fibre plant of the northern regions, and the hemp is grown for the fibre that is used in the production of the cordage, the rope, and the canvas that the ships of the coastal cities require.

The hemp is a tall and robust plant that grows to a height of ten feet or more, and the fibre that is extracted from the stems of the hemp is stronger and more durable than the fibre of the flax. The processing of the hemp is similar to the processing of the flax, but the hemp stems are thicker and the retting takes longer. The fibre that is extracted from the hemp is coarser than the flax fibre, and the hemp fibre is used in the production of the ropes that must withstand the stress of the heavy loads and the canvas that must endure the weather of the open sea.

The hemp that is grown in the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) and the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) is the principal source of the cordage for the ships of the [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) fleet, and the ropemakers of these regions are among the most skilled in the known world. The ropes that are produced in the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) are used in the rigging of the largest vessels, and the quality of the Northern Marches rope is recognised throughout the maritime world.

## Physical Characteristics

The hemp is an annual plant that grows to a height of eight to fifteen feet, the stem being thick and angular and the leaves being divided into the narrow leaflets that are arranged in the palm-like form that is characteristic of the species. The hemp is a dioecious plant, the male and the female flowers being borne on the separate plants, the male plants being taller and the female plants being stouter and more branched. The male plants produce the fibre that is finer and the female plants produce the fibre that is stronger, and the farmers who grow the hemp for the rope prefer the fibre of the female plants for the superior strength that it provides.

The fibre of the hemp is a bast fibre that is found in the inner bark of the stem, the fibre being arranged in the bundles that run the length of the stem and that give the plant its great strength. The individual hemp fibres are shorter than the flax fibres but the hemp fibres are thicker and stronger, and the fibre that is produced from the hemp is the strongest of the plant fibres that are available in the known world. The hemp fibre is coarse and stiff and has a natural resistance to the water and the decay that makes it the ideal material for the cordage and the canvas that are used in the marine environment.

## Cultivation and the Growing Season

The hemp is sown in the spring after the danger of the frost has passed, the seed being broadcast in the prepared fields and covered with a light layer of the soil. The hemp requires a deep and rich soil and a supply of the moisture that is sufficient to sustain the rapid growth of the plant, and the hemp is grown in the regions where the spring rains are reliable and the summer temperatures are moderate. The hemp is not grown in the hot and dry regions of the south, for the heat and the drought cause the plants to mature too quickly and the fibre that is produced in the hot conditions is short and weak.

The cultivation of the hemp is a demanding process that requires the careful preparation of the soil and the dense sowing that produces the tall and unbranched stems that yield the best fibre. The hemp grows rapidly, the plants reaching the full height in the three months after the sowing, and the dense growth of the hemp suppresses the weeds and the competing plants that would reduce the yield of the fibre. The hemp is harvested in the late summer or the early autumn, the stems being cut close to the ground and tied in the bundles that are called the beets, and the beets are carried to the retting ponds where the fibre is separated from the woody core of the stem.

## Retting and the Processing of the Fibre

The retting of the hemp is the process that separates the fibre from the woody core of the stem, the process being the same in the principle as the retting of the [flax](/content/species/flora/flax) but requiring a longer period of the soaking due to the greater thickness and the toughness of the hemp stems. The retting ponds are the shallow pools that are dug in the ground and filled with the water from the streams and the rivers, and the beets of hemp are submerged in the ponds and weighted with the stones to keep them below the surface. The retting of the hemp takes from two to four weeks, the longer period being required for the hemp that is harvested late in the season when the stems are more mature and the fibre is more firmly bound to the wood.

The retted hemp is removed from the ponds and spread in the field to dry, and the dried hemp is then broken and scutched to separate the fibre from the woody waste that is called the hurds. The breaking of the hemp is done by the heavy breaks that crush the thick stems, the breaking of the hemp being a more arduous task than the breaking of the flax due to the greater thickness and the toughness of the hemp stems. The scutched fibre is hackled by drawing it through the coarse combs that separate the long fibre from the short tow and that align the fibres for the spinning. The hemp fibre is spun into the yarn and the cordage on the spinning wheels and the ropewalks that are the specialised equipment of the ropemakers.

## The Ropewalks and the Cordage

The production of the rope from the hemp fibre is the craft of the ropemakers, the ropewalks being the long and narrow buildings where the hemp yarn is spun and the rope is laid. The ropewalk is the structure that is essential to the production of the rope, the length of the ropewalk determining the length of the rope that can be produced, and the ropewalks of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) are the longest in the known world, the ropes that are produced in these ropewalks being the ropes that are used in the rigging of the largest vessels. The ropemakers who work in the ropewalks are the skilled craftsmen who have served the apprenticeship and who have mastered the art of the spinning and the laying of the rope.

The rope that is produced in the ropewalks is sold to the [shipbuilding](/content/economy/shipbuilding) yards and the merchants who supply the ships, the rope being the essential component of the rigging that holds the masts and the sails. The [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) fleet is the principal consumer of the rope that is produced in the Northern Marches, and the ropemakers of the Northern Marches are organised into the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) that regulate the quality and the terms of the trade. The rope that bears the guild mark of the Northern Marches ropemakers is the rope that is trusted by the shipwrights and the sailors who depend on the cordage in the storms and the gales of the open sea.

## The Canvas and the Sails

The hemp fibre is also used in the production of the canvas that is woven into the sails of the ships, the canvas being the cloth that is strong enough to withstand the wind and the water that the sails must endure. The canvas that is woven from the hemp is coarser and heavier than the [Flax](/content/species/flora/flax) sailcloth, but the hemp canvas is more durable and more resistant to the rot that the damp conditions of the sea would cause in the lighter cloth. The canvas that is used in the sails of the [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) fleet is the canvas that is woven from the hemp of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast), and the weavers of the Stormfall Coast are among the most skilled in the production of the canvas that the maritime trade requires.

The canvas is woven in the widths and the weights that the sailmakers specify, the canvas being finished with the treatments that make it resistant to the water and the wind. The sailmakers who work in the shipyards of the coastal cities cut the canvas into the panels that are sewn together to form the sails, the seams being stitched with the hemp thread that is the same material as the canvas. The sails that are made from the hemp canvas are the sails that carry the ships of Aethelar across the [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea) and the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea), and the quality of the canvas is a factor in the speed and the safety of the voyages that the ships undertake.

## Economic Significance and the Trade

The hemp is a crop of great economic significance in the northern regions, the fibre that is produced from the hemp being the material that is essential to the maritime trade and the fishing industry that are the foundations of the economy of the coast. The [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) and the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) are the regions where the hemp is grown in the greatest quantity, and the [timber and logging](/content/economy/timber-and-logging) and the [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) trades of these regions are supplemented by the hemp production that provides the cordage and the canvas that the ships require. The hemp is also used in the production of the sacking and the coarse cloth that are the standard materials for the packaging and the transport of the goods, and the hemp sacking is the sacking that is used in the grain trade and the other bulk trades that are the commerce of the northern settlements.
