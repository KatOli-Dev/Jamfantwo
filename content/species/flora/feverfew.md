## Feverfew and the Treatment of Fevers

Feverfew is among the most widely used medicinal herbs in the known world, its leaves being used in the treatment of the fevers that accompany the seasonal illnesses that afflict the populations of the cities and the settlements. The plant grows wild in the meadows and the waste places of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and it is cultivated in the gardens of the healers for the reliability of the supply that is required in the fever season.

The leaves of the feverfew are harvested in the early summer, when the plant is in full leaf and the medicinal compounds are at their highest concentration. The leaves are dried in the shade and stored in sealed pots that preserve their properties for up to a year. The remedy is prepared by infusing the dried leaves in hot water and straining the infusion before it is administered to the patient. The fever that results from the seasonal illnesses usually breaks within a day of the administration of the feverfew infusion, and the patient is left weakened but alive.

The feverfew of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) margins is considered to be of superior quality, and the healers of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) prefer the Heartwood feverfew for their preparations. The plant is also used in the treatment of the headaches and the joint pains that are the common complaints of the elderly, and the regular consumption of feverfew tea is recommended by the healers for the prevention of the migraines that afflict some individuals.

## Habitat and Distribution

The feverfew is a plant of the temperate regions, and the wild populations are found in the meadows, the hedgerows, and the waste places of the lowland and the foothill regions of Aethelar. The plant prefers the well-drained soils and the open positions where the sun is plentiful, and the feverfew is not found in the dense forests or the cold uplands where the frost arrives early and the growing season is short. The wild feverfew is most abundant in the regions that lie between the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) foothills and the river valleys of the interior, where the climate is moderate and the rainfall is distributed through the year.

The feverfew is also found in the waste places of the settled regions, where the plant colonises the disturbed soils around the settlements and the abandoned fields that have been left to fallow. The plant is not considered an aggressive weed, for the feverfew does not spread into the cultivated fields in the manner of the thistle or the [nettle weed](/content/species/flora/nettle-weed), and the plant is tolerated by the farmers who recognise its value to the healers. The wild populations are sufficient to supply the needs of the smaller settlements, but the larger cities require the cultivated supplies that the healers' gardens provide.

## Physical Characteristics

The feverfew is a perennial herb that grows to a height of one to two feet, the stems being slender and branching and the leaves being divided into lobed segments that give the foliage a delicate and feathery appearance. The leaves are a yellowish green colour and emit a strong and distinctive odour when they are crushed, a pungent aroma that is familiar to every healer and herbalist in the northern regions. The flowers of the feverfew are small and daisy-like, with white petals arranged around a yellow centre, and the flowers appear in the midsummer and persist into the early autumn.

The root system of the feverfew is fibrous and shallow, the plant spreading by the seeds and by the division of the root clumps in the established plants. The feverfew is not a long-lived perennial, and the individual plants decline in vigour after three or four years, the older clumps being replaced by the seedlings that establish themselves in the vicinity of the parent plant. The healer who maintains a feverfew bed must dig and divide the clumps every few years to ensure that the plants remain productive, and the surplus divisions are planted in the new beds or given to the other healers who wish to establish their own supplies.

## Cultivation in the Healer's Gardens

The cultivation of the feverfew in the healer's gardens is a practice that is found in every settlement of consequence in Aethelar, the plant being among the first that the apprentice healer is taught to grow. The seed is sown in the spring in the prepared beds that have been enriched with the compost and the well-rotted manure, and the seedlings are thinned to a spacing of one foot between the plants. The feverfew requires little attention once it is established, the plant being resistant to the pests and the diseases that afflict the more tender herbs, and the beds need only to be kept free of the competing weeds and watered in the dry periods.

The gardens of the healers are typically located near the apothecaries and the hospices where the remedies are prepared and administered, and the feverfew beds are a characteristic feature of these gardens. The [inns and hospices](/content/economy/inns-and-hospices) of the larger cities maintain their own feverfew beds for the treatment of the travellers who fall ill on the roads, and the hospice gardens of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) and the other coastal cities are noted for the quality and the quantity of the feverfew that they produce. The healers who tend these gardens keep careful records of the planting, the harvesting, and the yields, and the knowledge that is accumulated through these records is passed from one generation of healers to the next.

## Preparation and Administration

The preparation of the feverfew remedy is a simple process that can be performed in any kitchen, but the healers have refined the methods over the centuries to ensure the consistency and the potency of the preparations. The dried leaves are measured by weight and placed in a pot of water that has been brought to the boil, the pot being removed from the heat and covered with a lid to allow the infusion to steep for a period of fifteen to twenty minutes. The infusion is then strained through a cloth to remove the leaf fragments, and the clear liquid is administered to the patient in doses that vary with the age and the condition of the patient.

The fresh leaves of the feverfew may also be chewed directly, a method that is favoured by the individuals who use the feverfew for the prevention of the migraines, but the fresh leaves have a bitter taste that is unpleasant to many, and the mouth ulcers that can result from the prolonged chewing of the fresh leaves are a side effect that limits the popularity of this method. The healers recommend the infusion as the preferred form of administration, and the dried leaves that are stored in the sealed pots retain their properties for a full year or more if they are kept dry and away from the light.

## Cultural Significance

The feverfew is a plant that is woven into the daily life of the northern settlements, the herb being a common sight in the gardens of the towns and the villages and a staple of the healer's pharmacopoeia. The fever season that arrives in the autumn is a time of anxiety in the crowded settlements, and the healers who are prepared with adequate supplies of the feverfew are valued by the communities that they serve. The reputation of the healer who can break a fever quickly and reliably is built upon the quality of the feverfew that the healer has in store, and the competition among the healers for the best Heartwood feverfew is a feature of the trade in the medicinal herbs.

The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the Aethelar Heartwood regard the feverfew as a plant that is under their protection, and the wild populations that grow in the margins of the Heartwood are not harvested by the human gatherers without the permission of the elven communities. The elves permit the healers of the neighbouring settlements to gather the feverfew in the margins, but the gathering is restricted to the quantities that are needed for the local use, and the commercial harvesting of the Heartwood feverfew is prohibited. The elven protection of the feverfew is a source of occasional friction between the elven communities and the herb merchants of the cities, but the arrangement has persisted for the centuries and is generally respected by the authorities of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth).
