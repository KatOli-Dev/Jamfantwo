## Jute

The jute that is grown in the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) is a fibre plant that produces a soft and strong fibre that is used in the production of the sacking, the carpeting, and the coarse cloth of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) settlements.

The jute is a tall and fast-growing plant that is harvested in the summer, and the fibre is extracted from the stems by the retting process that is similar to the retting of the flax and the hemp. The jute fibre is not as strong or as durable as the hemp fibre, but the jute is much cheaper to produce and the jute products are the standard materials for the packaging and the transport of the goods. The sacks that are made from the jute are used in the transport of the grains, the spices, and the other products of the Sahrani interior, and the trade in the jute sacks is a significant part of the economy of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent).

## Physical Characteristics

The jute is an annual plant that grows to a height of eight to twelve feet, the stem being slender and unbranched and the leaves being simple and lance-shaped and arranged alternately along the stem. The jute is a plant of the mallow family, the flowers being small and yellow and the fruit being a capsule that contains the seeds of the plant. The fibre of the jute is a bast fibre that is found in the inner bark of the stem, the fibre being arranged in the bundles that run the length of the stem, and the fibre is extracted from the stem by the retting process that breaks down the pectins and the gums that bind the fibre to the woody core.

The jute fibre is long and soft and has a natural lustre that gives the jute cloth its characteristic sheen, the fibre being a pale yellow or a golden colour that is the natural colour of the jute. The jute fibre is weaker than the hemp and the [flax](/content/species/flora/flax) fibres, the breaking strength of the jute being about half the strength of the hemp, but the jute is sufficient for the purposes for which it is used, and the low cost of the jute production makes the jute the most economical of the fibres for the packaging and the coarse cloth. The jute fibre is also sensitive to the moisture, the fibre weakening when it is wet and the jute products being unsuitable for the uses where the wet conditions are encountered.

## Cultivation in the Green Crescent

The cultivation of the jute is a practice that is confined to the warm and the humid regions of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), the plant requiring the warm climate and the abundant water that the Sahrani coast provides. The jute is sown in the spring after the rains have begun, the seed being broadcast in the prepared fields and covered with a light layer of the soil. The jute grows rapidly in the warm and the wet conditions, the plants reaching the full height in the three months after the sowing, and the dense growth of the jute suppresses the weeds and the competing plants that would reduce the yield of the fibre.

The jute is harvested in the summer, the stems being cut close to the ground when the plants are in the early flower and the fibre is at its best, the stage of the growth that the experienced jute farmer recognises by the colour and the texture of the stems. The harvested jute is tied in the bundles and carried to the retting ponds where the fibre is separated from the woody core of the stem. The [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) farmers who cultivate the jute are the specialists who have developed the methods for the optimal harvesting and the retting of the crop, and the jute that is produced in the Green Crescent is the jute that is recognised as the standard for the quality and the quantity that the trade demands.

## Retting and the Processing of the Fibre

The retting of the jute is the process that separates the fibre from the woody core of the stem, the process being the same in the principle as the retting of the [flax](/content/species/flora/flax) and the [hemp](/content/species/flora/hemp) but being completed more quickly due to the softer texture of the jute stems. The retting ponds are the shallow pools that are dug in the ground and filled with the water from the rivers and the canals, and the bundles of jute are submerged in the ponds and weighted with the stones. The retting of the jute takes from eight to twelve days, the warm water of the Sahrani coast accelerating the fermentation that breaks down the binding materials and releases the fibre.

The retted jute is removed from the ponds and washed in the clean water to remove the debris and the decomposed tissue, the washed fibre being dried in the sun and pressed into the bales that are the form in which the jute is traded. The processing of the jute is a less arduous operation than the processing of the flax and the hemp, the softer texture of the jute making the breaking and the scutching easier, and the jute fibre is ready for the spinning after the simple washing and the drying. The spinning of the jute is done on the spinning wheels and the looms that are similar to the equipment used for the other fibres, and the jute yarn is woven into the sacking and the coarse cloth that are the products of the jute industry.

## The Sacking and the Packaging Trade

The principal product of the jute industry is the sacking that is used in the packaging and the transport of the goods that are produced in the Sahrani interior. The jute sacks are the standard containers for the grain and the [spices and dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) and the other bulk commodities that are carried by the caravans and the ships, and the jute sacking is the material that is used in every region of the known world where the goods are packed and the transported. The jute sacks that are produced in the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) are exported to the markets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), and the sacks that bear the marks of the Sahrani producers are the sacks that are recognised throughout the trade.

The jute is also used in the production of the carpeting and the matting that are the floor coverings of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) settlements, the jute providing the backing and the weft for the carpets that are woven in the workshops of the coast. The jute carpet is a coarse and the practical floor covering that is used in the houses and the public buildings of the Sahrani interior, and the jute matting is the material that is used in the packing and the wrapping of the goods that are transported by the caravans. The [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) that are produced from the jute are the textiles that are the everyday materials of the Sahrani people, and the jute is the fibre that is the foundation of the textile industry of the Green Crescent.

## Economic Significance and the Trade

The jute is a crop of great economic significance in the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent), the fibre that is produced from the jute being the material that is essential to the packaging and the transport of the goods that are the commerce of the Sahrani interior. The jute industry employs the large numbers of the people who are engaged in the cultivation and the processing and the weaving of the fibre, and the jute is a crop that supports the livelihoods of the farmers and the labourers and the weavers who are the people of the Green Crescent. The trade in the jute and the jute products is a significant component of the [agriculture](/content/economy/agriculture) and the [trade and tariffs](/content/economy/trade-and-tariffs) of the Sahrani states, and the revenues that are derived from the jute are a significant source of the wealth of the region.

The jute is also traded to the northern continents, the jute sacking and the jute cloth being the materials that are used in the packaging of the goods that are imported from the south. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the merchants who deal in the imported goods regulate the trade in the jute products, the guilds setting the standards of the quality and the prices that are paid for the different grades of the jute. The jute that is traded to the north is the jute that is used in the [cartage and transport](/content/economy/cartage-and-transport) of the goods that are carried along the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) and the other trade routes that connect the Sahrani interior to the markets of the coast and the northern continents. The jute is the fibre that connects the Sahrani farmer to the northern merchant, and the trade in the jute is the trade that supports the commerce that is the lifeblood of the known world.
