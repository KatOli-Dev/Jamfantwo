## Willow Bark and the Relief of Pain

The bark of the willow tree is the most widely used remedy for the relief of pain in the known world, its properties being known to the healers of every region and every tradition. The willow grows in the wet soils of the river banks and the marsh edges of Aethelar and [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and the bark is harvested in the spring when the sap is rising and the medicinal compounds are at their highest concentration.

The outer bark of the willow is scraped from the branches and the trunk, and the inner bark is stripped in long pieces that are dried in the sun. The dried bark is ground to a powder that is mixed with water or wine and administered to the patient for the relief of the pain of injuries, the inflammation of the joints, and the headaches that disrupt the daily life of the sufferer. The remedy is not a cure but a palliation that allows the patient to rest and to recover the strength that is needed for the healing of the underlying condition.

The willow bark of the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) is considered to be the finest available, and the apothecaries of the coastal cities pay premium prices for the bark that is harvested from the willows that grow in the wet soils of the marsh. The trade in willow bark is a minor but steady component of the commerce of the region, and the harvest of the bark provides an income for the marsh dwellers who gather it in the spring.

## Physical Characteristics

The willow is a deciduous tree that grows to a height of thirty to fifty feet in the mature specimens, the trunk being short and the branches being numerous and spreading, the crown of the tree being broad and the shape being the rounded and the open form that the willows display. The bark of the willow is a grey-brown and deeply furrowed in the older trees, the bark being the thick and the rough exterior that protects the inner bark that is the source of the medicinal compounds. The inner bark of the willow is the layer that lies between the outer bark and the wood, the inner bark being the tissue that carries the sap and the nutrients that the tree transports between the roots and the leaves.

The leaves of the willow are narrow and lance-shaped, the leaves being arranged alternately along the stems and being a bright green on the upper surface and a paler green on the underside. The leaves of the willow are among the first to appear in the spring and among the last to fall in the autumn, the long growing season of the willow being the feature that distinguishes the tree from the other species of the riverbank. The catkins of the willow are the flowers that the tree produces in the early spring before the leaves appear, the catkins being the cylindrical and the furry structures that bear the reproductive parts of the tree and that are pollinated by the [honey bees](/content/species/fauna/honey-bee) and the other insects that visit the catkins for the pollen and the nectar that they provide.

## Habitat and Distribution

The willow grows in the wet soils of the river banks and the marsh edges of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), the tree being found in the regions where the soil is moist and the water is near the surface. The willow is most abundant along the [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river) and the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael) and the other watercourses of the Aethelari continent, the willows forming the continuous stands that line the banks of the rivers and the streams. The willow is also found in the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) where the wet soils and the shallow water provide the conditions that the tree requires.

The willow is a tree that is adapted to the wet conditions that the riverbank and the marsh provide, the root system of the willow being the extensive and the shallow network that the tree uses to extract the moisture from the saturated soil. The willow is also tolerant of the flooding that the rivers and the marshes experience, the tree being able to survive the inundation that the seasonal floods produce and the waterlogging that the other trees cannot endure. The willow is the pioneer species that colonises the bare ground that the floods create, the seeds of the willow being dispersed by the water and the germination being rapid in the moist soil that the flood leaves behind.

## Medicinal Properties

The inner bark of the willow contains the compounds that are the source of the medicinal properties that the tree is valued for, the principal compound being the substance that the healers and the apothecaries identify as the active agent in the relief of the pain and the inflammation that the willow bark remedies address. The concentration of the compound is highest in the bark that is harvested in the spring when the sap is rising, the spring being the season that the [healing and restoration](/content/magic/healing-and-restoration) practitioners and the herbalists of the known world recommend for the gathering of the bark.

The preparation of the willow bark remedy involves the drying and the grinding of the bark that has been harvested, the powder being the form in which the bark is stored and the form that is used in the preparation of the remedies that the healers administer. The powder is mixed with the water or the wine and the mixture is drunk by the patient for the relief of the pain that the injury or the inflammation causes, the effect of the remedy being the reduction of the pain and the swelling that the condition produces. The remedy is also used for the relief of the [fever](/content/species/flora/feverfew) that the infections and the other illnesses cause, the willow bark being the remedy that the healers use for the conditions that the [feverfew](/content/species/flora/feverfew) and the other herbs also address.

## The Willow Bark Trade

The trade in the willow bark is the commerce that the marsh dwellers and the riverbank communities of the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) and the other regions where the willow grows conduct, the bark being the product that the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the apothecaries and the [spices and dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) merchants purchase. The [prices and livelihood](/content/economy/prices-and-livelihood) of the marsh communities are partly dependent on the willow bark, the bark being one of the resources that the marsh economy yields. The bark is transported from the marsh to the markets of the coastal cities where the apothecaries process the bark and prepare the remedies that the [markets and fairs](/content/economy/markets-and-fairs) distribute.

The willow bark that is harvested from the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) is the bark that is considered the finest in the known world, the quality being the result of the conditions that the marsh provides and the care that the marsh dwellers apply to the harvesting. The bark of the Greywash willow commands the premium prices that the apothecaries of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) and the other coastal cities pay, the bark being the ingredient that the apothecaries select for the remedies that the wealthy households and the institutions use. The [cartage and transport](/content/economy/cartage-and-transport) of the willow bark from the marsh to the cities is the trade that the merchants and the carriers conduct, the bark being the commodity that the [trade and tariffs](/content/economy/trade-and-tariffs) of the region regulate.

## The Willow in Basketry and Craft

The willow is also valued for the flexible and the tough branches that the tree produces, the branches being the material that the basket weavers and the furniture makers of the Aethelari and the Sahrani regions use for the production of the baskets and the wicker furniture that the daily life of the populations employs. The willow branches are harvested in the winter when the tree is dormant, the branches being cut and sorted by the size and the quality that the different uses require. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the basket weavers regulate the harvest of the willow branches and the trade in the woven products that the craft produces.

The willow baskets are the containers that the [agriculture](/content/economy/agriculture) and the [fishing and whaling](/content/economy/fishing-and-whaling) and the other industries of the known world use for the transport and the storage of the goods that the production involves, the baskets being the products that the [markets and fairs](/content/economy/markets-and-fairs) of the cities and the villages trade. The [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) industry also uses the willow baskets for the carrying of the wool and the yarn that the textile production requires, the baskets being the equipment that the industries rely on. The willow furniture is the product that the [inns and hospices](/content/economy/inns-and-hospices) and the households of the Aethelari regions use, the wicker chairs and the tables being the furniture that the comfort and the convenience of the daily life employs.

## The Willow in Culture and Folklore

The willow has a place in the [folk omens and superstitions](/content/mythology/folk-omens-and-superstitions) and the mythology of the Aethelari peoples that reflects the association of the tree with the water and the healing that the willow provides, the willow being the symbol of the flexibility and the resilience that the tree embodies. The willow is associated with the [sea mother](/content/religion/sea-mothers-court) and the other water spirits of the Aethelari mythology, the willow being the tree that the water deities are connected to and the tree that the rituals of the water worship involve. The [halfling hearth traditions](/content/religion/halfling-hearth-traditions) and the other cultural practices of the Aethelari peoples include the willow as the element of the seasonal rituals that the agricultural year marks.
