The prickly pear is the introduced cactus that has spread across the Sahrani margins and the Sunfire Steppe, its dense thickets crowding out the native plants and reducing the pasture and the arable land that is available to the herders and the farmers of the region.

## The Prickly Pear of the Sahrani Margins

The prickly pear cactus that was introduced to the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) margins in the centuries past has become an invasive species in the regions where the cactus has spread beyond the areas of the cultivation. The prickly pear is a cactus that grows in the dense thickets that crowd out the native plants of the [Sunfire Steppe](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sunfire-steppe) and the transition zone between the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh).

The prickly pear is difficult to control, for the cactus reproduces from the fragments of the stem as well as from the seeds, and the removal of the established thickets is a labour-intensive process that requires the cutting and the removal of every piece of the cactus from the infested area. The spread of the prickly pear in the Sahrani margins is a matter of the concern for the herders and the farmers of the region, for the cactus reduces the area of the pasture and the arable land that is available for the use.

## Physical Characteristics of the Prickly Pear

The prickly pear is a cactus that grows in the form of the flat and fleshy pads that are joined together to form the branches of the plant, the pads being the modified stems that store the water and the nutrients that the plant requires. The pads of the prickly pear are oval and flattened, the surface being covered with the spines and the small hairs that are called the glochids, the glochids being the tiny and barbed structures that are the most troublesome feature of the plant, for the glochids detach from the pad at the slightest touch and embed themselves in the skin of the handler, causing the irritation and the inflammation that can last for days.

The flowers of the prickly pear are large and showy, the petals being yellow, orange, or red, and the flowers are produced on the edges of the pads in the summer. The fruit of the prickly pear is a fleshy berry that is called the prickly pear fig, the fruit being red or purple when ripe and containing the sweet and juicy pulp that is edible, though the spines and the glochids on the surface of the fruit must be removed before the fruit is handled or eaten.

## The Introduction and Spread of the Prickly Pear

The prickly pear cactus was introduced to the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) margins in the centuries past, the cactus being brought from the regions beyond the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) as an ornamental plant and as a source of the fruit and the fodder that the cactus produces. The prickly pear was planted in the gardens and the hedgerows of the settlements, and the cactus was valued for the dense and impenetrable barriers that the thickets formed, the spines and the glochids making the prickly pear the most effective of the hedging plants.

The prickly pear has become an invasive species in the regions where the cactus has spread beyond the areas of the cultivation, the cactus reproducing from the seeds that are dispersed by the birds and the animals that eat the fruit, and from the fragments of the pads that are broken from the plants by the wind and the water and carried to the new sites where the fragments root and establish the new plants. The spread of the prickly pear in the Sahrani margins is a matter of the concern for the herders and the farmers of the region, for the cactus reduces the area of the pasture and the arable land that is available for the use.

## The Prickly Pear in the Sahrani Margins

The prickly pear is difficult to control, for the cactus reproduces from the fragments of the stem as well as from the seeds, and the removal of the established thickets is a labour-intensive process that requires the cutting and the removal of every piece of the cactus from the infested area. The fragments that are left on the ground will root and establish the new plants, and the removal of the prickly pear requires the burning of the cut material and the repeated cultivation of the soil to destroy the regrowth that emerges from the fragments that have been missed.

The thickets of the prickly pear that have spread across the [Sunfire Steppe](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sunfire-steppe) and the transition zone between the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) have reduced the area of the pasture that is available for the herds of the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides and the other peoples who graze their animals in the region. The cactus also competes with the native plants of the steppe, the dense thickets crowding out the grasses and the shrubs that are the natural vegetation of the region, and the reduction of the native vegetation has led to the decline of the animals that depend on the native plants for their food and their shelter.

## The Uses of the Prickly Pear

The prickly pear is not without its uses, and the fruit of the cactus is gathered by the peoples of the region and eaten fresh or preserved in the form of the jams and the syrups that are the products of the kitchens. The fruit of the prickly pear is sweet and refreshing, and the juice that is extracted from the fruit is used in the preparation of the beverages that are drunk in the hot weather of the summer. The seeds of the prickly pear are ground into a flour that is used in the preparation of the flatbreads, and the flour is also used as a thickener for the soups and the stews.

The pads of the prickly pear are also edible, the young pads being harvested before the spines have hardened and the glochids being removed by the scraping and the washing that prepares the pads for the cooking. The pads are called the nopales by the peoples of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) margins, and the nopales are cooked in the manner of the other vegetables, the flavour being tart and the texture being mucilaginous. The nopales are also used as a feed for the animals, the pads being burned to remove the spines and the glochids before they are fed to the cattle and the goats.

## Control Measures and the Future of the Prickly Pear

The authorities of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) and the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) have attempted to control the spread of the prickly pear by the introduction of the insects that feed on the cactus and by the mechanical removal of the thickets that have invaded the most valuable land. The insects that have been introduced are the cochineal bugs, the bugs that feed on the pads of the prickly pear and produce the red dye that is used in the colouring of the textiles and the other products of the region. The cochineal bugs have reduced the vigour of the prickly pear in the areas where they have been established, and the dye that is produced from the bugs is a valuable product that compensates for the damage that the cactus has caused.

The mechanical removal of the prickly pear is a costly and labour-intensive operation that is undertaken by the communities that are most affected by the spread of the cactus, and the land that is cleared of the prickly pear must be replanted with the native vegetation or the cultivated crops to prevent the re-establishment of the cactus. The cleared land is often planted with the [saltbush](/content/species/flora/saltbush) or the other plants that are adapted to the arid conditions of the region, and the restoration of the land is a slow process that requires the patience and the persistence of the communities that undertake it.
