The potato is a tuber that grows beneath the ground and has become a staple crop in the northern regions where the cultivation of the [Wheat](/content/species/flora/wheat) is difficult and uncertain. The plant produces a crop in the cool and wet conditions that are the characteristic climate of the northern growing season.

## The Potato of the Present Age

The potato was introduced to the known world in recent centuries and the cultivation of the potato has spread rapidly through the northern settlements. The potato is planted in the spring after the ground has thawed, and the tubers are harvested in the autumn when the leaves of the plant have begun to yellow and die. The potato is a productive crop, yielding more food per acre than the grain crops, and the potato has become a significant part of the diet of the northern peoples.

The potato of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) and the northern settlements is a variety that is blue-skinned and yellow-fleshed, a variety that is more resistant to the frost and the diseases that afflict the potato in the cold and the wet conditions of the north. The potato is stored in cellars and in pits dug into the ground, and the stored potatoes provide a supply of fresh food that lasts through the winter months.

## Physical Characteristics of the Potato Plant

The potato is a herbaceous perennial that grows to a height of two to three feet, the stems being erect and spreading, and the leaves being compound and composed of the leaflets that are arranged along the central stalk. The flowers of the potato are white or pale blue, the petals being fused into a corolla that is the characteristic shape of the family to which the potato belongs, and the flowers produce the small berries that contain the seeds of the plant, though the potato is not commonly grown from the seed in the known world.

The tubers of the potato are the swollen underground stems that store the starches and the nutrients that the plant produces during the growing season, and the tubers are the part of the plant that is harvested for food. The tubers of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) variety are blue-skinned and yellow-fleshed, the skin being thin and the flesh being dense and waxy, and the tubers range in size from the small marbles to the large fists that are the product of the most productive plants.

## The Introduction and Spread of the Potato

The potato was introduced to the known world in recent centuries, the tubers being brought from the lands beyond the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) by the explorers who travelled to the western shores. The potato was at first regarded with the suspicion that is the usual response to the unfamiliar foods, and the adoption of the potato as a staple crop was slow in the regions where the [wheat](/content/species/flora/wheat) and the [barley](/content/species/flora/barley) were the established grains. The potato was first grown in the gardens of the curious and the experimental, and the value of the crop was recognised only after the trials had demonstrated the productivity and the reliability of the plant in the cold and the wet conditions of the north.

The spread of the potato through the northern settlements was rapid once the value of the crop had been demonstrated, and the potato is now grown in every region of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the northern districts of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The potato has transformed the diet of the northern peoples, the tubers providing a source of the calories and the nutrients that the grain crops cannot produce in the same quantities, and the population of the northern settlements has increased in the centuries since the potato was introduced.

## Cultivation and the Growing Season

The potato is planted in the spring after the ground has thawed, the tubers being cut into the pieces that contain the eyes from which the new plants will grow, and the pieces being planted in the furrows that are dug by the plough or the spade. The potato is a crop that requires the cultivation of the soil, the tubers being planted in the ridges that are formed by the mounding of the soil around the plants, and the ridges being maintained by the hoeing that controls the weeds and the earthing-up that protects the tubers from the light that would turn them green and bitter.

The potato is harvested in the autumn when the leaves of the plant have begun to yellow and die, the tubers being dug from the ground by the forks and the spades that are the tools of the harvest. The harvest is a labour-intensive operation that employs the whole community, and the potatoes that are dug from the ground are sorted by the size and the quality, the best being saved for the seed and the remainder being stored for the food.

## Storage and the Winter Supply

The potato is stored in the cellars and the pits that are dug into the ground, the storage being designed to protect the tubers from the frost and the light that would damage the crop. The cellars of the northern settlements are the structures that are the most important to the survival of the communities, and the cellars that are filled with the potatoes and the [root vegetables](/content/species/flora/root-vegetable) are the assurance that the winter will not bring the hunger that was the constant companion of the northern peoples before the potato was introduced.

The stored potatoes provide a supply of fresh food that lasts through the winter months, the tubers being boiled, roasted, or mashed into the dishes that are the staple of the northern diet. The potato is also used in the preparation of the spirits that are distilled in the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark), the tubers being fermented and distilled to produce the clear and strong spirit that is the drink of the northern peoples.

## The Potato in the Northern Diet

The potato has become a significant part of the diet of the northern peoples, the tubers being prepared in the soups and the stews that are the common meals of the winter, and the boiled potato being the accompaniment to the salted fish and the cured meats that are the other staples of the northern table. The potato is also baked in the ashes of the fire, the baked potato being a simple and filling meal that is eaten by the labourers and the hunters who work in the cold.

The potato is also used as a feed for the animals that the northern settlements keep, the tubers being boiled and mixed with the other feeds to provide the nourishment that the [horses](/content/species/fauna/horse) and the cattle require during the winter months when the grazing is not available. The potato is a productive crop, yielding more food per acre than the grain crops, and the surplus that is produced in the good years is fed to the animals or traded to the regions where the harvest has been less successful.

## Diseases and the Vulnerability of the Crop

The potato is susceptible to the diseases that afflict the plants of the family, the blight being the most destructive of the diseases that can destroy the crop in a matter of days. The blight is a fungus that attacks the leaves and the tubers of the potato, the leaves turning black and the tubers rotting in the ground, and the blight can spread through the fields in the wet and the warm conditions that favour the fungus. The farmers of the north watch the weather with the anxiety that the danger of the blight demands, and the harvest of the potato is begun early in the years when the blight threatens the crop.

The varieties of the potato that are grown in the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) are selected for their resistance to the frost and the diseases, and the blue-skinned variety that is the most common in the north is the result of the selection that the farmers have practised for generations. The diversity of the varieties is the defence against the diseases that could destroy the crop, and the farmers who grow more than one variety are less likely to lose the entire harvest to the disease that afflicts a single variety.
