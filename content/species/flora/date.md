## Date

The date palm is the most important fruit tree of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), its presence marking the location of the oases that sustain the trade routes across the desert.

The date palm requires abundant water and intense heat, conditions that are found only in the oases and the river valleys of the interior. The fruit of the palm is harvested in the late summer, when the dates have turned brown and wrinkled and the sugar content is at its highest. The dates of the Kharesh are a staple food of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides who lead the caravans across the desert. The dates are eaten fresh during the harvest season and dried for the rest of the year, providing a concentrated source of energy that sustains the travellers through the long crossings of the desert. The trade in dates is a minor but significant part of the Sahrani economy, and the finest dates are exported to the markets of Aethelar and Morvind, where they are sold at prices that reflect the difficulty of their production and transport.

## Physical Characteristics of the Fruit

The date is a drupe of a cylindrical to the ovoid shape that measures from one to three inches in the length and up to an inch in the diameter in the cultivated varieties, the size and the shape varying with the variety and the conditions of the cultivation. The skin of the date is thin and papery, the colour changing from the green through the yellow and the amber to the deep brown as the fruit ripens on the tree, and the skin becomes wrinkled and translucent as the moisture content of the fruit decreases in the final stage of the ripening. The flesh of the date is thick and sweet, the texture ranging from the soft and the melting to the firm and the chewy depending on the variety and the degree of the ripening, and the flesh surrounds a single hard and elongated seed that occupies a significant portion of the volume of the fruit.

The sugar content of the ripe date is among the highest of any fruit that is cultivated in the known world, and the sugars of the date are principally the glucose and the fructose that are easily absorbed by the body and that provide the immediate and the sustained energy that makes the date such a valuable food for the travellers and the labourers. The date also contains the significant quantities of the potassium and the other minerals that are essential for the maintenance of the health in the conditions of the desert travel, and the date is a food that provides the nutrition as well as the energy to the people who depend on it for the sustenance. The seed of the date is hard and woody, and the seed is not consumed but is discarded or fed to the animals that are kept in the oasis communities.

The date passes through the four stages of the ripening that are recognised by the cultivators of the Kharesh, and each stage produces a fruit that is consumed in a different manner and that has a different place in the diet and the economy of the desert. The first stage, the kimri, is the green and the astringent fruit that is not eaten but is sometimes used in the preparation of the pickles and the preserves. The second stage, the khalal, is the fruit that has reached the full size and that is crisp and sweet but still moist, and the fruit of this stage is eaten fresh and is the favourite of the children of the oasis communities. The third stage, the rutab, is the fruit that has begun to soften and to ripen to the brown colour, and the fruit of this stage is the sweetest and the most prized of the fresh dates. The fourth stage, the tamr, is the fruit that has dried on the tree to the point where the moisture content is reduced to the level that allows the storage for the long periods, and the dates of this stage are the staple food that sustains the desert communities through the year and that is traded along the caravan routes.

## Varieties of the Kharesh Dates

The date palms of the Kharesh produce the many varieties that are distinguished by the size, the colour, the texture, and the flavour of the fruit, and the varieties are known by the names that the cultivators of the oases have given them over the centuries of the cultivation. The most highly regarded of the varieties is the medjool date, a large and the soft fruit that is prized for the rich and the caramel-like flavour and for the succulent texture that melts on the tongue. The medjool dates are the variety that is reserved for the consumption of the wealthy and the powerful of the Sahrani cities and for the export to the markets of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and the price of the medjool dates reflects the quality and the scarcity of the fruit.

The deglet noor date is a smaller and a firmer variety that is the most widely cultivated of the date varieties of the Kharesh, the fruit being the translucent amber colour when the fruit is ripe and the texture being firm and the flavour being delicate and honeyed. The deglet noor dates are the variety that is most commonly traded along the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way), and the deglet noor dates are the staple food of the desert travellers and the common people of the Sahrani cities. The halawy date is a soft and a sweet variety that is favoured for the fresh consumption, and the zahidi date is a dry and a firm variety that stores well and that is used in the preparation of the date paste and the date syrup.

The varieties of the dates are propagated by the offshoots of the parent trees, and the cultivators of the oases select the offshoots from the trees that produce the fruit of the desired quality. The preservation of the varieties is a matter of the importance for the oasis communities, and the cultivators who maintain the collections of the varieties are the guardians of the genetic heritage of the date palm that has been accumulated over the millennia of the cultivation.

## Harvesting and Processing

The harvest of the dates is conducted in the late summer and the early autumn, and the harvesters climb the trunks of the [date palms](/content/species/flora/date-palm) with the aid of the ropes and the harnesses that are designed for the purpose. The bunches of the dates are cut from the crowns of the palms with the knives that the harvesters carry, and the bunches are lowered to the ground where the dates are separated from the stems and sorted into the grades by the ripeness and the quality. The harvest of the dates is the labour-intensive process that requires the skill and the experience, and the harvesters who perform the work are among the most highly paid of the agricultural workers of the Sahrani interior.

The dates that are destined for the fresh consumption are packed carefully to prevent the bruising and the damage to the soft fruit, and the fresh dates are transported quickly to the markets of the coastal cities where they are sold at the premium prices. The dates that are destined for the drying are spread on the mats of the palm fronds and exposed to the sun and the dry air of the desert, and the sun-drying process reduces the moisture content of the fruit to the level where the dates can be stored without the spoilage. The dried dates are packed into the sacks of the camel-hair cloth and stored in the cool and the dry chambers of the oasis buildings, and the dried dates are the food that can be carried for the months on the journeys across the desert without the deterioration.

The processing of the dates into the secondary products is the activity that extends the usefulness of the harvest and that produces the commodities that are traded to the markets beyond the desert. The date syrup is produced by the boiling of the dates in the water and the reduction of the liquid to the thick and the sweet concentrate that is used as the sweetener in the dishes and the drinks of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) cuisine. The date paste is produced by the grinding of the dried dates into the dense and the nutritious mass that can be stored for the long periods, and the date paste is the form in which the dates are carried by the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) on the journeys across the deep desert where the space is limited and the weight of the provisions must be kept to the minimum.

## Nutritional Value and Sustenance

The date is among the most nutritious of the foods that are produced in the known world, and the date provides the combination of the energy, the minerals, and the vitamins that sustains the health and the strength of the people who consume the fruit as the staple of the diet. The date is a food of the high energy density, the sugar content of the dried date being among the highest of any food that the agriculture produces, and a handful of the dates provides the energy that sustains a person through the hours of the labour or the travel. The [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the caravans that cross the desert rely upon the dates as the primary source of the sustenance on the journeys that may last for the weeks or the months.

The date is also a source of the potassium and the magnesium and the other minerals that are essential for the maintenance of the health in the conditions of the desert travel, and the date provides the hydration as well as the nutrition when the fresh dates are consumed, the moisture content of the fresh fruit being the supplement to the water that the travellers carry. The date is a food that is easily digested and that does not burden the stomach on the long journeys through the heat, and the date is the ideal food for the travellers who must conserve the energy and the water on the crossings of the desert.

The dates are combined with the other foods to create the dishes that are the staples of the oasis cuisine, and the dates are often eaten with the milk of the camels and the goats that are kept in the oasis communities, the combination of the sweet and the creamy being the favourite of the desert peoples. The dates are also stuffed with the nuts and the almond paste, and the stuffed dates are the delicacy that is served at the feasts and the celebrations of the oasis communities. The dates are used in the preparation of the breads and the cakes, and the date syrup is the sweetener that replaces the honey and the sugar in the cooking of the desert peoples.

## Trade and Commerce

The trade in the dates is a significant component of the economy of the Sahrani interior, and the dates are transported along the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) to the markets of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the coastal cities of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the date merchants regulate the trade and maintain the standards of the quality and the grading of the fruit, and the guilds also arrange the transport of the dates through the caravans that ply the trade routes of the desert. The prices of the dates are determined by the quality and the variety and the season, and the finest dates command the prices that reflect the difficulty of the cultivation and the harvest and the transport from the remote oases of the interior.

The dates are exported to the markets of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) where the fruit is a luxury that is consumed by the wealthy households and the institutions, and the dates that reach the distant markets are the dried and the packaged product that has survived the long journey across the desert and the sea. The dates are sold in the [markets and fairs](/content/economy/markets-and-fairs) of the Aethelari cities, and the dates are displayed in the stalls of the merchants who specialise in the goods of the Sahrani trade. The price of the dates in the Aethelari markets is many times the price at the source in the oases, the difference reflecting the cost and the risk of the transport from the desert interior.

The [cartage and transport](/content/economy/cartage-and-transport) of the dates is the service that the caravan masters and the merchants provide, and the dates are among the most valuable of the goods that the caravans carry. The [trade and tariffs](/content/economy/trade-and-tariffs) that are imposed on the dates by the authorities of the regions through which the caravans pass add to the cost of the dates at the destination, and the tariffs that are levied by the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) on the dates that cross the border between the Sahrani and the Aethelari territories are a source of the revenue for the states that control the trade routes.

## Cultural Significance

The date is a food of the central importance in the culture and the religion of the desert peoples, and the date is the symbol of the hospitality and the generosity that the culture of the Kharesh values above all the other virtues. The offering of the dates to the guest is the first gesture of the welcome that the host extends, and the refusal of the dates that are offered is a grave insult in the etiquette of the desert. The date is also a symbol of the sustenance and the provision in the mythology of the Kharesh, and the date palm is the tree that the gods gave to the people of the desert so that they might survive in the land that the gods had given them.

The date is mentioned in the poetry and the song of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) tradition, and the poets of the desert describe the date as the fruit of the sun and the earth of the Kharesh, the food that is the taste of the home for the people of the desert. The date is also a symbol of the beauty and the sweetness in the Sahrani culture, and the eyes of the beloved are compared to the ripe date in the love poetry of the desert. The festivals that mark the harvest of the dates are the occasions for the celebration and the feasting in the oasis communities, and the sharing of the dates at the festivals is the expression of the community and the solidarity that sustain the life of the oasis.

The date is associated with the [folk omens and superstitions](/content/mythology/folk-omens-and-superstitions) of the desert peoples, and the appearance of the date crop is read as the sign of the fortune and the fate of the community in the year to come. The date palm that fails to produce the fruit is a sign of the disfavour of the spirits, and the rituals of the propitiation are performed to restore the fertility of the tree. The date palm that produces the abundant crop is a sign of the blessing, and the harvest of the abundant year is celebrated with the particular joy and the gratitude of the community.
