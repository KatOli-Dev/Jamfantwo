## The Alpine Pastures of the Morvind Uplands

The alpine pastures of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) are the summer grazing grounds of the herds of the northern peoples, and the grasses and the herbs that grow in the high meadows provide the forage that sustains the animals through the brief but intense growing season of the alpine zone.

## The Alpine Pastures of Morvind

The grasses of the alpine pastures are the low-growing and cold-tolerant species that are adapted to the conditions of the high elevation. The alpine meadows are used for the summer grazing of the [Reindeer](/content/species/fauna/reindeer) and the mountain sheep of the northern herds, and the animals are moved to the higher elevations as the snow retreats and the grasses begin to grow. The herders who accompany the animals spend the summer months in the temporary camps that are established in the high valleys, and the animals graze the alpine pastures until the return of the cold weather in the autumn.

The grass of the alpine pastures is not cut for the hay, for the growing season is too short and the conditions for the drying of the hay are not reliable. The animals that graze the alpine pastures must rely upon the fresh growth of the summer for the feeding, and the animals must be moved to the lower elevations in the winter when the snow covers the high pastures.

## Plant Communities of the High Meadows

The alpine pastures of the Morvind Uplands are composed of a mixture of the grasses, the sedges, and the herbs that are adapted to the conditions of the high elevation. The principal grasses are the fescues and the bents that form the dense turfs of the meadow, and the sedges that grow in the wetter areas where the snow lies late into the summer. The herbs of the alpine pasture include the [arnica](/content/species/flora/arnica) that is harvested for its medicinal properties, and the [clover](/content/species/flora/clover) that provides the protein that the grazing animals require.

The alpine pasture is a community of plants that has adapted to the harsh conditions of the high elevation in different ways. The grasses of the alpine zone are low-growing and spreading, forming the dense mats that resist the wind and the cold. The herbs of the alpine pasture are typically small and compact, with the leaves that grow close to the ground where the temperature is a few degrees warmer than the air above. The root systems of the alpine plants are extensive and shallow, spreading through the thin soil that lies above the rock of the mountain, and the roots absorb the nutrients that are released by the slow weathering of the mineral substrate.

The flowering of the alpine plants is concentrated in the brief period of the summer, and the meadows of the Morvind Uplands are a carpet of colour in the weeks of the July and the August. The flowers of the alpine pasture are small and vivid, and the colours of the flowers are the whites and the yellows and the purples that are visible to the insects that pollinate them in the short season.

## Seasonal Migration and Grazing

The movement of the herds to the alpine pastures is a migration that follows the retreat of the snow and the advance of the growing season. The herders of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) settlements drive their animals to the lower pastures in the spring, and the animals are moved to the higher meadows as the snow retreats and the grasses begin to grow. The migration is timed to the conditions of the season, and the experienced herders can read the signs of the weather and the snowpack to determine the moment at which the move to the higher pastures is safe.

The animals that graze the alpine pastures are the [reindeer](/content/species/fauna/reindeer) and the mountain sheep that are adapted to the conditions of the high elevation. The reindeer are the primary grazing animals of the alpine pastures, and the reindeer eat the lichens and the grasses that grow on the exposed slopes. The mountain sheep graze the herbs and the grasses of the meadows, and the sheep are more selective in their feeding than the reindeer. The herders manage the movement of the animals to prevent the overgrazing of the pastures, and the animals are moved from one meadow to another as the forage is consumed.

The temporary camps of the herders are established in the sheltered valleys that lie below the alpine pastures, and the camps are occupied for the duration of the summer grazing season. The shelters of the herders are the tents of the hide and the felt that are similar to the shelters of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) of the desert, and the shelters are designed to be dismantled and moved when the herds are moved. The herders return to the settlements of the lower valleys in the autumn, and the alpine pastures are left to the snow and the wind until the following summer.

## The Role of the Pasture in the Northern Economy

The alpine pastures of the Morvind Uplands are a resource that is essential to the economy of the northern peoples, for the pastures provide the grazing that sustains the herds that are the basis of the wealth of the northern settlements. The animals that graze the alpine pastures produce the meat and the milk and the wool that are the products of the northern economy, and the animals that are fattened on the alpine pastures are more productive than the animals that graze the lower pastures through the year.

The products of the alpine pastures are traded in the markets of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) settlements, and the wool of the mountain sheep that graze the alpine pastures is particularly valued for its fineness and its warmth. The cheese that is made from the milk of the animals that graze the alpine pastures has a distinctive flavour that is attributed to the herbs and the grasses of the high meadows, and the cheese is a product that is sought after in the markets of the northern regions.

## Threats to the Alpine Pastures

The alpine pastures of the Morvind Uplands are vulnerable to the changes in the climate that affect the timing and the duration of the growing season. A late spring or an early autumn can reduce the period of the grazing and the quantity of the forage that is available, and the herders must adjust the movement of the herds to the conditions of the season. The overgrazing of the pastures is a threat that is managed by the herders through the rotation of the grazing and the movement of the animals between the meadows.

The erosion of the soil of the alpine pastures is a threat that is exacerbated by the trampling of the animals and the action of the wind and the water on the exposed ground. The herders of the Morvind Uplands are careful to avoid the concentration of the animals in the areas where the soil is thin and the vegetation is sparse, and the traditional practices of the herders include the rotation of the grazing and the resting of the pastures to allow the recovery of the vegetation.
