## The Desert Puffball of the Kharesh

The desert puffball is the most common of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) fungi, appearing in the sandy soils of the desert after the winter rains. The puffball is a round and white fungus that grows to the size of a fist, and the flesh of the young puffball is white and firm. The puffball is edible when it is young, and the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) gather the puffballs in the season of the rains and cook them in the stews that accompany the travel rations of the desert.

## Physical Characteristics

The desert puffball is a globular fungus that ranges from the size of a walnut to the size of a fist in the mature specimens, the shape being the round and the roughly spherical and the base of the fungus being attached to the soil by the thin and the fragile cord of the mycelial strands. The outer surface of the puffball is white when the fungus is fresh and the outer skin is smooth or the texture of the surface being the slightly granular that the species displays, and the outer skin of the puffball is thin and papery and the skin is easily broken by the fingers. The interior of the young puffball is the solid and the white flesh that is firm and the texture being the spongy and the somewhat the resilient, the flesh being the homogeneous mass that is the undifferentiated tissue before the spores have begun to develop.

The puffball that has matured beyond the stage of the edibility undergoes the transformation of the interior tissue, and the solid white flesh of the young puffball becomes the mass of the powdery spores of the colour that is the brownish and the drab olive in the fully mature specimens. The outer skin of the mature puffball becomes thin and the brittle, and the pressure on the surface of the puffball causes the skin to break and the spores to be ejected in the cloud that is the method of the dispersal of the species. The mature puffballs are the objects that the children of the desert communities delight to step on for the puff of the spores that is released, and the children call the mature puffballs by the names that reflect the nature of the spore discharge.

The mycelium of the desert puffball is the network of the fine white threads that permeates the sandy soil of the desert, the mycelium extending through the upper layers of the soil and the mycelium surviving the long periods of the drought in the dormant state that the fungus adopts between the rains. The mycelium is associated with the roots of the [acacia](/content/species/flora/acacia) trees and the other desert plants, and the relationship between the fungus and the plant is the mycorrhizal association that benefits both organisms, the fungus receiving the sugars that the plant produces through the photosynthesis and the plant receiving the water and the minerals that the mycelium extracts from the soil in the exchange that the partnership involves.

## Habitat and Distribution

The desert puffball is a fungus of the arid and the semi-arid regions of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and the desert puffball is found throughout the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert and in the transition zone between the desert and the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent). The puffball appears in the sandy and the gravelly soils of the desert floor after the winter rains that provide the moisture that triggers the fruiting of the fungus, and the puffball is one of the first of the organisms to respond to the rains that transform the dormant desert into the brief period of the growth and the activity that the plants and the fungi experience in the season of the winter moisture.

The distribution of the desert puffball is limited to the regions where the winter rainfall is sufficient to wet the soil to the depth that the mycelium requires for the production of the fruiting bodies, and the puffball is not found in the deepest desert where the rains are too infrequent and too light to support the fruiting of the fungus. The puffball is most abundant in the areas of the desert that are the margins of the wadis and the dry watercourses, where the soil is deeper and the organic matter that the wash of the floods has accumulated provides the nutrients that the fungus requires for the fruiting. The puffball also appears in the shade of the [acacia](/content/species/flora/acacia) groves, where the shade reduces the evaporation of the moisture from the soil and the microclimate of the grove extends the period of the adequate soil moisture into the weeks after the rains have ceased.

The desert puffball is also found in the oases of the Kharesh where the cultivation of the [date palm](/content/species/flora/date-palm) and the other oasis crops creates the conditions of the permanent moisture and the organic enrichment of the soil that allow the puffball to fruit in the seasons beyond the immediate aftermath of the rains. The puffballs that grow in the gardens of the oases are larger and more abundant than the puffballs of the open desert, and the oasis communities gather the puffballs from their gardens in addition to the puffballs that the guides collect from the desert floor on the journeys through the interior.

## Life Cycle and Fruiting

The life cycle of the desert puffball is adapted to the extreme conditions of the desert environment, and the fungus has evolved the strategies of the survival that allow the species to persist through the years of the drought and to reproduce rapidly when the conditions of the moisture and the temperature are favourable. The mycelium of the puffball remains dormant in the soil through the months and the years of the drought, the mycelium being the network of the dehydrated threads that are the resting state of the fungus, and the mycelium is revived by the first of the rains that wet the soil to the sufficient depth and for the sufficient duration.

The fruiting of the puffball is triggered by the combination of the soil moisture and the temperature that occurs in the winter months, when the rains of the winter storms penetrate the soil and the temperature of the desert is cool but not cold. The fruiting bodies of the puffball emerge from the soil within the days of the rain that triggers the fruiting, and the growth of the puffball is rapid, the puffball reaching the full size within the week of the emergence. The rapidity of the growth is the adaptation that allows the puffball to complete the life cycle in the brief window of the moisture that the winter rains provide before the soil dries and the desert returns to the dormant state.

The spores of the desert puffball are released when the mature fruiting body is disturbed by the wind or the animal or the human that touches the surface of the puffball, and the spores are carried by the wind to the new sites where the mycelium may become established if the conditions of the moisture and the soil are favourable. The spores of the puffball are thick-walled and the spores are resistant to the desiccation and the high temperatures of the desert, and the spores can survive for the years in the soil before the conditions of the moisture trigger the germination and the growth of the new mycelium. The longevity of the spores in the soil is the adaptation that allows the puffball to persist through the years of the drought that would otherwise eliminate the species from the habitat.

## Harvesting and Gathering

The gathering of the desert puffballs is the activity that the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides conduct during the season of the winter rains, the puffballs being one of the foods that the desert provides at the time of the year when the travel rations are supplemented by the fresh produce of the season. The gatherers search the desert floor for the puffballs that have emerged after the rains, and the puffballs are located by the sight of the white spheres that contrast with the brown and the grey of the desert soil. The gatherers select the puffballs that are firm and white when the surface is broken, and the puffballs that have begun to turn yellow or brown in the interior are rejected, for the puffballs that are past the stage of the edibility are not palatable and may cause the digestive disturbances.

The gathering of the puffballs requires the knowledge of the habits and the distribution of the species, and the experienced gatherers know the areas of the desert where the puffballs are most likely to appear after the rains. The knowledge is transmitted from the generation to the generation in the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), and the children of the tribes accompany the adults on the gathering expeditions and learn the identification and the selection of the puffballs by the practice and the example. The [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides possess the particularly detailed knowledge of the locations and the conditions that favour the fruiting of the puffball, and the salawa guides are the resource that the tribes rely upon for the location of the puffball patches in the unfamiliar areas of the desert.

The gathering of the puffballs is conducted with the respect for the resource that the desert provides, and the gatherers are careful to leave the mature puffballs and the spores that they contain undisturbed so that the species may continue to reproduce and to provide the food for the future seasons. The disturbance of the mycelium by the digging or the trampling is avoided, and the puffballs are harvested by the gentle twisting of the fruiting body from the mycelial cord, the technique that minimises the damage to the underground network of the fungus.

## Culinary Uses and Preparation

The desert puffball is prepared by the simple methods that the desert peoples use for the cooking of the foods that are gathered from the wild, and the puffball is cleaned of the sand and the debris that adhere to the outer surface before the preparation. The outer skin of the puffball is peeled or the skin is wiped clean with the damp cloth, and the interior flesh of the puffball is cut into the slices or the cubes of the size that the recipe requires. The flesh of the puffball is white and firm and the flavour of the flesh is mild and the slightly nutty, the flavour being the delicate that is complemented by the fats and the seasonings that the cooks add in the preparation.

The most common of the methods of the preparation of the desert puffball is the cooking in the stews that are the daily fare of the desert camps, and the puffball is added to the pot with the meat and the [sorghum](/content/species/flora/sorghum) and the other vegetables that the stew includes. The slices of the puffball absorb the flavours of the broth and the seasonings, and the puffball contributes the texture and the body that the stew acquires from the presence of the fungus in the pot. The puffball is also cooked by the frying in the fat of the camel or the goat, the slices of the puffball being browned on the both sides in the hot pan and the fried puffball being served with the flatbread that is the accompaniment of the desert meals.

The desert puffball is also dried for the storage and the transport, the fresh puffballs being sliced and the slices being spread on the mats and exposed to the sun and the dry air of the desert until the moisture is removed. The dried puffball slices are stored in the cloth sacks and the dried puffball is carried on the journeys across the desert, the dried slices being reconstituted by the soaking in the water and the reconstituted puffball being used in the same manner as the fresh. The drying of the puffball is the method that the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) use to preserve the surplus of the harvest for the use in the months when the fresh puffballs are not available, and the dried puffball is the food that is stored against the scarcity of the other provisions in the desert.

## Nutritional Value

The desert puffball provides the nutrition that is the valuable supplement to the diet of the desert peoples, and the puffball contains the protein and the fibre and the minerals that the diet of the desert requires. The puffball is a food that is low in the fat and the carbohydrates, and the puffball is the addition to the meals that increases the volume and the variety of the food without the addition of the components that the diet of the desert peoples already contains in the excessive quantities. The puffball is also a source of the potassium and the phosphorus and the other minerals that are essential for the health of the people who live in the conditions of the desert, and the consumption of the puffball is the contribution to the balanced nutrition of the desert diet.

The puffball is a food that is easily digested and that does not burden the stomach of the traveller who must travel the long distances in the heat of the day, and the puffball is the food that the guides recommend for the meals that precede the journeys of the particular length or the difficulty. The salawa guides have observed over the centuries of the desert travel that the puffball is a food that sustains the traveller through the hours of the effort without the discomfort that the heavier foods can cause in the conditions of the travel.

## Cultural and Economic Significance

The desert puffball is a food that is valued by the desert peoples for the contribution to the diet during the season of the rains, and the puffball is one of the foods that mark the season of the winter in the culture of the desert. The appearance of the puffballs after the rains is the event that is celebrated in the communities of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), and the gathering of the puffballs is the occasion for the expeditions that involve the whole of the family or the group and that are the social activity as well as the economic one.

The desert puffball is also a food that is traded in the local markets of the desert communities, and the puffballs that are gathered in the surplus of the season are sold to the households that are unable to gather the puffballs for themselves. The trade in the desert puffballs is the component of the informal economy of the desert that supplements the income of the families that gather the puffballs, and the puffballs that are sold in the markets of the oasis settlements provide the cash that the gatherers use to purchase the goods that they require from the merchants of the oases.

The desert puffball is also a food that is shared in the traditions of the hospitality of the desert peoples, and the gift of the puffballs from the one household to the other is the gesture that expresses the solidarity and the community that sustain the life of the desert. The puffball is a food that is associated with the season of the rains and the renewal of the desert, and the sharing of the puffballs is the expression of the thanks for the bounty that the rains have brought and the hope for the continued provision of the resources that the desert yields to the peoples who know its ways.
