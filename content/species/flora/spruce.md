The spruce is the resonant conifer of the high elevations, its light and even-grained wood preferred by the instrument makers of Aethelar for the soundboards of lutes, harps, and dulcimers.

## Spruce for the Soundboards of Instruments

The spruce is a coniferous tree of the higher elevations and the colder regions, growing in the mountain forests of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the highlands of western [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The wood of the spruce is light and resonant, properties that make it the preferred material for the soundboards of the musical instruments that are produced by the instrument makers of the cities.

The spruce of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) is particularly valued for its even grain and its lack of knots, and the timber from this region is used in the production of the finest lutes, harps, and dulcimers that are crafted in the workshops of Aethelar. The instrument makers travel to the ports of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) to select their timber personally, and the pieces of spruce that they choose are aged for years before they are worked into instruments.

## Physical Characteristics

The spruce is a tall and the conical tree that grows to the height of the eighty to the hundred and twenty feet in the mature specimens that are found in the old growth forests of the mountains, and the spruce is one of the largest of the coniferous trees of the northern forests. The trunk of the spruce is the straight and the cylindrical, the trunk rising from the ground without the taper that the other conifers display until the trunk reaches the upper portion of the tree where the branches begin. The spruce that grows in the dense stands of the mountain forests produces the trunks that are free of the branches for the lower half or the lower two-thirds of the height, and the form of the trunk is the result of the shading of the lower branches by the canopy of the neighbouring trees and the shedding of the shaded limbs.

The bark of the spruce is the thin and the scaly, the colour being the reddish-brown on the young trees and the greyish-brown on the older specimens, and the bark is the surface that is covered with the small and the rounded scales that are the characteristic of the spruce species. The bark of the spruce that grows in the old forests of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) is the thin and the smooth in the comparison to the bark of the other conifers, and the thinness of the bark is the feature that the instrument makers inspect when they select the trees for the timber, for the thin bark is the indicator of the slow and the even growth that produces the wood of the finest quality.

The needles of the spruce are the short and the stiff and the four-sided, the needles being arranged singly around the twigs and the colour being the dark and the bluish-green. The needles of the spruce persist on the tree for the several years before they are shed, and the retention of the needles through the winter is the feature that distinguishes the spruce from the larch and the other deciduous conifers. The needles of the spruce are the sharp and the pointed, and the touch of the spruce needle is the prickly and the uncomfortable for the hand that brushes against the foliage the wrong way.

The cones of the spruce are the pendant and the cylindrical, the cones hanging from the upper branches of the tree and the cones being the colour that is the green when the cones are young and the brown when the cones have matured and the seeds have been released. The cones of the spruce are the long and the narrow, and the scales of the cone are the thin and the papery, the scales being the structures that the cone sheds after the seeds have been released and the empty cone falls from the tree to the ground. The seeds of the spruce are the small and the winged, the seeds being the structures that are dispersed by the wind from the cones in the autumn when the cones have dried and the scales have opened to release the seeds.

## The Timber and the Properties of the Wood

The wood of the spruce is the light and the even-grained and the resonant, and the properties of the spruce wood are the qualities that the instrument makers and the carpenters and the shipwrights of the known world value for the different uses that the spruce timber serves. The weight of the spruce wood is among the lightest of the timbers that the conifers produce, and the strength of the spruce wood for the weight is the high, the spruce being the timber that the builders choose for the structures where the weight of the material is the concern and the strength is the requirement.

The grain of the spruce wood is the straight and the even, the grain being the arrangement of the annual rings that the tree produces in the growing seasons of the years. The evenness of the grain is the result of the slow and the uniform growth that the spruce achieves in the cool and the moist conditions of the mountain forests, and the spruce that grows in the dense stands produces the wood that has the tight and the closely spaced rings that the instrument makers seek. The colour of the spruce wood is the pale and the cream to the light brown, and the heartwood of the spruce is not distinguishable from the sapwood by the colour, the uniformity of the colour being the feature that the carpenters prefer for the work where the appearance of the wood is the consideration.

The resonance of the spruce wood is the property that distinguishes the spruce from the other timbers and that makes the spruce the preferred material for the soundboards of the musical instruments. The soundboard of an instrument is the surface that vibrates in response to the strings and that projects the sound into the air, and the quality of the sound that the instrument produces depends on the properties of the wood that the soundboard is made from. The spruce wood that is light and the stiff and the even in the density transfers the vibrations of the strings to the air with the efficiency and the fidelity that the instrument makers require for the production of the instruments that the musicians play.

## The Spruce in Instrument Making

The use of the spruce for the soundboards of the musical instruments is the tradition that the instrument makers of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) have practised for the centuries, and the craft of the instrument making has developed the methods of the selection, the preparation, and the working of the spruce that produce the instruments of the highest quality. The instrument makers travel to the ports of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) and to the mountain forests of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to select the timber from the trees that have been cut by the foresters who supply the instrument makers with the wood of the quality that the craft demands.

The selection of the spruce for the instrument making is the process that the instrument makers conduct with the attention to the features of the tree that are the indicators of the quality of the wood. The instrument maker examines the bark for the indication of the growth rate and the straightness of the trunk for the indication of the grain, and the instrument maker taps the trunk with the hammer and listens to the sound that the wood produces, the resonance of the trunk being the clue to the properties of the wood that the tree contains. The instrument maker selects the trees that have the straight and the knot-free trunks and the bark that indicates the slow and the even growth, and the trees that the instrument maker selects are marked for the cutting by the foresters who will harvest the timber.

The aging of the spruce timber is the essential stage in the preparation of the wood for the instrument making, and the instrument makers age the timber for the periods of the years before the wood is worked into the instruments. The aging of the timber is the process that allows the wood to dry slowly and to stabilise the moisture content and the dimensions, and the slow drying of the spruce in the controlled conditions of the instrument maker's workshop is the practice that produces the wood that is the stable and the resonant and the workable for the craft. The instrument makers store the timber in the lofts and the storerooms of the workshops where the air circulates and the temperature is moderated, and the instrument maker checks the condition of the timber through the years of the aging.

The working of the spruce into the soundboard of an instrument is the task that the instrument maker performs with the tools and the techniques that the craft has developed. The timber is sawn into the thin planks that are the thickness of the soundboard, and the planks are planed and scraped to the final thickness and the smoothness that the soundboard requires. The joint between the two halves of the soundboard is the task that the instrument maker performs with the precision that ensures the perfect matching of the grain and the seamless gluing of the joint, the quality of the joint being the critical factor in the performance of the finished instrument.

The instruments that are made with the spruce soundboards include the lutes and the harps and the dulcimers and the many other stringed instruments that the musicians of the known world play, and the spruce soundboard is the component that the musicians regard as the essential to the quality of the sound that the instrument produces. The instrument makers of the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the luthiers and the instrument crafters compete for the finest timber of the spruce, and the price of the spruce that is of the instrument quality is the highest of any of the timbers that the instrument makers purchase.
