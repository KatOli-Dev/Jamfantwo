## Beech

The beech is the hard and close-grained tree of the Aethelaran lowlands, its uniform texture making it the choice for the workbenches of craftsmen and the floorboards of buildings where the traffic is heavy.

## Beech for the Workbenches and the Floorboards

The beech is a tree of the deeper soils of the Aethelaran lowlands, growing in pure stands or in mixture with oak and ash in the forests that cover the central and eastern regions of the continent. The wood of the beech is hard and close-grained, with a uniform texture that makes it ideal for the workbenches of the craftsmen and the floorboards of the buildings where the traffic is heavy.

The beech is not as resistant to decay as the oak, and the beech timber that is used in outdoor construction must be treated with preservatives or protected from the weather. The principal use of beech is in the production of the furniture that equips the households of the cities and the towns, where the pale colour of the wood and the smooth finish that it accepts make it a popular choice for the pieces that are painted or stained to imitate more expensive timbers.

The beech nuts that the tree produces in autumn are a food source for the wild boar and the deer that inhabit the forests, and the pigs that are pastured in the beech woods in the autumn months grow fat on the fallen nuts. The relationship between the beech forests and the pig herds of the Aethelaran settlements is an ancient one, and the right to pasture pigs in the beech woods is a valuable privilege that is granted by the owners of the forests.

## Physical Characteristics

The beech is a large deciduous tree that reaches a height of eighty to one hundred feet in the mature forests of the lowlands, and the trunk of the beech is straight and columnar, covered with the smooth and grey bark that is the distinctive feature of the species. The crown of the beech is dense and spreading, and the branches are arranged in the horizontal layers that cast a deep shade beneath the tree. The leaves of the beech are oval and entire, with the wavy margins that distinguish the beech from the toothed leaves of the [oak](/content/species/flora/oak) and the [ash](/content/species/flora/ash).

The leaves of the beech are bright green in the spring and the summer, turning to the copper and the bronze in the autumn before they fall. The leaves of the younger trees and the lower branches of the older trees are retained through the winter, and the brown leaves that cling to the branches are a characteristic of the beech that is observed in the hedges and the young plantings of the species. The beech produces the small and triangular nuts that are enclosed in the spiny husks, and the nuts are produced in the years that are called the mast years when the trees bear the heavy crops.

## Habitat and Distribution

The beech is a tree of the temperate lowlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and the beech is the dominant tree of the forests that cover the central and the eastern regions of the continent. The beech prefers the deeper soils of the lowlands and the lower slopes of the highlands, and the beech is found in the areas where the limestone and the chalk provide the alkaline soils that the tree favours. The beech is not found in the cold regions of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) uplands or the arid regions of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and the distribution of the beech is limited by the requirements of the tree for the warmth and the moisture.

The beech forests of the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) and the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) are the most extensive of the beech forests of the continent, and the forests of these regions are the source of the beech timber that is used in the furniture and the construction industries. The beech is also found in the mixed forests of the [Brackenshield Wood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/brackenshield-wood) and the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood), where the beech grows in the company of the oak and the ash. The [timber and logging](/content/economy/timber-and-logging) trade of these regions includes the harvest of the beech, and the beech is managed as a component of the mixed forests.

## Timber and Woodworking

The wood of the beech is hard and close-grained, with a uniform texture that makes it the ideal material for the workbenches and the floorboards that must withstand the abrasion and the impact of the daily use. The beech is the wood that is preferred for the workbenches of the carpenters and the joiners, and the surface of the beech workbench is smooth and stable, providing the flat and the true surface that the precise work requires. The floorboards of the beech are laid in the buildings where the traffic is heavy, and the beech floors are durable and resistant to the wear that would damage the softer woods.

The beech is also used in the production of the furniture that equips the households of the cities and the towns. The chairs and the tables that are made from the beech are simple and sturdy, and the pale colour of the beech wood is the ground for the paint and the stain that is applied to the furniture. The beech is the wood that is used in the production of the turned items, and the legs of the chairs and the spindles of the furniture are turned on the lathes that are operated by the furniture makers of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) settlements.

## The Mast and the Pannage

The beech nuts that the tree produces in the mast years are a food source of the great importance for the wild animals of the forests and the domestic animals that are pastured in the woods. The wild boar and the deer that inhabit the beech forests feed on the fallen nuts, and the animals that fatten on the mast are the prized game of the hunters of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) settlements. The pigs that are pastured in the beech woods in the autumn are the source of the pork that is the winter meat of the northern households, and the pigs that feed on the beech mast produce the meat that is considered the finest of the pork.

The right to pasture the pigs in the beech woods is a privilege that is called the pannage, and the pannage is a valuable right that is granted by the owners of the forests to the communities that are adjacent to the woods. The pannage is a practice that is regulated by the customs and the laws of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) settlements, and the number of the pigs that may be pastured in the woods and the duration of the pannage season are determined by the agreements that are made between the communities and the forest owners.

## Cultural Significance

The beech is a tree of the cultural significance in the traditions of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) peoples, and the beech is associated with the learning and the writing. The smooth bark of the beech has been used as a surface for the carving of the inscriptions and the messages, and the word for the book in the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) language is derived from the word for the beech, a reflection of the ancient practice of the writing on the bark of the tree. The [Reading Cloisters](/content/religion/cloisters-of-reading) maintain the groves of the beech in the grounds of the cloisters, and the beech is a tree that is associated with the knowledge and the scholarship in the traditions of the cloisters.

The beech is also a tree of the beauty and the shade in the landscapes of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) lowlands, and the great beeches of the parks and the avenues are the landmarks of the estates of the wealthy. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) consider the beech a tree of the wisdom, and the beech groves of the Heartwood are the places of the contemplation and the study. The beech is a tree that is woven into the fabric of the culture and the landscape of the continent, and the tree is valued for the timber and the mast that it provides and the symbolism that it carries.
