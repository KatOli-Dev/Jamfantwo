## Nettle and the Purification of the Blood

The stinging nettle is a plant that is considered both a weed and a medicine, its leaves being used in the treatment of the skin complaints and the joint pains that are the result of the accumulation of impurities in the blood. The nettle grows in the waste places and the disturbed soils of every region of Aethelar, and the plant is gathered by the healers in the spring when the leaves are young and tender.

The nettle is prepared as an infusion that is drunk as a tea, or the fresh leaves are applied directly to the skin in the treatment of the rheumatic pains that afflict the elderly. The sting of the nettle is neutralised by the drying or the cooking of the leaves, and the resulting preparation is safe for consumption or application. The spring tonic of nettle tea is a tradition in many of the northern settlements, and the regular consumption of the tea is believed by the healers to purify the blood and to restore the vitality that is lost during the winter months.

## Physical Characteristics

The stinging nettle is a perennial herb that grows to a height of three to five feet, the stems being erect and square and the leaves being arranged in the opposite pairs that are characteristic of the species. The leaves of the nettle are oval and toothed, the surfaces being dark green and the undersides being paler, and the leaves and the stems being covered with the stiff hairs that are the stinging apparatus of the plant. The stinging hairs are the hollow structures that contain the formic acid and the histamine that are released when the hairs are broken, the chemicals causing the painful rash and the inflammation that the contact with the nettle produces.

The flowers of the nettle are small and inconspicuous, the flowers being borne in the clusters that hang from the upper leaf axils, and the flowers being of the greenish colour that is difficult to distinguish from the foliage. The nettle is a dioecious plant, the male and the female flowers being borne on the separate plants, and the female flowers producing the small seeds that are dispersed by the wind and the animals that brush against the plants. The root system of the nettle is extensive and creeping, the roots spreading by the underground stems that are called the rhizomes, and the nettle forming the dense colonies that are the characteristic of the waste places where the plant is established.

## Habitat and Distribution

The nettle is a plant of the waste places and the disturbed soils, the species being found in every region of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) where the soil has been disturbed and the vegetation has been removed. The nettle is a plant that is associated with the human habitation, the plant being found in the gardens and the pastures and the roadsides and the rubbish tips that are the features of the settled landscape. The nettle is a plant that thrives in the rich and the disturbed soils that the human activity creates, the plant being one of the species that has followed the human settlement and the cultivation across the continent.

The nettle is a plant that is found in the greatest abundance in the temperate regions of Aethelar, the plant being less common in the cold regions of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the warm regions of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The nettle is a plant that is adapted to the moist and the fertile soils that the temperate climate provides, the plant requiring the moisture and the nutrients that the waste places and the disturbed soils of the settled regions contain. The nettle is also found in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) margins and the clearings where the forest has been disturbed, the nettle being one of the plants that colonise the openings in the forest canopy and the edges of the paths and the tracks that the travellers and the foresters use.

## The Medicinal Uses and the Spring Tonic

The medicinal uses of the nettle are the uses that have been developed by the healers of the northern settlements over the centuries of the practice, the nettle being one of the plants that is most widely used in the folk medicine of the northern regions. The spring tonic of the nettle tea is the most common of the preparations, the tea being made by the infusing of the dried nettle leaves in the hot water and the drinking of the infusion as a beverage that is both refreshing and medicinal. The nettle tea is the tonic that is drunk in the early spring when the fresh greens are scarce and the body is weakened by the winter, the tea being the means by which the vitamins and the minerals that the nettle contains are introduced into the diet after the months of the preserved and the dried foods.

The nettle is also used in the treatment of the rheumatic pains and the joint complaints that afflict the elderly, the fresh leaves being applied to the skin in the practice that is known as the urtication, the stinging of the nettle being used to stimulate the blood flow and to reduce the inflammation that causes the pain. The urtication is a treatment that is painful but effective, the counter-irritation of the nettle sting providing the relief that the other remedies do not, and the healers who practice the urtication are the specialists who know the method and the duration that the treatment requires. The nettle is also used in the treatment of the skin complaints and the wounds, the nettle being one of the plants that the healers use for the astringent and the haemostatic properties that the plant exhibits.

## The Nutritional Value and the Food

The nettle is a plant that is not only a medicine but also a food, the young leaves of the nettle being edible when they are cooked and the stinging properties are neutralised by the heat. The nettle is gathered in the spring when the leaves are young and tender, the leaves being picked with the gloves that protect the hands from the stinging, and the gathered leaves are boiled in the water that destroys the stinging hairs and makes the leaves safe to eat. The nettle is prepared as a soup or a potherb, the cooked leaves being similar to the spinach in the flavour and the texture, and the nettle being a source of the vitamins and the minerals that are scarce in the early spring.

The nettle soup is a traditional dish of the northern settlements, the soup being made by the boiling of the nettle leaves with the onions and the potatoes and the seasoning that the regional cuisine provides. The nettle soup is the food that is associated with the spring and the renewal, the soup being the first fresh food that is available after the winter, and the nettle soup being the dish that is served in the households and the inns of the northern settlements in the weeks when the winter stores are exhausted and the new crops are not yet ready. The [inns and hospices](/content/economy/inns-and-hospices) of the northern regions serve the nettle soup as a seasonal specialty, and the nettle soup is one of the dishes that the travellers and the local people associate with the arrival of the spring and the end of the winter.

## Cultural Significance and the Weed of the Waste Places

The nettle is a plant that is woven into the culture and the folklore of the northern settlements, the plant being the symbol of the resilience and the endurance that the people of the north value in the harsh conditions of the climate. The nettle is a plant that is both feared and respected, the stinging of the nettle being the experience that every child in the northern settlements learns to avoid, and the nettle being the plant that is the sign of the waste places and the disturbed ground that the settlement and the cultivation leave behind. The nettle is also the plant that is the sign of the spring and the renewal, the first green shoots of the nettle being the sign that the winter is ending and the new growth is beginning.

The nettle is also associated with the folklore and the superstitions of the northern people, the nettle being the plant that is believed to have the protective properties that ward off the evil and the misfortune. The nettle that is hung above the door of the house is the nettle that is believed to protect the household from the spirits and the creatures that walk in the night, and the nettle that is carried in the pocket is the nettle that is believed to bring the luck and the prosperity to the bearer. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) regard the nettle as a plant that is under the protection of the forest spirits, and the gathering of the nettle in the Heartwood is accompanied by the rituals that the elves observe to honour the spirits and the plant.
