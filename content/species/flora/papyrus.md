## Papyrus

The papyrus that grows in the shallow pools and the slow-moving waterways of the Sahrani interior is a plant that has been used for the production of the writing material since the earliest times.

The papyrus plant is a tall and triangular-stemmed sedge that grows to a height of fifteen feet or more, and the stems of the papyrus are used in the production of the paper that is used by the scribes of the [Reading Cloisters](/content/religion/cloisters-of-reading) for the production of the manuscripts. The production of the papyrus paper is a skilled process that involves the cutting of the stems of the papyrus into thin strips, the laying of the strips in the overlapping layers, and the pressing and the drying of the strips into a sheet that is smooth and durable. The papyrus paper that is produced in the Sahrani interior is the finest in the known world, and the manuscripts that are written on the Sahrani papyrus are valued for the quality of the writing surface.

## Physical Characteristics of the Papyrus Plant

The papyrus is a member of the sedge family, a group of plants that are characterised by the triangular stems and the absence of the leaves that are typical of the other grasses. The papyrus grows to a height of fifteen feet or more, the stems being topped by the umbels of the green and the thread-like rays that give the plant the appearance of a feather duster. The stems of the papyrus are green and smooth, the surface being covered by a waxy coating that repels the water and protects the plant from the excessive loss of the moisture in the heat of the Sahrani sun.

The root of the papyrus is a rhizome, a horizontal stem that grows beneath the surface of the mud and sends up the new stems at intervals along its length. The rhizome is the means by which the papyrus spreads, the new stems emerging from the rhizome to form the dense stands that are the characteristic growth form of the plant in the shallow water. The rhizome is also the organ that stores the starches and the sugars that sustain the plant through the periods of the drought, when the water levels in the marshes fall and the stems die back to the ground.

## Habitat and Distribution

The papyrus grows in the shallow pools and the slow-moving waterways of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) interior, the plant being found in the marshes and the swamps that line the rivers of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent). The papyrus requires the shallow water and the rich mud that is deposited by the rivers, and the plant does not grow in the deep water or in the fast-flowing streams that scour the mud from the bed. The papyrus is also found in the oases of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), where the permanent water supplies the conditions that the plant requires, and the papyrus stands of the oases are among the most productive of the plant communities of the desert.

The largest of the papyrus swamps is the marsh that lies at the confluence of the rivers of the interior, a wetland that covers hundreds of square miles and that is the principal source of the papyrus that is used in the production of the writing material. The papyrus swamp is a dense and impenetrable thicket of the stems, the canopy of the umbels closing over the water and shutting out the light, and the swamp is inhabited by the birds and the reptiles that find their food and their shelter in the dense growth.

## The Production of Papyrus Paper

The production of the papyrus paper is a skilled process that has been practised in the Sahrani interior since the earliest times, and the techniques that are used in the production are the closely guarded secrets of the families that have specialised in the manufacture for generations. The stems of the papyrus are cut at the base and carried to the workshops, where the green outer skin is peeled away to reveal the white pith that is the raw material of the paper.

The pith is cut into thin strips, the strips being as long as the stem and as wide as a finger, and the strips are soaked in the water of the river for several hours to soften the fibres and to make them pliable. The softened strips are laid in two layers on a flat surface, the first layer being laid in one direction and the second layer being laid at right angles to the first, the overlapping strips forming a mat that is pressed and dried into a single sheet.

The pressing of the papyrus sheet is the most critical stage of the process, for the pressure must be sufficient to bond the strips together into a single surface but not so great that the strips are crushed and the surface is damaged. The pressed sheets are dried in the sun, the drying taking several days, and the dried sheets are then polished with a smooth stone or a piece of ivory to produce the smooth and glossy surface that the scribes require for the writing.

## The Uses of Papyrus in the Sahrani Economy

The papyrus paper is the principal writing material of the known world, and the scribes of the [Reading Cloisters](/content/religion/cloisters-of-reading) use the papyrus paper for the production of the manuscripts that are the records of the knowledge and the laws of the three continents. The papyrus paper is also used in the correspondence of the merchants and the officials, and the letters that are written on the papyrus are carried by the couriers to every city of the known world.

The papyrus plant has other uses in addition to the production of the writing material. The stems of the papyrus are used in the construction of the boats that navigate the waterways of the interior, the stems being bundled and tied to form the hulls of the lightweight craft that are the principal means of the transport in the marshes. The umbels of the papyrus are woven into the garlands that are worn in the festivals of the Sahrani coast, and the dried stems are used as the fuel for the fires that burn in the hearths of the marsh settlements.

## Trade and the Regulation of the Papyrus Industry

The trade in papyrus paper is one of the most profitable of the Sahrani trades, and the papyrus that is produced in the interior is exported to every region of the known world. The papyrus is carried in the bundles that are wrapped in the linen and transported on the boats that travel the rivers to the coast, where the papyrus is transferred to the ships that carry it to the markets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra). The papyrus is also carried by the caravans that travel the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) to the markets of Aethelar and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind).

The production of the papyrus paper is regulated by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the paper makers, who control the quality of the product and the prices at which the paper is sold. The guilds maintain the standards of the craft by the apprenticeship system, the young men who enter the trade being trained for years in the techniques of the production before they are allowed to produce the paper that is sold under the seal of the guild. The papyrus paper that is produced without the authorisation of the guild is confiscated and destroyed, and the penalties for the unauthorised production are severe.
