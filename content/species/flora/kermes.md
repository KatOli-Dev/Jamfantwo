## Kermes

The kermes insect that lives on the leaves of the kermes oak of the Sahrani foothills produces a red dye that is more intense and more permanent than the red of the northern madder.

The dried bodies of the female insects are ground to a powder that yields a crimson colour when mixed with a mordant, and the crimson of the kermes is the standard against which the red dyes of the known world are measured. The collection of the kermes insects is the work of the women and the children of the villages that surround the oak groves of the interior. The insects are collected in the spring, when the females are full of the eggs that contain the pigment, and the collected insects are dried in the sun and packed into sealed containers for transport to the markets of the coast. The crimson that the kermes produces is used in the dyeing of the robes of the high priests and the garments of the sovereigns, and the cloth that is dyed with the kermes crimson is the most expensive cloth that is produced in the known world.

## The Kermes Oak and the Insect

The kermes insect is a scale insect that lives on the branches and the leaves of the kermes oak, a species of oak that is found in the foothills and the uplands of the Sahrani interior. The kermes oak is a small and shrubby tree that grows in the dry and the rocky conditions of the foothills, the tree being adapted to the poor soil and the scarce water that characterise the regions where the oak is found. The kermes insect feeds on the sap of the oak, the female insect attaching itself to the branches and the leaves of the tree and remaining stationary for the duration of its adult life, the insect being covered by the protective scale that conceals the body and the eggs.

The kermes insect is a small and inconspicuous creature, the adult female being about the size of a grain of the barley and the colour of the dark reddish brown that is the colour of the dye that the insect produces. The female insect produces the eggs that contain the pigment, the eggs being laid in the chamber beneath the scale, and the pigment that is found in the eggs and the body of the female is the source of the crimson dye that has been valued for the centuries. The male kermes insect is smaller and winged, the male playing no part in the production of the dye and being present only for the brief period of the mating that occurs in the early spring.

## Collection and the Harvest

The collection of the kermes insects is a delicate and the labour-intensive operation that is performed by the women and the children of the villages that are located near the kermes oak groves. The insects are collected in the spring, the timing of the harvest being critical, for the females must be gathered when they are full of the eggs and before the eggs hatch and the pigment is lost. The collectors scrape the insects from the branches and the leaves of the oak with the small knives and the fingers, the insects being gathered into the baskets that are carried to the villages where the insects are dried and processed.

The collection of the kermes is a tradition that has been practiced for the centuries in the Sahrani interior, the villages that are located near the oak groves having the rights to the collection that are defined by the customs and the agreements that have been established over the generations. The [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) women who collect the kermes are the specialists who know the groves and the trees and the timing of the harvest, and the knowledge of the kermes collection is passed from the mother to the daughter in the families that have collected the insects for the generations. The collection of the kermes is a seasonal occupation that supplements the income of the villages, the kermes providing the cash that the villages need for the purchase of the goods that they cannot produce themselves.

## Processing and the Dye Preparation

The processing of the kermes insects is a simple but the careful operation that converts the collected insects into the dye that is traded to the markets. The collected insects are dried in the sun, the drying being done on the mats that are laid out in the courtyards of the villages, and the dried insects are sorted and cleaned to remove the fragments of the bark and the leaves that are mixed with the insects. The dried insects are then ground to a fine powder in the mortars that are reserved for the purpose, the powder being the form in which the kermes is used by the dyers.

The kermes dye is prepared for the dyeing by the mixing of the powder with the mordant that fixes the colour to the cloth, the mordant being the alum or the tartar that the dyers use for the red dyes. The cloth that is to be dyed is immersed in the dye bath that contains the kermes powder and the mordant, the cloth being simmered in the bath for the period that is required for the colour to penetrate and to fix. The crimson that the kermes produces is a deep and the intense colour that does not fade with the washing or the exposure to the light, the permanence of the kermes being one of the qualities that makes the dye so valued by the dyers and the wearers of the crimson cloth.

## Trade and the Commerce of Kermes

The trade in the kermes is a significant component of the [spices and dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) trade that connects the Sahrani interior to the markets of the coast and the northern continents. The kermes that is collected in the villages of the interior is sold to the merchants who specialise in the dye trade, the merchants who visit the villages in the spring to purchase the dried insects and the powder that the villages have produced. The kermes is then transported to the markets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), where the dye is sold to the dyers who produce the crimson cloth that is traded to every region of the known world.

The price of the kermes is high, the dye being one of the most expensive of the colourants that are traded in the known world, the cost reflecting the labour-intensive process of the collection and the limited quantity that the harvest produces. The kermes is sold by the weight, the dye being measured in the small quantities that are sufficient for the dyeing of the individual garments, and the price of a single garment's worth of the kermes may be equivalent to the income of a village for a season. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the dyers and the merchants regulate the trade in the kermes, the guilds setting the standards of the quality and the prices that are paid for the different grades of the dye.

The garments of the sovereigns and the nobles of the northern continents are also dyed with the kermes, the crimson being the colour that is reserved for the use of the highest ranks of the society.

The [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) that are dyed with the kermes are the textiles that are the most valued of the cloths that are produced in the known world, and the possession of the kermes-dyed cloth is the sign of the wealth and the status that the individual has achieved. The kermes is a dye that is woven into the culture and the traditions of the known world, the crimson being the colour that has been the symbol of the power and the authority for the centuries that the dye has been traded and used.
