## Lichen Dye

The lichens that grow on the rocks of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) and the cliffs of the [Glasslight Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/glasslight-coast) produce a purple dye that is used in the dyeing of the cloth of the northern settlements.

The lichens are collected by the shore dwellers who scrape the lichens from the rocks with iron tools, and the collected lichens are dried and processed to extract the purple pigment. The purple of the northern lichens is not as intense as the purple of the murex shellfish of the Sahrani coast, but the northern purple is much cheaper and more accessible, and the cloth that is dyed with the lichen purple is the common cloth of the middle classes of the northern cities. The trade in the lichen dyes is a minor but characteristic part of the economy of the northern coastal settlements.

## The Lichens of the Coastal Rocks

The lichens that are used for the dye are the species that grow on the rocks and the cliffs of the northern coasts, the lichens being the organisms that are composed of the symbiotic relationship between the fungi and the algae that form the body of the lichen. The lichens that produce the purple dye are the species that grow in the spray zone of the coast, the lichens being found on the rocks that are exposed to the salt spray and the wind that characterise the coastal environment. The lichens are slow-growing and the colonies that are found on the rocks are the result of the decades and the centuries of the growth that the lichens have accumulated.

The lichens that are used for the dye are the species that are known by the shore dwellers as the purple lichens, the lichens being identified by the colour of the dye that they produce rather than by the botanical characteristics that the scholars use. The purple lichens are found in the greatest abundance on the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) and the [Glasslight Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/glasslight-coast), the coasts that are exposed to the full force of the storms and the spray that the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) generates. The lichens are also found on the rocks of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) coast, the lichens of the western coast being the species that are collected by the shore dwellers of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and traded to the dyers of the inland settlements.

## Collection and the Shore Dwellers

The collection of the lichens is the work of the shore dwellers who live in the villages and the hamlets that are scattered along the northern coasts, the lichens being gathered in the low tide when the rocks are exposed and the lichens are accessible. The collectors use the iron tools that are made for the purpose, the tools being the knives and the scrapers that are designed to remove the lichens from the rock without damaging the substrate that the lichens grow on. The collected lichens are carried in the baskets that are made from the [reed](/content/species/flora/reed) that grows in the marshes of the coast, and the lichens are dried in the sun and the wind that the coast provides.

The collection of the lichens is a seasonal occupation that is combined with the other activities of the shore dwellers, the lichen collection being the work that is done in the spring and the autumn when the tides are low and the weather is settled. The shore dwellers who collect the lichens are the people who also fish and gather the [shellfish](/content/species/fauna/shellfish) and the other products of the coast, the lichens being one of the many resources that the coastal environment provides. The [fishing and whaling](/content/economy/fishing-and-whaling) and the lichen collection are the activities that define the economy of the shore dwellers, and the lichen dye is one of the products that the shore dwellers sell to the merchants who trade along the coast.

## Processing and the Dye Extraction

The processing of the lichens is the operation that extracts the purple pigment from the dried lichens, the process being done by the dyers who purchase the lichens from the shore dwellers. The lichens are soaked in the water that is mixed with the ammonia that is derived from the urine, the ammonia being the reagent that releases the pigment from the lichen tissue. The soaked lichens are left in the covered vats for the period of several weeks, the fermentation that occurs in the vat producing the conditions that dissolve the pigment and that create the dye bath that the dyers use.

The lichen dye bath is prepared by the straining of the fermented liquor and the addition of the mordant that fixes the colour to the cloth, the mordant being the alum or the iron that the dyers use for the different shades of the purple. The cloth that is to be dyed is immersed in the bath and simmered for the period that is required for the colour to penetrate and to fix, the cloth being removed and rinsed and dried when the desired shade has been achieved. The purple that the lichen produces is a colour that ranges from the lavender to the deep violet, the shade being determined by the species of the lichen and the mordant that is used and the duration of the dyeing.

## The Purple of the Northern Middle Classes

The lichen purple is the dye that is used for the cloth of the middle classes of the northern cities, the purple being the colour that is affordable to the people who cannot afford the expensive dyes that are imported from the south. The purple of the lichen is not as intense or as permanent as the purple of the murex shellfish that is the dye of the [kermes](/content/species/flora/kermes) and the other luxury dyes, but the lichen purple is the colour that is worn by the merchants and the artisans and the other prosperous people who are the middle class of the northern settlements. The [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) that are dyed with the lichen are the textiles that are the everyday wear of the northern people, and the lichen purple is the colour that is the sign of the moderate prosperity that the middle class represents.

The lichen purple is also used in the dyeing of the cloth that is used in the churches and the other religious buildings, the purple being the colour that is associated with the liturgical traditions of the northern faiths. The altar cloths and the vestments that are used in the ceremonies of the northern churches are the cloths that are dyed with the lichen, the purple being the colour that signifies the spiritual significance of the objects and the occasions. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the dyers regulate the production of the lichen-dyed cloth, the guilds setting the standards of the quality and the prices that are paid for the different grades of the lichen dye.

## Trade and the Coastal Economy

The trade in the lichen dyes is a minor but the characteristic part of the economy of the northern coastal settlements, the lichens being one of the products that the shore dwellers sell to the merchants who trade along the coast. The lichens are traded in the dried form, the lichens being packed in the sacks and the baskets that are carried by the ships and the carts to the dyers who work in the inland cities. The [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) is the principal conduit for the lichen trade, the league being the association of the coastal towns that regulates the commerce of the northern shore.

The lichen dye is also traded to the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth), the dyers of these regions using the lichen for the production of the purple cloth that is worn by the middle classes of the inland settlements. The lichen is one of the [spices and dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) that are traded from the coast to the interior, and the lichen dye is the dye that is the affordable alternative to the expensive dyes that are imported from the south. The lichen is a product of the coast that is valued in the interior, and the trade in the lichen is the trade that connects the shore dwellers to the dyers and the merchants of the inland cities, the lichen being one of the links that bind the coastal and the inland economies of the northern regions.
