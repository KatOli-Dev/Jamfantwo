## The Stone Fungus of the Dwarven Halls

The principal cultivated fungus of the dwarven halls is the stone fungus, a large and meaty species that grows in the beds of composted plant material that the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) prepare in the passages. The stone fungus has a texture that resembles the meat of the pig, and the flavour is earthy and rich. The fungus is harvested at the stage when the caps have opened but before the spores have been released, and the harvested fungi are consumed fresh or dried for storage.

## Physical Characteristics

The stone fungus is a large and robust species that produces a cap measuring up to a foot in the diameter in the mature specimens, the cap being a dense and fleshy structure that is firm to the touch and heavy in the hand. The cap of the stone fungus is a pale grey-brown on the upper surface, the colour resembling the stone from which the fungus takes its name, and the underside of the cap is covered with the fine gills that are white when the fungus is young and darkening to a deep brown as the spores mature. The stem of the stone fungus is thick and short, the texture of the stem being dense and fibrous and the flavour of the stem being more intense than the flavour of the cap.

The stone fungus does not produce the fruiting body above the ground in the manner of the surface fungi, and the cap of the stone fungus emerges from the composted substrate in the confined spaces of the cultivation chambers, the growth of the fungus being directed by the conditions of the temperature and the humidity that the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) maintain in the halls. The fungus does not require the light for the growth, and the cultivation chambers are kept in the continuous darkness that the underground environment provides naturally.

## Cultivation in the Dwarven Halls

The cultivation of the stone fungus is one of the principal agricultural activities of the [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar) holds, and the fungus is grown in the warm and humid passages that the dwarves have adapted for the purpose of the cultivation. The substrate for the fungus is prepared from the composted plant material that the dwarves collect from the surface and the trade, the material being mixed with the mineral dust that is a by-product of the mining and the stoneworking of the holds. The mineral dust provides the calcium and the other minerals that the stone fungus requires for the development of the dense and the firm texture that distinguishes the species.

The beds of the substrate are prepared in the shallow troughs that are carved into the floors of the cultivation chambers, and the troughs are filled to a depth of six inches with the composted material. The spores of the stone fungus are introduced to the substrate by the insertion of the plugs of the infected wood that the cultivators maintain as the parent stock, and the mycelium of the fungus spreads through the substrate in the weeks following the inoculation. The temperature of the cultivation chambers is maintained at the level that the stone fungus requires, the warmth of the volcanic springs that heat the holds providing the steady temperature that the cultivation demands.

The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) have developed the techniques of the cultivation over the centuries of the continuous production, and the methods that are used in the holds are the result of the accumulated experience of the generations of the cultivators. The cultivators monitor the moisture and the temperature of the beds with the instruments that the dwarven crafters have designed for the purpose, and the adjustments to the conditions are made with the precision that the dwarven approach to the agriculture demands.

## Preparation and Culinary Use

The stone fungus is prepared by the methods that the [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) cooks have developed to exploit the texture and the flavour of the species, and the dishes that feature the stone fungus are among the most distinctive of the dwarven cuisine. The fresh fungus is sliced and grilled on the stone slabs that the dwarves heat in the fires of the holds, the grilling producing a crust on the surface of the slices that seals in the moisture and the flavour of the flesh. The grilled slices of the stone fungus are served with the sauces that the dwarven cooks prepare from the [root vegetables](/content/species/flora/root-vegetable) and the salt that the dwarves trade from the surface.

The dried stone fungus is reconstituted by the soaking in the water before the use, and the reconstituted fungus is used in the stews and the soups that are the daily fare of the dwarven halls. The drying of the fungus is done in the air currents that the dwarves channel through the ventilation shafts of the holds, the dry air of the deep passages removing the moisture from the sliced fungus in a matter of days. The dried stone fungus is stored in the sealed containers that the dwarven potters produce, and the dried fungus retains the flavour and the texture for the periods of a year or more.

## Economic Significance

The stone fungus is a staple of the diet of the [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) population and a significant component of the economy of the holds. The cultivation of the fungus provides the food that sustains the population of the underground cities, and the surplus of the production is traded to the surface communities in the exchange for the goods that the dwarves cannot produce in the holds. The dried stone fungus is exported to the markets of the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) cities, where the fungus is sold as a specialty ingredient for the cooks of the wealthy households who value the earthy flavour and the meaty texture of the species.

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the dwarven cultivators regulate the production and the trade of the stone fungus, and the guilds maintain the standards of the quality and the pricing that govern the commerce in the product. The cultivators who produce the stone fungus are among the most respected of the agricultural workers of the holds, and the knowledge of the cultivation is passed from the master to the apprentice in the tradition that the dwarves apply to all of the skilled crafts.

## Cultural Importance

The stone fungus holds a place of the significance in the culture of the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) that extends beyond the role of the food, and the fungus is associated with the values of the endurance and the sustenance that the dwarven culture prizes. The stone fungus is served at the feasts that celebrate the founding of the holds and the achievements of the community, and the presence of the stone fungus at the table is the mark of the occasion that is marked by the gathering. The [dwarven ancestor worship](/content/religion/dwarven-ancestor-worship) includes the offering of the stone fungus at the shrines of the ancestors, the fungus being the symbol of the sustenance that the earth provides to the people who dwell within it.
