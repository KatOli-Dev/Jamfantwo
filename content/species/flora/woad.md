## Woad

Woad is the source of the blue dye that is used in the dyeing of the cloth that is produced in the northern settlements of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind).

The woad plant is a member of the cabbage family that grows in the temperate regions of the north, and the leaves of the plant contain the pigment that produces the blue colour that is characteristic of the cloth of the northern peoples.

The woad leaves are harvested in the summer, and the pigment is extracted from the leaves by a process of fermentation that is one of the most closely guarded secrets of the dyers' trade. The fermented leaves are formed into cakes that are dried and stored for the use of the dyers in the winter months when the fresh leaves are not available. The dye from the woad cakes is prepared by grinding the cakes and mixing the powder with water, and the cloth that is dyed in the woad bath takes on a blue colour that ranges from the pale sky blue of the diluted dye to the deep indigo of the concentrated bath.

The woad of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) is exported to the cities of the south, where the dyers use the woad as a base upon which they apply the more expensive dyes of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast. The cloth that is dyed with woad is the most common cloth of the northern settlements, and the blue garments that the northern peoples wear are the mark of their origin and their identity.

## Physical Characteristics

The woad is a biennial plant that in the first year produces a rosette of the leaves that lie close to the ground, the leaves being a pale green and being glaucous and mealy on the surface, the texture being the feature that distinguishes the woad from the other members of the cabbage family. The leaves of the first year are the leaves that are harvested for the dye, the pigment being concentrated in the tissue of the first-year leaves and the harvest being done before the plant sends up the flowering stem in the second year. The leaves of the woad are lance-shaped and the margins being the entire and the smooth edges that the leaves of the cabbage family display.

The flowering stem of the woad rises in the second year to a height of three to four feet, the stem being branched and bearing the numerous small flowers that are yellow and that are arranged in the clusters that the members of the cabbage family produce. The flowers of the woad are the flowers that produce the seeds that the plant reproduces from, the seeds being the small and the dark structures that are contained in the pods that follow the flowers. The seeds are the means by which the woad is propagated, the seeds being sown in the late summer for the production of the first-year leaves that the following year's harvest requires.

## Cultivation

The woad is cultivated in the fields of the northern settlements of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), the plant being the crop that the dyers and the farmers grow for the supply of the leaves that the dye production requires. The woad is sown in the late summer or the early autumn for the production of the first-year rosette, the seed being broadcast in the prepared seedbed and the young plants being thinned to the spacing that the growth requires. The woad grows through the autumn and the winter and the leaves are harvested in the following summer when the pigment is at the peak of the concentration.

The cultivation of the woad is the [agriculture](/content/economy/agriculture) that the northern settlements have practised for the centuries, the woad being the crop that the dyeing industry of the north is based on. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the dyers and the woad growers regulate the cultivation and the harvest of the woad, the guilds maintaining the standards of the quality and the pricing that govern the trade in the woad leaves and the woad cakes. The [prices and livelihood](/content/economy/prices-and-livelihood) of the woad growers and the dyers are dependent on the harvest and the quality of the woad, the woad being the crop that the northern textile industry is founded on.

## The Fermentation Process

The extraction of the pigment from the woad leaves is the process that the dyers of the northern settlements have developed and refined over the centuries of the practice, the process being the knowledge that the dyers' guilds guard as the most valuable of the trade secrets. The fresh leaves are crushed and the macerated in the water that is warmed by the fermentation that the process generates, the maceration being the stage that breaks the cells and releases the pigment that the leaves contain. The fermented mass is the material that is formed into the cakes that the dyers dry and store, the cakes being the form in which the woad is traded and the form that the dyers use in the preparation of the dye bath.

The fermentation of the woad is the process that requires the careful control of the temperature and the duration, the temperature being the factor that determines the quality of the pigment and the duration being the factor that determines the concentration of the dye in the cakes. The dyers who master the fermentation are the craftsmen who are most valued in the [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) industry of the north, the mastery being the skill that the [apprenticeship and training](/content/magic/apprenticeship-and-training) of the dyers' guilds produces. The fermentation is the process that is conducted in the dyers' workshops that the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) and the other northern regions maintain, the workshops being the facilities that the dyeing industry operates.

## Dyeing with Woad

The dyeing with the woad is the process that the dyers of the northern settlements conduct in the dye baths that the woad cakes are prepared in, the cakes being ground and the powder being mixed with the water that the alkaline additives are dissolved in, the alkaline being the agent that the dyeing requires for the development of the blue colour. The cloth is immersed in the dye bath and the dyeing is the process that the dyers repeat for the depth of the colour that the desired shade requires, the repeated dyeing being the method that the dyers use to produce the deep and the saturated blues that the northern cloth is known for.

The woad blue is the colour that the [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) industry of the north uses as the base for the colours that the dyers produce, the woad blue being the foundation upon which the [weld](/content/species/flora/weld) yellow and the [madder](/content/species/flora/madder) red are applied to produce the green and the purple that the northern designs include. The woad blue is also the colour that the dyers of the south use as the base for the [indigo](/content/species/flora/indigo) and the other dyes that the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast provides, the woad being the dye that the southern dyers purchase from the northern trade.

## Trade and Economics

The trade in the woad is the commerce that connects the northern settlements where the woad is grown and processed to the cities of the south where the woad is used in the dyeing that the [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) industry of the Aethelari and the [Morvindi](/content/language/spoken/morvindi) regions conducts. The [cartage and transport](/content/economy/cartage-and-transport) of the woad cakes from the north to the south is the trade that the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the merchants and the carriers conduct, the woad being the commodity that the [trade and tariffs](/content/economy/trade-and-tariffs) of the regions regulate. The [spices and dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) merchants of the coastal cities are the buyers of the woad that the northern trade provides, the woad being the dye that the southern dyers use as the base for the colours that the southern cloth produces.

The woad is also exported to [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) where the dyers of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip) use the woad in the combinations with the [indigo](/content/species/flora/indigo) that the Sahrani trade produces, the woad being the dye that the Sahrani dyers import from the north. The [Sahrani Desert Trail](/content/location/route/trade/sahrani-desert-trail) and the sea routes that connect the continents carry the woad from the northern settlements to the Sahrani markets, the woad being among the northern products that the Sahrani dyeing industry demands.

## Cultural Significance

The woad is the dye that the culture and the identity of the northern peoples is most closely associated with, the blue cloth that the woad produces being the garment that the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) and the other northern regions are recognised by. The blue garments that the northern peoples wear are the mark of the origin and the identity that the woad represents, the blue being the colour that the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) and the [Morvindi](/content/language/spoken/morvindi) peoples associate with the northern settlements and the northern traditions.

The [frostwall winter spirits](/content/mythology/frostwall-winter-spirits) and the other cultural traditions of the northern peoples include the woad as the element of the ritual and the decoration that the northern practices employ, the woad being the dye that the warriors and the celebrants use for the painting of the skin and the marking of the occasions that the northern culture observes. The [halfling hearth traditions](/content/religion/halfling-hearth-traditions) and the other practices of the northern communities include the woad-dyed cloth as the material that the ritual and the daily life employ, the woad being the dye that the northern household and the northern ceremony are founded on.
