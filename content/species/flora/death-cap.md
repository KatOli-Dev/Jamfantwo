## The Death Cap of the Aethelar Forests

The death cap of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) forests is the most dangerous of the poisonous fungi, a species that resembles the edible fungi of the forests and that causes a slow and painful death when it is consumed. The death cap is white and unremarkable in appearance, and the inexperienced gatherer may mistake the death cap for the edible white mushroom that grows in the same habitats. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) teach their children to recognise the death cap from the earliest age, and the elven communities have no deaths from the consumption of the poisonous fungi.

## Physical Characteristics

The death cap is a medium-sized fungus of the family Amanitaceae, the cap measuring from two to five inches in the diameter when the fruiting body is fully expanded. The cap of the death cap is a pale and the unremarkable colour that ranges from the white through the yellowish-green to the olive-brown, the colour varying with the conditions of the light and the moisture in which the fungus grows. The surface of the cap is smooth and dry when the weather is fair and slightly tacky when the air is damp, and the cap of the mature specimen is often marked with the faint and the radial striations at the margin that are the characteristic of the species.

The gills of the death cap are white and crowded, the gills being free of the attachment to the stem and the edges of the gills being smooth and the colour being the pure and the uniform white that contrasts with the cap. The stem of the death cap is white and smooth, rising to a height of three to six inches above the ground, and the stem is slender and the texture is firm. The base of the stem is swollen into a bulb that is surrounded by a cup-like structure called the volva, and the volva is a remnant of the universal veil that enclosed the young fruiting body before the cap expanded. The presence of the volva at the base of the stem is the most reliable of the features for the identification of the death cap, and the volva is often hidden beneath the leaf litter and the soil and must be exposed by the careful excavation of the base.

The death cap also possesses a ring or the annulus on the upper part of the stem, the ring being the remnant of the partial veil that covered the gills in the young specimen. The ring of the death cap is white and membranous, and the ring persists on the stem of the mature specimen as a skirt-like structure that is another of the identifying features of the species. The spores of the death cap are white, and the spore print that is obtained by the placement of the mature cap on a dark surface is the diagnostic tool that the experienced gatherer uses to distinguish the death cap from the edible species that produce the spores of the other colours.

## Habitat and Distribution

The death cap is a fungus of the broadleaf and the mixed woodlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and the death cap is found in the forests that contain the [oak](/content/species/flora/oak), the [beech](/content/species/flora/beech), and the [willow](/content/species/flora/willow) trees. The death cap forms the mycorrhizal association with the roots of these trees, the mycelium of the fungus enveloping the root tips and the fungus and the tree exchanging the nutrients and the water in a relationship that is beneficial to both organisms. The death cap is not found in the purely coniferous forests, and the distribution of the death cap in the landscape is a map of the distribution of the broadleaf trees that are the partners of the fungus.

The death cap is most abundant in the forests of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) where the old and the undisturbed woodlands provide the extensive areas of the suitable habitat. The death cap also occurs in the managed woodlands and the coppices of the agricultural regions of Aethelar, and the death cap is found in the parks and the gardens of the towns and the villages where the mature broadleaf trees grow. The fruiting season of the death cap extends from the late summer through the autumn, and the fruiting bodies appear most abundantly in the weeks of the warm and the moist weather that follow the first of the autumn rains.

The death cap is a species that is spreading in its distribution as the result of the movement of the timber and the soil that are transported in the commerce of the known world, and the death cap has been reported from the regions of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) where the species was not known in the earlier centuries. The spread of the death cap is a matter of the concern for the authorities of the Sahrani cities, for the knowledge of the identification of the poisonous fungi is not well established among the populations of the regions to which the fungus is newly introduced.

## The Toxins of the Death Cap

The death cap contains the two principal groups of the toxins that are responsible for the deadly effects of the fungus on the human and the animal that consume it. The amatoxins are the cyclic peptides that inhibit the activity of the enzyme that is responsible for the transcription of the genetic material in the cells, and the amatoxins cause the failure of the organs that are the most metabolically active, the liver and the kidneys being the organs that are most severely affected. The amatoxins are not destroyed by the cooking or the drying or any of the other methods of the food preparation that are known to the peoples of the world, and the toxins remain potent even after the prolonged boiling or the roasting of the fungus.

The phallotoxins are the second group of the toxins that the death cap contains, and the phallotoxins cause the damage to the membranes of the cells and the disruption of the transport of the substances across the cellular membranes. The phallotoxins exert the effect that is rapid but that is limited to the gastrointestinal system when the fungus is eaten, for the phallotoxins are poorly absorbed from the digestive tract and the toxicity of the phallotoxins is the cause of the initial symptoms that follow the consumption of the death cap.

The lethal dose of the death cap is the small amount of the tissue of the fungus, and a single mature cap of the death cap contains the quantity of the amatoxins that is sufficient to kill an adult human. The concentration of the toxins varies with the individual specimen and the conditions of the growth, and the same species of the fungus that grows in the different forests and in the different seasons may contain the different and the unpredictable amounts of the toxins.

## The Symptoms of the Poisoning

The consumption of the death cap produces the characteristic pattern of the symptoms that unfolds in the three distinct phases over the course of the days following the ingestion. The first phase begins the six to twelve hours after the fungus has been eaten, and the delay in the onset of the symptoms is the feature that makes the diagnosis of the death cap poisoning particularly difficult, for the meal that contained the fungus has been forgotten by the time that the symptoms appear. The first phase is characterised by the severe abdominal pain and the vomiting and the watery diarrhoea that may be so profuse as to cause the dehydration and the shock, and the symptoms of the first phase are often mistaken for the severe food poisoning or the cholera.

The first phase may last for the one to two days, and the symptoms of the first phase subside, and the patient enters the second phase that is known as the period of the apparent recovery. The patient appears to improve and the symptoms diminish, and the patient and the family and the healer may believe that the crisis has passed. The second phase is the period of the greatest danger, for the apparent improvement masks the progressive destruction of the liver and the kidneys that is occurring within the body, the damage being the result of the amatoxins that have been absorbed into the bloodstream and that are accumulating in the cells of the organs that the toxins target.

The third phase begins the three to five days after the ingestion, and the third phase is characterised by the rapid deterioration of the condition of the patient as the liver and the kidneys fail. The patient develops the jaundice and the bleeding and the confusion, and the patient progresses to the coma and the death within the days of the onset of the third phase. The mortality of the death cap poisoning that is not treated is very high, and the survivors of the poisoning suffer the permanent damage to the liver and the kidneys that may require the lifelong management or the transplantation of the organs, treatments that are not available in the medical knowledge of the known world.

## Diagnosis and Treatment

The diagnosis of the death cap poisoning is made on the basis of the history of the mushroom consumption and the characteristic pattern of the symptoms, and the identification of the mushrooms that remain from the meal or the fragments that are recovered from the vomit or the stools is the confirmatory evidence that the healer seeks. The [healing and restoration](/content/magic/healing-and-restoration) practitioners and the healers of the known world have developed the protocols for the treatment of the death cap poisoning that are based on the understanding of the mechanism of the toxins, and the treatment is directed at the support of the functions of the liver and the kidneys and the removal of the toxins from the body.

The treatments that are employed include the administration of the [willow](/content/species/flora/willow) bark tea for the relief of the pain, the use of the [milk thistle](/content/species/flora/thistle) seed for the protection of the liver, and the application of the magical and the alchemical remedies that are designed to bind and to neutralise the amatoxins in the bloodstream. The mages of the [Eldreth Library](/content/magic/relic/library-of-eldreth) have studied the properties of the antidotes that are derived from the rare plants and the minerals, and the reports of the studies are recorded in the volumes that are held in the restricted collections of the library. The effectiveness of the magical treatments is variable, and the survival of the patient depends on the amount of the fungus that was consumed and the speed with which the treatment is begun.

The prevention of the death cap poisoning is the strategy that is most effective in the reduction of the mortality from the fungus, and the education of the population in the identification of the poisonous fungi is the responsibility of the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the apothecaries and the herbalists. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) have the most comprehensive of the systems for the education in the fungi identification, and the elven children are taught to recognise the death cap from the earliest years and the knowledge is tested and reinforced through the childhood and the adolescence.

## The Death Cap and the Edible Lookalikes

The death cap is frequently mistaken for the several species of the edible fungi that share the white colour and the similar form, and the confusion between the death cap and the edible species is the cause of most of the poisonings that occur in the communities that gather the wild fungi for the food. The most common of the edible species that is mistaken for the death cap is the field mushroom that grows in the pastures and the meadows, and the inexperienced gatherer may collect the death cap from the woodland edge where the fungus appears among the grass and the herbage. The distinguishing features of the two fungi are the colour of the gills, the gills of the field mushroom being the pink when the mushroom is young and the brown when the older, and the field mushroom lacks the volva at the base and the ring on the stem.

The straw mushroom that is cultivated in the warm and the humid chambers of the Aethelari regions is another of the species that the inexperienced gatherer may confuse with the death cap, and the straw mushroom is the edible species that is grown on the beds of the rice straw and the other agricultural waste. The straw mushroom is distinguished from the death cap by the absence of the volva and the ring and by the thinner and the more fragile texture of the cap and the stem.

The [puffball](/content/species/flora/desert-puffball) species that grow in the fields and the forests are also mistaken for the young specimens of the death cap before the cap has expanded and the stem has extended, and the puffball that is cut in the cross-section reveals the solid white flesh that distinguishes the puffball from the death cap. The death cap that is cut in the cross-section reveals the outline of the developing cap and the stem within the universal veil, and the cross-section is the diagnostic test that the experienced gatherer performs on every specimen that is collected. The gatherers of the wild fungi are instructed to collect only the specimens that they can identify with the certainty, and the single mistake in the identification of a death cap is the error that is not survived.

## Cultural Significance

The death cap occupies a place of the fear and the respect in the culture of the Aethelari peoples, and the death cap is the most notorious of the poisonous fungi of the continent, a fungus that is known by the name and the appearance to every person who has the occasion to gather the wild fungi for the food. The death cap is the subject of the [folk omens and superstitions](/content/mythology/folk-omens-and-superstitions) that warn of the danger of the unknown mushrooms, and the parents caution the children by the name of the death cap, the fungus that brings the death to the one who eats it.

The death cap has been used as the instrument of the assassination and the murder in the history of the Aethelari courts and the noble houses, and the records of the kingdoms and the principalities of Aethelar contain the accounts of the poisonings that were carried out with the death cap. The delay in the onset of the symptoms and the similarity of the symptoms to the natural illness have made the death cap the poison of choice for the assassins who seek to avoid the detection, and the food tasters who serve at the tables of the nobility are vigilant for the taste of the mushroom that should not be present in the dishes.

The [halfling hearth traditions](/content/religion/halfling-hearth-traditions) include the warnings about the death cap among the teachings that the halfling parents transmit to the children, and the halfling communities of the agricultural regions of Aethelar are careful to inspect the mushrooms that the children bring from the fields and the woods. The death cap is not a fungus that is used in the magical or the ritual practices of the peoples of the known world, for the unpredictability of the dose and the difficulty of the treatment render the death cap unsuitable for the controlled use, and the death cap is a fungus that is avoided and feared rather than sought and used.
