The vetch is an annual legume grown in the fields of the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) for the fodder that feeds the animals through the winter months.

## The Legumes of the Improved Pastures

The vetch is sown in the autumn and harvested in the spring. The vetch is cut and dried into the hay that is stored in the barns, and the vetch hay is the most nutritious of the fodder crops that the farmers grow.

## Physical Characteristics

The vetch is a climbing or trailing plant that produces the slender stems reaching a length of two to three feet in the mature growth, the stems being angular and winged and bearing the leaves that are divided into the pairs of the leaflets that are arranged along the length of the rachis. The tip of the leaf is modified into a tendril that coils around the stems of the neighbouring plants and the supports that the vetch encounters, the tendrils being the means by which the vetch climbs through the vegetation and elevates the leaves and the flowers into the light. The leaves of the vetch are a blue-green colour that is paler than the leaves of the [clover](/content/species/flora/clover) and the other legumes that are grown for the fodder.

The flowers of the vetch are produced in the pairs and the small clusters that emerge from the axils of the leaves, the flowers being a purple to the violet in the colour and being shaped in the papilionaceous form that is characteristic of the legume family. The flowers are pollinated by the [Honey Bee](/content/species/fauna/honey-bee) that visit the flowers for the nectar and the pollen that the flowers produce. The fruit of the vetch is a pod that contains the seeds, the pod being a flattened and the somewhat hairy structure that splits open when the seeds are mature and that releases the seeds to the ground.

## Nitrogen Fixation and Soil Improvement

The vetch is a legume that fixes the nitrogen from the atmosphere through the symbiotic relationship with the bacteria that inhabit the nodules on the roots of the plant, the bacteria converting the atmospheric nitrogen into the forms that the plant can use and that remain in the soil when the vetch is ploughed under. The nitrogen fixation is the property that makes the vetch a valuable crop in the rotation that the farmers of the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) follow, the vetch adding the nitrogen to the soil that the subsequent crops of the [wheat](/content/species/flora/wheat) and the [barley](/content/species/flora/barley) require for the growth.

The vetch is also used as the green manure that is ploughed under to add the organic matter to the soil, the farmers sowing the vetch in the fields that are to be left fallow and the vetch being incorporated into the soil before the flowers are produced. The green manure crop of the vetch improves the structure of the soil and the content of the nitrogen, and the farmers who use the vetch in the rotation find that the yields of the grain crops that follow the vetch are higher than the yields that the continuous cultivation of the grain produces.

## Cultivation and Harvest

The vetch is sown in the autumn after the harvest of the grain crop that has occupied the field in the summer, the seed being broadcast or drilled into the prepared seedbed that the ploughing and the harrowing of the field have produced. The vetch germinates in the autumn and the young plants establish before the onset of the winter, the vetch being a hardy plant that tolerates the frosts and the cold of the Aethelari winter. The vetch grows slowly through the winter and the early spring, the growth accelerating as the temperatures rise and the days lengthen, and the vetch reaches the full growth in the late spring when the plants are in the flower and the vegetative mass is at the maximum.

The harvest of the vetch for the hay is done in the late spring or the early summer when the plants are in the full bloom and before the pods have begun to form, the cutting being done with the scythe or the mower that the farmer uses for the [hay crop](/content/species/flora/hay-crop). The cut vetch is left in the swath to dry in the sun and the wind, the drying being the process that reduces the moisture content of the hay to the level that the storage in the barn requires. The dried vetch is gathered into the cocks and the loads that the farmer transports to the barn, and the hay is stored in the loft where the dry conditions maintain the quality of the fodder through the months of the storage.

## Fodder and Animal Nutrition

The vetch hay is the most nutritious of the fodder crops that the farmers of the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) produce, the protein content of the vetch hay being higher than the protein content of the [Hay Crop](/content/species/flora/hay-crop) that is made from the grasses and the other fodder plants. The vetch hay is fed to the [cattle](/content/species/fauna/cattle) and the [sheep](/content/species/fauna/sheep) that the farmers maintain through the winter months, the nutrition that the vetch provides being the factor that maintains the condition and the productivity of the animals through the season when the pasture is not available.

The vetch is also grazed in the spring before the hay is made, the [cattle](/content/species/fauna/cattle) and the [sheep](/content/species/fauna/sheep) being turned into the fields where the vetch is growing and the animals consuming the fresh forage that the vetch provides. The grazing of the vetch is managed to prevent the over-grazing that would reduce the yield of the hay, the farmers removing the animals from the field when the vetch has been grazed to the level that the farmer determines by the experience and the judgement.

## Role in the Rotation

The vetch is a component of the rotation that the farmers of the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) follow to maintain the fertility of the soil and the productivity of the land. The rotation typically involves the wheat in the first year, the barley in the second year, and the vetch in the third year, the vetch being followed by the wheat that benefits from the nitrogen that the vetch has added to the soil. The rotation is the system that the [agriculture](/content/economy/agriculture) of the region has developed over the centuries of the practice, and the rotation is the foundation of the sustainable production that the Aethelari farmers have achieved.

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the farmers in the Vintners Vale and the Aldreth regulate the practices of the rotation and the production of the fodder crops, the guilds maintaining the standards of the quality and the [prices and livelihood](/content/economy/prices-and-livelihood) that govern the trade in the fodder and the grain. The [livestock and herding](/content/economy/livestock-and-herding) economy of the regions depends on the supply of the vetch hay that the rotation produces, and the vetch is the crop that links the arable and the pastoral components of the agricultural system that the Aethelari farmers maintain.

## Vetch in the Wider Economy

The vetch seed is a commodity that is traded among the farmers and the seed merchants of the [Aethelari](/content/language/spoken/aethelari) regions, the seed being saved from the crop by the farmers who produce the vetch and the surplus being sold to the farmers who need the seed for the sowing of the new crop. The seed trade is regulated by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the seed merchants who maintain the standards of the purity and the germination that the quality of the seed requires, and the seed that is traded is the product of the selection that the farmers and the merchants have practised over the generations of the cultivation.

The vetch is also used as a component of the [fodder crop](/content/species/flora/fodder-crop) mixtures that the farmers sow for the combined hay that includes the vetch and the [oats](/content/species/flora/oats) and the other grains that are grown together and harvested as the mixed fodder. The mixtures are the practice that the farmers use to increase the yield and the nutritional value of the hay, the vetch contributing the protein and the oats contributing the energy that the balanced fodder provides. The mixed fodder is the product that the [markets and fairs](/content/economy/markets-and-fairs) of the Aethelari regions trade, and the mixed fodder is the principal winter feed of the livestock that the Aethelari farmers maintain.
