The flowers of the saffron crocus are lilac or purple, the petals being six in number and the flower being trumpet-shaped, and the most distinctive feature of the flower is the three red stigmas that extend from the centre of the flower, the stigmas being the source of the saffron that is the valuable product of the plant.

The leaves of the saffron crocus are narrow and grass-like, the leaves emerging from the bulb after the flowers have appeared, and the leaves persist through the winter and the spring, the bulb storing the nutrients that are produced by the photosynthesis of the leaves. The bulb of the saffron crocus is a corm, a swollen underground stem that stores the nutrients and produces the new corms that are the means of the propagation, the corms being dug and divided in the summer when the plant is dormant.

The saffron is used in the dyeing of the ceremonial garments that are worn by the priests and the sovereigns of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) states, the saffron producing a golden yellow colour that is the symbol of the rank and the authority of the wearer. The trade in saffron is regulated by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the spice merchants, who control the quality and the prices of the saffron that is sold in the markets. The guilds maintain the standards of the quality by the inspection of the saffron that is offered for sale, the saffron being graded by the colour and the aroma and the lack of the adulterants that the dishonest merchants might add to increase the weight of the product.
