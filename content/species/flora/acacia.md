## The Acacia of the Kharesh

The acacia trees that grow along the dry watercourses of the Kharesh are the largest plants of the desert, their deep root systems reaching the underground water that sustains them through the driest years. The acacia is a thorny tree with a spreading crown that provides shade for the travellers and the animals that cross the desert, and the acacia groves that mark the lines of the dry rivers are the sites of the camps and the resting places on the caravan routes.

## Physical Characteristics

The acacia is a small to medium-sized tree that reaches a height of fifteen to twenty feet in the favourable locations of the Kharesh, though the specimens that grow in the driest areas may be no more than shrubs. The trunk of the acacia is short and thick, and the bark is dark and deeply furrowed, forming a protective layer that shields the inner tissue from the intense heat of the desert sun. The branches of the acacia spread outward in a wide and flattened crown that casts a circle of shade many times the width of the trunk, and the shape of the crown is an adaptation that minimises the exposure of the leaves to the midday sun while maximising the shade that the tree provides.

The leaves of the acacia are small and compound, composed of many tiny leaflets that reduce the surface area through which the water can evaporate. The thorns of the acacia are long and sharp, growing in pairs at the base of each leaf, and the thorns are a defence against the browsing animals that would otherwise strip the tree of its foliage. The acacia produces clusters of small yellow flowers in the brief period of moisture that follows the rare rains, and the flowers are pollinated by the insects that emerge in the same period. The seed pods of the acacia are flat and twisted, containing several hard seeds that are dispersed by the animals that eat the pods.

## Habitat and Distribution

The acacia is a tree of the arid and semi-arid regions of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and the acacia is the dominant tree of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert. The acacia grows along the dry watercourses that traverse the desert, where the roots of the tree can reach the underground water that flows beneath the beds of the wadis. The acacia is not found in the deep desert where the water table is too deep for the roots to reach, and the distribution of the acacia groves is a map of the underground water resources of the Kharesh.

The acacia is also found in the transition zone between the Kharesh and the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent), where the rainfall is sufficient to support the tree without the need for the deep roots that the desert specimens develop. The acacia groves of the transition zone are taller and denser than the groves of the deep desert, and the trees of the transition zone produce more gum and more wood than the trees of the arid interior.

## Timber and Fuel

The wood of the acacia is hard and dense, and the wood is used by the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) for the construction of the frames of the tents and the handles of the tools that the desert peoples use. The wood is also used as fuel for the fires that the travellers build in the desert, and the smoke that the acacia wood produces is aromatic and pleasant, keeping the insects away from the camp.

The density of the acacia wood makes it suitable for the construction of the implements that must withstand the abrasion and the shock of daily use. The handles of the knives and the tools of the Kharesh Tribes are made from the acacia wood, and the wooden pegs and the pins that hold the tents together are cut from the branches of the tree. The acacia wood is also used for the construction of the frames of the saddles that the desert riders use, and the saddles that are made with the acacia frames are light and durable.

The charcoal that is produced from the acacia wood is the finest of the fuels that are available in the Kharesh, burning hot and clean with a smoke that is faintly scented. The charcoal burners of the desert tribes select the oldest and the densest of the acacia wood for the production of the charcoal, and the charcoal is traded to the settlements of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) where it is used in the forges and the kitchens of the cities.

## Gum Arabic and the Trade in Resin

The acacia produces a resin that is called gum arabic in the trade of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) merchants, and the resin is used in the preparation of the inks and the paints that the scribes of the [Reading Cloisters](/content/religion/cloisters-of-reading) use in the production of the manuscripts. The resin is harvested by tapping the trunk of the acacia in the dry season, and the dried resin is collected and packed into sacks for transport to the markets of the coast.

The gum arabic that is produced by the acacia of the Kharesh is of a quality that is recognised throughout the known world, and the gum that is harvested from the trees of the deep desert is clearer and more soluble than the gum that is produced by the trees of the transition zone. The gum is graded by the merchants according to the colour and the clarity of the resin, and the finest grade commands a price that reflects the difficulty of the harvest and the transport of the gum from the remote groves of the interior.

The gum arabic is used in the preparation of the inks that the scribes of the [Reading Cloisters](/content/religion/cloisters-of-reading) use in the copying of the manuscripts, and the gum is dissolved in the water and mixed with the pigments to produce an ink that flows smoothly from the pen and adheres to the parchment. The gum is also used in the preparation of the paints that decorate the manuscripts, and the gum acts as a binder that holds the pigment to the surface of the vellum. The trade in gum arabic is a significant source of the income of the Kharesh Tribes, and the caravans that carry the gum to the coast are among the most valuable of the trade routes that cross the desert.

## Cultural Significance

The acacia is a tree of great significance in the culture of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), for the tree provides the shade and the materials that sustain the life of the desert peoples. The acacia groves are the sites of the councils and the gatherings of the tribes, and the largest of the acacia trees are the meeting places where the elders of the tribes assemble to settle the disputes and to plan the movements of the caravans. The acacia is considered a sacred tree in the traditions of the desert peoples, and the cutting of a living acacia is forbidden except in the cases of necessity.

The poetry of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) tradition is rich with references to the acacia, and the tree is a symbol of the endurance and the resilience of the desert peoples. The poets of the Kharesh describe the acacia as the tree that stands between the traveller and the sun, and the shade of the acacia is a metaphor for the protection and the mercy that the desert offers to those who know its ways. The acacia is also a symbol of the hospitality of the desert, for the shade of the tree is the place where the traveller is received and the water is shared.

## Relation to Other Desert Species

The acacia is the keystone species of the Kharesh desert ecosystem, and the groves of the acacia support a community of the plants and the animals that are dependent on the tree for the shade and the moisture that it provides. The [cactus](/content/species/flora/cactus) species of the Kharesh grow in the shelter of the acacia groves, where the shade of the tree reduces the evaporation of the water from the soil. The [desert puffball](/content/species/flora/desert-puffball) fungi appear in the soils beneath the acacia trees after the winter rains, and the fungi are associated with the roots of the acacia in a relationship that benefits both organisms.

The [date palm](/content/species/flora/date-palm) and the acacia are the two tree species that define the oasis communities of the Kharesh, and the two trees are often found growing together in the oases where the water is close to the surface. The acacia provides the hard wood and the gum, and the date palm provides the fruit and the shade, and the combination of the two trees is the foundation of the settled life of the oasis communities.
