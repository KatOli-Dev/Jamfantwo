## The Mountain Arnica of the High Pastures

The mountain arnica of the high pastures of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) is used in the treatment of the bruises and the sprains that are the common injuries of the dwarven miners and the human labourers of the region. The flowers of the arnica are harvested in the brief summer and steeped in oil that is applied to the injured area, reducing the swelling and the discolouration that accompany the injury. The preparation must not be applied to broken skin, for the arnica is toxic when absorbed through wounds, and the healers of the north are careful in their use of this potent remedy.

The leaves of the arnica are opposite and oval, arranged in the pairs that clasp the stem, and the leaves are covered with the fine hairs that give the plant a greyish-green appearance. The flowers of the arnica are the bright yellow daisies that are the most distinctive feature of the plant. Each flower head is composed of the ray florets that form the outer ring of the petals and the disc florets that fill the centre of the head. The arnica produces one to three flower heads on each stem, and the flowers appear in the July and the August in the alpine pastures of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) uplands. The seed of the arnica is a small achene that is equipped with the pappus that allows the seed to be dispersed by the wind.

The arnica is a plant of the alpine and the subalpine zones of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands), and the plant grows at the elevations between three thousand and seven thousand feet. ## Medicinal Properties and Preparations

The arnica is a remedy of great importance in the medicine of the northern regions, and the plant is used in the treatment of the bruises, the sprains, and the muscle pains that are the common injuries of the dwarven miners and the human labourers of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) settlements. The flowers of the arnica are harvested in the brief summer and steeped in the oil that is applied to the injured area, and the oil of the arnica is a preparation that is found in the medicine chest of every household in the northern regions.

The preparation of the arnica oil requires the steeping of the fresh flowers in the oil for a period of several weeks, and the oil is warmed by the sun during the day and allowed to cool in the night. The oil extracts the active compounds from the flowers, and the resulting preparation is a golden oil that has the distinctive smell of the arnica. The oil is applied to the bruised or the sprained area, and the arnica reduces the swelling and the discolouration and accelerates the healing of the injury.

The internal use of the arnica is dangerous, and the arnica is never taken by the mouth except in the most minute doses prepared by the skilled healers. The toxicity of the arnica is a property that is well known to the peoples of the northern regions, and the arnica is handled with the care that a potent remedy requires. The healers of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) settlements are trained in the preparation and the use of the arnica, and the knowledge of the correct dosage and the method of the application is passed from the master to the apprentice.
