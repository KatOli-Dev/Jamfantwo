## The Sorghum of the Dry Margins

Sorghum is one of the grains of the dry margins where the rainfall is insufficient for wheat or barley and where the cultivation of rice is impossible. It is grown in the transition zone between the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and in the eastern districts of Aethelar where the climate is warmer and the rains are less reliable than in the west.

Sorghum is a larger plant than the millet, growing to the height of a man or more, and its grain is used for the same purposes as millet but with a coarser texture and a nuttier flavour. The cultivation of sorghum requires less water and less labour than the cultivation of wheat or rice, and the plant itself is more resistant to pests and diseases. The farmers of the transition zone rely upon sorghum as the foundation of their diet and their economy, and the grain that they produce is traded along the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) for the goods of the coast.

## Physical Characteristics

The sorghum plant is a tall and the robust grass that grows to the height of six to twelve feet in the favourable conditions of the cultivation, the plant being one of the largest of the grain crops that the farmers of the known world cultivate. The stem of the sorghum is the thick and the solid cane that is the cylindrical and the jointed, the stem being the structure that supports the weight of the seed head and that transports the water and the nutrients from the roots to the leaves and the grain. The stem of the sorghum is the green when the plant is young and the stem becomes the brown and the woody as the plant matures and the grain ripens, and the cane of the sorghum is the tough and the fibrous structure that is resistant to the lodging and the breakage.

The leaves of the sorghum are the long and the narrow blades that emerge from the joints of the stem, the leaves being the lance-shaped and the surface being the smooth and the waxy. The leaves of the sorghum are arranged alternately along the stem, and the leaves are the surfaces for the photosynthesis that produces the carbohydrates that the plant stores in the grain. The leaves of the sorghum are adapted to the dry conditions of the habitat, and the waxy coating of the leaves reduces the loss of the water through the transpiration, and the leaves fold inward when the water is scarce to reduce the exposure of the surface to the sun and the wind.

The seed head of the sorghum is the terminal panicle that emerges from the apex of the stem, the panicle being the branching structure that bears the numerous spikelets that contain the grains. The panicle of the sorghum is the compact and the dense in the varieties that are cultivated for the grain production, and the panicle of the sorghum is the open and the spreading in the varieties that are cultivated for the forage and the syrup. The grains of the sorghum are the small and the round seeds that range from the white through the yellow and the red to the brown in the colour, the colour of the grain being the characteristic of the variety and the indicator of the content of the tannins and the other compounds that affect the flavour and the nutrition of the grain.

## Habitat and Distribution

The sorghum is a grain of the warm and the dry regions of the known world, and the sorghum is cultivated in the transition zone between the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), where the rainfall is the marginal for the cultivation of the wheat or the barley but sufficient for the sorghum. The sorghum is a plant that tolerates the drought and the high temperatures that would kill the other grain crops, and the sorghum is the grain that the farmers plant in the areas where the rains are unreliable and the summers are hot and dry. The sorghum is also cultivated in the eastern districts of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) where the climate is warmer and the rainfall is less predictable than in the western regions of the continent.

The sorghum is also grown in the [Sahrani Wastes](/content/location/settlement/region/sahrani-wastes) in the small fields that are irrigated by the water that is drawn from the wells and the springs, and the sorghum is one of the few crops that can produce the grain in the conditions of the marginal water supply that characterise the wastes. The farmers of the wastes plant the sorghum in the season of the rains and harvest the grain before the soil dries completely, and the sorghum that is grown in the wastes is the subsistence crop that sustains the farmers and the families through the year.

The cultivation of the sorghum extends from the Sahrani interior to the eastern provinces of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), where the sorghum is grown in the lowlands and the valleys that experience the warm and the dry summers. The sorghum is the grain of the regions that are the marginal for the other crops, and the distribution of the sorghum cultivation is the map of the areas where the climate and the soil combine to make the cultivation of the grain the best use of the land that the farmers have.

## Cultivation and Management

The cultivation of the sorghum is the agricultural practice that requires the less of the water and the labour than the cultivation of the other grain crops, and the sorghum is the crop that the farmers in the dry regions choose for the reliability and the yield. The sorghum is planted in the spring when the soil has warmed and the rains of the spring have moistened the soil to the depth that the seed requires for the germination and the establishment of the seedling. The seed of the sorghum is sown in the rows that are the distance apart that the size of the mature plant requires, and the seed is planted at the depth of the inch or the two in the soil that has been prepared by the ploughing and the harrowing.

The sorghum requires the less of the water than the wheat or the barley, and the sorghum can produce the grain in the conditions where the rainfall is as low as the fifteen to the twenty inches in the growing season. The sorghum is the crop that the farmers plant in the years when the rains are scarce and the risk of the crop failure is high, and the sorghum is more likely to produce the harvest than the wheat or the barley in the years of the drought. The sorghum is also the crop that the farmers plant in the soils of the poor quality where the other grains would not thrive, and the sorghum is the crop that the farmers use to improve the soil through the rotation with the [vetch](/content/species/flora/vetch) and the other legumes that restore the fertility of the land.

The weed control in the fields of the sorghum is the task that is performed by the hoeing and the hand weeding in the weeks after the emergence of the seedlings, and the sorghum is the crop that is able to suppress the weeds by the shade of the canopy that the tall and the dense stand of the plants produces once the crop has reached the full height. The pests and the diseases of the sorghum are the less of the problem than for the other grains, and the sorghum is the crop that requires the less of the attention and the less of the expense for the cultivation than the wheat or the barley or the rice.

## The Harvest and the Processing of the Grain

The harvest of the sorghum is conducted in the late summer or the early autumn when the grain has ripened and the moisture content of the seed has fallen to the level that allows the storage without the spoilage. The seed heads of the sorghum are cut from the stems with the sickles that the harvesters carry, and the seed heads are collected and bound into the sheaves that are stood in the field to dry for the period of the days or the weeks before the threshing. The sorghum is the crop that is harvested by the hand in the manner that is the traditional for the grain crops of the small-scale agriculture that the farmers of the dry margins practise.

The threshing of the sorghum is the process that separates the grain from the stalks and the husks that surround the seed, and the threshing is performed by the beating of the sheaves on the ground or by the treading of the animals over the sheaves that have been spread on the threshing floor. The grain that is released by the threshing is collected and winnowed, the chaff and the dust being separated from the grain by the tossing of the grain into the air on the day of the steady wind, the wind carrying the lighter material away and the grain falling back to the ground in the heap that is gathered and bagged for the storage.

The grain of the sorghum is stored in the granaries and the silos that the farmers construct for the protection of the harvest from the pests and the moisture, and the grain is stored in the whole form and the grain is milled into the flour as the need arises. The milling of the sorghum is the process that is performed by the hand mills and the querns in the households of the farmers, and the flour of the sorghum is coarser than the flour of the wheat and the flour of the sorghum is used for the porridge and the flatbread. The [prices and livelihood](/content/economy/prices-and-livelihood) of the farmers who grow the sorghum are dependent on the harvest and the market that the grain commands, and the sorghum is the staple food that the farmers consume and the commodity that the farmers trade for the goods that they require from the markets of the towns and the cities.

## Culinary Uses and Preparation

The grain of the sorghum is prepared by the methods that the communities of the dry margins have developed for the cooking of the coarse grains, and the sorghum is most commonly consumed as the porridge that is the staple food of the farmers and the labourers of the transition zone and the eastern districts. The flour or the cracked grain of the sorghum is boiled in the water or the milk until the grain has softened and the mixture has thickened to the consistency of the porridge that the cook desires, and the porridge is eaten with the salt and the fat or the butter as the flavourings. The sorghum porridge is the food that is the breakfast and the dinner of the farming households, and the sorghum porridge is the food that sustains the labourers through the long days of the work in the fields.

The flatbread that is made from the sorghum flour is the other of the principal foods that the sorghum grain yields, and the sorghum flatbread is made by the mixing of the flour with the water and the salt to form the dough that is flattened into the rounds and cooked on the hot griddle or the flat stone that is heated by the fire. The sorghum flatbread is the accompaniment to the stews and the vegetable dishes that the farming households prepare, and the flatbread is used as the utensil for the scooping of the food from the communal dish. The sorghum flatbread is the denser and the coarser than the bread that is made from the wheat flour, and the sorghum flatbread has the nuttier and the earthier flavour that the communities of the dry margins prefer.

The sorghum grain is also used in the preparation of the fermented beverages, and the beer that is brewed from the sorghum is the drink that the communities of the transition zone consume in the place of the barley beer and the grape wine that are the drinks of the wetter regions. The sorghum beer is brewed by the mashing of the malted grain and the boiling of the wort and the fermentation by the yeasts that the brewers cultivate, and the beer of the sorghum is the opaque and the slightly sour and the mildly alcoholic beverage that is the refreshment after the labour and the drink of the celebrations and the gatherings. The brewing of the sorghum beer is the domestic craft that the women of the farming households practise, and the beer that is brewed is the beverage that the household provides for the family and the guests and the workers who assist in the harvest and the other tasks of the agricultural year.

## The Sorghum in the Economy

The sorghum is the foundation of the economy of the transition zone and the dry margins, and the cultivation of the sorghum is the activity that provides the food and the income for the farmers who work the land in the regions where the other crops cannot be grown. The harvest of the sorghum is the principal source of the wealth of the farming communities of the transition zone, and the grain is the commodity that the farmers trade for the goods that they require from the [markets and fairs](/content/economy/markets-and-fairs) of the towns and the cities. The trade in the sorghum grain is the commerce that connects the communities of the dry interior with the markets of the coast, and the grain is transported along the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) to the markets of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the coastal cities.

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the grain merchants regulate the trade in the sorghum and maintain the standards of the quality and the grading of the grain that govern the commerce. The sorghum is graded by the colour and the size and the cleanliness of the grain, and the different grades command the different prices in the markets. The [trade and tariffs](/content/economy/trade-and-tariffs) that are imposed on the sorghum by the authorities of the regions through which the grain passes add to the cost of the grain at the destination, and the tariffs that are levied by the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) on the grain that crosses the borders are the source of the revenue for the states.

The sorghum is also the fodder that the livestock of the transition zone consume, and the straw and the grain that are the by-products of the grain production are fed to the cattle and the goats and the sheep that the farmers keep. The integration of the livestock with the grain production is the feature of the farming system of the transition zone, and the manure of the animals is returned to the soil as the fertiliser that maintains the fertility of the fields through the years of the cultivation.

## Comparison to the Other Grains

The sorghum is the grain that is cultivated in the conditions where the [wheat](/content/species/flora/wheat) and the [barley](/content/species/flora/barley) and the [rice](/content/species/flora/rice) cannot be grown, and the sorghum is the grain that the farmers of the dry regions prefer for the reliability and the low requirements of the water and the labour. The sorghum is the more drought-resistant than the wheat and the barley, and the sorghum can survive the periods of the drought that would kill the other grains and produce the harvest from the remainder of the season. The sorghum is also the more tolerant of the high temperatures than the wheat and the barley, and the sorghum is the grain that the farmers plant in the regions where the summer heat is the limiting factor for the other grains.

The [millet](/content/species/flora/millet) is the grain that is the closest to the sorghum in the cultivation and the use, and the millet and the sorghum are the two grains that are grown in the dry regions and that provide the staple foods for the communities of the dry margins. The sorghum is the larger of the two plants and the grain of the sorghum is the larger and the coarser than the grain of the millet, and the sorghum is the grain that the farmers plant in the soils of the better quality and the moisture of the better supply, while the millet is the grain that the farmers plant in the poorest of the soils and the driest of the conditions. The flavour of the sorghum is the nuttier and the richer than the flavour of the millet, and the sorghum is the grain that the cooks prefer for the porridge and the flatbread while the millet is the grain that is used for the gruel and the porridge that are the simpler of the preparations.
