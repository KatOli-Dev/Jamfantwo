## Fig

The fig is the fruit of the transition zone between the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), planted in the courtyards and gardens where its roots can reach the moisture that lies beneath the dry surface.

The fig tree produces two crops each year, the first in early summer and the second in autumn, and the fruit that is not eaten fresh is dried for storage and for trade. The dried figs of the Sahrani interior are exported to Aethelar, where they are eaten as a sweetmeat or used in the preparation of the rich fruit cakes that are a feature of the northern winter festivals.

## Physical Characteristics

The fig is a deciduous tree that grows to a height of fifteen to twenty feet, the trunk being short and thick and the branches spreading wide to form a rounded and spreading canopy that provides shade in the hot climate of the Sahrani interior. The leaves of the fig are large and deeply lobed, the broad surfaces being a dark green colour that turns to yellow before the leaves fall in the autumn. The bark of the fig is smooth and grey, and the wood of the fig is soft and weak and of no value for construction or for the manufacture of tools.

The fruit of the fig is a synconium, a fleshy structure that encloses the flowers and the seeds within the interior of the fruit, and the fig does not produce the open flowers that are characteristic of the other fruit trees. The pollination of the fig is achieved by a species of small wasp that enters the interior of the fig through a narrow opening and lays its eggs within the fruit, the wasp larvae developing within the fig and the adult wasps emerging to carry the pollen to the other figs. The relationship between the fig and the wasp is one of the curiosities of the natural world, and the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) farmers who cultivate the fig are familiar with the wasp and the role that it plays in the production of the fruit.

## Cultivation in the Courtyards

The fig is planted in the courtyards and the gardens of the settlements and the farms of the transition zone, the tree being valued for the shade that it provides and for the fruit that it produces in the seasons of the year. The fig is propagated by the cuttings that are taken from the selected trees in the winter and planted in the nursery beds where they root in the moist soil, and the rooted cuttings are transplanted to the courtyard positions in the following spring. The fig tree begins to produce the fruit in the third or the fourth year after the planting, and the tree continues to produce for fifty years or more if it is well tended.

The cultivation of the fig requires a warm climate and a supply of water that is sufficient to sustain the tree through the dry season, and the fig is not grown in the regions where the frost is severe or the winter is long. The trees are planted in the positions where the roots can reach the water table or the underground channels that carry the water from the springs and the wells, and the fig does not require the irrigation that the other crops of the transition zone demand. The farmers of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) plant the fig in the corners of the courtyards and along the walls of the gardens, the tree being a characteristic feature of the domestic architecture of the Sahrani interior.

## The Two Crops of the Fig

The fig tree produces two crops in each year, the first crop appearing on the wood of the previous season and the second crop appearing on the wood of the current season. The first crop, known as the breba crop, is produced in the early summer and the figs of the breba crop are large and sweet and few in number, the fruit being considered a delicacy that is eaten fresh and not dried. The breba crop is consumed by the family that owns the tree and by the guests who are invited to share the fruit, and the breba figs are not traded in the markets of the region.

The second crop, known as the main crop, is produced in the autumn and the figs of the main crop are smaller and more numerous than the breba figs, the fruit being harvested in quantity and dried for the storage and the trade. The main crop is the crop that is of economic significance, and the dried figs of the main crop are the figs that are exported to the northern markets. The [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) farmers judge the success of the season by the yield of the main crop, and the years in which the main crop is abundant are the years of prosperity for the fig-growing regions.

## The Drying and Preservation of Figs

The drying of the figs is the principal method of preservation in the Sahrani interior, the warm and dry climate of the autumn providing the conditions that are ideal for the drying of the fruit. The harvested figs are laid out on the mats of woven grass in the sun, the figs being turned regularly to ensure that the drying is even and that the fruit does not ferment or mould in the heat. The drying process takes from three to seven days depending on the size and the moisture content of the figs, and the dried figs are soft and pliable and dark in colour when the process is complete.

The dried figs are packed in the baskets and the boxes that are lined with the leaves of the fig tree, the leaves protecting the fruit from the dust and the insects that would damage the figs in the storage. The packed figs are stored in the cool and dry rooms of the houses and the warehouses, and the dried figs will keep for a year or more if they are protected from the moisture and the pests. The figs that are destined for the export to [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are packed in the wooden boxes that are sealed with wax, and the sealed boxes are carried by the caravans that travel the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) to the ports of the coast.

## Trade and Economic Significance

The trade in the dried figs is a significant component of the commerce between the Sahrani interior and the northern continents, and the figs that are produced in the transition zone are among the most valued of the [spices and dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) and the dried fruits that are carried by the caravans and the ships. The dried figs of the Sahrani interior are sold in the markets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), and the merchants who deal in the figs are among the most prosperous of the traders of the Sahrani coast.

The dried figs that are exported to Aethelar are sold in the markets of the northern cities, where the figs are eaten as a sweetmeat and used in the preparation of the fruit cakes and the puddings that are a feature of the winter festivals. The price of the dried figs in the northern markets is high, for the cost of the transport from the Sahrani interior to the northern cities is a significant component of the final price, and the dried figs are a luxury that is afforded by the wealthy and the prosperous middle classes of the northern settlements. The trade in the dried figs is regulated by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the merchants who deal in the imported goods, and the quality of the figs that are sold in the northern markets is guaranteed by the guild marks that are stamped on the boxes.

## Cultural Role in the Sahrani Interior

The fig tree is a symbol of the domestic life of the Sahrani interior, the tree being planted in the courtyards of the houses and the gardens of the mosques and the public buildings, and the shade of the fig tree being the place where the family gathers in the heat of the day. The fig is mentioned in the songs and the poems of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) tradition as a symbol of the abundance and the generosity of the land, and the giving of the figs to the guest is a gesture of hospitality that is recognised throughout the region.

The fig is also associated with the religious traditions of the Sahrani interior, the tree being regarded as a sacred plant in the older traditions of the region, and the fig trees that grow near the shrines and the holy places are protected from the cutting and the harvesting by the customs of the people. The [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) who inhabit the desert margins regard the fig as a gift of the land that must be shared with the traveller and the poor, and the fig trees that grow along the caravan routes are left for the use of the travellers and the caravans that pass through the region.
