## Habitat and Distribution

Millet is one of the grains of the dry margins where the rainfall is insufficient for wheat or barley and where the cultivation of rice is impossible. It is grown in the transition zone between the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and in the eastern districts of Aethelar where the climate is warmer and the rains are less reliable than in the west.

## Physical Characteristics

The millet is a grass that grows to a height of two to four feet, the stems being slender and the leaves being broad and flat and arranged alternately along the stem. The millet is a plant that is adapted to the dry and the hot conditions of the Sahrani interior, the root system being fibrous and deep, the roots penetrating the soil to the depth that allows the plant to access the moisture that lies beneath the dry surface. The millet is a plant that is tolerant of the heat and the drought that would kill the wheat and the barley, the millet being able to survive the periods of the moisture stress that occur in the regions where the rainfall is unreliable.

The seed heads of the millet are the panicles that are produced at the top of the stem, the panicles being dense and bristly and the seeds being small and round and numerous. The seeds of the millet are among the smallest of the cereal grains, the individual seeds being less than the size of a grain of the sand, and the colour of the seeds varies from the pale yellow to the dark brown depending on the variety. The millet that is grown in the transition zone is the variety that is known as the pearl millet, the pearl millet being the variety that is the most drought-tolerant and the most productive in the conditions that the dry margins provide.

## Cultivation in the Dry Margins

Millet produces tiny seeds that are harvested by cutting the heads of the plant and threshing them to release the grain. The seeds are ground into a flour that is used to make a thin porridge or a flatbread called kisra that is a staple of the Sahrani interior. The cultivation of millet requires less water and less labour than the cultivation of wheat or rice, and the plant itself is more resistant to pests and diseases. The farmers of the transition zone rely upon millet as the foundation of their diet and their economy, and the grain that they produce is traded along the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) for the goods of the coast.

The millet is sown in the fields that are prepared by the hand cultivation that the Sahrani farmers practice, the seed being broadcast in the soil that has been loosened by the hoe and the plough. The millet is sown at the beginning of the rainy season, the seed germinating in the moist soil and the seedlings establishing before the dry period that follows the rains. The millet grows rapidly in the warm conditions, the plants reaching the maturity in the three months that the growing season provides, and the millet is harvested by the cutting of the seed heads with the sickles that the farmers use for the grain harvest. The millet that is grown in the transition zone is the millet that is the staple of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) interior, the grain being the food that sustains the people who live in the regions where the other crops cannot be grown.

## The Harvesting and the Threshing

The harvesting of the millet is the operation that is performed when the seed heads have turned from the green to the brown and the seeds have hardened, the stage of the maturity that the experienced farmer recognises by the colour and the texture of the panicles. The seed heads are cut with the sickles and gathered into the baskets that are carried to the threshing floors where the grain is separated from the chaff. The threshing is done by the beating of the seed heads with the flails that the farmers use for the grain, the beating releasing the small seeds from the panicles, and the winnowing is done by the tossing of the threshed grain in the wind that blows away the chaff and leaves the clean grain.

The threshed millet is stored in the granaries that are built of the mud and the straw that are the materials of the Sahrani construction, the granaries being raised on the platforms that protect the grain from the pests and the moisture that would damage the stored crop. The millet that is stored in the granaries will keep for the year or more if the conditions are dry and the pests are controlled, and the farmer who has stored a sufficient quantity of the millet can feed the family through the year and have the surplus for the trade. The millet that is traded to the markets of the coast is the millet that is carried by the caravans that travel the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way), the grain being one of the commodities that the caravans carry to the ports where the millet is sold to the merchants who trade with the other regions.

## The Preparation and the Kisra

The millet that is consumed by the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) people is prepared in the several forms that the culinary traditions of the interior have developed, the most common being the porridge that is made by the boiling of the millet flour in the water. The millet porridge is the food that is eaten at the breakfast and the evening meal, the porridge being the staple that is the foundation of the daily diet of the Sahrani people. The porridge is prepared by the women of the household, the millet flour being ground in the stone mills that are the equipment of every Sahrani kitchen, and the porridge being cooked in the clay pots that are set on the fires that the women tend.

The kisra is the flatbread that is the other principal form of the millet preparation, the kisra being a thin and flexible bread that is baked on the hot metal plates that are heated over the fire. The kisra is made by the pouring of the thin batter of the millet flour and the water onto the hot plate, the batter being spread into the thin sheet that is cooked in the minutes and then peeled from the plate and stacked. The kisra is the bread that is eaten with the stews and the sauces that are the accompaniments of the Sahrani meals, the bread being used to scoop the food and to convey it to the mouth in the manner that is the tradition of the Sahrani interior. The kisra is the bread that is the characteristic food of the Sahrani people, and the millet that is used in the kisra is the millet that is the staple of the diet and the culture of the interior.
