## Indigo

The indigo plant of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) produces a blue dye that is deeper and more intense than the blue of the northern woad, and the indigo of the Sahrani interior is one of the most valuable of the [spices and dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) that are traded from the southern continent.

The indigo plant is cultivated in the irrigated gardens of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the oases of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), where the climate is warm and the water is abundant. The indigo is harvested in the summer, and the leaves are processed by a method that involves the fermentation of the leaves in water and the precipitation of the pigment from the fermented liquor. The resulting paste is dried and formed into cakes that are exported to the markets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), where the indigo is sold at prices that reflect its quality and its rarity.

The indigo of Sahrana is the finest that can be obtained, and the blue that it produces is the standard against which the blues of the other dyes are measured. The dyers of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) prefer the Sahrani indigo for the dyeing of the finest cloths, and the trade in indigo is a significant part of the commerce between the northern and the southern continents.

## Physical Characteristics

The indigo is a perennial shrub that grows to a height of three to five feet, the stems being erect and branching and the leaves being pinnate and composed of the small leaflets that are arranged along the central stem. The flowers of the indigo are small and pink or purple, the flowers being borne in the clusters that appear in the midsummer, and the flowers give way to the small pods that contain the seeds of the plant. The leaves of the indigo are the part of the plant that contains the pigment, the indigotin being present in the leaves in the form of the precursor that is converted to the dye by the process of the fermentation and the oxidation.

The indigo plant is a member of the legume family, the roots being associated with the bacteria that fix the nitrogen from the air and that enable the plant to thrive in the soils that are too poor for the other crops. The nitrogen fixation is the property that makes the indigo a useful plant in the rotation of the crops, the indigo being planted in the fields that are being rested from the other crops, and the soil being enriched by the nitrogen that the indigo leaves behind when the crop is harvested. The farmers of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) understand the value of the indigo in the rotation, and the plant is grown not only for the dye but also for the improvement of the soil that the crop provides.

## Cultivation in the Green Crescent

The cultivation of the indigo is a practice that is confined to the warm and the irrigated regions of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the oases of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), the plant requiring the warm climate and the abundant water that the Sahrani interior provides. The indigo is propagated by the seed or by the cuttings, the seed being sown in the nursery beds and the seedlings being transplanted to the fields when they are large enough to handle, and the cuttings being taken from the selected plants and rooted in the moist soil of the nursery. The indigo is planted in the rows that are spaced to allow the cultivation and the weeding, and the plants are kept irrigated through the dry season by the water that is brought from the rivers and the wells.

The indigo is harvested in the summer, the stems being cut close to the ground when the leaves are at their maximum pigment content, and the cut stems are carried to the processing vats where the pigment is extracted. The indigo may be harvested twice in a single year, the first harvest being in the early summer and the second harvest being in the late summer, the regrowth from the first harvest providing the material for the second. The [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) farmers who cultivate the indigo are the specialists who have developed the methods for the optimal harvesting and the processing of the crop, and the knowledge of the indigo cultivation is passed from the master to the apprentice in the families that have grown the plant for the generations.

## The Processing of the Dye

The processing of the indigo is a complex and the skilled operation that involves the fermentation of the leaves and the precipitation of the pigment from the fermented liquor. The harvested indigo is placed in the vats that are filled with the water, and the leaves are weighted with the stones to keep them below the surface. The fermentation begins within a day of the soaking, the natural microorganisms that are present on the leaves breaking down the precursor compounds and releasing the indigotin into the water, the liquor turning from the green to the yellow to the blue as the fermentation progresses.

The fermented liquor is drained from the vats and the liquid is agitated by the beating with the wooden paddles that introduce the air and that cause the oxidation of the indigotin, the pigment precipitating from the liquor as the blue particles that settle to the bottom of the vat. The precipitated pigment is collected and dried, the paste that is formed being shaped into the cakes that are the form in which the indigo is traded. The processing of the indigo is a craft that requires the experience and the skill that the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) processors have developed over the centuries, and the quality of the indigo that is produced depends upon the care and the timing that the processor brings to the operation.

## The Dyeing Process and the Blue Cloth

The dyeing of the cloth with the indigo is a process that is different from the dyeing with the other pigments, the indigo being insoluble in the water and requiring the reduction to the soluble form that the cloth can absorb. The dyer prepares the indigo bath by the addition of the reducing agents that convert the indigo to the soluble leuco-indigo, the cloth being immersed in the bath and the fibres absorbing the soluble form of the dye. The cloth is then removed from the bath and exposed to the air, the leuco-indigo being oxidised back to the insoluble indigo within the fibres, the blue colour appearing as the oxidation proceeds.

The indigo dyeing is a process that may be repeated to build up the depth of the colour, the cloth that is dyed multiple times achieving the deep and the intense blue that is the characteristic of the finest indigo cloth. The dyers of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) who work with the indigo are the specialists who have mastered the techniques of the reduction and the oxidation that the dyeing requires, and the [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) that are produced by the indigo dyers are the most valued of the blue cloths that are available in the northern markets. The indigo blue is the colour that is associated with the wealth and the status, and the garments that are dyed with the indigo are the garments that are worn by the prosperous and the powerful.

## Trade and the Commerce of Indigo

The trade in indigo is a significant component of the commerce between the northern and the southern continents, the indigo that is produced in the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) being carried by the caravans and the ships to the markets of the north. The indigo cakes that are exported from the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) interior are sold in the markets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), and the merchants who deal in the indigo are among the most prosperous of the traders of the Sahrani coast. The indigo that is destined for the northern markets is packed in the chests that are lined with the waxed cloth, the chests being carried by the ships that trade across the [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea) to the ports of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar).

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the dyers and the merchants regulate the trade in the indigo, the guilds setting the standards of the quality and the prices that are paid for the different grades of the dye. The indigo that is of the finest quality commands the highest prices, and the indigo that is of the lower quality is used in the dyeing of the coarser cloths that are worn by the common people. The trade in the indigo is a major source of the wealth of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the other Sahrani states, and the revenues that are derived from the indigo trade are a significant component of the treasuries of the southern powers.

## Cultural Significance and the Blue of Sahrana

The indigo is a plant that is woven into the culture and the traditions of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) people, the blue that is produced from the indigo being the colour that is associated with the sky and the water and the spiritual life of the region. The indigo blue is the colour that is used in the garments of the priests and the officials of the Sahrani states, and the cloth that is dyed with the indigo is the cloth that is worn in the ceremonies and the rituals that mark the passages of the life. The indigo is also associated with the protective traditions of the Sahrani people, the blue cloth being believed to ward off the evil eye and the malevolent spirits that are the concerns of the people who live in the dangerous regions of the interior.

The indigo is also a symbol of the wealth and the sophistication of the Sahrani civilisation, the production of the dye being a craft that requires the knowledge and the skill that the Sahrani people have developed over the centuries. The indigo that is produced in the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) is the indigo that is sought by the dyers of the known world, and the reputation of the Sahrani indigo is the reputation that the Sahrani people have earned through the quality of the product and the reliability of the trade. The indigo is the plant that connects the Sahrani interior to the northern markets, and the trade in the dye is the trade that has brought the peoples of the two continents into the contact and the exchange that has shaped the history of the known world.
