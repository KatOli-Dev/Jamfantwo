The root vegetables that are grown in the gardens of the northern settlements are the most reliable source of fresh food in the diet of the northern peoples, for the roots store well through the winter and provide the vitamins and the minerals that the diet of the preserved and the dried foods lacks.

## The Root Vegetables of the Northern Gardens

The turnip is the most common of the northern root vegetables, sown in the spring after the frost has left the ground and harvested in the autumn before the return of the frost. The turnip is eaten fresh in the summer and stored in the cellars for the winter, and the turnip is also used as a feed for the animals that the northern settlements keep.

The carrot of the north is a variety that is shorter and thicker than the carrot of the south, a variety that has been selected for its ability to grow in the heavy and stony soils of the northern fields. The carrot is eaten fresh in the summer, and the stored carrots provide a source of sweetness in the winter diet that is otherwise lacking in sugar.

The cabbage of the north is the most important of the leafy vegetables, and the cabbage is grown in the gardens of every northern settlement. The cabbage is harvested in the autumn and stored in the cellars, and the cabbage is used in the preparation of the soups and the stews that are the common meals of the northern winter. The cabbage is also fermented into the sauerkraut that is a staple provision of the ships that carry the trade of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) and the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea).

## Physical Characteristics of the Root Vegetables

The root vegetables are the plants that produce the edible roots or the swollen underground stems that are the parts of the plant that are harvested for food, and the group includes the turnip, the carrot, the cabbage, and the other vegetables that are grown in the gardens of the northern settlements. The root vegetables are the biennials and the annuals that complete the cycle of the growth in one or two years, the plants producing the roots in the first year and the seeds in the second, though the vegetables are harvested in the first year before the plant has produced the seed stalk.

The turnip is a member of the brassica family, the plant producing the round or the elongated root that is white or the purple above the ground and white below. The leaves of the turnip are edible and are used as the greens that are the accompaniment to the meals of the northern table, and the turnip is a plant that grows quickly, the crop being ready for the harvest in six to eight weeks from the sowing. The carrot is a member of the umbellifer family, the plant producing the tapering root that is orange or the yellow, and the carrot is a slower-growing plant that requires the longer season to produce the root that is the edible product.

## The Turnip and Its Uses

The turnip is the most common of the northern root vegetables, sown in the spring after the frost has left the ground and harvested in the autumn before the return of the frost. The turnip is eaten fresh in the summer and stored in the cellars for the winter, and the turnip is also used as a feed for the animals that the northern settlements keep. The turnip is a productive crop, the plant yielding a large quantity of the food from a small area of the ground, and the turnip is a crop that can be grown in the poor soils that are not suitable for the more demanding vegetables.

The turnip is also used in the preparation of the soups and the stews that are the common meals of the northern winter, the turnip being boiled with the meats and the other vegetables that are the ingredients of the dishes. The turnip is also used in the preparation of the fermented products that are the preserves of the northern diet, the turnip being fermented in the brine that preserves the vegetable for the long storage that is the requirement of the winter provision.

## The Carrot and Its Varieties

The carrot of the north is a variety that is shorter and thicker than the carrot of the south, a variety that has been selected for its ability to grow in the heavy and stony soils of the northern fields. The carrot is eaten fresh in the summer, and the stored carrots provide a source of sweetness in the winter diet that is otherwise lacking in sugar. The carrot is also used in the preparation of the sweet dishes that are the desserts of the northern table, the carrot being boiled and mashed with the honey and the spices that are the ingredients of the puddings and the cakes.

The carrot is a plant that requires the deep and the stone-free soil to produce the straight and the uniform roots that are the product of the well-grown crop, and the preparation of the soil for the carrot is the most thorough of the preparations that are required for any of the root vegetables. The soil is dug to the depth of a spade and the stones are removed, the ground being raked to the fine tilth that is the condition that the carrot requires for the germination of the seed and the growth of the root.

## The Cabbage and the Fermentation

The cabbage of the north is the most important of the leafy vegetables, and the cabbage is grown in the gardens of every northern settlement. The cabbage is harvested in the autumn and stored in the cellars, and the cabbage is used in the preparation of the soups and the stews that are the common meals of the northern winter. The cabbage is also fermented into the sauerkraut that is a staple provision of the ships that carry the trade of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) and the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea).

The sauerkraut is prepared by the shredding of the cabbage and the packing of the shredded cabbage in the salt that preserves the vegetable by the fermentation that is the action of the bacteria that are present on the leaves. The sauerkraut is fermented in the barrels that are stored in the cellars, the fermentation taking several weeks to complete, and the sauerkraut that is the product of the fermentation is a sour and crunchy vegetable that is eaten as a relish and a side dish. The sauerkraut is a valuable provision of the ships, the fermented cabbage being a source of the vitamins that prevent the scurvy that afflicts the crews that are at sea for the long voyages.

## Storage and the Winter Cellars

The storage of the root vegetables in the cellars is the practice that ensures the supply of the fresh food through the winter months, the cellars being the structures that are dug into the ground and the walls that maintain the temperature and the humidity that are the conditions that preserve the vegetables. The root vegetables are stored in the bins and the racks that are arranged in the cellars, the vegetables being packed in the sand or the straw that prevents the loss of the moisture and the decay that would be the result of the exposure to the air.

The cellars of the northern settlements are the structures that are the most important to the survival of the communities, and the cellars that are filled with the root vegetables and the [potatoes](/content/species/flora/potato) are the assurance that the winter will not bring the hunger that was the constant companion of the northern peoples before the storage of the vegetables was perfected. The cellars are also used for the storage of the other foods that are the provisions of the winter, the cellars being the repositories of the cheeses and the meats and the fermented products that are the other components of the northern diet.

## The Root Vegetables in the Northern Diet

The root vegetables are the most reliable source of the fresh food in the diet of the northern peoples, for the roots store well through the winter and provide the vitamins and the minerals that the diet of the preserved and the dried foods lacks. The root vegetables are the accompaniment to the meats and the fish that are the other staples of the northern table, the vegetables being boiled and mashed and roasted in the fats that are the products of the animals that the northern settlements keep.

The root vegetables are also used in the preparation of the dishes that are the specialities of the northern cuisine, the vegetables being the ingredients of the pies and the puddings that are the foods of the winter festivals. The root vegetables are also traded to the southern markets, the turnips and the carrots being the products that are exported to the cities of the south where the fresh vegetables are valued in the winter season when the local supplies are exhausted. The trade in the root vegetables is a minor but significant component of the agricultural economy of the northern regions, and the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the vegetable merchants regulate the trade and the prices of the products.
