The water hyacinth is the invasive floating plant that was introduced to the Greywash Reed Marshes in recent decades, its dense mats choking the channels and the open water and demanding the constant labour of the marsh-dwelling communities to maintain navigation and drainage.

## The Water Hyacinth of the Reed Marshes

The water hyacinth that has been introduced to the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) in the recent decades is an invasive aquatic plant that has spread rapidly through the waterways of the marsh, forming the dense mats that choke the channels and the open water and that block the passage of the boats and the flow of the water.

The water hyacinth is a floating plant that reproduces rapidly, and the mats of the hyacinth can double in size in a matter of weeks under the favourable conditions. The control of the water hyacinth in the Reed Marshes is a constant struggle for the marsh-dwelling communities, who must clear the channels of the hyacinth to maintain the navigation and the drainage of the marsh.

The methods of the control of the water hyacinth include the mechanical removal of the mats by the boats that drag the nets through the infested channels, and the application of the plant preparations that suppress the growth of the hyacinth without harming the other species of the marsh. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the reed cutters and the marsh farmers cooperate with the authorities of the region in the management of the hyacinth infestation.

## Physical Characteristics

The water hyacinth is a free-floating aquatic plant that produces the rosette of the thick and the waxy leaves that are held above the surface of the water by the inflated petioles that act as the floats, the petioles being the swollen and the spongy structures that contain the air that keeps the plant at the surface. The leaves of the water hyacinth are a bright and the glossy green, the surface of the leaf being coated with the wax that repels the water and that gives the leaf the shiny appearance that distinguishes the species. The rosette of the leaves may reach a diameter of two feet in the mature specimens, the leaves being arranged in the open and the spreading formation that maximises the exposure to the light.

The flowers of the water hyacinth are among the most beautiful of the aquatic flowers, the blooms being a lavender to the violet in the colour with the yellow marking on the uppermost petal that is the signal for the pollinators. The flowers are produced on the spike that rises above the leaves, the spike bearing the several flowers that open in the succession over the days of the flowering period. The beauty of the water hyacinth flowers is the quality that led to the introduction of the plant to the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash), the plant being brought to the marsh as an ornamental species for the gardens of the marsh communities before the invasive potential of the species was recognised.

## Reproduction and Spread

The water hyacinth reproduces by the vegetative and the sexual means, the vegetative reproduction being the principal mode of the spread that has enabled the plant to colonise the waterways of the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) in the decades since the introduction. The vegetative reproduction is accomplished by the stolons that the plant produces, the stolons being the horizontal stems that extend from the parent rosette and that produce the daughter plants at the tips, the daughter plants becoming the independent individuals that are capable of the further reproduction. The rate of the vegetative reproduction is the factor that makes the water hyacinth the invasive species that it is, the doubling of the area of the mat in the weeks being the measure of the reproductive capacity that the plant displays.

The sexual reproduction of the water hyacinth produces the seeds that are contained in the pods that develop from the fertilised flowers, the seeds being small and numerous and being dispersed by the water that carries them to the new locations. The seeds of the water hyacinth are viable for the years that they may lie in the sediment of the marsh before the conditions trigger the germination, the seed bank that the plant establishes in the mud being the reservoir from which the new infestations emerge even after the visible plants have been removed.

## Ecological Impact

The ecological impact of the water hyacinth in the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) is the severe and the multifaceted effect that the dense mats of the plant have on the ecosystem of the marsh. The mats of the water hyacinth block the light from the submerged plants and the algae that are the base of the food web of the marsh, the reduction of the light causing the decline of the submerged vegetation and the organisms that depend on the plants. The decomposition of the submerged plants and the accumulation of the organic matter from the water hyacinth mats cause the depletion of the oxygen in the water, the anoxic conditions that result being the lethal environment for the [Carp](/content/species/fauna/carp) and the other aquatic animals that the marsh supports.

The water hyacinth also affects the physical structure of the marsh, the mats of the plant altering the flow of the water and the deposition of the sediment that shapes the channels and the open water of the marsh. The blocked channels cause the water to back up and the flooding of the areas that were previously the dry ground, the flooding displacing the communities and the activities that the marsh supports. The [reed](/content/species/flora/reed) beds that the marsh economy depends on are the habitats that the water hyacinth invades, the reed beds being the resources that the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the reed cutters harvest for the thatch and the fibre that the marsh provides.

## Control and Management

The control of the water hyacinth in the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) is the ongoing effort that the marsh communities and the authorities of the region maintain, the effort being the combination of the mechanical removal and the application of the plant preparations that suppress the growth. The mechanical removal is the labour-intensive method that the marsh communities use, the boats dragging the nets and the rakes through the mats to pull the plants from the water and to pile the removed material on the banks where the plants are left to decompose. The mechanical removal is the method that is most effective in the confined channels where the boats can operate, the open water being the area where the removal is the most difficult.

The plant preparations that suppress the growth of the water hyacinth are the extracts of the [willow](/content/species/flora/willow) bark and the other plants that the [healing and restoration](/content/magic/healing-and-restoration) practitioners and the herbalists of the marsh have developed, the extracts being applied to the mats in the quantities that inhibit the growth without the destruction of the other species. The preparations are the product of the research that the mages of the region have conducted, the research being the response to the infestation that the conventional methods have been unable to contain. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the reed cutters and the marsh farmers cooperate with the authorities in the application of the preparations and the monitoring of the results.

## Economic Consequences

The economic consequences of the water hyacinth infestation in the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) are the significant and the growing costs that the infestation imposes on the marsh economy. The costs of the control and the removal are the direct expenses that the communities and the authorities bear, the labour and the materials that the control requires being the expenditures that the [prices and livelihood](/content/economy/prices-and-livelihood) of the marsh communities must absorb. The indirect costs of the infestation include the loss of the [reed](/content/species/flora/reed) beds that the marsh economy harvests and the reduction of the [fishing and whaling](/content/economy/fishing-and-whaling) that the marsh waters support, the loss of the resources being the economic damage that the infestation causes.

The [cartage and transport](/content/economy/cartage-and-transport) through the marsh is also affected by the water hyacinth, the blocked channels impeding the movement of the goods that the marsh communities produce and the supplies that the communities require. The [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river) and the waterways of the marsh are the routes that the trade of the region follows, and the blockage of the routes by the water hyacinth is the disruption that the [trade and tariffs](/content/economy/trade-and-tariffs) of the region must accommodate. The [public works](/content/economy/public-works) programme of the region includes the maintenance of the channels that the water hyacinth blocks, the programme being the response of the authorities to the infrastructure challenge that the infestation presents.

## Attempts at Utilisation

The attempts to find the uses for the water hyacinth that the marsh communities have harvested have been the subject of the research and the experimentation that the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) and the mages of the region have conducted, the objective being the conversion of the nuisance plant into the resource that the costs of the removal can be offset by. The water hyacinth has been tested as the material for the compost that the marsh farmers produce, the high content of the nutrients in the plant being the quality that the compost can exploit. The water hyacinth has also been tested as the fibre for the [textiles and cloth](/content/economy/textiles-and-cloth) that the marsh produces, the stems of the plant yielding the fibre that can be spun into the coarse yarn.

The utilisation of the water hyacinth as the animal feed has been the application that the [livestock and herding](/content/economy/livestock-and-herding) communities of the region have explored, the [cattle](/content/species/fauna/cattle) and the [goats](/content/species/fauna/goats) being fed the fresh and the dried water hyacinth in the quantities that the animals will consume. The feed value of the water hyacinth is the moderate and the variable quality that the nutritional analysis has determined, the plant being the feed that the animals will accept but not the feed that the animals prefer. The utilisation of the water hyacinth is the ongoing project that the marsh communities pursue, the conversion of the problem into the resource being the goal that the communities and the authorities share.
