## The Grain of the Poorest Soils

Rye is the grain of the poorest soils and the coldest climates, capable of producing a crop where no other grain will grow. The rye fields of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and the high pastures of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) are sown in the spring after the snow has melted and are harvested in the short summer before the frost returns. The grain that results is dark and dense, producing a flour that makes a heavy bread with a distinctive sour taste that the people of the north have come to prefer.

## The Rye Bread of the Dwarven Holds

The rye bread of the dwarven holds is a dark loaf called khaz-brot that is baked from a mixture of rye flour and coarsely ground [barley](/content/species/flora/barley). The bread is dense and moist, and it keeps for months without spoiling, making it the ideal provision for the long journeys that dwarven merchants and miners undertake. The human communities of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) bake a lighter rye bread that is leavened with a sourdough starter maintained over generations in each household.

## The Distillation of the Rye Spirits

Rye is also used in the distillation of spirits, particularly in the highlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) where the [potato](/content/species/flora/potato) has not yet become established. The distilleries of the dwarven holds produce a spirit from fermented rye that is the strongest alcoholic beverage known in the three continents, and the trade in this spirit is one of the minor but profitable exports of the northern kingdoms.

## Physical Characteristics of the Rye Plant

The rye is a cereal grass that grows to a height of three to five feet, the stems being erect and slender, and the leaves being long and narrow with the sheaths that wrap the stem at the nodes. The rye produces the flowers in the spikes that are borne at the top of the stem, the spikes being the elongated clusters that carry the spikelets, and each spikelet containing the two or three grains that are the edible product of the plant. The grain of the rye is long and slender, the colour being a greyish green when the grain is immature and a golden brown when the grain is ripe.

The root of the rye is a fibrous system that spreads through the soil, the roots being deeper and more extensive than the roots of the [wheat](/content/species/flora/wheat) or the barley, and the deep roots are the feature that enables the rye to grow in the dry and the stony soils where the other grains cannot find the moisture that they require. The rye is also more tolerant of the cold than the other grains, the plant being able to survive the temperatures that would kill the wheat, and the rye is the grain that is grown in the regions where the winter is too severe for the other cereals.

## Cultivation and the Growing Season

The rye is sown in the spring after the snow has melted, the seed being broadcast by the hand or drilled by the machines that are the tools of the modern agriculture. The rye grows quickly in the short summer of the highlands, the plant reaching the maturity in the ninety to the one hundred days that are the length of the growing season in the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands). The rye is harvested in the late summer before the frost returns, the stalks being cut by the sickle or the scythe and gathered into the sheaves that are carried to the threshing floor.

The rye is a crop that requires less attention than the other grains, the plant being hardy and resistant to the diseases and the pests that afflict the more demanding cereals. The rye is also a crop that can be grown in the soils that are too poor for the wheat or the barley, the rye being able to extract the nutrients from the stony and the acidic soils that are the characteristic of the highland pastures. The rye is the grain that is grown in the regions where the other grains cannot be grown, and the rye is the crop that sustains the communities that live in the harshest of the environments of the north.

## The Rye in the Northern Diet

The rye is the staple grain of the northern regions, the flour of the rye being the ingredient of the bread that is the daily food of the peoples of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range). The rye bread is a dark and dense loaf that is baked in the ovens that are the feature of every household, the bread being the accompaniment to the soups and the stews that are the common meals of the northern winter. The rye bread is also the provision of the travellers and the hunters, the bread being durable and sustaining for the long journeys that are the requirement of the northern life.

The rye is also used in the preparation of the porridge that is the breakfast of the northern households, the rye being ground into the meal that is cooked in the milk or the water to produce the thick and filling dish that is the start of the day. The rye porridge is eaten with the butter and the honey that are the products of the northern farms, and the rye porridge is the food that sustains the labourers and the miners through the long working day.

## The Trade in Rye and Rye Spirits

The trade in rye is a component of the agricultural economy of the northern regions, the rye that is produced in the highlands being traded to the lowland settlements where the rye is the grain that is used for the bread and the spirits. The rye is carried in the sacks that are loaded onto the carts and the sledges that are the transport of the winter, and the rye is traded for the goods that the highland communities cannot produce, the iron and the salt and the textiles that are the products of the south.

The trade in the rye spirits is a profitable business that is controlled by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the distillers, the spirits being exported to the markets of the south where the strong and the fiery character of the rye spirit is appreciated by the consumers who can afford the price. The rye spirit is also used in the preparation of the medicinal remedies that the healers of the north prescribe for the colds and the infections of the winter, the spirit being the base of the tinctures and the elixirs that are the remedies of the northern apothecaries.
