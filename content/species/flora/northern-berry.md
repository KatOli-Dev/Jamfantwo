The berries that grow in the wilds of the north are not cultivated in the fields and the gardens, but the berries are gathered by the northern peoples in the autumn and preserved for the winter.

## The Berries of the Northern Wilds

The cloudberry is the most valued of the northern berries, a golden fruit that grows in the bogs and the wet meadows of the north. The cloudberry prevents the scurvy that afflicts the peoples who subsist on the preserved and the dried foods of the winter.

The bilberry and the lingonberry grow on the open slopes of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and in the clearings of the forests, and the berries are gathered by the women and the children of the northern settlements in the late summer and the early autumn. The berries are preserved as jams and as jellies, and the berries are also stored in barrels of water that keep the berries fresh for months.

## The Cloudberry and the Bogs

The cloudberry is the berry of the bogs and the wet meadows, the plant being a low and creeping herb that produces the single amber-coloured fruit on the stem that rises from the mat of the leaves. The cloudberry is the berry that is found in the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and the northern wilds, the plant being adapted to the cold and the wet conditions of the regions where the berry is found. The cloudberry is the berry that ripens in the late summer, the fruit changing from the red to the amber as it ripens, and the ripe berry being soft and juicy and rich in the vitamin C that prevents the scurvy.

The cloudberry is the berry that is most valued by the northern peoples, the fruit being the source of the vitamin C that is the essential nutrient that the winter diet lacks, and the cloudberry being the berry that has prevented the scurvy that would otherwise afflict the people who subsist on the preserved and the dried foods of the winter. The cloudberry is gathered in the bogs and the wet meadows where the plant grows, the gathering being done by the women and the children who know the locations of the cloudberry patches and the timing of the harvest. The cloudberry is a berry that is not cultivated, the plant being too specific in its requirements and too slow in its growth to be grown in the gardens, and the cloudberry is a berry that is only available from the wild.

## The Bilberry and the Forest Clearings

The bilberry is the berry of the forest clearings and the open slopes, the plant being a low shrub that produces the small dark blue berries that are similar to the blueberry in the flavour and the appearance. The bilberry is the berry that is found in the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and the forests of the north, the plant being adapted to the acid soils and the cool conditions that the upland regions provide. The bilberry ripens in the late summer, the berries being gathered by the women and the children who pick the fruit from the low shrubs that cover the slopes and the clearings.

The bilberry is the berry that is used in the preparation of the jams and the jellies that are the preserves of the northern settlements, the bilberry being the fruit that is boiled with the sugar and the honey that are the sweeteners that the northern regions provide. The bilberry jam is the preserve that is used on the bread and the porridge that are the staple foods of the winter, the jam being the source of the flavour and the nutrition that the winter diet lacks. The bilberry is also used in the preparation of the pies and the tarts that are the baked goods of the autumn, the bilberry being the fruit that is combined with the [apple](/content/species/flora/apple) and the other fruits that are available in the season of the harvest.

## The Lingonberry and the Preserves

The lingonberry is the berry of the open slopes and the moorlands, the plant being a low evergreen shrub that produces the small red berries that are the most acidic of the northern berries. The lingonberry is the berry that is found in the greatest abundance in the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands), the plant being adapted to the cold and the exposed conditions that the upland regions provide. The lingonberry ripens in the early autumn, the berries being gathered in the quantity that the abundance of the plant allows, and the lingonberry being the berry that is the most commonly preserved of the northern berries.

The lingonberry is the berry that is preserved by the method of the storage in the barrels of water, the berries being packed in the barrels and covered with the water that is kept cool and clean by the conditions of the storage. The lingonberry that is stored in the water will keep for the months that the winter lasts, the acidity of the berry being the natural preservative that prevents the spoilage, and the lingonberry being the berry that is available fresh through the winter when the other berries are long gone. The lingonberry is also preserved as the jam and the jelly that are the standard preserves of the northern settlements, the lingonberry being the berry that is the most versatile and the most reliable of the northern fruits.

## The Preservation and the Winter Store

The preservation of the berries is the practice that is essential to the survival of the northern peoples, the berries being the source of the vitamins and the minerals that the winter diet of the preserved and the dried foods does not provide. The berries are preserved in the several forms that the traditions of the northern regions have developed, the jams and the jellies being the most common, and the berries stored in the water being the other method that is used for the berries that are too acidic or too soft for the jam. The berries are also dried, the [northern berry](/content/species/flora/northern-berry) being the fruit that is dried in the sun or in the kilns that are built for the purpose, and the dried berries being the fruit that is used in the porridge and the bread that are the winter foods.

The preservation of the berries is the work that is done by the women of the northern settlements, the women being the people who gather the berries and who prepare the preserves that are the winter store of the household. The [long winter](/content/history/the-long-winter) that afflicts the northern regions is the challenge that the preservation of the berries is designed to meet, and the household that has a sufficient store of the berry preserves is the household that will survive the winter without the scurvy and the deficiency that the poor diet would cause. The [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) and the other northern peoples regard the berry preserves as the essential provision of the winter, and the gathering and the preservation of the berries is the activity that is the rhythm of the autumn in the northern settlements.

## Cultural Significance and the Gathering Tradition

The gathering of the berries is a tradition that is woven into the culture of the northern peoples, the gathering being the activity that marks the transition from the summer to the autumn and the preparation for the winter that is the season of the indoor life. The gathering of the berries is the work that is done by the families and the communities, the women and the children going to the berry grounds in the parties that are the social occasions as well as the work, and the gathering being the activity that is the source of the stories and the songs that the northern peoples maintain. The berry grounds are the places that are known by the families and the communities, the locations of the best patches being the knowledge that is passed from the mother to the daughter and that is guarded from the outsiders who might gather the berries that the family claims.

The berries are also associated with the folklore and the traditions of the northern peoples, the berries being the fruits that are regarded as the gifts of the land that the northern peoples inhabit, and the gathering of the berries being the activity that is accompanied by the rituals and the customs that the respect for the land requires. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the northern forests regard the berries as the fruits that are under the protection of the forest spirits, and the gathering of the berries in the forests is accompanied by the offerings and the prayers that the elves and the other northern peoples observe to honour the spirits and the land that provides the berries. The berries are the fruits that connect the northern peoples to the land and the seasons, and the gathering and the preservation of the berries is the tradition that is the foundation of the northern way of life.
