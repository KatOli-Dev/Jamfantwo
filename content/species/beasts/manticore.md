## Monstrosity of the Rocky Country

The manticore is one of the great monstrosities of the rocky country of the known world, a creature of considerable size and singular ferocity whose range extends across the broken lands of the three continents. It takes its name from the maned head and leonine body it displays in its conformation. Naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) consider it one of the most dangerous predators of the warm rocky country. The manticore requires the broken country of cliffs and rocky hills and the shelter of caves found there; it is found in greatest numbers in the foothills of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and in the broken country bordering the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana).

## Maned Head and Barbed Tail

The manticore is a creature of considerable size, with a body set upon four powerful legs in the manner of the great lion and a head that is broad and maned, bearing a manlike face that chroniclers have found singularly unsettling. The body is covered in a short tawny coat shading to darker brown across the back and shoulders; the coat is of a thickness suited to the warm climates in which the creature is found. The forelegs are armed with retractable claws characteristic of the great cats, the principal weapons of close combat. The head is the most singular feature: a broad maned face with three rows of sharp teeth employed in the tearing of prey, and eyes of a dark piercing quality likened to polished obsidian.

The wings are broad and membranous, supported by a single claw at the joint of the wing; the manticore is capable of strong and sustained flight in the manner of great bats, though it does not soar at the great heights attained by the [stormwing roc](/content/species/beasts/stormwing-roc). The tail is the most celebrated attribute, armed with a cluster of long spines that the manticore can discharge at prey or pursuer with force sufficient to penetrate the body of a horse at a considerable distance. Size varies with subspecies and range; a mature male may measure fifteen feet from the tip of the snout to the root of the tail, and the wingspan is considerable.

## Caves and Broken Ground

The manticore inhabits the broken country of the three continents, with the largest populations in the foothills of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the rocky country bordering the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and a smaller population in the broken country of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). It requires broken country and the shelter of caves; it is not found in open plains or dense forest. The lair is typically situated in a cave opening upon a sheltered ledge of a cliff, marked at the entrance by the remains of creatures consumed in the course of hunting.

The manticore is solitary; mature animals hold territories marked by scent left upon prominent rocks and by great claw-marks scored upon the stone. A territory may extend for several miles through the broken country; the creature ranges widely in search of prey. It is most active at dusk and dawn, emerging from the lair to hunt upon game trails winding through the rocky country, and returns before the full light of day.

## Ambush and Projectile Attack

The manticore is a predator of considerable power that feeds upon a wide range of prey according to opportunity. The principal prey is larger mammals of the broken country, including deer of the rocky hills and goats of the highland cliffs; a mature manticore will take a yearling deer with a sudden rush from cover. It also takes smaller mammals such as hares and rock coneys when larger prey is not available. The manticore does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but accounts of the hill communities include incidents in which a manticore has killed a traveller who unwarily approached the lair; hill folk take care to mark the lairs and give them a wide berth.

The hunting technique is to ambush from cover and seize prey with a sudden rush; the great claws of the forepaw deliver a blow sufficient to disable prey at the first contact. The manticore is capable of a short rapid flight sufficient to overtake fleeing prey upon broken ground, taking to the wing to pursue a deer that has broken away from the initial rush. The tail is used at a distance; the creature discharges the spines with a flick likened to the flight of arrows. A manticore engaged at a distance is a hazard that hunters of the broken country have learned to respect.

## Lore and Hunting of the Hills

The manticore occupies a singular place in the traditions of the hill communities and is the subject of a body of lore among the most elaborate of the cautionary traditions of the broken country. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) do not hunt the manticore; their traditions hold that the manticore is a creature of the upper world and the dwarves the lords of the lower, and they seal the upper passages of their halls against intrusion. The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the hill communities regard the manticore with wary respect; the hunter who has killed a manticore is honoured with a title second only to that of the hunter who has taken a [dragon](/content/species/beasts/dragon).

The hunting of the manticore is a recognised pursuit of the hill communities; hunters take a small number each year under licence from local authorities. The hunt is conducted with the heavy bow or the crossbow; the hunter must have the skill and courage to bring the creature down before it can close the distance or discharge the spines of the tail, for the charge is of terrifying speed and the spines are sufficient to disable a man at a considerable distance. The manticore is not held in particular reverence; it is regarded as a hazard of the hills and is hunted with the same practical purpose that hill folk bring to the hunting of the wolf and the bear.

## Claw-Marks Upon the Stone

The manticore is the foremost of the great monstrosities of the broken country, and its presence in the rocky lands of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the hills. It is the predator that keeps the populations of deer of the rocky country in balance with the carrying capacity of the broken ground; the loss of the manticore from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the pasture. The manticore endures in the broken country of the three continents and in the cautionary lore of the hill folk who share the world with the creature as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the rocks. The great claw-marks upon the stone of the cliff are a reminder that the broken country is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
