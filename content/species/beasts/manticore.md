## Overview

The manticore is one of the great monstrosities of the rocky country of the known world, a creature of considerable size and of singular ferocity whose range extends across the broken lands of the three continents. The manticore takes its name from the maned head and the leonine body that the creature displays in its conformation, and the animal is held by the naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) to be one of the most dangerous of the predators of the warm and rocky country. The chronicler notes that the manticore is not a creature of the open plains or the dense forest, for the animal requires the broken country of the cliffs and the rocky hills and the shelter of the caves that are found there, and the manticore is found in greatest numbers in the foothills of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and in the broken country that borders the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana).

## Form and Appearance

The manticore is a creature of considerable size, with a body that is set upon four powerful legs in the manner of the great lion and a head that is broad and maned and that bears the features of a manlike face that the chroniclers have found singularly unsettling. The body of the manticore is covered in a short tawny coat that shades to a darker brown across the back and the shoulders, and the coat is of a thickness that is suited to the warm climates in which the creature is found. The forelegs of the manticore are armed with the retractable claws that are characteristic of the great cats, and the claws are the manticore's principal weapons of close combat. The head of the manticore is the creature's most singular feature, for the head bears a broad maned face with three rows of sharp teeth that the creature is able to employ in the tearing of the prey, and the eyes are of a dark and a piercing quality that the chroniclers of the desert have likened to the colour of polished obsidian.

The wings of the manticore are broad and membranous and are supported by a single claw at the joint of the wing, and the manticore is capable of a strong and sustained flight in the manner of the great bats, though the creature does not soar at the great heights that are attained by the [stormwing roc](/content/species/beasts/stormwing-roc) of the mountain ranges. The tail of the manticore is the creature's most celebrated attribute, for the tail is armed with a cluster of long spines that the manticore is able to discharge at the prey or at the pursuer with a force that is sufficient to penetrate the body of a horse at a considerable distance. The size of the manticore varies with the subspecies and the range, but a mature male may measure some fifteen feet from the tip of the snout to the root of the tail, and the wingspan of the same animal is considerable.

## Habitat and Range

The manticore inhabits the broken country of the three continents, with the largest populations in the foothills of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and in the rocky country that borders the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and a smaller population in the broken country of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The manticore requires the broken country of the cliffs and the rocky hills and the shelter of the caves that are found there, and the creature is not found in the open plains or in the dense forest. The lair of the manticore is typically situated in a cave that opens upon a ledge of a cliff that is sheltered from the prevailing wind, and the lair is marked at the entrance by the remains of the creatures that the manticore has consumed in the course of its hunting.

The manticore is a solitary creature, and the mature animals hold territories that they mark by the scent that they leave upon the prominent rocks and by the great claw-marks that they score upon the stone. The territory of a mature manticore may extend for several miles through the broken country, and the creature ranges widely in search of the prey that sustains it. The manticore is most active in the hours of dusk and of dawn, when the creature emerges from the lair to hunt upon the game trails that wind through the rocky country, and the manticore returns to the lair before the full light of the day, when the heat of the sun would render the creature less effective in the hunt.

## Diet and Behaviour

The manticore is a predator of considerable power, and the creature feeds upon a wide range of prey according to the opportunity. The principal prey of the manticore is the larger mammals of the broken country, including the deer of the rocky hills and the goats of the highland cliffs, and a mature manticore will take a yearling deer with a sudden rush from the cover of the rocks. The manticore is also a predator of the smaller mammals of the broken country, and the creature will take the hares and the rock coneys when the larger prey is not available. The manticore does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but the accounts of the hill communities include a number of incidents in which a manticore has killed a traveller who has unwarily approached the lair, and the hill folk take care to mark the lairs of the manticores and to give them a wide berth.

The hunting technique of the manticore is to ambush the prey from the cover of the rocks and to seize the prey with a sudden rush, the great claws of the forepaw delivering a blow that is sufficient to disable the prey at the first contact. The manticore is capable of a short and rapid flight that is sufficient to overtake a fleeing prey upon the broken ground, and the creature will take to the wing to pursue a deer that has broken away from the initial rush. The tail of the manticore is used at a distance, the creature discharging the spines with a flick of the tail that the chroniclers of the desert have likened to the flight of a flight of arrows, and a manticore that is engaged at a distance is a hazard that the hunters of the broken country have learned to respect.

## The Manticore in Tradition

The manticore occupies a singular place in the traditions of the hill communities, and the creature is the subject of a body of lore that is among the most elaborate of the cautionary traditions of the broken country. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) do not hunt the manticore, for the dwarven traditions hold that the manticore is a creature of the upper world and that the dwarves are the lords of the lower, and the dwarves take care to seal the upper passages of their halls against the intrusion of the creature. The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the hill communities regard the manticore with a wary respect, and the hunter who has killed a manticore is honoured by his community with a title that is second only to that of the hunter who has taken a [dragon](/content/species/beasts/dragon).

The hunting of the manticore is a recognised pursuit of the hill communities, and the hunters of the rocky country take a small number of manticores each year under licence from the local authorities. The hunt is conducted with the heavy bow or with the crossbow, and the hunter who engages a manticore must have the skill and the courage to bring the creature down before it can close the distance or discharge the spines of the tail, for the charge of the manticore is a thing of terrifying speed and the spines of the tail are sufficient to disable a man at a considerable distance. The chronicler notes that the manticore is not held in any particular reverence by the communities of the broken country, for the creature is regarded as a hazard of the hills and not as a symbol of the wildness of the country, and the manticore is hunted with the same practical purpose that the hill folk bring to the hunting of the wolf and the bear.

## Significance

The manticore is the foremost of the great monstrosities of the broken country, and the presence of the creature in the rocky lands of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the hills. The manticore is the predator that keeps the populations of the deer of the rocky country in balance with the carrying capacity of the broken ground, and the loss of the manticore from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the pasture that the chroniclers have noted in the records of the hill communities. The chronicler notes that the manticore endures, in the broken country of the three continents and in the cautionary lore of the hill folk who share the world with the creature, as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the rocks, and the great claw-marks upon the stone of the cliff are a reminder that the broken country is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
