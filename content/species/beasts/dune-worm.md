## Overview

The dune worm is the largest of the invertebrates of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), a great annelid of considerable length whose range extends across the deep sands of the desert interior of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The dune worm is a creature of the deep erg, and it is rarely encountered by the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) of the caravan routes, but its presence in the desert is a familiar feature of the lore of the caravan masters, and the worm is the subject of a substantial body of legend among the desert peoples. The chronicler notes that the dune worm is among the most controversial of the creatures of the desert, for the scholars of the coastal academies have debated the reality of the creature for centuries, and the question of whether the worm is a true animal or a creature of the campfire tale has not been settled to the satisfaction of all parties.

## Form and Appearance

The dune worm is a segmented creature of considerable length, with a body that may extend for many yards beneath the surface of the sand and that is composed of a great number of similar segments, each bearing a pair of the bristles that are the worm's principal organs of locomotion. The body is cylindrical and tapering, with the anterior end slightly broader than the posterior, and the surface of the body is covered by a tough cuticle that is adapted to the abrasion of the sand in which the worm lives. The colour of the cuticle is a pale buff that blends with the colour of the desert sand, and the worm is effectively invisible when it lies just beneath the surface.

The head of the dune worm is a simple structure, without the eyes or the antennae that are found upon the more familiar invertebrates, for the worm is a creature of the subterranean environment and the sense organs that would be useful upon the surface are absent. The mouth is set at the anterior end of the body, and the worm draws the sand into the mouth and extracts the organic matter that the sand contains, the cleaned sand being passed out at the posterior end of the body. The worm does not possess the powerful jaws of the predatory invertebrates, and the worm is not dangerous to the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) or to the larger animals of the desert.

## Habitat and Lair

The dune worm inhabits the deep sands of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), particularly the regions of the great dunes where the sand is deep and the movement of the surface is most pronounced. The worm lives in a burrow that it excavates in the sand, the burrow extending horizontally beneath the surface for the length of the worm's body and being marked at the surface by a low ridge of sand that the worm has displaced in the course of its progress. The burrow is not a permanent structure, for the worm is constantly extending the burrow in search of the organic matter upon which it feeds, and the worm may move many yards in the course of a single night.

The worm is most active in the hours of darkness and in the cooler hours of the dawn, when the surface of the sand is not too hot for the worm to remain in the upper layers. During the heat of the day the worm descends to the deeper layers of the sand, where the temperature is more moderate and where the worm is safe from the heat of the surface. The worm does not surface voluntarily, for the worm is not adapted to the conditions of the open air, and the worm remains beneath the sand throughout its life. The worm is occasionally exposed by the shifting of the dunes, and the worm that is uncovered by the wind is a great and a helpless creature upon the surface, the segments of the body contracting and expanding in a vain attempt to return to the sand.

## Diet and Behaviour

The dune worm feeds upon the organic matter that is found in the sand of the desert, and the worm is one of the principal agents in the cycling of the nutrients that sustain the limited vegetation of the deep erg. The worm draws the sand into the mouth and passes it through the gut, the organic matter being digested and the cleaned sand being passed out at the posterior end of the body. The castings of the worm are deposited upon the surface of the sand, and the castings are a familiar feature of the deep desert, the small piles of clean sand that mark the route of the worm beneath.

The worm is not a social animal, and the mature animals are widely dispersed through the sands of the desert. The worms do not appear to communicate with one another, and the encounter of two worms within the sand is a rare event that the scholars of the desert have not been able to observe. The worm is a long-lived creature for an invertebrate of its kind, and a worm that escapes the hazards of the deep desert may live for many years, the oldest worms attaining a size that is impressive even by the standards of the great invertebrates of the world.

## The Legend of the Worm

The dune worm occupies a prominent place in the lore of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), and the worm is the subject of a substantial body of legend among the desert peoples. The legends of the worm are of two principal kinds, the first of which describes the great worms that lie beneath the dunes and that occasionally swallow a caravan that is unwise enough to camp upon the shifting sands, and the second of which describes the wisdom of the worms and their role as the keepers of the deep desert. The scholars of the coastal academies have long debated the relation of the legends to the reality of the creature, and the question has not been settled.

The chronicler notes that the existence of the dune worm is attested by the castings that are found upon the surface of the deep desert, and the scholars of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) have examined the castings and have concluded that they are the product of a creature of considerable size that lives beneath the sand. The naturalists of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) have observed the worm upon the occasions when the shifting of the dunes has exposed the creature, and the descriptions that they have provided are consistent with the creature that the legends describe. The legend of the worm that swallows the caravan is, however, regarded with scepticism by the scholars of the coastal academies, and the question of whether the worm is capable of taking a creature as large as a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) or a camel remains a matter of debate.

## Significance

The dune worm is one of the principal inhabitants of the deep erg of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and the worm's role in the cycling of the nutrients of the desert sand is a matter of considerable importance to the ecology of the deep desert. The worm is not a creature of the caravan routes, and the worm is not ordinarily encountered by the travellers of the desert, but the worm's presence in the deep sands is a reminder that even the most inhospitable of the environments of the world is inhabited by creatures that are adapted to the conditions. The chronicler notes that the worm endures, in the sands of the deep erg and in the legends of the desert peoples, as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the deep desert, and the castings of the worm upon the surface of the sand are a reminder that the desert is not empty of life, and that the great dunes conceal a community of creatures whose lives are ordered by the rhythms of the sand and the slow passage of the seasons.
