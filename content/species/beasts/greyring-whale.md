## Overview

The Greyring whale is the largest of the cetaceans of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean), a great baleen whale of dark grey-blue colour whose range extends across the cold waters of the western ocean from the polar south to the temperate latitudes of the western coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). It takes its name from the colour of its skin and of the waters it inhabits; the cold currents of the Greyring give the ocean a distinctive grey cast that mariners of the western coast have likened to a great ring of haze upon the horizon. The Greyring whale is the object of a substantial fishery conducted from the ports of the western Aethelaran coast; the oil and baleen recovered are among the most valuable exports of the western settlements.

## Form and Appearance

The Greyring whale is a creature of extraordinary size, with a body that may attain a length greater than a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) span by many times and a weight measured in tons. The body is streamlined and tapered, adapted to the long migrations the whale undertakes through the waters of the Greyring; the head is a great blunt mass constituting a substantial portion of the total length. The mouth is set with plates of baleen that hang from the upper jaw in a long series, filtering small creatures of the sea from the water the whale takes into the mouth.

The skin is a dark grey-blue across the back and flanks, with a paler grey upon the underparts and a mottling of lighter patches upon the head and flippers characteristic of the species. The skin is smooth and remarkably free of parasites that infest smaller cetaceans; the whale is a clean and graceful creature in the water, rising to breathe in a manner that mariners have described as a breach of the grey. The spout, the column of vapour expelled from the blowhole when the whale surfaces, is distinctive and is one of the signs by which whalers identify the presence of the whale from a distance.

## Habitat and Range

The Greyring whale inhabits the cold waters of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean), with the greatest concentration of the population in the temperate and sub-polar latitudes. The whale is migratory: the populations move northward in spring to feed in the rich waters of the polar latitudes and southward in autumn to breed in the warmer waters of the temperate latitudes. The migrations are closely followed by the whalers of the western coast, and the timing of the passages is one of the principal determinants of the rhythm of the fishing year.

The whale is most numerous in the vicinity of upwellings that bring small creatures to the surface; the great congregations upon the feeding grounds in the summer months are among the most impressive spectacles of the natural world. The whale is social during the migrations; the pods upon the feeding grounds may number several dozen individuals moving in a loose formation and cooperating in the taking of food. The whale is solitary outside the migrations; mature animals are widely dispersed through the waters of the Greyring during the winter and breeding season.

## Diet and Feeding

The Greyring whale is a filter feeder, taking small creatures of the sea from the water drawn into the mouth. The principal prey is krill, the small crustaceans that swarm in the cold waters of the Greyring in incredible numbers; the whale also takes small fish found in the same waters. It feeds by taking a great gulp of water into the mouth and forcing it out through the plates of baleen, trapping small creatures upon the inner surface and swallowing them with the tongue.

Feeding is most active during the summer months when the whale is upon the feeding grounds of the polar latitudes; it lays down the fat that will sustain it through the lean months of the breeding season. Feeding is conducted at all hours, for the prey is uniformly distributed through the surface waters, and the whale may rise to breathe every few minutes during active feeding. The whale does not feed to any substantial degree during the migrations, drawing upon the fat laid down during the summer for the energy of the long passage.

## The Whalers of the Western Coast

The whalers of the western coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) have pursued the Greyring whale for centuries; the fishery is one of the recognised pursuits of the maritime communities of that shore. It is conducted from small boats launched from larger vessels, the boats pursuing the whale with the hand harpoon and long lance; the crews are among the most skilled of the mariners of the western coast. The fishery is regulated by the customs of the coastal communities: the taking of whales is forbidden during the breeding season, and the smallest whales must be released.

The oil rendered from the blubber is the most valuable product of the fishery; it is used in the lamps of coastal towns and interior settlements and is exported to the markets of the eastern coast. The baleen recovered from the jaw is used in the manufacture of combs and brush backs by craftsmen of the western towns. The meat is eaten by the coastal communities; dried and salted meat is a trade good that reaches interior settlements. The bones are used in the construction of larger buildings; the ribs of a great whale may support the roof of a barn or workshop in coastal towns.

## Reproduction and Life Cycle

The Greyring whale breeds in the warmer waters of the temperate latitudes; females give birth to a single calf after a gestation of nearly a year. The calf is nursed upon the rich milk of the mother for several months and remains with the mother through the first migration northward to the feeding grounds. Mortality among calves is high, for they are preyed upon by larger sharks of the temperate waters; mothers are vigilant in defence of the young. Whales that survive to maturity are long-lived; one that escapes the hazards of the fishery and predation by sharks may live for many decades.

Whales reach maturity at several years of age; mature animals return to the breeding grounds each year to mate and calve. Courtship is a complex affair, the males competing for the attention of females through displays of strength and endurance; the pair bond formed during courtship is not maintained beyond the breeding season. The whales are highly vocal during the breeding season; the songs males sing upon the breeding grounds are among the most remarkable natural sounds of the sea, a series of low resonant calls that may continue for hours upon the still water.

## Significance

The Greyring whale is the largest of the creatures of the cold waters of the western ocean; its presence in the Greyring is a measure of the productivity of those waters. It is the object of a substantial fishery conducted from the ports of the western coast, and the products of the fishery are a significant component of the economy of the coastal communities. The whale endures in the cold waters of the Greyring and in the traditions of the western coast as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the waters that wash the shores of the western continent. The great shape of the whale rising from the grey water is a reminder that the ocean is not empty of life and that the largest of the creatures of the known world makes its home in the cold and the mist of the Greyring.
