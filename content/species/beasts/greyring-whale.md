## Overview

The Greyring whale is the largest of the cetaceans of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean), a great baleen whale of dark grey-blue colour whose range extends across the cold waters of the western ocean from the polar south to the temperate latitudes of the western coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The whale takes its name from the colour of the skin and from the colour of the waters that it inhabits, for the cold currents of the Greyring give the ocean a distinctive grey cast that the mariners of the western coast have likened to a great ring of haze upon the horizon. The chronicler notes that the Greyring whale is the object of a substantial fishery that is conducted from the ports of the western Aethelaran coast, and the oil and the baleen that are recovered from the whale are among the most valuable of the exports of the western settlements.

## Form and Appearance

The Greyring whale is a creature of extraordinary size, with a body that may attain a length greater than the length of a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) span by many times and a weight that is measured in tons rather than in any smaller unit. The body is streamlined and tapered, adapted to the long migrations that the whale undertakes through the waters of the Greyring, and the head is a great blunt mass that constitutes a substantial portion of the total length of the animal. The mouth is set with the plates of baleen that are the whale's principal organ of feeding, the plates hanging from the upper jaw in a long series that filters the small creatures of the sea from the water that the whale takes into the mouth.

The skin of the Greyring whale is a dark grey-blue across the back and the flanks, with a paler grey upon the underparts and a mottling of lighter patches upon the head and the flippers that is characteristic of the species. The skin is smooth and remarkably free of the parasites that infest the smaller cetaceans, and the whale is a clean and a graceful creature in the water, rising to the surface to breathe in a manner that the mariners of the western coast have described as a "breach of the grey". The spout of the whale, the column of vapour that is expelled from the blowhole when the whale surfaces, is distinctive and is one of the signs by which the whalers identify the presence of the whale from a distance.

## Habitat and Range

The Greyring whale inhabits the cold waters of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean), with the greatest concentration of the population in the temperate and the sub-polar latitudes of the western ocean. The whale is migratory, and the populations move northward in the spring to feed in the rich waters of the polar latitudes, and southward in the autumn to breed in the warmer waters of the temperate latitudes. The migrations of the whale are closely followed by the whalers of the western coast, and the timing of the northward and the southward passages is one of the principal determinants of the rhythm of the fishing year.

The whale is most numerous in the vicinity of the upwellings that bring the small creatures of the sea to the surface, and the great congregations that gather upon the feeding grounds in the summer months are among the most impressive spectacles of the natural world. The whale is a social animal during the migrations, and the pods that gather upon the feeding grounds may number several dozen individuals, the members of the pod moving in a loose formation and cooperating in the taking of the food. The whale is solitary outside the migrations, and the mature animals are widely dispersed through the waters of the Greyring during the winter and the breeding season.

## Diet and Feeding

The Greyring whale is a filter feeder, taking the small creatures of the sea from the water that it draws into the mouth. The principal prey of the whale is the krill, the small crustaceans that swarm in the cold waters of the Greyring in incredible numbers, and the whale also takes the small fish that are found in the same waters. The whale feeds by taking a great gulp of water into the mouth and then forcing the water out through the plates of baleen, the small creatures being trapped upon the inner surface of the plates and then swallowed by the tongue.

The feeding of the whale is most active during the summer months, when the whale is upon the feeding grounds of the polar latitudes, and the whale lays down the fat that will sustain it through the lean months of the breeding season. The feeding is conducted at all hours of the day and the night, for the prey is uniformly distributed through the surface waters, and the whale may rise to the surface to breathe every few minutes during the active feeding. The whale does not feed during the migrations to any substantial degree, the animal drawing upon the fat that has been laid down during the summer for the energy of the long passage.

## The Whalers of the Western Coast

The whalers of the western coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are a hardy folk who have pursued the Greyring whale for centuries, and the fishery of the whale is one of the recognised pursuits of the maritime communities of that shore. The fishery is conducted from small boats that are launched from the larger vessels, the boats pursuing the whale with the hand harpoon and the long lance, and the crews of the boats are among the most skilled of the mariners of the western coast. The fishery is regulated by the customs of the coastal communities, and the taking of the whale is forbidden during the breeding season, and the smallest of the whales must be released.

The oil that is rendered from the blubber of the whale is the most valuable of the products of the fishery, and the oil is used in the lamps of the coastal towns and the interior settlements, and it is also exported to the markets of the eastern coast. The baleen that is recovered from the jaw is used in the manufacture of a variety of articles, including the combs and the brush backs that are produced by the craftsmen of the western towns. The meat of the whale is eaten by the coastal communities, and the dried and salted meat is a trade good that reaches the interior settlements. The bones of the whale are used in the construction of the larger buildings, and the ribs of a great whale may be seen supporting the roof of a barn or a workshop in the coastal towns.

## Reproduction and Life Cycle

The Greyring whale breeds in the warmer waters of the temperate latitudes, and the females give birth to a single calf after a gestation of nearly a year. The calf is nursed upon the rich milk of the mother for a period of several months, and the calf remains with the mother through the first migration northward to the feeding grounds. The mortality of the calves is high, for the calves are preyed upon by the larger sharks of the temperate waters, and the mothers are vigilant in the defence of the young. The whales that survive to maturity are long-lived, and a whale that escapes the hazards of the fishery and the predation of the sharks may live for many decades.

The whales reach maturity at an age of several years, and the mature animals return to the breeding grounds each year to mate and to calve. The courtship of the whales is a complex affair, the males competing for the attention of the females through displays of strength and endurance, and the pair bond that is formed during the courtship is not maintained beyond the breeding season. The whales are highly vocal during the breeding season, and the songs that the males sing upon the breeding grounds are among the most remarkable of the natural sounds of the sea, a series of low and resonant calls that may continue for hours upon the still water.

## Significance

The Greyring whale is the largest of the creatures of the cold waters of the western ocean, and its presence in the Greyring is a measure of the productivity of those waters. The whale is the object of a substantial fishery that is conducted from the ports of the western coast, and the products of the fishery are a significant component of the economy of the coastal communities. The chronicler notes that the whale endures, in the cold waters of the Greyring and in the traditions of the western coast, as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the waters that wash the shores of the western continent, and the great shape of the whale rising from the grey water is a reminder that the ocean is not empty of life, and that the largest of the creatures of the known world makes its home in the cold and the mist of the Greyring.
