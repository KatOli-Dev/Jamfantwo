## The Great Crayfish of the Inland Waters

The amber crawler is the largest crustacean of the inland waters of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), a great crayfish of amber shell and powerful claws whose range extends across the rivers and lakes of the central and northern lowlands. The crawler takes its name from the colour of its shell, which fishermen of the river valleys have likened to the resin gathered from the pine forests of the interior. The creature is a familiar feature of the diet of the riverside communities of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation). Smaller populations are found in the rivers of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and in the streams of the western coast, but the great crawlers of the Vaelic rivers are the most celebrated, and the fishery is one of the recognised pursuits of the riverside villages.

## Amber Shell and Powerful Claws

The amber crawler is a heavily built crustacean whose body is enclosed within a thick rugged shell divided into the characteristic regions of the crayfish, the cephalothorax and the abdomen. The cephalothorax bears the great claws that are the crawler's principal weapons and feeding organs; the abdomen is the muscular tail used for the sudden retreat that is its principal defence. The eyes are set upon stalks that can be folded back into grooves of the shell when at rest, and the long sensitive antennae detect the movement of a creature in the water at a considerable distance through transmitted vibrations.

The shell is the most distinctive feature and the source of the common name. It is a warm amber in colour, ranging from the pale yellow of young animals to the deep orange of the oldest males, and the surface is marked by the tubercles and ridges characteristic of the great crayfish. The shell is shed periodically as the crawler grows; the soft-shelled crawler recovering from the moult is a familiar feature of the riverside diet during the weeks following the shedding. The claws of mature males are of considerable size; a great male may have a claw as long as a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) forearm, and the grip is sufficient to break the bones of a careless fisherman.

## Rivers, Lakes, and Burrows

The amber crawler inhabits the rivers and lakes of the central and northern lowlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), with the greatest concentration in the valley of the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael) and in the lakes within the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael). The crawler prefers slow-moving water of the lower reaches, where the mud of the bottom provides cover for burrows and where the marginal vegetation is rich enough to support the smaller creatures that constitute its prey. It is not found in fast water of the upper reaches nor in the brackish water of the estuary; the populations are confined to the fresh water of the middle reaches.

The crawler excavates a burrow in the mud of the riverbank that extends back from the entrance to a chamber sufficiently large to accommodate the body of the mature animal. The entrance is typically concealed beneath the roots of a waterside tree or beneath a bank ledge, and the crawler lies within the burrow with the great claws presented to the entrance. The crawler is most active at dusk and night, when it emerges to forage upon the bottom, and returns to the burrow before dawn, when daylight would render it vulnerable to larger fish and birds that prey upon crayfish.

## Omnivore of the River Bottom

The amber crawler is an omnivore, feeding upon small creatures of the river bottom, plants that grow in the shallows, and carrion. It takes snails and insect larvae found among the stones of the shallows and is also capable of taking small fish that venture too close to the burrow. The principal method of feeding is to wait at the entrance of the burrow and seize prey that passes within reach of the great claws; the crawler also forages actively upon the bottom in the shallows during darkness.

The crawler is solitary outside the brief mating season, and mature animals hold the burrows they have excavated. Fights between males for possession of a burrow are conducted with great violence, the combatants locking claws and striving to overturn one another; the victor assumes the burrow and drives the loser to seek another site. Mating occurs in the late autumn, and the female carries the eggs beneath the tail through the winter months. The eggs hatch in the spring when the water begins to warm. The young crawlers remain with the mother for several weeks before dispersing to find their own burrows. Mortality of the young is high, for the eggs and hatchlings are preyed upon by a number of riverine creatures.

## Traps, Hauls, and Riverside Economy

The fishery of the amber crawler is a recognised pursuit of the riverside communities of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), and the catch is a significant component of the diet of the villages of the river valley. The fishery is conducted with a baited trap, a wicker basket baited with offal or river fish that is set upon the bottom near the burrows. The trap is visited in the early morning, and crawlers that have entered during the night are removed to baskets carried upon the river. The fishery is regulated by customary law: the taking of crawlers is forbidden during the season of egg-bearing, and the smallest individuals must be returned to the water.

The meat of the amber crawler is highly esteemed by the riverside communities. The boiled crawler is a feature of the diet, the meat taken from the claws and tail and served with a sauce of butter and herbs; the dish is one of the recognised delicacies of Vaelic cuisine. The shells are burned to produce lime used in the preparation of fields, and the ash is a valuable addition to the compost of kitchen gardens. The fishery is a small but valued part of the riverside economy, and the harvest is one of the events of the riverside year.

## The Crawler's Place in River Life

The amber crawler is the largest freshwater crustacean of the central lowlands, and its presence in the rivers and lakes is a measure of the health of the inland waters. It is a valued component of the diet of the riverside communities, and the fishery is one of the recognised pursuits of the villages of the valley. The creature is not held in particular reverence by the river communities, for the crawlers of the inland waters are not the subject of the superstitions that attend some riverine creatures, but they are respected for the quality of their meat and for the role they play in the diet of the riverside year. The crawler endures in the rivers and lakes of the central lowlands as a feature of the inland waters that the riverside communities have known for centuries, and the harvest is a reminder that the wealth of a country is measured not only in the grain of the field but also in the smaller provisions the waters provide.
