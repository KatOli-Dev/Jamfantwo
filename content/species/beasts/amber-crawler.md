## Overview

The amber crawler is the largest of the crustaceans of the inland waters of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), a great crayfish of amber shell and powerful claws whose range extends across the rivers and the lakes of the central and northern lowlands. The crawler takes its name from the colour of the shell, which the fishermen of the river valleys have likened to the resin that is gathered from the pine forests of the interior, and the creature is a familiar feature of the diet of the riverside communities of the [Kingdom of Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael) and the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation). The chronicler notes that the amber crawler is not confined to the inland waters of Aethelar, for smaller populations are found in the rivers of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and in the streams of the western coast, but the great crawlers of the Vaelic rivers are the most celebrated, and the fishery of the crawler is one of the recognised pursuits of the riverside villages.

## Form and Appearance

The amber crawler is a heavily built crustacean, with a body that is enclosed within a thick and rugged shell and that is divided into the characteristic regions of the crayfish, the cephalothorax and the abdomen. The cephalothorax bears the great claws that are the crawler's principal weapons and the principal organs of feeding, and the abdomen is the muscular tail that the creature uses for the sudden retreat that is its principal defence. The eyes are set upon stalks that can be folded back into the grooves of the shell when the crawler is at rest, and the antennae are long and sensitive, the crawler being able to detect the movement of a creature in the water at a considerable distance by the vibrations that are transmitted through the antennae.

The shell of the amber crawler is the creature's most distinctive feature, and it is the source of the crawler's common name. The shell is a warm amber in colour, ranging from the pale yellow of the young animals to the deep orange of the oldest males, and the surface is marked by the tubercles and the ridges that are characteristic of the great crayfish. The shell is shed periodically as the crawler grows, and the soft-shelled crawler that is recovering from the moult is a familiar feature of the riverside diet during the weeks that follow the shedding. The claws of the mature males are of considerable size, and a great male may have a claw that is as long as a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) forearm, and the grip of the claw is sufficient to break the bones of a careless fisherman.

## Habitat and Range

The amber crawler inhabits the rivers and the lakes of the central and northern lowlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), with the greatest concentration of the population in the valley of the River Vael and in the lakes that lie within the [Kingdom of Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael). The crawler prefers the slow-moving water of the lower reaches of the rivers, where the mud of the bottom provides a cover for the burrows and where the vegetation of the margins is rich enough to support the smaller creatures that constitute the crawler's prey. The crawler is not found in the fast water of the upper reaches, nor in the brackish water of the estuary, and the populations are confined to the fresh water of the middle reaches of the rivers.

The crawler excavates a burrow in the mud of the riverbank, and the burrow extends back from the entrance to a chamber that is sufficiently large to accommodate the body of the mature animal. The entrance is typically concealed beneath the roots of a waterside tree or beneath a ledge of the bank, and the crawler lies within the burrow with the great claws presented to the entrance. The crawler is most active in the dusk and the night, when it emerges from the burrow to forage upon the bottom of the river, and the crawler returns to the burrow before the dawn, when the light of the day would render it vulnerable to the larger fish and the birds that prey upon the crayfish.

## Diet and Behaviour

The amber crawler is an omnivore, feeding upon the small creatures of the river bottom, the plants that grow in the shallows, and the carrion that is found upon the bottom. The crawler takes the snails and the insect larvae that are found among the stones of the shallows, and the crawler is also capable of taking the small fish that venture too close to the burrow. The crawler's principal method of feeding is to wait at the entrance of the burrow and to seize the prey that passes within reach of the great claws, and the crawler is also capable of foraging actively upon the bottom in the shallows during the hours of darkness.

The crawler is a solitary creature outside the brief season of the mating, and the mature animals hold the burrows that they have excavated in the bank. The fights between the males for the possession of a burrow are conducted with great violence, the combatants locking claws and striving to overturn one another, and the victor of a contest assumes the burrow and drives the loser to seek another site. The mating occurs in the late autumn, and the female carries the eggs beneath the tail through the winter months, the eggs hatching in the spring when the water begins to warm. The young crawlers remain with the mother for several weeks before they disperse to find their own burrows, and the mortality of the young is high, for the eggs and the hatchlings are preyed upon by a number of the riverine creatures.

## The Fishery of the Crawler

The fishery of the amber crawler is a recognised pursuit of the riverside communities of the [Kingdom of Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael), and the catch of the crawler is a significant component of the diet of the villages of the river valley. The fishery is conducted by means of the baited trap, a wicker basket that is baited with offal or with the flesh of the river fish and that is set upon the bottom of the river in the vicinity of the burrows. The trap is visited in the early morning, and the crawlers that have entered the trap during the night are removed to the baskets that the fishermen carry upon the river. The fishery is regulated by the customary law of the riverside villages, and the taking of the crawler is forbidden during the season of the egg-bearing, and the smallest of the crawlers must be returned to the water.

The meat of the amber crawler is highly esteemed by the riverside communities, and the boiled crawler is a feature of the diet of the villages of the valley. The meat is taken from the claws and the tail, and it is served with a sauce of butter and herbs, and the dish is one of the recognised delicacies of the Vaelic cuisine. The shells of the crawlers are not wasted, for the shells are burned to produce a lime that is used in the preparation of the fields, and the ash that remains from the burning is a valuable addition to the compost of the kitchen gardens. The chronicler notes that the fishery is a small but valued part of the riverside economy, and the harvest of the crawler is one of the events of the riverside year.

## Significance

The amber crawler is the largest of the freshwater crustaceans of the central lowlands, and its presence in the rivers and the lakes is a measure of the health of the inland waters. The crawler is a valued component of the diet of the riverside communities, and the fishery of the crawler is one of the recognised pursuits of the villages of the valley. The creature is not held in any particular reverence by the communities of the river, for the crawlers of the inland waters are not the subject of the superstitions that attend some of the creatures of the rivers, but the crawlers are respected for the quality of the meat and for the role that they play in the diet of the riverside year. The chronicler notes that the crawler endures, in the rivers and the lakes of the central lowlands, as a feature of the inland waters that the riverside communities have known for centuries, and the harvest of the crawler is a reminder that the wealth of a country is measured not only in the grain of the field but also in the smaller provisions that the waters provide.
