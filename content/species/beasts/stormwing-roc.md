## Overview

The stormwing roc is the largest of the flying birds of the known world, a great eagle of the mountain ranges and the high cliffs whose wingspan is sufficient to carry a sheep or a young goat from the ground to the eyrie. The roc is found in the high mountains of all three continents, though it is most numerous in the ranges of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and in the southern highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and the bird is held in wary respect by the mountain peoples of every nation. The chronicler notes that the roc is not a creature of the open plains, for it requires the high cliffs and the strong updraughts of the mountains to support its great weight in the air, and the bird is rarely encountered outside the country of the high peaks.

## Form and Appearance

The stormwing roc is a bird of extraordinary size, with a wingspan that may reach the length of a small sailing vessel from tip to tip, and a body that is heavily muscled through the breast and the shoulders to power the great wings. The head is large and hooked, with a beak of considerable size that is used for the tearing of the prey, and the eyes are keen and far-sighted, the bird being able to detect the movement of a small creature upon the ground from a height that would render it invisible to the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) eye. The legs are short and powerful, and the talons of the feet are the principal weapons of the bird, capable of delivering a grip that is sufficient to break the back of a young goat.

The plumage of the roc varies with the subspecies and the range, but the typical colouring is a dark brown across the back and the wings, with a paler buff or grey upon the head and the underparts. The feathers of the wings are long and stiff, suited to the soaring flight that is the roc's characteristic mode of progression, and the primary feathers are shed in the late summer after the breeding season, the birds being grounded for a brief period until the new feathers grow in. The shed feathers of the roc are valued by the mountain peoples, who use them for the fletching of the heavy arrows that are employed in the hunt of the larger game, and a single primary feather is a prized possession that is often set above the hearth of the forester's lodge.

## Habitat and Range

The stormwing roc inhabits the high mountains of the three continents, with the largest populations in the ranges of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and in the southern highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and a smaller population in the western highlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The bird requires the high cliffs for the eyrie and the strong updraughts of the mountains for the soaring flight, and the roc is not found in the lowlands or in the country of the gentle hills. The eyrie is typically situated upon a ledge of a cliff that is sheltered from the prevailing wind and that commands a view of the country below, and the eyrie is built up over many years of use into a great mass of sticks and bones that may be several feet in depth.

The roc ranges widely from the eyrie in search of the prey that sustains it, and a single bird may cover many miles in a day as it circles upon the updraughts and descends upon the game that it has detected. The bird is most active in the morning and the late afternoon, when the thermals are most reliable and the prey is moving in the open, and the roc rests during the middle of the day upon a sheltered ledge or upon the eyrie itself. The roc is a solitary bird outside the breeding season, and the pairs that come together for the nesting are bonded for the duration of the season, the male and the female sharing the duties of the incubation and the rearing of the young.

## Diet and Hunting

The stormwing roc is a predator of considerable power, and the bird takes a wide range of prey according to the opportunity. The principal prey of the roc is the young of the mountain goats and the deer, and a pair of rocs with a nest of young will take several kids or fawns in the course of a week. The roc also takes the smaller mammals of the high country, including the marmots and the hares, and the bird is known to take the lambs of the highland pastures when the flocks are grazing in the open. The roc does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), though there are records of attacks upon children who have wandered from the protection of the village, and the mountain communities take care to keep the young within sight of the elders when the rocs are nesting.

The hunting technique of the roc is to soar at a great height upon the updraughts and to scan the country below for the movement of the prey. The bird descends upon the prey with a stoop that may attain a speed that is astonishing for a creature of its size, and the prey is seized in the talons of the great feet and carried to a sheltered spot to be consumed. The roc is capable of carrying a load of considerable weight, and a large female has been known to carry a young goat for several miles to the eyrie. The bird will also scavenge upon the carcasses that are found upon the high country, and the roc is a frequent visitor to the kills that the snow cat has made upon the alpine meadows.

## Reproduction and Life Cycle

The stormwing roc nests once a year, in the early spring, and the nest is the eyrie that the pair has occupied for many seasons. The female lays a single egg, rarely two, and the egg is incubated by both birds over a period of several weeks. The chick is cared for by both parents, and the chick remains in the eyrie for several months before it is capable of flight. The young roc is at risk from a number of predators during the period when it is confined to the eyrie, and the parents are vigilant in the defence of the nest, driving off any creature that approaches too closely.

The mortality of the young is high, and only a fraction of the chicks that hatch in any year survive to maturity. The young roc that survives to flight is cared for by the parents for a further period of several months, the parents teaching the young bird the techniques of the hunt and the soaring flight, and the young bird disperses from the parental range in the autumn of its first year. The roc is a long-lived bird for a creature of its size, and a bird that survives the hazards of the early years may live for several decades, the oldest birds holding the same eyrie for many seasons and being known to the mountain communities by the marks upon the plumage and the peculiarities of the nesting.

## The Mountain Peoples and the Roc

The mountain peoples of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) regard the stormwing roc with a mixture of respect and wary caution, and the creature occupies a prominent place in the lore of the high country. The roc is the symbol of the strength and the freedom of the mountain winds, and the warriors of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) take the feathers of the roc as a token of their prowess in the hunt. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the mountain holds do not hunt the roc, for the bird does not ordinarily descend to the country of the dwarven halls, but the dwarves respect the bird as a creature of the upper world and the highest of the peaks that they claim.

The hunting of the stormwing roc is not a customary pursuit of the mountain communities, for the bird is too formidable a quarry for any but the most determined of hunters, and the practical value of the kill is limited to the feathers and the talons that may be recovered. The young roc is taken from the eyrie on rare occasions by the trappers of the highland valleys, and the bird is trained to the fist by the falconers of the mountain courts, though the training of a roc is a pursuit that requires considerable skill and patience, and a trained roc is a possession that is known only to the wealthiest of the highland lords.

## Significance

The stormwing roc is the foremost of the flying birds of the high mountains, and its presence in the country of the peaks is a measure of the wildness that endures in the upper ranges. The bird is the predator that keeps the populations of the mountain goats and the deer in balance with the carrying capacity of the high meadows, and the loss of the roc from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the pasture that the chroniclers have noted in the records of the mountain communities. The roc is a creature of the open air and the strong winds, and its circling upon the thermals is a sight that the mountain travellers regard as one of the great spectacles of the high country. The chronicler notes that the bird endures, in the high places of the three continents, as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the world of the mountains, and the shadow of its wings upon the snow is a reminder that the upper country is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the valleys below.
