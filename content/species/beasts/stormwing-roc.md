## The Largest Flying Bird

The stormwing roc is the largest of the flying birds of the known world, a great eagle of the mountain ranges and high cliffs whose wingspan is sufficient to carry a sheep or young goat from the ground to the eyrie. The roc is found in the high mountains of all three continents, though it is most numerous in the ranges of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and in the southern highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar); the bird is held in wary respect by the mountain peoples of every nation. The roc is not a creature of the open plains, for it requires the high cliffs and strong updraughts of the mountains to support its great weight in the air, and it is rarely encountered outside the country of the high peaks.

## Great Wings and Keen Sight

The stormwing roc is a bird of extraordinary size, with a wingspan that may reach the length of a small sailing vessel from tip to tip, and a body heavily muscled through the breast and shoulders to power the great wings. The head is large and hooked, with a beak of considerable size used for the tearing of prey; the eyes are keen and far-sighted, the bird able to detect the movement of a small creature upon the ground from a height that would render it invisible to the [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) eye. The legs are short and powerful; the talons of the feet are the principal weapons, capable of delivering a grip sufficient to break the back of a young goat.

The plumage varies with subspecies and range, but the typical colouring is dark brown across the back and wings with a paler buff or grey upon the head and underparts. The feathers of the wings are long and stiff, suited to the soaring flight that is the characteristic mode of progression; the primary feathers are shed in late summer after the breeding season, the birds being grounded for a brief period until new feathers grow in. The shed feathers are valued by mountain peoples, who use them for fletching heavy arrows employed in the hunt of larger game; a single primary feather is a prized possession often set above the hearth of a forester's lodge.

## High Cliffs and Mountain Updraughts

The stormwing roc inhabits the high mountains of the three continents, with the largest populations in the ranges of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and in the southern highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and a smaller population in the western highlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The bird requires high cliffs for the eyrie and strong updraughts for soaring flight; it is not found in the lowlands or in the country of gentle hills. The eyrie is typically situated upon a sheltered ledge of a cliff commanding a view of the country below; it is built up over many years of use into a great mass of sticks and bones that may be several feet in depth.

The roc ranges widely from the eyrie in search of prey; a single bird may cover many miles in a day as it circles upon updraughts and descends upon game it has detected. The bird is most active in the morning and late afternoon, when thermals are most reliable and prey is moving in the open; it rests during the middle of the day upon a sheltered ledge or the eyrie itself. The roc is solitary outside the breeding season; pairs that come together for nesting are bonded for the duration of the season, the male and female sharing the duties of incubation and rearing of the young.

## Soaring and Stooping for Prey

The stormwing roc is a predator of considerable power that takes a wide range of prey according to opportunity. The principal prey is the young of mountain goats and deer; a pair of rocs with a nest of young will take several kids or fawns in the course of a week. The roc also takes smaller mammals of the high country, including marmots and hares, and is known to take lambs of highland pastures when flocks are grazing in the open. The roc does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), though there are records of attacks upon children who have wandered from the protection of the village; mountain communities take care to keep the young within sight of elders when the rocs are nesting.

The hunting technique is to soar at great height upon updraughts and scan the country below for movement of prey. The bird descends upon the prey with a stoop that may attain astonishing speed for a creature of its size; the prey is seized in the talons of the great feet and carried to a sheltered spot to be consumed. The roc is capable of carrying a load of considerable weight; a large female has been known to carry a young goat for several miles to the eyrie. The bird also scavenges upon carcasses found upon the high country and is a frequent visitor to kills the snow cat has made upon the alpine meadows.

## Eyries, Eggs, and Fledging

The stormwing roc nests once a year, in early spring, in the eyrie the pair has occupied for many seasons. The female lays a single egg, rarely two; the egg is incubated by both birds over a period of several weeks. The chick is cared for by both parents and remains in the eyrie for several months before it is capable of flight. The young roc is at risk from a number of predators during the period it is confined to the eyrie; the parents are vigilant in defence of the nest, driving off any creature that approaches too closely.

Mortality among the young is high; only a fraction of chicks that hatch in any year survive to maturity. The young roc that survives to flight is cared for by the parents for a further period of several months, the parents teaching the young bird the techniques of the hunt and soaring flight; the young bird disperses from the parental range in the autumn of its first year. The roc is a long-lived bird for a creature of its size; one that survives the hazards of early years may live for several decades, the oldest holding the same eyrie for many seasons and being known to mountain communities by marks upon the plumage and peculiarities of nesting.

## Feathers, Training, and Wariness

The mountain peoples of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) regard the stormwing roc with a mixture of respect and wary caution; the creature occupies a prominent place in the lore of the high country. It is the symbol of the strength and freedom of the mountain winds; warriors of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) take the feathers as a token of their prowess in the hunt. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the mountain holds do not hunt the roc, for the bird does not ordinarily descend to the country of the dwarven halls, but they respect the bird as a creature of the upper world and the highest of the peaks.

The hunting of the stormwing roc is not a customary pursuit of the mountain communities, for the bird is too formidable a quarry for any but the most determined of hunters, and the practical value of the kill is limited to the feathers and talons that may be recovered. The young roc is taken from the eyrie on rare occasions by trappers of the highland valleys and trained to the fist by falconers of the mountain courts. The training of a roc requires considerable skill and patience; a trained roc is a possession known only to the wealthiest of the highland lords.

## The Great Eagle of the Peaks

The stormwing roc is the foremost of the flying birds of the high mountains; its presence in the country of the peaks is a measure of the wildness that endures in the upper ranges. It is the predator that keeps the populations of mountain goats and deer in balance with the carrying capacity of the high meadows; the loss of the roc from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the pasture. The roc is a creature of the open air and the strong winds; its circling upon the thermals is a sight that travellers regard as one of the great spectacles of the high country. The bird endures in the high places of the three continents as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the world of the mountains; the shadow of its wings upon the snow is a reminder that the upper country is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the valleys below.
