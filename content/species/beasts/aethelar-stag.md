## Overview

The [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) stag is the largest of the deer of the known world and the most celebrated of the great herbivores of the temperate forests of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). A mature bull stands taller at the shoulder than any of the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) who hunt him, and his antlers, when fully developed, span a width sufficient to turn aside any but the stoutest spear. The stag is not found outside the ancient woodlands of the central continent, and his range has retreated with the cutting of the forest and the spread of agriculture, so that what was once the most numerous of the great deer is now a creature of the deeper woods and the protected hunting grounds of the highland valleys. The chronicler notes that the stag is accounted the noblest of the beasts of the chase throughout the kingdoms of Aethelar, and the hunt of the stag is a ceremonial pursuit of the nobility of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth).

## Form and Appearance

The Aethelar stag carries the conformation of the deep-wood deer in its most pronounced form. His body is long and deep through the chest, set on legs that are both sturdy and remarkably swift, and the musculature of his shoulders and hindquarters is the admiration of the foresters who watch him from the concealment of the woodland. The summer coat is a warm reddish brown, shading to a paler buff along the flanks and the underside, and the winter coat thickens to a dense grey-brown that stands him in good stead against the cold of the highland forests. The rump patch, which is characteristic of all the great deer of the temperate woodlands, is a pale cream bordered by a darker line, and it is conspicuous when the stag flees through the underbrush at the onset of the hunt.

The antlers of the bull are the creature's most distinguished feature. They rise from heavy burrs in a broad forward sweep and then curve upward and inward in a series of tines, the typical mature bull carrying ten or more points and a crown of short tines above the brow. New antlers are grown each year and shed in the late winter, and the velvet that covers them during the growing season is the object of a small trade among the foresters, who value it for several medicinal preparations. The antlers of an old bull, polished by many seasons of forest rub, are pale and ridged, and a pair recovered from a stag that has died of old age is set above the hearths of the forest lodges as a mark of the household's standing among the hunters.

## Habitat and Range

The stag inhabits the mature deciduous and mixed forests of central and northern Aethelar, with his greatest concentration in the deep woodlands that border the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) and the river valleys of the interior. He prefers the forest edge and the clearings where the new grass springs up after the burnings of the foresters, and he ranges widely through the woodlands in the spring and the summer in search of the best grazing. In the autumn the mature bulls hold small territories in the deeper wood, and they are difficult to find, for they lie up in the thickets during the day and move to the water only at dusk. The does and the young remain in larger herds through the autumn, and the herd gathers on the ridge tops where the wind keeps the flies at bay.

The historical range of the stag once extended across the whole of the temperate forest of Aethelar, from the foothills of the mountain spine in the west to the coastal woodlands of the east, but the clearing of the land for the plough has reduced his range to the great forests that remain, principally in the northern valleys and along the slopes of the highland interior. The noble houses of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) maintain large hunting forests in which the stag is protected and hunted according to strict seasons and quotas, and these forests are the last stronghold of the great deer in the heart of the continent. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the deep forest tolerate the stag as a fellow inhabitant of the wood, and they do not hunt him except in time of famine, for their traditions hold that the stag is the elder of the woodland and that his death is to be balanced by solemn rite.

## Diet and Behaviour

The stag is a grazer and a browser, feeding upon the grasses and the herbs of the woodland floor in the spring and the summer, and upon the bark, the buds, and the fallen mast of the trees in the autumn and the winter. He is fond of the acorns and the beechnuts where these are to be found, and the autumn feeding upon the mast is the period when the bull lays down the fat that carries him through the lean weeks of late winter. The does nurse their fawns upon the rich milk of the early summer, and the fawns are weaned by the autumn and are capable of independent foraging before the first snow. The stag is a creature of the early morning and the late afternoon, and he rests during the heat of the day in the cool of the deeper wood, a habit that the hunter uses to his advantage when the wind is right and the forest is still.

The social order of the stag is most visible in the rutting season of the autumn, when the mature bulls gather the herds of does into their territories and challenge one another for the possession of the choicest grounds. The roar of the bull at the height of the rut is one of the great sounds of the woodland, a deep and resonant call that carries for miles through the still air of the autumn forest, and the fights between the bulls are conducted with a deliberation and a gravity that the chroniclers of the hunt have likened to a tournament. The victor of a contest assumes the herd and drives the younger bulls to the periphery, where they wait in restless bands for the coming of the next season.

## The Hunt

The hunt of the Aethelar stag is a ceremonial pursuit of the nobility of the central kingdoms, and the rules by which it is conducted are among the most elaborate of the hunting codes of the known world. The king of Vael maintains a court of hunt-masters whose duty is the management of the royal forests and the organisation of the seasonal hunt, and the hunters who are admitted to the royal chase are bound by strict conventions of conduct and dress. The quarry is the mature bull of not less than ten tines, and the young bulls, the does, and the fawns are protected by the laws of the chase and may not be taken except by commoners for the pot.

The methods of the hunt vary with the season and the terrain. In the early autumn the stalk is the customary approach, the hunter and his beaters moving quietly through the wood to find the bull lying up in his thicket, and the shot is taken at close range with the heavy bow that is the traditional weapon of the chase. In the later autumn, when the leaves have fallen and the rut is at its height, the hunter may call the bull by imitating his roar, and the duel of sound between the man and the beast is one of the most celebrated traditions of the noble hunt. The kill is followed by the ritual of the breaking of the quarry, in which the hunter cuts the throat of the fallen stag according to the ancient forms, and the meat is distributed to the company and to the foresters whose ground has been honoured by the chase.

## Relationship with Other Species

The stag shares his range with the great predators of the forest in numbers that vary with the region and the season. The grey wolf of the central woodlands will take a fawn or a sick adult when the opportunity offers, but the mature bull is too formidable a quarry for any but the largest pack, and the wolves of the forest are not numerous enough to threaten the population of the stag as a whole. The lynx of the highland forests takes the fawns in the spring, and the bear of the deep wood, where the bear is found, will contest the carcass of a stag that has fallen to the hunter's arrow, though the bear does not ordinarily hunt the healthy stag. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) tolerate the stag as a fellow dweller of the wood and do not hunt him except in the strictest necessity, and the elves of the central forest are known to drive the stag from the deeper wood at the time of the autumn cull, lest the hunters of the human kingdoms take more than the laws of the chase permit.

## Significance

The Aethelar stag is the symbol of the temperate forest and the noble hunt of the central continent, and his antlers adorn the halls of the kings and the great lords of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The creature's decline with the cutting of the forest is a quiet measure of the cost of the expansion of the ploughland, and the great hunting forests that remain are a testament to the value that the noble houses of the central kingdoms continue to set upon the chase and the ancient rites that attend it. The chronicler notes that the stag endures, in the protected forests and in the traditions of the hunt, as a living memory of the deep wood that once covered the continent, and his presence in the landscape is a reminder that the wealth of a kingdom is measured not only in the grain of the valley but also in the wildness that the kingdom has had the wisdom to preserve.
