## The Greatest of Reptiles

The dragon is the greatest of the reptiles of the known world and the foremost of the creatures that the peoples of the three continents have set apart from the ordinary beasts of the field. A mature dragon is a creature of such size and power that an encounter with one is recorded in the annals of the kingdoms as a singular event; chroniclers of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and the courts of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) have each preserved accounts of meetings between dragons and the kings of their lands. The dragon is not numerous. Naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) have estimated that fewer than a hundred dragons of mature age survive in all the world. The creature is found only in the most remote and inaccessible high places: the mountain ranges of the interior and the cliffs of distant coasts where the foot of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) does not ordinarily fall.

## Scales, Wings, and Fiery Breath

The dragon is a great reptilian beast, four-legged and winged, with a body covered in a thick hide of overlapping scales and a head set upon a long muscular neck. Measurements taken from the few carcasses recovered by naturalists suggest that a great male may measure forty feet from the tip of the snout to the root of the tail; the wingspan is greater than the length of the body. The scales are of considerable thickness; a breastplate of dragon scale is a possession dwarven smiths have fashioned for the kings of the highland realms, though the cost is reckoned in the labour of years and in the favour of princes.

The colour varies with the individual and range, but the predominant colours are deep red, black of considerable sheen, or pale silver-grey that mountain peoples have likened to the colour of high snow. The head bears a pair of horns that curve backward over the skull; the jaws are armed with teeth of considerable length that are renewed throughout the animal's life. The eyes are the most remarkable feature, for they are of a colour not found in any other creature: deep amber or pale gold. The breath of the dragon is the subject of its most celebrated attribute; accounts preserved by the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) and dwarven chroniclers describe a blast of fire sufficient to consume a barn or strip the flesh from the bones of a horse.

## High Mountains and Remote Lairs

The dragon inhabits the highest mountains of the three continents, with the largest populations in the interior ranges of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the southern highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and a smaller population in the western highlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) found in the cliffs above the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). The dragon requires high cliffs for the lair and wide valleys for the hunt; it is not found in the lowlands or in the country of gentle hills. The lair is typically situated in a cave that opens upon a sheltered ledge commanding a view of the country below; the cave is the residence for the greater part of the dragon's life, ranging out to hunt and returning to the same cave season after season.

A mature male holds a territory of considerable extent, ranging many miles from the lair in search of prey. The territory is marked by scratches of great claws upon the rocks and by scent left upon prominent outcrops; territories of mature dragons do not ordinarily overlap, the dragons spacing themselves across the high country at intervals estimated by naturalists to be many tens of miles. Encounters between two mature dragons are consequently rare; the few such meetings recorded have been conducted with displays of size and fire that ended, in accounts that have been preserved, in the withdrawal of one animal.

## Predator of the High Country

The dragon is a predator of considerable power that takes a wide range of prey according to opportunity. The principal prey is the larger mammals of the high country, including mountain goats and deer; a mature dragon will take several such animals in the course of a week. It also takes cattle of highland pastures when they are grazing in the open; shepherds of the mountain valleys must keep their herds within the protection of dogs and watchers, for a dragon that has found a herd will take several beasts in a single night. The dragon does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but accounts of the mountain communities include incidents in which a dragon has taken a shepherd or traveller who unwarily approached the lair.

The dragon is a creature of considerable intelligence. Accounts of the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the mountain holds include descriptions of dragons that have answered the speech of dwarven chroniclers with sounds interpreted as a form of language. The dragon is not social; encounters outside the brief mating season are conducted with wariness. Mating is rare; the female lays a small number of eggs in a chamber excavated in the deepest part of the lair. The young remain with the mother for several years before dispersing to find their own territories. Mortality among the young is very high, and only a fraction of dragons that hatch in any century survive to maturity.

## Legend and Captivity

The dragon occupies a singular place in the traditions of the three continents and is the subject of a body of legend without parallel among the beasts of the world. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the deep forests hold that the dragon is the eldest of the reptiles, old when the first elven kind set foot upon the world; they regard the dragon with a wary respect not accorded to any other beast. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the mountain holds do not hunt the dragon; their traditions hold that the dragon is the lord of the upper world and the dwarves the lords of the lower, and they seal the upper passages of their halls against intrusion.

The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the lowland kingdoms regard the dragon with a mixture of awe and fear; the creature is the subject of a substantial body of romance and cautionary tale. The kings of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) have at times maintained dragons in captivity, held in great enclosures built for the purpose; chroniclers of the royal courts have preserved accounts of dragons brought to the courts in the early years of their lives and tended by keepers of the royal menageries. The custom has fallen out of practice in the present age; the cost and danger are considerable, and the last of the royal dragons died in the reign of the seventh king of the Vaelic line.

## The Wildness of the Upper World

The dragon is the greatest of the reptiles of the world, and its presence in the high mountains of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the upper ranges. It is the predator that keeps the populations of larger mammals of the high country in balance with the carrying capacity of the alpine meadows; the loss of the dragon from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the pasture. The dragon endures in the high places of the three continents and in the legends of every people that shares the world with the creature as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the peaks. The shadow of the dragon upon the snow is a reminder that the upper country is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the valleys below.
