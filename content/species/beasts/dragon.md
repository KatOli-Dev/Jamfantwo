## Overview

The dragon is the greatest of the reptiles of the known world and the foremost of the creatures that the chroniclers of the three continents have set apart from the ordinary beasts of the field. A mature dragon is a creature of such size and power that the encounter with one is recorded in the annals of the kingdoms as a singular event, and the chroniclers of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) and the courts of [Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael) and [Aldreth](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-aldreth) have each preserved accounts of meetings between dragons and the kings of their lands. The dragon is not numerous. The naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) have estimated that fewer than a hundred dragons of mature age survive in all the world, and the creature is found only in the most remote and the most inaccessible of the high places, the mountain ranges of the interior and the cliffs of the distant coasts where the foot of [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) does not ordinarily fall.

## Form and Appearance

The dragon is a great reptilian beast, four-legged and winged, with a body that is covered in a thick hide of overlapping scales and a head that is set upon a long and muscular neck. The size of a mature dragon is the subject of considerable legend, but the measurements that have been taken from the few carcasses that have been recovered by the naturalists suggest that a great male may measure some forty feet from the tip of the snout to the root of the tail, and the wingspan of the same animal is greater than the length of the body. The scales of the dragon are of considerable thickness, and a breastplate of dragon scale is a possession that the dwarven smiths are known to have fashioned for the kings of the highland realms, though the cost of such a piece is reckoned in the labour of years and in the favour of princes.

The colour of the dragon varies with the individual and the range, but the predominant colours are a deep red, a black of considerable sheen, or a pale silver-grey that the mountain peoples have likened to the colour of the high snow. The head of the dragon is set with a pair of horns that curve backward over the skull, and the jaws are armed with teeth of considerable length that are renewed throughout the life of the animal. The eyes are the most remarkable feature of the dragon, for they are of a colour that is not found in any other creature of the world, a deep amber or a pale gold, and the chroniclers agree that the gaze of the dragon is a thing of singular intensity. The breath of the dragon is the subject of the most celebrated of the dragon's attributes, and the accounts that have been preserved by the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) and the dwarven chroniclers describe a blast of fire that is sufficient to consume a barn or to strip the flesh from the bones of a horse.

## Habitat and Range

The dragon inhabits the highest mountains of the three continents, with the largest populations in the interior ranges of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and in the southern highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and a smaller population in the western highlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) that is found in the cliffs above the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). The dragon requires the high cliffs for the lair and the wide valleys for the hunt, and the creature is not found in the lowlands or in the country of the gentle hills. The lair of the dragon is typically situated in a cave that opens upon a ledge of a cliff that is sheltered from the prevailing wind and that commands a view of the country below, and the cave is the residence of the dragon for the greater part of its life, the dragon ranging out from the lair to hunt and returning to the same cave season after season.

The dragon holds a territory of considerable extent, and a mature male will range for many miles from the lair in search of the prey that sustains him. The territory is marked by the scratches of the great claws upon the rocks and by the scent that the dragon leaves upon the prominent outcrops, and the territories of the mature dragons do not ordinarily overlap, the dragons spacing themselves across the high country at intervals that the naturalists of the academies have estimated to be many tens of miles. The encounter of two mature dragons is consequently a rare event, and the chroniclers have recorded only a few such meetings, the meetings being conducted with displays of size and of fire that ended, in the accounts that have been preserved, in the withdrawal of one of the animals.

## Diet and Behaviour

The dragon is a predator of considerable power, and the creature takes a wide range of prey according to the opportunity. The principal prey of the dragon is the larger mammals of the high country, including the mountain goats and the deer, and a mature dragon will take several such animals in the course of a week. The dragon will also take the cattle of the highland pastures when the cattle are grazing in the open, and the shepherds of the mountain valleys must keep their herds within the protection of the dogs and the watchers, for a dragon that has found a herd will take several beasts in the course of a single night. The dragon does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but the accounts of the mountain communities include a number of incidents in which a dragon has taken a shepherd or a traveller who has unwarily approached the lair.

The dragon is a creature of considerable intelligence, and the accounts of the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the mountain holds include descriptions of dragons that have answered the speech of the dwarven chroniclers with sounds that the chroniclers have interpreted as a form of language. The dragon is not a social animal, and the encounter of two dragons outside the brief season of the mating is conducted with the wariness of two creatures that recognise the danger that the other represents. The mating is a rare event, and the female lays a small number of eggs in a chamber that is excavated in the deepest part of the lair, and the young dragons remain with the mother for several years before they disperse to find their own territories. The mortality of the young is very high, and only a fraction of the dragons that hatch in any century survive to maturity.

## The Dragon in the Traditions of the Realms

The dragon occupies a singular place in the traditions of the realms of the three continents, and the creature is the subject of a body of legend that is without parallel among the beasts of the world. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the deep forests hold that the dragon is the eldest of the reptiles and that the creature was old when the first of the elven kind set foot upon the world, and the elves regard the dragon with a wary respect that is not accorded to any other beast. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the mountain holds do not hunt the dragon, for the dwarven traditions hold that the dragon is the lord of the upper world and that the dwarves are the lords of the lower, and the dwarves take care to seal the upper passages of their halls against the intrusion of the creature.

The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the kingdoms of the lowlands regard the dragon with a mixture of awe and fear, and the creature is the subject of a substantial body of romance and of cautionary tale. The kings of [Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael) and of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-aldreth) have at times maintained dragons in captivity, the creatures being held in great enclosures that have been built for the purpose, and the chroniclers of the royal courts have preserved accounts of dragons that have been brought to the courts in the early years of their lives and that have been tended by the keepers of the royal menageries. The custom has fallen out of practice in the present age, for the cost of the keeping of a dragon is considerable and the danger is great, and the last of the royal dragons is recorded to have died in the reign of the seventh king of the Vaelic line.

## Significance

The dragon is the greatest of the reptiles of the world, and the presence of the creature in the high mountains of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the upper ranges. The dragon is the predator that keeps the populations of the larger mammals of the high country in balance with the carrying capacity of the alpine meadows, and the loss of the dragon from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the pasture that the chroniclers have noted in the records of the mountain communities. The chronicler notes that the dragon endures, in the high places of the three continents and in the legends of every people that shares the world with the creature, as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the peaks, and the shadow of the dragon upon the snow is a reminder that the upper country is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the valleys below.
