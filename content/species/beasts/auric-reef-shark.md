## Overview

The Auric reef shark is the most numerous of the predatory sharks of the warm waters of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), a sleek and powerful fish whose range extends across the reef country of the eastern Aethelaran coast and the waters of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden). The shark takes its name from the colour of the warm waters that it inhabits, the golden cast of the Auric giving the shark's back a metallic sheen that the fishermen of the coastal towns have likened to the metal of the coins that are struck in the eastern mints. The chronicler notes that the reef shark is not a creature of the open ocean, for it prefers the broken country of the reefs and the straits, and it is rarely encountered by the vessels that ply the deep waters of the Auric.

## Form and Appearance

The Auric reef shark is a fish of moderate size, with a body that may attain a length of twice the height of a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) of average stature, though the great majority of the sharks that are encountered by the fishermen are of more modest size. The body is streamlined and fusiform, adapted to the rapid and the agile motion that is the shark's principal mode of progression, and the tail is a powerful and asymmetrical fin that provides the thrust that drives the fish through the water. The skin is covered in the small dermal denticles that are characteristic of the sharks, and the colour is a metallic grey-bronze upon the back and the flanks, with a paler underside that renders the shark less visible from below.

The head of the reef shark is blunt and powerful, with a jaw that is set with several rows of sharp and serrated teeth that are constantly being replaced as the outer teeth are worn or broken. The eyes are small and black, and the shark is reputed to be able to detect the movement of a fish at a considerable distance in the clear water of the reef. The nostrils are set upon the underside of the snout and are sensitive to the scent of the blood that may be carried in the water from a wounded creature. The shark possesses the ampullae of Lorenzini that are characteristic of the sharks, a network of sensory organs that detect the electrical fields of the prey, and the shark is able to detect a creature that is hidden beneath the sand or in the crevices of the reef.

## Habitat and Range

The Auric reef shark inhabits the warm waters of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), with the greatest concentration of the population in the vicinity of the reefs and the submerged ridges of the eastern coast. The shark prefers the broken country of the reef, where the prey is concentrated by the currents and where the shark can find the cover of the rocks and the weed for the ambush. The shark is not found in the colder waters of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea), and it is rarely encountered outside the strait and the adjacent coast.

The shark is most active in the warm months of the late spring and the summer, when the waters of the Auric are at their warmest and the prey is most abundant. The shark ranges widely through the reef country in the course of the day, the fish moving with the currents and the tides in search of the prey, and the shark returns to the deeper water beyond the reefs at night to rest upon the bottom. The shark is a solitary fish outside the brief season of the mating, and the mature animals hold ranges that they mark by the scent that they leave upon the prominent rocks and by the bites that they inflict upon the reef.

## Diet and Hunting

The reef shark is an opportunistic predator, feeding upon the fish of the reef, the squid and the octopus that haunt the rocky bottom, and the crustaceans that are found among the crevices. The shark hunts by a combination of stealth and speed, the fish approaching the prey from the cover of the reef and then accelerating with a sudden burst that takes the prey before it can escape. The shark is also a scavenger, and the carcasses of the larger animals that have died upon the reef are visited by the shark, who will drive off the smaller scavengers and consume the flesh.

The shark does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), though the fishermen of the coastal towns report occasional encounters with sharks that have been provoked or that have been attracted by the blood of the catch. The pearl divers of the strait are the most exposed of the maritime communities to the danger of the shark, and the traditions of the diving guilds include a number of practices that are designed to reduce the risk. The divers are careful to work in pairs, and they refrain from diving when the water is warm and the sharks are unusually active, and the guilds maintain a careful record of the sightings of the sharks in the vicinity of the pearl beds.

## Reproduction

The reproduction of the reef shark is a matter that the naturalists of the strait have studied with care, for the shark is a creature of interest to the fishermen and the gathering of the eggs is a customary practice of certain of the coastal communities. The female gives birth to a small number of well-developed young, typically two or three, the eggs being retained within the body of the female until they hatch, and the young sharks are capable of independent life from the moment of the birth. The young sharks remain in the shallows of the reef for the first months of their lives, where they are less exposed to the larger predators of the open water, and they disperse to the deeper water of the reef as they grow.

The mortality of the young is high, for the juvenile sharks are preyed upon by the larger fish of the reef, and only a small fraction of the brood survives to maturity. The sharks that survive to adulthood are long-lived, and a reef shark that escapes the hazards of the fishery and the predation of the larger sharks may live for several decades. The sharks reach maturity at an age of several years, and the mature animals return to the same reef year after year to breed, the females gathering in the shallows to give birth and the males patrolling the deeper water in search of the receptive females.

## The Coastal Communities and the Shark

The communities of the strait have a long acquaintance with the reef shark, and the lore of the fishermen includes a substantial body of knowledge of the creature's habits and its moods. The fishermen read the signs of the shark's presence in the behaviour of the other fish, and they know that a sudden cessation of the feeding of the smaller fish, or a scattering of the birds from a particular spot, may indicate a shark in the vicinity. The fishing boats are careful to discard the offal from the catch at a distance from the nets, and the crews carry sharp hooks with which to fend off a shark that approaches too closely.

The shark is not held in any particular reverence by the coastal communities, for the strait peoples are a practical folk and the shark is regarded as a hazard to be managed rather than a spirit to be propitiated. The shark is, however, respected for its power and its speed, and the fishermen of the coastal towns regard a large reef shark with the same wary respect that the mountain peoples accord to the snow cat or the thunder boar. The shark is occasionally taken by the fishermen for the oil that is rendered from the liver, and the oil is used in the lamps of the coastal towns and in the preparation of the caulking that is applied to the hulls of the fishing boats.

## Significance

The Auric reef shark is the foremost of the predatory sharks of the warm waters of the eastern coast, and its presence in the reef country is a measure of the health of the marine ecosystem. The shark is the predator that keeps the populations of the reef fish in balance with the productivity of the waters, and the loss of the shark from any district would be followed by an overpopulation of the prey species that would in time upset the order of the reef. The shark is not held in particular reverence by the coastal communities, but it is respected as a creature of the waters, and the customary practices that have grown up around the management of the shark are among the most practical of the maritime traditions of the strait. The chronicler notes that the shark endures, as it has endured for centuries, in the warm waters of the strait and the neighbouring reaches of the Auric, and the fishermen of the coastal villages continue to read the signs of its presence and to give it the wide berth that its power and its speed demand.
