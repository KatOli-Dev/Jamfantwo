## Overview

The owlbear is one of the great monstrosities of the deep forests of the known world, a creature of considerable size and of singular ferocity whose range extends across the wooded interior of the three continents. The owlbear takes its name from the combination of the great horned head of the owl and the heavy body of the bear that the creature displays in its conformation, and the animal is held by the naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) to be one of the most dangerous of the predators of the deep wood. The chronicler notes that the owlbear is not a creature of the open country or the settled lands, for the animal requires the cover of the mature forest and the shelter of the densest thickets, and the owlbear is found in greatest numbers in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and in the [Brackenshield Wood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/brackenshield-wood) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind).

## Form and Appearance

The owlbear is a creature of considerable size and of remarkable heaviness of build, with a body that is set upon four powerful legs and a head that is broad and feathered in the manner of the great horned owl. The body of the owlbear is covered in a thick shaggy coat of dark brown that shades to a blackish hue upon the back and the shoulders, and the coat is dense enough to shed the rain of the wetter forests and to provide a measure of protection against the bites of the lesser predators. The forelegs of the owlbear are armed with great claws of considerable length, and the claws are the owlbear's principal weapons, capable of delivering a blow that is sufficient to break the back of a deer or to strip the bark from a mature tree with a single stroke. The head of the owlbear is the creature's most singular feature, for the head is feathered in the manner of the owl and bears a pair of great horned tufts above the ears, and the eyes are large and of a pale yellow that the foresters have likened to the colour of the harvest moon.

The beak of the owlbear is hooked and sharp, and the beak is used for the tearing of the prey in the manner of the birds of prey, though the owlbear is not a swift flier and the wings that are concealed beneath the heavy coat are too small to lift the body of the creature from the ground. The wings are used in the manner of the display of the great owls, the owlbear spreading the wings to its full extent when the creature is threatened or when the male is displaying to the female in the season of the mating. The size of the owlbear varies with the subspecies and the range, but a mature male may stand as high as a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) at the shoulder and may weigh several times as much, and the footprint of the great male is a familiar feature of the game trails of the deep wood.

## Habitat and Range

The owlbear inhabits the mature forests of the three continents, with the largest populations in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the [Brackenshield Wood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/brackenshield-wood) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and a smaller population in the [Ashen Heath](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/ashen-heath) of the eastern continent. The owlbear requires the cover of the mature forest and the shelter of the densest thickets, and the creature is not found in the open country or in the settled lands. The lair of the owlbear is typically situated in a hollow beneath the roots of a great tree or in a natural cave of the broken country, and the lair is marked at the entrance by the great claw-marks that the creature has scored upon the bark of the surrounding trees.

The owlbear is a solitary creature, and the mature animals hold territories that they mark by the scent that they leave upon the prominent trees and by the trails that they wear through the underbrush of the forest. The territory of a mature owlbear may extend for several miles through the deep wood, and the creature ranges widely in search of the prey that sustains it. The owlbear is most active in the hours of dusk and of dawn, when the creature emerges from the lair to hunt upon the game trails that wind through the forest, and the owlbear returns to the lair before the full light of the day, when the heat of the sun would render the creature less effective in the hunt.

## Diet and Behaviour

The owlbear is a predator of considerable power and of singular ferocity, and the creature feeds upon a wide range of prey according to the opportunity. The principal prey of the owlbear is the deer of the deep forest, and a mature owlbear will take a yearling deer with a sudden rush from the cover of the underbrush. The owlbear is also a predator of the smaller mammals of the forest, including the hares and the squirrels, and the creature will take the wild boar of the forest margins when the opportunity offers. The owlbear does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but the accounts of the forest communities include a number of incidents in which an owlbear has killed a forester who has unwarily approached the lair, and the foresters take care to mark the lairs of the owl bears and to give them a wide berth.

The hunting technique of the owlbear is to ambush the prey from the cover of the underbrush and to seize the prey with a sudden rush, the great claws of the forepaw delivering a blow that is sufficient to disable the prey at the first contact. The owlbear is not a swift runner, and the creature is not capable of the long pursuit of a fast-moving prey, but the owlbear is capable of a sudden and explosive rush over a short distance that is sufficient to overtake any but the swiftest of the forest deer. The owlbear will also forage upon the carrion that is found upon the forest floor, and the creature is a familiar visitor to the kills that the [iron-backed bear](/content/species/beasts/iron-backed-bear) has made upon the forest margin.

## The Owlbear in the Traditions of the Forest

The owlbear occupies a singular place in the traditions of the forest communities, and the creature is the subject of a body of lore that is among the most elaborate of the cautionary traditions of the woodland. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the deep forest hold that the owlbear is a creature of the old wood and that the creature was old when the first of the elven kind set foot upon the world, and the elven wardens of the deep forest are reputed to be able to lead a party through the territory of an owlbear without disturbing the creature. The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the forest margins regard the owlbear with a dread that is not accorded to any other beast of the woodland, and the forester who has killed an owlbear is honoured by his community with a title that is second to none among the orders of the chase.

The hunting of the owlbear is a recognised pursuit of the forest communities, and the hunters of the deep wood take a small number of owlbears each year under licence from the local authorities. The hunt is conducted with the heavy spear or with the crossbow, and the hunter who engages an owlbear must have the skill and the courage to bring the creature down before it can close the distance, for the rush of the owlbear is a thing of terrifying speed and the blow of the great paw is sufficient to break the bones of a man. The chronicler notes that the owlbear is not held in any particular reverence by the communities of the forest, for the creature is regarded as a hazard of the deep wood and not as a symbol of the wildness of the forest, and the owlbear is hunted with the same practical purpose that the foresters bring to the hunting of the wolf and the bear.

## Significance

The owlbear is the foremost of the great monstrosities of the deep forest, and the presence of the creature in the wooded interior of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the mature wood. The owlbear is the predator that keeps the populations of the deer of the deep forest in balance with the carrying capacity of the woodland, and the loss of the owlbear from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the forest that the chroniclers have noted in the records of the forest communities. The chronicler notes that the owlbear endures, in the deep forest of the three continents and in the cautionary lore of the foresters who share the world with the creature, as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the deep wood, and the great claw-marks upon the trunks of the ancient trees are a reminder that the interior of the wood is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
