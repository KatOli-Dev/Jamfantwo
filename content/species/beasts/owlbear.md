## Monstrosity of the Deep Forest

The owlbear is one of the great monstrosities of the deep forests of the known world, a creature of considerable size and singular ferocity whose range extends across the wooded interior of the three continents. It takes its name from the combination of the great horned head of the owl and the heavy body of the bear that it displays in its conformation. Naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) consider it one of the most dangerous predators of the deep wood. The owlbear requires the cover of mature forest and the shelter of the densest thickets; it is found in greatest numbers in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and in the [Brackenshield Wood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/brackenshield-wood) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind).

## Horned Owl Head and Bear's Body

The owlbear is a creature of considerable size and remarkable heaviness of build, with a body set upon four powerful legs and a head that is broad and feathered in the manner of the great horned owl. The body is covered in a thick shaggy coat of dark brown shading to a blackish hue upon the back and shoulders; the coat is dense enough to shed the rain of wetter forests and to provide protection against the bites of lesser predators. The forelegs are armed with great claws of considerable length; the claws are the principal weapons, capable of delivering a blow sufficient to break the back of a deer or strip the bark from a mature tree with a single stroke. The head is the most singular feature, feathered in the manner of the owl and bearing a pair of great horned tufts above the ears; the eyes are large and of a pale yellow that foresters have likened to the colour of the harvest moon.

The beak is hooked and sharp, used for the tearing of prey in the manner of birds of prey, though the owlbear is not a swift flier and the wings concealed beneath the heavy coat are too small to lift the body from the ground. The wings are used in display, the owlbear spreading them to full extent when threatened or when the male is displaying to the female in the mating season. Size varies with subspecies and range; a mature male may stand as high as a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) at the shoulder and may weigh several times as much. The footprint of the great male is a familiar feature of the game trails of the deep wood.

## Mature Woodland and Dense Thickets

The owlbear inhabits the mature forests of the three continents, with the largest populations in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the [Brackenshield Wood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/brackenshield-wood) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and a smaller population in the [Ashen Heath](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/ashen-heath) of the eastern continent. The owlbear requires the cover of mature forest and the shelter of the densest thickets; it is not found in open country or settled lands. The lair is typically situated in a hollow beneath the roots of a great tree or in a natural cave of the broken country, marked at the entrance by great claw-marks scored upon the bark of surrounding trees.

The owlbear is solitary; mature animals hold territories marked by scent left upon prominent trees and by trails worn through the underbrush. A territory may extend for several miles through the deep wood; the creature ranges widely in search of prey. It is most active at dusk and dawn, emerging from the lair to hunt upon game trails, and returns before the full light of day.

## Ambush Predator of the Forest Floor

The owlbear is a predator of considerable power and singular ferocity that feeds upon a wide range of prey according to opportunity. The principal prey is deer of the deep forest; a mature owlbear will take a yearling deer with a sudden rush from cover. It also takes smaller mammals such as hares and squirrels and will take the wild boar of the forest margins when opportunity offers. The owlbear does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but accounts of the forest communities include incidents in which an owlbear has killed a forester who unwarily approached the lair; foresters take care to mark the lairs and give them a wide berth.

The hunting technique is to ambush from cover and seize prey with a sudden rush; the great claws of the forepaw deliver a blow sufficient to disable prey at the first contact. The owlbear is not a swift runner and is not capable of long pursuit of fast-moving prey, but it is capable of a sudden explosive rush over a short distance sufficient to overtake any but the swiftest forest deer. The owlbear also forages upon carrion found upon the forest floor and is a familiar visitor to the kills the [iron-backed bear](/content/species/beasts/iron-backed-bear) has made upon the forest margin.

## Cautionary Lore and the Hunt

The owlbear occupies a singular place in the traditions of the forest communities and is the subject of a body of lore among the most elaborate of the cautionary traditions of the woodland. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the deep forest hold that the owlbear is a creature of the old wood, old when the first elven kind set foot upon the world; elven wardens are reputed to be able to lead a party through the territory of an owlbear without disturbing the creature. The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the forest margins regard the owlbear with a dread not accorded to any other beast; the forester who has killed an owlbear is honoured with a title second to none among the orders of the chase.

The hunting of the owlbear is a recognised pursuit of the forest communities; hunters take a small number each year under licence from local authorities. The hunt is conducted with the heavy spear or the crossbow; the hunter must have the skill and courage to bring the creature down before it can close the distance, for the rush is of terrifying speed and the blow of the great paw is sufficient to break the bones of a man. The owlbear is not held in particular reverence; it is regarded as a hazard of the deep wood and is hunted with the same practical purpose that foresters bring to the hunting of the wolf and the bear.

## The Deep Wood's Great Monstrosity

The owlbear is the foremost of the great monstrosities of the deep forest, and its presence in the wooded interior of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the mature wood. It is the predator that keeps the populations of deer of the deep forest in balance with the carrying capacity of the woodland; the loss of the owlbear from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the forest. The owlbear endures in the deep forest of the three continents and in the cautionary lore of the foresters who share the world with the creature as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the deep wood. The great claw-marks upon the trunks of ancient trees are a reminder that the interior of the wood is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
