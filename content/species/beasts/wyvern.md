## Overview

The wyvern is a great reptilian beast of the coastal cliffs and the inland marshes, a creature of considerable size and of forbidding aspect whose range extends across the broken country of the three continents. The wyvern is a distant kinsman of the [dragon](/content/species/beasts/dragon), and the creature shares with the dragon the scaly hide and the membranous wings that are the marks of the great reptiles, but the wyvern is a smaller beast than the dragon and is possessed of a more bestial intelligence, and the wyvern is regarded by the naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) as a creature apart from the great dragons of the high mountains. The chronicler notes that the wyvern is a more familiar hazard to the communities of the coast and the marsh than the dragon is to the peoples of the interior, and the wyvern is the subject of a substantial body of practical lore among the fishermen of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) and the reed-cutters of the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash).

## Form and Appearance

The wyvern is a creature of considerable size, with a body that is set upon two powerful hind legs and a pair of wings that spring from the shoulders of the beast. The forelimbs of the wyvern are not developed as legs, but are reduced to a pair of clawed appendages that are set upon the leading edge of the wing and that are used for the grasping of the prey. The head is elongated and set with a hooked beak that is edged with a row of sharp teeth, and the eyes are large and of a pale yellow that the marsh-folk have likened to the colour of the brackish water. The tail of the wyvern is long and sinuous and is armed at the tip with a barb of considerable size, and the barb is the wyvern's principal weapon of defence and the source of the creature's fearsome reputation.

The hide of the wyvern is a thick and leathery skin that is covered in small scales of a dark green or a blackish hue, and the scales of the wyvern are not the great plates of the [dragon](/content/species/beasts/dragon) but a closer covering that is adapted to the humid climates in which the wyvern is found. The wings are broad and are supported by a single claw at the joint of the wing, and the wyvern is capable of a strong and sustained flight in the coastal winds, though the creature is not a soaring flier in the manner of the great birds. The size of the wyvern varies with the subspecies and the range, but a mature male may measure some twenty feet from the tip of the snout to the tip of the tail, and the wingspan of the same animal is considerable.

## Habitat and Range

The wyvern inhabits the coastal cliffs and the inland marshes of the three continents, with the largest populations in the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) that border the lower reaches of the Greywash, and a smaller population in the brackish lagoons of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip). The wyvern requires the cliffs for the nest and the open water or the open marsh for the hunt, and the creature is not found in the dense forest or in the high mountain. The nest is typically situated upon a ledge of a cliff that is sheltered from the prevailing wind and that overlooks the water, and the nest is a substantial structure of sticks and of reeds that is built up over many seasons of use.

The wyvern is a solitary creature outside the brief season of the breeding, and the mature animals hold territories along the coast or the margin of the marsh that they defend against the intrusion of other wyverns. The territory of a mature wyvern may extend for several miles along the coast, and the creature ranges widely in search of the prey that sustains it. The wyvern is most active in the hours of daylight, when the creature may be observed circling above the cliffs or the marsh, and the wyvern rests during the night upon a sheltered ledge of the cliff or among the reeds of the marsh.

## Diet and Behaviour

The wyvern is a predator of considerable voracity, and the creature feeds upon a wide range of prey according to the opportunity. The principal prey of the wyvern is the fish of the coastal waters and the birds of the cliff colonies, and a wyvern will take a considerable weight of fish in the course of a day. The wyvern is also a predator of the smaller mammals of the marsh and of the cliff, and the creature will take the lambs of the highland pastures when the lambs are grazing near the coast. The wyvern does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but the accounts of the coastal communities include a number of incidents in which a wyvern has taken a fisherman from the boats or a child from the beach, and the communities take care to keep the young within sight of the elders when the wyverns are nesting.

The hunting technique of the wyvern is to quarter the coast or the marsh at a height that is sufficient to allow the keen eyes to detect the movement of the prey, and the wyvern descends upon the prey with a swift and powerful stoop. The prey is seized in the clawed appendages of the wings and borne to a sheltered spot to be consumed, and the prey is torn by the hooked beak and by the clawed forelimbs. The wyvern is also an active hunter upon the ground, and the creature will pursue a wounded bird across the marsh with a speed that is considerable for a beast of its size. The barb of the tail is used in the defence of the creature, and a wyvern that is brought to bay will strike with the tail with a force that is sufficient to break the bones of a man.

## The Wyvern as Hazard

The wyvern is a familiar hazard to the communities of the coast and the marsh, and the creature is regarded by the fishermen and the reed-cutters as one of the principal dangers of their calling. The wyvern takes a small but steady toll of the fishing boats that work the coastal waters, the creature descending upon the boats to take the fish that are being brought aboard, and the fishermen must keep a watch for the circling of the wyvern above the boats. The wyvern is also a hazard to the flocks of the coastal pastures, and the shepherds of the cliff country must keep their flocks within the protection of the dogs and the watchers, for a wyvern that has found a flock will take several lambs in the course of a single morning.

The hunting of the wyvern is a recognised pursuit of the coastal communities, and the fishermen of the cliff villages take a small number of wyverns each year under licence from the local authorities. The hunt is conducted with the heavy bow or with the spear, and the hunter who engages a wyvern must have the skill and the courage to bring the creature down before it can close the distance, for the charge of the wyvern from the air is a thing of considerable speed and the strike of the barbed tail is sufficient to disable a man. The chronicler notes that the wyvern is not the equal of the [dragon](/content/species/beasts/dragon) in size or in intelligence, and the wyvern is regarded by the hunters of the coastal communities as a creature of the second order of danger, but the wyvern is a hazard that the communities of the coast and the marsh must take seriously.

## Significance

The wyvern is the foremost of the reptilian predators of the coast and the marsh, and the presence of the creature in the broken country of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the cliffs and the reeds. The wyvern is the predator that keeps the populations of the coastal birds and the marsh fish in balance with the carrying capacity of the environment, and the loss of the wyvern from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the fishery that the chroniclers have noted in the records of the coastal communities. The chronicler notes that the wyvern endures, in the cliffs and the marshes of the three continents and in the practical lore of the peoples who share the world with the creature, as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the coast, and the shadow of the wyvern upon the water is a reminder that the broken country is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
