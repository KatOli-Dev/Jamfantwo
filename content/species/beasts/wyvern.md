## Overview

The wyvern is a great reptilian beast of the coastal cliffs and inland marshes, a creature of considerable size and forbidding aspect whose range extends across the broken country of the three continents. The wyvern is a distant kinsman of the [dragon](/content/species/beasts/dragon), sharing with it the scaly hide and membranous wings that mark the great reptiles, but it is a smaller beast possessed of a more bestial intelligence; naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) regard it as a creature apart from the great dragons of the high mountains. The wyvern is a more familiar hazard to the communities of the coast and marsh than the dragon is to the peoples of the interior; it is the subject of substantial practical lore among the fishermen of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) and the reed-cutters of the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash).

## Form and Appearance

The wyvern is a creature of considerable size, with a body set upon two powerful hind legs and a pair of wings springing from the shoulders. The forelimbs are not developed as legs but reduced to a pair of clawed appendages set upon the leading edge of the wing, used for grasping prey. The head is elongated and set with a hooked beak edged with a row of sharp teeth; the eyes are large and of a pale yellow that marsh-folk have likened to brackish water. The tail is long and sinuous, armed at the tip with a barb of considerable size that is the principal weapon of defence and the source of the creature's fearsome reputation.

The hide is a thick leathery skin covered in small scales of a dark green or blackish hue; the scales are not the great plates of the [dragon](/content/species/beasts/dragon) but a closer covering adapted to the humid climates in which the wyvern is found. The wings are broad, supported by a single claw at the joint; the wyvern is capable of strong and sustained flight in coastal winds, though it is not a soaring flier in the manner of great birds. Size varies with subspecies and range; a mature male may measure twenty feet from the tip of the snout to the tip of the tail, and the wingspan is considerable.

## Habitat and Range

The wyvern inhabits the coastal cliffs and inland marshes of the three continents, with the largest populations on the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) bordering the lower reaches of the Greywash, and a smaller population in the brackish lagoons of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip). It requires cliffs for the nest and open water or open marsh for the hunt; it is not found in dense forest or high mountain. The nest is typically situated upon a sheltered ledge of a cliff overlooking water; it is a substantial structure of sticks and reeds built up over many seasons of use.

The wyvern is solitary outside the brief breeding season; mature animals hold territories along the coast or the margin of the marsh that they defend against intrusion by other wyverns. A territory may extend for several miles along the coast; the creature ranges widely in search of prey. It is most active in the hours of daylight, observed circling above cliffs or marsh, and rests at night upon a sheltered ledge or among the reeds.

## Diet and Behaviour

The wyvern is a predator of considerable voracity that feeds upon a wide range of prey according to opportunity. The principal prey is fish of the coastal waters and birds of cliff colonies; a wyvern will take a considerable weight of fish in the course of a day. It is also a predator of smaller mammals of the marsh and cliff, taking lambs of highland pastures when they graze near the coast. The wyvern does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but accounts of the coastal communities include incidents in which a wyvern has taken a fisherman from the boats or a child from the beach; communities keep the young within sight of elders when the wyverns are nesting.

The hunting technique is to quarter the coast or marsh at a height sufficient for its keen eyes to detect movement, then descend with a swift powerful stoop. Prey is seized in the clawed appendages of the wings and borne to a sheltered spot to be consumed, torn by the hooked beak and clawed forelimbs. The wyvern is also an active hunter upon the ground, pursuing a wounded bird across the marsh with considerable speed. The barb of the tail is used in defence; a wyvern brought to bay will strike with the tail with force sufficient to break the bones of a man.

## The Wyvern as Hazard

The wyvern is a familiar hazard to the communities of the coast and marsh; it is regarded by fishermen and reed-cutters as one of the principal dangers of their calling. It takes a small but steady toll of fishing boats that work the coastal waters, descending upon boats to take fish being brought aboard; fishermen must keep watch for the circling of the wyvern above the boats. The wyvern is also a hazard to flocks of the coastal pastures; shepherds of the cliff country must keep their flocks within the protection of dogs and watchers, for a wyvern that has found a flock will take several lambs in the course of a single morning.

The hunting of the wyvern is a recognised pursuit of the coastal communities; fishermen take a small number each year under licence from local authorities. The hunt is conducted with the heavy bow or spear; the hunter must have the skill and courage to bring the creature down before it can close the distance, for the charge from the air is of considerable speed and the strike of the barbed tail is sufficient to disable a man. The wyvern is not the equal of the [dragon](/content/species/beasts/dragon) in size or intelligence and is regarded by hunters of the coastal communities as a creature of the second order of danger, but it is a hazard the communities of the coast and marsh must take seriously.

## Significance

The wyvern is the foremost of the reptilian predators of the coast and marsh; its presence in the broken country of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the cliffs and reeds. It is the predator that keeps the populations of coastal birds and marsh fish in balance with the carrying capacity of the environment; the loss of the wyvern from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the fishery. The wyvern endures in the cliffs and marshes of the three continents and in the practical lore of the peoples who share the world with the creature as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the coast. The shadow of the wyvern upon the water is a reminder that the broken country is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
