## The Great Swine of the Grasslands

The plains thunder boar is the largest of the wild swine of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), a great and shaggy beast of formidable temper whose range extends across the grasslands from the foothills of the western mountains to the river valleys of the interior. The boar is the quarry of the great mounted hunt of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation); the hunt is a ceremonial pursuit conducted according to traditions among the most ancient of the plains peoples. The boar is not confined to the plains; smaller populations are found in woodland margins and river valleys of the central continent, but the great boars of the open grasslands are the most celebrated, and the lore of the hunt is principally concerned with the boars of the open country.

## Tusks, Crest, and Shaggy Coat

The thunder boar is a heavily built animal, with a body broad and deep through the chest set on short powerful legs capable of remarkable bursts of speed over short distances. The head is large and wedge-shaped, with a snout set with a disc of tough cartilage used in rooting; the jaw is armed with a pair of great tusks that curve upward and outward from the lower jaw, capable of delivering a wound of considerable severity. Older males carry a second pair of tusks in the upper jaw, smaller than the lowers, that serve to sharpen the lower tusks in the manner of a whetstone.

The coat is a dense shaggy pelage of dark brown, with a paler stripe along the spine and a distinct crest of longer bristles running from the crown of the head to the shoulders. The crest is raised when the boar is alarmed or angered; the sudden elevation of the crest combined with the clattering of the tusks is the source of the creature's common name, for the sound carries across the plains in a manner likened to distant thunder. Boars of the open grasslands are the largest; a mature male may stand taller at the shoulder than a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) of average height and may weigh several times as much.

## Open Plains and River Valleys

The thunder boar inhabits the grasslands of central Aethelar, with the greatest concentration in the open country managed by the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) and in the river valleys that thread between the grasslands. It is a creature of the open country and the woodland edge; it does not range into deep forest nor onto exposed peaks of high mountains. The boar is most numerous in regions where grass is tall enough to provide cover for rooting and where water is sufficient to support the wallowing that is one of its characteristic habits. The wallowing places, where the boar rolls in the mud of watercourses, are the most reliable places to find the boar at the close of day.

The boar ranges widely through the grasslands in search of new growth following the burning of the plains; herds may move many miles in a single season as grazing shifts with weather and the passage of herds of the plains peoples. Boars are most active in early morning and late afternoon, resting during the heat of day in the shade of tall grass or in wallows excavated in the damp margins of streams. Sows and young remain in larger herds through the year; mature boars are more solitary, holding a range marked by scent left upon prominent tussocks and by deep rooting at the margins of their territory.

## Rooting, Grazing, and Herd Life

The thunder boar is an omnivore, feeding upon grasses and roots of the plains, tubers excavated with the disc of the snout, and small animals taken opportunistically in the course of its rooting. It is fond of the roots of certain plains grasses; pastures rooted by the boar show characteristic upturned circles that herdsmen recognise as a sign of its recent presence. The boar is also a scavenger, visiting carcasses of animals that have died upon the plains, driving off smaller scavengers and consuming flesh and hide.

The boar is social outside the breeding season; herds of the plains may number several dozen individuals, sows and young of the year moving together under the leadership of an old sow. Mature boars join the herds only during the breeding season, when competition for the sows is at its height. Contests between boars for possession of herds are conducted with great violence; they charge one another with head lowered and tusks presented, the sound of clashing great heads heard at considerable distance across open country. The victor assumes the herd and drives younger males to the periphery, where they wait in restless bands for the coming season.

## Ceremonial Pursuit of the Plains Confederation

The hunt of the thunder boar is the ceremonial pursuit of the mounted warriors of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation); its rules are among the most elaborate hunting traditions of the central continent. The hunt is conducted by a company of mounted hunters who ride out from the encampment in early morning and spread across the grasslands in search of a herd. The hunters work the herd to a single boar of mature size and great tusks; the chosen boar is then driven from the herd by beaters and pursued across the open country.

The chase is conducted at speed, the horses of the plains being among the swiftest in the known world; the boar is run until it turns at bay upon some chosen ground, often a rocky outcrop or the bank of a stream. The hunters then dismount and approach on foot, taking turns engaging the boar with the spear; the boar is brought down only after a contest that may last many minutes. The kill is followed by the breaking of the quarry; the hunter who delivers the final stroke claims the tusks and the crest as trophies. The meat is distributed among the company, and the feast that follows is one of the great celebrations of the plains year.

## Trophies, Materials, and Conflicts

The plains peoples regard the thunder boar with a mixture of respect and wary caution; the creature occupies an important place in the lore of the grasslands. It is the symbol of the strength and temper of the open country; warriors of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) take the tusks as a token of their prowess in the hunt. The boar is also a source of useful materials: the hide is tough and well suited to the manufacture of leather for harnesses and shields, and the bristles of the crest are used in making brushes employed in the decoration of lodges and ceremonial objects.

The boar is not ordinarily aggressive toward [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans); herds will allow a rider to approach to a reasonable distance before taking flight, but a wounded boar or a sow with young is a creature of extreme danger. Plains hunters give boars a wide berth when the sows are nursing. The boar is also a pest of cultivated margins; farmers of the river valleys must maintain strong fences to keep the boar from ripening grain. Disputes between farmers and hunters over the management of boar populations are a recurring feature of the political life of the central continent.

## Keystone of the Grasslands

The plains thunder boar is the great beast of the grasslands and the quarry of the most celebrated of the mounted hunts of the plains peoples. Its presence in the grasslands is a measure of the health of the open country; the boar is one of the keystone species of the plains ecosystem, the rooting of the soil being a factor in the maintenance of the diversity of grasses that sustain the herds of pastoral peoples. The hunt of the boar is a ceremonial pursuit that binds the warriors of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) to the traditions of the grasslands; the tusks and the crest are the trophies that mark the passage of the hunter from youth to maturity. The boar endures in the grasslands and in the lore of the plains as a symbol of the wildness that remains even in a country closely inhabited by pastoral peoples; the hunt of the boar is a reminder that the grasslands are not merely a pasture but also a country of ancient and enduring wildness.
