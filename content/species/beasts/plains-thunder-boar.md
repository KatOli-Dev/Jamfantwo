## Overview

The plains thunder boar is the largest of the wild swine of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), a great and shaggy beast of formidable temper whose range extends across the grasslands from the foothills of the western mountains to the river valleys of the interior. The boar is the quarry of the great mounted hunt of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), and the hunt of the thunder boar is a ceremonial pursuit that is conducted according to traditions that are among the most ancient of the plains peoples. The chronicler notes that the boar is not confined to the plains, for smaller populations are found in the woodland margins and the river valleys of the central continent, but the great boars of the open grasslands are the most celebrated, and the lore of the hunt is principally concerned with the boars of the open country.

## Form and Appearance

The thunder boar is a heavily built animal, with a body that is broad and deep through the chest and set on short and powerful legs that are capable of remarkable bursts of speed over the short distances of the chase. The head is large and wedge-shaped, with a snout that is set with a disc of tough cartilage that the boar uses in the rooting of the soil, and the jaw is armed with a pair of great tusks that curve upward and outward from the lower jaw and that are capable of delivering a wound of considerable severity. The boars of the older males carry a second pair of tusks in the upper jaw, smaller than the lowers, that serve to sharpen the lower tusks in the manner of a whetstone.

The coat of the thunder boar is a dense and shaggy pelage that is dark brown in colour, with a paler stripe along the spine and a distinct crest of longer bristles that runs from the crown of the head to the shoulders. The crest is raised when the boar is alarmed or angered, and the sudden elevation of the crest, combined with the clattering of the tusks, is the source of the creature's common name, for the sound carries across the plains in a manner that the plains peoples have likened to distant thunder. The boars of the open grasslands are the largest of the species, and a mature male may stand taller at the shoulder than a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) of average height, and may weigh several times as much.

## Habitat and Range

The thunder boar inhabits the grasslands of central Aethelar, with his greatest concentration in the open country that is managed by the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) and in the river valleys that thread between the grasslands. The boar is a creature of the open country and the woodland edge, and he does not range into the deep forest nor onto the exposed peaks of the high mountains. The boar is most numerous in the regions where the grass is tall enough to provide a cover for the rooting and where the water is sufficient to support the wallowing that is one of the boar's characteristic habits. The wallowing places, where the boar rolls in the mud of the watercourses, are the most reliable places in which to find the boar at the close of the day.

The boar ranges widely through the grasslands in search of the new growth that follows the burning of the plains, and the herds of the boar may move many miles in a single season as the grazing shifts with the weather and the passage of the herds of the plains peoples. The boars are most active in the early morning and the late afternoon, and they rest during the heat of the day in the shade of the tall grass or in the wallows that they have excavated in the damp margins of the streams. The sows and the young remain in the larger herds through the year, while the mature boars are more solitary, holding a range that they mark by the scent that they leave upon the prominent tussocks and by the deep rooting that they conduct at the margins of their territory.

## Diet and Behaviour

The thunder boar is an omnivore, feeding upon the grasses and the roots of the plains, the tubers that he excavates with the disc of the snout, and the small animals that he takes opportunistically in the course of his rooting. The boar is fond of the roots of certain of the plains grasses, and the pastures that have been rooted by the boar show the characteristic upturned circles that the herdsmen of the plains recognise as a sign of the boar's recent presence. The boar is also a scavenger, and the carcasses of the animals that have died upon the plains are visited by the boar, who will drive off the smaller scavengers and consume the flesh and the hide.

The boar is a social animal outside the breeding season, and the herds of the plains may number several dozen individuals, the sows and the young of the year moving together through the grasslands under the leadership of an old sow. The mature boars join the herds only during the breeding season, when the competition for the sows is at its height, and the contests between the boars for the possession of the herds are conducted with great violence. The boars charge one another with the head lowered and the tusks presented, and the sound of the clashing of the great heads may be heard at a considerable distance across the open country. The victor of a contest assumes the herd and drives the younger males to the periphery, where they wait in restless bands for the coming of the next season.

## The Mounted Hunt

The hunt of the thunder boar is the ceremonial pursuit of the mounted warriors of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), and the rules by which it is conducted are among the most elaborate of the hunting traditions of the central continent. The hunt is conducted by a company of mounted hunters, who ride out from the encampment in the early morning and spread across the grasslands in search of a herd of boars. The hunters work the herd to a single boar of mature size and great tusks, and the chosen boar is then driven from the herd by the beaters and pursued across the open country.

The chase is conducted at speed, the horses of the plains being among the swiftest in the known world, and the boar is run until he turns at bay upon some chosen ground, often a rocky outcrop or the bank of a stream. The hunters then dismount and approach the boar on foot, the hunters taking it in turn to engage the boar with the spear, and the boar is brought down only after a contest that may last for many minutes. The kill is followed by the breaking of the quarry, the hunter who delivers the final stroke claiming the tusks and the crest of the boar as the trophy of the hunt. The meat of the boar is distributed among the company, and the feast that follows the hunt is one of the great celebrations of the plains year.

## The Boar and the Plains Peoples

The plains peoples regard the thunder boar with a mixture of respect and wary caution, and the creature occupies an important place in the lore of the grasslands. The boar is the symbol of the strength and the temper of the open country, and the warriors of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) take the tusks of the boar as a token of their prowess in the hunt. The boar is also a source of useful materials, for the hide is tough and well suited to the manufacture of the leather that is used for the harnesses and the shields of the plains, and the bristles of the crest are used in the making of the brushes that are employed in the decoration of the lodges and the ceremonial objects.

The boar is not ordinarily aggressive toward [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), and the herds will allow a rider to approach to a reasonable distance before they take to flight, but a wounded boar or a sow with young is a creature of extreme danger, and the plains hunters are careful to give the boars a wide berth when the sows are nursing. The boar is also a pest of the cultivated margins, and the farmers of the river valleys must maintain strong fences to keep the boar from the ripening grain, and the disputes between the farmers and the hunters over the management of the boar populations are a recurring feature of the political life of the central continent.

## Significance

The plains thunder boar is the great beast of the grasslands and the quarry of the most celebrated of the mounted hunts of the plains peoples. The creature's presence in the grasslands is a measure of the health of the open country, and the boar is one of the keystone species of the plains ecosystem, the rooting of the soil by the boar being a factor in the maintenance of the diversity of the grasses that sustain the herds of the pastoral peoples. The hunt of the boar is a ceremonial pursuit that binds the warriors of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) to the traditions of the grasslands, and the tusks and the crest of the boar are the trophies that mark the passage of the hunter from youth to maturity. The chronicler notes that the boar endures, in the grasslands and in the lore of the plains, as a symbol of the wildness that remains even in a country that is closely inhabited by the pastoral peoples, and the hunt of the boar is a reminder that the grasslands are not merely a pasture but also a country of ancient and enduring wildness.
