## The Flame-Plumed Bird of the High Mountains

The phoenix is a great bird of the high mountains and volcanic country, a creature of singular beauty and singular reputation whose range extends across the highest ranges of the three continents. It takes its name from the plumage of flame-coloured feathers it displays in its aspect. Naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) consider it one of the most singular of the avifauna of the world. The phoenix requires the high cliffs of volcanic peaks and the shelter of craters and warm caves found there; it is found in greatest numbers in the highest ranges of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and in the volcanic peaks of the eastern continent.

## Gold and Crimson Feathers

The phoenix is a great bird, with a body set upon two powerful legs and a pair of wings that are its most celebrated feature. The plumage is the most singular attribute: a brilliant gold shading to deep crimson at the tips of the wings and tail. The feathers are said to be of a luminosity visible at a considerable distance. The feathers of the phoenix possess a peculiar quality that naturalists have not been able to explain; those preserved in cabinets of the curious are said to retain a measure of warmth long after the bird that bore them has departed.

The head is set upon a long graceful neck with a hooked beak of considerable size; the eyes are large and of a deep lustrous black likened to the obsidian of volcanic country. The tail is long, composed of feathers longer than the body of the bird; it is spread in the manner of display of great birds of warm countries, carried in a train that trails behind the creature in flight. The wingspan of a mature bird is greater than that of the [stormwing roc](/content/species/beasts/stormwing-roc) of the mountain ranges, though the body is lighter in proportion to the wings.

## Volcanic Peaks and Warm Caves

The phoenix inhabits the high mountains and volcanic country of the three continents, with the largest populations in the highest ranges of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the volcanic peaks of the eastern continent, and a smaller population in the southern highlands of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) above the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). It requires high cliffs of volcanic peaks and the shelter of craters and warm caves; it is not found in open country or dense forest. The nest is typically situated upon a ledge warmed by the vapours of volcanic country; it is a substantial structure of sticks and shed feathers of the parent birds built up over many seasons of use.

The phoenix is solitary outside the brief breeding season; mature birds hold ranges marked by the song the male utters at dawn and dusk, a song of singular beauty likened to the sound of a bell of fine silver. The range may extend for many miles through the high country; the bird ranges widely in search of prey. It is most active in the hours of daylight and is a familiar sight in the high country, circling upon updraughts above volcanic peaks in a manner mountain peoples regard as one of the great spectacles of the high country.

## Hunter of the High Country

The phoenix is a bird of considerable appetite that feeds upon a wide range of prey according to opportunity. The principal prey is smaller creatures of the high country, including mountain hares and marmots of the alpine meadows; a phoenix will take several such creatures in the course of a day. It is also a predator of birds of the high country, taking chicks of cliff-nesting birds when the nests are unguarded. The phoenix does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but accounts of the high country include incidents in which a phoenix has defended a nest with a ferocity the mountain communities have learned to respect.

The hunting technique is to quarter the high country at a height sufficient for the keen eyes to detect movement, then descend in a swift powerful stoop. Prey is seized in the talons of the great feet and borne to a sheltered spot upon the cliff to be consumed, torn by the hooked beak. The phoenix is capable of long sustained flight and will range many miles from the nest in a day's hunting. It is also an active forager upon the ground, walking the alpine meadows in search of insects and small creatures found among the rocks.

## Renewal from Ashes

The phoenix occupies a singular place in the traditions of the high country and is the subject of a body of lore among the most celebrated legendary traditions of the three continents. The bird is the symbol of renewal and the perseverance of the bright things of the world. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the high country hold that the phoenix is the eldest of the birds, old when the first elven kind set foot upon the world; they regard the phoenix with a reverence not accorded to any other bird. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) do not hunt the phoenix; their traditions hold that it is a creature of the upper world and the dwarves the lords of the lower, and they seal the upper passages of their halls against intrusion.

The phoenix is the subject of a celebrated tradition found in the lore of every people: the renewal of the bird from the ashes of its own pyre. Accounts of the high country describe how a phoenix that has come to the end of its long life builds a nest of sweet-scented woods and sets the nest alight with the heat of its own body, the bird being consumed in the flames and a young phoenix rising from the ashes. Naturalists of the academies have debated the reality of the tradition for centuries; the question has not been settled to the satisfaction of all parties, but the chroniclers of the high country are unanimous that the bird is possessed of a longevity not matched by any other creature of the world.

## Symbol of Bright Persistence

The phoenix is the foremost of the great birds of the high mountains, and its presence in the volcanic country of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the peaks. It is a predator that contributes to the balance of the smaller creatures of the high country; the loss of the phoenix from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the highland pasture. The phoenix endures in the high country of the three continents and in the legendary lore of every people that shares the world with the bird as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the peaks. The song of the phoenix at dawn is a reminder that the upper country is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the valleys below.
