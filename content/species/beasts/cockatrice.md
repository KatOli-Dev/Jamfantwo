## Overview

The cockatrice is a small but singularly feared reptilian beast of the broken country and the rocky hills, a creature of modest size and of singular reputation whose range extends across the warmer lands of the three continents. The cockatrice takes its name from the combination of the head of the rooster and the body of the lizard that the creature displays in its conformation, and the animal is held by the naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) to be one of the most singular of the predators of the rocky country. The chronicler notes that the cockatrice is not a creature of the open plains or the dense forest, for the animal requires the broken country of the rocky hills and the shelter of the crevices that are found there, and the cockatrice is found in greatest numbers in the [Ashen Heath](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/ashen-heath) of the eastern continent and in the broken country of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar).

## Form and Appearance

The cockatrice is a small creature, with a body that is set upon two slender legs in the manner of the great rooster and a tail that is long and serpentine and that is the source of the creature's most celebrated attribute. The body of the cockatrice is covered in a close covering of small scales of a dull green or a brownish hue, and the scales of the older animals are tipped with a pale yellow that the herders of the rocky country have likened to the colour of old brass. The head of the cockatrice is the creature's most singular feature, for the head bears the comb and the wattles of the great rooster and the hooked beak of a bird of prey, and the eyes are small and of a pale and a piercing red that the chroniclers of the hill country have found unsettling to behold. The tail of the cockatrice is long and sinuous and is tipped with a barb of considerable sharpness, and the barb is the cockatrice's principal weapon of offence.

The wings of the cockatrice are short and are not capable of sustained flight, the creature being limited to a brief and laborious flapping that is sufficient to carry it across a short distance or to break the force of a fall from a ledge of the rocks. The size of the cockatrice is modest, the creature standing no higher than a large domestic fowl at the shoulder, and the cockatrice is not a creature that is encountered at a distance by the traveller of the broken country. The cockatrice is, however, a creature that is encountered with regret, for the creature's reputation is such that the herders of the hill country will give a wide berth to any rocky hollow in which a cockatrice may be suspected to lie.

## Habitat and Range

The cockatrice inhabits the rocky country of the three continents, with the largest populations in the [Ashen Heath](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/ashen-heath) of the eastern continent and in the broken country of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and a smaller population in the foothills of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The cockatrice requires the broken country of the rocky hills and the shelter of the crevices and the small caves that are found there, and the creature is not found in the open plains or in the dense forest. The lair of the cockatrice is typically situated in a crevice of the rocks that is sheltered from the prevailing wind and that commands a view of the country around, and the lair is marked at the entrance by a small pile of the stones and the feathers that the creature has scratched together.

The cockatrice is a solitary creature, and the mature animals hold territories that they mark by the scent that they leave upon the prominent rocks and by the strident cry that the male utters at the dawn and the dusk of the day. The territory of a mature cockatrice is not extensive, the creature being a sedentary beast that ranges no more than a few hundred yards from the lair in the course of the hunting. The cockatrice is most active in the hours of midday, when the sun is at its height and the smaller creatures of the rocky country are at rest in the shade of the rocks, and the cockatrice returns to the lair before the dusk, when the cooler air would render the creature less effective in the hunt.

## Diet and Behaviour

The cockatrice is a predator of modest size but of singular effectiveness, and the creature feeds upon a wide range of prey according to the opportunity. The principal prey of the cockatrice is the smaller creatures of the rocky country, including the lizards and the large insects of the broken ground, and a cockatrice will take several such creatures in the course of a day. The cockatrice will also take the small mammals of the rocky hills, including the mice and the voles that inhabit the crevices, and the creature is known to take the chicks of the ground-nesting birds when the chicks are encountered upon the rocks. The cockatrice does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) or the larger mammals, but the accounts of the hill communities include a number of incidents in which a cockatrice has bitten a herder who has unwarily placed a hand upon a rock beneath which the creature was lying.

The hunting technique of the cockatrice is to lie in wait upon a flat stone and to seize the prey with a sudden dart of the head, the hooked beak of the creature delivering a bite that is sufficient to kill the prey at the first contact. The tail of the cockatrice is used in the defence of the creature, and a cockatrice that is brought to bay will strike with the tail with a speed that is astonishing for a beast of its size. The chroniclers of the hill country have long debated the precise nature of the cockatrice's venom, and the naturalists of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) have prepared a number of essays upon the subject, but the consensus of the forest communities is that the bite of the cockatrice is a thing to be avoided at all costs.

## The Cockatrice in Tradition

The cockatrice occupies a singular place in the traditions of the hill communities, and the creature is the subject of a body of lore that is among the most elaborate of the cautionary traditions of the rocky country. The herders of the hill country hold that the cockatrice is a creature that was old when the first of the herds was set upon the hills, and the herders take care to mark the lairs of the cockatrices and to give them a wide berth. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the deep forest hold that the cockatrice is a creature of the rocks and that the creature is to be respected for the potency of its bite, and the elven wardens of the forest are reputed to be able to lead a party through the territory of a cockatrice without disturbing the creature.

The hunting of the cockatrice is a recognised pursuit of the hill communities, and the hunters of the rocky country take a small number of cockatrices each year under licence from the local authorities. The hunt is conducted with the long spear or with the sling, and the hunter who engages a cockatrice must have the skill to bring the creature down before it can close the distance, for the bite of the cockatrice is a thing of singular potency and the strike of the barbed tail is sufficient to disable a man at close quarters. The chronicler notes that the cockatrice is not held in any particular reverence by the communities of the hill country, for the creature is regarded as a hazard of the rocks and not as a symbol of the wildness of the country, and the cockatrice is hunted with the same practical purpose that the hill folk bring to the hunting of the snake and the lizard.

## Significance

The cockatrice is the foremost of the small monstrosities of the rocky country, and the presence of the creature in the broken lands of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the hills. The cockatrice is a predator that contributes to the balance of the smaller creatures of the rocky country, and the loss of the cockatrice from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the pasture that the chroniclers have noted in the records of the hill communities. The chronicler notes that the cockatrice endures, in the rocky country of the three continents and in the cautionary lore of the hill folk who share the world with the creature, as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the rocks, and the strident cry of the cockatrice at the dawn is a reminder that the broken country is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
