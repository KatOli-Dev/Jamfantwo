## Overview

The cockatrice is a small but singularly feared reptilian beast of the broken country and rocky hills, a creature of modest size and singular reputation whose range extends across the warmer lands of the three continents. It takes its name from the combination of the head of the rooster and the body of the lizard that it displays in its conformation. Naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) consider it one of the most singular predators of the rocky country. The cockatrice requires the broken country of rocky hills and the shelter of crevices found there; it is found in greatest numbers in the [Ashen Heath](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/ashen-heath) of the eastern continent and in the broken country of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar).

## Form and Appearance

The cockatrice is a small creature, with a body set upon two slender legs in the manner of the great rooster and a long serpentine tail that is the source of its most celebrated attribute. The body is covered in a close covering of small scales of a dull green or brownish hue; the scales of older animals are tipped with a pale yellow that herders have likened to the colour of old brass. The head is the creature's most singular feature, bearing the comb and wattles of the great rooster and the hooked beak of a bird of prey. The eyes are small and of a pale piercing red. The tail is long and sinuous, tipped with a barb of considerable sharpness that serves as the principal weapon of offence.

The wings are short and not capable of sustained flight, limited to a brief laborious flapping sufficient to carry the creature across a short distance or break the force of a fall from a rocky ledge. The cockatrice stands no higher than a large domestic fowl at the shoulder; it is not a creature encountered at a distance by the traveller of the broken country. It is, however, encountered with regret, for its reputation is such that herders of the hill country will give a wide berth to any rocky hollow in which a cockatrice may be suspected to lie.

## Habitat and Range

The cockatrice inhabits the rocky country of the three continents, with the largest populations in the [Ashen Heath](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/ashen-heath) of the eastern continent and the broken country of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and a smaller population in the foothills of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). It requires broken country and the shelter of crevices and small caves; it is not found in open plains or dense forest. The lair is typically situated in a crevice sheltered from the prevailing wind that commands a view of the surrounding country, marked at the entrance by a small pile of stones and feathers the creature has scratched together.

The cockatrice is solitary, and mature animals hold territories marked by scent left upon prominent rocks and by the strident cry the male utters at dawn and dusk. The territory is not extensive, the creature being sedentary and ranging no more than a few hundred yards from the lair in the course of hunting. It is most active in the hours of midday, when the sun is at its height and smaller creatures are at rest in the shade, and returns to the lair before dusk.

## Diet and Behaviour

The cockatrice is a predator of modest size but singular effectiveness, feeding upon a wide range of prey according to opportunity. The principal prey is smaller creatures of the rocky country, including lizards and large insects; a cockatrice will take several such creatures in the course of a day. It also takes small mammals of the rocky hills, including mice and voles that inhabit crevices, and is known to take the chicks of ground-nesting birds encountered upon the rocks. The cockatrice does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) or larger mammals, but accounts of the hill communities include incidents in which a cockatrice has bitten a herder who unwarily placed a hand upon a rock beneath which the creature was lying.

The hunting technique is to lie in wait upon a flat stone and seize the prey with a sudden dart of the head; the hooked beak delivers a bite sufficient to kill the prey at the first contact. The tail is used in defence; a cockatrice brought to bay will strike with the tail with astonishing speed. The precise nature of the cockatrice's venom has long been debated; naturalists of the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) have prepared a number of essays upon the subject, but the consensus is that the bite is a thing to be avoided at all costs.

## The Cockatrice in Tradition

The cockatrice occupies a singular place in the traditions of the hill communities and is the subject of a body of lore among the most elaborate of the cautionary traditions of the rocky country. Herders hold that the cockatrice was old when the first of the herds was set upon the hills; they take care to mark the lairs and give them a wide berth. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the deep forest hold that the creature is to be respected for the potency of its bite; elven wardens are reputed to be able to lead a party through its territory without disturbing it.

The hunting of the cockatrice is a recognised pursuit of the hill communities; hunters take a small number each year under licence from local authorities. The hunt is conducted with the long spear or the sling. The hunter must have the skill to bring the creature down before it can close the distance, for the bite is of singular potency and the strike of the barbed tail is sufficient to disable a man at close quarters. The cockatrice is not held in particular reverence; it is regarded as a hazard of the rocks and is hunted with the same practical purpose that hill folk bring to the hunting of the snake and the lizard.

## Significance

The cockatrice is the foremost of the small monstrosities of the rocky country, and its presence in the broken lands of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the hills. It is a predator that contributes to the balance of the smaller creatures of the rocky country; the loss of the cockatrice from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the pasture. The cockatrice endures in the rocky country of the three continents and in the cautionary lore of the hill folk who share the world with the creature as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the rocks. The strident cry of the cockatrice at dawn is a reminder that the broken country is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
