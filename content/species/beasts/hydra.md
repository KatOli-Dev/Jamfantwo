## Overview

The hydra is one of the great reptilian beasts of the swamps and the inland waters of the known world, a creature of considerable size and of singular dread whose range extends across the marshy lowlands of the three continents. The hydra takes its name from the many-headed conformation that the creature displays in its aspect, and the animal is held by the naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) to be one of the most singular of the predators of the inland waters. The chronicler notes that the hydra is not a creature of the open country or the fast-flowing rivers, for the animal requires the still water of the swamps and the shelter of the reed beds that are found there, and the hydra is found in greatest numbers in the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) that border the lower reaches of the Greywash and in the brackish lagoons of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana).

## Form and Appearance

The hydra is a creature of considerable size, with a body that is set upon four powerful legs in the manner of the great lizard and a long and sinuous neck that bears at its crown a cluster of heads that is the source of the creature's most celebrated and most dreaded attribute. The body of the hydra is covered in a thick hide of small scales of a dark green or a blackish hue, and the scales of the older animals are tipped with a pale moss that the marsh-folk have likened to the colour of the duckweed that floats upon the still water. The legs of the hydra are short and powerful, and the creature is capable of a surprising speed upon the marshy ground, the broad feet of the beast being adapted to the soft footing of the swamp. The tail of the hydra is long and heavy and is used for the propulsion of the creature through the water, and the hydra is capable of a swift and silent passage through the reed beds that the marsh-folk have learned to fear.

The heads of the hydra are the creature's most singular feature, and the number of heads varies with the age and the vigour of the individual. A hydra of moderate age may bear five heads, while the oldest of the creatures that have been recorded by the chroniclers of the marsh have been said to bear nine or more. Each head is set upon its own short neck that springs from the common base of the creature's crown, and each head is armed with a jaw of considerable size and a row of sharp teeth that are renewed throughout the life of the animal. The heads act in concert when the creature is hunting, the heads turning in different directions to survey the surrounding water and the reed beds, and the heads are capable of an independent action that the chroniclers of the marsh have found singularly unsettling. The most celebrated attribute of the hydra is the creature's ability to regrow a head that has been severed, and the accounts of the marsh include a number of records of a hydra that has been beheaded only to grow two heads in the place of the one that was lost.

## Habitat and Range

The hydra inhabits the swamps and the inland waters of the three continents, with the largest populations in the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and in the brackish lagoons of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and a smaller population in the lake country of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) that lies within the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands). The hydra requires the still water of the swamps and the shelter of the reed beds, and the creature is not found in the open country or in the fast-flowing rivers. The lair of the hydra is typically situated in a deep pool that is surrounded by the densest of the reed beds, and the lair is marked at the surface by a peculiar stillness of the water that the marsh-folk have learned to recognise as a sign of the presence of the creature.

The hydra is a solitary creature, and the mature animals hold territories that they mark by the scent that they leave upon the prominent tussocks and by the trails that they wear through the reed beds. The territory of a mature hydra may extend for several miles through the marsh, and the creature ranges widely in search of the prey that sustains it. The hydra is most active in the hours of dusk and of dawn, when the creature emerges from the pool to hunt upon the game trails that wind through the marsh, and the hydra returns to the pool before the full light of the day, when the heat of the sun would render the creature less effective in the hunt.

## Diet and Behaviour

The hydra is a predator of considerable power, and the creature feeds upon a wide range of prey according to the opportunity. The principal prey of the hydra is the larger mammals that come to the water to drink, including the deer of the marsh margins and the cattle of the marshland pastures, and a mature hydra will take a yearling deer with a sudden rush from the cover of the reeds. The hydra is also a predator of the fish of the still water and the birds of the reed beds, and the creature is known to take the wildfowl that nest among the reeds when the nests are unguarded. The hydra does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but the accounts of the marsh communities include a number of incidents in which a hydra has killed a fisherman or a reed-cutter who has unwarily approached the pool, and the marsh-folk take care to mark the lairs of the hydras and to give them a wide berth.

The hunting technique of the hydra is to lie in wait beneath the surface of the water and to seize the prey with a sudden dart of the heads, the multiple heads of the creature delivering a series of bites that are sufficient to disable the prey at the first contact. The hydra is capable of a short and rapid rush through the water that is sufficient to overtake a swimming prey, and the creature will pursue a wildfowl that has taken to the wing with a leap from the water that the marsh-folk have learned to respect. The heads of the hydra act in concert when the creature is hunting, and the chroniclers of the marsh have recorded instances of a hydra that has seized a deer with two heads at once, the additional heads serving to drag the prey beneath the water with a force that no single throat could have exerted.

## The Hydra in Legend and the Slayer's Tradition

The hydra occupies a singular place in the traditions of the marsh communities, and the creature is the subject of a body of lore that is among the most elaborate of the cautionary traditions of the inland waters. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the forest margins hold that the hydra is a creature of the old marsh and that the creature was old when the first of the elven kind set foot upon the world, and the elven wardens of the marsh are reputed to be able to lead a party through the territory of a hydra without disturbing the creature. The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the marsh communities regard the hydra with a dread that is not accorded to any other beast of the water, and the hunter who has killed a hydra is honoured by his community with a title that is second to none among the orders of the chase.

The hunting of the hydra is a recognised pursuit of the marsh communities, and the hunters of the reed country take a small number of hydras each year under licence from the local authorities. The hunt is conducted with the heavy spear and with brands that are set alight and applied to the stumps of the severed necks, for the naturalists of the academies have determined that the fire is the surest means of preventing the regrowth of the head. The hunter who engages a hydra must have the skill and the courage to sever the heads in quick succession and to apply the fire to each stump before the next head can be brought to bear, for the rush of the hydra from the water is a thing of terrifying speed and the bite of the creature is sufficient to disable a man at the first contact. The chronicler notes that the hydra is not held in any particular reverence by the communities of the marsh, for the creature is regarded as a hazard of the water and not as a symbol of the wildness of the country, and the hydra is hunted with the same practical purpose that the marsh-folk bring to the hunting of the [Velden Reef Serpent](/content/species/beasts/velden-reef-serpent) and the [shark](/content/species/beasts/auric-reef-shark).

## Significance

The hydra is the foremost of the great reptilian beasts of the inland waters, and the presence of the creature in the swamps of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the reeds. The hydra is the predator that keeps the populations of the larger mammals of the marsh margins in balance with the carrying capacity of the water, and the loss of the hydra from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the fishery that the chroniclers have noted in the records of the marsh communities. The chronicler notes that the hydra endures, in the swamps and the inland waters of the three continents and in the cautionary lore of the marsh-folk who share the world with the creature, as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the reeds, and the peculiar stillness of the water above the lair is a reminder that the marsh is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
