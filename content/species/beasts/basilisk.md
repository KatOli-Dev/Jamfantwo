## Overview

The basilisk is a great reptilian beast of the deep forest and the broken cave country, a creature of considerable size and of singular reputation whose range extends across the wooded interior of the three continents. The basilisk is held by the naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) to be the most venomous of the reptiles of the world, and the creature is the subject of a body of cautionary lore among the foresters of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) and the [Brackenshield Wood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/brackenshield-wood) that is without parallel among the beasts of the woodland. The chronicler notes that the basilisk is a creature of the deep forest and the dark cave, and the encounter with a basilisk is recorded in the annals of the forest communities as a singular event of the most grave kind, and the hunters who have taken a basilisk are honoured by their communities with a distinction that is second to none among the orders of the chase.

## Form and Appearance

The basilisk is a heavily built reptile, with a body that is set upon four short and powerful legs and a head that is broad and flattened in the manner of the great lizards. The body of the basilisk is covered in a thick hide of small scales that is of a dark green or a blackish hue, and the scales of the older animals are tipped with a pale yellow that the foresters have likened to the colour of old bone. The head is set with a pair of small eyes that are of a pale and luminous yellow, and the eyes of the basilisk are the source of the creature's most celebrated attribute, for the gaze of the basilisk is held by the traditions of the forest to be lethal to the creatures upon which the gaze falls. The tail of the basilisk is long and heavy and is not prehensile, and the tail is used for the balance of the creature as it moves through the underbrush of the forest.

The size of the basilisk varies with the subspecies and the range, but a mature male may measure some twelve feet from the tip of the snout to the root of the tail, and the body of the same animal is heavy in proportion to the length. The basilisk is not a swift creature, for the legs are short and the body is heavy, and the creature is not capable of the pursuit of a fast-moving prey. The basilisk is, however, a creature of considerable strength, and the bite of the basilisk is sufficient to break the bones of a small deer, and the venom that is delivered by the bite is of a potency that the naturalists of the academies have not been able to equal in any of the preparations of the apothecaries.

## Habitat and Range

The basilisk inhabits the deep forest and the broken cave country of the three continents, with the largest populations in the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the [Brackenshield Wood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/brackenshield-wood) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), and a smaller population in the broken country of the [Ashen Heath](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/ashen-heath) of the eastern continent. The basilisk requires the cover of the deep forest or the concealment of the cave for the lair, and the creature is not found in the open country or in the high mountain. The lair of the basilisk is typically situated in a cave that opens into a dense thicket or in a hollow beneath the roots of a great tree, and the lair is marked at the entrance by a peculiar musty odour that the foresters have learned to recognise as a sign of the presence of the creature.

The basilisk is a solitary creature, and the mature animals hold territories that they mark by the scent that they leave upon the prominent rocks and by the trails that they wear through the underbrush of the forest. The territory of a mature basilisk may extend for several miles through the deep wood, and the creature ranges widely in search of the prey that sustains it. The basilisk is most active in the hours of dusk and of dawn, when the creature emerges from the lair to lie in wait upon the game trails that wind through the forest, and the basilisk returns to the lair before the full light of the day, when the heat of the sun would render the creature sluggish.

## Diet and Behaviour

The basilisk is a predator that hunts by ambush, and the creature takes a wide range of prey according to the opportunity. The principal prey of the basilisk is the smaller mammals of the forest, including the hares and the squirrels, and a mature basilisk will take several such animals in the course of a week. The basilisk will also take the birds of the forest floor and the reptiles of the underbrush, and the creature is known to take the fawns of the forest deer when the fawns are encountered upon the game trails. The basilisk does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), but the accounts of the forest communities include a number of incidents in which a basilisk has bitten a forester who has unwarily approached the lair, and the foresters take care to mark the lairs of the basilisks and to give them a wide berth.

The hunting technique of the basilisk is to lie in wait upon the game trail and to seize the prey with a sudden strike of the heavy head as the prey passes within reach. The prey is held in the jaws of the basilisk until the venom has done its work, and the basilisk then follows the trail of the stricken animal to the place where it has fallen. The venom of the basilisk is of a peculiar nature, for the venom acts upon the musculature of the prey and leaves the carcass untainted, and the meat of an animal that has been killed by a basilisk is held by the foresters to be fit for the table, provided that the flesh is well cooked. The gaze of the basilisk is the subject of a substantial body of legend, and the foresters of the deep wood hold that the gaze of the creature is sufficient to kill a man who meets it directly, though the naturalists of the academies have debated the reality of the attribute.

## The Basilisk in the Traditions of the Forest

The basilisk occupies a singular place in the traditions of the forest communities, and the creature is the subject of a body of lore that is among the most elaborate of the cautionary traditions of the woodland. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the deep forest hold that the basilisk is a creature of the old wood and that the gaze of the basilisk is a thing that the elves have learned to avoid by the use of mirrors of polished silver, and the elven wardens of the deep forest are reputed to be able to lead a party through the territory of a basilisk without disturbing the creature. The [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the forest margins regard the basilisk with a dread that is not accorded to any other beast of the woodland, and the forester who has killed a basilisk is honoured by his community with a title that is second to none among the orders of the chase.

The hunting of the basilisk is a recognised pursuit of the forest communities, and the hunters of the deep wood take a small number of basilisks each year under licence from the local authorities. The hunt is conducted with the heavy spear or with the crossbow, and the hunter who engages a basilisk must have the skill and the courage to bring the creature down before it can close the distance, for the strike of the basilisk is a thing of considerable speed and the venom of the bite is lethal in the greater part of cases. The chronicler notes that the basilisk is not held in any particular reverence by the communities of the forest, for the creature is regarded as a hazard of the deep wood and not as a symbol of the wildness of the forest, and the basilisk is hunted with the same practical purpose that the foresters bring to the hunting of the wolf and the bear.

## Significance

The basilisk is the foremost of the venomous reptiles of the deep forest, and the presence of the creature in the wooded interior of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the deep wood. The basilisk is the predator that keeps the populations of the smaller mammals of the forest in balance with the carrying capacity of the woodland, and the loss of the basilisk from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the forest that the chroniclers have noted in the records of the forest communities. The chronicler notes that the basilisk endures, in the deep forest and the broken cave country of the three continents and in the cautionary lore of the foresters who share the world with the creature, as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the deep wood, and the musty scent of the lair upon the still air of the forest is a reminder that the interior of the wood is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
