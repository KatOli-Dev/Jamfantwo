## Overview

The griffin is a great beast of the open country, a creature of eagle and of lion that the naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) have classified among the most striking of the predators of the grasslands. The griffin takes its name from the combination of the foreparts of the great eagle and the hindparts of the lion that the creature displays in its conformation, and the animal is held in a particular esteem by the mounted peoples of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) and the [Sunfire Steppe](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sunfire-steppe), who regard the griffin as the foremost of the beasts of the open country. The chronicler notes that the griffin is not a creature of the dense forest or the high mountain, for the animal requires the open ground for the hunt and the cliffs of the river valleys for the eyrie, and the griffin is found in greatest numbers in the country of the rolling plains that border the interior mountains of the three continents.

## Form and Appearance

The griffin is a creature of considerable size, with a body that is set upon four powerful legs and a head that is the head of the great eagle. The forequarters of the griffin are feathered in the manner of the eagle, the plumage being a tawny gold upon the head and the neck and shading to a darker brown across the back and the wings. The wings are broad and powerful, and the griffin is capable of a strong and sustained flight, though the creature does not soar at the great heights that are attained by the [stormwing roc](/content/species/beasts/stormwing-roc) of the mountain ranges. The hindquarters of the griffin are those of the lion, with a short tawny coat and a long tail that ends in a tuft of dark hair, and the hind legs are armed with the retractable claws that are characteristic of the great cats.

The head of the griffin is set with a hooked beak of considerable strength, and the beak is used for the tearing of the prey in the manner of the eagle. The eyes are keen and far-sighted, and the griffin is able to detect the movement of a creature upon the ground from a height that would render it invisible to the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) eye. The ears are small and pointed and are set upon the sides of the head, and the hearing of the griffin is acute. The size of the griffin varies with the subspecies and the range, but a mature male may stand as high as a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) at the shoulder, and the wingspan of the same animal is sufficient to carry the creature aloft with a considerable load of prey in the talons.

## Habitat and Range

The griffin inhabits the open country of the three continents, with the largest populations in the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the [Sunfire Steppe](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sunfire-steppe) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and a smaller population in the rolling country of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) that borders the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands). The griffin requires the open ground for the hunt and the cliffs of the river valleys for the eyrie, and the creature is not found in the dense forest or in the high mountain. The eyrie is typically situated upon a ledge of a cliff that overlooks the open country, and the griffin is found in greatest numbers in the country where the cliffs and the open ground are found in close association.

The griffin ranges widely from the eyrie in search of the prey that sustains it, and a single pair may cover many miles in a day as they hunt upon the open ground. The griffin is a social animal, and the creatures are found in pairs or in small family groups, the male and the female hunting together and sharing the duties of the rearing of the young. The pairs hold territories that they mark by the scratches of the great talons upon the rocks and by the scent that they leave upon the prominent outcrops, and the territories of the mated pairs are defended against the intrusion of other griffins with a vigour that the mounted peoples of the plains have learned to respect.

## Diet and Behaviour

The griffin is a predator of considerable power, and the creature takes a wide range of prey according to the opportunity. The principal prey of the griffin is the larger mammals of the open country, including the deer and the antelope of the plains, and a mated pair will take several such animals in the course of a week. The griffin is also a predator of the smaller mammals of the open ground, and the creature will take the hares and the ground squirrels when the larger prey is not available. The griffin does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) or the horses of the mounted peoples, but the accounts of the plains include a number of incidents in which a griffin has taken a foal or a young calf that has strayed from the protection of the herd.

The hunting technique of the griffin is to quarter the open ground at a height that is sufficient to allow the keen eyes to detect the movement of the prey, and the griffin descends upon the prey with a swift and powerful stoop. The prey is seized in the talons of the forefeet and borne to a sheltered spot upon the ground, and the prey is consumed upon the ground in the manner of the great cats. The griffin is also an active hunter upon the ground, and the creature will pursue a wounded antelope across the open plain with a speed that is considerable for a beast of its size. The hunting of the griffin is most often conducted by the mated pair together, the one flushing the prey from the cover and the other descending upon it from the air, and the cooperation of the pair in the hunt is the admiration of the naturalists who have observed the creatures in the country of the plains.

## The Knights of the Griffon

The mounted peoples of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) have a long acquaintance with the griffin, and the creature occupies a prominent place in the traditions of the horse-peoples of the open country. The griffin is the symbol of the hunt and of the speed of the plain, and the warriors of the mounted peoples take the feathers of the griffin as a token of their prowess in the chase. The [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) of the northern coast has at times maintained a company of knights who rode griffins into battle, the creatures having been taken from the eyrie and trained to the saddle, and the chroniclers of the northern courts have preserved accounts of the charges of the griffin cavalry that are among the most celebrated of the military traditions of the northern kingdoms.

The training of a griffin is a pursuit that requires considerable skill and patience, for the griffin is a creature of strong will and of considerable intelligence, and the training is conducted from the time that the creature is taken from the eyrie as a young bird. The griffin that has been trained to the saddle is a possession that is known only to the wealthiest of the highland lords and to the kings of the northern realms, and the cost of the training is reckoned in the labour of years and in the favour of the master of the aviary. The chronicler notes that the practice of the griffin cavalry has fallen out of use in the present age, for the supply of the creatures is limited and the danger is great, and the last of the great companies of the northern kings is recorded to have been disbanded in the reign of the fourth king of the Nordhavic line.

## Significance

The griffin is the foremost of the predators of the open country, and the presence of the creature in the plains and the steppes of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the grass. The griffin is the predator that keeps the populations of the deer and the antelope in balance with the carrying capacity of the open ground, and the loss of the griffin from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the pasture that the chroniclers have noted in the records of the mounted peoples. The chronicler notes that the griffin endures, in the open country of the three continents and in the traditions of the mounted peoples who share the world with the creature, as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the plains, and the shadow of the griffin upon the grass is a reminder that the open ground is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
