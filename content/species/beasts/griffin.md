## The Winged Predator of the Open Country

The griffin is a great beast of the open country, a creature of eagle and of lion that naturalists of the academies of [Amurra](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) have classified among the most striking predators of the grasslands. It takes its name from the combination of the foreparts of the great eagle and the hindparts of the lion that it displays in its conformation. The creature is held in particular esteem by the mounted peoples of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) and the [Sunfire Steppe](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sunfire-steppe), who regard it as the foremost beast of the open country. The griffin requires open ground for the hunt and the cliffs of river valleys for the eyrie; it is found in greatest numbers in the rolling plains that border the interior mountains of the three continents.

## Eagle Foreparts and Lion Hindquarters

The griffin is a creature of considerable size, with a body set upon four powerful legs and a head that is the head of the great eagle. The forequarters are feathered in the manner of the eagle, the plumage tawny gold upon the head and neck shading to darker brown across the back and wings. The wings are broad and powerful; the griffin is capable of strong and sustained flight, though it does not soar at the great heights attained by the [stormwing roc](/content/species/beasts/stormwing-roc) of the mountain ranges. The hindquarters are those of the lion, with a short tawny coat and a long tail ending in a tuft of dark hair; the hind legs are armed with the retractable claws characteristic of the great cats.

The head is set with a hooked beak of considerable strength, used for the tearing of prey in the manner of the eagle. The eyes are keen and far-sighted; the griffin can detect the movement of a creature upon the ground from a height that would render it invisible to the [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) eye. The ears are small and pointed, set upon the sides of the head; the hearing is acute. Size varies with subspecies and range, but a mature male may stand as high as a [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) at the shoulder; the wingspan is sufficient to carry the creature aloft with a considerable load of prey in the talons.

## Plains, Cliffs, and Eyries

The griffin inhabits the open country of the three continents, with the largest populations in the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the [Sunfire Steppe](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sunfire-steppe) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and a smaller population in the rolling country of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) bordering the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands). It requires open ground for the hunt and cliffs of river valleys for the eyrie; it is not found in dense forest or high mountain. The eyrie is typically situated upon a ledge of a cliff overlooking open country; griffins are found in greatest numbers where cliffs and open ground occur in close association.

The griffin ranges widely from the eyrie in search of prey; a single pair may cover many miles in a day hunting upon open ground. It is a social animal, found in pairs or small family groups; the male and female hunt together and share the duties of rearing the young. Pairs hold territories marked by scratches of great talons upon rocks and by scent left upon prominent outcrops; territories are defended against intrusion by other griffins with a vigour that the mounted peoples have learned to respect.

## Mated Hunters of the Grasslands

The griffin is a predator of considerable power that takes a wide range of prey according to opportunity. The principal prey is the larger mammals of the open country, including deer and antelope of the plains; a mated pair will take several such animals in the course of a week. It is also a predator of smaller mammals, taking hares and ground squirrels when larger prey is not available. The griffin does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) or the horses of the mounted peoples, but accounts of the plains include incidents in which a griffin has taken a foal or young calf that has strayed from the protection of the herd.

The hunting technique is to quarter the open ground at a height sufficient to allow the keen eyes to detect the movement of prey, then descend in a swift and powerful stoop. The prey is seized in the talons of the forefeet and borne to a sheltered spot to be consumed upon the ground in the manner of the great cats. The griffin is also an active hunter upon the ground, pursuing a wounded antelope across the open plain with considerable speed. Hunting is most often conducted by the mated pair together, one flushing the prey from cover and the other descending from the air; the cooperation of the pair in the hunt is admired by naturalists.

## Trained to the Saddle

The mounted peoples of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) have a long acquaintance with the griffin; the creature occupies a prominent place in the traditions of the horse-peoples of the open country. It is the symbol of the hunt and of the speed of the plain; warriors take the feathers of the griffin as a token of their prowess in the chase. [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) of the northern coast has at times maintained a company of knights who rode griffins into battle, the creatures having been taken from the eyrie and trained to the saddle; chroniclers of the northern courts have preserved accounts of the charges of the griffin cavalry that are among the most celebrated of the military traditions of the northern kingdoms.

The training of a griffin requires considerable skill and patience, for the griffin is a creature of strong will and considerable intelligence; training is conducted from the time the creature is taken from the eyrie as a young bird. A griffin trained to the saddle is a possession known only to the wealthiest of the highland lords and the kings of the northern realms; the cost is reckoned in the labour of years and in the favour of the master of the aviary. The practice of the griffin cavalry has fallen out of use in the present age; the supply of creatures is limited and the danger is great, and the last of the great companies is recorded to have been disbanded in the reign of the fourth king of the Nordhavic line.

## Emblem of the Open Country

The griffin is the foremost predator of the open country, and its presence in the plains and steppes of the three continents is a measure of the wildness that endures in the country of the grass. It is the predator that keeps the populations of deer and antelope in balance with the carrying capacity of the open ground; the loss of the griffin from any district would be followed by a deterioration of the pasture. The griffin endures in the open country of the three continents and in the traditions of the mounted peoples who share the world with the creature as a symbol of the wildness that persists in the country of the plains. The shadow of the griffin upon the grass is a reminder that the open ground is not empty of life nor subject to the order of the settled lands beyond.
