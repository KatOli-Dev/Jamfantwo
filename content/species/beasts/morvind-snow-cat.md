## Overview

The [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) snow cat is the apex predator of the alpine zone of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands), a great feline of pure white coat and considerable bulk whose range extends across the full extent of the high mountains of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The cat is solitary and elusive, seldom seen by the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) and [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) who inhabit the valleys and the lower slopes, and its presence in the high country is known chiefly through the tracks that it leaves in the snow and the occasional loss of livestock from the valley farms. The chronicler notes that the snow cat is held in a peculiar reverence by the mountain peoples, who regard the creature as the spirit of the high peaks made flesh, and the killing of a snow cat is forbidden by the customary law of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) and the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) alike.

## Form and Appearance

The snow cat is a powerfully built feline, larger than any of the lowland cats of the known world, with a body that is broad through the chest and set on legs of moderate length that are nevertheless capable of remarkable leaps among the rocks of the high country. The head is broad and rounded, the ears short and tufted, and the eyes a pale blue-grey that is adapted to the brilliant light of the snowfields. The tail is long and thick, nearly as long as the body itself, and it is used as a counterweight in the descent of the steep slopes.

The coat of the snow cat is its most distinctive feature, and it changes with the seasons in a manner that is closely matched to the background of the high country. In the winter the coat is a dense and pure white, with a slight silvering along the flanks and the back that breaks the outline of the cat against the snow. In the summer the coat is shorter and a pale grey, with faint darker markings along the shoulders and the haunches that match the colour of the exposed rock of the alpine meadows. The winter coat is among the most valuable of the mountain furs, and the pelts of the snow cat are the prized possessions of the noble houses of the mountain kingdoms, who pay handsomely for the rare specimens that are taken by the trappers of the high valleys. The trapping of the snow cat is, however, strictly limited by the customary law of the mountain communities, and the sale of the pelts is permitted only in the case of cats that have been killed in defence of livestock or in self-defence.

## Habitat and Range

The snow cat inhabits the alpine zone of the Morvind Uplands, the region above the treeline where the mountain grasses and the flowering herbs of the high meadows give way to the rock and the permanent snow. The cat ranges from the high meadows of the summer to the lower slopes of the winter, following the prey that migrate between the elevations with the seasons, and a single cat may hold a territory that extends across many miles of broken country. The territory is marked by the scratches of the great claws upon the rocks and by the scent that the cat leaves upon the prominent boulders, and the boundaries of the territory are respected by other cats of the same sex, who will avoid a marked ground except in the season of the mating.

The cat prefers the broken country of the ridges and the cirques, where the rock provides a cover for the stalk and the open meadows beyond provide the grazing for the prey. The deep snow of the high valleys is avoided in the winter, for the bulk of the cat is such that it sinks deeply into the soft snow, and the cat moves to the wind-scoured slopes where the snow is thin and the prey is more easily overtaken. The summer range extends higher, into the meadows where the mountain goats graze, and the cat will lie upon a rocky outcrop above a game trail and wait for hours for the opportunity to take a kid or a yearling.

## Diet and Hunting

The principal prey of the snow cat is the mountain goat of the Morvind Uplands, a sure-footed herbivore whose populations fluctuate with the severity of the winter and the availability of the grazing in the summer. The cat stalks the goat by approaching from above, where its white coat blends with the snow and the broken rock, and it takes the prey by a sudden rush from a short distance, the great forepaw breaking the neck of the goat with a single blow. The cat is capable of carrying a young goat up the steepest slope to a sheltered ledge where the carcass may be eaten at leisure, and an adult male is capable of taking a goat of considerable size, though the cat will not attempt a full-grown billy unless the snow favours the stalk.

When the goat is scarce, the cat takes the smaller mammals of the high country, including the alpine hare and the marmot, and it will also take the ptarmigan and the grouse of the high meadows. The cat occasionally descends into the forests of the lower slopes in the winter, where it preys upon the red deer and the wild boar, and the occasional loss of a calf from the highland farms is laid to the snow cat that has been forced down from the mountains by the depth of the snow. The cat does not ordinarily attack [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans), though there are records of attacks upon solitary travellers in the high passes, and the mountain peoples regard an unprovoked attack by a snow cat as an omen of ill fortune.

## Life Cycle

The snow cat is a long-lived animal for a creature of its size, with a typical lifespan of fifteen to twenty years in the wild. The cubs are born in the late spring, usually in a den among the rocks that is lined with the fur that the mother has shed from her own coat. The litter is small, typically two or three cubs, and the cubs remain with the mother through the summer and the autumn, learning the hunting grounds and the techniques of the stalk. The young cats disperse in the late winter of their second year, and the males range widely in search of a territory that they may claim against the established adults.

The adults are solitary outside the brief season of the mating, which falls in the late winter, when the males and the females come together for a few days before the cats separate again to the solitary ranges. The fights between the males for the possession of a female in season are conducted with great violence, and the scars upon the face and the forequarters of an old male are the record of many such contests. The successful male remains with the female for the period of her receptivity and then departs, taking no further part in the rearing of the cubs.

## The Mountain Peoples and the Cat

The mountain peoples of Morvind regard the snow cat with a mixture of reverence and wary respect, and the customary law of the highland communities forbids the killing of a snow cat except in the case of an unprovoked attack upon livestock or upon a person. The prohibition is enforced by the councils of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) and the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), and the fines that are levied upon a transgressor are among the heaviest of the customary penalties. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) do not hunt the cat, for their traditions hold that the snow cat is a creature of the high places that the dwarves do not claim, and the dwarven mountain halls are designed with the avoidance of the cat in mind, the lower entrances being kept clear of the scent that would otherwise attract the great predator.

The trappers of the highland valleys do take a small number of snow cats each year, under licence from the councils, and the pelts that are recovered are among the most valuable of the mountain furs. The licence is granted only in the case of a cat that has been killed in defence of livestock, and the trapper is required to present the carcass to the council before the pelt may be sold. The meat of the cat is not eaten, for the mountain peoples hold that the flesh of so noble a creature is not to be consumed, and the carcass is burned upon a pyre of mountain wood in accordance with the ancient custom.

## Significance

The Morvind snow cat is the spirit of the high places and the unrivalled hunter of the alpine meadows, and its presence in the mountains of the northern continent is a measure of the health of the high country. The cat is the predator that keeps the populations of the mountain goat in balance with the carrying capacity of the meadows, and the loss of the cat from any district is followed by a deterioration of the pasture that the chroniclers have noted in the records of the mountain communities. The reverence that the mountain peoples accord to the cat is a measure of the importance of the predator to the order of the high country, and the protection of the cat by the customary law is one of the oldest of the conservation traditions of the known world.
