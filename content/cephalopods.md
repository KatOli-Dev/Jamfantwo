## Origins

The origins of the cephalopods are among the most alien of any thinking people, for the cephalopod body is unlike that of any other species and their habitat is the most remote and inaccessible of any sapient people. Cephalopods are a tentacled people, found on the deep ocean floor of the [Auric Ocean](/content/auric-ocean) and the [Greyring Ocean](/content/greyring-ocean), and their bodies are adapted to a life in the extreme conditions of the abyssal depths. Some naturalists have proposed a distant kinship between cephalopods and [merfolk](/content/merfolk), citing the aquatic adaptation shared by both peoples, but the differences are too great for the comparison to be sustained, for the cephalopods are creatures of the deep sea rather than the shallows, and their bodies are entirely unlike the humanoid form of the merfolk. The cephalopods themselves are almost wholly unknown to the surface peoples, and what little is known of their origins comes from the rare encounters between the cephalopods and the merfolk of the deep waters.

What is certain is that cephalopods have inhabited the deep ocean floor for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in the abyssal depths is attested in the oldest oral traditions of the merfolk. The cephalopods and the merfolk share the ocean, but the two peoples inhabit different worlds, for the merfolk are creatures of the light and the shallows, while the cephalopods are creatures of the darkness and the deep.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Cephalopods are a large people, and their form is immediately recognisable as cephalopod. The body is soft and muscular, divided into a large, domed head and a ring of eight tentacled arms that surround the mouth. The head houses the brain, the eyes, and the organs of sense, and it is covered in a smooth, moist skin that is capable of extraordinary changes of colour and texture. The arms are long, powerful, and flexible, equipped with rows of suckers that are capable of gripping, tasting, and the fine manipulation of objects. The whole body is supported not by bone but by a combination of muscular hydrostats and a rigid internal pen, and the cephalopod is capable of altering the shape and the stiffness of its body to suit the demands of the moment.

The cephalopod body is superbly adapted to the deep ocean environment. Their skin is capable of extraordinary colour changes, produced by arrays of pigment cells that can expand and contract in milliseconds, and the cephalopods use this ability for both communication and camouflage, their bodies rippling with patterns that convey information or blend seamlessly with the surrounding seafloor. Their eyes are the largest of any sapient people, and they are extraordinarily sensitive, adapted to the near-total darkness of the abyssal depths, capable of detecting the faintest traces of light, including the bioluminescence of the deep-sea organisms that share their habitat.

Cephalopods breathe through gills, and their bodies are adapted to the enormous pressures of the deep sea, with flexible tissues and gas-filled spaces that can compress without damage. They move by a combination of tentacled crawling across the seafloor and jet propulsion through the water, expelling water from a muscular funnel to drive themselves forward with a speed that is remarkable for a creature of the deep. Their sense of touch is extraordinary, for each of the suckers on their arms is capable of tasting and feeling independently, and the cephalopod can explore and manipulate eight objects simultaneously with a precision that no other people can match.

## Life Cycle

Cephalopods are short to moderately long-lived, with a lifespan that is difficult to estimate, as no naturalist has been able to study a cephalopod community in its natural environment, but the merfolk accounts suggest a lifespan of forty to sixty years. They mature quickly, reaching full size and physical adulthood within five to eight years. Cephalopod women lay clutches of several thousand eggs in protected crevices on the deep seafloor, and the eggs are guarded and tended by the mother until they hatch, a period that may last many months. The hatchlings are small but fully formed, miniature versions of the adult, and they are capable of swimming and feeding from the moment of hatching.

Cephalopod populations are difficult to assess, for the deep ocean floor is vast and the cephalopods are widely dispersed, but the merfolk who have encountered them report that their communities are small and widely separated, each occupying a territory on the seafloor that may span many miles of the abyssal plain. The mortality rate among the young is high, for the deep sea is a harsh environment and the hatchlings are vulnerable to predation by the larger organisms of the deep, but the enormous number of eggs ensures that enough survive to sustain the population.

## Habitat and Distribution

Cephalopods are found on the deep ocean floor of the [Auric Ocean](/content/auric-ocean) and the [Greyring Ocean](/content/greyring-ocean), at depths that no other sapient people can reach, and their habitat is the most remote and inaccessible of any in the known world. They favour the rocky outcrops, the canyon walls, and the thermal vents of the abyssal floor, where the hard surfaces provide shelter and the organisms of the deep sea provide food. Their settlements are built in the crevices and the caves of the deep seafloor, constructed from the materials that the abyssal environment provides, and they are situated where the currents bring food and the terrain offers protection.

The [geography](/content/geography) of the deep ocean floor is almost entirely unknown to the surface peoples, and the distribution of cephalopod populations can only be inferred from the rare encounters reported by the merfolk and the occasional discovery of cephalopod remains in the nets of deep-water fishermen. Cephalopods are absent from the shallows, the coasts, and the surface, for their bodies are adapted to the enormous pressures and the total darkness of the deep, and they cannot survive in the low-pressure, sunlit waters near the surface.

## Diet

The cephalopod diet is dominated by the organisms of the deep sea, and cephalopods are formidable predators in the abyssal environment they inhabit. They prey upon the fish, crustaceans, and other deep-sea creatures that share their habitat, and their tentacled arms and suckers are well suited to the capture and the consumption of prey in the darkness of the deep. The hunt is conducted by ambush and by active pursuit, and the cephalopod's ability to alter its colour and texture allows it to approach prey undetected on the seafloor before launching a swift attack with its arms.

The cephalopod digestive system is robust and well adapted to a diet of raw marine foods, and the cephalopods consume their prey raw, for fire is impossible in the deep sea. Their beak-like jaws are strong enough to crack the shells of deep-sea crustaceans and to tear the flesh of the larger fish, and their digestion is efficient, extracting nourishment from the sparse and often poor-quality food that the deep sea provides. The cephalopod approach to food is that of a patient and opportunistic predator, for the deep ocean is a place of scarcity, and the cephalopod must take what it can find when it can find it.

## Ecology

Cephalopods occupy the niche of a large-bodied, intelligent predator on the deep ocean floor, and their impact on the ecology of the abyssal depths is significant, for the cephalopods are among the largest and most capable organisms of the deep sea. Their hunting regulates the populations of the deep-sea creatures that share their habitat, and their presence on the seafloor is a stabilising force in an ecosystem that is governed by scarcity and the slow descent of organic matter from the sunlit waters above.

Cephalopods have almost no contact with other sapient peoples, for their habitat is separated from the world of the surface peoples by miles of water and the enormous pressure of the deep. [Merfolk](/content/merfolk) are the only other sapient people who inhabit the ocean, and the merfolk of the deep waters occasionally encounter the cephalopods at the margins of their respective ranges, where the shallows meet the deep. These encounters are rare and generally cautious, for the two peoples are too different in form and in habitat for either to benefit from sustained contact, and each regards the other with a mixture of curiosity and wariness.
