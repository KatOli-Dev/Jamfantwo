## Overview

Coldmoor is a settlement on the cold steppes of the interior of [Morvind](/content/morvind), situated on a stretch of open grassland that lies between the interior ranges and the foothills of the far north. The settlement is the only permanent community on the cold steppes, and its people are among the most adapted to the extreme conditions of the interior. Coldmoor is a small, self-sufficient settlement, and its primary function is the management of the pastoral economy that sustains the steppe and the maintenance of the routes that cross the open country.

## The Steppes and the Pastures

The cold steppes of the Morvind interior are a vast expanse of open grassland that stretches from the interior ranges to the foothills of the north, and the grass that covers the steppe is among the hardiest in the known world, capable of surviving the deep freezes and the short growing seasons that characterise the region. The pastures that surround Coldmoor are grazed by the herds of hardy cattle and the flocks of mountain sheep that are the primary resource of the settlement, and the wool, the meat, and the leather that are produced from these animals are the main exports of the community. The cattle of the cold steppes are a distinctive breed, short-legged and thick-bodied, with a coat that grows dense and long during the winter months, and the meat of these animals is noted for its richness and its flavour.

The climate of the steppe is the most demanding of any inhabited region in Morvind, and the temperature can drop to levels that would be fatal to an unprepared person. The people of Coldmoor have adapted to the conditions over many generations, and their clothing, their dwellings, and their way of life are shaped by the demands of the cold. The steppe is covered in snow for much of the year, and the settlement is isolated during the winter months, when the routes that connect it to the interior are blocked by snow and the cold. The winter dwellings of Coldmoor are constructed with an inner lining of hides and felt that provides an additional layer of insulation, and the hearths that heat the buildings are fed with the dried dung of the cattle, for firewood is a scarce resource on the treeless steppe.

## The Routes and the Lupines

The routes that cross the cold steppes are the primary connection between the interior ranges and the foothills of the north, and the maintenance of these routes is a key function of the Coldmoor community. The routes are navigable only during the summer months, when the snow retreats and the ground becomes firm enough for the passage of animals and wagons. The trade that flows through the routes is modest but vital, for the settlement is dependent on the import of the goods that it cannot produce for itself, including the metalwork and the manufactured goods that are produced in the interior ranges and the coast.

The [lupines](/content/lupines) of the cold steppes are the primary inhabitants of the region, and the relationship between the settled community of Coldmoor and the nomadic lupine kindreds is one of cautious co-operation and trade. The lupines provide the fur and the meat of the steppe in exchange for the metalwork, the cloth, and the manufactured goods that the settlement imports from the interior, and the exchange is conducted at the settlement during the summer months, when the lupine kindreds pass through the region on their seasonal migrations. The lupine kindreds follow the herds of the wild steppe animals that are their primary prey, and the migrations of the kindreds are dictated by the movements of the herds and the cycle of the seasons. The summer gathering at Coldmoor is one of the few occasions when the lupine kindreds and the settled humans meet in a peaceful context, and the exchange that takes place is valued by both peoples.

The routes themselves are marked by the cairns that the people of Coldmoor have built at intervals along the way, for the steppe is a featureless expanse and the traveller who loses the route in a snowstorm or a fog is in grave danger. The cairns are maintained by the community, and the rebuilding of the cairns that have been damaged by the winter weather is one of the first tasks of the spring, when the people of Coldmoor venture out after the long confinement of the winter months. The routes are also marked by the shelters that are constructed at the points where a traveller might need to take refuge, and the shelters are stocked with provisions and fuel by the community, a practice that has saved the lives of many a traveller who has been caught on the steppe in unexpected weather.

## The Settlement

Coldmoor is a small, compact settlement, and its buildings are constructed of the stone that is gathered from the foothills and the timber that is imported from the interior, with walls that are thick and roofs that are weighted with stone and earth to provide insulation against the cold. The buildings are low and close-set, and the streets are narrow and sheltered, for the wind on the steppe is fierce and the cold is intense. The population of Coldmoor is chiefly [human](/content/humans), with a small community of [dwarves](/content/dwarves) who are involved in the metal trade and the maintenance of the routes. The dwarves of Coldmoor are skilled in the repair of the metal tools and the implements that the settlement depends upon, and their forge is one of the few sources of warmth in the settlement that is not dependent on the burning of dung.

The community of Coldmoor is organised around the pastoral economy, and the herds and the flocks are the collective responsibility of the settlement. The grazing lands are divided among the families of the community, and the management of the pastures is co-ordinated by a council of the household heads who meet in the common hall that is the centre of the settlement. The common hall is the largest building in Coldmoor, and it serves as the meeting place, the storehouse, and the refuge of the community during the worst of the winter storms, when the families gather there for warmth and for the sharing of the provisions that have been laid by for the season.

## Significance

Coldmoor is the only permanent settlement on the cold steppes of the Morvind interior, and its people are the guardians of the routes that cross the open country. The settlement is a dependency of the [Valdmark](/content/valdmark), and its pastoral products are a valued contribution to the economy of the interior. The significance of Coldmoor lies in its survival and its productivity in an environment that is among the most demanding in the known world, and the settlement is a point of contact between the settled peoples of the interior and the nomadic lupines of the steppe. The wool and the leather that are produced at Coldmoor are recognised throughout Morvind for their quality, and the hardy cattle of the steppe are prized as breeding stock by the pastoral communities of the lower regions.
