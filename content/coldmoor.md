## Overview

Coldmoor is a settlement on the cold steppes of the interior of [Morvind](/content/morvind), situated on a stretch of open grassland that lies between the interior ranges and the foothills of the far north. The settlement is the only permanent community on the cold steppes, and its people are among the most adapted to the extreme conditions of the interior. Coldmoor is a small, self-sufficient settlement, and its primary function is the management of the pastoral economy that sustains the steppe and the maintenance of the routes that cross the open country.

## The Steppes and the Pastures

The cold steppes of the Morvind interior are a vast expanse of open grassland that stretches from the interior ranges to the foothills of the north, and the grass that covers the steppe is among the hardiest in the known world, capable of surviving the deep freezes and the short growing seasons that characterise the region. The pastures that surround Coldmoor are grazed by the herds of hardy cattle and the flocks of mountain sheep that are the primary resource of the settlement, and the wool, the meat, and the leather that are produced from these animals are the main exports of the community.

The climate of the steppe is the most demanding of any inhabited region in Morvind, and the temperature can drop to levels that would be fatal to an unprepared person. The people of Coldmoor have adapted to the conditions over many generations, and their clothing, their dwellings, and their way of life are shaped by the demands of the cold. The steppe is covered in snow for much of the year, and the settlement is isolated during the winter months, when the routes that connect it to the interior are blocked by snow and the cold.

## The Routes and the Lupines

The routes that cross the cold steppes are the primary connection between the interior ranges and the foothills of the north, and the maintenance of these routes is a key function of the Coldmoor community. The routes are navigable only during the summer months, when the snow retreats and the ground becomes firm enough for the passage of animals and wagons. The trade that flows through the routes is modest but vital, for the settlement is dependent on the import of the goods that it cannot produce for itself, including the metalwork and the manufactured goods that are produced in the interior ranges and the coast.

The [lupines](/content/lupines) of the cold steppes are the primary inhabitants of the region, and the relationship between the settled community of Coldmoor and the nomadic lupine kindreds is one of cautious co-operation and trade. The lupines provide the fur and the meat of the steppe in exchange for the metalwork, the cloth, and the manufactured goods that the settlement imports from the interior, and the exchange is conducted at the settlement during the summer months, when the lupine kindreds pass through the region on their seasonal migrations.

## The Settlement

Coldmoor is a small, compact settlement, and its buildings are constructed of the stone that is gathered from the foothills and the timber that is imported from the interior, with walls that are thick and roofs that are weighted with stone and earth to provide insulation against the cold. The buildings are low and close-set, and the streets are narrow and sheltered, for the wind on the steppe is fierce and the cold is intense. The population of Coldmoor is chiefly [human](/content/humans), with a small community of [dwarves](/content/dwarves) who are involved in the metal trade and the maintenance of the routes.

## Significance

Coldmoor is the only permanent settlement on the cold steppes of the Morvind interior, and its people are the guardians of the routes that cross the open country. The settlement is a dependency of the [Valdmark](/content/valdmark), and its pastoral products are a valued contribution to the economy of the interior. The significance of Coldmoor lies in its survival and its productivity in an environment that is among the most demanding in the known world, and the settlement is a point of contact between the settled peoples of the interior and the nomadic lupines of the steppe.
