## Two Reckonings, One World

The chronicles of the known world are not dated by a single universal calendar, and a reader moving between the archives of different nations must know which reckoning a given chronicle uses before its dates can be placed in sequence. Two systems account for nearly every date cited in the surviving chronicles: Republic Reckoning, the standard adopted by the academies and chancelleries of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and the Post-Founding count, an older system still used in [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and particularly in the north. A handful of institutions, most notably the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), also keep an internal era of their own for ceremonial purposes, alongside the Republic year by which their chronicles are cross-referenced to the rest of the known world.

## Republic Reckoning

Republic Reckoning dates events from the founding of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), and despite its origin in a single city-state it has become the standard by which the academies of Aethelar, the Sahrani coast, and the city-states that trade with them cross-reference their chronicles. Its adoption as a shared standard, rather than a purely Amurran one, reflects the long reach of the Republic's spice trade and the seniority of its academies: a scholar of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) or [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) dating a treatise by the Republic year can expect it to be understood in any academy of the three continents, whereas the regnal years of any single kingdom would mean nothing beyond that kingdom's own chronicles.

Republic Reckoning had already been in use for centuries before [Sera Ambervale](/content/people/historical/sera-ambervale) unified the central river valleys and founded the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael); her coronation, dated by the chroniclers of the Vael as the first year of the Vaelic era, corresponds to a year in the middle of the third century of Republic Reckoning. The [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic)'s own First Merchants are not numbered by regnal year in the manner of a monarchy, since the office changes hands by election rather than succession, but the Republic's chronicles record every First Merchant's term against the Republic year in which it began, which is how the tenure of [Ilena Amurra](/content/people/historical/ilena-amurra) is placed in relation to the [Twin-Reaches Wars](/content/history/twin-reaches-wars) and the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace) that followed. [Aldric Vael](/content/people/notable/aldric-vael), the present ruler of the Vael, acceded to the throne in the nine hundred and eleventh year of the Republic, and the present year is conventionally reckoned as somewhere in the middle of the tenth century of Republic Reckoning — the chronicles of the present age rarely give the precise year, in keeping with the convention of dating recent events by reign or generation rather than by a number that will need revision within a chronicler's own lifetime.

## The Post-Founding Count

The Post-Founding count, abbreviated PF in the surviving records, is the older reckoning of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and especially of the northern region of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim), dating from a founding event so far in the past that even the northern chroniclers disagree on precisely what it commemorates — the founding of the first permanent settlement on the Stormfall coast, according to some; the founding of the earliest dwarven hold of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands), according to others. Whatever its origin, the count has run continuously and without the resets that have periodically shortened other regional calendars, so that PF years now run into the high forty-nine-hundreds: the [Stormfall Pilots' Guild](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) was founded in 4100 PF, the deepest sounding of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) was taken in 4878 PF, and the most recent survey expedition into its western fog banks, led by Captain Eirik Steadfast, was turned back in 4918 PF.

Scholars of Khalisar and Nordhaven have long attempted to fix a precise correlation between the Post-Founding count and Republic Reckoning, and several conversion tables circulate among the academies, but none commands universal acceptance; the difficulty lies in the scarcity of events attested in both systems at once; most chronicles of Morvind restrict themselves to the Post-Founding count throughout, and most chronicles of Aethelar and Sahrana never cite it at all. The academies' best estimate places the present PF year in the high 4900s, corresponding to the middle of the tenth century of Republic Reckoning, but the correlation is treated by careful scholars as an approximation rather than an established equivalence, and dates given in one system should not be assumed to translate precisely into the other without a documented conversion.

## Reading a Date

A reader encountering an undated event — one described only as belonging to the Age of Founding, the middle period, or the present age, in the manner of the [World Timeline](/content/history/world-timeline) — should take the qualitative placement as the more reliable guide, for the chroniclers who compiled these broader histories were themselves working from sources in both reckonings and preferred the safety of relative sequence to the false precision of a converted date. A reader encountering a bare number, by contrast, should first ask which reckoning the source uses: a number in the low thousands or under, appearing in a chronicle of the Vael, the Amurra Republic, the Southern Reach, or Valdmark, is almost certainly a Republic year; a number in the four-thousands, appearing in a chronicle of Morvind or the Stormfall Sea, is almost certainly a Post-Founding year. The two should never be subtracted from one another to calculate an interval, for they do not share an epoch.

## Why Two Calendars Persist

The persistence of two calendars, rather than the general adoption of one, is itself a subject the academies have studied. The academies of Khalisar argue that the Post-Founding count has survived in the north for the same reason that Republic Reckoning has survived in the south: each region's chronicles, treaties, and legal codes are already dated in the reckoning native to them, and the labour of converting centuries of archival material to a new standard has never been judged worth the benefit, particularly since the two regions trade and correspond with one another only occasionally compared with the volume of business each conducts within its own sphere. The merchants who carry goods across the Stormfall Sea have developed their own practical shorthand, dating cargo manifests and insurance contracts by the sailing season rather than by either calendar, a solution that sidesteps the question of conversion entirely for the purposes of commerce, whatever difficulties it leaves for the historian.

A minority of northern scholars, chiefly those trained at the academy that the [Stormfall Pilots' Guild](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) maintains at Nordheim, have argued that Morvind should adopt Republic Reckoning outright, on the grounds that the growing trade between the continents makes a single standard increasingly valuable and that the antiquity of the Post-Founding count is no longer a sufficient reason to preserve it. The proposal has found little support beyond the guild itself, for the chronicles, legal codes, and religious calendars of Morvind's oldest institutions are reckoned in Post-Founding years throughout, and a wholesale conversion would strand centuries of documents in a system no longer in active use. The debate recurs periodically in the correspondence between the northern and southern academies, without any prospect of early resolution.
