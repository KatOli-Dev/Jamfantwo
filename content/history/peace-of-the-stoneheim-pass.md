## The Treaty That Ended the Mountain War

The Peace of the [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) Pass is the name given by the chroniclers of the cold north and the warm coast to the treaty that concluded the [Stoneheim Pass War](/content/history/stoneheim-pass-war), the brief but sharp engagement between the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and a coalition of lowland princes. The peace was signed in the great hall of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) in the presence of the elders of the dwarven communities of the lower slopes and the magistrates of the principal towns of the lowlands, and it established the compromise that has governed the tolls of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass) to the present day.

The peace is one of the most consequential instruments in the history of the mountain and the lowlands, for it brought an end to the most recent major conflict fought at the approaches of the pass and established the arrangements that have sustained the trade between the cold north and the warm coast for several generations.

## The Negotiations

The negotiations that led to the Peace of the Stoneheim Pass were conducted in the great hall of Stoneheim, following the suspension of arms that the council of the lowland coalition had requested after the withdrawal of its levy from the approaches of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass). The negotiations were mediated by the elders of the dwarven communities of the lower slopes, who had remained neutral throughout the war and who were respected by both parties for their wisdom and their impartiality.

The negotiations were conducted with the urgency that the circumstances required, for the war had cost both parties heavily and neither could sustain a prolonged conflict. The [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) insisted upon the right to collect the tolls for the maintenance of the road and the watch-towers of the pass, basing their claim upon the ancient accord that the founders of the holds had made with the magistrates of the principal towns of the lowlands. The lowland coalition disputed the claim, arguing that the accord had been made before the coalition had been formed and that the coalition was not bound by an instrument to which it had not been a party.

## The Compromise

The mediators proposed a compromise that reduced the tolls that the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) had collected to a small transit fee shared between the holds and the magistrates of the principal towns of the lowlands. The fee was set at a rate sufficient to maintain the road and the watch-towers but not so high as to burden the trade that passed through the pass.

The compromise was accepted by the king of Stoneheim and by the council of the coalition, after each party had consulted with its constituents and satisfied itself that the compromise was the best that could be obtained. The peace was signed in the great hall of Stoneheim in the presence of the elders of the dwarven communities and the magistrates of the principal towns, and the ceremony was marked by a feast attended by the representatives of both parties.

## The Terms of the Peace

The Peace of the Stoneheim Pass established the transit fee for the use of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass) and the arrangements for the maintenance of the road and the watch-towers. The fee was set at a rate that was reviewed at regular intervals by the king of Stoneheim and the magistrates of the principal lowland towns and adjusted to reflect the costs of maintenance and the volume of trade.

The peace also established a joint commission of the holds and the lowland towns to oversee the maintenance of the road and the watch-towers and to resolve disputes arising from the use of the pass. The commission met annually at the great hall of Stoneheim and reported to the king and the magistrates on the state of the pass and the compliance of those who used it.

The peace further provided for the mutual defence of the pass against external threats, requiring the holds and the lowland towns to contribute warriors to a joint force that would protect the pass from raiders and invaders.

## The Implementation and Revision

The implementation of the peace was accomplished through the cooperation of the magistrates of the holds and the lowland towns, who worked together to repair the damage that the war had caused to the road and the watch-towers. The joint commission met for the first time in the year following the signing of the peace and began the work of overseeing the maintenance of the pass.

The peace has been revised from time to time to reflect changes in the volume of trade and the costs of maintenance, but the revisions have been few and carefully considered. The transit fee has become a substantial source of revenue for the holds and the principal towns alike, used to maintain the road, the watch-towers, and the quarters of the pass.

## The Significance of the Peace

The Peace of the Stoneheim Pass is remembered in the chronicles of the cold north and the warm coast as the foundation of the enduring peace that has prevailed between the holds and the lowlands since the conclusion of the [Stoneheim Pass War](/content/history/stoneheim-pass-war). The peace is regarded by the academies of both regions as a model of settlement achieved through patient negotiation, and it is commemorated in the ceremonies that both regions maintain.

The peace has also served as a model for the resolution of other disputes over the use of mountain passes and trade routes in other regions. The joint commission established by the peace has been studied by the magistrates of other passes, who have adopted similar institutions for the maintenance of roads and the resolution of disputes. The magistrates of the lowland principalities who negotiated the peace have been consulted by the representatives of other regions facing similar disputes, and the arrangements established by the peace have been adapted to the conditions of different passes and different trade routes.
