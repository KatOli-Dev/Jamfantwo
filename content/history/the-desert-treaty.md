## The Accord of the Sahrani Sands

The Desert Treaty is the principal instrument governing the relations between the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The treaty was negotiated in the middle period of the present age, after a century of conflict and competition between the two powers over the control of the trade routes and the oases of the desert interior. The treaty established the terms of the relationship between the two powers and has remained in force, with revisions and renewals, for the centuries that followed.

The treaty is preserved in the archives of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) council of elders and in the hall of records of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) at [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari). The text is preserved in the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) script and in the common tongue of the coastal trade, and the two versions are considered equally authoritative. The academies of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) have studied the treaty and have produced a number of commentaries on its provisions and its significance.

## The Contest for the Desert

The century before the treaty was marked by a contest between the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) Pact for the control of the desert interior of Sahrana. The Kharesh Tribes were the older inhabitants of the desert, organised into a number of independent kindreds that controlled the oases and the trade routes of the interior. The Vashari Pact was a younger power, a confederation of city-states that had grown rich on the trade of the coast and that sought to extend its influence into the interior.

The contest was fought through a combination of diplomatic manoeuvres, economic pressure, and occasional armed conflict. The Kharesh Tribes sought to maintain their independence and to control the trade routes that passed through their territory. The Vashari Pact sought to secure access to the resources of the interior, including the salt, the spices, and the rare woods that the desert produced, and to establish a presence in the oases.

The conflict was not a continuous war but a series of skirmishes and confrontations that flared up and subsided over the years. The Kharesh kindreds were not united, and some kindreds made common cause with the Vashari Pact against their rivals. The Vashari Pact was also divided, with the member cities disagreeing about the wisdom of the expansion into the interior. The shifting alliances and the complex web of relationships made the conflict difficult to resolve.

## The Negotiations

The negotiations that led to the Desert Treaty were initiated by the mediators of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), who had a strong interest in the stability of the Sahrani trade. The merchants of the Republic had been trading with both the Kharesh Tribes and the Vashari Pact for several generations, and the disruption of the Sahrani trade was costing them money. The Republic offered to mediate a settlement and sent a delegation of experienced diplomats to Sahrana to conduct the negotiations.

The negotiations were held at a neutral site in the desert, a large oasis that was equidistant from the territories of the two powers. The site was chosen because it was accessible to both parties and because it was not claimed by either. The negotiations lasted for several months, with the mediators shuttling between the camps of the two parties to carry proposals and counter-proposals.

The principal issue was the control of the trade routes. The Kharesh Tribes claimed the right to regulate the trade that passed through their territory by virtue of their ancient occupation of the desert. The Vashari Pact claimed the right to trade freely by virtue of the natural liberty of commerce. The mediators proposed a compromise that would allow the Kharesh Tribes to collect a modest transit fee for goods passing through their territory while guaranteeing the Vashari merchants the right to travel and trade without hindrance.

## The Terms of the Treaty

The Desert Treaty established three principal provisions that have governed the relations between the Kharesh Tribes and the Vashari Pact ever since. The first provision defined the territories of the two powers. The Kharesh Tribes were confirmed in their control of the interior of the desert, including the principal oases and the trade routes that connected them. The Vashari Pact was confirmed in its control of the coast and the coastal cities. The boundary between the two territories was defined by a line drawn from the Cape of Storms in the west to the Mountains of the Sun in the east.

The second provision established the rules for trade and travel between the territories. The merchants of the Vashari Pact were granted the right to travel through the territory of the Kharesh Tribes and to trade at the oases, subject to the payment of a transit fee. The fee was set at a modest rate and was to be collected by the elders of the Kharesh kindreds. The merchants of the Kharesh Tribes were granted the same right to travel and trade in the territory of the Vashari Pact, with the same fee.

The third provision established a joint council to adjudicate disputes between the two powers. The council was composed of three elders from each side, appointed by the council of the Kharesh kindreds and by the council of the Vashari Pact. The council was to meet twice each year, once at the oasis where the treaty had been negotiated and once at a coastal city chosen by the Vashari Pact. The judgments of the council were binding upon both parties.

## The Ratification and the Aftermath

The treaty was ratified by the council of the Kharesh kindreds and by the council of the Vashari Pact in the same year. The ratification ceremonies were held at the oasis where the treaty had been negotiated, attended by the elders of both sides and by the mediators of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic). The chronicles record that the ceremonies were marked by the exchange of gifts and by the sharing of a feast prepared from the products of both the desert and the coast.

The aftermath of the treaty was a period of peace and prosperity for both powers. The trade between the Kharesh interior and the Vashari coast flourished, and the merchants of both sides profited from the increased commerce. The oases that had been the sites of conflict became the sites of trade and cooperation, and the caravans moved freely across the desert.

The joint council established by the treaty met regularly and heard a number of disputes in the years that followed. The council was generally effective in resolving the disputes, and there were few occasions on which either side refused to accept its judgments. The council became an established part of the governance of the Sahrani desert and contributed to the stability of the region.

## Revisions and Renewals

The Desert Treaty has been revised and renewed on several occasions over the centuries. The first revision occurred in the generation after the original signing, when the boundaries between the territories of the two powers were adjusted to reflect the changing patterns of settlement and the discovery of new oases. The second revision occurred in the early fifth century, when the transit fees were reduced in response to the complaints of the merchants of the Vashari Pact.

The third revision was the most substantial. It was negotiated in the late fifth century, after the [Sahrani Plague](/content/history/the-sahrani-plague) had devastated the coastal population and disrupted the trade of the region. The revision reduced the transit fees further, established a new procedure for the appointment of the joint council, and added a provision for the mutual assistance of the two powers in times of natural disaster.

The treaty was renewed in its current form in the early sixth century and has not been revised since. The provisions of the current version are substantially the same as those of the original, with the adjustments that have been made over the centuries to reflect the changing circumstances of the two powers.

## The Legacy of the Treaty

The Desert Treaty is one of the most successful instruments of the present age, having maintained the peace between the Kharesh Tribes and the Vashari Pact for several centuries. The treaty established the framework for the relationship between the two powers and provided the mechanisms for the resolution of disputes. The joint council, the transit fees, and the rules for trade and travel are all the legacy of the treaty.

The treaty also established a model for the relations between the desert peoples and the coastal powers. The principle of mutual recognition, the establishment of a joint council, and the regulation of trade through transit fees have been adopted in a number of subsequent treaties between the powers of Sahara and the powers of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar).

The memory of the treaty is preserved in the ceremonies of the Kharesh Tribes and the Vashari Pact. The anniversary of the ratification is marked by a joint ceremony at the oasis where the treaty was negotiated, attended by the elders of both sides and by the magistrates of the coastal cities. The ceremony includes the reading of the text of the treaty, the exchange of gifts, and a feast that symbolises the peace between the desert and the coast.
