## The Age of the Great Highways

The Century of Roads is the name given to the period in the middle of the present age when the principal road networks that connect the cities and the regions of the three continents were built. The century extended from the late fourth century to the late fifth century, a period of approximately one hundred years during which the rulers and the councils of the principal powers invested heavily in the construction of roads, bridges, and causeways. The roads that were built in this period remain the principal arteries of travel and trade in the present age.

The construction of the roads was a response to the growth of trade and the increasing integration of the economies of the three continents. The merchants of the great cities required reliable routes for the movement of goods, and the rulers of the principal powers required reliable routes for the movement of troops and officials. The roads that were built in the Century of Roads met both needs and transformed the travel and the trade of the present age.

## The State of the Roads Before the Century

Before the Century of Roads, the routes that connected the cities and the regions of the three continents were little more than tracks and trails. The principal routes followed the natural features of the landscape, following the valleys and the coastlines, crossing the rivers at the fords and the passes at the lowest points. The tracks were often impassable in the winter and the spring, when the rain and the snow turned them to mud, and the travellers were required to wait for the summer and the autumn to make their journeys.

The tracks were not maintained by any authority, and the travellers were responsible for finding their own way and for dealing with the obstacles that they encountered. The merchants who travelled the tracks were required to carry their own supplies and to defend themselves against the bandits and the wild animals that infested the routes. The tracks were also uncertain, as the routes shifted from year to year with the changes in the landscape and the vegetation.

The principal exception to the general neglect of the roads was the network of dwarven roads that the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) had built in the passes and the highlands. The dwarven roads were built of stone and were maintained by the holds through which they passed. The roads were the best in the present age, and they provided a model for the roads that were built in the Century of Roads.

## The Beginning of the Century

The Century of Roads began in the late fourth century, when the council of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) authorised the construction of a road from [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) to the northern frontier of the Republic. The road was intended to aid the movement of troops to the northern frontier, where the Republic was engaged in a dispute with the kingdom of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) over the control of the trade routes. The road was built by the engineers of the Republic, who had studied the dwarven roads and who adopted the dwarven techniques of construction.

The road was a success, and the council of the Republic authorised the construction of a second road, connecting Khalisar to the eastern ports of the Republic. The second road was longer and more ambitious than the first, requiring the construction of a number of bridges across the rivers that flowed from the interior to the coast. The road was completed in the early fifth century and was followed by a third road, connecting the Republic to the southern regions of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach).

The example of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) inspired the other powers of the present age to undertake their own road-building projects. The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) built a road from the great summer camp to the principal cities of the Confederation, using the techniques that the dwarven engineers had developed for the construction of roads across the grasslands. The kingdom of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) built a road from the capital to the ports of the eastern coast, connecting the interior to the sea.

## The Techniques of Road Construction

The roads of the Century of Roads were built using techniques that were derived from the dwarven tradition of road construction and that were adapted to the conditions of the different regions of the three continents. The roads were built of stone, with a foundation of large stones set in the ground, a middle layer of smaller stones and gravel, and a surface layer of compacted stone chips that was smooth and durable. The roads were raised above the surrounding ground to allow the water to drain away, and they were provided with ditches and culverts to carry off the rain.

The roads were built to a standard width that allowed two carts to pass each other, and they were provided with milestones that marked the distances between the principal settlements. The roads were also provided with bridges at the principal river crossings, built of stone and timber using the techniques that the dwarven engineers had developed. The bridges were the most impressive structures of the period, spanning the rivers with arches that were the highest and the widest of the present age.

The construction of the roads was organised by the authorities of the powers that built them. The authorities appointed surveyors to plan the routes and engineers to supervise the construction. The labour was provided by the local populations, who were required to contribute a certain number of days of labour to the construction of the roads as a form of taxation. The labour was not popular, but the roads were valued by the merchants and the travellers who used them, and the benefits of the roads were generally recognised.

## The Impact of the Roads

The impact of the roads upon the travel and the trade of the present age was transformative. The roads reduced the time and the cost of travel, allowing the merchants to move their goods more quickly and more cheaply than before. The roads also reduced the risks of travel, as the travellers were no longer required to find their own way across the landscape and as the roads were patrolled by the authorities that had built them.

The roads also had a profound impact upon the integration of the economies of the three continents. The roads connected the regions that had been isolated from one another and allowed the merchants to trade with a wider range of partners. The roads also connected the cities to the interior, allowing the farmers and the craftsmen of the interior to sell their goods in the markets of the cities.

The roads also had a military significance. The roads allowed the rulers of the principal powers to move their troops quickly from one region to another, and they improved the ability of the powers to defend their territories against invasion. The roads also allowed the powers to project their influence into the regions that had been beyond the reach of their authority.

## The Completion of the Network

The network of roads that was built in the Century of Roads was largely complete by the late fifth century. The principal routes of the present age had been built, including the road from Khalisar to the northern frontier, the road from Khalisar to the eastern ports, the road from the summer camp to the cities of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), the road from [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) to the eastern coast, and the roads that connected the dwarven holds of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) to the passes and the valleys.

The network was not complete in every detail, and there were gaps in the coverage of the routes. The roads of the [Sahrani](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) coast were less extensive than the roads of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and the roads of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) coast were less extensive still. The roads of the interior of the [Southern Lands](/content/location/natural/region/southern-lands) were not built until the later centuries. But the network that was built in the Century of Roads was sufficient for the needs of the trade and the travel of the present age.

## The Legacy of the Century

The Century of Roads was one of the most productive periods of construction in the history of the present age, and the roads that were built in the period remain the principal arteries of travel and trade. The roads transformed the travel and the trade of the present age, connecting the regions that had been isolated from one another and integrating the economies of the three continents.

The legacy of the Century of Roads is visible in the roads themselves, which are still in use in the present age. The roads are maintained by the authorities that built them, and they continue to serve the travellers and the merchants of the present age. The bridges that were built in the period are still standing, and the milestones that mark the distances between the settlements are still in place.

The memory of the Century of Roads is preserved in the chronicles of the principal powers and in the ceremonies of the road wardens. The anniversary of the completion of the first road from Khalisar to the northern frontier is marked by a ceremony at the principal milestone, attended by the magistrates of the Republic and by the road wardens who maintain the road.
