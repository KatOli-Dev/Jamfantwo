## The Conflicts Over the Eastern Grain

The Grain Fleet Wars were a series of conflicts fought over the control of the grain shipments that sustained the eastern coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) during the middle period of the present age. The wars were fought between the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) and involved a number of smaller powers that were drawn into the conflict by alliance or necessity. The wars lasted for several decades and were marked by naval engagements, blockades, and raids upon the coastal settlements.

The name of the wars derives from the grain fleets that the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) dispatched each year from the fertile plains of the interior to the hungry cities of the eastern coast. The grain was essential to the survival of the eastern cities, which were too densely populated to feed themselves from their own hinterlands. The control of the grain shipments was therefore a matter of life and death for the eastern cities, and the wars were fought with a desperation that the chronicles of the period record with great vividness.

## The Grain Trade of the Eastern Coast

The grain trade of the eastern coast was one of the most important commercial activities of the present age. The fertile plains of the interior of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) produced grain in abundance, and the grain was shipped down the [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river) to the ports of the eastern coast, where it was distributed to the cities that needed it. The trade was organised by the Northern Coast League, which controlled the principal ports of the eastern coast and maintained a fleet of grain vessels that made the journey each year.

The grain trade was vulnerable to disruption. The vessels were slow and heavily laden, and they were easy targets for pirates and privateers. The trade routes were long and passed through waters that were contested by the rival powers. The merchants who organised the trade were required to pay protection money to the local rulers and to hire armed escorts for the vessels. The cost of the protection was passed on to the consumers in the eastern cities, who paid high prices for the grain that they needed to survive.

The grain trade was also a source of tension between the Northern Coast League and the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league). The Thessaran League controlled the southern ports of the eastern coast and claimed the right to tax the grain vessels that passed through its waters. The Northern Coast League disputed the claim, arguing that the grain was essential to the survival of the eastern cities and that the tax was an unjust burden upon the trade.

## The Outbreak of the First War

The first of the Grain Fleet Wars broke out in the late fourth century, when the Thessaran League imposed a new tax upon the grain vessels passing through the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), the narrow passage that connected the eastern waters to the open sea. The tax was substantial, and the Northern Coast League refused to pay it. The Thessaran League responded by seizing a grain vessel that was passing through the strait and holding it for ransom.

The Northern Coast League dispatched a fleet to recover the vessel and to protect the grain shipments. The fleet met the Thessaran fleet in the waters of the strait, and a naval battle was fought that lasted for two days. The battle was indecisive, with both fleets sustaining heavy losses and neither able to claim a clear victory. The grain vessel was eventually released through the mediation of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), but the underlying dispute remained unresolved.

The war continued for several years, with each side raiding the shipping of the other and attacking the coastal settlements. The war was costly for both sides, as the trade of the eastern coast was disrupted and the cities suffered from shortages of grain and other essential supplies. The war ended with a treaty that established a compromise on the tax, with the Thessaran League reducing the tax and the Northern Coast League agreeing to pay it. The compromise was accepted by both sides, and the grain fleets resumed their regular voyages.

## The Second War

The second of the Grain Fleet Wars broke out in the early fifth century, when the Thessaran League attempted to extend its control over the grain trade by establishing a monopoly upon the purchase of grain from the interior. The Northern Coast League regarded the monopoly as a threat to its own trade and refused to recognise it. The Thessaran League responded by blockading the ports of the Northern Coast League and seizing the grain vessels that attempted to pass through the blockade.

The blockade was effective, and the Northern Coast League was unable to ship its grain to the eastern cities. The cities suffered from a severe shortage of grain, and prices rose sharply. The Northern Coast League assembled a large fleet to break the blockade, and a series of naval engagements was fought in the waters of the eastern coast. The fleet was commanded by Admiral [Thessa Nordhaven](/content/people/historical/thessa-nordhaven), who had served in the fleet of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) before accepting the command of the Northern Coast League.

Admiral Thessa's campaign was one of the most celebrated naval campaigns of the present age. She led the fleet through the blockade in a series of daring engagements, capturing several Thessaran vessels and forcing the blockade to be lifted. The campaign was recorded in the chronicles of the Northern Coast League and is still studied in the academies as a model of naval strategy.

The war ended with a treaty that established a new framework for the grain trade. The Thessaran League abandoned its claim to a monopoly and accepted the right of the Northern Coast League to ship grain freely through the waters of the eastern coast. The treaty also established a joint commission to regulate the grain trade and to resolve disputes between the two leagues.

## The Third War and the Final Settlement

The third of the Grain Fleet Wars broke out in the mid-fifth century, when a dispute over the quality of the grain led to a renewal of the conflict. The Northern Coast League accused the Thessaran League of selling grain that was of poor quality and that had been stored too long. The Thessaran League denied the accusation and blamed the Northern Coast League for spreading false rumours to undermine the Thessaran trade.

The dispute escalated into a war that lasted for several years and that was marked by a series of raids and counter-raids upon the ports of the eastern coast. The war was less intense than the earlier wars but was still costly for both sides. The war ended with a treaty that established quality standards for the grain trade and that required both leagues to submit their disputes to the arbitration of the [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) Republic.

The final settlement of the Grain Fleet Wars was achieved in the late fifth century, when the Northern Coast League and the Thessaran League agreed to a comprehensive treaty that resolved all outstanding disputes. The treaty established the framework for the grain trade that has operated to the present day, with a joint commission regulating the trade, quality standards enforced by the academies, and disputes resolved by the arbitration of the Amurra Republic.

## The Legacy of the Wars

The Grain Fleet Wars were among the most disruptive conflicts of the middle period of the present age, causing great hardship to the cities of the eastern coast and costing the lives of many sailors and soldiers. The wars also demonstrated the importance of the grain trade to the survival of the eastern cities and the vulnerability of the trade to disruption.

The legacy of the wars is visible in the institutions that were established to regulate the grain trade. The joint commission of the Northern Coast League and the Thessaran League, the quality standards enforced by the academies, and the arbitration of the Amurra Republic are all the legacy of the wars. The wars also established the principle that the grain trade was a matter of common concern to all the powers of the eastern coast and that the disruption of the trade was a threat to the peace of the region.

The memory of the wars is preserved in the chronicles of the eastern coast and in the ceremonies of the ports. The anniversary of Admiral Thessa's victory in the second war is marked by a ceremony in the principal port of the Northern Coast League, attended by the magistrates and merchants of the eastern cities. The ceremony includes the blessing of the grain fleet and the distribution of bread to the citizens.
