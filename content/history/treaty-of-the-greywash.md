## Overview

The Treaty of the Greywash is the agreement that the magistrates of the principal towns along the [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river) signed in the middle of the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities) for the management of the river, the protection of the fisheries, and the regulation of the navigation. The treaty took its name from the river at whose confluence the magistrates convened to sign it, and the treaty is recorded in the chronicles of the academies of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) as the most comprehensive of the river agreements of the present age.

The treaty was negotiated over the course of several years, and the negotiation was conducted through the mediators of the academies. The mediators included scholars who had studied the older river agreements that had been in force since the founding of the cities, and the scholars brought to the negotiation a knowledge of the traditions that the parties had observed for several centuries. The chronicler notes that the negotiation was a model of patient and careful diplomacy, and the negotiation is held in high esteem by the magistrates and the scholars of the present age.

## The Background

The Greywash is one of the principal rivers of the three continents, and the river rises in the southern flank of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) and flows south and west to the sea at [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara). The river is navigable for the larger part of its length, and the river has been the principal highway of the inland valleys since the founding of the cities. The river supports a substantial fishery, and the fishery has been the foundation of the prosperity of the towns along the river since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities).

By the middle of the Long Peace, the older river agreements had become inadequate to the management of the river. The growth of the trade had increased the traffic upon the river, and the increase had led to disputes between the merchants of the upper towns and the merchants of the lower towns. The fishery had been over-fished in some sections of the river, and the over-fishing had led to disputes between the fishermen of the river and the farmers of the banks. The magistrates of the principal towns along the river judged that a new agreement was required, and the magistrates convened at the confluence of the Greywash to sign the treaty.
