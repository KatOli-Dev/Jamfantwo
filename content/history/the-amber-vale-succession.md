## The Contest for the Throne of Vael

The Amber Vale Succession was the period of uncertainty and conflict that followed the death of [Sera](/content/people/historical/sera-ambervale), the queen who had ended the [Twin-Reaches Wars](/content/history/twin-reaches-wars) and begun the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace). Sera had ruled for more than thirty years and had left no clear heir to the throne of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic). The succession was contested by three claimants, each supported by a faction of the magistrates and merchants of the Republic, and the contest lasted for several years before a settlement was reached.

The succession takes its name from the Amber Vale, the region of the [Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) where Sera's principal estate was located and where the claimants gathered to present their cases to the council. The vale was a fertile valley lying between the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the foothills of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range), and it had been the seat of Sera's family for several generations. The vale was chosen for the council that decided the succession because it was regarded as neutral ground, belonging to Sera's estate rather than to any of the factions.

## The Death of Sera

Sera died in the winter of her thirtieth year of reign, after a brief illness that the physicians of the Republic were unable to treat. The chronicles of the Republic record that Sera's death was unexpected, as she had been in good health until the onset of the illness, and that the council of the Republic was unprepared for the event. Sera had not named an heir in her will, and the magistrates of the Republic were divided among themselves about who should succeed her.

The constitution of the Republic provided that the succession should be decided by the council of magistrates, which was composed of the senior magistrates of the principal cities. The council was to elect a new queen from among the candidates who presented themselves, and the election was to be confirmed by the assembly of the citizens. The procedure had not been tested in several centuries, as the succession had passed smoothly from parent to child through the generations. The death of Sera without a clear heir revealed the weakness of the procedure, as the council was divided and the assembly was unable to agree.

The three claimants who presented themselves to the council were Sera's nephew Halric Vael, her cousin Thessa Nordhaven, and the magistrate Oren Greywash. Each claimant had a base of support among the magistrates and merchants of the Republic, and each advanced a different argument for the right to the throne.

## The Claim of Halric Vael

Halric Vael was the son of Sera's younger sister, who had died before Sera's accession. Halric had been raised in the household of Sera and had served as a magistrate of the Republic for several years before Sera's death. He was known for his careful administration of the district under his jurisdiction and for his reputation for fairness in trade disputes. Halric was supported by the magistrates of the northern districts of the Republic, who judged that he was the most competent of the claimants and the most likely to continue Sera's policies.

The argument advanced by Halric's supporters was based upon the principle of blood. Halric was the closest living relative of Sera through the female line, and the tradition of the Republic held that the throne should pass to the nearest relative of the previous ruler. The supporters argued that the tradition was clear and that the council should confirm Halric's accession without delay.

The opposition to Halric's claim came from those who doubted his ability to command the respect of the military and from those who regarded his northern supporters with suspicion. The military commanders of the Republic had been loyal to Sera but were not certain of Halric, and the merchants of the southern cities were concerned that Halric would favour the northern districts at their expense.

## The Claim of Thessa Nordhaven

Thessa Nordhaven was the daughter of Sera's elder brother, who had been a commander of the Republic's fleet before his death in the Twin-Reaches Wars. Thessa had been educated in the military academies and had served as a commander of the Republic's fleet for several years. She was known for her forceful personality and her ambition and was supported by the military commanders and by the merchants of the western districts of the Republic.

The argument advanced by Thessa's supporters was based upon the principle of capacity. Thessa was the most experienced commander of the Republic and was best qualified to lead the Republic in a time of uncertainty. The supporters argued that the Republic needed a ruler who could command the respect of the military and protect the interests of the Republic against its rivals.

The opposition to Thessa's claim came from those who feared her ambition and from those who regarded her as a warmonger who would undo the peace that Sera had achieved. The peace with the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) was still fragile, and there were many who feared that Thessa would renew the war. The supporters of Halric argued that Thessa's accession would lead to a return to the conflict that Sera had ended.

## The Claim of Oren Greywash

Oren Greywash was a senior magistrate of the Republic who had served as Sera's principal adviser in the later years of her reign. Oren was not related to Sera by blood but had been a trusted counsellor and had been instrumental in the negotiation of the [Twin-Reaches Peace](/content/history/twin-reaches-peace). The opposition to Oren's claim came from those who doubted his legitimacy and from those who regarded him as a usurper who had no right to the throne. The tradition of the Republic favoured the succession of blood, and many magistrates were reluctant to set aside the tradition in favour of a candidate who had no family connection to the throne.

## The Council of the Amber Vale

The contest was resolved by a council held at Sera's estate in the Amber Vale, convened by the chief magistrate of the Republic. The council was attended by the magistrates of the principal cities and by the commanders of the military and the fleet. The proceedings lasted for several weeks, with each claimant presenting their case and answering the questions of the magistrates.

The chronicles of the Republic record that the debate was vigorous but orderly. The magistrates weighed the arguments of the claimants and considered the implications of each choice for the future of the Republic. The council was unable to reach a decision by a simple majority, and the proceedings were suspended for a period of reflection. The chief magistrate proposed a compromise under which Halric Vael would be confirmed as queen, with Thessa Nordhaven appointed as commander of the fleet and Oren Greywash appointed as chief adviser. The compromise was accepted by the claimants and confirmed by the council, and Halric Vael was crowned as the queen of the Republic shortly afterward.

## The Legacy of the Succession

The Amber Vale Succession established a precedent that has guided the Republic in every subsequent succession. The principle of the compromise, under which the successful claimant shares power with the principal rivals, has been applied in a number of later successions and has contributed to the stability of the Republic. The precedent is regarded as one of the most important innovations of the Republic's constitutional tradition.

The legacy of the succession is also visible in the relations between the factions of the Republic. The division between the supporters of Halric, Thessa, and Oren persisted for a generation after the succession and shaped the politics of the Republic for decades. The factions learned to work together under the terms of the compromise, but the memory of the contest was preserved in the chronicles of the period.
