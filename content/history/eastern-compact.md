## The Agreement with Serren

The Eastern Compact is the name given by the chroniclers of the inland valleys and the warm coast to the agreement negotiated between the parties of the [Twin-Reaches Peace](/content/history/twin-reaches-peace) and the city of [Serren](/content/location/settlement/city/serren). The compact extended the principles of the [Twin-Reaches Peace](/content/history/twin-reaches-peace) to the trade routes connecting the inland valleys to the [eastern reach](/content/location/natural/continent/eastern-reach)es of the warm coast, establishing transit fees, navigation rules, and an office of mediator modelled upon the office established by the Twin-Reaches Peace.

The compact was negotiated in the period following the conclusion of the Twin-Reaches Peace, when the parties to the peace recognised that the principles they had established could be applied to their relations with other cities and regions. [Serren](/content/location/settlement/city/serren) was the first city to seek such an agreement, recognising that the stability and prosperity that the Twin-Reaches Peace had brought to the inland valleys could be extended to the eastern trade routes.

## The Position of Serren

Serren was a prosperous trading city located at the junction of the principal trade routes connecting the inland valleys to the eastern coast. The city had grown wealthy from the transit of goods between the interior and the coast, and its magistrates had maintained a policy of neutrality in the conflicts of the surrounding regions. The prosperity of the city depended upon the free flow of trade, and the magistrates of Serren had an interest in the stability of the trade routes.

The parties to the [Twin-Reaches Peace](/content/history/twin-reaches-peace) recognised that Serren's cooperation was essential to the success of their agreement, for the trade routes that passed through the city were the principal arteries of the commerce of the inland valleys. The parties approached the magistrates of Serren with a proposal for a compact that would extend the principles of the Twin-Reaches Peace to the eastern routes.

## The Provisions of the Compact

The Eastern Compact established a system of transit fees and navigation rules for the trade routes connecting the inland valleys to Serren and the eastern coast. The fees were set at a rate that was sufficient to maintain the roads and the way stations but not so high as to discourage trade, and they were collected by the port masters and the road wardens of the signatory towns.

The compact also established an office of mediator, modelled upon the office established by the Twin-Reaches Peace, to resolve disputes between merchants and carriers using the trade routes. The mediator was appointed by the parties to the compact and was given the authority to hear disputes and to propose settlements that would be binding upon the parties if they were accepted.

The mediator was also responsible for the oversight of the transit fees and the condition of the trade routes, reporting regularly to the signatory parties on the state of the commerce and the compliance of those who used the routes.

## The Implementation

The implementation of the compact was accomplished without significant difficulty, for the trade routes were already well established and the merchants and carriers who used them were accustomed to paying transit fees and observing rules of the road. The mediator appointed under the compact was a respected merchant of Serren who had served as a mediator in commercial disputes for many years.

The mediator surveyed the trade routes and recorded the condition of the roads and the way stations, identifying the sections that required repair and the stations that needed new facilities. The mediator also consulted with the merchants and carriers who used the routes, listening to their concerns and adjusting the regulations of the compact to accommodate the practical needs of the trade.

## The Compact and the Trade of Serren

The Eastern Compact had a significant effect upon the trade of Serren and the surrounding region. The transit fees established by the compact provided a reliable source of revenue for the maintenance of the roads and the way stations, and the condition of the trade routes improved steadily in the years following the adoption of the compact. The merchants and carriers who used the routes benefited from the improved roads and the more efficient way stations, and the volume of trade passing through Serren increased.

The compact also contributed to the stability of the region, for the mediator established under the compact provided a means of resolving disputes that had previously led to conflicts between the merchant houses and the carriers. The mediator's decisions were respected by the parties, and the number of disputes that required the intervention of the magistrates declined.

## The Elder of the Mediator's Office

The first mediator appointed under the Eastern Compact served for twenty-seven years, a period during which the office of the mediator became an established institution of the eastern trade. The mediator's reports were read with attention by the signatory parties, and the recommendations that the mediator made were adopted in the great majority of cases. The mediator's authority was accepted by the merchants and the carriers, who regarded the mediator as a fair and impartial arbiter of the disputes that arose on the trade routes.

The mediation of disputes was conducted in a chamber set aside for the purpose in the principal market hall of Serren, with the parties appearing before the mediator to present their cases. The hearings were conducted in the common speech of the trade, and the decisions of the mediator were recorded in a register that was kept in the chamber. The register was the record of the practice of the mediator's office, and it was consulted by the mediators who succeeded to the office as a guide to the principles that had been established.

## The Compact and the Wider Order

The Eastern Compact is one of several agreements that the parties to the [Twin-Reaches Peace](/content/history/twin-reaches-peace) negotiated with the principal cities of the three continents, and it is regarded by the chroniclers of the inland valleys as a successful extension of the principles of the peace to the eastern trade. The compact is placed in the broader chronology of the age by the [World Timeline](/content/history/world-timeline), which records the adoption of the compact alongside the other agreements modelled upon the Twin-Reaches Peace.
