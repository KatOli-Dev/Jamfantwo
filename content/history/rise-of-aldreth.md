## From Fishing Village to Commercial Power

[Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) is the principal commercial power of the present age, a city whose merchants trade with every port of the three continents and whose fleets are among the largest in the known world. The rise of Aldreth from a modest fishing village to a commercial power of the first rank is one of the most remarkable stories of the present age. The city was founded in the early generations of the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era) and grew slowly for several centuries before beginning the rapid expansion that made it the city of the present day.

The chronicles of [Aldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) are carefully preserved in the archives of the Aldreth Council and are among the most complete of the commercial cities of the present age. The chronicles record the principal events of the city's history in considerable detail and provide a valuable source for the study of the economic development of the present age. The academies of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) have studied the chronicles and have produced a substantial body of commentary on the rise of Aldreth.

## The Natural Advantages of the Site

The site upon which Aldreth was founded possessed natural advantages that contributed to its later prosperity. The city was built upon a natural harbour at the mouth of the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael), where the river opened into a broad estuary that provided shelter for vessels of all sizes. The harbour was the best along the northern coast of Aethelar, and the river provided a route into the interior that the merchants of Aldreth would exploit to great effect.

The hinterland of Aldreth was fertile and well-watered, supporting farms that produced grain, vegetables, and livestock in abundance. The forests of the interior provided timber for shipbuilding, and the hills of the surrounding region contained deposits of iron and coal that would later become the foundation of Aldreth's manufacturing industry. The combination of a good harbour, a navigable river, a fertile hinterland, and accessible mineral deposits was rare and gave Aldreth a base of resources that few other cities could match.

The first settlers of Aldreth were fishermen who built their huts upon the banks of the estuary and launched their boats into the river. The settlement was small for the first several generations, consisting of a few dozen families who lived by fishing and by trading their catch with the farmers of the interior. The chronicles record that the first permanent structure was a stone quay built in the second century of the present age, which allowed the fishermen to land their catch in all weather.

## The Beginnings of Trade

The growth of Aldreth began with the expansion of trade in the middle period of the present age. The merchants of the smaller coastal towns began to use Aldreth's harbour as a staging point for longer voyages, and the Aldreth fishermen found that they could profit by supplying the passing vessels with fresh provisions and water. The trade grew gradually, and the settlement expanded to accommodate the increased activity. New quays were built, warehouses were constructed, and a market was established on the ground behind the harbour.

The turning point came in the fourth century of the present age, when a merchant of Aldreth named Orin Aldmere organised a trading voyage to the ports of the Sahrani coast. Orin had served as a pilot on the Sahrani run and knew the winds and currents of the Auric Ocean. He judged that the trade with Sahrana was growing and that Aldreth was well-placed to take a share of it. Orin's voyage was successful, and he returned with a cargo of spices and textiles that sold for a great profit in the markets of the north.

Orin's success inspired other merchants to follow his example, and the number of vessels trading from Aldreth increased rapidly. The merchants of Aldreth established trading posts at the principal ports of the Sahrani coast and at the island of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). The trade brought wealth to Aldreth, and the city expanded to accommodate the increased population and activity.

## The Shipbuilding Industry

The growth of trade required vessels, and Aldreth developed a shipbuilding industry that became the largest of the present age. The forests of the interior provided the timber, the hills provided the iron for fittings, and the harbour provided the space for the shipyards. The shipwrights of Aldreth were known for the quality of their workmanship and for their willingness to adopt new techniques and designs.

The first shipyard was established on the northern bank of the estuary in the early fifth century and was followed by a number of others as the demand for vessels grew. The yards built vessels of all types, from the small coasting boats that served the local trade to the large ocean-going vessels that carried the goods of Aldreth to the ports of the three continents. The yards also built vessels for the navies of the neighbouring kingdoms, including the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic).

The shipbuilding industry was the foundation of Aldreth's prosperity and the source of the city's commercial power. The vessels of Aldreth were the finest of the present age, and the merchants of Aldreth commanded the principal trade routes of the known world. The industry employed a substantial portion of the city's population and supported a network of ancillary trades, including rope-making, sail-making, and the forging of anchors and fittings.

## The Organisation of the Merchant Houses

The prosperity of Aldreth was also the result of the organisation of the merchant houses. The merchants of Aldreth had formed themselves into a number of family houses, each house controlling a portion of the city's trade and maintaining its own fleet of vessels. The houses were governed by the senior members of the family, who made the decisions about the voyages and the investments and who appointed the captains of the vessels.

The houses collaborated in a number of ways, sharing information about the markets and the conditions of the trade routes and pooling their resources for particularly large or risky ventures. The collaboration was not always smooth, and the chronicles record a number of disputes between houses that were resolved by the arbitration of the council or by the intervention of the magistrates. The rivalry between the houses was intense but was generally conducted within the bounds of the law, as the merchants understood that their prosperity depended upon the stability of the city.

The houses also established a number of institutions that supported the trade of the city. The House of Exchange, established in the early sixth century, provided a market for the exchange of currencies and the negotiation of loans. The House of Insurance, established in the late sixth century, provided insurance for vessels and cargoes. The House of the Sea, established in the early seventh century, provided a registry for vessels and a record of their ownership and voyages.

## Relations with the Neighbouring Powers

The rise of Aldreth was not accomplished in isolation. The merchants of Aldreth maintained relations with the neighbouring powers, including the kingdom of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), and the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league). The relations were generally friendly, as the merchants of Aldreth brought goods that the other powers needed and paid the taxes and dues that the other powers demanded.

The [Three-Banners War](/content/history/three-banners-war) and the wars of the plains affected the trade of Aldreth but did not interrupt it. The merchants of Aldreth were careful to remain neutral in the wars of the neighbouring kingdoms, and the council of Aldreth maintained a policy of non-intervention that was respected by the warring parties. The neutrality of Aldreth was one of the foundations of its prosperity, as the merchants of the city could trade with all parties without fear of reprisal.

The rivalry with [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) and the other commercial cities of the coast was more intense. The merchants of Aldreth competed with the merchants of the other cities for the control of the trade routes and the markets of the three continents. The competition was conducted through the normal mechanisms of trade, with each city seeking to undercut the prices of the others and to secure exclusive access to the most profitable markets.

## The Legacy of the Rise

The rise of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) from a fishing village to a commercial power of the first rank is one of the most remarkable stories of the present age. The natural advantages of the site, the enterprise of the merchants, the skill of the shipwrights, the organisation of the houses, and the neutrality of the council all contributed to the prosperity that Aldreth enjoys in the present age.

The legacy of the rise is visible in the streets and buildings of modern Aldreth, in the quays and warehouses that line the harbour, in the shipyards that occupy the northern bank of the estuary, and in the houses of the merchants that dominate the principal thoroughfares. The city is a monument to the enterprise of the merchants who built it and to the generations of shipwrights, sailors, and traders who made it the principal commercial power of the present age. The [World Timeline](/content/history/world-timeline) records the principal events of the rise in relation to the other major developments of the age.
