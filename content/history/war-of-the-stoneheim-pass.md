## The Brief Engagement

The War of the Stoneheim Pass is the name given by the chroniclers of the academies to a brief but sharp engagement between the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) and the forces of a coalition of lowland princes that was fought in the later period of the recorded history of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The war lasted for a period of less than a year, and the war was concluded by a peace that has endured to the present day. The chronicler notes that the war is recorded in the chronicles of the holds and in the chronicles of the lowland princes, and the chronicles agree upon the principal events but disagree upon their meaning, and the disagreement is itself a measure of the importance that the war has assumed in the traditions of the mountain and the lowlands.

The war was fought at the approaches of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass), the broad rocky pass at the head of the highlands that carries the road from the cold north into the warm coast. The chronicler notes that the pass has been the principal route of the land trade between the cold north and the warm coast since the [Era of the Founding Cities](/content/history/era-of-the-founding-cities), and the pass has been the subject of careful engineering by the magistrates of the towns of both regions. The war was the most recent of the major conflicts that the pass has occasioned, and the war is held in the memory of every hold of the mountain and every town of the lowland principalities.

## The Outbreak of the War

The war began with a dispute over the tolls that the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) had long collected from the merchants who crossed [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass) into the warm coast. The holds had claimed the right to collect the tolls by virtue of the ancient accord that the founders of the holds had made with the magistrates of the principal towns of the lowland principalities, and the tolls were the foundation of the maintenance of the road that crossed the pass. The coalition of lowland princes disputed the claim on the grounds that the accord had been made before the coalition had been formed, and that the coalition was not bound by an instrument to which it had not been a party. The dispute was referred to the magistrates of the principal towns of the lowlands, and the magistrates decided against the holds. The holds refused to accept the decision, and the coalition dispatched a levy to enforce the ruling.

The levy was met upon the approaches of the pass by the garrison of the holds, and the meeting was the first of the principal engagements of the war. The chronicler notes that the levy was insufficient to the task that the council of the coalition had set it, for the approaches of the pass are narrow and the holds had fortified them with care. The levy was held at the foot of the pass for several weeks, and the council of the coalition reinforced the levy with a second body of troops drawn from the principal towns of the lowlands. The chronicler notes that the outbreak of the war is recorded in the chronicles of the holds as the moment at which the ancient accord of the pass gave way to open conflict, and the chronicles describe the haste with which the council of the coalition dispatched the levy as a measure of the council's confidence in the coalition's right.

## The Course of the War

The course of the war is conventionally divided into two phases: the phase of the coalition's investment of the pass, and the phase of the holds' counter-offensive that broke the investment and forced the coalition to seek terms. The phase of the coalition's investment lasted for the first half of the war, and the phase was marked by the siege of the watch-towers that the holds had maintained at the northern and southern ends of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass). The phase of the holds' counter-offensive lasted for the second half of the war, and the phase was marked by the arrival of the levies of the allied holds and by the sortie that the garrisons of the towers conducted against the lines of the coalition.

The phase of the coalition's investment saw the coalition in control of the approaches of the pass and the watch-towers of the holds invested from the lowland side. The coalition maintained a close blockade of the towers, and the garrison of the northern tower was cut off from relief for several weeks. The chronicler notes that the garrison of the northern tower held out through the blockade with a stubbornness that the chronicles of the holds celebrate, and the chronicles describe the labours of the garrison in terms that the chroniclers of the lowlands regard as excessive. The chronicler notes that the phase of the investment was marked by a great deal of hardship on both sides, and the hardship is recorded in the chronicles of the lowlands as the darkest period of the war.

The phase of the holds' counter-offensive began with the arrival of the levies of the allied holds, which were summoned by the king of Stoneheim and marched by the lesser passes that the coalition had not thought to blockade. The levies of the allied holds descended upon the lines of the coalition from the high ground to the north, and the sortie of the garrison of the northern tower was conducted at the same hour. The coalition found itself beset upon two sides, and the coalition was forced to withdraw from the approaches of the pass with heavy loss. The chronicler notes that the counter-offensive is recorded in the chronicles of the holds as the turning of the war, and the chronicles describe the discipline of the holds' levies in terms that the chroniclers of the holds regard as the measure of the mountain's character.

## The Negotiation of the Peace

The phase of the negotiations began with the withdrawal of the coalition from the approaches of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass) and with the request of the council of the coalition for a suspension of arms. The [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) were content to grant the request, for the counter-offensive had cost the holds a number of their best warriors, and the king of Stoneheim judged that the holds could not press the war further without incurring costs that would be disproportionate to the benefits. The negotiations were conducted at the great hall of Stoneheim, and the negotiations were mediated by the elders of the dwarven communities of the lower slopes of the mountain, who had remained neutral in the war.

The negotiations were complicated by the insistence of each party upon the rights that it had claimed at the beginning of the war. The coalition insisted upon the abolition of the tolls by virtue of the natural liberty of trade; the holds insisted upon the tolls by virtue of the ancient accord. The mediators proposed a compromise that reduced the tolls to a small transit fee that was shared between the holds and the magistrates of the principal towns of the lowlands, and the compromise was accepted by the king of Stoneheim and by the council of the coalition. The peace was signed in the great hall of Stoneheim in the presence of the elders of the dwarven communities and the magistrates of the principal towns, and the peace is known in the chronicles as the Peace of the Stoneheim Pass.

## The Aftermath

The aftermath of the war was marked by a long period of reconstruction upon the approaches of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass) and in the principal towns of the lowland principalities. The [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) rebuilt the watch-towers that the war had damaged, and the holds re-established the tolls that the peace had confirmed. The lowland principalities rebuilt the levies that the war had cost them, and the principalities entered into the arrangements that the peace had established. The chronicler notes that the war left a permanent mark upon the relations of the mountain and the lowlands, and the relations have been marked by a mixture of rivalry and cooperation ever since.

The peace that has prevailed since the conclusion of the war has been among the most enduring of the treaties that the mountain and the lowlands have negotiated, and the peace has held for several generations. The chronicler notes that the peace is held in high esteem by the magistrates and the merchants of both regions, and the peace is regarded by the academies of the cold north and the warm coast as a model of the kind of settlement that can be achieved by patient negotiation. The transit fee that the peace established has become a substantial source of revenue for the holds and the principal towns alike, and the revenue is used to maintain the road, the watch-towers, and the quarters of the pass.

## The War in the Chronicles of the Holds

The war is recorded in the chronicles of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) as a brief but glorious engagement, and the chronicles celebrate the stubbornness of the garrison of the northern watch-tower and the discipline of the levies of the allied holds. The chronicler notes that the chronicles of the holds place the war in the context of the long history of the mountain and the lowlands, and the chronicles describe the war as a confirmation of the mountain's capacity to defend its own. The chronicles also record the names of the warriors who fell in the war, and the names are recited at the annual ceremonies that the holds maintain in their honour. The chronicler notes that the ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the holds, and the ceremonies are attended by the elders and the principal citizens of every hold of the realm.

## The War in the Chronicles of the Lowlands

The war is recorded in the chronicles of the lowland principalities as a brief but instructive misadventure, and the chronicles describe the haste with which the council of the coalition dispatched the levy as a measure of the council's overconfidence. The chronicler notes that the chronicles of the lowlands place the war in the context of the long history of the trade of the pass, and the chronicles describe the war as a confirmation of the lowlands' need to maintain the freedom of the trade. The chronicles also record the names of the magistrates who counselled against the war, and the names are invoked by the magistrates of the principal towns whenever a dispute arises over the tolls of the pass. The chronicler notes that the chronicles are regarded by the academies of the lowlands as a warning against the kind of precipitate action that the coalition had undertaken.

## Significance

The War of the Stoneheim Pass is the most recent of the major conflicts that have been fought at the approaches of [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass), and the war is held in particular memory by the elders of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) and the magistrates of the lowland principalities. The war lasted for less than a year and was concluded by a peace that has endured to the present day, and the peace is the foundation of the trade that flows between the cold north and the warm coast. The chronicler notes that the war is the measure of the importance that the mountain and the lowlands attach to the pass, and the war is commemorated in the ceremonies that both regions maintain. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the mountain and the lowlands, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every hold and every town of the two regions.
