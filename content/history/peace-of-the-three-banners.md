## The Treaty That Ended the Great Plains War

The Peace of the Three Banners is the name given by the chroniclers of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) to the treaty that concluded the [Three-Banners War](/content/history/three-banners-war), the conflict fought between the Confederation and two rival confederations that contested the control of the central grasslands. The peace was signed at the great summer camp that the three confederations maintained upon the central grasslands, and it established the council of the three confederations to regulate the grazing of the central grasslands.

The peace is one of the most consequential instruments in the history of the plains, for it brought an end to the most destructive war the grasslands had known and established the institutions that have governed the use of the summer pastures for the centuries that followed. The chronicles of the three confederations agree upon the terms of the peace but differ in their interpretation of its significance, reflecting the different perspectives of the parties that signed it.

## The Negotiations

The negotiations that led to the Peace of the Three Banners were conducted over two years, following the conclusion of the active campaigning of the [Three-Banners War](/content/history/three-banners-war). The mediators of the neighbouring kingdoms, who had offered their services at the beginning of the third phase of the war, brought the parties together at the great summer camp that stood at the centre of the disputed grasslands.

The negotiations were protracted and difficult. The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) insisted upon the customary arrangements for the use of the summer pastures by virtue of the original agreement that the founders of the Confederation had made with the elders of the principal kindreds. The rival confederations insisted upon the right to share in the grazing by virtue of the natural liberty of the kindreds to use the grasslands that had supported their herds for generations before the Confederation was founded.

The mediators proposed a compromise that would establish a council of the three confederations to regulate the grazing of the central grasslands, with representation proportional to the size of the herds each confederation brought to the summer pastures. The council would meet annually at the great summer camp and would determine the allocation of the grazing grounds for the coming season, and its decisions would be binding upon all three confederations.

## The Terms of the Peace

The Peace of the Three Banners established the council of the three confederations as the institution responsible for the regulation of the summer pastures. The council was composed of elders appointed by each confederation in numbers proportional to the size of its herds. The council met annually at the great summer camp and determined the allocation of the grazing grounds for the coming season.

The peace also established the rules for the use of the summer pastures, including the rotation of the grazing grounds to prevent overgrazing, the protection of the water sources, and the resolution of disputes between the herds of different confederations. The rules were enforced by the council, which had the authority to impose fines upon confederations that violated the terms of the peace.

The peace further provided for the mutual defence of the summer pastures against external threats, requiring the three confederations to contribute warriors to a joint levy that would protect the central grasslands from raiders and invaders. The joint levy was commanded by a war leader chosen by the council from among the elders of the three confederations.

## The Implementation and Revision

The implementation of the peace required the cooperation of the three confederations, which had been enemies in the war that the peace had concluded. The council of the three confederations met for the first time in the year following the signing of the peace, and the elders of the three confederations began the work of allocating the summer pastures.

The peace has been revised from time to time to reflect changes in the size of the herds and the condition of the grasslands, but the revisions have been few and carefully considered. The council of the three confederations continues to meet annually at the great summer camp, and the peace remains in force in the present age.

## The Significance of the Peace

The Peace of the Three Banners is one of the most important treaties in the history of the plains, and it is remembered in the chronicles of the plains as the foundation of the long peace that the grasslands have enjoyed since the conclusion of the Three-Banners War. The peace established the institutions that have governed the use of the summer pastures for the centuries that followed, and it is regarded by the elders of the three confederations as a model of settlement achieved through patient negotiation.

The peace is commemorated in the ceremonies of the three confederations, whose elders gather at the great summer camp each year to mark the anniversary of the signing. The ceremonies include the recitation of the terms of the peace, the renewal of the commitments that the confederations made to one another, and the feast that celebrates the unity of the plains. The banners of the three confederations are displayed at the ceremonies, and the elders of each confederation speak the words that the tradition of the peace requires.

The peace has also been studied by the scholars of the neighbouring kingdoms, who have examined the institutions that the peace established and the methods by which the parties reached the compromise. The scholars have noted that the success of the peace depended on the willingness of the three confederations to accept a compromise that none of them regarded as ideal, and they have drawn from the experience of the plains general lessons about the resolution of conflicts over shared resources. The scholars of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) have produced a substantial literature on the peace, and their writings have been consulted by the magistrates of other regions who seek to establish similar institutions for the regulation of shared resources.
