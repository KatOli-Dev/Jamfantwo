## Overview

Following the [Twin-Reaches Wars](/content/history/twin-reaches-wars) and the [Twin-Reaches Peace](/content/history/twin-reaches-peace) that concluded them, a long stretch of years without open war between the principal cities set in. Chroniclers of the academies refer to these generations as the Long Peace, although the name is more a convenience of periodisation than a description of unbroken calm. Skirmishes on the border marches, disputes between rival merchant houses, and the occasional descent of reavers upon an undefended coast were never absent from the age. What set the Long Peace apart from earlier generations was the absence of a general war between [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm), and the other principal cities of the three continents.

The Long Peace is conventionally dated from the accession of [Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale) and the signing of the peace, and it is taken to end with the outbreak of the Stone Plague. The intervening generations therefore cover more than a century, and the period is the longest stretch of years without open war in the recorded history of the present age. The chronicler notes that the Long Peace is held in high esteem by the magistrates and the merchants of the cities, and the magistrates mark the anniversaries of the peace with appropriate ceremonies.

## Recovery and Rebuilding

In the years immediately following the peace, both parties to the wars set about the repair of the damage that the long conflict had inflicted. The [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) rebuilt the towns that had been occupied by the [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) garrisons, and the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) rebuilt the fleets that had been broken by [Thessa](/content/people/historical/admiral-thessa-of-the-northern-fleet). The transit fee that the peace had established became a substantial source of revenue, and the revenue was applied to the roads, the bridges, the harbours, and the lighthouses of the trade routes. The chronicler notes that the rebuilding was the work of two generations, and the work is recorded in the chronicles of both parties as a period of patient and necessary labour.

The rebuilding was accompanied by a growth in the trade that crossed the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) from the inland valleys to the warm coast. The merchants of both parties invested in the new caravans and the new vessels that the growth required, and the merchants established new houses in the principal towns of the trade routes. The chronicler notes that the growth was the foundation of the prosperity of the Long Peace, and the prosperity is the measure by which the chroniclers judge the success of the peace.

## The Founding of the Academies

The Long Peace saw the founding of the principal academies of the present age. The Academy of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) was established in the second generation of the Long Peace, and the academy was housed in a complex of buildings that the council of Khalisar set aside for the purpose. The Academy of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) was established a generation later, and the academy was housed in a hall that the magistrates of Vassholm erected near the harbour. The academies were intended to provide a place for the scholars of the cities to gather, to teach, and to record their observations, and the academies have continued to serve this purpose ever since.

The academies soon attracted scholars from every city of the three continents, and the scholars brought with them the traditions of the older temples, the older libraries, and the older schools. The chronicler notes that the academies were the foundation of the learned culture of the present age, and the academies are held in high esteem by every magistrate and every merchant of the three continents. The most important of the productions of the academies are the chronicles of the cities, the atlases of the trade routes, and the tables of the tides and the stars that the scholars have compiled.

## The Works of the Builders

The Long Peace was also a period of great building in the principal cities, and the buildings that were erected in this period are among the most admired of the present age. The great temple of the [Sea Mother's Court](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sea-mothers-court) at Khalisar was completed in the third generation of the Long Peace, and the temple is held to be the finest of the temples of the warm coast. The harbour works at Vassholm were enlarged and rebuilt, and the new quays accommodated the larger vessels that the growth of the trade required. The chronicler notes that the building was the work of several generations, and the building is recorded in the chronicles of the cities as a period of patient and necessary labour.

The building was sustained by the revenues of the trade and by the patronage of the magistrates and the merchant houses. The chronicler notes that the patronage was generous, and the generosity is the measure of the prosperity of the Long Peace. The buildings that were erected in this period are the buildings in which the present age lives and works, and the buildings are the foundation of the reputation of the cities as centres of the learned and the mercantile traditions.

## Disputes and Adjustments

The Long Peace was not without its disputes, and the principal disputes of the period were settled by the magistrates and the councils without recourse to arms. The most notable of these disputes was the quarrel between the merchants of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and the merchants of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) over the customs dues that the merchants of Nessara paid at the harbour of Khalisar. The quarrel was referred to the magistrates of both cities, and the magistrates appointed a commission of inquiry that included scholars from the academies of Khalisar and Vassholm. The commission reported that the dues were in accordance with the ancient accord, but recommended a reduction that would reflect the growth of the trade. The reduction was accepted by both parties, and the quarrel was settled.

The chronicler notes that the settlement of disputes by inquiry and mediation has been one of the most important of the legacies of the Long Peace, and the procedure has been followed in many subsequent generations. The academies have served as the principal source of the mediators, and the scholars of the academies are consulted by the magistrates and the councils of the cities in every dispute that requires an impartial judgement. The chronicler observes that the procedure is one of the quiet foundations of the public order of the present age.

## The End of the Long Peace

The Long Peace came to an end with the outbreak of the Stone Plague, a long pestilence that swept through the principal cities of the three continents and that carried off a substantial fraction of the population. The plague is recorded in the chronicles of the cities as the most severe visitation of disease in the recorded history of the present age, and the plague is the subject of several pages of the chronicles of the [Plague and Remedy](/content/belief/science/physical/plague-and-remedy). The plague was followed by a period of disruption in the trade routes and by a brief renewal of the rivalry between the parties to the long-settled peace.

The chronicler notes that the Long Peace has been remembered as a period of unusual prosperity and unusual concord, and the period is held in particular esteem by the magistrates and the merchants of the cities. The anniversaries of the peace are marked with appropriate ceremonies, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every city of the three continents. The chronicler observes that the Long Peace is the measure by which the magistrates and the councils judge the public order of their own day, and the public order is judged by the standard of the peace that was established in the generations following the Twin-Reaches Wars.

## Significance

The Long Peace is the longest stretch of years without open war in the recorded history of the present age, and the period is the foundation of the public order of the three continents for several generations. The academies, the buildings, and the procedures of mediation that were established in the Long Peace are the foundation of the learned and the mercantile traditions of the present age. The chronicler notes that the Long Peace is held in particular memory by the magistrates and the merchants of the cities, and the magistrates mark the anniversaries of the peace with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the present age, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every city of the three continents.
