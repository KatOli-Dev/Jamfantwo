## Generations Without General War

Following the [Twin-Reaches Wars](/content/history/twin-reaches-wars) and the [Twin-Reaches Peace](/content/history/twin-reaches-peace) that concluded them, a long stretch of years without open war between the principal cities set in. The academies refer to these generations as the Long Peace, although the name is more a convenience of periodisation than a description of unbroken calm. Skirmishes on the border marches, disputes between rival merchant houses, and occasional descents of reavers upon undefended coasts were never absent. What set the Long Peace apart was the absence of a general war between [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar), [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm), and the other principal cities of the three continents.

The Long Peace is conventionally dated from the accession of [Sera](/content/people/historical/sera-ambervale) and the signing of the peace, and it is taken to end with the outbreak of the [Stone Plague](/content/history/stone-plague). The intervening generations cover more than a century, the longest stretch of years without open war in the recorded history of the present age. The magistrates mark the anniversaries of the peace with ceremonies.

## Repair After the Twin-Reaches Wars

In the years immediately following the peace, both parties to the wars repaired the damage the long conflict had inflicted. The [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) rebuilt the towns that had been occupied by [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) garrisons, and the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) rebuilt the fleets that had been broken by [Thessa](/content/people/historical/thessa-nordhaven). The transit fee that the peace had established became a substantial source of revenue, applied to the roads, bridges, harbours, and lighthouses of the trade routes. The rebuilding was the work of two generations and required a sustained effort from both parties.

The rebuilding was accompanied by growth in the trade that crossed the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) from the inland valleys to the warm coast. Merchants of both parties invested in new caravans and new vessels and established new houses in the principal towns of the trade routes. The volume of goods crossing the highlands increased several times over the course of a single generation, and the transit fees that the peace had established grew accordingly. The growth was the foundation of the prosperity of the Long Peace.

## Establishment of the Great Academies

The Long Peace saw the founding of the principal academies of the present age. The Academy of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) was established in the second generation, housed in a complex of buildings the council of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) set aside for the purpose. Its first scholars were drawn from the older temple schools and included mathematicians, chroniclers, and students of the natural world. The Academy of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) was established a generation later, housed in a hall the magistrates erected near the harbour, with a particular emphasis on maritime studies and navigation. The academies provided a place for scholars to gather, to teach, and to record their observations, and they have continued to serve this purpose ever since.

The academies soon attracted scholars from every city of the three continents, who brought with them the traditions of the older temples, libraries, and schools. The academies became the foundation of the learned culture of the present age. Their most important productions include the chronicles of the cities, the atlases of the trade routes, and the tables of the tides and the stars.

## Temples, Harbours, and Public Architecture

The Long Peace was also a period of great building in the principal cities. The great [temple of the Sea Mother](/content/religion/temple-of-the-sea-mother)'s Court at Khalisar was completed in the third generation and is held to be the finest of the temples of the warm coast, with a main hall that can accommodate several hundred worshippers and a tower that serves as a landmark for vessels approaching the harbour. The harbour works at [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) were enlarged and rebuilt, with new quays accommodating the larger vessels that the growth of trade required and with a new lighthouse at the mouth of the harbour. The building was sustained by the revenues of the trade and the patronage of the magistrates and merchant houses. The buildings erected in this period are the buildings in which the present age lives and works.

## Settled by Mediation

The Long Peace was not without its disputes, but these were settled by the magistrates and councils without recourse to arms. The most notable was the quarrel between the merchants of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and the merchants of [Nessara](/content/location/settlement/city/nessara) over customs dues. The quarrel was referred to the magistrates of both cities, who appointed a commission of inquiry including scholars from the academies of Khalisar and Vassholm. The commission reported that the dues were in accordance with the ancient accord but recommended a reduction to reflect the growth of trade. The reduction was accepted by both parties.

The settlement of disputes by inquiry and mediation became one of the most important legacies of the Long Peace. The academies served as the principal source of mediators, and the scholars of the academies are consulted by magistrates and councils in every dispute requiring impartial judgement. The procedure was followed in later disputes between the fishing towns of the northern coast and between the mining settlements of the highlands.

## The Stone Plague Interruption

The Long Peace came to an end with the outbreak of the Stone Plague, a long pestilence that swept through the principal cities and carried off a substantial fraction of the population. The plague is recorded as the most severe visitation of disease in the recorded history of the present age and is the subject of several pages of the Plague and Remedy. The plague was followed by a period of disruption in the trade routes and a brief renewal of the rivalry between the parties to the long-settled peace.

The Long Peace has been remembered as a period of unusual prosperity and concord. The anniversaries of the peace are marked with ceremonies attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every city of the three continents. The Long Peace is the measure by which the magistrates and councils judge the public order of their own day.

## Measure of Public Order

The Long Peace is the longest stretch of years without open war in the recorded history of the present age and the foundation of the public order of the three continents for several generations. The academies, the buildings, and the procedures of mediation established in the Long Peace are the foundation of the learned and mercantile traditions of the present age. The magistrates mark the anniversaries of the peace with ceremonies attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every city of the three continents. The events that preceded and followed the peace are recorded in the [World Timeline](/content/history/world-timeline).
