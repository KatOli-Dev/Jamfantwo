## The Explorations from the Southern Academies

The Southern Reach Expeditions were a series of voyages and overland journeys undertaken by the scholars and the explorers of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach) in the middle period of the present age. The expeditions were organised by the academies of the Reach, which were among the oldest and most respected centres of learning in the present age, and they were intended to explore the unknown regions of the [Southern Lands](/content/location/natural/continent/southern-lands) and to document the peoples, the plants, and the animals that were found there.

The Southern Reach occupies the southernmost portion of the continent of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), a region of fertile valleys and rolling hills that is sheltered from the cold winds of the south by the [Whitewall Range](/content/location/natural/feature/whitewall-range). The Reach is known for its academies, which attract scholars from all three continents, and for its tradition of exploration and discovery, which has extended the knowledge of the known world to its present boundaries.

## The Tradition of the Reach

The tradition of exploration in the Southern Reach dates from the earliest centuries of the present age. The founders of the academies had been scholars of the ancient world, and they had preserved a body of knowledge that included accounts of the distant lands of the south. The accounts were fragmentary and often contradictory, but they suggested the existence of a vast continent to the south, separated from Aethelar by a narrow sea.

The scholars of the Reach were determined to verify the accounts and to explore the southern continent. They equipped a number of small vessels in the early centuries of the present age and sent them southward across the sea. The early voyages were not successful, as the vessels were too small to withstand the storms of the southern waters, and several of them were lost with all hands. The scholars persisted, however, and the improvements in shipbuilding and navigation that were made in the later centuries enabled them to undertake more ambitious expeditions.

The academies of the Reach also encouraged overland exploration of the interior of Aethelar. The scholars of the Reach were interested in the natural history of the continent, and they sent expeditions into the forests, the mountains, and the plains to document the plants and the animals that were found there. The expeditions returned with specimens and records that were preserved in the collections of the academies.

## The First Southern Expeditions

The first successful expedition to the Southern Lands was made in the early fourth century of the present age, by a scholar named Aeliana Truevoice of the academy of Khalisar. Aeliana had studied the accounts of the ancient scholars and had concluded that the southern continent could be reached by sailing westward from the Cape of Storms, following the prevailing current. She persuaded the council of the Southern Reach to equip a vessel for the attempt, and she set out from the port of [Southport](/content/location/settlement/town/southport) in the spring of the year 312.

Aeliana's voyage was successful. She sailed westward from the Cape of Storms for twenty days and sighted the coast of the Southern Lands. She landed upon a stretch of coast that was covered with a dense forest of unfamiliar trees, and she spent several weeks exploring the coast and collecting specimens. She encountered the people of the Southern Lands, who were a small and friendly people who lived in villages along the coast. Aeliana traded with the people and learned something of their language and their customs before returning to Southport in the autumn of the same year.

The report of Aeliana's voyage was received with great excitement in the Southern Reach. The council of the Reach equipped a second expedition in the following year, with a larger vessel and a company of scholars and naturalists. The second expedition explored the coast of the Southern Lands more thoroughly and established a permanent station on a small island off the coast, which served as a base for subsequent expeditions.

## The Expeditions of the Fifth Century

The expeditions of the fifth century were more ambitious than the earlier voyages. The scholars of the Reach had accumulated a substantial body of knowledge about the Southern Lands, and they were eager to explore the interior of the continent. The expeditions of the fifth century were larger and better equipped than the earlier voyages, and they included scholars of the natural sciences, the languages, and the cultures of the Southern Lands.

The most famous of the fifth-century expeditions was the expedition of the scholar Orin Aldmere, who led a party of scholars into the interior of the Southern Lands in the year 452. Orin's party travelled up a great river that flowed from the interior to the coast, and they documented the plants and the animals that they encountered along the way. Orin's expedition was notable for the quality of the records that it produced. Orin was a skilled artist, and he made detailed drawings of the plants, the animals, and the people that the party encountered. The fifth century also saw a number of expeditions into the interior of Aethelar. The Southern Reach Expeditions are among the most important achievements of the present age, extending the knowledge of the known world and documenting the peoples, the plants, and the animals of the Southern Lands.
