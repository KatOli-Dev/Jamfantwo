## The First Test of the Confederation

The First Plains War is the name given by the chroniclers of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) to the conflict fought between the Confederation and a rival confederation of kindreds from the eastern grasslands in the decade following the founding of the Confederation. The war lasted two years and resulted in the consolidation of the Confederation's control over the summer pastures of the central grasslands, establishing the Confederation as the dominant power of the plains. The chronicles of the member kindreds agree upon the principal events but differ in their interpretation, and the disagreement reflects the significance the war has assumed in the traditions of the plains.

The war was fought upon the open grasslands of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), stretching from the summer pastures claimed by the Confederation in the central grasslands to the eastern marches where the rival confederation held its seasonal camps. The war was decided by the mounted warriors of the two confederations in a series of engagements that the chronicles record in detail, for the war was the first occasion upon which the Confederation's levy had been called to battle.

## The Dispute Over the Summer Pastures

The war began with a dispute over the control of the great summer pastures of the central grasslands. The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) claimed the right to regulate the summer grazing by virtue of the agreements made at the founding council, to which the principal kindreds of the central grasslands had subscribed. The rival confederation of the eastern grasslands had not been invited to the founding council and regarded the Confederation as a threat to its own liberty and to the customary arrangements by which the eastern kindreds had used the summer pastures for generations.

The council of the Confederation sent a delegation to the eastern confederation, proposing a negotiation over the boundaries of the summer pastures and the rules by which the grazing would be regulated. The eastern confederation rejected the offer, arguing that the Confederation had no authority over the eastern kindreds and that the summer pastures were the common inheritance of all the grassland peoples. The council of the Confederation, after deliberation, resolved to summon the levy of the member kindreds to enforce the Confederation's claim.

## The Campaign of the First Year

The first year of the war was marked by the advance of the Confederation's levy into the eastern grasslands and the response of the eastern confederation's mounted warriors. The Confederation's levy was commanded by the chief elder of the council, who had been granted the authority of war leader by the assembled elders of the member kindreds. The levy numbered approximately two thousand mounted warriors, drawn from the principal kindreds of the central grasslands, and was supported by a train of remounts and supply wagons that followed the line of march.

The eastern confederation met the levy at a crossing of the principal river of the eastern grasslands. The battle was joined at dawn and continued through the morning hours, with the mounted warriors of the two confederations charging and withdrawing in the patterns that the grassland warfare had developed over generations. The Confederation's levy was the larger force, and the discipline of the member kindreds, who had trained together in the years since the founding, gave them an advantage over the looser formation of the eastern confederation.

The eastern confederation was driven back across the river with heavy loss. The levy pursued the retreating eastern warriors for several days, capturing the principal summer camps and taking many prisoners. The eastern confederation's council, recognising that the campaign was lost, sent a delegation to the Confederation's war leader to request a suspension of arms.

## The Negotiations of the Second Year

The second year of the war was dominated by the negotiations between the two confederations, conducted at a great camp established upon the central grasslands midway between the territories of the two parties. The elders of the member kindreds of the Confederation and the elders of the eastern confederation met under the mediation of the elders of a neutral kindred from the northern grasslands, who had maintained their neutrality throughout the war.

The negotiations were protracted and difficult. The Confederation insisted upon the recognition of its authority over the summer pastures of the central grasslands, basing its claim upon the agreements of the founding council. The eastern confederation disputed the claim, arguing that the founding council had not included the eastern kindreds and that the agreements made there could not bind those who had not consented to them.

The mediators proposed a compromise that recognised the Confederation's authority over the summer pastures of the central grasslands while reserving to the eastern kindreds the use of the eastern pastures and the right of transit across the central pastures during the migration seasons. The compromise was accepted by the councils of both confederations. The peace was signed at the great camp and marked by a feast attended by the elders of all the kindreds.

## The Consolidation of the Confederation's Authority

The aftermath of the war was a period of consolidation for the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation). The recognition of the Confederation's authority by the eastern confederation established the Confederation as the principal power of the plains, and the institutions of the council and the levy were strengthened by the experience of the war. The eastern kindreds were not required to join the Confederation, but over the following decades many of them chose to seek admission, recognising the benefits of membership in the alliance.

The war also established the pattern of plains warfare that would be followed in the later conflicts of the grasslands. The mounted levy of the member kindreds, the organisation of the war train, and the methods of negotiation and mediation that brought the war to an end were all developed during the First Plains War and refined in the wars that followed, most notably the [Three-Banners War](/content/history/three-banners-war) of the later period.

## The Memory of the War

The First Plains War is remembered in the chronicles of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) as the first test of the alliance and the proof of the strength of the institutions established at the founding. The anniversaries of the principal engagements are marked by ceremonies at the great summer camp, attended by the elders of the member kindreds and by the descendants of the warriors who fought in the war.

The eastern kindreds remember the war differently, as a defeat that led eventually to a lasting peace and to the prosperity that the eastern grasslands have enjoyed under the protection of the Confederation. The memory of the war is preserved in the songs and stories of the eastern kindreds, who recite the names of the warriors who fell in the defence of the eastern pastures.

The war is placed in the broader chronology of the age by the [World Timeline](/content/history/world-timeline), which records the First Plains War alongside the other conflicts and treaties of the period.
