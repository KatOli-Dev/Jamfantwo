## The Alliance of the Grasslands

The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) was founded in the year 340 of the present age, when the elders of the principal [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) and [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) gathered at the great summer camp and agreed to unite their kindreds into a single confederation. The founding was the result of a century of gradual cooperation among the kindreds, driven by the need to defend the grazing lands against the incursions of rival kindreds and to regulate the use of the summer pastures that were essential to the survival of the herds.

The year 340 is the date accepted by the academies, but the process of founding was not a single event. The confederation grew from a series of councils and accords that extended over several years, and the kindreds joined the confederation at different times. The chronicles of the plains record the founding as a gradual movement that was completed when the last of the principal kindreds acceded to the confederation, an event that some chronicles place as late as the year 348. The year 340 is maintained as the conventional date and is the date that the confederation marks in its ceremonies.

## The Gathering at the Summer Camp

The council that founded the confederation was convened by the elders of the Horseheart kindred, the largest and most influential of the plains kindreds of the period. The Horseheart elders had seen that the kindreds of the plains could not prosper while they were divided among themselves, each kindred claiming the right to graze its herds wherever it pleased and each kindred disputing the claims of the others. The elders judged that the only way to secure the peace and prosperity of the plains was to unite the kindreds under a single council that would regulate the grazing and adjudicate disputes.

The council was held at the great summer camp of the Horseheart kindred, which was the largest settlement of the plains at the time. The camp was located at a bend of the upper [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river), where the grass was tall and the water was abundant. The elders of the principal kindreds attended the council with their retinues, and the camp swelled to several thousand people for the duration of the proceedings. The council lasted for the whole summer, from the first greening of the grass to the first chill of the autumn winds.

The chronicles of the plains record the speeches that were made at the council. The elders of the Horseheart kindred argued that the kindreds of the plains shared a common way of life and a common interest in the preservation of the grazing lands. The elders of the other kindreds responded with their own arguments, some in favour of the confederation and some against it. The debate was vigorous but respectful, and the elders listened to each other with patience. The speeches are preserved in the memory of the kindreds and are recited at the ceremonies of the confederation.

## The Provisions of the Confederation

The confederation was established upon a set of provisions that were agreed by the council and recorded in the memory of the elders. The provisions served as the foundation of the confederation and have been revised from time to time but never replaced.

The first provision established the council of the confederation, which was composed of the elders of the member kindreds. The council was to meet twice each year, at the spring gathering and the autumn gathering, and was to hear disputes between kindreds and to regulate the use of the summer pastures. The council was to elect a chief elder from among its members, who would serve for a term of three years and would be responsible for executing the decisions of the council.

The second provision established the rules for the use of the summer pastures. The pastures were divided into districts, each district assigned to a particular kindred for the summer grazing season. The assignment was to be reviewed each year by the council, which would take account of the size of the herds and the condition of the pastures. The provision required the kindreds to respect the boundaries of the districts and to submit disputes over the boundaries to the council for adjudication.

The third provision established the military levy of the confederation. Each kindred was required to provide a number of mounted warriors proportional to the size of its herds, and the levy was to be commanded by the chief elder in time of war. The provision required the kindreds to maintain their warriors in readiness and to respond promptly to the summons of the chief elder.

## The Accession of the Centaur Kindreds

The accession of the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds to the confederation was a matter of particular importance. The centaurs were the oldest inhabitants of the plains and were the most skilled of the mounted warriors. The centaur kindreds had not been consulted in the early stages of the founding, as the human elders had assumed that the centaurs would not wish to join a confederation of human kindreds. The assumption proved incorrect, and the centaur elders sent a delegation to the council to request admission.

The delegation was received with honour by the council. The centaur elders argued that the centaur kindreds shared the same interest in the regulation of the pastures and the defence of the plains and that the confederation would be incomplete without them. The council agreed and invited the centaur kindreds to accede to the confederation on the same terms as the human kindreds. The centaur elders accepted, and the confederation was strengthened by the addition of their warriors and their knowledge of the plains.

The accession of the centaur kindreds is regarded by the chronicles of the plains as one of the most important acts of the founding council. The centaurs brought a depth of knowledge of the grasslands that the human kindreds lacked, and their warriors were the most formidable of the plains. The confederation that included both human and centaur kindreds was a more powerful and more complete institution than a confederation of human kindreds alone could have been.

## The Consolidation of the Confederation

The years following the founding were a period of consolidation, in which the kindreds that had joined the confederation learned to work together under the council. The council met regularly and heard the disputes that arose among the kindreds. The judgments of the council were accepted by the kindreds, and the peace of the plains was maintained.

The confederation faced its first test in the decade after the founding, when a rival confederation of kindreds from the eastern grasslands challenged the confederation's control of the summer pastures. The rival confederation had not been invited to the founding council and regarded the confederation as a threat to its own liberty. The council summoned the levy, and the mounted warriors of the member kindreds marched to meet the rival confederation. The war that followed is known as the [First Plains War](/content/history/first-plains-war) and is recorded in the chronicles of the confederation as the test that proved the strength of the new institution.

The war lasted for two years and was fought upon the open grasslands. The confederation was victorious, and the rival confederation was compelled to recognise the confederation's authority over the summer pastures. The victory established the confederation as the dominant power of the plains and secured the peace for a generation. The [Three-Banners War](/content/history/three-banners-war) of the later period followed a similar pattern of challenge and response, but the First Plains War was the first of the wars of the confederation and the foundation of its military tradition.

## The Legacy of the Founding

The founding of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) in the year 340 is one of the most important events in the history of the Great Grass Plains. The confederation brought peace to a region that had been divided by conflict for generations and established the institutions that have governed the plains for the centuries that followed. The council of the confederation, the rules for the use of the pastures, and the military levy are all the legacy of the founding.

The confederation has grown and changed over the centuries, as new kindreds have joined and old kindreds have merged or divided. The institutions of the confederation have been adapted to the changing circumstances of the plains, but the essential provisions of the founding have never been displaced. The confederation remains one of the principal powers of the present age, and its mounted warriors are among the most accomplished cavalry in the known world.

The memory of the founding is preserved in the ceremonies of the confederation. The anniversary of the founding council is marked by a gathering at the great summer camp, attended by the elders of the member kindreds and by the chief elder of the confederation. The gathering includes the recitation of the speeches of the founding council, the renewal of the oaths of the kindreds, and a great feast that celebrates the unity of the plains.
