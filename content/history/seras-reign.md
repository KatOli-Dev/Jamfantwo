## The Forty-Seven Years That Founded the Vael

[Sera Ambervale](/content/people/historical/sera-ambervale) is the conventional name for the forty-seven years during which she unified the warring principalities of the central river valleys of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and established the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) as a single kingdom. The reign is dated from her coronation in the hall of the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale) and is reckoned by the chroniclers of the kingdom as the first year of the Vaelic era. It is among the best documented reigns of the ancient age, for the royal archives of [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) preserve its records in unusual detail, and the chronicles of neighbouring realms corroborate the principal events with independent testimony.

## Conquest, Marriage, and Treaty

The unification that defines Sera's Reign was achieved by three means working in concert: military conquest of the principalities that resisted her authority, diplomatic marriage that bound the great houses of the valley to her line, and the negotiation of treaties that granted conquered territories a measure of autonomy within the new kingdom. The [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth), the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches), and the lords of the [Emberlands](/content/location/settlement/region/emberlands) were each brought to terms in turn, and the boundaries fixed by these agreements have remained largely unchanged through the centuries since. The conquest of each principality followed a similar pattern: a winter campaign timed to catch the defenders unprepared, followed by the offer of a treaty that left the conquered lords a measure of local authority in exchange for their allegiance to the new crown. This combination of decisive force and generous terms was the foundation of the loyalty the newly incorporated territories showed to Sera's successors, for few of the conquered houses had reason to regret the arrangement once the initial resistance had been overcome.

The diplomatic marriages that accompanied the military campaigns bound the great houses of the valley to Sera's line through two generations of alliances, and the treaties that followed the conquests were negotiated individually with each principality rather than imposed as a single settlement, a practice that allowed Sera to address the particular grievances and customs of each region. The chronicles record that Sera personally reviewed the terms of every treaty before it was signed, a degree of attention to detail that her contemporaries regarded as remarkable in a monarch still consolidating a newly won kingdom.

## The Code of Sera

The most enduring institution of the reign is the code of law Sera instituted, preserved in the royal archives of [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) and studied by jurists of the present age. The code was built upon the customary practices of the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale) and incorporated the [legal traditions](/content/law/legal-traditions-overview) of conquered regions where they did not conflict with the authority of the crown. Its provisions on the management of the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael), including the [Vael Water Law](/content/law/vael-water-law), are read aloud each year at the [Crown Harvest](/content/culture/crown-harvest) and remain the foundation of water governance in the valley.

The code was notable among the legal instruments of its age for the care with which it reconciled the differing customs of the principalities it absorbed. Rather than imposing the practices of the Amber Vale wholesale upon conquered regions, Sera's jurists catalogued the customary law of each territory and retained those provisions that did not conflict with the authority of the crown or the interests of the kingdom as a whole. This method of reconciliation, still taught to jurists of the present age as the Vaelic method, is regarded as one of the more significant contributions of the reign to the legal traditions of the continent, for it allowed a diverse collection of principalities to be governed by a single code without erasing the local customs that had governed them for generations.

## Governors, Roads, and the Court at Vaelmouth

Sera established a system of royal governors to administer the provinces of the new kingdom, a system her successors, including [Halric Vael](/content/people/historical/halric-vael), maintained without significant alteration. She invested in the construction of the Amber Road, connecting the western foothills to the eastern lowlands, and founded the city of [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) at the confluence of the two principal rivers, where she built the palace that has housed the rulers of Vael ever since. The court she established there drew poets, scholars, and artisans from every region of the continent, and she herself maintained correspondence with the scholars of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles).

The governors Sera appointed were drawn, wherever possible, from the local nobility of the province they administered rather than from courtiers of the Amber Vale, a policy that eased the transition of conquered territories into the kingdom by preserving a measure of local representation in the government. Each governor was confirmed in office by the crown for a customary term and was required to travel to Vaelmouth at intervals to render an account of the province's affairs, a practice that kept the provinces bound to the capital without requiring the constant presence of royal officials in every corner of the realm. The Amber Road itself, running the length of the kingdom, was as much an instrument of governance as of commerce, for it allowed the governors and their reports to reach Vaelmouth in a fraction of the time the older tracks had required.

## The Standard by Which Successors Are Measured

Sera died in the sixty-eighth year of her life and was interred in the tomb overlooking Vaelmouth, where the tomb remains a place of pilgrimage. The institutions of her reign — the code of law, the governors, the roads, and the capital she founded — have endured through the centuries that followed, adapted by her successors but recognisably the same in their essential structure. Sera is invoked by the rulers of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) in every age as the model of the just and effective sovereign, and her reign remains the standard by which the [World Timeline](/content/history/world-timeline) reckons the founding of the kingdom.

The chronicles of the Vael record that no subsequent monarch has matched the length of Sera's reign, though several, including Halric Vael, have come close, and none has matched the scale of the transformation she achieved in uniting the warring principalities of the river valleys into a single, enduring kingdom. The anniversary of her coronation remains the principal observance of the Vaelic calendar, marked in every province of the kingdom she founded.
