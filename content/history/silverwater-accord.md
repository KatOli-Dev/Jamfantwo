## The Eastern River Agreement

The Silverwater Accord is the name given by the chroniclers of the eastern coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) to the river agreement modelled upon the [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact), regulating the navigation, the fishery, and the use of the banks of the principal river of the eastern coastal plain. The accord was negotiated between the magistrates of the river towns of the eastern coast and the representatives of the farming and fishing communities that depended upon the river for their livelihood, and it established the office of the river warden for the eastern river system.

The accord was adopted several centuries after the Greywash Compact and followed the same structure, establishing similar rules for the use of the river and similar offices for the administration of those rules. The framers of the accord explicitly acknowledged their debt to the Greywash Compact, which they described in the preamble of the accord as the model and the inspiration for their own agreement.

## The Need for Regulation on the Eastern Coast

The river of the eastern coastal plain, known in the common speech as the Silverwater for the pale gleam of its surface in the morning light, had been the subject of increasing conflict in the centuries before the accord was negotiated. The river was the principal waterway of the eastern coast, carrying the trade of the interior to the port cities of the coast and supporting a substantial fishery that fed the communities along its banks.

The conflicts arose from the competing uses of the river and the absence of any agreed rules for their resolution. The navigation of the river was impeded by fishing weirs and nets that stretched across the channel, and the fishery was damaged by the pollution from the tanneries and dye-works that lined the banks. The farmers of the floodplain diverted water for irrigation in a manner that reduced the flow of the river and harmed the navigation and the fishery.

The magistrates of the principal river towns resolved to negotiate a comprehensive agreement that would establish the rules for the use of the river and create the institutions necessary to administer those rules.

## The Provisions of the Accord

The Silverwater Accord established a system of rules and institutions modelled on the [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact). The accord defined the rights of navigation, the regulation of the fishery, the protection of the banks, and the management of the waters of the eastern river in terms adapted to the conditions of the eastern coastal plain.

The navigation provisions of the accord established the right of free passage for all vessels upon the river, subject to the payment of a small transit fee at the principal towns. The fee was set at a rate sufficient to maintain the navigation works and the marker posts that guided the vessels through the shallow reaches and was collected by the port masters of the river towns.

The fishery provisions of the accord regulated the taking of fish from the river and established the seasons during which fishing was permitted, the types of nets that could be used, and the minimum size of the fish that could be taken. The provisions were designed to preserve the fishery for the future while allowing the fishing communities to earn their livelihood from the river.

The bank protection provisions of the accord required the owners of the land along the river to maintain the banks in good condition and prohibited the dumping of waste and the discharge of pollutants into the river. The provisions were enforced by the river warden, who had the authority to inspect the banks and to impose fines upon those who violated the accord.

## The Office of the Eastern River Warden

The office of the river warden was established by the Silverwater Accord on the model of the Greywash office, with a single warden appointed by the magistrates of the principal river towns to oversee the administration of the accord. The warden was given the authority to enforce the provisions of the accord, to settle disputes between users of the river, and to report to the magistrates on the state of the river and the compliance of those who used it.

The warden was supported by a small staff of deputies and assistants who patrolled the river and inspected the banks. The warden also maintained a register of the vessels that used the river, the fishing permits that had been granted, and the fines that had been imposed for violations of the accord.

The warden's decisions could be appealed to the magistrates of the principal river towns, providing a check upon the warden's authority and ensuring that the accord was administered fairly and consistently.

## The Implementation and Revision

The implementation of the accord required several years of careful work by the magistrates and the river warden. The warden surveyed the river from its mouth to the head of navigation, recording the state of the channel, the location of the fishing weirs and the irrigation diversions, and the condition of the banks. The violators of the accord were brought before the magistrates, who imposed the prescribed fines.

The accord has been revised from time to time to reflect changes in the navigation, the fishery, and the settlement patterns of the eastern coastal plain, but the revisions have been few and carefully considered. The accord remains in force in the present age and is the foundation of public order for the eastern river system.

## The Accord and the Communities of the River

The Silverwater Accord was not merely a legal instrument but a social compact that bound together the communities of the river in a common enterprise. The magistrates of the river towns, the fishing communities, and the farmers of the floodplain each had a stake in the success of the accord, and each contributed to the work of maintaining the river and regulating its use.

The accord established an annual assembly of the representatives of the river communities, at which the state of the river was reviewed, the accounts of the transit fees were examined, and the proposals for revision of the accord were discussed. The assembly met at the principal river town and was presided over by the river warden, who reported on the work of the past year and the challenges that lay ahead.

The assembly was also the occasion for the renewal of the commitments that the communities had made to one another. The representatives of the fishing communities affirmed their commitment to the fishery provisions, the representatives of the farming communities affirmed their commitment to the bank protection provisions, and the magistrates of the river towns confirmed the appointment of the river warden for the coming year.

## The Accord in the Wider Order

The Silverwater Accord is one of several river agreements that have been modelled upon the [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact), and it is regarded by the magistrates of the eastern coast as a successful adaptation of the Greywash model to the conditions of a different region. The accord is placed in the broader chronology of the age by the [World Timeline](/content/history/world-timeline), which records the adoption of the accord alongside the other river agreements and treaties of the period.
