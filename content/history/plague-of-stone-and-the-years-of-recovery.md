## Overview

The Plague of Stone is the conventional name for the long pestilence that swept through the principal cities of the three continents in the generations following the [Long Peace of the Twin Cities](/content/history/long-peace-of-the-twin-cities). The plague took its name from the appearance of the afflicted, whose skin took on a grey, stony cast in the final stages of the disease. The plague is recorded in the chronicles of every city of the present age, and the plague is the most severe visitation of disease in the recorded history of the age. The chroniclers of the academies date the plague by the reign of the kings and the magistrates who presided over the response, and the convention varies from city to city.

The plague first appeared in the trading ports of the warm coast in the spring of a year that is now lost, and the plague spread rapidly along the trade routes into the inland valleys and the cold north. The plague reached the cities of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) within the same season, and the plague reached the dwarven holds of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) within the year. The chroniclers of the [Study of Plague and Remedy](/content/belief/science/physical/study-of-plague-and-remedy) note that the rapid spread of the plague is a measure of the growth of the trade that had taken place in the Long Peace, and the growth is both the foundation of the prosperity of the preceding generations and the vehicle by which the plague was carried.

## The Course of the Disease

The plague began with a fever and a swelling of the glands of the neck and the armpits, and the disease advanced through several stages that the healers of the academies have recorded in detail. The first stage lasted three or four days, and the patient suffered from the fever, the thirst, and a restlessness that prevented sleep. The second stage lasted as long again, and the patient broke out in dark spots upon the skin and suffered from a cough that brought up blood. The third stage lasted a few days more, and the patient took on the grey, stony cast that gave the plague its name, and the patient died.

The healers of the academies tried many remedies, and the chronicles of the [Study of Plague and Remedy](/content/belief/science/physical/study-of-plague-and-remedy) record the trials in detail. The trials included the bleeding of the patient, the dosing with herbs and minerals, the application of poultices, and the recitation of prayers and incantations. The trials were mostly unsuccessful, and the healers who recorded the trials often noted that the patient died despite the remedy, or that the patient recovered without any remedy that could be distinguished from the natural course of the disease. The chronicler observes that the healers of the academies were honest in their records, and the honesty is the foundation of the modern practice of the management of disease.

## The Magistrates and the Response

The magistrates of the principal cities responded to the plague with the measures that the public health tradition of the present age had established. The magistrates of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) closed the harbour to vessels from the infected ports, and the magistrates of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) closed the gates of the city to travellers from the south. The magistrates of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) sealed the passes of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) and refused entry to all who could not prove that they had not been in contact with the disease. The measures were effective in slowing the spread of the plague, but the measures were not sufficient to prevent the plague from entering the cities, and the plague took hold in every principal settlement of the three continents within a few years.

The chronicler notes that the magistrates were tireless in their response, and the magistrates worked in concert with the healers and the priests of the temples. The magistrates appointed commissioners of public health in every district of the cities, and the commissioners oversaw the separation of the sick from the well, the purification of the air and the water, and the careful disposal of the dead. The measures were not always successful, and the commissioners recorded the failures as carefully as they recorded the successes. The chronicler observes that the measures were the foundation of the modern practice of the management of a plague, and the modern practice is the legacy of the magistracy of the years of the Plague of Stone.

## The Loss of Life

The plague carried off a substantial fraction of the population of the three continents, and the chronicles of the cities record the losses in detail. The cities of the warm coast lost nearly a third of their inhabitants; the cities of the cold north lost nearly a quarter; the dwarven holds of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) lost a fifth. The countryside lost less, but the countryside was severely affected in the regions that lay along the trade routes. The chronicler notes that the losses were greatest in the cities that had grown most rapidly during the Long Peace, and the growth is the measure of the exposure of the cities to the disease.

The loss of life was accompanied by a loss of learning and a loss of skill, and the losses were recorded by the chroniclers of the academies as the most severe of the consequences of the plague. Many of the scholars and the craftsmen of the Long Peace died in the plague, and their successors had to reconstruct the traditions that the plague had interrupted. The chronicler notes that the reconstruction took several generations, and the reconstruction is the measure of the severity of the loss.

## The Years of Recovery

The years following the plague were a period of recovery, and the recovery was the work of several generations. The magistrates and the councils of the cities encouraged the return of the trade, and the trade brought the merchants and the craftsmen back to the cities. The academies reopened their doors, and the scholars resumed the compilation of the chronicles, the atlases, and the tables that the plague had interrupted. The temples reopened their altars, and the priests resumed the rites that the plague had suspended. The chronicler notes that the recovery was patient and necessary, and the recovery is recorded in the chronicles of the cities as a period of quiet rebuilding.

The recovery was accompanied by a renewed interest in the practical disciplines, and the [Study of Plague and Remedy](/content/belief/science/physical/study-of-plague-and-remedy) in particular was the subject of a great deal of attention from the scholars of the academies. The scholars compiled the records of the plague that the healers had made during the visitation, and the scholars revised the catalogues of remedies in light of the experience. The chronicler notes that the renewed interest was the foundation of the modern practice of the management of disease, and the modern practice is the legacy of the years of recovery.

## The Memories of the Plague

The plague is remembered in the chronicles of every city of the present age, and the memories of the plague are preserved in the public observances that the magistrates and the priests conduct each year. The observances include a procession through the streets, a service in the temples, and a reading of the names of the dead. The chronicler notes that the observances are among the most solemn of the public ceremonies of the present age, and the observances are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every city of the three continents.

The plague is also remembered in the chronicles of the academies, and the chronicles are consulted by every scholar who must judge a question of public health. The chronicles of the [Study of Plague and Remedy](/content/belief/science/physical/study-of-plague-and-remedy) are the most complete of the records of the plague, and the chronicles are the foundation of the modern practice of the management of disease. The chronicler observes that the plague has been the source of much of the most important work of the academies, and the work is the measure of the severity of the visitation.

## Significance

The Plague of Stone is the most severe visitation of disease in the recorded history of the present age, and the plague is the foundation of the modern practice of the management of disease. The measures that the magistrates established in response to the plague, the records that the healers compiled during the visitation, and the renewed interest in the practical disciplines that the plague provoked are the legacy of the years of the Plague of Stone. The chronicler notes that the plague is held in particular memory by the magistrates and the healers of every city, and the plague is the measure by which the magistrates and the healers judge the public health of their own day.
