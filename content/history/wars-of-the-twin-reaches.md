## Overview

The Wars of the Twin Reaches is the conventional name for the long conflict that was waged, in the generations following the founding of the principal cities, between the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) for the control of the inland valleys and the trade routes that crossed the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) into the warm coast. The wars lasted for several generations and were fought by land and by sea, and the wars are remembered as the most destructive of the conflicts of the early history of the present age. The chroniclers of the academies date the wars by the reign of [Queen Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale), whose reign is conventionally placed in the latter half of the conflict, and whose death is taken as the conventional end of the wars.

The wars are documented in the chronicles of both parties, and the chronicles agree upon the principal events but disagree upon their meaning. The chronicles of the Amurra Republic present the wars as a defensive struggle against the aggression of the Vashari Pact; the chronicles of the Vashari Pact present the wars as a defensive struggle against the encirclement of the Amurra Republic. The chroniclers of the academies note that both accounts contain elements of truth, and that the wars were the product of a long-standing rivalry for the trade of the inland valleys rather than the deliberate aggression of either party.

## The Outbreak of the War

The wars began with a dispute over the customs dues that the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) collected from the merchants who crossed the Stoneheim Highlands into the warm coast. The Republic had long claimed the right to collect the dues by virtue of the ancient accord that the founders of the Republic had made with the founders of the cities of the warm coast. The [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) disputed the claim on the grounds that the accord had been made before the Pact was founded, and that the Pact was not bound by an instrument to which it had not been a party. The dispute was referred to the magistrates of the principal towns, and the magistrates decided against the Pact. The Pact refused to accept the decision, and the Republic imposed a blockade upon the ports of the Pact.

The blockade was effective, for the Pact depended upon the export of grain and of salt to the warm coast, and the Republic controlled the principal outlets. The Pact retaliated by sending raiders into the valleys of the Republic, and the raiders burned the warehouses and drove off the cattle of the principal merchants. The Republic responded by sending an army into the valleys, and the army occupied the principal towns of the Pact and imposed a garrison upon each. The war had begun, and the war continued for several generations with varying fortunes.

## The Course of the War

The course of the war is conventionally divided into three phases: the phase of the Republic's ascendancy, the phase of the Pact's recovery, and the phase of the negotiations that led to the peace. The phase of the Republic's ascendancy lasted for the first generation of the war, and the phase was marked by the victories of the Republic's armies in the valleys and the Republic's fleets upon the sea. The phase of the Pact's recovery lasted for the second and third generations, and the phase was marked by the appointment of [Admiral Thessa](/content/people/historical/admiral-thessa-of-the-northern-fleet) to the command of the Pact's fleet and by the construction of new ships in the Pact's yards. The phase of the negotiations lasted for the last generation, and the phase was marked by the efforts of the mediators of the academies to bring the parties to a settlement.

The phase of the Republic's ascendancy saw the Republic in control of the principal trade routes and the principal towns of the valleys. The Republic imposed a garrison upon each town, and the garrisons were sustained by the customs dues that the Republic continued to collect. The Republic also maintained a fleet that controlled the principal harbours of the warm coast, and the fleet prevented the Pact from exporting its goods by sea. The chronicler notes that the Republic's ascendancy was marked by a great deal of hardship in the Pact, and the hardship is recorded in the chronicles of the Pact as the darkest period of the wars.

The phase of the Pact's recovery began with the appointment of [Admiral Thessa](/content/people/historical/admiral-thessa-of-the-northern-fleet) to the command of the Pact's fleet. Thessa was a veteran of the smaller engagements of the wars, and she had won the confidence of the merchants of the Pact by her refusal to accept the Republic's terms. Thessa rebuilt the fleet from the resources of the Pact's shipyards, and she trained the crews in the new tactics that she had observed in the Republic's fleet. Thessa met the Republic's fleet in the waters off the southern coast, and Thessa won a decisive victory that broke the Republic's control of the harbours. The victory is recorded in the chronicles of the Pact as the turning of the war.

## The Phase of the Negotiations

The phase of the negotiations began with the accession of [Queen Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale) to the throne of the Amurra Republic. Sera had been a junior magistrate of the Republic at the time of her accession, and she had no experience of war. Sera judged that the war had reached a stalemate, and that the Republic could not win without incurring costs that would be disproportionate to the benefits. Sera opened negotiations with the Pact through the mediators of the academies of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm), and the negotiations continued for several years.

The negotiations were complicated by the insistence of each party upon the rights that it claimed at the beginning of the war. The Republic insisted upon the customs dues by virtue of the ancient accord; the Pact insisted upon the right to export its goods without hindrance by virtue of the natural liberty of trade. The mediators proposed a compromise that abolished the customs dues and replaced them with a small transit fee that was shared between the two parties, and the compromise was accepted by Sera and by the council of the Pact. The peace was signed at the temple of [the Faith of the Sea Mother](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/faith-of-the-sea-mother) at Khalisar, and the peace is known in the chronicles as the Wars of the Twin Reaches.

## The Aftermath

The aftermath of the wars was marked by a long period of reconstruction in both parties. The Pact rebuilt the towns that had been occupied by the Republic, and the Republic rebuilt the fleets that had been destroyed by Thessa. The transit fee that the peace had established became a substantial source of revenue for both parties, and the revenue was used to repair the roads, the bridges, and the harbours of the trade routes. The chronicler notes that the wars left a permanent mark upon the relations of the two parties, and the relations have been marked by a mixture of rivalry and cooperation ever since.

The wars are remembered in the chronicles of both parties as a long and unnecessary conflict, and the memory of the wars has been the foundation of the reluctance of both parties to take up arms against each other in the generations that have followed. The chronicler notes that the peace has held for several centuries, and the peace is held in high esteem by the magistrates and the merchants of both parties. The academies of the warm coast and the cold north have preserved the chronicles of the wars, and the chronicles are consulted by every magistrate who must judge a dispute upon the trade routes.

## Significance

The Wars of the Twin Reaches are the most destructive of the conflicts of the early history of the present age, and the wars are the foundation of the political relations of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) for several centuries. The peace that was negotiated by [Queen Sera](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale) is the foundation of the trade of the inland valleys and the warm coast, and the peace is the measure by which the magistrates and the merchants of both parties judge the disputes of their own day. The chronicler notes that the wars are held in particular memory by the citizens of both parties, and the citizens mark the anniversaries of the principal battles with the appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of both parties, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every city of the two parties.
