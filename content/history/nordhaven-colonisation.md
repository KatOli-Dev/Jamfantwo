## The Human Settlement of the Cold Coast

The Nordhaven Colonisation is the name given to the period in which [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) settlers from the northern shores of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) established permanent settlements upon the coast of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), the cold and mountainous continent of the north. The colonisation was one of the most difficult and dangerous enterprises of the present age, requiring the settlers to cross the stormy waters of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) and to establish themselves upon a coast that was both inhospitable and already inhabited by the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) and other peoples of the cold north.

The colonisation began in the early centuries of the present age, in the generation following the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era), and continued at intervals for several centuries. The settlers came from a number of the northern communities of Aethelar, including the fishing villages of the [Grey Coast](/content/location/settlement/region/grey-coast) and the farming settlements of the [Velden Coastlands](/content/location/settlement/region/velden-coastlands). They were driven by a combination of population pressure, the search for new fishing grounds, and the hope of finding a land that they could make their own.

## The Voyage Across the Stormfall Sea

The crossing of the Stormfall Sea was the first and most dangerous part of the colonisation. The sea was known for its violent storms, its strong and unpredictable currents, and its cold waters. The vessels available to the settlers were small and poorly suited to the crossing, and many of the early attempts ended in disaster. The chronicles of the period record a number of voyages that set out from the Grey Coast and were never heard from again.

The first successful crossing was made by a pilot named Astrid the Sea-Wife, who set out from the fishing village of Torshavn in the third century of the present age. Astrid had studied the winds and currents of the Stormfall Sea for several years and judged that a route following the northern edge of the prevailing current would carry a vessel to the Morvind coast. She set out with a crew of twenty and made the crossing in fifteen days, landing upon the southern coast of Morvind at a natural harbour that she named Nordhaven, meaning northern harbour in the old tongue.

The chronicles record that Astrid and her crew spent the first winter in a shelter built from the timbers of their vessel, which had been damaged in the crossing and could not be repaired until the spring. The winter was harsh, but the settlers survived by hunting the seals and seabirds of the coast and by trading with the people of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), who inhabited the interior. In the spring, Astrid returned to Torshavn with a cargo of furs and seal-oil and with reports of a land of abundant fish and game.

## The Establishment of the First Settlements

Astrid's report inspired a wave of migration to the Morvind coast. The fishing villages of the Grey Coast were overcrowded, and the young people were eager for land of their own. Vessels began to make the crossing on a regular basis, carrying settlers, livestock, and supplies to the new land. The settlement at Nordhaven grew rapidly and was soon joined by other settlements along the coast.

The first settlements were small and vulnerable. The settlers built their houses from timber and stone, relying upon the abundant forests of the coast for materials. They lived by fishing, hunting, and farming, supplementing their diet with seal and whale meat when they could obtain it. The settlements were connected by a network of coastal trails that were hazardous in the winter and that required constant maintenance to keep them passable.

The settlers also established relations with the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), who inhabited the interior of the Morvind peninsula. The initial contacts were cautious, as the clans were wary of the newcomers and uncertain of their intentions. The settlers offered gifts of steel tools and woven cloth, which the clans accepted and reciprocated with furs and dried fish. The trade that developed between the settlers and the clans was essential to the survival of the early settlements, providing the settlers with goods that they could not produce themselves.

## The Conflicts with the Frostwall Clans

The relations between the settlers and the Frostwall Clans were not always peaceful. As the settlements grew and expanded inland, the settlers encroached upon territories that the clans regarded as their own. The clans resisted the encroachment, and a series of conflicts broke out that lasted for several generations.

The first conflict occurred in the early fourth century, when a party of settlers from the settlement of Bjornsholm attempted to establish a farm upon land that a Frostwall clan used for summer grazing. The clan attacked the farm and drove the settlers back to the coast. The settlers retaliated by raiding the clan's winter camp, and the conflict escalated into a war that lasted for three years.

The war was fought in the forests and mountains of the Morvind coast, where the terrain favoured the clans, who knew the land well and moved through it with ease. The settlers were at a disadvantage, as they lacked the knowledge of the land and the skills of the clans. The war ended with a treaty that established a boundary between the territory of the settlers and the territory of the clans, but the boundary was disputed from the beginning and the conflicts continued at intervals for the next century.

The conflicts were eventually resolved by a more comprehensive treaty, negotiated by the elders of the clans and the magistrates of the settlements. The treaty established a council of elders from both communities to adjudicate disputes over land and resources. The council has operated for several centuries and has been one of the most successful institutions of the Morvind coast, maintaining the peace between the settlers and the clans for most of the period that followed.

## The Development of the Nordhaven Kingdom

The settlements of the Morvind coast grew and prospered over the centuries, and in the late fifth century the principal settlements united to form the kingdom of [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven). The kingdom was established by a council of the magistrates of the settlements, who agreed to establish a single government for the coast. The council elected a king from among the most respected of the magistrates, and the king was confirmed by the assembly of the citizens.

The kingdom of Nordhaven grew to control the entire southern coast of Morvind and the principal passes of the interior. The kingdom established a fleet that patrolled the Stormfall Sea and protected the trade routes from pirates and rival powers. The fleet was built in the shipyards of the capital, which had grown from the original settlement of Nordhaven into a substantial city.

The kingdom also established relations with the other powers of the present age, including the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) and the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm). The relations were generally friendly, as the kingdoms shared a common interest in the trade of the northern seas and in the defence of the north against external threats. The kingdoms of the north have maintained an alliance that has lasted for several centuries.

## The Legacy of the Colonisation

The Nordhaven Colonisation was one of the most remarkable achievements of the present age, establishing human settlements upon a continent that was both inhospitable and already inhabited. The settlers who crossed the Stormfall Sea and built their homes upon the cold coast of Morvind demonstrated a courage and determination that is admired by the peoples of all three continents.

The legacy of the colonisation is visible in the cities and towns of the Nordhaven coast and in the institutions that the settlers established. The kingdom of [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven), the council of elders that maintains the peace with the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), and the fleet that patrols the Stormfall Sea are all the legacy of the colonisation. The memory of the first crossing, the early settlements, and the conflicts with the clans is preserved in the chronicles of the kingdom and in the ceremonies of the settlements.
