## The Sickness That Devastated the Desert Coast

The [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) Plague was a severe epidemic that swept the coastal regions of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) in the middle period of the present age, causing great loss of life and disrupting the trade and the governance of the desert coast. The plague was distinct from the [Stone Plague](/content/history/stone-plague) that affected the dwarven highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) in the same period, though the two epidemics share a number of features in the manner of their spread and their impact upon the affected populations.

The plague is described in the chronicles of the Sahrani coastal cities, including [Sahran](/content/location/settlement/city/sahran), [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir), and [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar). The chronicles record the arrival of the plague, its progress along the coast, and the measures that the authorities took to contain it. The academies have studied the chronicles and have compared the Sahrani Plague with the [Stone Plague](/content/history/stone-plague) and other epidemics of the present age, but the cause of the plague remains uncertain.

## The Arrival of the Plague

The plague is said to have arrived in [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) aboard a merchant vessel from the distant southern continent, a land that the scholars of the present age know as the [Southern Lands](/content/location/natural/continent/southern-lands). The vessel had been trading along the southern coast of the continent and had taken on a cargo of spices and woods from the interior. The crew of the vessel had fallen ill during the voyage, and several of them had died before the vessel reached the port of [Sahran](/content/location/settlement/city/sahran).

The magistrates of Sahran quarantined the vessel upon its arrival and prevented the crew from going ashore. The quarantine was effective for a time, but the plague spread from the vessel to the shore through contact with the cargo. The spices and woods that the vessel carried were inspected by merchants and porters before the quarantine was fully established, and the handlers carried the sickness into the city.

The plague spread rapidly through Sahran, which was a crowded and unsanitary port city with a large population of merchants, sailors, and labourers. The sickness struck the poor first, as they lived in the most crowded conditions and had the least access to the physicians. The mortality rate was high, and the chronicles record that the dead were buried in mass graves outside the city walls.

## The Progress of the Plague Along the Coast

From Sahran, the plague spread along the coast to the other cities of the Sahrani coast. The spread was facilitated by the trade routes that connected the cities, as the merchants and sailors who travelled between the ports carried the sickness with them. The plague reached [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir) within a month of its arrival in Sahran and reached [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) within two months.

The plague spread more slowly along the coast than it had within Sahran, as the cities were separated by stretches of open country that the sickness could not cross easily. The authorities of the cities were able to take some measures to contain the spread, including the establishment of quarantine stations at the principal ports and the restriction of travel between the cities. The measures were not fully effective, as the merchants and sailors found ways to evade the quarantines, but they slowed the progress of the plague and saved many lives.

The plague reached its peak in the second year of the epidemic, when it was present in every city of the Sahrani coast and in many of the inland settlements. The mortality rate was highest in the cities, where the population was densest and the sickness spread most easily. The rural settlements were less affected, as the population was more dispersed and the contacts between households were less frequent.

## The Response of the Authorities

The response of the authorities to the plague varied from city to city, depending upon the resources and the traditions of the local government. The authorities of Sahran imposed a strict quarantine upon the affected districts of the city, isolating the sick from the healthy and providing food and water to the quarantined households through a system of designated carriers. The authorities of Qassir took a different approach, closing the city to all traffic and requiring the citizens to remain in their homes for the duration of the epidemic.

The authorities of Khalisar, which was the largest and richest city of the coast, took the most comprehensive approach. The council of Khalisar established a commission of physicians and magistrates to manage the response to the plague. The commission imposed a quarantine upon the port, restricted travel between the city and the surrounding country, and established a system of hospitals for the care of the sick. The commission also organised the burial of the dead and the disposal of the belongings of the deceased, which were considered to be a source of contagion.

The response of the authorities was not always effective, and the chronicles record a number of failures and shortcomings. The quarantines were difficult to enforce, as the citizens resented the restrictions and the merchants lost money from the interruption of trade. The hospitals were overcrowded and understaffed, and many of the sick died from neglect as well as from the sickness itself. The burial of the dead was a particular problem, as the number of bodies overwhelmed the capacity of the cemeteries and the mass graves were dug in haste.

## The Economic and Social Impact

The economic impact of the plague was severe and long-lasting. The trade of the Sahrani coast was disrupted for several years, as the ports were closed or restricted and the merchants were unable to move their goods. The loss of trade was a heavy blow to the economies of the coastal cities, which depended upon the trade for their prosperity. The cities that were most dependent upon the trade, including Sahran and Qassir, suffered the most severe economic decline.

The social impact of the plague was equally severe. The loss of life was great, and the chronicles record that many families were left without breadwinners and that the orphaned children were taken in by the temples and the charitable institutions. The plague also affected the governance of the cities, as many magistrates and administrators died of the sickness and the councils were forced to appoint replacements who were less experienced and less capable.

The plague also had a lasting effect upon the culture of the Sahrani coast. The experience of the plague led to a greater emphasis upon the care of the sick and the burial of the dead, and the charitable institutions of the coast were expanded and strengthened. The temples of the coast established new rites for the treatment of the sick and the comfort of the dying, and the academies of the coast studied the causes and the treatment of the plague and established the foundations of the medical knowledge of the present age.

## The End of the Plague

The plague began to subside in the third year of the epidemic, as the population of the affected cities developed a resistance to the sickness and as the measures of the authorities began to take effect. The mortality rate declined gradually, and the number of new cases decreased. By the end of the third year, the plague had largely run its course, and the cities of the coast began to return to normal.

The chronicles record that the plague ended as mysteriously as it had begun. The physicians of the time were unable to explain why the sickness had arrived when it did or why it had subsided when it did. The academies of the present age have studied the records of the plague and have proposed a number of theories, but the cause of the plague remains uncertain. The scholars of the academies agree that the plague was carried by the merchant vessel from the southern continent, but they disagree about the nature of the sickness and the reasons for its spread and its decline.

## The Legacy of the Plague

The Sahrani Plague was one of the most devastating epidemics of the present age, causing great loss of life and disrupting the trade and the governance of the desert coast. The legacy of the plague is visible in the institutions that were established in its aftermath, including the quarantine stations, the hospitals, and the charitable institutions that serve the cities of the coast.

The memory of the plague is preserved in the chronicles of the Sahrani cities and in the ceremonies of the coast. The anniversary of the arrival of the plague is marked by a service in the temples of the coast, at which the citizens remember the dead and give thanks for the survival of the living.
