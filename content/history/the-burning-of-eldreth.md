## The Great Fire of the Merchant City

The Burning of Eldreth was a great fire that destroyed a substantial portion of the city of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) in the late fifth century of the present age. The fire broke out in the warehouse district of the city and spread rapidly through the densely built streets, consuming warehouses, workshops, markets, and dwellings over the course of three days. The fire was the most destructive event in the history of Eldreth and is remembered in the chronicles of the city as a turning point in its development.

Eldreth was one of the principal cities of the coast of Aethelar, a centre of trade and commerce that rivalled [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) in wealth and influence. The city was built upon a natural harbour at the mouth of the Greywash River and was renowned for its markets, its temples, and its public buildings. The fire destroyed many of the most important buildings of the city and caused great hardship to the citizens who lost their homes and their livelihoods.

## The Conditions That Favoured the Fire

The conditions in Eldreth at the time of the fire were favourable to the rapid spread of the flames. The city was densely built, with narrow streets and closely spaced buildings constructed from timber and plaster. The buildings were tall, with upper storeys that projected over the streets and that could carry a fire from one side of the street to the other. The warehouse district, where the fire broke out, was particularly vulnerable, as the warehouses were filled with combustible goods, including timber, cloth, and grain.

The chronicles record that the summer of the fire had been unusually dry and that the city was suffering from a shortage of water. The public wells were low, and the cisterns that supplied the city with water for fighting fires were not fully charged. The conditions were made worse by the strong winds that were blowing from the sea, driving the flames inland and spreading the fire from building to building with great speed.

The fire broke out in a warehouse in the southern district of the city, near the harbour. The cause of the fire was never determined with certainty. The chronicles of the city record a number of theories, including the spontaneous combustion of oils stored in the warehouse, the carelessness of a labourer who had been working with fire, and the deliberate act of an arsonist. The academies have studied the records of the fire but have been unable to reach a consensus on the cause.

## The Spread of the Fire

The fire broke out in the late evening, when most of the citizens were in their homes. The flames were first noticed by a watchman who was patrolling the warehouse district, and the alarm was raised quickly. The fire brigades of the city responded promptly, but they were unable to contain the flames. The fire had already spread to the neighbouring warehouses by the time the brigades arrived, and the strong winds were driving the flames beyond the reach of the brigades' buckets and pumps.

The fire spread through the warehouse district and into the market district, which was the commercial heart of the city. The markets were filled with stalls and booths that burned quickly, and the flames spread from the markets into the residential streets beyond. The citizens fled from their homes, carrying what possessions they could save, and the streets were filled with people and animals attempting to escape the flames.

The fire burned through the first night and the following day, consuming everything in its path. The fire brigades worked through the night, but their efforts were ineffective against the scale of the fire. The magistrates of the city ordered the demolition of buildings to create a firebreak, but the work was slow and dangerous and the fire had already passed the point where a firebreak could have contained it.

## The Response of the Citizens

The response of the citizens to the fire was a mixture of courage, despair, and opportunism. Many citizens risked their lives to save their neighbours and their possessions, and there are records of heroic acts that are preserved in the chronicles of the city. Other citizens were less admirable, taking advantage of the chaos to loot the abandoned buildings and to steal the goods that had been salvaged from the flames.

The magistrates of the city imposed a curfew and ordered the arrest of looters. The council of Eldreth met in emergency session and authorised the expenditure of public funds for the relief of the victims and the reconstruction of the destroyed districts. The council also sent messages to the neighbouring cities, requesting assistance in the form of food, supplies, and skilled craftsmen.

The neighbouring cities responded generously to the request. [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) sent a shipload of grain and timber, and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) sent a party of stonemasons and carpenters to assist in the rebuilding. The [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic) sent a grant of silver from the public treasury, and the elders of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) sent a herd of cattle for the feeding of the victims.

## The Aftermath and the Toll

The fire burned for three days before it was finally extinguished by a change in the wind and the arrival of rain. The toll of the fire was severe. A substantial portion of the city had been destroyed, including the warehouse district, the market district, and several residential neighbourhoods. The number of dead was uncertain, as many bodies were consumed by the flames, but the chronicles estimate that several hundred citizens had lost their lives. The number of homeless was much larger, with thousands of citizens left without shelter.

The economic toll was also severe. The merchants of Eldreth lost their warehouses, their goods, and their trading connections. The markets that had been the heart of the city's commerce were reduced to ashes. The shipbuilding industry, which had been a source of prosperity for the city, was damaged by the loss of the timber stored in the warehouses. The city's trade was disrupted for several years, and it took a generation for the city to recover its former prosperity.

The fire also destroyed a number of the city's principal buildings, including the old guildhall, the temple of the Sea Mother's Court, and the market arcade. The loss of the guildhall was particularly damaging, as it had housed the archives of the city, including the charters and the records of the council. The archives were lost, and the early history of the city was preserved only in the chronicles that had been kept elsewhere.

## The Reconstruction of the City

The reconstruction of Eldreth was one of the great undertakings of the late fifth century. The council of the city appointed a commission of magistrates and architects to plan the rebuilding, and the commission drew up a plan for the reconstruction that was more orderly than the original layout of the city. The new streets were wider, the buildings were required to be built of stone and brick rather than timber and plaster, and the markets were relocated to a more spacious site.

The reconstruction was financed by a combination of public funds, private investment, and contributions from the neighbouring cities. The council imposed a special tax upon the citizens for the reconstruction, and the merchants who had lost their warehouses were required to contribute to the rebuilding of the district. The contributions were collected over a period of years, and the reconstruction was completed within a decade of the fire.

The new buildings of Eldreth were more substantial than the old. The warehouses were built of stone, with iron doors and tile roofs that were resistant to fire. The markets were housed in a new arcade that was built of brick and glass, with a water system that supplied the fountains and the fire cisterns. The temples were rebuilt in the same style as the original, using the old foundations and the surviving fragments of the original buildings.

## The Legacy of the Fire

The Burning of Eldreth was the most destructive event in the history of the city and a turning point in its development. The fire destroyed the old city and forced the citizens to rebuild, and the new city that emerged from the ashes was more orderly and more resilient than the old. The fire also brought the city closer to the neighbouring powers, which had assisted in the relief and the reconstruction and which had formed lasting ties with the council of Eldreth.

The memory of the fire is preserved in the chronicles of the city and in the ceremonies of the citizens. The anniversary of the fire is marked by a service in the temple of the Sea Mother's Court, at which the citizens remember the dead and give thanks for the survival of the city. The service is followed by a procession through the streets of the rebuilt districts, and the citizens offer prayers for the protection of the city from future fires.
