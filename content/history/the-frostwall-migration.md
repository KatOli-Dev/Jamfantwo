## The Movement to the Northern Marches

The Frostwall Migration was the movement of peoples from the southern and western regions of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) to the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches), the cold and mountainous region of the far north of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the neighbouring coasts of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The migration occurred over several centuries and involved a number of distinct peoples, each drawn by different circumstances but united by a common search for land, resources, and freedom from the pressures of the more densely populated regions of the continent.

The [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) were the first people to inhabit the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches), and they remain the principal inhabitants of the region in the present age. The clans are a hardy and independent people, adapted to the harsh conditions of the north and skilled in the arts of hunting, fishing, and herding that the region demands. The migration added new elements to the population of the marches, including [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) settlers from the south and the west and a number of [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) and [gnomish](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) families from the holds of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands).

## The Conditions That Drove the Migration

The migration was driven by a combination of factors that varied from group to group. The principal factor was population pressure in the southern and western regions of Aethelar, where the land was limited and the competition for resources was intense. The farmers and the herders of the southern plains and the western valleys found it increasingly difficult to support their families upon the land that they held, and many of them looked to the north as a place where land was still available.

The political conditions of the southern and western regions also contributed to the migration. The consolidation of the great powers of the present age, including the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), and the kingdom of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), had reduced the autonomy of the smaller communities and had imposed taxes and obligations upon them. The communities that valued their independence were reluctant to submit to the authority of the great powers, and many of them chose to migrate to the north rather than to accept the loss of their autonomy.

The climate of the period may also have played a role. The chronicles record that the period of the migration was marked by a series of harsh winters and poor harvests in the southern and western regions, and the scholars of the academies have suggested that a cooling of the climate may have driven the peoples of the south to seek a warmer refuge. The theory is debated among the scholars, but it is supported by the records of the agricultural production of the period, which show a decline in the yields of the principal crops.

## The Routes of the Migration

The migration followed two principal routes into the Frostwall Marches. The first route followed the [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river) northward from the plains into the foothills of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) and then across the range through the passes that the dwarven engineers had cleared in the [Stoneheim Expansion Era](/content/history/stoneheim-expansion-era). The route was long and difficult, requiring the migrants to cross the range at high altitude and to descend into the cold valleys of the north.

The second route followed the coast of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) northward from the ports of the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league) to the harbours of the Frostwall Marches. The route was shorter than the overland route but was more dangerous, as the waters of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) were known for their storms and their strong currents. The migrants who chose the sea route were required to build or to purchase vessels that were capable of the journey, and many of them lost their lives in the crossing.

The two routes converged at the settlement of Frostwall, which was the principal settlement of the region and the point at which the migrants entered the Frostwall Marches. The settlement had been established by the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) in the early centuries of the present age and had grown into a substantial town that served as the centre of the trade of the region.

## The Settlement of the Marches

The migrants who arrived in the Frostwall Marches were required to adapt to a way of life that was very different from the life they had known in the south and the west. The climate was colder, the growing season was shorter, and the land was less fertile. The migrants were required to learn new methods of farming and herding that were suited to the conditions of the north, and they were required to build houses and barns that could withstand the harsh winters.

The migrants also had to establish relations with the Frostwall Clans, who were the original inhabitants of the region. The relations were initially cautious, as the clans were wary of the newcomers and uncertain of their intentions. The migrants offered gifts of steel tools and woven cloth, which the clans accepted, and the two peoples established a pattern of trade and cooperation that has persisted to the present day.

The migrants established a number of settlements in the Frostwall Marches, including the towns of Frostwall, [Bjornsholm](/content/location/settlement/town/bjornsholm), and [Torshavn](/content/location/settlement/town/torshavn). The settlements were small at first, consisting of a few houses and a marketplace, but they grew over the years as new migrants arrived and as the original settlers raised families. The settlements were connected by a network of trails that were maintained by the settlers and that served as the principal means of communication and trade in the region.

## The Formation of the Frostwall Clans

The migrants and the original inhabitants of the Frostwall Marches gradually merged into a single people, known as the Frostwall Clans. The process of merging took several centuries and was marked by a mixture of cooperation and conflict. The migrants and the original inhabitants learned from each other, adopting each other's customs and technologies, and they intermarried over the generations until the distinction between the two groups became meaningless.

The Frostwall Clans that emerged from the process of merging were a confederation of independent kindreds, each governing its own territory and maintaining its own customs. The kindreds were united by a common culture and a common language, and they cooperated in the defence of the region and in the regulation of the trade. The kindreds were also united by a common council of elders, which met at the principal settlement of Frostwall to adjudicate disputes between the kindreds and to coordinate the defence of the marches.

The council of the Frostwall Clans became the principal institution of the region, and it has governed the Frostwall Marches for the centuries that have followed. The council is composed of the elders of the principal kindreds, and it meets twice each year to hear disputes and to discuss matters of common concern. The council also elects a chief elder, who serves for a term of three years and who is responsible for the execution of the council's decisions.

## The Legacy of the Migration

The Frostwall Migration was a movement of peoples that transformed the population and the culture of the [northern marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) of Aethelar and the neighbouring coasts of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The migration brought new peoples, new technologies, and new ideas to the region, and it laid the foundations for the emergence of the Frostwall Clans as a distinct people with their own culture and institutions.

The legacy of the migration is visible in the towns and the villages of the Frostwall Marches, in the farms and the herding camps that dot the landscape, and in the institutions that the migrants and the original inhabitants established together. The council of the clans, the network of trails, and the system of trade and cooperation are all the legacy of the migration.

The memory of the migration is preserved in the chronicles of the clans and in the ceremonies of the Frostwall Marches. The anniversary of the arrival of the first migrants is marked by a gathering at the settlement of Frostwall, attended by the elders of the clans and by the magistrates of the towns. The gathering includes the recitation of the stories of the migration, the singing of the songs that the migrants brought with them, and a feast that celebrates the unity of the clans.
