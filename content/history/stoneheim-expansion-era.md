## The Age of Dwarven Growth

The Stoneheim Expansion Era is the name given to the period in which the [dwarven](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) people of the Stoneheim Highlands expanded their territory through the mountain ranges of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), establishing new holds and extending their influence over the passes and the highlands. The expansion began in the early centuries of the present age, in the period following the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era), and continued for several centuries, transforming the dwarven people from a confederation of scattered holds into a unified and powerful realm.

The era is well-documented in the dwarven chronicles, which are preserved in the hall of records at [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) and in the archives of the principal holds. The chronicles record the principal events of the expansion in considerable detail, including the founding of new holds, the wars with the orc and goblin tribes, and the treaties with the neighbouring kingdoms. The academies of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) have studied the chronicles and have produced a substantial body of commentary on the expansion.

## The State of the Dwarven People Before the Expansion

Before the expansion, the dwarven people were organised into a number of independent holds, each governing its own territory and maintaining its own customs. The holds were located in the Stoneheim Highlands and in the northern slopes of the [Stonespire](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire), and they were connected by a network of tunnels and passes that the dwarven engineers had carved through the rock. The holds were prosperous, as the mountains contained rich deposits of metal and stone, but they were also vulnerable to attack from the orc and goblin tribes of the deeper mountains.

The holds had a tradition of mutual assistance in times of danger. When a hold was threatened, the other holds would send warriors to its defence, and the combined forces of the holds were usually sufficient to repel the attackers. But the holds had no formal institutions of government beyond the council of the elders, which met only in times of crisis. The lack of a central authority was a weakness that the dwarven elders recognised, but the holds valued their independence and were reluctant to surrender it to a central council.

The pressure of the orc and goblin attacks increased over the years, as the tribes grew bolder and more numerous. The holds found it increasingly difficult to defend themselves with the resources of a single hold, and the elders began to discuss the possibility of a more permanent form of cooperation.

## The Council of Stoneheim

The turning point came in the early second century of the present age, when the elders of the principal holds gathered at Stoneheim for a council that would decide the future of the dwarven people. The council was convened by King Thrain Ironbrow of Stoneheim, who was the most respected of the dwarven rulers of the period. Thrain had seen that the holds could not survive indefinitely as separate entities and that a more permanent union was necessary.

The council lasted for the whole summer and was marked by a vigorous debate about the nature of the proposed union. The elders of the larger holds favoured a strong central authority that would command the warriors of all the holds and that would have the power to tax the holds for the common defence. The elders of the smaller holds were more cautious, fearing that a central authority would infringe upon their independence and that the taxes would be a burden upon their people.

The compromise that emerged from the council was the establishment of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) as a confederation of the dwarven holds, governed by a council of the elders of the principal holds and led by a king elected by the council. The confederation had the power to call upon the warriors of the holds for the common defence and to collect a tax for the maintenance of the passes and the tunnels. The holds retained their independence in all other matters, and the council was required to respect the customs and traditions of the individual holds.

## The Wars of Expansion

The establishment of the confederation was followed by a period of military expansion that lasted for several generations. The dwarven warriors, united under the command of the king, drove the orc and goblin tribes from the mountain passes and established new holds in the territories that had been taken. The wars were hard-fought, as the orc and goblin tribes were numerous and tenacious, but the dwarven warriors were better armed and better disciplined, and they prevailed in the end.

The first of the wars of expansion was the campaign to clear the [Stonespire Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/stonespire-pass), which had been blocked by orc tribes for several generations. The campaign was commanded by King Thrain himself and lasted for three years. The dwarven warriors fought their way through the pass, clearing the tribes from the heights and establishing a chain of fortified posts to hold the territory. The pass was reopened to traffic, and the trade between the dwarven highlands and the elven forests resumed.

The second war was the campaign to clear the [Amber Pass](/content/location/natural/feature/amber-pass), which connected the Stoneheim Highlands to the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the fertile lands of the south. The campaign was commanded by King Thrain's son, who succeeded his father upon the throne of Stoneheim. The campaign was more difficult than the clearance of the Stonespire Pass, as the goblin tribes of the Amber Pass were more numerous and better organised. The war lasted for six years and ended with the capture of the principal goblin stronghold and the dispersal of the surviving tribes.

The third war was the campaign to clear the [Thunderback Range](/content/location/natural/feature/thunderback-range), which connected the Stoneheim Highlands to the plains of the Confederation. The campaign was commanded by King Bron Stoneheim, the grandson of Thrain, and lasted for four years. The campaign was marked by a great battle at the foot of the range, in which the dwarven warriors defeated a combined force of orc and goblin tribes and broke the power of the tribes in the region.

## The Founding of New Holds

The clearing of the passes was followed by the founding of new holds in the territories that had been taken. The new holds were built at strategic points along the passes and in the highlands that the dwarven warriors had secured. The holds were smaller than the principal holds of the Stoneheim Highlands but were built to the same standard of dwarven engineering, with deep foundations, strong walls, and reliable water supplies.

The new holds were intended to serve as bases for the dwarven warriors and as centres of settlement for the dwarven population that was growing rapidly in the period. The holds attracted settlers from the older holds, who were drawn by the promise of land and resources. The holds also attracted merchants from the neighbouring kingdoms, who traded the goods of the dwarven highlands for the goods of the plains and the forests.

The new holds became prosperous in their own right, and several of them grew into substantial settlements that rivalled the older holds in size and importance. The hold of Khazgar, founded at the foot of the Stonespire Pass, became the principal centre of the trade between the dwarven highlands and the elven forests. The hold of Grimhold, founded at the foot of the Amber Pass, became the principal centre of the trade between the dwarven highlands and the southern plains.

## The Legacy of the Expansion

The Stoneheim Expansion Era was one of the most important periods in the history of the dwarven people. The expansion transformed the dwarven people from a confederation of scattered holds into a unified and powerful realm that commanded the principal passes of the mountain ranges and controlled the trade between the highlands and the plains. The institutions that were established during the expansion, including the confederation of the holds and the council of the elders, have endured to the present day.

The legacy of the expansion is visible in the holds and the passes that the dwarven engineers carved through the rock. The network of tunnels and roads that connects the holds, the fortifications that guard the passes, and the cities that grew from the original settlements are all the legacy of the expansion. The memory of the wars, the founding of the new holds, and the kings who commanded the campaigns is preserved in the dwarven chronicles and in the ceremonies of the holds.

The expansion also established the relationship between the dwarven people and the other peoples of the present age. The trade routes that were opened by the expansion brought the dwarven highlands into contact with the elven forests, the human plains, and the kingdoms of the coast. The treaties that were negotiated during the expansion established the terms of the relationship between the dwarven people and their neighbours and have served as the foundation of dwarven diplomacy for the centuries that followed.
