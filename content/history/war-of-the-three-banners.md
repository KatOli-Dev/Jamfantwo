## The Conflict of the Central Grasslands

The War of the Three Banners is the most celebrated of the wars of the plains, the conflict that was fought between the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) and the two rival confederations that contested the control of the central grasslands in the middle period. The war lasted for a period of seven years, and the war resulted in the consolidation of the Confederation's control over the central grasslands, a position that the Confederation has maintained to the present day. The chronicler notes that the war is recorded in the chronicles of the peoples of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) as the central event of the middle period of their history, and the war is held in the memory of every kindred of the plains.

The war was fought upon the open grasslands that stretch from the foothills of the mountain spine in the west to the broad river valleys that drain toward the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) in the east, and the war was decided by the mounted warriors of the three confederations in a series of engagements that the chroniclers have preserved in unusual detail. The chronicler notes that the war is documented in the chronicles of all three parties, and the chronicles agree upon the principal events but disagree upon their meaning, and the disagreement is itself a measure of the importance that the war has assumed in the traditions of the plains.

## The Outbreak of the War

The war began with a dispute over the control of the great summer grazing lands of the central grasslands, the lands that the three confederations had shared by a series of customary arrangements that had been in place for several generations. The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) had long claimed the right to regulate the grazing of the central grasslands by virtue of the agreement that the founders of the Confederation had made with the elders of the principal kindreds in the early centuries of the present age. The two rival confederations disputed the claim on the grounds that the agreement had been made before either of the rival confederations had been founded, and that the rival confederations were not bound by an instrument to which they had not been a party. The dispute was referred to the elders of the principal kindreds, and the elders decided in favour of the Confederation. The rival confederations refused to accept the decision, and the Confederation dispatched a mounted levy to enforce the ruling.

The levy was met upon the open grasslands by the combined forces of the rival confederations, and the meeting was the first of the principal engagements of the war. The chronicler notes that the levy was insufficient to the task that the council of the Confederation had set it, and the levy was driven back to the principal settlements of the Confederation with heavy loss. The council of the Confederation responded by calling upon the full levy of the member cities and the centaur kindreds, and the response was the beginning of the war as it was fought for the next seven years. The chronicler notes that the outbreak of the war is recorded in the chronicles of the Confederation as the moment at which the customary arrangements of the central grasslands gave way to open conflict, and the chronicles describe the haste with which the council of the Confederation dispatched the levy as a measure of the council's confidence in the customary arrangements.

The course of the war is conventionally divided into three phases: the phase of the rival confederations' ascendancy, the phase of the Confederation's recovery, and the phase of the negotiations that led to the peace. The phase of the rival confederations' ascendancy saw the rival confederations in control of the principal grazing lands of the central grasslands. The phase of the Confederation's recovery began with the appointment of the council of war and with the construction of the fortified camps. ## The Phase of the Negotiations

The phase of the negotiations began with the accession of a new council of elders to the leadership of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), a council that included several of the elders who had been instrumental in the construction of the fortified camps. The new council judged that the war had reached a stalemate, and that the Confederation could not win without incurring costs that would be disproportionate to the benefits. The council opened negotiations with the rival confederations through the mediators of the neighbouring kingdoms, and the negotiations continued for several years.

The negotiations were complicated by the insistence of each party upon the rights that it had claimed at the beginning of the war. The Confederation insisted upon the customary arrangements by virtue of the original agreement; the rival confederations insisted upon the right to share in the grazing of the central grasslands by virtue of the natural liberty of the kindreds. The mediators proposed a compromise that established a council of the three confederations to regulate the grazing of the central grasslands, and the compromise was accepted by the three councils. The peace was signed at the great summer camp that the three confederations maintained upon the central grasslands, and the peace is known in the chronicles as the Peace of the Three Banners.

## The Aftermath

The aftermath of the war was marked by a long period of reconstruction in all three confederations. The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) rebuilt the fortified camps and re-established the customary arrangements that the peace had confirmed. The rival confederations rebuilt the herds that had been lost in the war, and the rival confederations entered into the council of the three confederations that the peace had established. The chronicler notes that the war left a permanent mark upon the relations of the three confederations, and the relations have been marked by a mixture of rivalry and cooperation ever since.

The wars of the plains have been less frequent in the later period, for the Confederation has been successful in establishing the institutions that have made the wars unnecessary, and the negotiation of the treaties between the Confederation and the neighbouring kingdoms has reduced the occasions of conflict. The Confederation remains, however, a formidable military power, and the mounted warriors of the plains are among the most accomplished cavalry in the known world. The chronicler notes that the annual hunt of the thunder boar is the occasion upon which the warriors of the Confederation demonstrate their prowess, and the hunt is also a ceremonial occasion that binds the peoples of the plains to the traditions of the grasslands.

## The Three Banners

The war takes its name from the three banners that the three confederations carried into the field, and the banners are preserved in the chronicles of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) as the most distinctive of the relics of the conflict. The banner of the Confederation is a field of green with a single white horse rampant, and the banner is the standard that the mounted levy of the Confederation has carried since the founding of the alliance. The banner of the first rival confederation is a field of blue with a single yellow sun in splendour, and the banner is the standard that the mounted levy of the first rival carried into the field. The banner of the second rival confederation is a field of red with a single black bull, and the banner is the standard that the mounted levy of the second rival carried into the field. The chronicler notes that the three banners are held in the hall of the council of the Confederation, and the banners are displayed at the principal ceremonies of the Confederation.

The three banners are the measure of the importance that the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) attaches to the war, and the war is commemorated in the annual ceremonies that the Confederation maintains. ## The Setting of the War

The war was fought upon the open grasslands of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and the grasslands shaped the war in ways that the chroniclers of the neighbouring kingdoms have found difficult to understand. The grasslands are a country of marked seasonal contrasts, with hot summers and cold winters separated by brief but dramatic periods of transition, and the seasons determined the rhythm of the war. The principal engagements were fought in the summer, when the grass was at its tallest and the herds were in their best condition, and the winter was a time of relative quiet in which the levies of the three confederations retired to their fortified camps. The chronicler notes that the seasonal rhythm of the war is recorded in the chronicles of all three parties, and the chronicles describe the haste with which the levies returned to the field at the opening of each summer.

The grasslands also shaped the tactics of the war, for the open country favoured the mounted warriors of the three confederations and gave little advantage to the foot soldiers that the neighbouring kingdoms had employed in their own conflicts. The mounted warriors of the three confederations were drawn from the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds and from the [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) riders of the member cities, and the warriors were trained from youth in the arts of the mounted combat that the grasslands demand. The chronicler notes that the tactics of the war were refined over the seven years of the conflict, and the refinements are recorded in the chronicles of the three parties as the foundation of the mounted warfare of the present age.

## Significance

The War of the Three Banners is the most celebrated of the wars of the plains, and the war is the central event of the middle period of the history of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) and of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation). The war lasted for seven years and resulted in the consolidation of the Confederation's control over the central grasslands, a position that the Confederation has maintained to the present day. The chronicler notes that the war is held in particular memory by the elders of the member cities and the centaur kindreds of the Confederation, and the elders mark the anniversaries of the principal engagements with appropriate ceremonies. The ceremonies are among the most important of the public observances of the plains, and the ceremonies are attended by the magistrates and the principal citizens of every member city of the Confederation.
