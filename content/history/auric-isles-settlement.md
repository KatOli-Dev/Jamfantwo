## The Colonisation of the Eastern Islands

The settlement of the Auric Isles was one of the great maritime enterprises of the present age, a prolonged endeavour by which the peoples of Aethelar established a permanent presence upon the chain of islands that lies in the eastern [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The isles were known to the pilots of the warm coast for many years before the first permanent settlement was established, but their distance from the continent and their exposure to the storms of the ocean made them difficult to reach and hazardous to inhabit.

The Auric Isles are a chain of volcanic islands that stretch across the eastern Auric Ocean, from the [First-Light Cape](/content/location/natural/feature/cape-of-the-first-light) in the west to the [Drowned Isles](/content/location/natural/feature/drowned-isles) in the east. The isles are fertile and well-watered, with a mild climate that supports a dense forest and a rich variety of plant and animal life. The waters around the isles are abundant with fish, and the harbours are numerous and well-sheltered.

## The First Sightings and Visits

The first sighting of the Auric Isles is attributed to Kaelen the Navigator, who recorded the presence of islands in the eastern ocean during his famous voyage to the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh). Kaelen noted the position of the islands in his log and reported them to the council of Khalisar upon his return. The council sent a small vessel to investigate the following year, and the vessel confirmed the presence of a chain of islands stretching across the ocean.

The first landings were made by pilots from [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm), who visited the isles to take on fresh water and provisions during the long voyage to the Kharesh. The pilots reported that the isles were uninhabited and that they were covered with a dense forest that would provide an abundance of timber for shipbuilding. The reports attracted the attention of the merchants of the coastal cities, who saw the potential for the establishment of a permanent settlement upon the isles.

The early visits were seasonal, with the pilots landing upon the isles in the spring and summer and returning to the continent in the autumn. The pilots cut timber, collected fresh water, and hunted the birds and the seals that inhabited the isles. Several pilots proposed the establishment of a permanent settlement, but the council of Khalisar was reluctant to commit the resources required for the endeavour.

## The First Permanent Settlement

The first permanent settlement upon the Auric Isles was established in the early fourth century of the present age, by a group of settlers from the [Northern Coast League](/content/government/national/northern-coast-league). The settlers were led by a pilot named Vaelen Eldreth, who had visited the isles on several occasions and had judged that they could sustain a permanent population. Vaelen persuaded the council of the Northern Coast League to support the establishment of a settlement, and the council provided a vessel, supplies, and a party of settlers for the endeavour.

The settlers landed upon the largest of the isles, which they named Aurica, and built a settlement upon the shore of a natural harbour. The settlement was small, consisting of a few houses, a warehouse, and a chapel. The settlers lived by fishing, hunting, and gathering the fruits of the forest, and they traded the timber and the salted fish that they produced for the goods that they needed from the continent.

The first years of the settlement were difficult. The settlers were isolated from the continent, and the supply vessels that were sent from the coast did not always arrive on schedule. The settlers suffered from shortages of food and supplies, and several of them died from illness and exposure. The settlement survived through the determination of the settlers and the support of the Northern Coast League, which sent additional settlers and supplies each year.

## The Growth of the Settlement

The settlement grew slowly over the decades, as new settlers arrived from the continent and as the original settlers raised families and established households. The population of the settlement increased, and the settlers began to explore the other isles of the chain. They found that the isles were more numerous than the early pilots had reported and that many of them were suitable for settlement.

The settlers established new settlements upon the other isles of the chain, including the isle of Aurea, the isle of Aurum, and the isle of Aurus. The settlements were connected by a network of small vessels that carried people and goods between the isles. The settlers established a council that governed the affairs of the isles and that maintained relations with the Northern Coast League and the other powers of the continent.

The settlement of the isles was not always peaceful. The settlers faced competition from the pilots of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm), who also claimed the right to use the isles as a staging point for the voyage to the Kharesh. The competition sometimes led to conflict, as the settlers and the pilots disputed the control of the harbours and the forests. The disputes were resolved by the mediation of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), which established a framework for the shared use of the isles.

## The Development of the Auric Isles Government

The settlements of the Auric Isles were united under a single government in the late fourth century, with the establishment of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) as a self-governing territory within the sphere of the Northern Coast League. The government was established by a council of the elders of the settlements, who met at the principal settlement of Aurica and agreed upon a constitution for the isles.

The constitution established a council of the elders of the settlements, which would govern the affairs of the isles and maintain relations with the continent. The council would elect a chief elder from among its members, who would serve for a term of five years and would be responsible for the execution of the council's decisions. The constitution also established a system of courts for the adjudication of disputes and a militia for the defence of the isles.

The government of the Auric Isles has operated under the same constitution for several centuries, with only minor revisions. The council of the elders has met regularly and has governed the isles with a measure of consensus that the chronicles attribute to the small size of the population and the absence of the factional divisions that afflict the larger powers of the continent.

## The Economy of the Isles

The economy of the Auric Isles was based upon the resources of the islands and the sea. The settlers fished the waters around the isles, which were abundant with cod, herring, and mackerel. They hunted the seals and the birds that nested upon the cliffs, and they harvested the timber of the forests for export to the continent. The settlers also cultivated the fertile soil of the isles, growing grain, vegetables, and fruit for their own consumption.

The most important resource of the isles was the timber. The forests of the isles produced a dense, hard wood that was prized by the shipbuilders of the continent for the construction of masts, spars, and decking. The timber was exported to the ports of Aethelar, where it commanded a high price in the shipyards. The trade in timber was the foundation of the prosperity of the isles for several centuries, and the settlers managed the forests with care to ensure that the supply would not be exhausted.

The isles also produced a number of other goods that were valued in the markets of the continent. The settlers produced salted and dried fish, seal oil, and whale oil that were shipped to the cities of the coast. They also produced a distinctive type of pottery from the clay of the isles, which was known for its strength and its beauty. The pottery was traded to the merchants of the continent, who carried it to the markets of the three continents.

## The Legacy of the Settlement

The settlement of the Auric Isles was one of the great maritime achievements of the present age, establishing a permanent presence upon a chain of islands that had been known but uninhabited for generations before the first settlers arrived. The settlers who crossed the Auric Ocean and built their homes upon the isles demonstrated a courage and determination that is admired by the peoples of all three continents.

The legacy of the settlement is visible in the towns and the villages that dot the isles, in the harbours and the shipyards that serve the vessels of the continent, and in the institutions that the settlers established. The council of the elders, the constitution of the isles, and the system of courts are all the legacy of the settlement. The memory of the first settlement, the early years of hardship, and the growth of the settlements is preserved in the chronicles of the isles and in the ceremonies of the settlers.
