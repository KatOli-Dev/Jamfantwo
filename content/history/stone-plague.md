## The Great Pestilence of the Present Age

The Stone Plague is the conventional name for the long pestilence that swept through the principal cities of the three continents in the generations following the [Long Peace](/content/history/long-peace). The plague took its name from the appearance of the afflicted, whose skin took on a grey, stony cast in the final stages of the disease. It is recorded in the chronicles of every city of the present age as the most severe visitation of disease in the recorded history of the age. The academies date the plague by the reign of the kings and magistrates who presided over the response, and the convention varies from city to city.

The plague first appeared in the trading ports of the warm coast in the spring of a year now lost and spread rapidly along the trade routes into the inland valleys and the cold north. It reached [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) within the same season and the dwarven holds of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) within the year. The Plague and Remedy records that the rapid spread reflects the growth of trade that had taken place in the Long Peace, which was both the foundation of the prosperity of the preceding generations and the vehicle by which the plague was carried.

## Stages of the Affliction

The plague began with a fever and a swelling of the glands of the neck and the armpits and advanced through several stages that the healers of the academies have recorded in detail. The first stage lasted three or four days, with the patient suffering fever, thirst, and a restlessness that prevented sleep. The second stage lasted as long again, with the patient breaking out in dark spots upon the skin and suffering a cough that brought up blood. The third stage lasted a few days more, during which the patient took on the grey, stony cast that gave the plague its name, and the patient died.

The healers of the academies tried many remedies, including bleeding, dosing with herbs and minerals, the application of poultices, and the recitation of prayers and incantations. The trials were mostly unsuccessful, and the healers who recorded them often noted that the patient died despite the remedy or recovered without any remedy that could be distinguished from the natural course of the disease. The honesty of these records became the foundation of the practice of the present age in disease management.

## Harbour Closures and Public Health Measures

The magistrates of the principal cities responded with the measures the public health tradition of the present age had established. The magistrates of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) closed the harbour to vessels from infected ports. The magistrates of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) closed the city gates to travellers from the south. The magistrates of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) sealed the passes of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands) and refused entry to all who could not prove they had not been in contact with the disease. The measures were effective in slowing the spread but were not sufficient to prevent the plague from entering the cities. The plague took hold in every principal settlement of the three continents within a few years.

The magistrates worked in concert with the healers and the priests of the temples. They appointed commissioners of public health in every district, who oversaw the separation of the sick from the well, the purification of the air and the water, and the disposal of the dead. The measures were not always successful, and the commissioners recorded the failures as carefully as the successes. These measures became the foundation of the practice of the present age in plague management.

## Population Decline Across Three Continents

The plague carried off a substantial fraction of the population of the three continents. The cities of the warm coast lost nearly a third of their inhabitants; the cities of the cold north lost nearly a quarter; the dwarven holds of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) lost a fifth. The countryside lost less, though it was severely affected in regions along the trade routes. The losses were greatest in the cities that had grown most rapidly during the Long Peace.

The loss of life was accompanied by a loss of learning and skill. Many of the scholars and craftsmen of the Long Peace died in the plague, and their successors had to reconstruct the traditions the plague had interrupted. The reconstruction took several generations.

## Rebuilding Trade, Learning, and Worship

The years following the plague were a period of recovery that was the work of several generations. The magistrates and councils of the cities encouraged the return of trade, which brought merchants and craftsmen back. The academies reopened their doors, and scholars resumed the compilation of the chronicles, atlases, and tables the plague had interrupted. The temples reopened their altars, and the priests resumed the rites the plague had suspended.

The recovery was accompanied by a renewed interest in the practical disciplines, particularly the Plague and Remedy. Scholars compiled the records the healers had made during the visitation and revised the catalogues of remedies in light of the experience. The renewed interest was the foundation of the practice of the present age in disease management.

## Annual Observances and Learned Records

The plague is remembered in the chronicles of every city of the present age and preserved in public observances conducted each year. The observances include a procession through the streets, a service in the temples, and a reading of the names of the dead. These observances are among the most solemn ceremonies of the present age and are attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every city of the three continents.

The chronicles of the Plague and Remedy are the most complete records of the plague and are consulted by every scholar who must judge a question of public health. The plague has been the source of much of the most important work of the academies in the management of disease.

## Foundation of Disease Management in the Present Age

The Stone Plague is the most severe visitation of disease in the recorded history of the present age and the foundation of the practice of the present age in disease management. The measures the magistrates established in response, the records the healers compiled during the visitation, and the renewed interest in the practical disciplines the plague provoked are the legacy of the years of the Stone Plague. The plague is the measure by which the magistrates and the healers judge the public health of their own day.
