## The Agreement with the Velden Strait Ports

The Harbour Accord is the name given by the chroniclers of the inland valleys and the warm coast to the agreement negotiated between the parties of the [Twin-Reaches Peace](/content/history/twin-reaches-peace) and the ports of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden). The accord extended the principles of the [Twin-Reaches Peace](/content/history/twin-reaches-peace) to the maritime trade passing through the strait, establishing harbour fees, navigation rules, and an office of maritime mediator modelled upon the office established by the Twin-Reaches Peace.

The accord was negotiated in the period following the conclusion of the Twin-Reaches Peace, when the parties to the peace recognised that the stability and prosperity that the peace had brought to the inland trade routes could be extended to the maritime trade of the strait. The ports of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) were the natural partners for such an agreement, for they controlled the principal passage between the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) and their cooperation was essential to the success of any attempt to regulate the maritime trade.

## The Importance of the Velden Strait

The [Velden](/content/location/settlement/city/velden) Strait was the principal conduit of trade between the eastern and western waters, and its ports were among the wealthiest and most prosperous of the known world. The strait towns had long maintained a loose association known as the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), which regulated the passage of vessels through the strait and the collection of tolls, but the compact had not extended to the regulation of the trade routes connecting the strait to the inland valleys and the warm coast.

The parties to the Twin-Reaches Peace recognised that the absence of a comprehensive agreement for the maritime trade was a source of instability, for disputes between merchants and ship captains could disrupt the flow of goods and lead to conflicts that might draw in the signatory parties. The parties approached the council of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) with a proposal for an accord that would regulate the harbour fees and the navigation rules for the vessels trading between the strait ports and the inland valleys.

## The Provisions of the Accord

The Harbour Accord established a system of harbour fees and navigation rules for the vessels using the ports of the Velden Strait and the trade routes connecting the strait to the inland valleys and the warm coast. The fees were set at a rate that was sufficient to maintain the harbours and the navigation aids but not so high as to discourage trade, and they were collected by the harbour masters of the strait ports.

The accord also established an office of maritime mediator, modelled upon the office established by the Twin-Reaches Peace, to resolve disputes between merchants, ship captains, and harbour masters. The mediator was appointed by the signatory parties and was given the authority to hear disputes and to propose settlements that would be binding if accepted.

The maritime mediator was responsible for the oversight of the harbour fees and the condition of the harbours, reporting regularly to the signatory parties on the state of the maritime trade and the compliance of those who used the ports.

## The Implementation

The implementation of the accord was accomplished through the cooperation of the harbour masters of the principal strait ports and the magistrates of the inland valleys. The maritime mediator appointed under the accord was a ship captain of considerable experience who had served as a harbour master in one of the principal strait towns.

The mediator surveyed the harbours and recorded the condition of the quays, the warehouses, and the navigation aids, identifying the sections that required repair and the facilities that needed improvement. The mediator also consulted with the merchants and ship captains who used the harbours, adjusting the regulations of the accord to accommodate the practical needs of the maritime trade.

## The Accord and the Maritime Communities

The Harbour Accord had a significant effect upon the maritime communities of the Velden Strait. The harbour fees established by the accord provided a reliable source of revenue for the maintenance of the quays and the navigation aids, and the condition of the harbours improved steadily in the years following the adoption of the accord. The ship captains and merchants who used the harbours benefited from the improved facilities and the more efficient handling of cargo, and the volume of trade passing through the strait increased.

The accord also contributed to the safety of the maritime trade, for the navigation rules established by the accord reduced the risk of collisions and groundings in the busy waters of the strait. The harbour masters who enforced the rules reported fewer incidents in the years following the adoption of the accord, and the insurers who underwrote the maritime trade adjusted their rates to reflect the improved safety.

## The First Maritime Mediator

The first maritime mediator appointed under the Harbour Accord served for twenty-two years and established the practices that the office of the mediator has followed since. The mediator's reports were submitted to the signatory parties at regular intervals, and the mediator's recommendations were adopted in the great majority of the cases that came before the office. The mediator's authority was accepted by the merchants, the ship captains, and the harbour masters, who regarded the mediator as a fair and impartial arbiter.

The mediation of disputes was conducted in a chamber set aside for the purpose in the principal harbour building of the strait town where the mediator had the office. The parties appeared before the mediator to present their cases, and the hearings were conducted in the common speech of the sea. The decisions of the mediator were recorded in a register that was kept in the chamber, and the register became the record of the practice of the office.

## The Accord in the Wider Order

The Harbour Accord is one of several agreements that the parties to the [Twin-Reaches Peace](/content/history/twin-reaches-peace) negotiated with the principal cities and regions of the three continents, and it is regarded by the chroniclers of the inland valleys as a successful extension of the principles of the peace to the maritime trade of the Velden Strait. The accord is placed in the broader chronology of the age by the [World Timeline](/content/history/world-timeline), which records the adoption of the accord alongside the other agreements modelled upon the Twin-Reaches Peace.
