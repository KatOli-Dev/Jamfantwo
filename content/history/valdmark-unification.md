## The Joining of the River Holdings

The Valdmark Unification was the process by which the independent holdings of the principal river valley of the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) interior were joined under a single governance in the late fifth century of the present age. The unification was the culmination of a generation of negotiation among the valley's community leaders, and it produced the Accord of the River, the agreement that established the office of a common lord and the council of the valley settlements that still governs [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) in the present age.

The Valdmark region is a land of fertile river bottomland flanked by the higher, colder country of the Morvind interior, where the population is concentrated along the banks of the principal river and in a scattering of highland pastures and steppe settlements beyond. The holdings of the valley had been independent for many generations, each governed by its own council of elders or leading families, and each maintaining its own customs. The unification was a difficult and gradual process that required the reconciliation of differences of custom and interest rather than the conquest of one holding by another.

## The Condition of the Holdings Before the Unification

The holdings of the Valdmark valley were prosperous by the standards of the interior but exposed by their disunity. The soil along the river was the richest in the Morvind interior, and the holdings depended upon grain, wool, and river trade for their livelihood, exchanging these products for the metalwork of the dwarven cities of the surrounding mountains. Yet the individual holdings, negotiating separately with the dwarven cities and with one another, could not agree on common tolls, common defence, or the shared maintenance of the river's dykes and irrigation works, and the resulting inefficiencies cost the valley dearly in years of flood or poor harvest.

The holdings were also divided among themselves over the management of the river. Disputes over the diversion of water for irrigation, the placement of weirs and mills, and the maintenance of the flood defences were frequent, and a holding upstream could work great hardship upon a holding downstream by neglecting its share of the dykes or by diverting more than its accustomed portion of the water. These disputes consumed the energy of the valley's leaders and left the holdings poorly placed to respond to the pressures gathering beyond the valley's borders.

The holdings were also vulnerable to pressure from beyond the valley. The tribes and raiders of the cold steppes to the east made periodic incursions into the eastern holdings, and the dwarven cities of the surrounding mountains, while generally cordial trading partners, held the upper hand in negotiations with any single holding that treated with them alone. The valley's leaders had begun to see, independently of one another, that a common governance would strengthen the valley's hand both in trade and in defence.

## The Leadership of the Unification

The unification of the Valdmark holdings is credited to Valden of Valdheim, the leading elder of the holding that controlled the principal ford of the river and the richest stretch of bottomland in the valley. Valden had concluded that the holdings could not manage the river, the trade with the dwarven cities, or the defence of the eastern border as independent entities, and that a common governance was necessary if the valley was to prosper and endure.

Valden began the process by proposing to the neighbouring holdings a shared register of the river's dykes and diversion rights, a modest measure of cooperation that required no surrender of independence but demonstrated the benefits of common management. He travelled to the principal settlements of the valley, meeting with the elders and the leading families and setting out, holding by holding, the case for a wider accord. The response was mixed, with some holdings welcoming the proposal readily and others regarding it with suspicion as the first step toward a surrender of their autonomy.

Valden then pursued a patient course of intermarriage between the leading families of the valley, creating bonds of kinship that eased the negotiation of the wider accord, and he negotiated a series of agreements on trade, the defence of the eastern border, and the resolution of disputes over the river, each agreement building upon the last until the holdings had accumulated a body of shared practice that made a single governance the natural next step.

## The Opposition to the Unification

The unification was resisted by a number of the valley's leading families, who regarded the proposed common governance as a surrender of the independence the holdings had exercised for centuries. The opposition was led by the elders of the upstream holding of Millreach, foremost among them a woman remembered in the valley's traditions as Hilde the Unyielding, who had resisted Valden's overtures from the outset.

Hilde argued that a common lord would inevitably favour the interests of the richer downstream holdings at the expense of the poorer holdings upstream, and that the register of the dykes Valden had proposed was merely the means by which the downstream holdings intended to fix the upstream holdings' water rights permanently to their disadvantage. Several other upstream holdings shared her concerns, and a loose confederation of the opposing holdings formed around Millreach to resist the wider accord.

## The Long Negotiation

The opposition could not be overcome by argument alone, and the negotiation between Valden's faction and Hilde's confederation occupied nearly a decade. The disputed question was not whether the valley should combine its river management and its defence, for even Hilde's faction conceded the necessity of that much, but what safeguards the upstream holdings would receive against the dominance of the wealthier downstream settlements. The negotiations were conducted through repeated councils held at neutral ground along the river, mediated by elders of holdings that belonged to neither faction.

The turning point came when Valden's faction agreed to Hilde's central demand: that the common lord be chosen not by simple inheritance or by the wealth of a holding, but by the vote of a council on which every holding, however small, held an equal voice, and that the council retain the power to remove a lord who abused the office. This concession transformed the character of the union being proposed, from an arrangement that a powerful holding might dominate into one in which every holding held a permanent check upon the office it had created. With this concession secured, the confederation at Millreach dissolved, and Hilde herself sat on the first council that convened to choose a lord.

## The Establishment of the Governance

The unification of the Valdmark holdings was completed in the year 489, when the elders of the holdings gathered at Valdheim for a council that established the Accord of the River. The council was attended by the elders of every holding of the valley, and it agreed upon the constitution that has governed Valdmark ever since: the valley would be governed by a lord chosen by the council of the valley settlements from among candidates the council itself might consider, serving for life but removable by the council for cause, and advised at all times by that same council in matters of trade, defence, and the management of the river. The individual holdings retained their local courts and customs, subject to the common governance in matters that touched the valley as a whole.

Valden was chosen as the first lord of Valdmark by the council convened at Valdheim, an honour the traditions of the valley attribute to the patience of his long negotiation as much as to the wealth of his holding. He held the office for a period of nineteen years, during which he established the practical workings of the council and the register of the river that had first brought the holdings into cooperation.

## The Legacy of the Unification

The Valdmark Unification was one of the more significant events in the history of the Morvind interior, joining the scattered river holdings under the governance that still administers the valley, connects its trade to the dwarven cities of the surrounding mountains, and defends its eastern border against the tribes of the cold steppes.

The legacy of the unification is visible in the institutions of Valdmark today: the office of the lord, the council of the valley settlements that chooses and may remove him, and the system of local holding courts that preserves the autonomy of the individual settlements within the common governance. The unification also gave the valley the strength to negotiate as a single voice with the dwarven cities of [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar) and [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) and to maintain the [March Wardens](/content/military/valdmark-march-wardens) who guard its eastern border.

The memory of the unification is preserved in the chronicles of the valley and in the ceremonies of the holdings. The anniversary of the council that established the Accord of the River is marked by a gathering at Valdheim, attended by the lord, the council of the valley settlements, and the magistrates of the holding courts. The gathering includes a renewal of the council's oath to uphold the Accord and a feast celebrating the unity of the valley.
