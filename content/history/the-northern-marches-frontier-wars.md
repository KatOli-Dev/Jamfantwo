## The Conflicts of the Northern Border

The [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) Frontier Wars were a series of conflicts fought on the northern frontier of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) in the middle and late periods of the present age. The wars were fought between the settlers and the militia of the Marches and the peoples of the Frostwall and the [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) interior, including the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm), and a number of smaller kindreds that inhabited the region. The wars lasted for several generations and were marked by raids, skirmishes, and occasional larger battles that shaped the frontier of the Marches.

The Northern Marches occupy the region between the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) and the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea), a cold and rugged territory that is exposed to the winds of the north and the storms of the sea. The Marches were settled by migrants from the south and the west during the period of the [Frostwall Migration](/content/history/the-frostwall-migration), and the settlers were required to defend their territory against the incursions of the peoples who had occupied the region before them.

## The Causes of the Wars

The causes of the frontier wars were complex and varied from conflict to conflict. The principal cause was competition for the control of the land and the resources. The settlers of the Marches were expanding their territory northward and eastward, encroaching upon the lands that the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) and the other northern peoples regarded as their own. The competition for the hunting territories, the fishing waters, and the grazing lands was intense, and the conflicts arose when the settlers and the northern peoples disputed the right to use the resources.

The trade also contributed to the tensions. The merchants of the Northern Marches traded with the northern peoples, exchanging steel tools and woven cloth for furs, seal oil, and dried fish. The trade was profitable for both sides, but it was also a source of conflict when the merchants were perceived to be taking advantage of the northern peoples or when the northern peoples were perceived to be demanding too much in exchange for their goods.

The political conditions of the north also contributed to the wars. The Frostwall Clans were not a unified people, and the individual clans often acted independently, raiding the settlements of the Marches for the cattle and the goods that they could carry off. The [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) was also a source of instability, as the kings of [Karhold](/content/location/settlement/city/karhold) sought to extend their influence into the Marches and to bring the settlers under their authority.

## The Early Wars

The first of the frontier wars was fought in the late fourth century of the present age, when a party of settlers from the Marches attempted to establish a settlement upon the coast north of the [Greywash River](/content/location/natural/feature/greywash-river). The land was claimed by a Frostwall clan, and the clan attacked the settlement and drove the settlers back to the Marches. The settlers retaliated by raiding the clan's winter camp, and the conflict escalated into a war that lasted for several years.

The war was fought in the forests and the valleys of the north, where the terrain favoured the Frostwall clans, who knew the land well and moved through it with ease. The settlers were at a disadvantage, as they lacked the knowledge of the land and the skills of the northern peoples. The war ended with a truce that established a boundary between the territory of the settlers and the territory of the Frostwall clans, but the boundary was disputed from the beginning and the conflicts continued at intervals for the next century.

The second of the frontier wars was fought in the early fifth century, when the Karhold Realm attempted to bring the settlements of the Northern Marches under the authority of the Karhold king. The king of Karhold sent an army into the Marches, demanding that the settlers recognise his authority and pay taxes to the Karhold treasury. The settlers refused, and a war ensued that lasted for three years.

The war was marked by a series of battles in the passes of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range), where the settlers defended the approaches to the Marches against the Karhold army. The settlers were outnumbered and outmatched in the open field, but they were fighting in defence of their homes and they were determined to resist the Karhold advance. The war ended with a treaty that recognised the independence of the Northern Marches and established a boundary between the Marches and the Karhold Realm.

## The Middle Wars

The middle period of the frontier wars was the most intense, as the population of the Marches grew and the competition for the land and the resources increased. The wars of the middle period were fought on a larger scale than the early wars, with larger armies and more extensive campaigns.

The most significant of the middle wars was the war of the years 462 to 465, which was fought between the Northern Marches and a confederation of Frostwall clans that had united under a single leader for the first time. The confederation was led by a chieftain named Ulf the Red, who had gathered the clans under his banner by a combination of persuasion and force.

Ulf's campaign was the most serious threat that the Northern Marches had faced. He led his warriors through the passes of the Crown Range and into the heart of the Marches, burning the settlements and driving the settlers before him. The militia of the Marches was unable to stop him in the open field, and the settlers were forced to retreat to the fortified towns and to wait for the winter to stop Ulf's advance.

The winter was the salvation of the Marches. Ulf's army was not prepared for a winter campaign, and the cold and the snow forced him to withdraw from the Marches. The militia pursued him through the passes and attacked his army as it retreated, inflicting heavy losses. Ulf survived the retreat but his confederation did not, and the clans returned to their independent ways after the war.

## The Later Wars

The later period of the frontier wars was marked by a gradual reduction in the intensity of the conflicts. The settlers of the Marches had established themselves firmly in the region, and the Frostwall clans and the Karhold Realm had accepted the presence of the Marches as a permanent feature of the north. The wars of the later period were smaller in scale and less frequent than the wars of the earlier periods.

The last of the frontier wars was fought in the early sixth century, when a dispute over the fishing waters of the coast led to a conflict between the Northern Marches and the Karhold Realm. The conflict was resolved by the mediation of the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), which established a joint commission to regulate the fishing and to resolve disputes between the parties.

## The Legacy of the Wars

The Northern Marches Frontier Wars shaped the frontier of the Marches and determined the boundaries of the territories of the northern peoples. The wars established the independence of the Marches from the Karhold Realm and the Frostwall clans, and they defined the territory that the Marches would control for the centuries that followed.

The legacy of the wars is visible in the fortified towns and the watchtowers that dot the frontier of the Marches, in the militia that the settlers maintain for the defence of their territory, and in the treaties that established the boundaries between the Marches and the neighbouring powers. The memory of the wars is preserved in the chronicles of the Marches and in the ceremonies of the settlers.

The wars also established the character of the people of the Northern Marches. The settlers who defended their territory against the northern peoples became known for their independence, their resilience, and their determination to defend their freedom. The character of the Marches that was forged in the frontier wars has persisted to the present day.
