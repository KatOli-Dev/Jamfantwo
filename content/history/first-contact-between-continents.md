## The Crossing That Changed the World

The first contacts between the peoples of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) following [Kaelen's Voyage](/content/history/kaelens-voyage) rank among the most consequential events of the present age. The voyage had demonstrated that the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) could be crossed, and the merchants and pilots of the warm coast were quick to act upon the knowledge. Within a decade of Kaelen's return, the first vessels from [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) had made landfall upon the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), opening a new world of trade, diplomacy, and conflict that would reshape the relations of the three continents.

The period of first contacts is poorly documented in the surviving records. The chronicles of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) preserve the names of the first pilots to reach Sahrana but offer few details of their encounters with the peoples of the desert coast. The chronicles of [Sahran](/content/location/settlement/city/sahran) and [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir) are more detailed but were composed several generations after the events they describe and include elements of legend that the academies discount. The reconstruction of the period relies upon a comparison of the chronicles with the archaeological evidence of the earliest trading posts and upon the oral traditions of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact).

## The First Landfall

The first confirmed landfall upon Sahrana was made by a pilot named Orin the Far-Sighted, who set out from [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the third year following Kaelen's return. Orin had served as a junior pilot on the Grey Gull during [Kaelen's voyage](/content/history/kaelens-voyage) and had studied the winds and currents of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) with great care. He judged that a more southerly course would carry a vessel to a warmer coast than the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and he persuaded three merchants of Khalisar to equip a small vessel for the attempt.

Orin sailed south of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) for fifteen days before sighting the high dunes of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast. He landed upon a stretch of coast that the later chronicles identify as the [Glasslight Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/glasslight-coast), so named for the pale sand that sparkles in the sun. The people he encountered were the ancestors of the present-day members of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), who received him with cautious hospitality. Orin remained upon the coast for three months, trading steel tools and woven cloth for the spices, dried fruits, and fine-woven carpets that the desert peoples produced. He returned to Khalisar with a modest but profitable cargo and with reports of a vast and populous land stretching beyond the horizon.

The second voyage was equipped by the council of Khalisar itself, which judged that the trade with Sahrana promised even greater profit than the trade with the Kharesh. A fleet of five vessels was assembled, commanded by Orin and by a second pilot, Vaelen of the [Grey Coast](/content/location/settlement/region/grey-coast). The fleet carried a larger cargo of trade goods and a company of twenty armed men to protect the trading post that the council intended to establish. The fleet made landfall at the same stretch of coast and established a permanent post at a natural harbour sheltered by a low headland. The post was named [Southport](/content/location/settlement/town/southport), and it remains the principal port of the Sahrani coast to the present day.

## Encounters with the Sahrani Peoples

The chronicles of the period record a series of encounters between the Khalisar merchants and the various peoples of the Sahrani coast. The first contacts were with the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes), who inhabited the northern coast and the interior deserts. The Kharesh peoples were organised into a number of independent kindreds, each governing its own territory and maintaining its own customs. The Khalisar merchants found the kindreds to be divided among themselves, with some kindreds welcoming the trade and others regarding the newcomers with suspicion.

The kindreds that welcomed the trade were those that had the most to gain from the exchange. The kindreds of the coast controlled the harbours and the trading routes, and they saw in the Khalisar merchants a source of steel and cloth that they could not produce themselves. The kindreds of the interior were more cautious, as they had less need of the goods the merchants offered and more reason to fear the disruption of the established order. The tensions between the welcoming and the cautious kindreds shaped the early period of contact and laid the foundations for the later conflicts that the chronicles record.

The first contact with the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) occurred several years after the establishment of the Southport post. The [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) were a confederation of city-states that controlled the southern coast of Sahrana and the rich trade routes of the [Lesser Inland Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/lesser-inland-sea). The Vashari were more powerful and more organised than the Kharesh kindreds, and they regarded the Khalisar merchants as potential rivals rather than as useful trading partners. The first Vashari envoy to visit Southport was received with courtesy but with a clear demonstration of the Vashari's military strength. The envoy made no threats but made it plain that the Vashari would not tolerate any encroachment upon their territories or their trade.

## The Establishment of Regular Trade

The first decade of contact was marked by the gradual establishment of regular trade between the ports of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the ports of Sahrana. The merchants of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) invested heavily in the Sahrani trade, equipping larger vessels and sending them on regular voyages. The trade was profitable from the beginning, as the Sahrani coast produced goods that commanded high prices in the markets of Aethelar: spices, rare woods, fine textiles, and the distinctive blue glass that the Sahrani artisans produced.

The trade brought wealth to the ports of both continents and transformed the economies of the coastal regions. The merchants of Khalisar grew rich on the Sahrani trade, and the city itself expanded to accommodate the increased commerce. New warehouses, wharves, and markets were built along the waterfront, and the population swelled with merchants, pilots, and craftsmen drawn by the opportunities the trade offered. [Sahran](/content/location/settlement/city/sahran) and [Qassir](/content/location/settlement/city/qassir) experienced a similar expansion, as the trade with Aethelar brought new goods and new wealth to the desert coast.

The trade also brought new knowledge. The scholars of the academy of Khalisar studied the languages, customs, and natural history of Sahrana and compiled the first accounts of the desert continent. The accounts were imperfect, as the scholars relied upon the reports of merchants and pilots who had limited contact with the interior, but they represented the best understanding that the present age could offer. The maps of the Sahrani coast were revised and improved with each voyage, and the pilots of the warm coast accumulated a body of knowledge that would serve for generations.

## Conflict and Accommodation

The period of first contacts was not without conflict. The tensions between the welcoming and the cautious kindreds of the Kharesh Tribes occasionally erupted into violence, and the Khalisar merchants found themselves caught between the rival kindreds. The most serious incident occurred in the seventh year of the trade, when a party of merchants from Khalisar was attacked by a band of raiders from the interior. The attack was repulsed by the armed men of the Southport post, but several merchants lost their lives, and the trade was suspended for a season while the council of Khalisar considered its response.

The council judged that the trade was too valuable to abandon and that the attack had been the work of a single kindred rather than a general hostility. The council dispatched an envoy to the elders of the offending kindred and demanded restitution for the losses. The elders of the kindred were reluctant to comply, but the other kindreds of the coast, who had profited from the trade, pressured them to settle the matter. The restitution was paid, and the trade resumed under stricter rules that required all parties to submit their disputes to the arbitration of a council of elders from the friendly kindreds.

The encounter with the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) was more complex. The Vashari did not attack the Khalisar merchants but made it clear that they would not permit the establishment of a permanent post within their territory. The merchants of Khalisar were permitted to trade at the ports of the Vashari Pact but were required to pay the same customs dues that the Vashari imposed upon all foreign merchants. The arrangement was acceptable to the council of Khalisar, which judged that the trade was profitable enough to bear the cost of the dues, and the relationship between the two powers remained formal but peaceful.

## The Legacy of the First Contacts

The period of first contacts lasted approximately two generations, from Orin's first landfall to the establishment of the regular trading routes that connected the ports of Aethelar and Sahrana. The period ended with the recognition of Sahrana as a distinct continent with its own peoples, governments, and customs, and with the acceptance of the trade between the two continents as a permanent feature of the economic order of the present age.

The legacy of the first contacts is preserved in the chronicles of the ports of both continents and in the institutions that the trade established. The Southport post remains the principal point of contact between the two continents, governed by a consul appointed by the council of Khalisar. The consul adjudicates disputes between the merchants of the two continents and maintains friendly relations with the elders of the Kharesh kindreds. The office of the consul is among the most ancient of the present age, established in the first generation of the trade and maintained without interruption for several centuries.

The first contacts also laid the foundations for the later conflicts that the chronicles record, including the [Twin-Reaches Wars](/content/history/twin-reaches-wars) and the [Grain Fleet Wars](/content/history/the-grain-fleet-wars). The competition for control of the trade routes and the harbours of the Sahrani coast would lead to a series of wars between the powers of Aethelar and the powers of Sahrana, but the period of first contacts was a time of cautious cooperation rather than open conflict. The wars came later, when the stakes had grown and the parties had become more confident in their knowledge of each other.

The memory of the first contacts is preserved in the ceremonies of the ports of the warm coast. The anniversary of Orin's first landfall is marked by a service in the [temple of the Sea Mother](/content/religion/temple-of-the-sea-mother)'s Court at Khalisar and by a procession through the streets of Southport. The service is attended by the pilots, merchants, and magistrates of the ports of both continents and is one of the principal observances of the warm coast.
