## The War of the Three Confederations

The Three-Banners War is the most celebrated of the wars of the plains, fought between the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) and two rival confederations that contested the control of the central grasslands in the middle period. The war lasted seven years and resulted in the consolidation of the Confederation's control over the central grasslands, a position it has maintained to the present day. The chronicles of the peoples of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) record it as the central event of the middle period of their history, held in the memory of every kindred of the plains.

The war was fought upon the open grasslands stretching from the foothills of the mountain spine in the west to the broad river valleys draining toward the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) in the east. It was decided by the mounted warriors of the three confederations in a series of engagements preserved in unusual detail. The chronicles of all three parties agree upon the principal events but disagree upon their meaning, and the disagreement reflects the importance the war has assumed in the traditions of the plains.

## Dispute Over Summer Grazing Lands

The war began with a dispute over the control of the great summer grazing lands of the central grasslands, which the three confederations had shared through customary arrangements in place for several generations. The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) claimed the right to regulate the grazing by virtue of an agreement the founders of the Confederation had made with the elders of the principal kindreds in the early centuries of the present age. The two rival confederations disputed the claim, arguing that the agreement had been made before either rival confederation had been founded and that they were not bound by it. The elders of the principal kindreds decided in favour of the Confederation. The rival confederations refused to accept the decision, and the Confederation dispatched a mounted levy to enforce the ruling.

The levy was met upon the open grasslands by the combined forces of the rival confederations. The levy was insufficient for the task the council of the Confederation had set it and was driven back to the principal settlements with heavy loss. The council responded by calling upon the full levy of the member cities and the centaur kindreds, marking the beginning of the war as it was fought for the next seven years.

## Three Phases of the Plains Conflict

The course of the war is conventionally divided into three phases: the ascendancy of the rival confederations, the Confederation's recovery, and the negotiations that led to peace. The first phase lasted two years and was marked by the victories of the rival confederations upon the open grasslands and the loss by the Confederation of several principal summer grazing grounds. The rival confederations controlled the principal grazing lands and imposed a tribute upon the herds of the member kindreds of the Confederation to sustain the war. The Confederation retreated to the river valleys of the eastern grasslands, and its council maintained the levy at the principal settlements. This phase was marked by great hardship in the Confederation.

The second phase lasted three years and began with the appointment of a council of war and the construction of new fortified camps at the principal water sources of the central grasslands. The council of war rebuilt the levy from the resources of the member cities and the centaur kindreds and trained the riders in new tactics observed in the rival confederations' levy. The council met the rival confederations in the open grasslands west of the principal summer grazing grounds and won a decisive victory that broke their control of the grazing.

The third phase lasted two years and was marked by the efforts of the mediators of the neighbouring kingdoms to bring the parties to a settlement. A new council of elders acceded to the leadership of the Confederation, including several elders who had been instrumental in the construction of the fortified camps. The new council judged that the war had reached a stalemate and opened negotiations through the mediators.

## Finding Common Ground

Each party insisted upon the rights it had claimed at the beginning of the war. The Confederation insisted upon the customary arrangements by virtue of the original agreement; the rival confederations insisted upon the right to share in the grazing by virtue of the natural liberty of the kindreds. The mediators proposed a compromise establishing a council of the three confederations to regulate the grazing of the central grasslands. The compromise was accepted by the three councils. The peace was signed at the great summer camp that the three confederations maintained upon the central grasslands and is known as the [Peace of the Three Banners](/content/history/peace-of-the-three-banners).

## Reconstruction and Lasting Institutions

The aftermath was marked by a long period of reconstruction in all three confederations. The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) rebuilt the fortified camps and re-established the customary arrangements the peace had confirmed. The rival confederations rebuilt the herds lost in the war and entered into the council of the three confederations the peace had established. The war left a permanent mark upon the relations of the three confederations, characterised by a mixture of rivalry and cooperation.

Wars of the plains have been less frequent in the later period, as the Confederation has established institutions that have made wars unnecessary, and the negotiation of treaties between the Confederation and neighbouring kingdoms has reduced occasions of conflict. The Confederation remains a formidable military power, and the mounted warriors of the plains are among the most accomplished cavalry in the known world. The annual hunt of the thunder boar is the occasion upon which the warriors demonstrate their prowess and a ceremonial occasion that binds the peoples of the plains to the traditions of the grasslands.

## Green, Blue, and Red Standards

The war takes its name from the three banners the three confederations carried into the field, preserved in the chronicles as the most distinctive relics of the conflict. The banner of the Confederation is a field of green with a single white horse rampant, the standard the mounted levy has carried since the founding of the alliance. The banner of the first rival confederation is a field of blue with a single yellow sun. The banner of the second rival confederation is a field of red with a single black bull. The three banners are held in the hall of the council of the Confederation and displayed at its principal ceremonies. The ceremonies are held at the great summer camp upon the central grasslands and include the recitation of customary songs that celebrate the victory of the Confederation.

## The Great Grass Plains Battlefield

The war was fought upon the open grasslands of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The grasslands are a country of marked seasonal contrasts, with hot summers and cold winters separated by brief but dramatic periods of transition, and the seasons determined the rhythm of the war. The principal engagements were fought in the summer, when the grass was tallest and the herds were in their best condition. The winter was a time of relative quiet in which the levies retired to their fortified camps.

The open country favoured the mounted warriors of the three confederations and gave little advantage to foot soldiers. The mounted warriors were drawn from the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds and the [human](/content/species/sapient/humans) riders of the member cities and were trained from youth in the arts of mounted combat the grasslands demand. The tactics of the war were refined over the seven years of the conflict, recorded as the foundation of mounted warfare in the present age.

## Consolidation of the Confederation's Control

The Three-Banners War is the most celebrated of the wars of the plains and the central event of the middle period of the history of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) and of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation). The war lasted seven years and resulted in the consolidation of the Confederation's control over the central grasslands. The elders of the member cities and the centaur kindreds mark the anniversaries of the principal engagements with ceremonies attended by the magistrates and principal citizens of every member city of the Confederation.
