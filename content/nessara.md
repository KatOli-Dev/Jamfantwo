## Overview

Nessara is a coastal city-state on the northern coast of [Sahrana](/content/sahrana), situated at the edge of the fertile strip where the coastal agriculture gives way to the drier country of the western coast. The city is the northernmost of the major Sahrani city-states, and its position at the boundary of the fertile and the arid coast gives it a character that is distinct from the other cities of the northern coast. Nessara is a prosperous, well-situated city, and its wealth is built upon the agriculture of the surrounding country and the trade that flows through its harbour.

## The Coast and the Agriculture

The coast around Nessara is the northernmost portion of the fertile strip that supports the agriculture of the Sahrani coast, and the land here is less productive than the regions to the south, for the rainfall is lower and the soil is thinner. The agriculture of the region is focused on the hardy crops that can tolerate the drier conditions, including the grains, the olives, and the vines that are the staples of the northern Sahrani diet, and the farms that surround the city produce enough to sustain the community and to provide a surplus for export. The vineyards of Nessara are among the most valued in Sahrana, and the wine that is produced here is noted for its quality and its distinctive character. The vines that are grown at Nessara are a variety that is adapted to the lower rainfall and the cooler nights of the northern coast, and the grapes that they produce are smaller and more concentrated than those of the southern vineyards, yielding a wine that is deeper in colour and more robust in flavour.

The position of Nessara at the edge of the fertile strip gives the city a strategic significance, for it is the last major settlement on the coast before the land becomes too arid for agriculture, and the trade that flows through its harbour includes the goods of the fertile south and the products of the arid west. The caravans that travel the coast road between the fertile and the arid regions pass through Nessara, and the city is a natural stopping point for the overland trade that connects the regions of the Sahrani coast. The caravans that arrive from the arid west carry the salt, the minerals, and the other products of the desert that are valued in the markets of the fertile coast, and the caravans that depart from Nessara carry the agricultural products and the manufactured goods that the arid regions cannot produce for themselves.

The olive groves of Nessara are another significant element of the agricultural economy, and the oil that is pressed from the olives of the region is a valued export that is prized for its flavour and its quality. The olive harvest is the most important event of the agricultural calendar after the grape harvest, and the pressing of the olives is a communal activity that involves the families of the surrounding farms and the workers of the city. The oil is stored in the large clay vessels that are produced by the potters of Nessara, and the vessels are a distinctive feature of the warehouses that line the waterfront, their rounded shapes and their sealed mouths a familiar sight to the merchants and the sailors who trade at the harbour.

## The Harbour and the Trade

The harbour of Nessara is a natural anchorage that is sheltered by a small island that lies offshore, and the approach is straightforward in most weather. The quays line the inner shore of the bay, and the warehouses and trading houses that serve the coastal trade are situated along the waterfront. The trade of Nessara is a mixture of the agricultural products of the surrounding country and the goods that are imported from the other regions of the coast and the northern continents. The wine, the oil, and the grain of Nessara are the primary exports, and the return cargo includes the spices and the dyes of the southern coast and the manufactured goods of the north.

The population of Nessara is chiefly [human](/content/humans), with a small community of traders from the other Sahrani cities and the northern continents. The [tortolans](/content/tortolans) of the warm coasts are occasional visitors to the harbour, and the trade between the coastal humans and the tortolan communities of the islands is a minor but valued part of the city's commerce.

## The City

Nessara is a well-built, prosperous city, and its architecture reflects the character of the northern coast, with buildings of light-coloured stone, shaded courtyards, and the gardens and fountains that are the hallmark of the Sahrani architectural tradition, though the gardens here are smaller and more modest than those of the southern cities, for the water is less abundant. The streets are broad and well-paved, and the public buildings are substantial and well-maintained. The city is walled, for the arid country to the west is a source of occasional trouble, and the fortifications are maintained in good order. The walls are of a substantial height and thickness, and they are fitted with the towers that allow the garrison to observe the approaches to the city from the land and the sea, and the gates are guarded by the soldiers of the city at all hours.

The governance of Nessara is managed by a council of the prominent citizens, including the landowners, the merchants, and the commanders of the garrison, and the council oversees the administration of the city, the regulation of the trade, and the maintenance of the fortifications. The council meets in the civic hall that stands in the central square of the city, and the hall is a building of considerable size and dignity, with a colonnaded front that is a feature of the Sahrani civic architecture. The council is concerned chiefly with the management of the trade that flows through the city and the defence of the frontier, and the decisions that are made in the hall affect the welfare of the entire northern coast.

## Significance

Nessara is the northernmost of the major Sahrani city-states, and its position at the edge of the fertile strip gives it a strategic and a commercial significance that is disproportionate to its moderate size. The city is a member of the [League of the Northern Coast](/content/league-of-the-northern-coast), and its agriculture and its trade are a valued contribution to the economy of the Sahrani coast. The significance of Nessara lies in its position at the boundary of the fertile and the arid coast, and the city is a symbol of the adaptability and the resilience of the Sahrani people in the face of the demanding conditions of the northern coast. The wine and the oil that are produced at Nessara are recognised throughout Sahrana for their quality, and the city's role as the gateway between the fertile and the arid regions of the coast ensures that its prosperity is sustained by the trade that flows through its gates.
