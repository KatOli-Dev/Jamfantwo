## Origins

The origins of the giants are among the most debated questions in the natural history of the thinking peoples, for the giant body is the largest and most powerful of any sapient species, and their numbers are so few and their habits so reclusive that reliable information is scarce. Some naturalists have proposed that giants share a kinship with [trolls](/content/trolls), citing the great size, the mountain habitat, and the remarkable longevity shared by both peoples, though the giants are far larger and far more specialised in their requirements. Others have argued for a connection to [goliaths](/content/goliaths), pointing to the high-altitude range and the adaptation to thin air that both display, but the giants are too different in scale and in form for the comparison to be sustained. The giants themselves preserve oral traditions that are known to other peoples only through the rarest of encounters, and these traditions speak of a time when the giants were more numerous and the world was younger, though the reliability of such accounts cannot be assessed.

What is generally accepted is that giants are an ancient people, and their lineage may be the oldest of any sapient species, for their bodies show a degree of specialisation that suggests a very long period of adaptation to their environment. The fossil record offers little clarity, for giant remains are seldom found in conditions that allow for confident identification, and the few bones that have been recovered are so large that they have been mistaken for geological formations.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Giants are the largest of the sapient peoples, standing twice the height of a [troll](/content/trolls) and more than three times the height of a [human](/content/humans), with a massive and broadly built frame. Their bones are extraordinarily thick and dense, capable of supporting the enormous weight of the giant body, and their musculature is vast, granting them a strength that is beyond the capacity of any other people to match or resist. A giant can move boulders that would require a team of [dwarves](/content/dwarves) to shift, and the force that a giant can bring to bear in a single effort is sufficient to reshape the landscape.

The giant body is built for endurance and stability rather than speed, for the great bulk of the frame demands a careful management of balance and momentum. Their movements are slow and deliberate, and a giant walking across a valley is a sight that few who have witnessed it forget, for each step covers a distance that would take a human several strides, and the ground trembles beneath their feet. Despite their size, giants are not clumsy, and their large hands are capable of fine and precise work, for their sense of touch is well developed and their fingers, though enormous, are strong and steady.

Giant skin is thick and tough, ranging from weathered grey to dark brown, and it is adapted to the intense cold and the ultraviolet light of the high-altitude environments they inhabit. Their hair is coarse and thick, worn long and unbound, and it provides additional insulation against the cold. Giant senses are adequate, though not exceptional relative to their size. Their eyes are large and set forward, granting good binocular vision and excellent distance sight, for the open vistas of the high peaks and the remote valleys where they dwell demand awareness of the far horizon. Their hearing is competent, and their sense of smell is moderate. The giant body is not specialised by sense but by sheer scale and resilience.

## Life Cycle

Giants are the longest-lived of the sapient peoples, with a lifespan that is estimated at three hundred to five hundred years, though reliable data are almost impossible to obtain, for the giants are so few and so reclusive that no naturalist has been able to study their life cycle in detail. They grow to maturity very slowly, not reaching their full size and strength until their fiftieth or sixtieth year, and they remain vigorous for centuries. Giant women carry their young for approximately two years before giving birth, and births are invariably single. The infant is enormous and well-developed, capable of walking within days, though it requires many decades of care before it is fully independent.

Giant populations are so small that the birth of a single infant is a significant event for the entire species, and the growth of the population is negligible over any period shorter than a century. The extreme longevity of the giants and the very low rate of death among adults mean that the population remains roughly stable despite the infrequency of births, for a giant that has reached maturity has no natural predators and is resistant to disease and environmental hardship. The small number of giants and the vast distances between them mean that encounters between potential mates are rare, and the reproductive rate is limited as much by geography as by biology.

## Habitat and Distribution

Giants are found in the most remote mountains of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/morvind), in the highest and most inaccessible ranges, where the terrain is too extreme for any other people to settle. They favour the high valleys, the remote plateaus, and the hidden basins that lie between the highest peaks, where the land is open and the views are vast and the approaches are so difficult that no other sapient people can reach them. The [geography](/content/geography) of each range determines the distribution of the giants, and they are found only where the mountains are tall enough and remote enough to provide the solitude that their way of life demands.

In Aethelar, giants are found in the highest reaches of the mountain spine, above the [goliaths](/content/goliaths) and the [trolls](/content/trolls), in the most remote and least accessible portions of the range. In Morvind, they inhabit the deepest interior, in the hidden valleys and the high plateaus that lie beyond the reach of the [dwarven](/content/dwarves) halls and the human coastal settlements. Giants are absent from the lowlands, the forests, and the coasts, for their great size requires the open ground and the solitude of the highest mountains, and they are uncomfortable in the warm, dense air of the low country.

## Diet

The giant diet is dominated by what the high mountains can provide, and giants are opportunistic omnivores who make use of whatever the remote valleys and the high plateaus offer. The staple is meat, provided by the hunting of the large mammals that share the high mountains, including the great elk, the mountain cattle, and the bears of the upper forests. A single giant consumes an enormous quantity of food, and the hunting range of a giant is correspondingly vast, encompassing many square miles of mountain and valley.

Plant foods are gathered from the alpine meadows and the high valleys, including the roots, berries, and hardy vegetables that grow in the thin soil of the uplands. Giants do not practise agriculture, for the growing season at their altitudes is too short and the terrain too steep for cultivation, and they rely entirely on what the high country provides. Their metabolism is slow and efficient, suited to the sparse and intermittent food supply of the mountain environment, and a giant can sustain long periods of fasting between successful hunts, drawing on the reserves of a body that is built to endure.

## Ecology

Giants occupy the niche of an enormous, solitary omnivore in the remote mountain ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is considerable in the immediate vicinity of their dwelling, for a giant consumes a great deal and alters the landscape through the sheer scale of its movements and activities, but the overall effect on the ecology of the mountains is modest, for their numbers are so few and their range so vast that the giant presence is barely detectable beyond the immediate area of habitation.

Giants have almost no contact with other sapient peoples, owing to the extreme remoteness of their habitats and their solitary, reclusive nature. [Goliaths](/content/goliaths) and giants occasionally share the same mountain ranges, but the two peoples separate by altitude and terrain, and encounters between them are so rare that they are the subject of legend rather than record. [Trolls](/content/trolls) are found at lower elevations in the same ranges, and the giants and the trolls are aware of one another, but each gives the other a wide berth, for the giant is too large and the troll too stubborn for either to benefit from proximity.
