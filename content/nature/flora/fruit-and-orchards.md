## The Orchard Traditions of Aethelar

The cultivation of fruit trees is among the oldest of agricultural practices in the known world, and the orchards of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are among the most highly developed examples of this art. The temperate climate of the continent, with its reliable rainfall and moderate temperatures, provides ideal conditions for the growing of pome fruits and stone fruits that are the foundation of the orchard economy. The principal fruit regions are the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale), the sheltered valleys of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) margins, and the coastal districts of the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) and the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael).

The orchard is a landscape shaped by deliberate human purpose, in which the trees are planted in orderly rows that permit the passage of carts and the movement of pickers at harvest time. The ground between the trees is kept clear of competing vegetation, and the soil is enriched with manure and compost that the orchardists apply in the autumn after the harvest is complete. The trees themselves are pruned each winter to maintain a shape that allows light to reach the fruiting branches and that makes the picking of the fruit easier in the following season.

The care of an orchard is the work of a lifetime, for the trees that an orchardist plants will not reach their full production for a decade or more, and the finest orchards are those that have been tended by successive generations of the same family. The knowledge of pruning, grafting, and pest management is passed from parent to child, and the varieties that grow in a given orchard are the product of centuries of selection and breeding.

## Apples and the Cider Presses of the North

The apple is the most widespread fruit tree of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), grown in every region where the winters are cold enough to provide the dormancy period that the tree requires and the summers are warm enough to ripen the fruit. The apple trees of the [Brackenshield Wood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/brackenshield-wood) margins and the slopes of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) produce fruit of exceptional quality, and the varieties that are grown in these regions are known by the names of the settlements that originated them.

The principal use of the apple is the production of cider, a fermented beverage that is consumed in vast quantities throughout the northern regions of Aethelar and in the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) settlements of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The cider presses of the northern orchards are among the most important agricultural implements in the region, and the pressing season in autumn is a time of intense activity when the orchardists and their families work from dawn to dusk to crush the fruit and collect the juice. The juice is fermented in oak barrels that are stored in cellars where the temperature remains steady through the winter, and the cider that emerges in the spring is a clear, sharp beverage that keeps well through the following year.

The apples that are not pressed for cider are stored in cool cellars that preserve them through the winter months, providing a source of fresh fruit that is valued in the northern settlements where few other fruits are available. The stored apples are eaten raw, baked into pies and pastries, or cooked into sauce that accompanies roasted meat, and the dried apple rings that are produced in the orchards of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) are a staple provision of the ships that carry the trade of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean).

## Pears and the Stone Fruits of the Vale

The pear is a more delicate fruit than the apple, requiring warmer conditions and more careful handling, and its cultivation is concentrated in the sheltered valleys of the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the coastal districts of the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth). The pear tree is long-lived, capable of bearing fruit for a century or more, and the oldest pear trees in the known world are found in the walled orchards of the central Aethelaran settlements.

The pear is consumed fresh when ripe, its flesh soft and sweet, and it is also used in the production of perry, a fermented beverage similar to cider but with a more delicate flavour. The perry of the Vintners Vale is exported to the cities of the coast, where it is served at the tables of the wealthy as an accompaniment to the rich dishes of the northern cuisine.

The stone fruits of Aethelar include the plum, the cherry, and the damson, each of which has its own season and its own uses. The plum is the most widely grown of the stone fruits, its varieties ranging from the small and tart damsons of the northern orchards to the large and sweet plums that ripen in the late summer sun of the southern valleys. The plum is eaten fresh, dried into prunes that keep through the winter, or cooked into preserves and jams that are the pride of the households that prepare them.

The cherry is a less common fruit, the cherry tree being more demanding in its requirements for soil and aspect, and its cultivation is limited to the most favoured districts of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the lower slopes of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood). The cherry harvest in early summer is a brief and intense period, for the fruit does not keep for more than a few days after picking, and the cherries that are not consumed immediately are preserved in syrup or distilled into a spirit that is highly valued by the connoisseurs of the coastal cities.

## The Olive Groves of the Sahrani Coast

The olive is the characteristic fruit of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast, grown in groves that cover the slopes of the hills overlooking the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The olive tree is long-lived and drought-tolerant, capable of surviving the dry summers of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip) and producing fruit from soils too shallow and stony for the cultivation of grains or vegetables. The olive groves of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) are among the oldest cultivated landscapes in [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), and the oil that is pressed from their fruit is the foundation of the Sahrani cuisine and a significant export to the northern markets.

The cultivation of the olive requires patience, for the tree does not bear fruit in commercial quantities until it has been established for at least five years, and the full production of a grove is not reached until the trees are twenty years old or more. The olive harvest begins in late autumn, when the fruit has changed from green to black and the oil content is at its highest. The olives are beaten from the branches with long poles, gathered in nets spread beneath the trees, and carried to the presses that extract the oil.

The oil of the first pressing, called virgin oil, is the finest and commands the highest price in the markets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra). The oil of the second pressing is used for cooking, and the residue of the pressing is used as fuel for the lamps that light the houses of the coastal settlements. The trade in olive oil is regulated by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the oil merchants, who maintain the standards of quality that ensure the reputation of Sahrani oil in the markets of the north.

## The Citrus Gardens of Khalisar

The citrus trees that grow in the gardens of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and the other coastal cities of Sahrana are the most celebrated fruit trees of the southern continent. The orange, the lemon, and the lime are grown in walled gardens where the trees are protected from the desert winds and irrigated by channels that carry water from the rivers of the interior. The citrus gardens of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) are planted in a pattern of concentric squares, with channels between the rows and the trees spaced to allow the sun to reach the fruit on every side.

The orange of Khalisar is a sweet variety that ripens in the winter months, providing fresh fruit in the season when the markets of the north have little else to offer. The oranges are exported to Aethelar, where they are sold at prices that make them a luxury of the wealthy households. The peels of the orange are candied and used in the baking of the elaborate confections that are served at the tables of the rich, and the blossoms of the orange tree are distilled into a fragrant water that is used in perfumery and in the preparation of the finest pastries.

The lemon and the lime are grown for their juice, which is used in the cooking of the Sahrani coast and in the preparation of the spiced dishes that are the hallmark of the region. The juice of the lemon is also used as a preservative, and the ships that carry the trade of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) are provisioned with lemons that prevent the scurvy that afflicts crews who subsist on salted provisions for months at a time.

## The Fig and the Date of the Desert Margins

The fig is the fruit of the transition zone between the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), planted in the courtyards and gardens where its roots can reach the moisture that lies beneath the dry surface. The fig tree produces two crops each year, the first in early summer and the second in autumn, and the fruit that is not eaten fresh is dried for storage and for trade. The dried figs of the Sahrani interior are exported to Aethelar, where they are eaten as a sweetmeat or used in the preparation of the rich fruit cakes that are a feature of the northern winter festivals.

The date palm is the most important fruit tree of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), its presence marking the location of the oases that sustain the trade routes across the desert. The date palm requires abundant water and intense heat, conditions that are found only in the oases and the river valleys of the interior. The fruit of the palm is harvested in the late summer, when the dates have turned brown and wrinkled and the sugar content is at its highest.

The dates of the Kharesh are a staple food of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides who lead the caravans across the desert. The dates are eaten fresh during the harvest season and dried for the rest of the year, providing a concentrated source of energy that sustains the travellers through the long crossings of the desert. The trade in dates is a minor but significant part of the Sahrani economy, and the finest dates are exported to the markets of Aethelar and Morvind, where they are sold at prices that reflect the difficulty of their production and transport.

## The Berries of the Northern Wilds

The berries that grow in the wilds of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the uplands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are not cultivated in orchards but gathered from the wild, and their harvest is an important part of the diet of the northern peoples. The cloudberry, the bilberry, and the lingonberry grow on the open slopes above the treeline, where they survive the harsh conditions of the alpine zone and produce their fruit in the brief intensity of the northern summer.

The gathering of berries is the work of the women and children of the northern settlements, who travel to the upland pastures in the late summer with baskets and buckets to harvest the fruit that will sustain the household through the winter. The berries are preserved as jams and jellies, or stored in barrels of water that keep them fresh for months. The juice of the berries is fermented into a wine that is consumed in the feasts of the northern strongholds, and the dried berries are added to the porridges and the breads that are the staple food of the cold season.

The berry harvest of the north is not regulated by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) or the authorities of the settlements, for the berries grow on land that is owned by no one and the right to gather them is a customary freedom that the northern peoples regard as a birthright. The berries of the north are a gift of the land that supplements the diet of the settlements and provides a source of sweetness and variety in the monotony of the winter fare.
