## The Fungal Kingdom and the Peoples of the World

The fungi of the known world are a kingdom of life that is distinct from the plants and the animals, and the relationships between the fungi and the sapient peoples are various and complex. The fungi are gathered from the wild and cultivated in the dark places of the settlements, and their uses range from the nourishment of the body to the healing of the sick and the alteration of the mind. The knowledge of the fungi is important for the survival of many peoples, and the traditions of the fungal harvest are passed from generation to generation.

The fungi that are consumed by the peoples of the three continents are divided into two categories: the cultivated fungi that are grown in the beds and the cellars of the settlements, and the wild fungi that are gathered from the forests and the fields. The cultivated fungi are reliable and predictable, while the wild fungi are varied and sometimes dangerous, and the knowledge of the difference between the edible and the poisonous species is a matter of life and death for the gatherers.

## The Cultivated Mushrooms of the Dwarven Halls

The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) are the most accomplished cultivators of fungi in the known world, and the fungi that are grown in the underground halls of the dwarven holds are the foundation of the diet of the dwarven people. The fungi of the dwarven halls are cultivated in the warm and humid passages where the geothermal heat of the earth maintains a constant temperature that is ideal for the growth of the fungal mycelium.

The principal cultivated fungus of the dwarven halls is the stone fungus, a large and meaty species that grows in the beds of composted plant material that the dwarves prepare in the passages. The stone fungus has a texture that resembles the meat of the pig, and the flavour is earthy and rich. The fungus is harvested at the stage when the caps have opened but before the spores have been released, and the harvested fungi are consumed fresh or dried for storage.

The second fungus of the dwarven halls is the glow fungus, a species that produces a faint bioluminescence that illuminates the passages with a pale green light. The glow fungus is not eaten, for the flavour is bitter and the texture is tough, but the fungus is cultivated for the light that it provides and for the medicinal properties that the healers of the dwarven holds attribute to it. The glow fungus is dried and ground into a powder that is used in the treatment of the wounds that are slow to heal.

## The Mushroom Forests of the Aethelar Heartwood

The forests of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) are rich with wild fungi that appear in the autumn after the rains have soaked the soil and the temperatures have begun to cool. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the Heartwood are the most knowledgeable gatherers of wild fungi in the known world, and the elven gatherers know the locations and the seasons of hundreds of species that the human communities do not recognise.

The most valued of the Heartwood fungi is the crimson cap, a large and striking fungus that appears in the weeks after the autumn rains beneath the oak and the beech trees of the old forest. The crimson cap has a cap of bright red that is dotted with white spots, and the flesh beneath the cap is white and firm. The fungus is edible and valued for its flavour, which is described by the elven cooks as nutty and delicate, and the crimson cap is used in the preparation of the elaborate dishes that are served in the feasts of the elven communities.

The second of the Heartwood fungi is the wood ear, a fungus that grows on the dead wood of the fallen trees and the rotting stumps of the forest. The wood ear is a jelly fungus that swells when it absorbs water, and the texture of the cooked fungus is crisp and chewy. The wood ear is used in the soups and the stews of the elven and the human communities that live near the forest, and the fungus is valued for the texture that it adds to the dishes of the autumn.

## The Truffles of the Vintners Vale

The truffles that grow in the soils of the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) are among the most expensive foods in the known world, valued by the cooks of the wealthy households for the intense flavour that the truffle imparts to the dishes in which it is used. The truffle is a fungus that grows underground in association with the roots of the oak and the hazel trees of the vale, and the truffle is gathered by the trained pigs and dogs that detect the scent of the ripe truffle beneath the soil.

The truffle season of the Vintners Vale runs from the autumn to the winter, and the gatherers of the truffles must compete with the wild boars and the other animals that also seek the truffles for their food. The truffles are harvested by the licensed gatherers who are granted the rights to the truffle grounds by the owners of the land, and the trade in truffles is regulated by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the merchants who deal in the luxury foods.

The truffle of the Vintners Vale is a black truffle that is rough on the outside and dark within, and the flavour of the truffle is earthy and pungent. The truffle is used in the preparation of the sauces and the stuffings that accompany the roasted meats and the pasta dishes of the wealthy households, and the price of the truffle in the markets of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) is measured in silver pieces per ounce.

## The Fungi of the Sahrani Desert

The [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) desert and its margins support a surprising diversity of fungi that have adapted to the extreme conditions of the arid environment. The desert fungi appear only after the rare rains that fall in the desert, and the life cycle of these fungi is compressed into the brief period when the moisture is available.

The desert puffball is the most common of the Sahrani fungi, appearing in the sandy soils of the desert after the winter rains. The puffball is a round and white fungus that grows to the size of a fist, and the flesh of the young puffball is white and firm. The puffball is edible when it is young, and the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) gather the puffballs in the season of the rains and cook them in the stews that accompany the travel rations of the desert.

The second of the desert fungi is the sand cup, a fungus that grows in the gravel soils of the rocky uplands of the interior. The sand cup is a cup-shaped fungus that is grey on the outside and black within, and the fungus is not edible, for the flesh is tough and the flavour is bitter. The sand cup is used by the healers of the Kharesh Tribes in the preparation of the poultices that are applied to the wounds and the infections that are the common ailments of the desert life.

## The Fungi of the Morvind Highlands

The highlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) are too cold for the cultivation of the fungi that grow in the temperate regions, but the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) support a distinctive community of fungi that have adapted to the harsh conditions of the alpine zone. The fungi of the uplands appear in the brief summer, when the snow has retreated and the ground is warm enough for the growth of the fungal mycelium.

The snow fungus is the most characteristic fungus of the Morvind highlands, appearing at the edge of the melting snow in the weeks after the spring thaw. The snow fungus is a small and white fungus that grows in clusters on the ground, and the flesh of the fungus is crisp and watery. The snow fungus is edible, and the human and the dwarven communities of the region gather the fungus in the spring and cook it in the soups and the stews that sustain the households through the last weeks of winter.

## The Poisonous Fungi and the Knowledge of the Gatherers

The knowledge of the difference between the edible and the poisonous fungi is a matter of survival for the peoples who gather the wild fungi. The poisonous fungi of the known world include species that cause illness and species that cause death, and the gatherers of the fungi must be able to identify the dangerous species with certainty before they harvest the wild fungi for the table.

The death cap of the Aethelar forests is the most dangerous of the poisonous fungi, a species that resembles the edible fungi of the forests and that causes a slow and painful death when it is consumed. The death cap is white and unremarkable in appearance, and the inexperienced gatherer may mistake the death cap for the edible white mushroom that grows in the same habitats. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) teach their children to recognise the death cap from the earliest age, and the elven communities have no deaths from the consumption of the poisonous fungi.

The regulation of the fungal trade is a matter of public health in the cities of the three continents, and the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the merchants who deal in the wild fungi are required to certify that the fungi that are sold in the markets are safe for consumption. The officers of the guilds inspect the fungi that are offered for sale, and the fungi that are not identified with certainty are destroyed to protect the public from the danger of poisoning.
