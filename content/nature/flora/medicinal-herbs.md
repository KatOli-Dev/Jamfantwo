## The Healing Arts and the Plants That Serve Them

The knowledge of medicinal herbs is among the most valued of all forms of learning in the known world, for the healers of the three continents rely upon the properties of plants to treat the ailments and the injuries that afflict the sapient peoples. The study and the practice of herbal medicine is pursued by the healers of every community, from the [Reading Cloisters](/content/religion/cloisters-of-reading) where the scribes compile the herbals and the formularies, to the villages of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) where the knowledge of the plants of the desert is passed from generation to generation.

The plants that are used in the healing arts are gathered from the wild or cultivated in the gardens of the healers, and the preparation of medicines from these plants is a skilled trade that requires knowledge of the methods of drying, infusing, distilling, and compounding that transform the raw plant material into the remedies that the healers administer to their patients. The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the apothecaries and the physicians regulate the practice of herbal medicine in the cities, setting the standards for the quality of the preparations and the competence of the practitioners.

## Feverfew and the Treatment of Fevers

Feverfew is among the most widely used medicinal herbs in the known world, its leaves being used in the treatment of the fevers that accompany the seasonal illnesses that afflict the populations of the cities and the settlements. The plant grows wild in the meadows and the waste places of Aethelar, and it is cultivated in the gardens of the healers for the reliability of the supply that is required in the fever season.

The leaves of the feverfew are harvested in the early summer, when the plant is in full leaf and the medicinal compounds are at their highest concentration. The leaves are dried in the shade and stored in sealed pots that preserve their properties for up to a year. The remedy is prepared by infusing the dried leaves in hot water and straining the infusion before it is administered to the patient. The fever that results from the seasonal illnesses usually breaks within a day of the administration of the feverfew infusion, and the patient is left weakened but alive.

The feverfew of the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) margins is considered to be of superior quality, and the healers of the [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) prefer the Heartwood feverfew for their preparations. The plant is also used in the treatment of the headaches and the joint pains that are the common complaints of the elderly, and the regular consumption of feverfew tea is recommended by the healers for the prevention of the migraines that afflict some individuals.

## Willow Bark and the Relief of Pain

The bark of the willow tree is the most widely used remedy for the relief of pain in the known world, its properties being known to the healers of every region and every tradition. The willow grows in the wet soils of the river banks and the marsh edges of Aethelar and Sahrana, and the bark is harvested in the spring when the sap is rising and the medicinal compounds are at their highest concentration.

The outer bark of the willow is scraped from the branches and the trunk, and the inner bark is stripped in long pieces that are dried in the sun. The dried bark is ground to a powder that is mixed with water or wine and administered to the patient for the relief of the pain of injuries, the inflammation of the joints, and the headaches that disrupt the daily life of the sufferer. The remedy is not a cure but a palliation that allows the patient to rest and to recover the strength that is needed for the healing of the underlying condition.

The willow bark of the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) is considered to be the finest available, and the apothecaries of the coastal cities pay premium prices for the bark that is harvested from the willows that grow in the wet soils of the marsh. The trade in willow bark is a minor but steady component of the commerce of the region, and the harvest of the bark provides an income for the marsh dwellers who gather it in the spring.

## Nettle and the Purification of the Blood

The stinging nettle is a plant that is considered both a weed and a medicine, its leaves being used in the treatment of the skin complaints and the joint pains that are the result of the accumulation of impurities in the blood. The nettle grows in the waste places and the disturbed soils of every region of Aethelar, and the plant is gathered by the healers in the spring when the leaves are young and tender.

The nettle is prepared as an infusion that is drunk as a tea, or the fresh leaves are applied directly to the skin in the treatment of the rheumatic pains that afflict the elderly. The sting of the nettle is neutralised by the drying or the cooking of the leaves, and the resulting preparation is safe for consumption or application. The spring tonic of nettle tea is a tradition in many of the northern settlements, and the regular consumption of the tea is believed by the healers to purify the blood and to restore the vitality that is lost during the winter months.

## Yarrow and the Wound Healing of the Battlefield

Yarrow is the most important of the wound-healing herbs, its leaves being used in the treatment of the cuts and the abrasions that are the common injuries of the working life and the battlefield. The plant grows in the meadows and the pastures of Aethelar, and it is cultivated in the gardens of the healers for the supply that is required in the times of conflict and the periods of intense labour.

The leaves of the yarrow are harvested in the summer, when the plant is in flower and the medicinal compounds are at their highest concentration. The leaves are dried and ground to a powder that is applied directly to the wound, or the fresh leaves are crushed and pressed onto the injury to staunch the bleeding and to prevent the infection that would otherwise complicate the healing. The healers of the armies of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) carry yarrow in their satchels as a standard part of their equipment, and the use of yarrow in the treatment of wounds is taught to every soldier as a basic skill of self-care.

## The Poppy of the Sahrani Gardens

The poppy that is cultivated in the gardens of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the oases of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) produces a sap that is the most powerful painkiller known to the healers of the three continents. The sap is collected by cutting the unripe seed pods of the poppy in the early morning, when the sap flows most freely, and the collected sap is dried into a dark resin that is stored in sealed containers.

The resin of the poppy is administered in tiny quantities, for the effect of the resin is powerful and the difference between a healing dose and a lethal dose is small. The healers who use the poppy resin are trained in its administration, and the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the apothecaries regulate the trade in the resin with the strictness that the danger of the substance demands. The resin is used in the treatment of the most severe pain, the pain of the major injuries and the terminal illnesses that the other remedies cannot relieve.

The cultivation of the poppy in the gardens of the Green Crescent is regulated by the authorities of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) and the [Amurra Republic](/content/government/national/amurra-republic), and the trade in the resin is restricted to the apothecaries who hold the licences that are granted by the guilds. The misuse of the poppy resin is a problem that the authorities of the cities are aware of, and the penalties for the possession of the resin without a licence are among the most severe in the legal codes of the Sahrani states.

## The Herbs of the Morvind Highlands

The cold climate of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) produces medicinal herbs that are not found elsewhere in the known world, the plants of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) having adapted to the harsh conditions of the alpine zone by developing compounds that are of value to the healers of the northern settlements.

The mountain arnica of the high pastures is used in the treatment of the bruises and the sprains that are the common injuries of the dwarven miners and the human labourers of the region. The flowers of the arnica are harvested in the brief summer and steeped in oil that is applied to the injured area, reducing the swelling and the discolouration that accompany the injury. The preparation must not be applied to broken skin, for the arnica is toxic when absorbed through wounds, and the healers of the north are careful in their use of this potent remedy.

The angelica that grows in the sheltered valleys of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) is used in the treatment of the chest complaints that are the common illnesses of the cold regions. The root of the angelica is harvested in the autumn of the second year of the plant's life, and the root is dried and powdered for use in the preparation of infusions that ease the cough and clear the congestion of the lungs. The angelica of the Valdmark is exported to the markets of Aethelar, where it is valued by the apothecaries of the northern cities.

## The Herb Gardens of the Cloisters

The [Reading Cloisters](/content/religion/cloisters-of-reading) maintain the most extensive gardens of medicinal herbs in the known world, the gardens being the source of the remedies that the healers of the cloisters prepare for the care of the sick and the injured who come to the cloisters for treatment. The gardens are laid out in orderly beds that contain the plants of the herbal tradition, arranged according to the purposes that the plants serve and the seasons in which they are harvested.

The cloister gardens are also the centres of the study of herbal medicine, and the scribes of the cloisters compile the herbals and the formularies that record the knowledge of the healing properties of the plants and the methods of preparing the remedies. The herbals of the Reading Cloisters are the most authoritative texts on the subject of medicinal herbs in the known world, and the copies that are made by the scribes are distributed to the healers of the cities and the towns.
