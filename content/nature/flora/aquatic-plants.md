## The Plants of the Waters and the Wetlands

The aquatic plants that grow in the rivers, the lakes, and the coastal waters of the known world are a diverse and often overlooked component of the natural environment, providing food and habitat for the animals of the waterways and serving the needs of the sapient peoples who live along the shores. The plants of the water are adapted to the conditions of the submerged and the waterlogged environment, and the forms that the aquatic plants take are various and ingenious.

The aquatic plants are divided into the categories of the emergent plants that grow with the roots in the water and the leaves above the surface, the floating plants that drift on the surface of the water, and the submerged plants that grow entirely beneath the surface. Each of these categories includes the species that are used by the peoples of the three continents for food, for medicine, and for the materials that the plants provide.

## The Reeds of the Greywash Reed Marshes

The [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) are the largest wetland in the known world, and the reeds that grow in the marshes are among the most important of the aquatic plants for the economy of the region. The common reed of the Greywash is a tall grass that can grow to a height of twelve feet or more, and the stems of the reed are used for the thatching of the roofs of the buildings, for the making of the mats and the baskets, and for the production of the paper that the scribes of the [Reading Cloisters](/content/religion/cloisters-of-reading) use in the production of the manuscripts.

The harvest of the reeds is the principal industry of the marsh-dwelling communities, and the reed cutters work in the winter months when the water is low and the reeds have died back for the season. The cut reeds are tied into bundles that are carried to the settlements on the flat-bottomed boats that navigate the shallow channels of the marsh. The bundles of the reeds are stacked in the yards of the merchants and the thatchers, who purchase the reeds for the use in the construction and the manufacturing industries.

The reed that is not harvested for the use of the human communities provides the habitat for the birds and the animals of the marsh, and the dense stands of the reeds are the nesting sites of the waterfowl and the hiding places of the fish and the amphibians that inhabit the waters of the marsh.

## The Water Lilies of the Lakes and the Ponds

The water lilies that grow in the lakes and the ponds of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are among the most beautiful of the aquatic plants, their large and fragrant flowers floating on the surface of the water and their broad leaves providing shade for the fish that gather beneath them. The water lilies are not cultivated but are gathered from the wild in the summer months when the flowers are open and the leaves are fully expanded.

The root of the water lily is a thick and fleshy rhizome that grows in the mud at the bottom of the pond, and the rhizome is harvested by the gatherers who wade into the water and pull the rhizome from the mud. The rhizome of the water lily is edible, and the rhizome is sliced and dried for the use in the soups and the stews of the households of the lake districts.

The flowers of the water lily are used in the preparation of the infusions that are drunk for the calming of the nerves and the relief of the anxiety that afflicts the people of the cities. The petals of the water lily are dried and added to the potpourris that are placed in the rooms of the houses to provide a pleasant scent.

## The Cattails of the Marshes and the Ditches

The cattail is the most useful of the aquatic plants for the peoples of the three continents, and the cattail grows in the marshes and the ditches of every region of the known world. The cattail is a tall and cylindrical plant that produces a distinctive brown head of the flowers that is the most recognizable feature of the plant.

The young shoots of the cattail are harvested in the spring, when the shoots are tender and the flavour is mild. The shoots are eaten fresh in the salads or cooked in the soups and the stews, and the shoots are a welcome addition to the diet of the spring when the fresh vegetables are still scarce.

The pollen of the cattail is harvested in the summer, when the heads of the flowers are covered in the yellow powder. The pollen is collected by shaking the heads into a bag, and the pollen is used as a flour that is mixed with the grain flour for the baking of the bread and the cakes. The pollen is rich in the protein and the nutrients, and the bread that is made with the addition of the cattail pollen is a nutritious and sustaining food.

The stems of the cattail are used for the making of the mats and the baskets, and the down of the mature heads of the cattail is used as a stuffing for the pillows and the mattresses. The cattail provides a remarkable range of the products from a single plant, and the cattail is valued by the peoples of every region where the plant grows.

## The Seaweed of the Coastal Waters

The seaweed that grows in the coastal waters of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) is a source of the food and the materials for the peoples of the coastal settlements. The seaweed is gathered from the rocks of the shore and from the beds of the seaweed that grow in the shallow waters of the coastal shelf.

The edible seaweed of the coastal waters is a species of the red algae that grows on the rocks of the lower shore, and the seaweed is harvested in the spring and the summer by the shore dwellers who gather the seaweed at the low tide. The seaweed is dried in the sun and stored for the use in the winter months, and the dried seaweed is used in the preparation of the soups and the stews that are the common meals of the coastal households.

The species of the brown seaweed that grows in the deeper waters of the coast is used in the production of the ash that is used in the making of the soap and the glass. The seaweed is harvested by the boats that drag the rakes across the beds of the seaweed, and the harvested seaweed is burned in the kilns that produce the ash that is sold to the soap makers and the glassblowers of the cities.

## The Rice of the Flooded Fields

The rice that is cultivated in the flooded fields of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip) is not a wild aquatic plant but a domesticated species that has been adapted to the conditions of the flooded cultivation. The rice is the most important of the aquatic crops, and the cultivation of the rice in the flooded fields is the foundation of the diet and the economy of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) coast.

The fields that are used for the cultivation of the rice are flooded by the channels that carry the water from the rivers of the interior, and the water in the fields is maintained at a depth of several inches through the growing season. The rice plants grow through the water, and the heads of the grain appear above the surface in the late summer. The water is drained from the fields before the harvest, and the grain is harvested by the reapers who cut the heads of the rice and carry them to the threshing floor.

The cultivation of the rice in the flooded fields is the most productive form of the agriculture in the known world, and the yields of the rice from the fields of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) are the highest of any grain crop.

## The Water Chestnut of the Lakes of Aethelar

The water chestnut is a plant that grows in the lakes and the slow-moving rivers of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), producing a nut that is valued for the flavour and the texture. The water chestnut is an aquatic plant that floats on the surface of the water, and the leaves of the plant are arranged in a rosette that floats on the surface.

The nut of the water chestnut is harvested in the autumn, when the nuts have fallen from the plant and are floating on the surface of the water. The nuts are collected by the boats that sweep the surface of the lake, and the collected nuts are dried and stored for the use in the winter months. The water chestnut is eaten fresh, boiled, or roasted, and the flavour of the water chestnut is sweet and nutty.

## The Management of the Aquatic Plant Resources

The management of the aquatic plant resources is a matter of the concern for the communities that depend upon the plants for the thatching, the food, and the materials. The reeds of the [Greywash Reed Marshes](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/reed-marshes-of-the-greywash) are managed by the marsh-dwelling communities who regulate the harvest of the reeds to ensure that the reed beds are not depleted and that the wildlife of the marsh is not harmed.

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the thatchers and the basket makers set the standards for the quality of the reeds that are harvested from the marshes, and the guilds enforce the regulations that limit the harvest of the reeds in the areas where the reeds are needed for the protection of the marsh habitat.
