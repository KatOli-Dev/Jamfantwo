## The Flora of the Arid Lands

The deserts of the known world, the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) and the smaller arid regions of the southern continent, support a community of plants that have adapted to the extreme conditions of the dry environment. The plants of the desert survive through strategies that conserve water, tolerate heat, and exploit the rare rainfall that occurs in the unpredictable seasons of the desert year. The knowledge of these plants is fundamental to the survival of the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) and the other peoples who inhabit the desert margins.

The adaptations of the desert plants are various and ingenious. Some species store water in their tissues, swelling after the rains and shrinking as the water is consumed through the dry months. Other species complete their life cycles in the brief period after the rains, germinating, flowering, and setting seed within weeks before the return of the dry conditions. The deep root systems of the desert perennials reach the moisture that lies beneath the surface, and the leaves of these plants are reduced to spines or scales that minimise the loss of water through transpiration.

## The Cacti of the Kharesh Interior

The cacti of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) interior are the most conspicuous plants of the desert landscape, their fleshy stems and spiny surfaces defining the character of the arid regions that they inhabit. The cacti of the Kharesh are of many species, ranging from the small and spherical barrel cacti that cluster in the gravel plains to the tall and branching column cacti that stand like sentinels along the dry watercourses.

The barrel cactus is the most important of the Kharesh cacti for the survival of the travellers who cross the desert. The barrel cactus stores water in its fleshy stem, and the traveller who knows how to extract the water can obtain a supply that sustains life for several days. The technique for extracting the water is to cut the top from the barrel cactus and to crush the flesh inside, collecting the liquid that flows from the crushed tissue. The water that is obtained from the barrel cactus is slightly bitter but safe to drink.

The column cactus of the Kharesh produces a fruit that is called the desert fig by the [salawa](/content/species/sapient/salawa) guides who gather it in the summer season. The fruit of the column cactus is red and spiny on the outside, but the flesh inside is sweet and juicy, and the fruit is eaten fresh or dried for storage. The seeds of the column cactus are ground into a flour that is used in the preparation of the flatbreads that the desert peoples eat with their meals.

## The Acacias of the Dry Watercourses

The acacia trees that grow along the dry watercourses of the Kharesh are the largest plants of the desert, their deep root systems reaching the underground water that sustains them through the driest years. The acacia is a thorny tree with a spreading crown that provides shade for the travellers and the animals that cross the desert, and the acacia groves that mark the lines of the dry rivers are the sites of the camps and the resting places on the caravan routes.

The wood of the acacia is hard and dense, and the wood is used by the [Kharesh Tribes](/content/government/national/kharesh-tribes) for the construction of the frames of the tents and the handles of the tools that the desert peoples use. The wood is also used as fuel for the fires that the travellers build in the desert, and the smoke that the acacia wood produces is aromatic and pleasant, keeping the insects away from the camp.

The acacia produces a resin that is called gum arabic in the trade of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) merchants, and the resin is used in the preparation of the inks and the paints that the scribes of the [Reading Cloisters](/content/religion/cloisters-of-reading) use in the production of the manuscripts. The resin is harvested by tapping the trunk of the acacia in the dry season, and the dried resin is collected and packed into sacks for transport to the markets of the coast.

## The Saltbush of the Alkaline Plains

The saltbush that grows in the alkaline plains of the Kharesh margins is a plant that has adapted to the soils that are too salty for the other species of the desert. The saltbush accumulates the salt from the soil in the leaves, and the leaves are shed when the salt concentration becomes too high for the plant to tolerate. The salt that accumulates in the leaves is harvested by the desert peoples, who use the salt in the preservation of the meat and the fish that are the staple provisions of the caravan trade.

The saltbush is also a source of fodder for the camels and the goats that the desert peoples keep, for the leaves of the saltbush are nutritious despite the salt that they contain. The animals that feed on the saltbush must have access to fresh water to balance the salt in their diet, and the desert peoples manage the grazing of their herds to ensure that the animals have access to the water sources that sustain them.

## The Oasis Plants of the Desert Settlements

The oases that punctuate the Kharesh are the sites of the most intensive plant cultivation in the desert, and the plants that grow in the oases are the foundation of the settled life of the desert communities. The date palm is the most important of the oasis plants, providing food, shade, and building material for the people of the oasis. The date palm requires abundant water and intense heat, and the palms that grow in the oases are among the most productive of the fruit trees in the known world.

The vegetables that are grown in the gardens of the oases include the okra, the eggplant, and the melon, all of which tolerate the heat of the desert and produce crops in the warm season. The gardens of the oases are irrigated by channels that carry water from the springs and the wells that sustain the oasis, and the water is allocated among the gardeners according to the customary rules that the desert communities have developed over the centuries.

## The Plants of the Sahrani Coastal Dunes

The coastal dunes of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip) are the habitat of a distinctive community of plants that have adapted to the conditions of the shifting sands and the salt spray of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). The plants of the dunes stabilise the sand with their root systems, preventing the erosion that would otherwise destroy the coastal landscape, and the dunes that are covered with vegetation are the most stable and the most productive of the coastal habitats.

The sea holly is the most characteristic plant of the coastal dunes, a spiny plant with blue-grey leaves that are covered in a waxy coating that reduces the loss of water through transpiration. The sea holly produces a root that is valued by the healers of the coastal settlements for the treatment of the fevers and the infections that afflict the coastal population.

The beach grass that grows in the dunes is a plant that spreads through the sand by underground stems, and the dense network of stems and roots holds the sand in place against the wind and the waves. The beach grass is not used for food or for medicine, but the plant is valued by the coastal communities for the role that it plays in the maintenance of the dunes, and the removal of the beach grass from the dunes is prohibited by the customs that the coastal settlements observe.

## The Plants of the Sunfire Steppe

The [Sunfire Steppe](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sunfire-steppe) is a region of the eastern Sahrana where the climate is intermediate between the desert and the savanna, and the plants of the steppe are adapted to the seasonal rainfall that is more abundant than the rainfall of the desert interior but less reliable than the rainfall of the coastal strip.

The grasses of the [Sunfire Steppe](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sunfire-steppe) are the dominant plants of the region, and the species that grow in the steppe are the same species that grow in the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), adapted to the warmer conditions of the southern continent. The grasses of the steppe support the herds of the antelope and the other grazing animals that are the prey of the predators of the region, and the grasses are also the principal food of the herds of the domesticated animals that the steppe peoples keep.

The acacia trees of the Sunfire Steppe are the same species as the acacias of the Kharesh, but the trees of the steppe are larger and more numerous than the trees of the desert, for the rainfall of the steppe is sufficient to support the growth of the trees in the regions where the soil is deep enough to retain the moisture.
