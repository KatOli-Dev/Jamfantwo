## Wheat and the Breadbasket of Aethelar

Wheat is the most widely cultivated grain in the known world and the foundation of the diet of the majority of its peoples. The wheat grown in the river valleys of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), particularly the fields of the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the terraced slopes of the central highlands, is of a quality that commands a premium in the markets of the coastal cities. The two principal varieties cultivated are common wheat, which is the staple of everyday bread, and durum wheat, a harder variety grown in the warmer southern districts of the continent from which the finest pasta flour is milled.

The cultivation of wheat follows a rhythm that has changed little over centuries. The fields are ploughed in autumn, the seed broadcast by hand, and the young shoots emerge before the onset of winter. The crop lies dormant through the cold months, then resumes growth in spring when the rains return. The harvest comes in late summer, when the stalks have turned golden and the heads have begun to droop under the weight of the grain. The reapers move through the fields with sickles, cutting the stalks at the base and binding them into sheaves that are left to dry before the threshing begins. The grain is separated from the chaff by flailing or by the trampling of oxen, and the winnowed grain is carried to the granaries and the mills.

The regions of Aethelar that produce the greatest volumes of wheat are the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) of Sahrana and the broad river valleys of eastern Aethelar, where the soils are deep and the rainfall is reliable. The wheat of the Aethelar Heartwood region is less abundant but of a quality that bakers prize for its strong gluten and its ability to rise in the oven. The [prices and livelihood](/content/economy/prices-and-livelihood) of the baking guilds are closely tied to the wheat harvest, and the price of bread in the cities is a matter of constant attention for the authorities who regulate the grain trade.

## Barley and the Brewing of the North

Barley is the hardiest of the grain crops capable of sustained cultivation in the cold climates of the northern regions. It is grown throughout [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and in the upland districts of Aethelar where the summers are too short or too cool for wheat to ripen reliably. The barley of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) is a six-row variety that has been selected over generations for its tolerance of cold and its short growing season, which requires only seventy days from planting to harvest.

The principal use of barley in all three continents is the brewing of beer and ale, a beverage consumed by peoples of every station from the dwarven halls of the deep mountains to the fishing villages of the coastal strips. The malting of barley is a skilled trade that involves steeping the grain in water, allowing it to germinate, and then drying it in a kiln to develop the sugars that fermentation will convert into alcohol. The maltsters of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) and the brewmasters of the dwarven holds are renowned throughout the known world for the quality of their product, and the trade in malted barley is a significant component of the agricultural economy of the northern regions.

Barley is also used as a food grain, ground into flour for flatbreads and porridges that are the staple diet of the poorer households of the northern settlements. The flour of barley is darker and heavier than wheat flour, and the bread made from it is dense and filling, sustaining the labourers and the miners who require a diet that provides lasting energy through the long working day. In the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark), barley porridge is a common breakfast dish, served with milk or buttermilk and occasionally sweetened with honey.

## Oats for Horse and Man

Oats are the third grain of the northern regions, grown primarily as feed for horses and other working animals but also consumed by human populations in the cooler climates of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the western coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The oat plant thrives in conditions that are too wet or too acidic for barley or wheat, and it is often grown in the upland pastures and the reclaimed moorlands of the western districts where the rainfall is heavy and the soil is poor.

The grain of the oat is harvested and processed in the same manner as wheat and barley, but the hard husk that encloses the grain requires a more vigorous threshing to separate it from the edible kernel. The husked grain is ground into oatmeal or rolled into flakes that are cooked into porridge or baked into oatcakes that are a staple of the western regions. The horses of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) and the cavalry troops of the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) are fed on oats that give them the energy for the long marches and the hard campaigns that the armies of Aethelar undertake.

The cultivation of oats serves a secondary purpose in the rotation of crops, for the oat plant produces a dense root system that improves the structure of the soil and suppresses the growth of weeds. Farmers who include oats in their rotation find that the following crop of wheat or barley yields more heavily than it would if oats had not preceded it, and the practice of including oats in the rotation is recommended by the agricultural advisors of the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) who study such matters.

## Rye and the Dark Breads of the Uplands

Rye is the grain of the poorest soils and the coldest climates, capable of producing a crop where no other grain will grow. The rye fields of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and the high pastures of the [Crown Range](/content/location/natural/feature/crown-range) are sown in the spring after the snow has melted and are harvested in the short summer before the frost returns. The grain that results is dark and dense, producing a flour that makes a heavy bread with a distinctive sour taste that the people of the north have come to prefer.

The rye bread of the dwarven holds is a dark loaf called khaz-brot that is baked from a mixture of rye flour and coarsely ground barley. The bread is dense and moist, and it keeps for months without spoiling, making it the ideal provision for the long journeys that dwarven merchants and miners undertake. The human communities of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) bake a lighter rye bread that is leavened with a sourdough starter maintained over generations in each household.

Rye is also used in the distillation of spirits, particularly in the highlands of Morvind where the potato has not yet become established. The distilleries of the dwarven holds produce a spirit from fermented rye that is the strongest alcoholic beverage known in the three continents, and the trade in this spirit is one of the minor but profitable exports of the northern kingdoms.

## Rice and the Paddies of the Green Crescent

Rice is the staple grain of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), where it is cultivated in flooded paddies that cover the lower slopes and valley floors of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent). The cultivation of rice is the most labour-intensive of all grain farming, requiring the construction of terraces and irrigation channels, the transplanting of seedlings by hand, and the careful management of water levels throughout the growing season.

The terraced paddies of the Green Crescent are constructed on slopes that would otherwise be unsuitable for cultivation, the level terraces held in place by stone walls that have been built and maintained over generations. Water from the rivers of the interior is diverted into channels that feed the uppermost terraces, and the water flows from one terrace to the next through controlled outlets, depositing silt that replenishes the fertility of the soil. The rice that grows in these paddies is a long-grain variety that cooks into separate, fluffy grains that accompany the spiced dishes of Sahrani cuisine.

Two harvests of rice are possible in the warmer districts of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip), the first in early summer and the second in late autumn. The labour of planting and harvesting the rice is shared among the members of the farming communities, and the rice that is not consumed locally is exported to the markets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), where it is traded for the goods of Aethelar and Morvind.

## Millet and Sorghum of the Dry Margins

Millet and sorghum are the grains of the dry margins where the rainfall is insufficient for wheat or barley and where the cultivation of rice is impossible. These grains are grown in the transition zone between the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), and in the eastern districts of Aethelar where the climate is warmer and the rains are less reliable than in the west.

Millet produces tiny seeds that are harvested by cutting the heads of the plant and threshing them to release the grain. The seeds are ground into a flour that is used to make a thin porridge or a flatbread called kisra that is a staple of the Sahrani interior. Sorghum is a larger plant that grows to the height of a man or more, and its grain is used for the same purposes as millet but with a coarser texture and a nuttier flavour.

The cultivation of these grains requires less water and less labour than the cultivation of wheat or rice, and the plants themselves are more resistant to pests and diseases. The farmers of the transition zone rely upon millet and sorghum as the foundation of their diet and their economy, and the grain that they produce is traded along the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) for the goods of the coast.

## The Granaries and the Regulation of the Grain Trade

The trade in grain is the most regulated sector of the agricultural economy in all three continents, for the price of bread and the availability of flour are matters of public order as well as commerce. The granaries of the cities are maintained by the authorities, who buy grain in the season of plenty and release it in the season of scarcity, moderating the fluctuations of price that would otherwise cause hardship and unrest. The granaries of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) and [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) are among the largest structures in those cities, their capacity measured in thousands of tons of grain stored against the possibility of a poor harvest.

The regulation of the grain trade is overseen by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the millers and the bakers, who set the standards for the quality of flour and the price of bread. The millers buy grain from the farmers and the merchants, grind it at their mills, and sell the flour to the bakers, who bake it into bread that is sold by weight at prices fixed by the guild. The system is designed to ensure a stable supply of bread to the urban population at a price that the labouring classes can afford, and the success of the system depends upon the cooperation of the farmers, the merchants, the millers, and the bakers under the authority of the guilds.

The grain crops of the known world are the foundation upon which the civilisation of the three continents has been built. The wheat and barley of Aethelar, the rice of Sahrana, and the oats and rye of Morvind together sustain a population that numbers in the millions, and the knowledge of their cultivation and processing is the heritage of the farming peoples who have tended the fields since the earliest times.
