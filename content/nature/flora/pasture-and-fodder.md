## The Grasses That Sustain the Herds

The pasture and the fodder plants that sustain the herds of the domesticated and the wild animals of the known world are the foundation of the livestock economy of the three continents. The grasses and the other plants that the animals feed upon grow in the meadows and the pastures of Aethelar, the savannas of Sahrana, and the alpine meadows of Morvind, and the management of these pastures is the principal concern of the herders and the farmers who depend upon the animals for the livelihood.

The pasture plants are of two principal types: the perennial grasses that persist in the pasture from year to year, and the annual legumes that are sown in the fields and harvested for the fodder that feeds the animals through the winter months. The composition of the pasture is determined by the climate and the soil of the region, and the farmers who manage the pastures select the species that are best suited to the conditions of their land.

## The Perennial Grasses of the Great Grass Plains

The [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are the largest area of the natural pasture in the known world, and the grasses that grow on the plains are the perennial species that have adapted to the conditions of the continental climate. The dominant grass of the plains is the spear grass, a tall and robust species that grows to the height of a man and that produces the seed heads that are the principal food of the herds of the [Plains Thunder Boar](/content/species/beasts/plains-thunder-boar) and the other grazing animals of the region.

The spear grass is a warm-season grass that begins its growth in the spring after the last of the frosts and that reaches its peak growth in the summer when the temperatures are high and the rainfall is abundant. The grass cures on the stem in the autumn, and the cured grass provides the forage that sustains the herds through the winter months when the fresh growth is not available.

The second grass of the plains is the buffalo grass, a shorter and more densely growing species that forms the turf that covers the lower and the wetter areas of the plains. The buffalo grass is more tolerant of the grazing than the spear grass, and the pastures that are dominated by the buffalo grass can support a higher density of the animals than the pastures of the spear grass.

## The Legumes of the Improved Pastures

The legumes that are sown in the improved pastures of the Aethelaran settlements are the clovers and the vetches that add the nitrogen to the soil and that provide the protein for the animals that feed upon them. The red clover is the most common of the pasture legumes, a perennial species that grows in the mixture with the grasses and that persists in the pasture for three to four years before it must be resown.

The white clover is a smaller and more persistent species that grows in the lawns and the pastures of the northern settlements. The white clover is not as productive as the red clover, but the white clover persists for many years in the pasture and the white clover is more tolerant of the close grazing by the animals.

The vetch that is grown in the fields of the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) is an annual species that is sown in the autumn and harvested in the spring for the fodder that feeds the animals in the winter months. The vetch is cut and dried into the hay that is stored in the barns, and the vetch hay is the most nutritious of the fodder crops that the farmers grow.

## The Hay Meadows of the River Valleys

The hay meadows that are found in the river valleys of Aethelar are the most productive of the pasture lands, and the hay that is cut from these meadows is the foundation of the winter feeding of the livestock. The meadows are flooded by the rivers in the spring, and the silt that is deposited by the flood water enriches the soil and promotes the growth of the grasses and the legumes.

The hay is cut in the late spring or the early summer, when the grasses have reached the peak of the growth and the nutrient content of the plants is at the highest. The grass is cut with the scythes and left to dry in the field, and the dried hay is turned and stacked before it is carried to the barns for the storage.

The quality of the hay depends upon the timing of the cutting and the care that is taken in the drying and the storage of the hay. The hay that is cut too late in the season is coarse and less nutritious, and the hay that is not properly dried before it is stored will rot and become mouldy.

## The Alpine Pastures of Morvind

The alpine pastures of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) are the summer grazing grounds of the herds of the northern peoples, and the grasses and the herbs that grow in the high meadows provide the forage that sustains the animals through the brief but intense growing season of the alpine zone. The grasses of the alpine pastures are the low-growing and cold-tolerant species that are adapted to the conditions of the high elevation.

The alpine meadows are used for the summer grazing of the [Northern Herds](/content/nature/fauna/northern-herds) of the reindeer and the mountain sheep, and the animals are moved to the higher elevations as the snow retreats and the grasses begin to grow. The herders who accompany the animals spend the summer months in the temporary camps that are established in the high valleys, and the animals graze the alpine pastures until the return of the cold weather in the autumn.

The grass of the alpine pastures is not cut for the hay, for the growing season is too short and the conditions for the drying of the hay are not reliable. The animals that graze the alpine pastures must rely upon the fresh growth of the summer for the feeding, and the animals must be moved to the lower elevations in the winter when the snow covers the high pastures.

## The Fodder Crops of the Northern Farms

The farmers of the northern regions of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) grow the fodder crops that provide the feed for the animals through the long winter months. The turnip is the most common of the fodder crops, and the turnip is sown in the spring and harvested in the autumn for the storage in the cellars and the pits that keep the roots fresh through the winter.

The oat is grown as a fodder crop in the northern regions, and the oat is cut and fed to the animals as the green fodder in the summer and as the dried straw in the winter. The oat is also grown for the grain that is fed to the horses and the other working animals that require the concentrated energy of the grain for the work that they perform.

## The Savanna Grasses of the Sunfire Steppe

The [Sunfire Steppe](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sunfire-steppe) of Sahrana is the grazing ground of the herds of the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) and the other peoples of the region, and the grasses of the steppe are the warm-season species that are adapted to the conditions of the dry and the hot climate. The grasses of the steppe are the same species that grow in the Great Grass Plains of Aethelar, but the grasses of the steppe are shorter and more widely spaced, for the rainfall of the steppe is less abundant than the rainfall of the northern plains.

The grazing of the steppe is managed by the herders who move the animals across the range in the patterns that allow the grasses to recover from the grazing before the animals return. The rotation of the grazing is the standard practice of the steppe herders, and the management of the pastures is the subject of the agreements that the herding communities make with each other.

## The Management of the Pasture and the Fodder

The management of the pasture and the fodder is the principal task of the livestock farmers, and the practices that the farmers use are the product of the generations of the experience. The rotation of the grazing is the most important of the management practices, and the rotation of the animals across the different fields of the farm allows the grasses to recover from the grazing and to regrow before the animals are returned to the field.

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the farmers and the herders regulate the use of the common pastures in the regions where the grazing lands are shared among the members of the community. The allocation of the grazing rights on the common pastures is determined by the councils of the herders, and the rules that govern the use of the common pastures are enforced by the officers of the guilds.
