## The Colours of the Known World

The dye plants of the three continents provide the colours that transform the plain cloth of the weavers into the garments and the tapestries that are the pride of every household and the mark of every station in society. The art of dyeing cloth is one of the most ancient of the crafts, and the knowledge of the plants that yield the colours and the methods of fixing the colours to the cloth is passed from master to apprentice in the workshops of the dyers.

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the dyers are among the most powerful of the merchant guilds, for the value that the dyeing process adds to the cloth is significant and the secrets of the trade are closely guarded. The dyes that are produced from the plants of the known world range from the common browns and yellows that are used in the dyeing of the cloth for the working classes, to the rare and expensive reds and purples that are reserved for the garments of the wealthy and the robes of the sovereigns and the high priests.

## Madder and the Red of the Dyers

The madder plant is the most widely used source of red dye in the known world, its roots containing a pigment that produces a range of colours from the palest pink to the deepest crimson. The madder is cultivated in the fields of the [Vintners Vale](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/vintners-vale) and the river valleys of the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth), where the soil is deep and the rainfall is reliable. The plant is a perennial that produces its roots in the second and the third years of its growth, and the roots are harvested in the autumn of the third year for the best results.

The harvested roots are washed, dried, and ground to a powder that is used in the dye bath. The cloth that is to be dyed is mordanted with alum to fix the colour, and the cloth is immersed in the dye bath for a period that varies from hours to days depending on the depth of the colour that is required. The red that the madder produces is a warm and earthy colour, and the best madder reds are achieved by the dyers who have mastered the art of managing the temperature and the timing of the dye bath.

The madder of the Vintners Vale is considered to be the finest in the known world, and the dyers of [Eldreth](/content/location/settlement/city/eldreth) and the other cities of the coast pay premium prices for the madder that is produced in the region. The trade in madder is a significant component of the agricultural economy of the vale, and the farmers who grow the madder are organised into a cooperative that negotiates with the dyers' guilds on the terms of the trade.

## Woad and the Blue of the Northern Cloth

Woad is the source of the blue dye that is used in the dyeing of the cloth that is produced in the northern settlements of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The woad plant is a member of the cabbage family that grows in the temperate regions of the north, and the leaves of the plant contain the pigment that produces the blue colour that is characteristic of the cloth of the northern peoples.

The woad leaves are harvested in the summer, and the pigment is extracted from the leaves by a process of fermentation that is one of the most closely guarded secrets of the dyers' trade. The fermented leaves are formed into cakes that are dried and stored for the use of the dyers in the winter months when the fresh leaves are not available. The dye from the woad cakes is prepared by grinding the cakes and mixing the powder with water, and the cloth that is dyed in the woad bath takes on a blue colour that ranges from the pale sky blue of the diluted dye to the deep indigo of the concentrated bath.

The woad of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) is exported to the cities of the south, where the dyers use the woad as a base upon which they apply the more expensive dyes of the Sahrani coast. The cloth that is dyed with woad is the most common cloth of the northern settlements, and the blue garments that the northern peoples wear are the mark of their origin and their identity.

## The Indigo of Sahrana

The indigo plant of Sahrana produces a blue dye that is deeper and more intense than the blue of the northern woad, and the indigo of the Sahrani interior is one of the most valuable of the [spices and dyes](/content/economy/spices-and-dyes) that are traded from the southern continent. The indigo plant is cultivated in the irrigated gardens of the [Green Crescent](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/green-crescent) and the oases of the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh), where the climate is warm and the water is abundant.

The indigo is harvested in the summer, and the leaves are processed by a method that involves the fermentation of the leaves in water and the precipitation of the pigment from the fermented liquor. The resulting paste is dried and formed into cakes that are exported to the markets of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) and [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), where the indigo is sold at prices that reflect its quality and its rarity.

The indigo of Sahrana is the finest that can be obtained, and the blue that it produces is the standard against which the blues of the other dyes are measured. The dyers of Aethelar and Morvind prefer the Sahrani indigo for the dyeing of the finest cloths, and the trade in indigo is a significant part of the commerce between the northern and the southern continents.

## Weld and the Yellow of the Meadows

Weld is a plant that grows wild in the meadows and the waste places of Aethelar, and its leaves and its flowers produce a yellow dye that is used in the dyeing of the cloth of the northern settlements. The weld is harvested in the summer when the plant is in flower, and the harvested plant material is dried and stored for the use of the dyers.

The yellow that the weld produces is a clear and bright colour that is used in the dyeing of the cloth that is worn by the young women of the northern settlements and in the production of the tapestries and the embroideries that decorate the houses of the wealthy. The weld is also used in combination with the woad and the madder to produce the green and the orange colours that the dyers require for the patterns that they create.

## The Kermes Insect and the Crimson of the Coast

The kermes insect that lives on the leaves of the kermes oak of the Sahrani foothills produces a red dye that is more intense and more permanent than the red of the northern madder. The dried bodies of the female insects are ground to a powder that yields a crimson colour when mixed with a mordant, and the crimson of the kermes is the standard against which the red dyes of the known world are measured.

The collection of the kermes insects is the work of the women and the children of the villages that surround the oak groves of the interior. The insects are collected in the spring, when the females are full of the eggs that contain the pigment, and the collected insects are dried in the sun and packed into sealed containers for transport to the markets of the coast. The crimson that the kermes produces is used in the dyeing of the robes of the high priests and the garments of the sovereigns, and the cloth that is dyed with the kermes crimson is the most expensive cloth that is produced in the known world.

## The Walnut and the Brown of the Common Cloth

The husk of the walnut produces a brown dye that is used in the dyeing of the cloth that is worn by the working classes of the three continents. The walnut tree grows in the forests of Aethelar and the cultivated gardens of Sahrana, and the husks that surround the nuts are collected in the autumn when the nuts are harvested.

The brown that the walnut husk produces is a dark and serviceable colour that does not show the dirt and the stains of the working life, and the cloth that is dyed with the walnut brown is the most common cloth of the labouring population. The walnut husk is also used in the dyeing of the ropes and the sails that the ships of the coastal cities use, and the brown sails of the fishing fleets are the result of the dyeing of the canvas with the walnut husk.

## The Lichens of the Northern Coast

The lichens that grow on the rocks of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) and the cliffs of the [Glasslight Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/glasslight-coast) produce a purple dye that is used in the dyeing of the cloth of the northern settlements. The lichens are collected by the shore dwellers who scrape the lichens from the rocks with iron tools, and the collected lichens are dried and processed to extract the purple pigment.

The purple of the northern lichens is not as intense as the purple of the murex shellfish of the Sahrani coast, but the northern purple is much cheaper and more accessible, and the cloth that is dyed with the lichen purple is the common cloth of the middle classes of the northern cities. The trade in the lichen dyes is a minor but characteristic part of the economy of the northern coastal settlements.

## The Regulation of the Dye Trade

The trade in the dye plants and the prepared dyes is regulated by the [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the dyers and the merchants who deal in the colours. The guilds set the standards for the quality of the dyes, and the officers of the guilds inspect the dye works and the markets to ensure that the dyes that are sold to the dyers and the weavers meet the standards of purity and concentration that the guilds require.

The secrets of the dyeing process are among the most closely guarded of the craft traditions, and the masters of the dyers' guilds pass the knowledge of the mordants and the dye baths to their apprentices only after years of service and the swearing of oaths of secrecy. The colours that the dyers produce are the mark of their skill and their tradition, and the reputation of a city's dyers is one of the factors that determines the value of the cloth that is produced in that city.
