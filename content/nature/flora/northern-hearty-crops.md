## Agriculture at the Edge of the Cold

The cultivation of crops in the cold northern lands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) is a matter of survival for the peoples who inhabit these regions, and the plants that are grown in the north are the product of centuries of selection for hardiness and for the ability to complete their life cycles in the short growing season that the northern climate permits. The northern crops are fewer in number and lower in yield than the crops of the temperate and the warm regions, but the techniques of the northern farmers are adapted to the conditions that they face, and the harvests that they achieve sustain the population of the northern settlements.

The growing season in the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) lasts no more than three months at the lower elevations, and the season is shorter at the higher elevations where the snow does not melt until the early summer. The northern farmers must choose the crops that can germinate, grow, and produce seed within this narrow window, and the varieties that are cultivated in the north are those that have been selected for their rapid growth and their tolerance of the cold.

## The Barley of the Northern Fields

Barley is the most important grain crop of the northern regions, and the barley of the [Morvind Uplands](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/morvind-uplands) and the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) is the foundation of the diet of the northern peoples. The northern barley is a six-row variety that has been selected over generations for its short growing season, which requires no more than seventy days from planting to harvest, and for its tolerance of the cold and the wet conditions that characterize the northern spring.

The barley is sown in the spring as soon as the ground has thawed enough for the plough to work, and the crop is harvested in the late summer before the frost returns. The grain is ground into flour for the flatbreads and the porridges that are the staple foods of the northern households, and the barley is also malted for the brewing of the ale that is the common beverage of the northern settlements.

The barley straw that remains after the harvest is used as bedding for the animals and as thatching for the roofs of the northern buildings. The straw is also used in the production of the bricks that are used in the construction of the walls of the northern houses, the straw being mixed with clay to add strength and insulation to the bricks.

## The Rye of the Upland Slopes

Rye is the grain of the poorest soils and the coldest exposures in the northern regions, and the rye fields of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) and the upland settlements of Morvind are sown in the spring after the snow has melted. The rye plant is more tolerant of cold and of poor soil than any other grain, and the rye of the north produces a crop in seasons when the barley and the oats would fail.

The rye of the north is ground into a dark flour that is used in the baking of the dense and sour bread that is the staple food of the dwarven and the human communities of the region. The bread of the north is baked in large loaves that are stored in the cellars and the pantries of the northern households, and the bread keeps for months without spoiling, providing a store of food that sustains the households through the winter.

The rye is also used in the distillation of a spirit that is the strongest alcoholic beverage produced in the northern regions. The distilleries of the dwarven holds of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) produce a rye spirit that is consumed in the feasts of the northern peoples and exported to the markets of the south.

## The Potato of the Present Age

The potato was introduced to the known world in recent centuries and has become a staple crop in the northern regions where the cultivation of the grain crops is difficult and uncertain. The potato is a tuber that grows beneath the ground, and the plant produces a crop in the cool and wet conditions that are the characteristic climate of the northern growing season.

The potato is planted in the spring after the ground has thawed, and the tubers are harvested in the autumn when the leaves of the plant have begun to yellow and die. The potato is a productive crop, yielding more food per acre than the grain crops, and the potato has become a significant part of the diet of the northern peoples.

The potato of the [Valdmark](/content/government/national/valdmark) and the northern settlements is a variety that is blue-skinned and yellow-fleshed, a variety that is more resistant to the frost and the diseases that afflict the potato in the cold and the wet conditions of the north. The potato is stored in cellars and in pits dug into the ground, and the stored potatoes provide a supply of fresh food that lasts through the winter months.

## The Root Vegetables of the Northern Gardens

The root vegetables that are grown in the gardens of the northern settlements are the most reliable source of fresh food in the diet of the northern peoples, for the roots store well through the winter and provide the vitamins and the minerals that the diet of the preserved and the dried foods lacks.

The turnip is the most common of the northern root vegetables, sown in the spring after the frost has left the ground and harvested in the autumn before the return of the frost. The turnip is eaten fresh in the summer and stored in the cellars for the winter, and the turnip is also used as a feed for the animals that the northern settlements keep.

The carrot of the north is a variety that is shorter and thicker than the carrot of the south, a variety that has been selected for its ability to grow in the heavy and stony soils of the northern fields. The carrot is eaten fresh in the summer, and the stored carrots provide a source of sweetness in the winter diet that is otherwise lacking in sugar.

The cabbage of the north is the most important of the leafy vegetables, and the cabbage is grown in the gardens of every northern settlement. The cabbage is harvested in the autumn and stored in the cellars, and the cabbage is used in the preparation of the soups and the stews that are the common meals of the northern winter. The cabbage is also fermented into the sauerkraut that is a staple provision of the ships that carry the trade of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) and the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea).

## The Berries of the Northern Wilds

The berries that grow in the wilds of the north are not cultivated in the fields and the gardens, but the berries are gathered by the northern peoples in the autumn and preserved for the winter. The cloudberry is the most valued of the northern berries, a golden fruit that grows in the bogs and the wet meadows of the north. The cloudberry prevents the scurvy that afflicts the peoples who subsist on the preserved and the dried foods of the winter.

The bilberry and the lingonberry grow on the open slopes of the uplands and in the clearings of the forests, and the berries are gathered by the women and the children of the northern settlements in the late summer and the early autumn. The berries are preserved as jams and as jellies, and the berries are also stored in barrels of water that keep the berries fresh for months.

## The Storage and the Preservation of the Northern Crops

The storage and the preservation of the crops are the most important skills of the northern farming communities, for the winter in the north lasts for many months and the fresh food that is available in the summer must be stored to sustain the population through the cold season.

The cellars of the northern settlements are dug into the ground, where the temperature remains above freezing through the winter, and the cellars are used for the storage of the root vegetables, the cabbages, and the barrels of the preserved foods. The grain is stored in the granaries that are built above the ground, and the grain is protected from the damp and the vermin by the careful construction of the granary and the regular inspection of the stored grain.

The [guilds](/content/economy/guilds) of the millers and the bakers regulate the trade in the grain and the flour in the northern settlements, and the guilds maintain the stores of the grain that are released in the season of scarcity to maintain the stability of the prices and the supply of the bread to the urban population. The system of the grain storage has been refined over the centuries, and the northern cities have not suffered the famines that have afflicted the cities of the south in the years of the poor harvests.

## The Trade in the Northern Crops

The trade in the crops of the north is a matter of the exchange of the northern products for the goods of the south. The grain of the north is not of the quality to compete with the grain of the Aethelaran river valleys, and the northern settlements import the wheat and the rice that supplement the diet of the northern population. The export of the northern crops is limited to the barley malt that is used in the brewing of the ale in the southern cities, and the rye spirit that is valued by the connoisseurs of the strong drink.

The trade in the northern crops is conducted through the ports of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) and the settlements of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) coast, and the merchants who deal in the northern crops are the members of the guilds that regulate the trade in the agricultural products.
