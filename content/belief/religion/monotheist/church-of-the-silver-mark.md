## Overview

The Church of the Silver Mark is a mercantile monotheist tradition held chiefly by the trading guilds, the weight-keepers, and the assayers of the cities and the market towns of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). Its central teaching holds that the divine is most clearly known in the honest dealing of one person with another, and that the small mark struck upon a weight of silver or upon a piece of finished metal is a kind of testimony in which the maker acknowledges the maker's work. The faithful call the divine the Truemarker, and they hold that the Truemarker has set the world into the discipline of measure, of contract, and of the careful word.

## Origins among the City Assayers

The church traces its origins to the assayers' guilds of the older market cities, whose members were charged with the verification of the weight and the fineness of the silver and the gold in circulation among the trading houses. The earliest records of the church speak of an assayer called Aldren of the Long Vault, who is held to have first observed that the strike of a guild mark upon a tested weight was an act of small witness to a larger truth, and that the assayers who attended to this witness were attentive to a reality that the casual trader did not see. Whether Aldren was a single master or a succession of senior assayers is left open, and the church is content that the testimony be remembered, whether or not the tester can be precisely named.

The church spread along the trade routes of the continent, and the [Kingdom of Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael) and the [Kingdom of Aldreth](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-aldreth) both permitted its practice within their borders without ever adopting it as a state observance. The trading houses valued the church's discipline for the steadiness it gave the markets, and the church valued the trading houses for the work they gave its faithful. The arrangement has been one of long cooperation without absorption, and neither party has sought to make the other its creature.

## The Truemarker

The doctrine of the church centres upon the figure of the Truemarker, who is held to be both the source of all measure and the patient witness to every honest dealing among the creatures of the world. The faithful are taught that the Truemarker has set all things into relationship with all other things, and that the discipline of measure is the discipline by which the creature acknowledges this setting. A weight of silver tested in the vault and found true is held to be, in its small way, a witness to the Truemarker, and a contract weighed and recorded without deceit is held to be a similar witness in a different register.

The doctrine does not teach that wealth is in itself a good, and the church is careful to mark the difference between the honest dealing of measure and the unchecked pursuit of gain. The masters speak of a discipline of sufficiency, by which the faithful are taught to distinguish between the gain that honours the Truemarker and the gain that diminishes the maker. The teaching is not ascetic, for the church has never counselled poverty, but it is restrained, and the church regards the restraint as one of its most valuable contributions to the wider life of the trading cities.

## The Houses of Account

The houses of the church are the houses of account, the quiet establishments where the trading records are kept and verified and where the discipline of mark and measure is maintained. The houses of account are attached to the larger guilds of the market cities, and they are kept by senior members of the church under the counsel of an elder called the master of account. The master of account is charged with the oversight of the records and the instruction of the juniors, and the office is regarded by the church as one of the more demanding of its lay disciplines.

A member of the church is admitted to the house of account after a probation in the guild workshop, during which the candidate attends the weighings and the verifications and contributes to the maintenance of the records. The house of account is supported by a small levy upon the trading of the guild, and the discipline of the church teaches that this levy is itself a form of testimony, in which the guild acknowledges the discipline that has given it its reputation. The masters caution the junior members against the temptation of the fast profit, which the doctrine holds to be the principal danger of the trading life.

## The Strike of the Mark

The central observance of the church is the strike of the mark, the careful test by which the weight and the fineness of a piece of silver or gold is determined and recorded. The strike is undertaken with a deliberate formality, and the elder of the church who performs it speaks a brief acknowledgment of the discipline as the weight is set upon the scale and the mark is struck. The faithful are taught to attend to this moment with care, and the doctrine holds that the attention paid to the strike is itself a form of prayer.

The strike is performed in two registers, the strike of the weight and the strike of the witness. The strike of the weight is the physical test of the scale and the assay of the metal, and it is undertaken by the guild's master of assay in the workshop. The strike of the witness is the speaking of the acknowledgment by which the strike is offered as testimony to the Truemarker, and it is undertaken by the master of account in the house of account. The two strikes together make the full observance, and the doctrine teaches that neither is complete without the other.

## Rites and Transitions

The church marks the major transitions with rites that join the household and the guild in a single observance. A child of a guild household is presented to the house of account within the first year and given a small token coin struck for the purpose, and the master of account speaks a brief blessing as the token is placed in the child's hand. A novice is admitted to the guild by being given a work at the workshop and a master under whom to serve, and the novice is permitted thereafter to attend the strike of the mark as a witness to the discipline of the older hands.

A marriage between two members of the church is solemnised by the striking of a joint mark upon a chosen weight of silver, which the two are permitted to keep as the bond of their household. Death is marked by the placing of the departed's mark in the records of the guild and the striking of a final account on the day of the funeral, and the masters speak briefly of the departed's keeping before the records are closed. The doctrine teaches that the departed's keeping has not ended but has passed into the common keeping of the guild, and the placing of the mark in the records is held to be the final act of the departed's calling.

## Relation to Other Faiths

The Church of the Silver Mark has long coexisted with the other monotheist traditions of the continent, and it has been particularly close to the [Doctrine of the Open Hand](/content/belief/ideology/doctrine-of-the-open-hand), which shares its concern with the honest dealings of merchants and travellers. The two teachings are not the same, for the Open Hand is a doctrine of hospitality without any claim upon the divine, and the church regards the Truemarker as the source of the discipline of measure, but the keepers of each have been careful to mark the kinship between them. A trader who is of the church may keep the Open Hand in his dealing with strangers, and the two are held to be complementary rather than competing.

The church has been particularly careful in its dealings with the [Faith of the Forged Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/faith-of-the-forged-flame), whose smiths supply much of the guild's trade in finished metal. The two traditions recognise a kinship of workshop, and a guild mark is sometimes struck at the order of a senior smith upon a piece finished in the presence of both disciplines. Among the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) the church has found a sympathetic hearing, for the dwarven smiths and assayers maintain their own traditions of weight and fineness, and the church regards this hearing as a privilege rather than a claim.

## Significance

The Church of the Silver Mark is a working presence in the trading life of Aethelar, and its significance is felt in the markets and the guilds of the great cities more than in the courts of the realm. Its practitioners are patient of small witness and devoted to the long discipline of the mark, and they have given to the trading cities of the continent a steadiness that the masters regard as the chief fruit of their keeping. The church asks of its faithful that they attend to the difference between the honest strike and the deceitful, and it offers in return the long discipline by which the attention is refined and the markets are kept honest. In a world of many faiths and many claims, the Church of the Silver Mark endures as the careful test by which the maker's work is given its small testimony, and the master of account who strikes a true mark upon a tested weight is held, by the faithful, to have spoken a word that will outlast the day's trading.
