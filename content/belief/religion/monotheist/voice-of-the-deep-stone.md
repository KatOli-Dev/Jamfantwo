## Overview

The Voice of the Deep Stone is a contemplative monotheist tradition of the mountain and the cavern, held chiefly by the stone-cutters, the miners, and the patient workers of the tunnels of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the older mining communities of the central highlands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). Its central teaching holds that the divine is most clearly known in the quiet of the deep places of the earth, where the noise of the day cannot reach and the stone keeps a patience that the surface has long forgotten. The faithful call the divine the Deep Voice, and they hold that the Deep Voice has set the world's foundations into the stone that the stone may speak, in its season, to those who have the patience to hear.

## Origins among the Stone-Cutters

The tradition traces its origins to a company of stone-cutters who worked the quarries of the central Aethelaran highlands, where the cutting of a single block required days of patient labour and the setting of the chisel against the grain was a discipline attended to with great care. The cutters taught that the stone held within it a quiet that the noise of the surface could not reach, and that the sound of the chisel against the stone was a kind of answer to a question the cutter did not always know he had asked. Whether the cutters were a single company or a succession of master craftsmen is left open, and the tradition is content that the cutting should be remembered whatever the case may be.

The tradition found a sympathetic hearing among the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind), whose crafts of the deep tunnel have given them a long acquaintance with the patience of the stone. The dwarven master craftsmen did not formally adopt the tradition, for they hold to their own ancient ways of hearth and tunnel, but they acknowledged a kinship with the Deep Voice and permitted the cutting of small listening-chambers in certain of the deeper workings, particularly those that the miners had not used for some years. The arrangement has been one of long cooperation without absorption, and the elders of the tradition regard the dwarven permission as a privilege they have never presumed upon.

## The Quiet of the Stone

The doctrine holds that the divine is most clearly known in the quiet of the deep stone, and that the surface world's noise is a kind of veil that prevents the soul from attending to the patience the stone has kept since the world's foundations. The faithful are taught that the divine does not speak in words but in the small sounds by which the stone yields to the chisel, and that the cutter who has the patience to attend to these sounds will come to perceive, behind the labour of the cutting, a corresponding silence that is more patient than any of the surface's clamour. The teaching is not a denial of the surface's life, for the tradition holds that the surface is a place of necessary labour, but it is a counsel that the soul withdraw, in its season, to the quiet of the deeper places.

The tradition maintains a number of small listening-chambers in the older quarries and in certain deep workings that the miners have set aside, and the elders teach that the maintenance of these chambers is one of the chief labours of the discipline. The chambers are reached by a careful descent and are kept in a state of patient quiet, and the elders counsel the novice that the chamber is to be approached without haste and without much speech. The tradition holds that a chamber entered with noise is a chamber that has been disturbed, and the disturbance cannot be repaired without the patient labour of returning.

## The Cutting and the Listening

The central observance of the tradition is the cutting and the listening, a discipline in which the stone-cutter sets the chisel to the stone and attends, between the cuts, to the small sounds the stone makes as it yields. The cutting is not the chief part of the discipline, for the elders teach that the cutting is the instrument and the listening is the task. A cutter who strikes the stone without attending to its answer is held to have missed the discipline, and the master cutters counsel the novice to attend to the difference between the cutting that listens and the cutting that merely strikes.

A novice is admitted to the discipline after a probation of some seasons in a quarry or a deep working, during which the candidate learns the tools and the methods of the cutting and is instructed in the doctrine of the Deep Voice. The probation is undertaken under a master cutter who is recognised by the elders of the tradition, and the elders counsel the novice that the probation is itself a discipline of patience and that the cutting and the listening cannot be properly undertaken without it. The master cutters are given the title of keeper of the chisel, and they are charged with the oversight of the probations and the instruction of the juniors.

## The Stone-Cutters' Guilds

The tradition is maintained in the stone-cutters' guilds of the older cities, where the cutters meet in the evenings and compare the day's cuttings and the small sounds they have heard. The guilds are supported by a small levy upon the trade of the quarries, and the elders teach that the levy is itself a discipline of common labour that complements the discipline of the cutting. The guilds are not unions in the modern sense, and the tradition has never sought to set its members against the merchants or the traders of the wider world, but the keepers of the chisels regard the guild as a worthy instrument for the maintenance of the discipline.

The guilds maintain the libraries of the tradition, in which the cuttings of the older cutters have been recorded and in which the small sounds the stone has made are preserved for the instruction of the juniors. The libraries are kept at the guildhalls, and the elder cutters counsel the assistants that the libraries are to be approached with the same patience as the listening-chambers themselves. The chronicler notes that the libraries have been among the more useful of the tradition's contributions to the wider world, for the cutters' records have been studied by naturalists of several peoples who have found in them a careful account of the stone that their own observations had not yet produced.

## The Listening-Chambers of the Deep

The tradition maintains a number of listening-chambers in the deeper workings of the older quarries and in certain tunnels of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) that the dwarves have permitted the tradition to share. The chambers are reached by a careful descent and are kept in the dim lamplight of the underground, and the elders teach that the listening is to be undertaken without speech and without unnecessary movement. A novice who has been admitted to the chamber is permitted to remain for a season of weeks or months, and the keepers of the chisels counsel that the time in the chamber is itself the discipline, for the patience of the stone cannot be apprehended in a single visit.

The chambers are not places of worship in the ordinary sense, and the tradition has no formal rite that the chamber requires. The cutter who enters the chamber is permitted to undertake the cutting and the listening in his or her own measure, and the keepers counsel that the cutter should not attempt to bring the surface's expectations into the chamber's quiet. The chronicler notes that the chambers have been among the more quietly maintained of the tradition's establishments, and that the keepers of the chisels regard them as the chief fruit of their discipline.

## Rites and Transitions

The tradition marks the major transitions with rites that join the household and the quarry in a single observance, and the elders counsel against any elaboration that might disturb the patience the rites are meant to honour. A child is presented to the quarry within the first year and is held by the elder cutter against a freshly cut block, and a small chisel is placed in the child's hand as a token of the calling. A novice is admitted to the discipline by being given a probation at the quarry and a keeper of the chisel under whom to learn the cutting, and the novice is permitted thereafter to share in the quiet of the listening-chambers.

A marriage between two members of the tradition is solemnised at the quarry by the cutting of a single block that the two are permitted to keep as the bond of their household. Death is marked by the placing of the departed's chisel upon the records of the guild and the observing of a brief silence at the next day's cutting, and the elder cutters speak briefly of the departed's listening before the records are closed. The tradition teaches that the departed's listening has not ended but has passed into the common quiet of the stone, and the placing of the chisel upon the records is held to be the final act of the departed's calling.

## Relation to Other Faiths

The Voice of the Deep Stone has long coexisted with the other contemplative traditions of the continent, and it has been particularly close to the [Path of the Sunless Veils](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/path-of-the-sunless-veils), whose contemplatives maintain their own discipline of withdrawal. The two traditions are not the same, for the Path is a discipline of bodily retreat in the cloister, while the Voice is a discipline of patient labour in the quarry, but the teachers of each have been careful to mark the kinship between the disciplines. A senior veiler of the Path will sometimes attend a session of the cutting and the listening, and a senior keeper of the chisel will sometimes undertake the long retreat at the cloister of the Veils, and each is welcomed by the other with the courtesy of kindred traditions.

The tradition has been perhaps closest to the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim), whose craftsmen maintain their own ancient ways of the chisel and the tunnel. The dwarves have not formally adopted the tradition, but they honour it in the deeper workings and they counsel their cutters to attend to the small sounds the stone makes as it yields. The chronicler notes that the arrangement has been a valuable one for the tradition and for the dwarves, and that the listening-chambers of the Holds have been among the more gently maintained of the tradition's establishments. Among the [humans](/content/species/sapient/humans) of the central highlands the Voice has been the natural complement to the [Faith of the Forged Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/faith-of-the-forged-flame) of the workshops, and the two traditions have been quietly combined in many a household whose keeper keeps both the forge and the chisel.

## Significance

The Voice of the Deep Stone is a contemplative presence in the religious life of the central highlands and the mountain country, and its significance is felt in the quarries and the listening-chambers of the older workings more than in the courts of the great. Its practitioners are patient of the cutting and devoted to the long discipline of the listening, and they have given to the wider world records of the stone that the naturalists of several peoples have found of uncommon use. The tradition asks of its faithful that they attend to the difference between the cutting that listens and the cutting that merely strikes, and it offers in return the patient quiet by which the stone's silence is heard and the world is set, in its deeper places, into the larger order of the foundations. In a world of many faiths and many claims, the Voice remains the patient attention of those who have learned that the stone has kept a quiet that the surface has long forgotten, and the cutter who strikes the true blow is held, by the elders, to have heard, in the answer, the Deep Voice's corresponding silence.
