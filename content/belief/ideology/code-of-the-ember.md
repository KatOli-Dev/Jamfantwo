## Overview

The Code of the Ember is a teaching of stewardship and endurance that takes as its emblem the small flame kept burning through the long night, neither fed to a blaze nor permitted to die. It is not a religion and makes no claim upon the divine, but a discipline of household, workshop, and watch-tower, taught by the keepers of hearths, forges, and signal fires throughout the lands where winter is long and the loss of heat is the loss of all. The central precept of the code is that the flame is entrusted to the keeper, and that the keeper's honour is measured by the survival of the flame through every watch of the night. The code has been refined over generations into a comprehensive ethic of duty, vigilance, and patient maintenance that governs far more than the tending of the fire.

## Origins among the Northern Watch

The Code of the Ember is generally held to have taken its present form among the hearth keepers of the [The Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), where the long winters of the northern continent test the constancy of those who tend the village fires and the signal towers that connect one settlement to another. The chroniclers of [Karhold](/content/location/settlement/city/karhold) preserve accounts of an older custom, by which the hearth keeper of a village was appointed for a season and held answerable for the survival of the communal flame through the deepest cold. From this practical office the present teaching is held to descend, expanded by the keepers and the elders into the moral code now observed throughout the northern lands and beyond.

As the signal towers of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches) and the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) spread the use of the ember code among the watchmen of the frontier, the teaching travelled southward into the settled kingdoms, and the hearth keepers of [Nordhaven](/content/location/settlement/city/nordhaven), of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim), and of the towns of the [League of the Northern Coast](/content/government/national/league-of-the-northern-coast) took up its precepts as their own. The [Deep Realm of Karhold](/content/government/national/deep-realm-of-karhold), whose entire polity depends upon the survival of its forges and its watch-fires through the dark months, has long regarded the Code of the Ember as the foundational discipline of its public order, and its thanes are sworn to its observance.

## The First Precept: The Flame Is Not Your Own

The first article of the code holds that the flame entrusted to the keeper is not his own, and that the keeper may not lawfully let it die for any cause of his own convenience or pleasure. The precept is taught from childhood by the elders of the northern villages, and the keepers recite it each evening as the watch begins. The chronicler observes that this precept is not a counsel of stoic rigour but a recognition of the fact that the flame serves the whole household, and that the household's need stands above the keeper's wish. A keeper who sleeps through his watch, or who quits his post in anger, is held by the code to have failed the household and to owe amends.

The precept is observed in its plainest form among the hearth keepers of the Uplands, but it extends in its full sense to the keepers of every office in which a trust has been placed. The watchman at the signal tower, the keeper of the granary, the master of the forge, and the tutor of the household children are all reckoned as keepers of an ember, and the code applies to each. The teaching holds that the difference between a small trust and a great one is only the difference of consequence, not the difference of kind, and that the keeper of the smallest ember is bound by the same discipline as the keeper of the greatest.

## The Second Precept: Feed Without Burning

The second article of the code is the art of feeding the flame without letting it rise to a wasteful blaze. The keeper is taught to add fuel in small measure and at regular intervals, and to read the signs of the fire so that he knows when to add and when to forbear. The teaching holds that the flame starved grows feeble and dies, and that the flame overfed leaps up and consumes itself, leaving only ash. The keeper's task is the maintenance of the mean between these two failures, and the mean is held to be a discipline of attention rather than of effort. The chronicler notes that this art, taught by the elders, requires a certain temperament and a long apprenticeship, and that not every hand is suited to the keeping of embers.

This precept has been extended by the masters of the code into a broader teaching of stewardship, by which every entrusted resource is to be maintained in the mean between waste and want. The granary is to be drawn upon so that it neither moulders nor empties, the household purse so that it neither overflows nor fails, and the goodwill of neighbours so that it is neither lavished nor neglected. The teaching holds that the same attention which keeps the ember alight, applied to the affairs of the household and the polity, will preserve them through every extremity. The chronicler observes that this extension has given the Code of the Ember an influence far beyond the hearth, and that the prudent lords of the northern kingdoms are known to govern their households by it.

## The Third Precept: The Watch Is Continuous

The third article of the code is the continuity of the watch. The keeper may not delegate his office without the knowledge of the household, and he may not abandon his post in the belief that another will take it up. The teaching holds that the flame does not know the hour, and that the hour of its need may fall when no hand is near to feed it. The watch is therefore continuous in the sense that the keeper is held to be at his post in spirit even when his body is at rest, so that the first sign of trouble brings him to the hearth. The chronicler notes that this precept has given to the northern households a habit of vigilant responsibility that strangers sometimes find oppressive, and that the southern kingdoms, where the winters are milder, do not enforce the watch with the same rigour.

The continuity of the watch is observed most strictly upon the signal towers of the frontier, where the loss of the flame may cost the lives of many in the settlement that depends upon the warning. The watchmen of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) are sworn to a rotation that allows no interval of unattended flame, and the chroniclers record no instance of a frontier settlement lost by signal failure in the generations since the code was established. The teaching holds that this constancy is itself a kind of honour, and that the watchman who keeps his post through the long night earns a credit of reputation that no single act of bravery could secure.

## Application in Household and Forge

Beyond the literal keeping of the fire, the Code of the Ember has shaped the conduct of household and forge throughout the northern lands and wherever the northern influence has reached. The mistress of a household is taught to maintain the stores and the routines of the home in the same mean between waste and want as the keeper maintains his flame, and the master of a forge is taught to order the labour of his apprentices so that the work proceeds steadily rather than by fits and starts. The chronicler notes that this teaching has given to the crafts of the north a steadiness of product that the workshops of the south, where the seasons are more lenient, sometimes lack.

The code is observed also in the conduct of public office, and the magistrates of the northern towns are required to attend to the small affairs of the household and the ward with the same patience as the keeper attends to his flame. The chronicler observes that this practice has done much to make the northern polities orderly in their daily administration, and that the southern embassies, accustomed to a more intermittent attention, are sometimes surprised by the regularity of northern governance. The teaching holds that the polity is an aggregate of households, and that the polity governed by the discipline of embers will endure where one governed only by the discipline of emergencies will fail.

## Relation to Crown and Creed

The Code of the Ember, like the other teachings of the world, makes no claim upon the crown or the altar. It asks only that the flame be kept and the watch be held, and it binds the followers of the northern faiths and the southern creeds alike where they tend the household fires. The thrones of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-aldreth) and of [Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael) have at times found the code inconvenient, for it sets the obligation of the watch above the command of the magistrate in matters of small daily governance, yet none has set it aside, for the alternative is held to be the disorder of the polity and the failure of the household. The teaching sits easily beside the [Doctrine of the Open Hand](/content/belief/ideology/doctrine-of-the-open-hand), which governs the relations of host and guest, and the two are often observed by the same household.

The code is distinct from the faiths of worship, including the [Faith of the Still Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/faith-of-the-still-flame), with which it might appear to have an affinity of emblem. The followers of the Still Flame venerate the fire as an emblem of the divine; the practitioners of the Ember code venerate the duty of the keeper. The chronicler notes that the two teachings have sometimes been confused by the hasty observer, but that the keepers themselves maintain a careful distinction, for the faith is a matter of worship while the code is a matter of conduct.

## Significance

The Code of the Ember endures as one of the practical disciplines of the world, a teaching of stewardship and constancy that has preserved the households and the forges of the northern lands through every extremity of season and history. It is a teaching without temple or priesthood, carried in the practice of the hearth and the watch-tower, and its strength lies in the daily observance of the keepers who maintain it. A household that observes the code is a household that endures, and a polity whose officers are trained in the code is a polity that survives the long winter. In the account of the chronicler, the Code of the Ember is among the chief supports of the northern order, and the world is the more constant for the patience of those who keep the small flame burning through the long night.
