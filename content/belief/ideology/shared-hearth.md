## Overview

The Shared Hearth is a teaching of household and community that takes as its emblem the single hearth about which the members of a household gather, and by extension the single hearth about which the households of a community gather in the ordinary passages of life. It is not a religion and claims no temple or priest, but a discipline of domestic conduct taught by the matrons and elders of the household throughout the settled lands where the household is held to be the foundation of the polity. The central axiom is that the hearth once kindled is the hearth of all who sit beside it; the master of the house who would deny the warmth of the fire to any of those who gather has denied the meaning of the hearth itself. The teaching has been refined over generations into a comprehensive ethic of household order, mutual dependence, and the proper governance of the shared life.

## Origins

The Shared Hearth arose in the settled farming communities of the river valleys, where the household was the fundamental unit of production and the hearth was the centre about which the labours of the household were ordered. The harvest was brought to a common hearth and divided among the households of the village; the matrons of the village met at a single hearth to order the affairs of the community. The matrons and elders refined this custom into the formal axiom now observed throughout the settled lands and the towns that have grown from them.

As trade grew and towns increased in size, the axiom was carried into the new communities by matrons trained in the older villages. The households of the towns took up the axiom as the foundation of their domestic order. It found a ready welcome in the households of [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth), whose settled agrarian communities had long experience of the village hearth, and in the households of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), whose member cities maintained the custom as a foundation of urban order. The axiom has been of especial service to larger households, where the management of common life requires a discipline that the informal custom of the small village does not supply.

## The Hearth as Foundation

The first article is the hearth as foundation: the household is the unit of the polity and the hearth is the centre of the household. The polity is an aggregate of households; the polity that disorders its households is itself disordered. The master and matron of the household are therefore the first officers of the commonweal. The hearth is not merely where the pot is hung — it is where the household gathers in counsel and rest, and the order of the household is read in the order of the fire. This teaching has given the matrons of settled communities a recognised authority that the matrons of more mobile communities do not always possess.

The hearth as foundation is observed most characteristically in the villages of the river valleys, where the household is large enough to require the ordering of many labours and the hearth is the centre of common life. The villages where the axiom is observed show a tidiness of the household and a steadiness of domestic routine that those where it is neglected do not possess. The orderly hearth is the foundation of the orderly village, and the orderly village is the foundation of the orderly realm.

## The Membership of the Fire

The second article is the membership of the fire: all who sit beside the hearth are within the protection of the household for as long as they sit there. The hearth is a place of refuge as much as a place of cooking; one admitted to the fire is admitted to the household for the duration. The master who would deny the protection of the hearth to a guest has denied the meaning of the fire; the matron who would set a stranger from the door has forgotten the membership that her own position confers. This teaching has softened the boundaries between household and stranger; households where the axiom is observed are held in good repute for their welcome of the traveller.

The membership of the fire is observed most elaborately in the households of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), where the formal courtesies of the hearth are prescribed by the customary law of the cities. The master and matron must maintain a watchfulness for the stranger at the door. The custom of the pact is more elaborate than the custom of the villages; the formal courtesies of the hearth have been developed by the pact into a fine discipline.

## The Labours of the Household

The third article is the labours of the household: the work of common life is ordered and apportioned among the members of the household. The household is a polity in miniature; its labour is to be ordered by the same justice as the labour of the realm. The master who would lay all the heavy work upon one member has broken the axiom. Labour is apportioned with attention to the capacity of each member, so that the strong bear the heavier part and the weak are not overburdened; the master and matron must set their own hands to the work they require of others.

The labours are observed most characteristically in the larger households of towns and manors, where the variety of the work requires deliberate apportionment and the matron maintains a watchfulness for the proper ordering of common labour. The custom of the apportionment is enforced by the regard of the household and by the periodic reckoning of the matron. The orderly apportionment of household labour is the foundation of the household's prosperity.

## The Keeping of the Common Store

The fourth article is the keeping of the common store: the resources of the household are gathered, husbanded, and distributed according to need. The store of the household is a common trust; the master and matron administer it for the benefit of all members. The master who would appropriate the store to his own pleasure has broken the axiom. The store must be husbanded with attention to season and need, so that the household is neither impoverished by failure to lay up nor starved by failure to distribute.

The keeping of the common store is observed most formally in the larger households of the manors and patrician houses of the towns, where the variety of resources and the length of the reckoning require precise administration. The matrons of the patrician houses of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) render an account of the common store at stated intervals; the custom of the reckoning is held to be a foundation of trust between the household and its officers.

## The Custom of the Common Meal

Many practitioners observe the custom of the common meal, at which all members of the household sit down together. The master and matron who would eat apart from the household have broken the fellowship of the fire. This custom is observed with particular rigour in the households of the [Southern Reach](/content/government/national/southern-reach), where the common meal is held to be the foundation of the household's daily life.

The custom of the common meal is distinct from the doctrines of hospitality observed by other teachings. The axiom does not bind the host to the formal reception of the stranger; it binds the household to the common sitting of its own members. The household where the common meal is observed is held to be a household of a particular kind. The common meal is the seal of the household's fellowship.

## Application in the Towns and Manors

The Shared Hearth has been of especial service to the larger households of towns and manors, where the management of common life requires a discipline that the simpler custom of the village does not supply. The patrician houses of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) have refined the axiom into a fine art of household management; the matrons of the pact are held in repute throughout the lands where the axiom is observed for the precision of their domestic administration.

The axiom has also shaped the town councils of the smaller polities, where affairs are ordered by the matrons and elders of the leading households. The [Khalisar Council](/content/government/local/khalisar-council) draws upon the axiom in its domestic ordinances; the custom of the shared hearth has been of service to the council in ordering the town's common life. The polity that observes the axiom in its own domestic ordering will be a polity whose households are themselves well ordered.

## Relation to Crown and Creed

The Shared Hearth is indifferent to the boundaries of crown and creed. It asks only that the hearth be shared and the common store kept, and it binds followers of the [Still Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/still-flame), the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and smaller local rites alike where they keep the household. The greater polities have sometimes viewed the axiom as suited only to settled communities; it is less generally observed among the mobile peoples of the plains and desert than among the settled peoples of the river valleys. Yet the axiom has been taken up by the settled communities of desert oases and plain towns, proving itself capable of crossing customary frontiers.

The axiom is distinct from doctrines of worship. It does not require belief in the divine or participation in temple rites; it asks only the observance of the household and the fellowship of the fire. This distinction has made the axiom acceptable to the practical folk of villages and towns, who hold their faith lightly and their household firmly.

## Significance

The Shared Hearth endures as one of the most quietly influential of the domestic teachings of the world — a discipline of household order and mutual dependence that has preserved the settled communities through every extremity of season and history. It has no temple or priesthood; it is carried in the custom of the household and the village, and its strength lies in the daily observance of the matrons and elders who maintain it. A household that observes the axiom endures; a village whose households observe it is steady in its domestic order. The Shared Hearth is among the chief supports of the settled order, and the world is the more orderly for the discipline of those who keep the common fire.
