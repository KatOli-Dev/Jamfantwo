## Overview

The Still-Waters is a teaching of contemplation and restraint that takes as its emblem the unrippled surface of a deep pool, in which the sky and the banks are clearly mirrored and the depths are undisturbed. It is not a religion and makes no claim upon the divine, but a discipline of conduct taught by the contemplatives, healers, and arbiters of disputes throughout the lands where the swift current and the loud voice are held to be faults of character. The central precept is that the well-governed mind is like still water, receiving all impressions without disturbance and returning them without distortion, and the practice of this stillness is the foundation of all right judgment and right action. The path has been refined over generations into a comprehensive ethic of inward quiet and outward measure that has shaped the conduct of magistrates, physicians, and counsellors in courts and households from the highlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to the cities of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles).

## Origins

The Still-Waters arose among the healers and arbitrators of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), whose practice required a composure of mind that the fevered and the disputatious could not maintain. An older order of wise women and men served the upland villages as both physician and peacemaker, training their apprentices in the discipline of inward quiet as the foundation of their art. The masters of this order systematised the training into the formal path now observed.

As the upland healers travelled southward into the courts of the kingdoms and the cities of the strait, the teaching travelled with them. The magistrates of [Nordhaven](/content/location/settlement/city/nordhaven), [Ashford](/content/location/settlement/city/ashford), and [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch) took up its precepts as a foundation of judicial temper. The arbiters of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), who sit in judgment upon disputes of the maritime communities, are among the most rigorous practitioners of the path; the reputation of the compact for fair judgment rests in no small part upon the composure of its members. The path has further found a home among the contemplative orders of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court), whose members cultivate the still mind as the precondition of right counsel.

## The Discipline of the Undisturbed Mind

The first article of the path is the discipline of the undisturbed mind: the practitioner is taught to receive provocation, grief, and surprise without disturbance of the inward quiet. The mind that ripples at every impression is unfit for judgment, for it cannot weigh what it cannot hold steady; the practitioner who would govern others must first govern himself. The discipline is taught through a graduated series of exercises, beginning with the bearing of small discomforts and rising to the bearing of great ones; the practitioner advances only so far as his composure is proven.

The discipline is not the suppression of feeling — the path teaches that feeling is part of nature and is not to be denied — but the regulation of feeling so that it does not overwhelm judgment. The practitioner feels fully and sets the feeling aside when the work of judgment requires it, returning to it in the hour of rest. This teaching requires a certain maturity of temperament and a long apprenticeship; not every aspirant is suited to the path. The masters test their apprentices by small provocations before entrusting them with greater ones.

## The Measure of the Spoken Word

The second article is the measure of the spoken word. The practitioner speaks only after deliberation, weighs each utterance for its truth and consequence, and refrains from speech when neither truth nor benefit is to be served. The still water speaks only when the wind moves it; the wind of speech should be permitted to rise only when it carries some truth. The practitioner listens more than he speaks, for the listener learns more than the talker and is less likely to err.

The measure of speech is observed most strictly among the arbiters of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), who hear the parties to a dispute without interruption and deliver judgment only after the matter has been fully weighed. This practice has given the compact a reputation for patient justice that the swifter courts of the southern kingdoms do not enjoy; litigants from distant ports sometimes bring their disputes to Veldenwatch in preference to the nearer tribunals. Justice delayed by deliberation is justice better served; haste of judgment is the parent of error.

## The Patience of the Counsellor

The third article is the patience of the counsellor: the practitioner gives counsel only when it is sought and gives it in such measure as the recipient can bear. Counsel offered unbidden is seldom heeded; counsel poured out in excess overwhelms rather than informs. The practitioner reads the face and bearing of the one who seeks counsel, gives so much and no more, and leaves the recipient with room to act upon what he has heard. This teaching requires a discernment that no mere study can confer; the masters of the path are known as much by their silence as by their speech.

The patience of the counsellor is observed in the courts of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), whose royal advisors are trained from youth in the disciplines of the path, and in the councils of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), whose elders give counsel in measured phrase. The path has softened the edges of the rougher polities; princes trained in it are known for deliberation in council that the untrained prince does not possess. The polity whose counsellors practice the patience of the path enjoys sound deliberations; the cost of the long counsel is repaid in the soundness of the decision.

## Application in Healing and Judgment

Beyond the formal teaching, the disciplines of the path have shaped the practice of healing and judgment throughout the lands where it is observed. The physicians of the upland order examine their patients with an undisturbed mind, for the physician disturbed by the suffering before him cannot read the signs of the body; the calm of the healer is itself a contribution to the cure. The magistrates of the maritime compacts hear the parties without display of feeling, for the magistrate whose face betrays his leaning has already failed in his office. No serious school of healing or arbitration in the known world is without some debt to the path.

The path has also shaped household management. The matron receives the disputes of the kitchen and courtyard with the same composure as the magistrate receives the disputes of the court; the master gives counsel to his children and servants in the same measured phrase as the counsellor of the prince. The household is a polity in miniature; the discipline of the path applies to it no less than to the greater affairs of the realm.

## Relation to Crown and Creed

The Still-Waters is indifferent to the boundaries of crown and creed. It asks only that the mind be stilled and the word measured, and it binds followers of the [Still Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/still-flame), the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and smaller local rites alike where they practice healing or arbitration. The polities of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), with their quicker tempers and louder councils, have sometimes viewed the path as suited only to the colder and more deliberate north; it is less generally observed in the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) courts than in those of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). Yet the Sahrani physicians and the arbiters of the strait are among the most respected practitioners of the path.

The path is distinct from doctrines of worship. It does not require belief in the divine or participation in temple rites; it asks only the discipline of the mind and the regulation of the tongue. This distinction has made the path acceptable to magistrates and physicians who hold no religious allegiance.

## Significance

The Still-Waters endures as one of the most widely influential of the contemplative teachings of the world — a discipline of inward quiet and outward measure that has shaped the practice of healing and judgment wherever the slow weighing of evidence is preferred to the swift assertion of will. It has no temple or priesthood; it is preserved by the masters of its practice and the careful training of apprentices, and its strength lies in the demonstrable usefulness of its disciplines. A physician trained in the path is a better physician; a magistrate trained in the path is a better magistrate. The Still-Waters is among the chief supports of the more deliberate offices of human society, and the world is the more just for the patience of those who walk it.
