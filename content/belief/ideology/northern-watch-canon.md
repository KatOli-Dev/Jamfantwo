## Overview

The Canon of the [Northern Watch](/content/location/settlement/outpost/northern-watch) is a teaching of vigilance and endurance that governs the conduct of those who hold the frontier against the wilderness and the winter. It is not a religion and claims no temple or priest, but a discipline of the watch-tower, the beacon, and the long patrol, taught by the officers of the marches and the captains of the northern levies throughout the lands where the reach of the settled realm is bounded by the wild. The central canon of the teaching is that the watch is not an office to be discharged at the convenience of the watcher but a duty to be held against every interruption, and that the people of the marches are bound to this duty by the necessity of their position. The canon has been refined over generations into a comprehensive ethic of vigilance, endurance, and the proper ordering of the frontier watch that has shaped the customs of the northern polities and the conduct of the patrols from the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) to the coast of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea).

## Origins among the Marcher Captains

The Northern Watch Canon is generally held to have arisen among the captains of the marcher levies of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches), whose duty required them to maintain a watch upon the frontier through the long winters when the wild beasts and the unsubdued clans beyond the marches were most active. The chroniclers of the marches preserve accounts of an older compact by which the captains of the several garrisons held a common watch and exchanged the signals of beacon and horn by which the frontier was warned of incursion. From this practical compact the present canon is held to descend, refined by the officers and the elders of the marches into the formal teaching now observed throughout the northern polities and in the watch-towers that line the frontier.

As the settlements of the marches grew and the trade of the northern kingdoms extended, the canon was carried into the towns and the courts of the interior, and the officers of the [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) and of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) took up its precepts as the foundation of their military order. The canon found a ready welcome among the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), whose life upon the very edge of the settled realm required of them a vigilance that the more southerly peoples did not possess. The chronicler notes that the canon has been of especial service to the smaller garrisons and the remote watch-towers, where the watch must be held by a handful of men against dangers that the larger settlements do not face, and that the discipline of the canon has preserved many a frontier post that would otherwise have been overwhelmed.

## The Continuity of the Watch

The first article of the canon is the continuity of the watch, by which the watchman is required to maintain his post from the beginning of his tour to the end without sleep or withdrawal. The teaching holds that the watch is not an aggregate of moments to be filled by the convenience of the watcher but a continuous duty that admits of no interruption, and that the watchman who sleeps at his post is held to have failed in his office. The discipline is enforced by a graduated series of punishments, beginning with the reprimand of the captain and rising to the dismissal of the watchman and the loss of his post. The chronicler notes that this rigour has been the subject of much criticism by the philosophers of the southern kingdoms, who hold that the watchman is but a man and is subject to the ordinary frailties, yet the canon has endured because the frontier has shown that the loss of the watch is the loss of all.

The continuity of the watch is observed in its most rigorous form upon the signal towers of the frontier, where the loss of the beacon may cost the lives of the settlements that depend upon the warning. The chronicler notes that the watchmen of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) are bound by the canon to maintain their post through every watch of the long winter, and that the rotation is so ordered that no single watchman is required to hold the tower for more than the span that his strength will bear. The teaching holds that the strength of the watch is the strength of the watchman, and that the canon requires the captain to husband the strength of his men so that the watch may be continuous without the ruin of those who hold it. The chronicler notes that this practical wisdom has been the salvation of many a garrison that would otherwise have failed in its duty.

## The Reading of the Signs

The second article of the canon is the reading of the signs, by which the watchman is required to read the weather, the movement of the wild beasts, and the bearing of any stranger who approaches the watch-post, so that he may give warning in time. The teaching holds that the watchman who cannot read the signs is a watchman in name only, and that the post is held by the knowledge of the watchman no less than by his presence. The discipline is taught by a graduated series of exercises, beginning with the reading of the weather and the tracks of the beasts, and rising to the reading of the bearing of strangers and the disposition of armed parties. The chronicler notes that this art requires a long apprenticeship and a certain natural shrewdness, and that not every recruit is suited to the watch.

The reading of the signs is observed in its most characteristic form among the scouts of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches), whose office requires them to range the frontier and to bring word of the movement of beasts and of strangers to the garrisons that depend upon the warning. The chronicler notes that the scouts of the marches are trained from youth in the reading of the signs, and that the daughters and sons of the frontier families are often sent to the scouts as apprentices. The teaching holds that the scout who misreads the signs is a scout who has failed, and that the misreading is visited by a punishment proportioned to the consequence. The chronicler notes that the discipline of the reading has done much to preserve the marches from surprise, and that the settlements of the marches have been spared many an incursion that the settlements of the less rigorous frontiers have not.

## The Order of the Beacon

The third article of the canon is the order of the beacon, by which the signals of the watch are prescribed and the occasions of their use are defined. The teaching holds that the beacon is the voice of the watch and that the beacon lit in vain is a beacon that destroys the trust of the settlements that depend upon it, and the beacon unlit when the warning is required is a beacon that has failed in its office. The discipline is enforced by a graduated series of penalties, beginning with the reprimand of the watchman and rising to the loss of his post and the censure of his captain. The chronicler notes that this rigour has preserved the trust of the settlements in the signals of the watch, and that the beacon of the marches is held throughout the northern lands to be a signal that may be relied upon.

The order of the beacon is observed in its most formal form in the watch-towers of the [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven), where the signals of the several towers are prescribed by the royal ordinance and the occasions of their use are set forth in the standing orders of the marches. The chronicler notes that the kingdom has maintained a beacon service along the northern frontier for as long as any chronicler can attest, and that the service has been preserved through every extremity of season and of conflict by the rigour of the canon. The teaching holds that the beacon is the bond between the watch and the settlement, and that the canon requires the watch to maintain the bond by the precision of its signals. The chronicler notes that this discipline has done much to make the marches a place of reliable warning, and that the settlements of the marches have grown in confidence by it.

## The Holding of the Post

The fourth article of the canon is the holding of the post, by which the watchman is required to maintain his post against every assault and to surrender it only by his own death or by the express command of his captain. The teaching holds that the post is a trust committed to the watchman and that the trust is not to be set down at the convenience of the watcher, and the watchman who surrenders his post without command is held to have broken the canon. The discipline is observed with a particular rigour upon the isolated watch-towers of the frontier, where the watchman must often hold his post against odds that would overwhelm a larger garrison. The chronicler notes that the holding of the post has been the source of many a celebrated deed in the history of the marches, and that the officers who have held their posts in extremity are honoured in the chronicles of the frontier.

The holding of the post is observed in its most extreme form among the garrisons of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm), whose watch-towers stand upon the very edge of the wastes and are sometimes cut off from relief for weeks at a time by the storms of the long winter. The chronicler notes that the watchmen of the Deep Realm are bound by the canon to hold their posts against every assault and to maintain the beacon even when the relief is long in coming. The teaching holds that the watchman who maintains the beacon against hope is the watchman who fulfils the canon in its highest form, and that the chronicle of the Deep Realm preserves the names of many such watchmen as a part of the treasure of the realm.

## The Relation of the Watch to the Settlement

Beyond the formal articles of the canon, the customary practice of the watch sets forth a particular relation between the watch and the settlement that depends upon it. The teaching holds that the watch and the settlement are bound together in a single duty, and that the settlement which fails to support the watch is held to have failed in the canon as surely as the watchman who sleeps at his post. The settlement is required to maintain the watch in provisions and in reinforcements, and the watch is required to maintain the settlement in warning and in defence. The chronicler notes that this mutual obligation has done much to preserve the harmony of the marches, where the settlement and the watch are accustomed to regard one another as parts of a single order.

The relation of the watch to the settlement is observed in its most characteristic form in the smaller villages of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches), where the watch-tower and the village are closely bound and where the villagers regard the watchmen as their own sons and brothers. The chronicler notes that the custom of the marches requires the village to maintain the watch in provisions and the watch to maintain the village in warning, and that the breach of the mutual obligation is visited by the censure of the community. The teaching holds that the harmony of the watch and the settlement is itself a discipline, and that the frontier which preserves the harmony is the frontier which endures.

## The Long Endurance

The fifth article of the canon is the long endurance, by which the watchman is required to maintain his duty through the long winter and the long watch without the comfort of relief or the solace of company. The teaching holds that the frontier is a place of solitude and that the watchman who cannot endure the solitude is not fitted for his office, and the captain who places an unfit watchman upon the solitary post is held to have failed in his own duty. The discipline is enforced by a careful selection of the watchmen for the solitary posts and by a graduated introduction to the solitude so that the watchman is not overwhelmed at the first. The chronicler notes that this practical wisdom has preserved the sanity of many a watchman who would otherwise have been broken by the solitude, and that the frontier service is held in honour by the communities of the marches for the endurance that it requires.

The long endurance is observed in its most extreme form among the watchmen of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), where the watch-towers stand at the very edge of the winter storms and where the watchman must often hold his post through weeks of unbroken cloud and driving snow. The chronicler notes that the watchmen of the uplands are bound by the canon to maintain their post through the long endurance and to give the warning when the warning is required, and that the discipline of the canon has preserved many a settlement that would otherwise have been surprised by the storms and the beasts. The teaching holds that the long endurance is the test of the watch and that the watchman who endures it is the watchman who fulfils the canon.

## Application in the Greater Polities

The Northern Watch Canon has been taken up by the greater polities of the north and adapted to the conditions of the standing armies and the royal watches. The [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) maintains a beacon service along its northern frontier by the canon, and the officers of the service are trained in the watch-towers of the marches before they are entrusted with the greater commands. The [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) has refined the canon into a comprehensive discipline of the frontier watch, and the chroniclers of the realm hold that the canon has been the foundation of the realm's enduring security. The teaching holds that the greater polity which maintains the canon is the greater polity which preserves its frontier, and the chronicler notes that this teaching has been borne out by the history of the northern kingdoms.

The canon has also shaped the conduct of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), whose life upon the very edge of the settled realm requires of them a vigilance that the more settled peoples do not possess. The chronicler notes that the clans have maintained the canon in their own form, adapted to the conditions of the clan levy and the clan watch, and that the discipline of the canon has preserved the clans from the incursions that have at times overwhelmed other frontier peoples. The teaching holds that the canon is suited to every form of frontier life, and that the people who observe it are the people who endure upon the edge.

## Relation to Crown and Creed

The Northern Watch Canon is, like the other teachings of the world, indifferent to the boundaries of crown and creed. It asks only that the watch be held and the post be maintained, and it binds the followers of the [Still Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/still-flame), of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and of the smaller local rites alike where they stand upon the watch-tower. The polities of the south, with their milder winters and their longer horizons, have sometimes viewed the canon as a peculiarity of the northern peoples, and the canon is in fact less generally observed in the settled south than in the marches. Yet the canon has been taken up by the standing armies of the settled kingdoms, and the chronicler notes that the discipline of the watch is recognised throughout the lands as a foundation of the military order.

The canon is distinct from the doctrines of worship, though it shares with them the language of duty and endurance. The canon does not require of its practitioners any belief in the divine nor any participation in the rites of the temples; it asks only the holding of the watch and the maintaining of the beacon. The chronicler notes that this distinction has made the canon acceptable to the practical folk of the marches, who hold their faith lightly and their watch firmly, and that it has been adopted in the frontier garrisons precisely because it makes no demands beyond its own observance.

## Significance

The Northern Watch Canon endures as one of the most efficacious of the military teachings of the world, a discipline of vigilance and endurance that has preserved the northern frontiers through every extremity of season and history. It is a teaching without temple or priesthood, carried in the custom of the watch-tower and the beacon, and its strength lies in the daily observance of the watchmen who maintain it. A frontier that observes the canon is a frontier that endures, and a settlement whose watch is held by the canon is a settlement that is warned in time. In the account of the chronicler, the Northern Watch Canon is among the chief supports of the northern order, and the world is the more secure for the patience of those who hold the beacon through the long winter night.
