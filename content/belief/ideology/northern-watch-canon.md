## Overview

The Canon of the [Northern Watch](/content/location/settlement/outpost/northern-watch) is a teaching of vigilance and endurance that governs the conduct of those who hold the frontier against the wilderness and the winter. It is not a religion and claims no temple or priest, but a discipline of the watch-tower, the beacon, and the long patrol taught by the officers of the marches and the captains of the northern levies. The central canon is that the watch is not an office to be discharged at the watcher's convenience but a duty to be held against every interruption. The canon has been refined over generations into a comprehensive ethic of vigilance, endurance, and proper ordering of the frontier watch that has shaped the customs of northern polities and the conduct of the patrols from the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) to the coast of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea).

## Origins

The Northern Watch Canon arose among the captains of the marcher levies of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches), whose duty required a watch upon the frontier through the long winters when wild beasts and unsubdued clans were most active. The captains of several garrisons held a common watch and exchanged signals of beacon and horn by which the frontier was warned of incursion. This practical compact was refined by the officers and elders into the formal teaching now observed throughout the northern polities and the watch-towers that line the frontier.

As the settlements of the marches grew and trade extended, the canon was carried into the towns and courts of the interior. The officers of [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) and the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) took up its precepts as the foundation of their military order. The [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), whose life upon the edge of the settled realm required a vigilance that more southerly peoples did not possess, found a ready welcome in it. The canon has been of especial service to smaller garrisons and remote watch-towers, where a handful of men must hold against dangers that larger settlements do not face.

## The Continuity of the Watch

The first article of the canon is the continuity of the watch: the watchman must maintain his post from beginning to end without sleep or withdrawal. The watch is not an aggregate of moments to be filled by the watcher's convenience but a continuous duty admitting no interruption. The watchman who sleeps at his post has failed in his office. The discipline is enforced by graduated punishments, from reprimand to dismissal and loss of post. The philosophers of the southern kingdoms have criticised this rigour, arguing that the watchman is but a man subject to ordinary frailties, yet the canon has endured because the frontier has shown that the loss of the watch is the loss of all.

The continuity of the watch is observed most rigorously upon the signal towers of the frontier, where the loss of the beacon may cost the lives of the settlements that depend upon the warning. The watchmen of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) are bound to maintain their post through every watch of the long winter; the rotation is ordered so that no single watchman holds the tower for more than his strength will bear. The canon requires the captain to husband the strength of his men so that the watch may be continuous without the ruin of those who hold it.

## The Reading of the Signs

The second article is the reading of the signs: the watchman must read the weather, the movement of wild beasts, and the bearing of any stranger who approaches the watch-post, so that he may give warning in time. The watchman who cannot read the signs is a watchman in name only; the post is held by the watchman's knowledge no less than by his presence. The discipline is taught through graduated exercises, from reading weather and animal tracks to reading the bearing of strangers and the disposition of armed parties.

This is observed most characteristically among the scouts of the [Northern Marches](/content/government/national/northern-marches), who range the frontier and bring word of movement to the garrisons that depend upon the warning. The scouts are trained from youth in the reading of signs; the daughters and sons of frontier families are often sent to the scouts as apprentices. The scout who misreads the signs has failed, and the misreading is visited by punishment proportioned to the consequence. This discipline has preserved the marches from surprise, sparing them many incursions that less rigorous frontiers have suffered.

## The Order of the Beacon

The third article is the order of the beacon: the signals of the watch are prescribed and the occasions of their use defined. The beacon is the voice of the watch; the beacon lit in vain destroys the trust of the settlements that depend upon it, and the beacon unlit when warning is required has failed in its office. The discipline is enforced by graduated penalties, from reprimand to loss of post and censure of the captain.

The order of the beacon is observed most formally in the watch-towers of [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven), where the signals of the several towers are prescribed by royal ordinance and the occasions of their use are set forth in the standing orders of the marches. The kingdom has maintained a beacon service along the northern frontier for as long as any account attests. The beacon is the bond between the watch and the settlement; the canon requires the watch to maintain that bond by the precision of its signals.

## The Holding of the Post

The fourth article is the holding of the post: the watchman must maintain his post against every assault and surrender it only by his own death or by the express command of his captain. The post is a trust committed to the watchman that may not be set down at the watcher's convenience. This is observed with particular rigour upon the isolated watch-towers of the frontier, where the watchman must often hold against odds that would overwhelm a larger garrison.

The holding of the post is observed most extremely among the garrisons of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm), whose watch-towers stand upon the edge of the wastes and are sometimes cut off from relief for weeks by the storms of the long winter. The watchmen are bound to hold their posts against every assault and maintain the beacon even when relief is long in coming. The watchman who maintains the beacon against hope fulfils the canon in its highest form.

## The Relation of the Watch to the Settlement

The canon sets forth a particular relation between the watch and the settlement that depends upon it. The watch and the settlement are bound together in a single duty; the settlement that fails to support the watch has failed in the canon as surely as the watchman who sleeps at his post. The settlement must maintain the watch in provisions and reinforcements, and the watch must maintain the settlement in warning and defence. This mutual obligation preserves the harmony of the marches, where settlement and watch regard one another as parts of a single order.

This relation is observed most characteristically in the smaller villages of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches), where the watch-tower and the village are closely bound and villagers regard the watchmen as their own sons and brothers. The village maintains the watch in provisions, the watch maintains the village in warning; breach of the mutual obligation is visited by the censure of the community.

## The Long Endurance

The fifth article is the long endurance: the watchman must maintain his duty through the long winter and the long watch without the comfort of relief or the solace of company. The frontier is a place of solitude; the watchman who cannot endure solitude is not fitted for his office, and the captain who places an unfit watchman upon the solitary post has failed in his own duty. The discipline is enforced by careful selection for the solitary posts and by a graduated introduction to solitude so that the watchman is not overwhelmed at the first.

The long endurance is observed most extremely among the watchmen of the [Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), where the watch-towers stand at the edge of the winter storms and the watchman must often hold his post through weeks of unbroken cloud and driving snow. The discipline has preserved many settlements that would otherwise have been surprised by storms and beasts.

## Application in the Greater Polities

The Northern Watch Canon has been taken up by the greater polities of the north and adapted to standing armies and royal watches. [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven) maintains a beacon service along its northern frontier by the canon; officers are trained in the watch-towers of the marches before they are entrusted with greater commands. The [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) has refined the canon into a comprehensive discipline of the frontier watch that the realm's chroniclers hold to be the foundation of its enduring security.

The canon has also shaped the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), who maintain it in their own form adapted to the clan levy and clan watch. The discipline of the canon has preserved the clans from incursions that have at times overwhelmed other frontier peoples.

## Relation to Crown and Creed

The Northern Watch Canon is indifferent to the boundaries of crown and creed. It asks only that the watch be held and the post maintained, and it binds followers of the [Still Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/still-flame), the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and smaller local rites alike where they stand upon the watch-tower. The polities of the south, with milder winters and longer horizons, have sometimes viewed the canon as a peculiarity of the northern peoples. Yet the canon has been taken up by the standing armies of the settled kingdoms, and the discipline of the watch is recognised throughout the lands as a foundation of military order.

The canon is distinct from doctrines of worship. It does not require belief in the divine or participation in temple rites; it asks only the holding of the watch and the maintaining of the beacon. This distinction has made the canon acceptable to the practical folk of the marches, who hold their faith lightly and their watch firmly.

## Significance

The Northern Watch Canon endures as one of the most efficacious of the world's military teachings — a discipline of vigilance and endurance that has preserved the northern frontiers through every extremity of season and history. It has no temple or priesthood; it is carried in the custom of the watch-tower and the beacon, and its strength lies in the daily observance of the watchmen who maintain it. A frontier that observes the canon endures; a settlement whose watch is held by the canon is warned in time. The Northern Watch Canon is among the chief supports of the northern order, and the world is the more secure for the patience of those who hold the beacon through the long winter night.
