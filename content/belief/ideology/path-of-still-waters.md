## Overview

The Path of Still Waters is a teaching of contemplation and restraint that takes as its emblem the unrippled surface of a deep pool, in which the sky and the banks are clearly mirrored and the depths are undisturbed. It is not a religion and makes no claim upon the divine, but a discipline of conduct taught by the contemplatives, the healers, and the arbiters of disputes throughout the lands where the swift current and the loud voice are held to be faults of character. The central precept of the path is that the well-governed mind is like a still water, receiving all impressions without disturbance and returning them without distortion, and that the practice of this stillness is the foundation of all right judgment and all right action. The path has been refined over generations into a comprehensive ethic of inward quiet and outward measure that has shaped the conduct of magistrates, physicians, and counselors in courts and households from the highlands of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to the cities of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles).

## Origins among the Upland Healers

The Path of Still Waters is generally held to have arisen among the healers and arbitrators of the [The Stoneheim Highlands](/content/location/settlement/region/stoneheim-highlands), whose practice required a composure of mind that the fevered and the disputatious could not maintain. The chroniclers of [Karhold](/content/location/settlement/city/karhold) preserve accounts of an older order of wise women and men, who served the upland villages as both physician and peacemaker and who trained their apprentices in the discipline of the inward quiet as the foundation of their art. From this practical training the present teaching is held to descend, systematized by the masters of the order into the formal path now observed.

As the upland healers travelled southward into the courts of the kingdoms and the cities of the strait, the teaching travelled with them, and the magistrates of [Nordhaven](/content/location/settlement/city/nordhaven), of [Ashford](/content/location/settlement/city/ashford), and of [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch) took up its precepts as a foundation of judicial temper. The arbiters of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), who sit in judgment upon the disputes of the maritime communities, are reckoned among the most rigorous practitioners of the path, and the chronicler notes that the reputation of the compact for fair judgment rests in no small part upon the composure of its members. The path has further found a home among the contemplative orders of the [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court), whose members are taught to cultivate the still mind as the precondition of right counsel.

## The Discipline of the Undisturbed Mind

The first article of the path is the discipline of the undisturbed mind, by which the practitioner is taught to receive provocation, grief, and surprise without the disturbance of the inward quiet. The teaching holds that the mind which ripples at every impression is unfit for judgment, for it cannot weigh what it cannot hold steady, and that the practitioner who would govern others must first govern himself. The discipline is taught by a graduated series of exercises, beginning with the bearing of small discomforts and rising to the bearing of great ones, and the practitioner is held to advance only so far as his composure is proven.

The discipline is not the suppression of feeling, for the path teaches that feeling is a part of nature and is not to be denied, but the regulation of feeling so that it does not overwhelm the judgment. The practitioner is taught to feel fully and to set the feeling aside when the work of judgment requires it, returning to it in the hour of rest. The chronicler notes that this teaching requires a certain maturity of temperament and a long apprenticeship, and that not every aspirant is suited to the path. The masters of the path are said to test their apprentices by small provocations before they entrust them with greater, and the chronicler records many instances in which an unfit apprentice has been quietly turned away.

## The Measure of the Spoken Word

The second article of the path is the measure of the spoken word. The practitioner is taught to speak only after deliberation, to weigh each utterance for its truth and for its consequence, and to refrain from speech when neither truth nor benefit is to be served. The teaching holds that the still water speaks only when the wind moves it, and that the wind of speech should be permitted to rise only when it carries some truth. The practitioner is further taught to listen more than he speaks, for the path holds that the listener learns more than the talker and is less likely to err.

The measure of speech is observed most strictly among the arbiters of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact), who are required to hear the parties to a dispute without interruption and to deliver judgment only after the matter has been fully weighed. The chronicler notes that this practice has given to the compact a reputation for patient justice that the swifter courts of the southern kingdoms do not enjoy, and that litigants from distant ports will sometimes bring their disputes to Veldenwatch in preference to the nearer tribunals. The teaching holds that justice delayed by deliberation is justice better served, and that the haste of judgment is the parent of error.

## The Patience of the Counselor

The third article of the path is the patience of the counselor, by which the practitioner is taught to give counsel only when it is sought and to give it in such a measure as the recipient can bear. The teaching holds that the counsel offered unbidden is seldom heeded, and that the counsel poured out in excess overwhelms rather than informs. The practitioner is taught to read the face and the bearing of the one who seeks counsel, to give so much and no more, and to leave the recipient with room to act upon what he has heard. The chronicler notes that this teaching requires a discernment that no mere study can confer, and that the masters of the path are known as much by their silence as by their speech.

The patience of the counselor is observed in the courts of the [Kingdom of Vael](/content/government/national/kingdom-of-vael), whose royal advisors are trained from youth in the disciplines of the path, and in the councils of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), whose elders are expected to give counsel in measured phrase. The chronicler notes that the path has done much to soften the edges of the rougher polities, and that the princes who have been trained in it are known for a deliberation in council that the untrained prince does not possess. The teaching holds that the polity whose counselors practice the patience of the path is a polity whose deliberations are sound, and that the cost of the long counsel is repaid in the soundness of the decision.

## Application in Healing and Judgment

Beyond the formal teaching of the path, its disciplines have shaped the practice of healing and judgment throughout the lands where the path is observed. The physicians of the upland order are required to examine their patients with an undisturbed mind, for the path holds that the physician who is disturbed by the suffering before him cannot read the signs of the body, and that the calm of the healer is itself a contribution to the cure. The magistrates of the maritime compacts are required to hear the parties without display of feeling, for the path holds that the magistrate whose face betrays his leaning has already failed in his office. The chronicler notes that these practices have made the path indispensable to the arts it serves, and that no serious school of healing or arbitration in the known world is without some debt to it.

The path has also shaped the conduct of household management in the communities where it is observed. The matron of the household is taught to receive the disputes of the kitchen and the courtyard with the same composure as the magistrate receives the disputes of the court, and the master of the household is taught to give counsel to his children and his servants in the same measured phrase as the counselor of the prince. The teaching holds that the household is a polity in miniature and that the discipline of the path applies to it no less than to the greater affairs of the realm. The chronicler notes that this teaching has produced a steadiness in the domestic order of the path's adherents that other communities sometimes remark upon.

## Relation to the Crowns and the Creeds

The Path of Still Waters is, like the other teachings of the world, indifferent to the boundaries of crown and creed. It asks only that the mind be stilled and the word be measured, and it binds the followers of the [Faith of the Still Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/faith-of-the-still-flame), of the [Old Pantheon of the Plains](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-pantheon-of-the-plains), and of the smaller local rites alike where they practice the healing or the arbitration. The polities of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana), with their quicker tempers and louder councils, have sometimes viewed the path as a teaching suited only to the colder and more deliberate north, and the path is in fact less generally observed in the Sahrani courts than in those of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). Yet the Sahrani physicians and the arbiters of the strait are among the most respected practitioners of the path, and the teaching has proved itself capable of crossing the customary frontiers.

The path is distinct from the doctrines of worship, though it shares with them the language of inward quiet. The path does not require of its practitioners any belief in the divine nor any participation in the rites of the temples; it asks only the discipline of the mind and the regulation of the tongue. The chronicler notes that this distinction has made the path acceptable to magistrates and physicians who hold no religious allegiance, and that it has been adopted in the cosmopolitan courts precisely because it makes no demands beyond its own practice.

## Significance

The Path of Still Waters endures as one of the most widely influential of the contemplative teachings of the world, a discipline of inward quiet and outward measure that has shaped the practice of healing and judgment wherever the slow weighing of evidence is preferred to the swift assertion of will. It is a teaching without temple or priesthood, preserved by the masters of its practice and by the careful training of apprentices, and its strength lies in the demonstrable usefulness of its disciplines. A physician trained in the path is a better physician, and a magistrate trained in the path is a better magistrate, and the world is the better served for the still minds of those who keep it. In the account of the chronicler, the Path of Still Waters is among the chief supports of the more deliberate offices of human society, and the world is the more just for the patience of those who walk it.
