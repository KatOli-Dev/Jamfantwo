## Overview

The Open Sky Pact is a teaching of freedom and fellowship that holds the unbound heavens to be the emblem of the right ordering of human society, and sets the freedom of the open air against every form of unjust dominion. It is not a religion and claims no temple or priest, but a discipline taught by the herders of the open plains, the carters of the long road, and the dwellers of the frontier where the reach of the crown is thin. The central precept is that the sky under which all people stand is one sky, that no lord may rightfully claim the air above another, and that the people who walk beneath it are bound to one another in a fellowship of mutual regard that precedes the claims of every polity. The pact has been refined over generations into a comprehensive ethic of openness, plain dealing, and resistance to oppression that has shaped the customs of the plains and long-distance trade from the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) to the harbours of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles).

## Origins

The Open Sky Pact arose among the herders of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains), whose life upon the open range required a mutual dependence that the customs of the settled town did not supply. The herders of one clan would assist those of another in the season of the roundup and share the pasturage of the open range without reckoning of ownership. The elders and captains of the clans refined this compact into the formal teaching now observed throughout the plains and in the trade towns that have grown up upon their borders.

As the confederation of the clans took shape and long-distance trade grew along the [Grassland Traderoad](/content/location/route/trade/grassland-traderoad), the pact spread into the trade towns and along the trade roads. The carters of the long routes took up its precepts; the factors of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) caravans found their life upon the open road required the same mutual dependence as the life of the herders. The [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) became one of the chief avenues by which the pact entered the desert communities. The pact has been of especial service to the smaller folk of the plains and trade roads, who have found in it a discipline that protects them against the heavier hand of the great lords.

## The Fellowship of the Open Air

The first article of the pact is the fellowship of the open air: all who stand beneath the sky are in a relation of mutual regard that precedes the claims of any lord. The sky is the common roof of all people; beneath it no man is wholly subject to another, and the lord who would claim the air above his people has overstepped the proper limit of his office. The fellowship is not a denial of all authority — the pact recognises the necessity of captains and magistrates in the ordering of common life — but it sets the freedom of the open air as the measure by which all authority is judged. This teaching has softened the more rigid claims of the older polities; even the settled kingdoms have come to acknowledge the fellowship in their customary law.

The fellowship is observed most strictly among the herders of the plains, whose custom requires that the traveller upon the open range be given such aid as the occasion requires without question of his lord or errand. The custom is enforced by the general regard of the clans; the herder who refuses aid to a traveller upon the range has broken the pact and is shunned accordingly.

## The Plainness of the Open Deal

The second article is the plainness of the open deal: the practitioner must deal openly with his neighbour and refrain from the concealment and wile that the pact holds to be the instruments of the unjust lord. The sky under which the bargain is struck is the same sky under which the bargain will be remembered; the dealer who conceals a flaw in his goods or a fault in his word has offended against the openness of the air. The pact prescribes that the bargain be struck in the presence of witnesses sufficient to recall it and that the terms be spoken plainly so that all may hear.

This is observed most characteristically in the trade towns at the meeting of the trade roads, where factors of many peoples strike their bargains in the open air of the market square. The markets of the plains are governed by the pact to a degree that the markets of the settled cities are not; the trader who brings concealed goods to the open market has offended against the custom and is corrected by the masters of the market. The open deal is the foundation of the open fellowship; concealment of word or goods is the first step toward the unjust dominion that the pact resists.

## The Right of the Open Road

The third article is the right of the open road: the traveller upon the king's highway and upon the plain track alike is under the protection of the common fellowship of the sky. The road is common to all people; no lord may rightfully close it to the traveller, and the people who dwell beside it are bound to maintain it so far as their means permit. This right has been a source of frequent dispute between the pact and the greater polities, for the latter have at times claimed the power to close the road for reasons of war or excise, while the pact holds that the right of the open road is a right of the fellowship not subject to the convenience of any single lord.

The right of the open road is observed most strictly upon the long trade routes that cross the plains and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) and connect the coasts of the known world. The carters of the long routes regard the pact as the foundation of their livelihood; the brotherhood of the carter is one of the most rigorous expressions of the pact. The carter who closes the road by force or fraud has offended against the fellowship of the sky, and the carters who assemble to keep the road open are acting within the pact. This has kept the long routes passable in seasons when the great polities would have closed them.

## The Resistance to the Unjust Lord

The fourth article is the resistance to the unjust lord: the practitioner is bound to withstand the lord who would claim the air above his people, the road before his people, or the open dealing of his people. The unjust lord has broken the fellowship of the sky; the people who suffer him have themselves failed in the pact. The resistance is not a counsel of frequent rebellion — the pact recognises that the order of the polity is itself part of the fellowship — but it is a duty owed to the fellowship when the lord has plainly overstepped. This article has been the most controversial of the pact; the greater polities have at times regarded it as a seditious teaching, yet the pact has endured because the article is enforced only in clear cases and by the general consent of the practitioners.

The resistance is observed most characteristically in the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), where customary law requires that the captain who exceeds his office be corrected by the council of the elders and, in extreme cases, deposed by the assembly of the clans. This practice has preserved the confederation from the more oppressive forms of dominion that have disfigured other polities.

## The Open Hearth and the Open Door

Many practitioners observe the custom of the open hearth and the open door. The open hearth is the hearth at which the traveller is received without question of errand; the open door is the door of the dwelling that is not closed against the stranger who stands without. These are the household expression of the fellowship of the open air; the practitioner of the pact who maintains a closed door has forgotten the meaning of the sky beneath which he dwells. This practice is observed with particular rigour in the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), where the custom of the open hearth is enforced by the general regard of the community.

The open hearth and open door are distinct from the formal hospitality observed by teachings such as the [Open Hand](/content/belief/ideology/open-hand). The pact does not bind the host to elaborate courtesies — the open hearth requires only the shelter of the roof and the sharing of the pot, the open door requires only that the door not be barred. This simplicity appeals to herders and carters, who hold the formal courtesies of the cities in light regard and prefer the plain dealing of the open air.

## Application in the Trade Communities

The Open Sky Pact has been of especial service to the trade communities of the plains and long routes. The factors of the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) are among the most rigorous practitioners; the caravan masters hold the plain dealing of the open market as the foundation of their commerce. The trader who conceals a flaw in his goods or a fault in his word has offended against the fellowship of the sky, and the caravan masters enforce this observance with a promptness that the slower courts of the cities do not possess.

The pact has also shaped the harbour towns of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), whose life upon the open sea and shore requires similar plainness of dealing. The harbour masters of the isles are bound by the customary law of the open deal as much as by the formal statutes of the island confederation.

## Relation to Crown and Creed

The Open Sky Pact is indifferent to the boundaries of crown and creed. It asks only that the fellowship of the open air be honoured and the unjust dominion resisted, and it binds followers of the [Still Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/still-flame), the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and smaller local rites alike where they walk beneath the sky. The greater polities have sometimes regarded the pact with suspicion, for it sets the fellowship of the sky above the command of the crown, yet the pact has endured because the trade upon which the polities depend is sustained by the plain dealing that the pact requires.

The pact is distinct from doctrines of worship. It does not require belief in the divine or participation in temple rites; it asks only the observance of the open fellowship and the plain dealing. This distinction has made the pact acceptable to herders and carters who hold their faith lightly and their custom firmly.

## Significance

The Open Sky Pact endures as one of the more practical teachings of the world — a discipline of openness and plain dealing that has sustained the long trade routes and the herders of the open plains through every extremity of season and history. It has no temple or priesthood; it is carried in the custom of the road and the range, and its strength lies in the demonstrable usefulness of its precepts. A trader who observes the open deal prospers; a community that observes the open fellowship is fed by the traffic of the road. The Open Sky Pact is among the chief supports of the open order of the plains and the long routes, and the world is the more free for the plainness of those who keep it.
