## Overview

The Shoreline Discipline is a teaching of conduct that governs the meeting of sea and land and the conduct of those who live upon the margin between them. It is not a religion and makes no claim upon the divine, but a practical code taught by the fisherfolk, salt-makers, harbour pilots, and dwellers of the coastal villages throughout the lands where the boundary between waters and shore is a daily presence. The central precept is that the shoreline is neither sea nor land but a place of transition to which a particular lawfulness belongs, and that those who dwell upon it are bound by the duties of both. The discipline has been refined over generations into a comprehensive ethic of the margin that shapes the customs of coastal communities from the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip).

## Origins

The Shoreline Discipline arose among the fisherfolk of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast), where the meeting of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) and the northern lands produces a coast of cliffs, shoals, and narrow harbours that has long required prudence and generosity of its dwellers. The fisherfolk of one village would assist those of another in the season of storm, and the dwellers of the shore would succour sailors wrecked upon their beaches, whether friend or stranger. The elders and pilots refined this compact into the formal teaching now observed.

As the commerce of coastal communities grew and the harbours of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) became the meeting place of many peoples, the discipline spread southward along trade routes. It was taken up by the harbour towns of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the island communities of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles). The pilots of [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch), whose office requires precise knowledge of the meeting of waters and shore, are among the most rigorous practitioners of the discipline; the [Grey Coast Holdings](/content/government/local/grey-coast-holdings), whose jurisdiction extends along a coast of mixed tenure, are governed in part by its precepts.

## The Two Jurisdictions

The discipline recognises that the shoreline is subject to two jurisdictions — that of the land and that of the sea — and that neither alone is sufficient for the governance of the margin. The law of the land holds from the high-water mark to the interior; the law of the sea holds from the low-water mark to the offing. The shoreline itself is where the two overlap and the strict application of either produces injustice. The discipline therefore sets forth a customary law of the margin by which the dwellers of the shore are governed in the ordinary passages of their lives. This customary law has been a model for the formal law of the maritime compacts; the statutes of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) draw upon it in many particulars.

The two jurisdictions are observed most distinctly in the harbours of the strait, where ships of many nations ride at anchor within sight of the shore and the customary law of the harbour prescribes the conduct of vessels and landfolk alike. The pilot who brings a ship into the harbour must know the law of both jurisdictions, for his office requires that he speak to the master of the ship as a sailor and to the harbour master as a landdweller. The practitioner of the shoreline is by nature a practitioner of both elements; the attempt to be wholly of one or the other is a failure of the office.

## The Duty of the Wreck

The first article of the discipline is the duty of the wreck: the dwellers of the shore are bound to succour sailors and vessels that come to grief upon their coast. The shipwrecked sailor is under the protection of the shore from the moment he sets foot upon it; the dwellers of the beach must give him shelter, food, and assistance in the repair of his vessel so far as their means permit. The duty is not discharged by the mere rendering of immediate aid — the discipline requires that the sailor be aided until he is again able to pursue his voyage or until he has been delivered to the care of his own people. This duty has been the foundation of the coastal communities' reputation for hospitality; mariners of the strait will sometimes choose a harbour by the reputation of its wreck duty rather than by its convenience.

The duty of the wreck is observed most strictly along the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast), where winter storms drive many vessels upon the cliffs and the dwellers of the shore maintain a watch for the purpose of succouring the wrecked. The villages maintain boats and beacons for this purpose; the cost of the watch is reckoned as part of the common burden of the village. The duty of the wreck is a duty of the shoreline and not of any particular village; the dwellers of the shore are bound to the duty wherever they are, even upon a coast not their own.

## The Right of Salvage

The second article is the right of salvage: the dwellers of the shore may claim a portion of the cargo of a vessel that has come to grief upon their coast in return for the succour they have given. The succour of the wreck is not a gift but an exchange; the dwellers of the shore are entitled to a customary portion of the value saved by their aid. The salvage must be measured, so that the sailor is not impoverished by his loss and the salvor is not enriched beyond his due. This right has been a frequent subject of dispute between coastal communities and the merchants of the strait; prudent harbour towns maintain a customary scale by which salvage is assessed.

The right of salvage is observed most formally in the harbours of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), where the law of the harbour prescribes the shares of vessel, cargo, and salvor with precision. The precision of salvage is itself a discipline — imprecise assessment breeds dispute, and dispute breeds the breaking of the more general duty. The harbours where salvage is precisely known are also the harbours where wreckers are most prompt to render aid.

## The Law of the Harbour Mouth

The third article is the law of the harbour mouth, governing the conduct of vessels entering and leaving the harbour. The harbour mouth is a place of particular care: the vessel within the harbour is under the protection of the shore, the vessel without is still upon the sea, and the moment of transition requires particular observance. The pilot must give such assistance as the entering vessel requires; the master must submit his ship to the pilot's direction until the harbour is reached.

The law of the harbour mouth is observed most elaborately at the great harbours of the strait, where the entry is narrow and the tide runs strong, and where the cooperation of pilot and master is essential to the safety of the vessel. The master who refuses the pilot's direction forfeits the protection of the harbour for the consequences of his refusal; the pilot who fails in his direction answers for the loss. This division of responsibility makes the harbour a place of clear account, where neither party may shift the blame for a known failure.

## The Custom of the Beach

Beyond the formal articles, the customary law of the shoreline includes smaller observances governing the daily life of coastal communities. The dwellers of the shore must keep the beach clear of obstacles that might hinder the launching of lifeboats, maintain the beacons and markers of the harbour mouth, and refrain from building upon the foreshore without community consent. The foreshore is common to all dwellers of the shore; private appropriation of it is a breach of the discipline. These observances are enforced by the custom of the community rather than by any formal court; the breach of them is followed by a withdrawal of neighbourly regard that coastal dwellers find more grievous than any fine.

The custom of the beach is observed most characteristically in the smaller fishing villages of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) and the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip), where the community is small enough that observance can be enforced by general regard. The villages where the custom is observed show a tidiness of the shore and a readiness of the lifeboat that those where it is neglected do not possess.

## Application in the Greater Communities

The Shoreline Discipline has been taken up by the greater maritime communities and adapted to their conditions. The statutes of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) incorporate many of its precepts; the compact is held to be the fullest expression of the discipline in a formal polity. Its officers are trained in the customary law of the shoreline as well as in the statutory law of the compact. The adaptation has not been without controversy — the greater community requires precision that the village does not — but the central teaching is preserved.

The discipline has also shaped the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), whose [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) neighbours are included within its customary law in a manner that mainland communities have sometimes found surprising. The islands have long maintained the discipline in a form that recognises the merfolk as dwellers of the shoreline as well as of the sea, making the islands a place of meeting between the two peoples.

## Relation to Crown and Creed

The Shoreline Discipline is indifferent to the boundaries of crown and creed. It asks only that the duties of the shoreline be observed and the customary law of the margin honoured, and it binds followers of the [Still Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/still-flame), the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and smaller local rites alike where they dwell upon the shore. The polities of the interior have sometimes viewed the discipline as a peculiarity of maritime communities; it is less generally observed in inland towns than in coastal villages. Yet the maritime trade of the inland kingdoms depends upon the harbours where the discipline is observed, and the inland polities have come to respect the discipline as a foundation of the trade by which they are fed.

The discipline is distinct from doctrines of worship. It does not require belief in the divine or participation in temple rites; it asks only the observance of the shoreline and the succour of those who come to grief upon it. This distinction has made the discipline acceptable to the practical folk of the coast, who hold their faith lightly and their custom firmly.

## Significance

The Shoreline Discipline endures as one of the most practically efficacious of the world's teachings — a code of the margin that has preserved the lives of countless sailors and the goods of countless merchants by its simple prescription of duty and right. It has no temple or priesthood; it is carried in the custom of coastal villages and the statutes of maritime compacts, and its strength lies in the immediacy of its obligation. A sailor upon the shore is under the protection of the dweller of the shore, and the dweller of the shore is bound to render that protection without reckoning of price. The Shoreline Discipline is among the chief supports of the maritime order, and the world is the more kindly for the observance of those who keep the beach and the harbour mouth in their proper discipline.
