## Overview

The Shoreline Discipline is a teaching of conduct that governs the meeting of sea and land, and the conduct of those who live upon the margin between the two. It is not a religion and makes no claim upon the divine, but a practical code taught by the fisherfolk, the salt-makers, the pilots of the harbours, and the dwellers of the coastal villages throughout the lands where the boundary between the waters and the shore is a daily presence. The central precept of the discipline is that the shoreline is neither sea nor land but a place of transition to which a particular lawfulness belongs, and that those who dwell upon it are bound by the duties of both. The discipline has been refined over generations into a comprehensive ethic of the margin that shapes the customs of the coastal communities from the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip).

## Origins among the Coastal Communities

The Shoreline Discipline is generally held to have arisen among the fisherfolk of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast), where the meeting of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) and the northern lands produces a coast of cliffs, shoals, and narrow harbours that has long required of its dwellers a particular prudence and a particular generosity. The chroniclers of the coastal villages preserve accounts of an older compact by which the fisherfolk of one village would assist those of another in the season of storm, and by which the dwellers of the shore would succour the sailors wrecked upon their beaches, whether friend or stranger. From this practical compact the present discipline is held to descend, refined by the elders and the pilots into the formal teaching now observed.

As the commerce of the coastal communities grew and the harbours of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) became the meeting place of many peoples, the discipline spread southward along the trade routes and was taken up by the harbour towns of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and by the island communities of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles). The pilots of [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch), whose office requires a precise knowledge of the meeting of waters and shore, are reckoned among the most rigorous practitioners of the discipline, and the [Grey Coast Holdings](/content/government/local/grey-coast-holdings), whose jurisdiction extends along a coast of mixed tenure, are governed in part by its precepts. The chronicler notes that the discipline has proved itself especially suited to the maritime communities, where the boundary between elements is a daily reality and the laws of either element alone are insufficient.

## The Two Jurisdictions

The discipline begins with the recognition that the shoreline is subject to two jurisdictions, that of the land and that of the sea, and that neither alone is sufficient for the governance of the margin. The law of the land holds from the high-water mark to the interior, and the law of the sea holds from the low-water mark to the offing, but the shoreline itself is a place where the two overlap and where the strict application of either produces injustice. The discipline therefore sets forth a customary law of the margin, by which the dwellers of the shore are governed in the ordinary passages of their lives. The chronicler notes that this customary law has been a model for the more formal law of the maritime compacts, and that the statutes of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) draw upon it in many particulars.

The two jurisdictions are observed in their most distinct form in the harbours of the strait, where the ships of many nations ride at anchor within sight of the shore and the customary law of the harbour prescribes the conduct of vessels and of landfolk alike. The pilot who brings a ship into the harbour is required by the discipline to know the law of both jurisdictions, for his office requires that he speak to the master of the ship as a sailor and to the harbour master as a landdweller, and the chronicler notes that the pilots of Veldenwatch are trained from youth in this double competence. The discipline holds that the practitioner of the shoreline is by nature a practitioner of both elements, and that the attempt to be wholly of one or the other is a failure of the office.

## The Duty of the Wreck

The first article of the discipline is the duty of the wreck, by which the dwellers of the shore are bound to succour the sailors and the vessels that come to grief upon their coast. The teaching holds that the shipwrecked sailor is under the protection of the shore from the moment he sets foot upon it, and that the dwellers of the beach are required to give him shelter, food, and assistance in the repair of his vessel so far as their means permit. The duty is not held to be discharged by the mere rendering of immediate aid, for the discipline requires that the sailor be aided until he is again able to pursue his voyage or until he has been delivered to the care of his own people. The chronicler notes that this duty has been the foundation of the reputation of the coastal communities for hospitality, and that the mariners of the strait will sometimes choose a harbour by the reputation of its wreck duty rather than by its convenience.

The duty of the wreck is observed most strictly along the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast), where the winter storms drive many vessels upon the cliffs and where the dwellers of the shore have long maintained a watch for the purpose of succouring the wrecked. The chronicler notes that the villages of the Stormfall Coast maintain boats and beacons for this purpose, and that the cost of the watch is reckoned as a part of the common burden of the village. The teaching holds that the duty of the wreck is a duty of the shoreline and not of any particular village, and that the dwellers of the shore are bound to the duty wherever they are, even upon a coast not their own.

## The Right of the Salvage

The second article of the discipline is the right of the salvage, by which the dwellers of the shore are permitted to claim a portion of the cargo of a vessel that has come to grief upon their coast in return for the succour they have given. The teaching holds that the succour of the wreck is not a gift but an exchange, and that the dwellers of the shore are entitled to a customary portion of the value saved by their aid. The discipline is careful that the salvage should be measured, so that the sailor is not impoverished by his loss and the salvor is not enriched beyond his due. The chronicler notes that this right has been a frequent subject of dispute between the coastal communities and the merchants of the strait, and that the prudent harbour towns maintain a customary scale by which the salvage is assessed.

The right of the salvage is observed in its most formal form in the harbours of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), where the law of the harbour prescribes the shares of vessel, cargo, and salvor with a precision that the chroniclers of the mainland sometimes admire. The teaching holds that the precision of the salvage is itself a discipline, for the imprecise assessment breeds dispute and the dispute breeds the breaking of the more general duty. The chronicler notes that the harbours where the salvage is precisely known are also the harbours where the wreckers are most prompt to render aid, and that the two parts of the discipline support one another in practice.

## The Law of the Harbour Mouth

The third article of the discipline is the law of the harbour mouth, by which the conduct of vessels entering and leaving the harbour is governed. The teaching holds that the harbour mouth is a place of particular care, for the vessel within the harbour is under the protection of the shore and the vessel without is still upon the sea, and the moment of transition requires a particular observance. The pilot is required to give such assistance as the entering vessel requires, and the master of the vessel is required to submit his ship to the direction of the pilot until the harbour is reached. The chronicler notes that this law has done much to make the harbours of the strait places of orderly passage, and that the pilots of [Veldenwatch](/content/location/settlement/town/veldenwatch) are renowned for the rigour of their direction at the harbour mouth.

The law of the harbour mouth is observed in its most elaborate form at the great harbours of the strait, where the entry is narrow and the tide runs strong, and where the cooperation of pilot and master is essential to the safety of the vessel. The discipline teaches that the master who refuses the direction of the pilot is held to have forfeited the protection of the harbour for the consequences of his refusal, and the pilot who fails in his direction is held to answer for the loss. The chronicler notes that this division of responsibility has done much to make the harbour a place of clear account, where neither party may shift the blame for a known failure.

## The Custom of the Beach

Beyond the formal articles of the discipline, the customary law of the shoreline includes a great number of smaller observances that govern the daily life of the coastal communities. The dwellers of the shore are required to keep the beach clear of obstacles that might hinder the launching of the lifeboat, to maintain the beacons and the markers of the harbour mouth, and to refrain from building upon the foreshore without the consent of the community. The teaching holds that the foreshore is common to all the dwellers of the shore and that the private appropriation of it is held to be a breach of the discipline. The chronicler notes that these smaller observances are enforced by the custom of the community rather than by any formal court, and that the breach of them is followed by a withdrawal of neighbourly regard that the coastal dwellers find more grievous than any fine.

The custom of the beach is observed in its most characteristic form in the smaller fishing villages of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) and of the [Sahrani Coastal Strip](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/sahrani-coastal-strip), where the community is small enough that the observance of the custom can be enforced by the general regard of the village. The chronicler notes that the villages where the custom is observed are marked by a tidiness of the shore and a readiness of the lifeboat that the villages where it is neglected do not possess, and that the customary law of the shoreline is in this respect a teacher of practical order. The teaching holds that the orderly shore is the foundation of the orderly harbour, and the orderly harbour is the foundation of the orderly voyage.

## Application in the Greater Communities

The Shoreline Discipline has been taken up by the greater maritime communities and adapted to their conditions, and the statutes of the [Velden Compact](/content/government/local/velden-compact) incorporate many of its precepts. The compact is held by the chroniclers to be the fullest expression of the discipline in a formal polity, and its officers are trained in the customary law of the shoreline as well as in the statutory law of the compact. The chronicler notes that the adaptation has not been without controversy, for the greater community requires a precision that the village does not, and the discipline has been required to yield a little of its customary flexibility to the formality of the compact. Yet the central teaching is preserved, and the compact is governed by the duty of the wreck and the right of the salvage as much as by any of its written statutes.

The discipline has also shaped the conduct of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), whose [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) neighbours are included within its customary law in a manner that the mainland communities have sometimes found surprising. The chronicler notes that the islands have long maintained the discipline in a form that recognises the merfolk as dwellers of the shoreline as well as of the sea, and that this recognition has done much to make the islands a place of meeting between the two peoples. The teaching holds that the discipline of the shoreline is a discipline of the margin, and that the merfolk, who dwell upon the margin as much as any landfolk, are rightly within its scope.

## Relation to Crown and Creed

The Shoreline Discipline is, like the other teachings of the world, indifferent to the boundaries of crown and creed. It asks only that the duties of the shoreline be observed and that the customary law of the margin be honoured, and it binds the followers of the [Still Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/still-flame), of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and of the smaller local rites alike where they dwell upon the shore. The polities of the interior, with their settled lands and their inland courts, have sometimes viewed the discipline as a peculiarity of the maritime communities, and the discipline is in fact less generally observed in the inland towns than in the coastal villages. Yet the maritime trade of the inland kingdoms depends upon the harbours where the discipline is observed, and the chronicler notes that the inland polities have come to respect the discipline as a foundation of the trade by which they are fed.

The discipline is distinct from the doctrines of worship, though it shares with them the language of duty and observance. The discipline does not require of its practitioners any belief in the divine nor any participation in the rites of the temples; it asks only the observance of the shoreline and the succour of those who come to grief upon it. The chronicler notes that this distinction has made the discipline acceptable to the practical folk of the coast, who hold their faith lightly and their custom firmly, and that it has been adopted in the cosmopolitan harbours precisely because it makes no demands beyond its own observance.

## Significance

The Shoreline Discipline endures as one of the most practically efficacious of the world's teachings, a code of the margin that has preserved the lives of countless sailors and the goods of countless merchants by its simple prescription of duty and right. It is a teaching without temple or priesthood, carried in the custom of the coastal villages and the statutes of the maritime compacts, and its strength lies in the immediacy of its obligation. A sailor upon the shore is under the protection of the dweller of the shore, and the dweller of the shore is bound to render that protection without reckoning of price. In the account of the chronicler, the Shoreline Discipline is among the chief supports of the maritime order, and the world is the more kindly for the observance of those who keep the beach and the harbour mouth in their proper discipline.
