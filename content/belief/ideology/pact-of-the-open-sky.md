## Overview

The Pact of the Open Sky is a teaching of freedom and fellowship that holds the unbound heavens to be the emblem of the right ordering of human society, and that sets the freedom of the open air against every form of unjust dominion. It is not a religion and claims no temple or priest, but a discipline taught by the herders of the open plains, the carters of the long road, and the dwellers of the frontier where the reach of the crown is thin. The central precept of the pact is that the sky under which all people stand is one sky, and that no lord may rightfully claim the air above another, and that the people who walk beneath it are bound to one another in a fellowship of mutual regard that precedes the claims of every polity. The pact has been refined over generations into a comprehensive ethic of openness, plain dealing, and resistance to oppression that has shaped the customs of the plains and the conduct of the long-distance trade from the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) to the harbours of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles).

## Origins among the Herders of the Plains

The Pact of the Open Sky is generally held to have arisen among the herders of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains), whose life upon the open range required of them a mutual dependence that the customs of the settled town did not supply. The chroniclers of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) preserve accounts of an older compact by which the herders of one clan would assist those of another in the season of the roundup and would share the pasturage of the open range without reckoning of ownership. From this practical compact the present pact is held to descend, refined by the elders and the captains of the clans into the formal teaching now observed throughout the plains and in the trade towns that have grown up upon their borders.

As the confederation of the clans took shape and the long-distance trade grew along the [Grassland Traderoad](/content/location/route/trade/grassland-traderoad), the pact spread into the trade towns and along the trade roads, and the carters of the long routes took up its precepts as their own. The pact found a ready welcome among the factors of the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) caravans, whose life upon the open road required the same mutual dependence as the life of the herders upon the open range, and the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) became one of the chief avenues by which the pact entered the desert communities. The chronicler notes that the pact has been of especial service to the smaller folk of the plains and the trade roads, who have found in it a discipline that protects them against the heavier hand of the great lords.

## The Fellowship of the Open Air

The first article of the pact is the fellowship of the open air, by which all who stand beneath the sky are held to be in a relation of mutual regard that precedes the claims of any lord. The teaching holds that the sky is the common roof of all people and that beneath it no man is wholly subject to another, and that the lord who would claim the air above his people is held to have overstepped the proper limit of his office. The fellowship is not a denial of all authority, for the pact recognises the necessity of captains and of magistrates in the ordering of the common life, but it sets the freedom of the open air as the measure by which all authority is to be judged. The chronicler notes that this teaching has done much to soften the more rigid claims of the older polities, and that even the settled kingdoms have come to acknowledge the fellowship in their customary law.

The fellowship of the open air is observed most strictly among the herders of the plains, whose custom requires that the traveller upon the open range be given such aid as the occasion requires without question of his lord or his errand. The chronicler notes that the custom is enforced by the general regard of the clans, and that the herder who refuses aid to a traveller upon the range is held to have broken the pact and is shunned accordingly. The teaching holds that the refusal of aid upon the open range is a refusal of the common fellowship of the sky, and that the herder who so refuses has denied the very principle by which his own life upon the range is sustained.

## The Plainness of the Open Deal

The second article of the pact is the plainness of the open deal, by which the practitioner is required to deal openly with his neighbour and to refrain from the concealment and the wile that the pact holds to be the instruments of the unjust lord. The teaching holds that the sky under which the bargain is struck is the same sky under which the bargain will be remembered, and that the dealer who conceals a flaw in his goods or a fault in his word has offended against the openness of the air. The pact prescribes that the bargain be struck in the presence of witnesses sufficient to recall it, and that the terms of the bargain be spoken plainly so that all may hear. The chronicler notes that this practice has done much to make the trade of the plains and the trade roads a commerce of plain dealing, and that the merchants of the plains are held in good repute throughout the lands where the pact is observed.

The plainness of the open deal is observed in its most characteristic form in the trade towns that have grown up at the meeting of the trade roads, where the factors of many peoples meet to strike their bargains in the open air of the market square. The chronicler notes that the markets of the plains are governed by the pact to a degree that the markets of the settled cities are not, and that the trader who brings concealed goods to the open market is held to have offended against the custom and is corrected by the masters of the market. The teaching holds that the open deal is the foundation of the open fellowship, and that the concealment of word or of goods is the first step toward the unjust dominion that the pact is bound to resist.

## The Right of the Open Road

The third article of the pact is the right of the open road, by which the traveller upon the king's highway and upon the plain track alike is held to be under the protection of the common fellowship of the sky. The teaching holds that the road is common to all people and that no lord may rightfully close it to the traveller, and that the people who dwell beside the road are bound to maintain it so far as their means permit. The chronicler notes that this right has been the source of frequent dispute between the pact and the greater polities, for the greater polities have at times claimed the power to close the road for reasons of war or of excise, and the pact has held that the right of the open road is a right of the fellowship and is not subject to the convenience of any single lord.

The right of the open road is observed most strictly upon the long trade routes that cross the plains and the [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) and connect the coasts of the known world. The chronicler notes that the carters of the long routes regard the pact as the foundation of their livelihood, and that the brotherhood of the carter is one of the most rigorous expressions of the pact in the present age. The teaching holds that the carter who closes the road by force or by fraud is held to have offended against the fellowship of the sky, and that the carters who assemble to keep the road open are held to be acting within the pact. The chronicler notes that this practice has done much to keep the long routes passable in seasons when the great polities would have closed them, and that the carters of the long routes are reckoned among the more reliable practitioners of the pact.

## The Resistance to the Unjust Lord

The fourth article of the pact is the resistance to the unjust lord, by which the practitioner is bound to withstand the lord who would claim the air above his people or the road before his people or the open dealing of his people. The teaching holds that the unjust lord has broken the fellowship of the sky, and that the people who suffer him have themselves failed in the pact. The resistance is not held to be a counsel of frequent rebellion, for the pact recognises that the order of the polity is itself a part of the fellowship, but it is held to be a duty that the practitioner owes to the fellowship when the lord has plainly overstepped the proper limit. The chronicler notes that this article has been the most controversial of the pact, and that the greater polities have at times regarded it as a seditious teaching, yet the pact has endured because the article is enforced only in clear cases and by the general consent of the practitioners.

The resistance to the unjust lord is observed most characteristically in the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), where the customary law of the clans requires that the captain who exceeds his office be corrected by the council of the elders and, in extreme cases, be deposed by the assembly of the clans. The chronicler notes that this practice has preserved the confederation from the more oppressive forms of dominion that have disfigured other polities, and that the confederation is held by many of the chroniclers to be a model of the open order. The teaching holds that the open order is the foundation of the open fellowship, and that the polity which preserves the right of the open air and the open road is the polity which best fulfils the pact.

## The Open Hearth and the Open Door

Beyond the formal articles of the pact, the customary practice of the open hearth and the open door is observed by many of its practitioners, particularly upon the plains and in the smaller trade towns. The open hearth is the hearth at which the traveller is received without question of his errand, and the open door is the door of the dwelling that is not closed against the stranger who stands without. The teaching holds that the open hearth and the open door are the household expression of the fellowship of the open air, and that the practitioner of the pact who maintains a closed door has forgotten the meaning of the sky beneath which he dwells. The chronicler notes that this practice is observed with particular rigour in the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), where the custom of the open hearth is enforced by the general regard of the community, and that the traveller upon the plains is seldom turned from any door.

The open hearth and the open door are distinct from the doctrines of formal hospitality that are observed by the other teachings of the world, for the pact does not bind the host to the elaborate courtesies of the [Doctrine of the Open Hand](/content/belief/ideology/doctrine-of-the-open-hand). The open hearth is a simpler practice, requiring only the shelter of the roof and the sharing of the pot, and the open door is a simpler practice still, requiring only that the door not be barred against the stranger. The chronicler notes that this simplicity has given the pact a particular appeal to the herders and the carters, who hold the formal courtesies of the cities in light regard and prefer the plain dealing of the open air.

## Application in the Trade Communities

The Pact of the Open Sky has been of especial service to the trade communities of the plains and the long routes, and the chronicler notes that the custom of the open deal has done much to make the trade of these regions more reliable than the trade of the more closely governed polities. The factors of the [Kharesh Caravan Way](/content/location/route/trade/kharesh-caravan-way) are reckoned among the most rigorous practitioners of the pact, and the caravan masters hold the plain dealing of the open market as the foundation of their commerce. The teaching holds that the trader who conceals a flaw in his goods or a fault in his word is held to have offended against the fellowship of the sky, and the caravan masters are said to enforce this observance with a promptness that the slower courts of the cities do not possess.

The pact has also shaped the conduct of the harbour towns of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), whose life upon the open sea and the open shore requires a similar plainness of dealing. The chronicler notes that the isles have taken up the pact in their own form, adapted to the conditions of the maritime trade, and that the harbour masters of the isles are bound by the customary law of the open deal as much as by the formal statutes of the island confederation. The teaching holds that the open deal is the foundation of the open trade, and that the trade communities which observe it are the communities which prosper by it.

## Relation to Crown and Creed

The Pact of the Open Sky is, like the other teachings of the world, indifferent to the boundaries of crown and creed. It asks only that the fellowship of the open air be honoured and that the unjust dominion be resisted, and it binds the followers of the [Faith of the Still Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/faith-of-the-still-flame), of the [Old Pantheon of the Plains](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-pantheon-of-the-plains), and of the smaller local rites alike where they walk beneath the sky. The greater polities have sometimes regarded the pact with suspicion, for the pact sets the fellowship of the sky above the command of the crown, yet the pact has endured because the trade upon which the polities depend is itself sustained by the plain dealing that the pact requires. The chronicler notes that even the kings of the settled kingdoms have been heard to praise the pact when the trade is good, and to forget it when the trade is otherwise.

The pact is distinct from the doctrines of worship, though it shares with them the language of the sky and the open air. The pact does not require of its practitioners any belief in the divine nor any participation in the rites of the temples; it asks only the observance of the open fellowship and the plain dealing. The chronicler notes that this distinction has made the pact acceptable to the herders and the carters, who hold their faith lightly and their custom firmly, and that it has been adopted in the cosmopolitan trade towns precisely because it makes no demands beyond its own observance.

## Significance

The Pact of the Open Sky endures as one of the more practical teachings of the world, a discipline of openness and plain dealing that has sustained the long trade routes and the herders of the open plains through every extremity of season and history. It is a teaching without temple or priesthood, carried in the custom of the road and the range, and its strength lies in the demonstrable usefulness of its precepts. A trader who observes the open deal is a trader who prospers, and a community that observes the open fellowship is a community that is fed by the traffic of the road. In the account of the chronicler, the Pact of the Open Sky is among the chief supports of the open order of the plains and the long routes, and the world is the more free for the plainness of those who keep it.
