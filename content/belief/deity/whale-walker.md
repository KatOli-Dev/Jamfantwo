## The Elder of the Sea

The Whale-Walker is the second of the greater powers in the [Old Faith of the Western Deep](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-faith-of-the-western-deep), the god of the great whales that migrate through the cold waters of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) and the temperate latitudes of the western coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). He is conceived as the elder of the sea, a creature of great age and great wisdom who moves through the cold water in advance of the great pods, and whose passage is honoured by the whalers and the fishers of the western coast as the signal of the season of the great hunt. The whalers of the [Grey Coast](/content/location/settlement/region/grey-coast) invoke him at the season of the hunt and at the offering of the first blubber of the kill, and his festival falls at the time of the northward migration, when the great whales return to the feeding grounds of the polar latitudes. The chronicler of the western records has set down that the figure is older than the first whaling boat and the first cliff town, and that the whalers of the cold coast have honoured him since before the first net was woven from the hemp of the inland valleys.

The figure of the Whale-Walker is not worshipped in the sense of a deity to be placated, but is regarded as the elder of the sea, a creature to be honoured and respected before the hunt and a being whose bounty is earned rather than given. The chronicler of the cold coast records has set down that this attitude is unusual among the whaling peoples of the known world, for most similar communities treat the whale as either an enemy to be hunted or a sacred creature not to be taken at all. The Whale-Walker occupies a place between these two extremes, a being whose presence in the cold water is a sign of the bounty of the sea and whose killing is a solemn undertaking that the community must approach with the customary acknowledgments.

## The Figure and His Domain

The Whale-Walker is conceived as the living presence of the great whales themselves, and his domain extends from the temperate waters of the western coast to the polar latitudes where the whales feed through the summer months. He is the great shape that the mariners of the cold coast have seen rising from the grey water, the spout that the whalers identify from a distance, the long dive that carries the whale beneath the surface for the better part of an hour, and the song that the males sing upon the breeding grounds in the late autumn. The chronicler of the western records has set down that the figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, and that the figure is invoked by the reading of the migration and the conduct of the hunt as much as by any formal rite.

The principal dwelling of the Whale-Walker is the cold water of the Greyring, and the figure is present in the great whales that move through the water in their seasonal procession. The whalers of the cold coast maintain the headland shrines at which the offerings of the first blubber are made, and the shrines are the gathering points of the whaling communities at the turning of the season. The Whale-Walker is honoured in the headland shrines as the spirit of the whales that the shrines commemorate, and the offerings are the formal acknowledgement that the whale is not the property of the hunter but the gift of the elder of the sea.

## The Greater Powers of the Western Deep

The Whale-Walker is the second of the greater powers of the [Old Faith of the Western Deep](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-faith-of-the-western-deep), and his position in the faith is the honouring of the great whales of the cold water. The Grey Mother is the first, the principal power of the grey sea, the fog, and the long swell. The Storm-Bringer is the third, the power of the long gales that sweep in from the open ocean and can capsize even the strongest vessel. Beneath these three are set the spirits of the particular rocks and headlands of the western coast, a catalogue that is long and localised. The communities of each cliff town maintain the names of the spirits of the nearby headlands and rocks in small books kept by the elder, and the names are updated only when a spirit has been known across several generations.

The Whale-Walker is honoured in the order of the greater powers as the elder of the sea, and the whalers speak his name before the name of any of the other powers at the season of the hunt. The chronicler of the cold coast records has noted that the order of the greater powers is fixed by the conditions of the western sea, and the figure of the Whale-Walker is honoured second because the whale is the second of the gifts that the sea bestows upon the coast. The Grey Mother provides the water and the weather, and the Whale-Walker provides the great creature that the whalers pursue, and the Storm-Bringer provides the gales that the watchkeepers must observe.

## The Migration and the Hunt

The migration of the great whales through the cold water is a predictable event, and the hunt is organised and conducted by the whaling communities in a manner that has changed little over centuries. The faith teaches that the whale is the elder of the sea, a creature to be honoured before the hunt and to be taken only according to the customary quotas that the community has established. The first blubber of the kill is offered at the headland shrine by the elder of the whaling community, and the offering is a formal acknowledgement that the whale is not the property of the hunter but the gift of the elder of the sea, lent for the keeping of the community.

The whalers of the cold coast are a hardy folk who have pursued the great whales for centuries, and the fishery of the whale is one of the recognised pursuits of the maritime communities of the western shore. The fishery is conducted from small boats that are launched from the larger vessels, the boats pursuing the whale with the hand harpoon and the long lance, and the crews of the boats are among the most skilled of the mariners of the cold coast. The fishery is regulated by the customs of the coastal communities, and the taking of the whale is forbidden during the breeding season, and the smallest of the whales must be released.

## The Greyring Whale and the Honouring

The [Greyring whale](/content/species/beasts/greyring-whale) is the largest of the cetaceans of the cold water, a great baleen whale of dark grey-blue colour whose range extends across the cold waters of the western ocean from the polar south to the temperate latitudes of the western coast. The whale takes its name from the colour of the skin and from the colour of the waters that it inhabits, for the cold currents of the Greyring give the ocean a distinctive grey cast that the mariners of the western coast have likened to a great ring of haze upon the horizon. The chronicler of the cold coast records has set down that the Greyring whale is the principal creature of the Whale-Walker's domain, and the whale is the object of the customary offerings that the whalers make at the headland shrines.

The Greyring whale is migratory, and the populations move northward in the spring to feed in the rich waters of the polar latitudes, and southward in the autumn to breed in the warmer waters of the temperate latitudes. The migrations of the whale are closely followed by the whalers of the western coast, and the timing of the northward and the southward passages is one of the principal determinants of the rhythm of the fishing year. The Whale-Walker is honoured in the migration as the spirit of the whales that move through the cold water, and the migration is the foundation of the seasonal labour of the whalers.

## The Quotas and the Sustained Yield

The quotas that govern the whaling hunt are among the strictest in the known world, and the chronicler records that the whaling communities that have adhered to the quotas have maintained stable whale populations over generations, while those that have expanded their catches during years of abundance have often found the populations depleted in subsequent seasons. The faith teaches that the sea is a common inheritance of the present and the future generations, and that the whaler who would take the last breeding stock has denied his grandchildren the bounty of the sea. The chronicler observes that this principle of restraint, while framed in religious terms, represents one of the earliest known formal management systems for marine megafauna in the known world.

The Whale-Walker is honoured in the quotas as the spirit of the elder of the sea whose bounty is sustained by the restraint of the hunters. The chronicler of the cold coast records has set down that the figure is not honoured by the largest catch but by the most careful catch, and the figure is present in the customary law that governs the taking of the whale as the spirit of the elder whose protection is earned by the observance of the customary limits.

## The First Blubber and the Offering

The first blubber of the kill is the principal offering of the whaling season, and the offering is made at the headland shrine of the whaling community by the elder of the community. The offering is a formal acknowledgement that the whale is not the property of the hunter but the gift of the elder of the sea, lent for the keeping of the community. The chronicler of the western records has set down that the offering is one of the most solemn of the observances of the cold coast, and the offering is attended by the whalers and the fishers of the community with a quietness that the chronicler has described as the deepest piety of the western year.

The offering is accompanied by the customary words that the elder of the community speaks at the shrine, and the words have changed little over the generations. The words acknowledge the figure of the Whale-Walker as the elder of the sea, and they request the continuation of the bounty that the elder provides. The chronicler records that the words are the same in every cliff town from the northern headlands to the southern estuaries, and the uniformity of the words is a measure of the unity of the observance across the cold coast.

## The Merfolk and the Whale-Walker

The [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) of the cold waters honour the Whale-Walker as the elder of the sea, and the merfolk observance of the figure is adapted to the life of the deep water rather than the cliff town. The merfolk communities are dwellers of the cold water itself, and their understanding of the movements of the whales and the currents of the Greyring is deep. The merfolk observe the Whale-Walker at the customary seasons, and they maintain the traditional quotas that govern the whaling hunt. The chronicler records that the merfolk have acted as the primary intermediaries between the whaling communities and the whale pods in recent centuries, warning the whalers of approaching pods and advising them on the conditions of the hunt.

The relationship between the merfolk and the human whalers is one of practical reciprocity. The humans provide the boats, the harpoons, and the technology of the hunt, while the merfolk provide the knowledge of the whales and the deep water. The exchange is formalised through customary agreements that govern the fishing grounds and the conduct of the hunt, and the chronicler notes that the system has generally worked well, with occasional friction but no sustained conflict. The Whale-Walker is honoured in the cooperation of the two peoples as the spirit of the elder whose bounty the cooperation sustains.

## The Songs of the Breeding Grounds

The whales are highly vocal during the breeding season, and the songs that the males sing upon the breeding grounds are among the most remarkable of the natural sounds of the sea, a series of low and resonant calls that may continue for hours upon the still water. The chronicler of the cold coast records has set down that the songs are held by the cliff communities to be the voice of the Whale-Walker, and the songs are listened for with care during the breeding season. The fishers of the cliff towns report the songs to the elders when they hear them, and the elders record the timing and the duration of the songs in the small books that the communities maintain.

The songs are not merely observed; they are answered in the traditions of the storm-cantors, the singers who perform the old songs at the height of the winter gales. The chronicler has noted that the songs of the storm-cantors are in turn answered by the songs of the whales in the deep water, and the exchange is held to be a form of communion between the cliff community and the elder of the sea. The Whale-Walker is honoured in the exchange as the spirit of the whales that the cantors sing to in the winter gales.

## The Cliff Settlements and the Watch

The cliff towns of the [Grey Coast](/content/location/settlement/region/grey-coast) are the principal settlements of the faith, and each town maintains its own headland shrine upon the highest point overlooking the sea. The watch is the central duty of the cliff settlements, and the watchkeepers of the cliff towns are responsible for the maintenance of the lighthouses, the signal towers, and the storm warnings that protect the fishing fleet and the merchant traffic that passes along the coast. The chronicler records that the watchkeepers of the cold coast maintain a level of vigilance that is exceptional, and that the lighthouses and signal towers of the western coast are among the most densely maintained in the known world.

The Whale-Walker is honoured in the watch as the spirit of the elder whose passage the watchkeepers must observe, and the watchkeepers are required to report the passage of the great whales during the migration seasons. The chronicler of the cold coast records has set down that the watchkeepers are drawn from the fisherfolk, and their role is not separate from their daily labour but an extension of it. The duty of the watch is considered a religious obligation, and the neglect of the lighthouse by its keeper is considered a betrayal of the elder of the sea and the fishing community alike.

## Significance

The Whale-Walker is the second of the greater powers of the cold western sea, and his observance is the foundation of the whaling tradition of the [Old Faith of the Western Deep](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-faith-of-the-western-deep). The figure endures because the figure asks nothing of his adherents that contradicts their daily survival, and the figure offers nothing that undermines it. The reverence for the migration of the great whales, the formalisation of the hunt through religious obligation, and the integration of merfolk knowledge into the everyday practice of the whalers have all contributed to the stability of the whale populations of the cold coast. The chronicler finds in the Whale-Walker one of the most distinctive and least displaced of the figures of the known world, honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the whalers who depend upon the great whales for their livelihood and whose livelihood's continuation depends upon the whales returning each year in their season. The figure endures, in the cold water and at the headland shrine, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the elder of the sea.
