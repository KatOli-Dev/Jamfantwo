## The Living Presence of the Wood

The Forest Mother is the principal power of the [Forest Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/forest-way), the goddess in whom the ancient temperate woodlands of the known world are embodied. She is the living presence of the wood itself, the canopy that shades the forest floor, the streams that gather beneath the trees, and the small creatures whose daily labours shape the soil. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) of the temperate woodlands invoke her at the turning of the seasons and at the presentation of the young to the sapling, and her festival falls at the unfolding of the first leaves in spring. The chronicler of the woodland records has set down that the figure is older than the first elven settlement in the forest, and that the elven people have honoured her since before the first clearing was made beneath the canopy.

The figure of the Forest Mother is patient and enduring, and her observance is among the most intimate of the woodland year. The elven communities do not ask her for blessings in the abstract; they ask her for the preservation of the trees, for the protection of the breeding creatures of the forest, and for the wisdom to manage the woodland without diminishing it. The chronicler records that the figure is honoured by every act of forest husbandry that the elven people perform, and that the enduring fertility of the ancient temperate forests is the clearest evidence of the figure's favour.

## The Figure and Her Domain

The Forest Mother is conceived as the encompassing presence of the wood, and her domain extends from the smallest grove of the forest margin to the greatest of the old growths of the central heartwood. She is the canopy that intercepts the rain and returns it to the streams, the leaf litter that feeds the soil, the fallen log that shelters the small creatures of the forest floor, and the seedling that rises in the gap that an old tree has left. The chronicler of the woodland records has set down that the figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, and that the figure is invoked by the tending of the trees and the management of the forest as much as by any formal rite.

The principal dwelling of the Forest Mother is the great tree, the ancient oak or elm or ironwood that has stood for centuries and that the elven communities have tended with care. The ancient trees are held to be the pillars of the forest and sometimes the dwelling of the Forest Mother herself, and the trees are honoured at the turning of the seasons by the elven communities of the surrounding woodland. The Forest Mother is present in the ancient trees as the spirit of the wood that they embody, and her presence is renewed each year by the rites of the forest year.

## The Spirits of the Living Wood

Beneath the Forest Mother are set the specific powers of individual trees and plants. The god of the oak, the goddess of the silver elm, the spirit of the ironwood - each is recognised in the groves where that tree predominates. The spirits of the streams, the mosses, and the ground cover are localised to the particular hollows and ridges where they dwell, and their names are maintained by the elders in lists that are updated only reluctantly. A new name is added only after the spirit has been known across several generations, and the practice ensures that the catalogue of lesser powers grows slowly and carefully.

The chronicler of the elven customs has recorded that the practice is conservative by design, for the teaching holds that to name a power without having lived with its effects is to invite neither favour nor misfortune but only the confusion of the young. The elders of each community are charged with the remembering of these names and the teaching of them to the young, and the elders maintain the small books in which the names are recorded. The Forest Mother is honoured in the careful maintenance of the catalogue as the spirit of the wood that the catalogue preserves.

## The Rites of the Forest Year

The elven calendar follows the forest cycle rather than the agricultural year. The rite of the leafing marks the spring renewal and is the principal festival of the faith. The elders gather in the principal clearing of the woodland and sing the old songs that name the spirits of the forest in order, from the canopy trees down to the mosses. The rite of the flowering celebrates the spring ephemerals that carpet the forest floor before the canopy closes above them, and it is a quieter observance attended mainly by women and children. The rite of the fruiting honours the mast - acorns, beechnuts, and other tree seeds that fall in autumn and sustain the winter. The rite of the leaf-fall marks the return of the year's growth to the soil.

A distinctive practice is the presentation of the young to the sapling, performed when a child reaches the age of understanding. The eldest member of the community leads the child to a young tree and asks the Forest Mother to receive the child as a new growth of the wood. The child touches the bark and receives a small token - a leaf pressed between flat stones or a fragment of bark from a fallen branch - that is kept for life. The chronicler records that this practice strengthens the emotional tie between the individual and the forest, and it is cited as one of the reasons elven communities show such remarkable continuity within a single woodland across many generations.

## The Elven Communities and the Stewardship

The management of the forest is itself a form of devotion. The [elves](/content/species/sapient/elves) do not clear land in the manner of the human farmers, and they do not fell living trees for timber except in the most limited circumstances and with formal rites of thanks. They gather fallen wood for fuel and fallen branches for the crafting of tools, and they shape the growth of the forest through selective planting and the careful pruning of valuable trees. The result of this patient stewardship is a woodland that is richer and more diverse than any purely natural forest. A visit to an elven-managed grove reveals a density of life that is difficult to match in untouched areas, for the elves understand that a healthy forest requires both continuity and change, and they manage accordingly.

The chronicler of the woodland records has observed that the elven approach to forestry has no precise equivalent among other peoples. The human foresters of the surrounding lowlands take timber by cutting and replanting, and the dwarven quarries of the mountain edges remove stone in quantity. The elves take only what falls and shape what grows, a method that demands patience and a long view of the landscape. The faith teaches that the forest is a trust, not a possession, and the elves who hold to this teaching maintain their settlements in the wood for centuries without exhausting their surroundings.

## The Sylvan Court and the Forest Governance

The [Sylvan Court](/content/government/local/sylvan-court) is the principal governance body of the elven forest, and its authority extends to the regulation of the woodland and the mediation of disputes between the elves and the surrounding communities. The court is not a kingdom in the manner of the human realms but a council of elders that governs the forest through consensus and tradition, and its authority is based not on military power or territorial control but on the deep connection between the elves and the woodland they inhabit. The Forest Mother is honoured in the Sylvan Court as the spirit of the wood that the court governs, and the authority of the court is the foundation of the elven stewardship of the ancient temperate forests.

The court does not maintain a standing army or a system of fortifications, for the forest itself is the defence of the elven people, and the authority of the court is based on tradition and respect rather than on force. The chronicler of the elven records has noted that the Sylvan Court is the oldest continuous political entity in [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar), and that its stability is largely attributable to the shared interest of the elven and human communities in preserving the boundary. The Forest Mother is honoured in the quiet governance of the court as the spirit of the wood that the court has tended for many centuries.

## The Heartwood Fellowship

The [Heartwood Fellowship](/content/government/local/heartwood-fellowship) is the standing body of the wardens, the beaters, and the trappers who tend the [Aethelar Heartwood](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/aethelar-heartwood) and the surrounding forests of the southern reach of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). The Fellowship is the regulator of the chase, the keeper of the bounds of the forest, and the adjudicator of the disputes that arise among the villagers, the charcoal-burners, and the small lords who hold land within or adjacent to the wood. The Foundation of the Fellowship is ascribed to the accord that the villages of the forest margin made with the lords of the Sylvan Court more than three centuries ago.

The Forest Mother is honoured in the work of the Fellowship as the spirit of the wood that the Fellowship tends. The chief warden and the sworn fellows maintain the bounds of the forest, regulate the chase, and adjudicate the disputes that arise among the foresters and the small lords. The chronicler of the Fellowship records has set down that the Fellowship has been one of the quiet foundations of the prosperity of the forest margin, and the Fellowship is held in high esteem by every magistrate and every forester of the region.

## The Forest Edge and the Human Settlements

The boundary between the elven forest and the human settlements of the lowlands is a zone of considerable interaction. The human woodcutters and farmers of the forest edge trade with the elves for rare woods, medicinal herbs, and crafted goods, and the elves permit the humans to harvest fallen timber and gather surplus mast within the agreed boundaries. The relationship is governed by a body of customary law that has been refined over centuries and that balances elven protection of the deep wood with human need for resources. Breaches of this law - the cutting of protected trees, the overharvesting of medicinal herbs - have been the occasion of dispute and, on occasion, of conflict, but chroniclers record that most disagreements are settled through formal negotiation between the elders of the forest and the local human authorities.

The Forest Mother is honoured in the negotiation as the spirit of the wood that the negotiation serves. The chronicler of the woodland records has set down that the elven approach is one of guarded coexistence, in which the faith's teachings are kept within the community but its practical benefits are shared with neighbouring settlements. The monotheistic missionaries who have visited the elven communities have been received politely but have found little ground for argument, for the elves do not deny the singular light that the missionaries speak of; they simply find it irrelevant to the life of the forest.

## The Ancient Trees and the Old Groves

The greatest expressions of the faith are the ancient trees and the old groves of the central heartwood. The ancient trees are the eldest of the canopy species, oaks and elms and ironwoods that have stood for centuries and perhaps millennia. They are held to be the pillars of the forest and sometimes the dwelling of the Forest Mother herself. The old groves are the untouched remnants of the original woodland, places where the canopy has never been broken and the understory has developed according to its own law. These are the sacred centres of the faith, and the elders of every elven community make pilgrimages to the principal old groves at the turning of the year.

There is no question among the elves that the ancient trees and old groves belong to all their kind in common. The communities of the central heartwood have agreed through long custom that no tree within the principal groves may be felled and no old growth disturbed, and this agreement is considered as fundamental as any law. The chronicler notes that the preservation of these areas has made the Aethelar Heartwood the most extensive and oldest continuous temperate forest in the known world, and that the elves' attachment to the old wood is one of the defining features of their civilisation. The Forest Mother is honoured in the preservation of the ancient trees and the old groves as the spirit of the wood that the preservation protects.

## Significance

The Forest Mother is the principal power of the deep wood, and her observance is the foundation of the [Forest Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/forest-way) and a defining element of the [elven](/content/species/sapient/elves) civilisation. The figure endures because the figure asks nothing of her adherents that contradicts their daily survival, and the figure offers nothing that undermines it. The reverence for the breeding cycles of the forest creatures, the formalisation of sustainable stewardship through religious obligation, and the integration of the elven foresters' knowledge into the everyday practice of the woodland communities have all contributed to the preservation of the ancient temperate forests. The chronicler finds in the Forest Mother one of the most patient and least displaced of the figures of the known world, honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the foresters who depend upon the wood for their livelihood and whose livelihood's continuation depends upon the forest remaining healthy. The figure endures, in the ancient trees and the old groves, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the deep wood.
