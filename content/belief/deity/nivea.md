## The Goddess of the Snow and the Long Winter

Nivea is the principal power of the [Snow Country](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/snow-country), the goddess in whom the snow that blankets the cold interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the high country of the great mountain ranges is embodied. She is the first snowfall of autumn that closes the high passes, the deep snow that buries the meadows of the high pasture, the ice that bridges the chasms between the peaks, and the slow melt of spring that releases the rivers down the valleys. The dwarven high clans and the human communities of the snow-bound valleys invoke her at the first snowfall and at the last melting of the winter, and her festival falls at midwinter. The figure is older than the first high clan and the first refuge hall, and the peoples of the cold interior have honoured her since before the great halls of the mountains were carved from the living rock.

Nivea is austere and exacting, and her observance is among the most earnest of the cold interior year. Highland communities ask her for a snow that lies deep enough to protect the winter stores and shallow enough to release the rivers in the spring, for the survival of the flocks through the long cold, and for the wisdom to read the signs of approaching storms. She is honoured by every act of highland husbandry the cold interior communities perform.

## The Figure and Her Domain

Nivea is conceived as the encompassing presence of the snow and ice. Her domain extends from the highest crests of the cold interior ranges to the snow-bound valleys where human communities maintain their settlements. She is the snow that falls in early autumn and lies upon the meadows until late spring, the ice that seals lakes and streams through the long cold months, the frost that cracks the rock of the highest peaks, and the slow melt that sends rivers down to the valleys when the sun returns. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, invoked by reading the snow and managing the high pasture as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of Nivea is the high peak, the summit that rises above the line of perpetual snow. Sacred peaks are the most prominent of the summits of the cold interior, and pilgrimages to the lesser sacred peaks are among the most arduous observances of the high country.

## The Greater Powers of the Ice

Beneath Nivea are set the other greater powers of the cold interior. [Aero](/content/belief/deity/aero) is the second, god of the high winds and great storms, honoured at the height of the storm season. [Long Night](/content/belief/deity/long-night) is the third, power of the polar darkness. Long Night is not feared but respected, for the teaching holds that the darkness is necessary to the renewal of the land and that those who endure it with patience will be rewarded when the light returns.

Beneath these three are the gods of individual peaks, glaciers, and valleys. The dwarven clans maintain lists of the gods of the principal summits of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) alongside the names of the passes and water sources beneath each peak. A new name is added only when a summit has been known across several generations.

## The Rites of the Long Winter

The religious year of the northern peaks is dominated by the winter, which occupies the greater part of the year. The observance of the winter is bound up with the custom of the long-night gathering, held at the principal hall of the high clan at midwinter. The gathering is a multi-day event during which stories of the past year are told, deeds of the watch are recounted, and the young are taught the old songs that name the spirits of the peaks in order. The gathering is also the occasion for settling disputes between clans and for formalising seasonal movements of flocks.

The brief summer occupies only a fraction of the year, and its rites are correspondingly simple. The rite of the first lambing marks the beginning of the high pastures, conducted at the customary shrine of the valley where flocks are brought to graze. The rite of the last snow falls at the end of summer, when herds are brought down from the high meadows and watchkeepers prepare the passes for the return of winter.

## The Frostwall Clans and the Watch

The [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) are the principal political and military entity of the cold interior, and the clans are inseparable from the faith. The watch is the central duty of the clans. Watchkeepers observe signs in the sky and snow that warn of approaching storms, the movement of enemy parties, and the conditions of the passes. The watch is conducted from high points of the escarpment, and signal fires are the chief means of communication across the broken country. The [Frostwall Fire Signs](/content/language/pseudo/frostwall-fire-signs) are observed with great strictness; failure to maintain signals at customary hours is regarded as a breach of religious obligation.

Watchkeepers are drawn from the warrior class of the clans, but their role extends beyond the martial. They are tasked with reading the weather, and their knowledge of the sky and patterns of storm is considered sacred. Stormwatchers have developed a highly refined understanding of meteorological phenomena, and their predictions are relied upon by lowland communities who have no comparable means of foreseeing gales from the interior.

## The High Pasture and the Flocks

The flocks of the high interior are the economic foundation of the clans and the human communities of the snow valleys, and their care is a central religious duty. The flocks are moved to high pastures at the beginning of summer and brought down before the first snowfall of autumn. The movement is governed by customary law determining the order of the clans, the timing of the crossing of passes, and the distribution of grazing lands.

The faith teaches that the high pasture is the inheritance of the clans and snow-valley communities in common, and no single clan or household has the right to monopolise it. This principle has prevented the kind of territorial conflicts that have destroyed other pastoral communities. The rite of the first lambing is the chief observance connected with the flocks. The first wool and first milk are offered at the shrine, ensuring the fertility of the herd for the coming year.

## The Dwarven Dimension

The dwarven high clans are the principal adherents of the Snow Country, and their observance is closely tied to the [Stone Reading](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/stone-reading). The two faiths are not contradictory; the gods of the ice are the powers of the surface of the cold interior, and the gods of the stone are the powers of the rock beneath the ice. Dwarven communities honour both sets of powers, and the dual observance is considered the mark of the complete dwarf.

The dwarven high clans are the principal builders of the passes and bridges of the cold interior. The road networks connecting remote valleys to the principal halls of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) are largely the product of dwarven engineering, maintained jointly by the clans and by tolls levied upon caravans.

## The North and the Far Cold

The cold interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) is not the only place where Nivea is honoured. The continent of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim) lies beyond the principal seas of the known world, and the long winters of the Nordheim tundra are as exacting as those of the Morvind interior. Human communities of Nordheim have long observed customs resembling those of the high country, though Nivea is not invoked by the same name in every community. The practice of the long-night gathering, the reading of the snow before the spring thaw, and the watch upon the conditions of the high country are customs the two regions refined in parallel.

## The Sacred Peaks and the Forbidden Places

The highest summits of the cold interior are considered the dwelling of the gods in their most immediate form, and the principal peaks are sacred centres of pilgrimage. The pilgrimage to the summit of a sacred peak is arduous and dangerous even in good weather, and several pilgrims have lost their lives attempting the ascent. The pilgrimage is held to be a test of devotion. The forbidden places are the highest peaks where the gods are held to dwell in their most terrible form, and these are not visited at all. The distinction between sacred and forbidden peaks is maintained through longstanding oral tradition.

## Significance

Nivea is the principal power of the snow and the long winter, and her observance is the foundation of the [Snow Country](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/snow-country) and the spiritual inheritance of the cold interior communities. The reverence for the long winter, the formalisation of the watch and the high pasture through religious obligation, and the integration of dwarven stone-reverence and human pastoral customs have contributed to the continuity of the cold interior. Nivea is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the watchkeepers and herders who depend upon the snow for their livelihood, whose continuation depends upon the snow returning each year in its season.
