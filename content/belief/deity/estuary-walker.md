## Overview

Estuary Walker is the second of the greater powers of the [River Mouths](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/river-mouths), the divine of the brackish water where the great rivers of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) meet the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea). Estuary Walker is the slow mixing of the fresh and the salt in the wide mouth of the river, the turning of the tide that pushes salt water upstream and draws it back twice in the day, the silt that settles in the shallows where the two waters mingle, and the fish that move between river and sea according to the season of their spawning. The fisherfolk who operate in the shallows of the estuaries invoke the figure at the turning of the tide. The figure is older than the first weir and the first fish-trap, and the [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga) of the warm southern rivers have honoured the figure since before the first human settlement was founded upon the estuary.

Estuary Walker is patient and observant, and the rites that attend the figure are marked by the same steady quality as the turning of the tide. The figure is invoked at the flood tide when salt water pushes upstream, at the ebb tide when fresh water flows back to the sea, at the setting of the nets in the shallows, and at the drawing of the nets at the turning of the season. Fisherfolk ask for a fair tide, for an abundance of fish in the shallows, and for wisdom to read the patterns of the brackish water. The figure is honoured by every act of stewardship of the estuaries, and the enduring productivity of the estuary fisheries is the clearest evidence of the figure's favour.

## The Figure and Her Domain

Estuary Walker is conceived as the living presence of the brackish water. Her domain extends from the upper reach of the tide to the mouth of the river where fresh water meets the open sea. She is the slow mixing of fresh and salt, the push of salt water upstream at the flood, the draw of fresh water downstream at the ebb, the silt in the shallows, and the fish that move between river and sea. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, invoked by reading the tide and setting the nets as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of Estuary Walker is the estuary itself, the broad mouth of the river where fresh and salt mingle. She is invoked at customary shrines of the estuary, marked places at the principal shore and sandbanks where river and sea communities have long gathered for seasonal rites. The shrines are not built upon; they are understood as natural dwellings of the divine, and rites are conducted at the shore and in the shallow water.

The figure is also invoked in the work of the net-setters, who set nets in the shallows at the turning of the tide. Net-setters are the principal labourers of the estuary communities, and setting the nets is a communal labour performed together at the customary season. They speak the customary words of acknowledgement as each net is set.

## Festival and Observances

The festival of Estuary Walker falls at the height of the fishing season in late summer, when fish are most abundant in the shallows and the tides are most regular. The rites are conducted at the principal shore of the estuary by the elder of the community, who speaks the traditional words asking for a fair tide and an abundance of fish. The late-summer festival is observed with great solemnity in every estuary community.

The rite of the first turning is conducted at the opening of the fishing season, when the elder asks Estuary Walker to grant that the fish enter the estuary in their season and that the nets be set in the customary places. The rite of the last turning follows at the close of autumn, with words of thanksgiving for the catch and the closing of the fishing grounds. These two rites form the pivot of the estuary year.

A distinctive observance is the rite of the closed estuary during the spawning season of the principal fish species, when fishing in the shallows is suspended to protect breeding stock. The closure is enforced by customary law as a religious obligation. Estuary communities that have adhered to the closure have maintained richer fish stocks over generations than those that abandoned it. The closure is not absolute; elders may grant permits for taking certain species during the closure period if the community faces hunger, but such permits are rare.

## The Order of the Greater Powers

Estuary Walker is the second of the greater powers of the [River Mouths](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/river-mouths), her position being the meeting of the river and the sea. [Potamia](/content/belief/deity/potamia) is the first, goddess of the moving water. [Deluge](/content/belief/deity/deluge) is the third, power of the high water that descends in spring. Estuary Walker is the second, divine of the brackish water. The three are acknowledged collectively at seasonal gatherings, but each has her own day of particular observance. The order is fixed by the river-mouth cycle, and Estuary Walker is honoured second because the estuary is the second of the gifts the gods bestow after the river itself.

Beneath the three greater powers are the spirits of individual rivers, tributaries, and principal species of fish. Spirits of the fish are localised to the species most important for the diet of the community. This localisation is both a strength and a source of occasional conflict between neighbouring communities that share an estuary but differ in dominant species.

## The Meeting of the River and the Sea

The estuary is the principal temple of the [River Mouths](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/river-mouths), and the estuaries of the great rivers are gathering points of river communities at the turning of the season. The principal estuaries of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the coastal regions of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) serve as centres of joint observance between river peoples and seafaring communities. The estuary is not built upon; it is the natural dwelling of the divine, and rites are conducted upon the shore and in the shallow water.

The estuary is also the meeting-place of river peoples and seafaring peoples, where the exchange of the harvest of the river for the harvest of the sea takes place. The joint management of the estuary fishing grounds requires river and sea communities to share knowledge about the habits of the fish, the patterns of the tides, and the seasonal movements of species. Estuary Walker is honoured in this cooperation.

The estuaries of the great rivers are among the most productive fishing grounds in the known world. The mixing of fresh and salt water brings nutrients that fish require. The figure is honoured at the principal shore with a customary word of welcome for returning fish and a word of thanks for the catch.

## The Auric and the Stormfall

The estuaries of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) and the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) are the principal dwelling of Estuary Walker. The principal estuaries of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) coast are gathering points of river communities at the turning of the season. The estuaries opening upon the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden) are the busiest of the river-mouths, serving traffic passing between the two oceans.

The figure is also honoured in the small estuaries of tributary rivers, the broad shallow mouths where smaller streams enter the sea. Small estuaries are the principal fishing grounds of coastal villages, and the figure is invoked at customary shrines with the same observance as the great river mouths receive. The small estuaries have been the foundation of the prosperity of coastal villages.

The estuaries of the Auric Ocean and the Stormfall Sea are subject to the rhythms of the tides that the figure governs. Watchkeepers of the estuary communities read the tides as a form of devotion, having developed a body of customary knowledge governing the setting of nets and the drawing of the catch.

## Significance

Estuary Walker is the second of the greater powers of the [River Mouths](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/river-mouths), and the figure's observance is the foundation of the estuary life of the river-mouth communities of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the coastal regions of [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana). The reverence for the breeding cycles of the fish that move between river and sea, the formalisation of the closure of the estuary through religious obligation, and the integration of river and sea knowledge into the everyday practice of the estuary communities have contributed to the prosperity of the river-mouth regions. Estuary Walker is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the net-setters and watchkeepers who depend upon the estuary for their livelihood, whose continuation depends upon the brackish water remaining patient.
