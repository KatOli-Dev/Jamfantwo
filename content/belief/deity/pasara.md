## The Spirit of the Grazing Beasts

Pasara is the second of the greater powers in the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), the spirit of the grazing beasts and of the fertility of the herds that the pastoral peoples of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) depend upon for their livelihood. She is conceived as the spirit that quickens the unborn calf, brings the lamb into the world in early spring, sustains the mare through gestation, and keeps the herd healthy through the lean months of winter. The pastoralists of the southern grasslands and the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the open country invoke her at the birthing of calves and lambs, at the founding of a new herd, and at the seasonal movement of herds between summer pastures and winter shelter. The figure is older than the first herd and the first camp, and the pastoral peoples of the grasslands have honoured her since before the first boundary stone was set upon the open country.

Pasara is patient and abundant, and her observance is among the most enduring of the pastoral year. Pastoral communities ask her for healthy calves, for a fair yield of milk, for the preservation of the herd through winter, and for wisdom to manage the grazing lands without diminishing them. She is honoured by every act of pastoral husbandry the grassland communities perform.

## The Figure and Her Domain

Pasara is conceived as the living presence of the herd animals in their passage across the open country. Her domain extends from the rich pastures of the eastern grasslands to the drier uplands of the western margins. She is the cow that drops the calf in early spring, the ewe that brings forth the lamb in the shelter of the rocks, the mare that foals upon the open grass, and the herd that moves across the country in a single great body under the guidance of the older animals. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, invoked by the management of the grazing and the care of the breeding animals as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of Pasara is the standing stone, the ancient marker that pastoralists have set upon the open country to mark the boundaries of grazing lands and the places where seasonal rites are conducted. The stones are not temples but natural stones marked with customary symbols, maintained by the elders of the kindreds.

## The Greater Spirits of the Plains

Pasara is the second of the greater spirits of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), her position being the honouring of the grazing beasts and the fertility of the herds. [Sky Walker](/content/belief/deity/sky-walker) is the first, spirit of the open heavens and the weather. [Stone Watcher](/content/belief/deity/stone-watcher) is the third, spirit of the standing stones and the buried dead. A fourth is reckoned in some traditions: [River Speaker](/content/belief/deity/river-speaker), spirit of the waters that cut the plains, honoured by kindreds whose grounds lie along the great rivers.

Beneath the greater spirits are a multitude of lesser powers, many local to a single hill, spring, or stand of trees. To know the name of the spirit of a place is to hold a claim upon its favour, and the elders of a kindred are charged with remembering these names and teaching them to the young.

## The Rites of the Pastoral Year

The rites of the pastoral year are bound up with the management of the grazing lands. The seasonal movement of herds is accompanied by offerings at the standing stones marking the old boundaries. The first greening of the grass after the cold months is greeted with a song of thanksgiving to Sky Walker and Pasara alike. Births, marriages, and the passage of the dead are each marked by rites addressed to the appropriate spirit.

A distinctive teaching of the pantheon is the duty of restraint in the use of the plains, founded upon the belief that the spirits lend the grass and the beasts to the people only for their keeping. To overgraze a ground is to offend Pasara and invite the thinning of the herds. The management of grazing by rotation, observed among the centaur kindreds and human pastoralists alike, is defended not merely as good husbandry but as a religious obligation.

## The Centaur Kindreds and the Herd

The [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) are the dominant sapient inhabitants of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains), and their presence has shaped the ecology of the region for centuries. Centaur kindreds move across the plains in a seasonal cycle mirroring the movements of the herds, following the grass as it greens and fades. They do not build permanent settlements; their dwellings are tents of hide and woven grass that can be dismantled and packed within the hour.

The relationship between centaurs and the grasslands is one of active stewardship. Centaurs manage the grazing of wild herds through their own movements and through controlled burning of the grass in the dry season, a practice that rejuvenates the sward and prevents encroachment of woody plants.

## The Plains Cattle and the Principal Herds

The plains cattle, a large humped animal roaming the grasslands in herds of hundreds or thousands, are the most conspicuous of the grazing animals. The cattle move in a constant cycle of grazing and migration, following the growth of grass northward through spring and southward through autumn. Their hooves and dung are essential elements of the grassland ecosystem.

The swift antelope, the shaggy-maned horse, and the smaller creatures of the plains are also within the domain of Pasara, for she is the spirit of all grazing animals of the open country. Centaur kindreds manage herds of wild horses as well as cattle, a particular art refined over many generations.

## The Boar of the Open Country

The [plains thunder boar](/content/species/beasts/plains-thunder-boar) is the largest of the wild swine of the grasslands, a great shaggy beast of formidable temper whose range extends across the plains. The boar is the quarry of the great mounted hunt of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), a ceremonial pursuit conducted according to ancient traditions. The boar is not the creature of Pasara but a being of the wild country that her domain does not encompass, for the boar is solitary and fierce, living apart from the herds.

The hunt of the thunder boar is honoured by centaurs and human pastoralists alike as a test of skill and a ritual of the open country. Pasara is not invoked in the hunt, for the hunt is of the wild country and not of the managed herd.

## The Calving Season and the First Milk

The calving season is the principal observance of Pasara, falling in early spring when the cattle and horses bring forth their young. Pastoralists gather at the customary standing stone of the kindred, and the elder leads the rite of the first calving. The first milk of the season is offered at the stone, a formal acknowledgement that Pasara has granted the fertility of the herd for the year to come.

The first milk is not consumed by the kindred but reserved for the calves and foals. Pastoralists hold that the first milk carries the blessing of Pasara, passed to the young animals through the milk they receive.

## The Movement of the Herds

The seasonal movement of herds is the central practical labour of the pastoral year, governed by a body of customary law administered by the elders. Pastoralists move their herds northward in spring to follow the growth of grass and southward in autumn to escape the winter cold. The movements are conducted in a prescribed order, ensuring the herds do not crowd one another upon the limited grazing lands.

## The Mortality of the Herd

The mortality of the herd is a constant concern, and deaths of animals are marked by rites acknowledging Pasara. Deaths are recorded in small books kept by the elders, and these records are the foundation of herd management through the seasons. Mortality is greatest in winter, when cold and shortage of grass thin the weakest animals. Pastoralists maintain winter shelters at customary grounds, stocked with hay and fodder.

## The Wandering Singers and the Memory

The wandering singers carry the songs of the pantheon between the kindreds, keeping the names of the spirits consistent across the broad expanses of the grasslands. They are the living memory of the faith, and a kindred that has lost its singer is held to be in danger of forgetting the spirits of its own ground. The songs of Pasara are among the most enduring of the plains songs, passed from singer to singer with care.

## Significance

Pasara is the second of the greater powers of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and her observance is the foundation of the pastoral life of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The reverence for the breeding cycles of the herd animals, the formalisation of the management of the grazing lands through religious obligation, and the integration of centaur knowledge into the everyday practice of the pastoral year have contributed to the enduring fertility of the grasslands. Pasara is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the pastoralists who depend upon the herd for their livelihood, whose continuation depends upon the herd remaining healthy.
