## Overview

River-Speaker is the fourth of the greater spirits of the [Old Pantheon of the Plains](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-pantheon-of-the-plains), the spirit of the rivers that thread the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) from the foothills of the mountain spine in the west to the broad valleys that drain toward the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean) in the east. River-Speaker is the moving water of the long rivers, the brown silt that the current carries in the season of the rains, the deep pools where the fish gather in the summer, and the fords where the pastoralists of the open country cross from one grazing ground to another. The kindreds whose grounds lie along the great rivers invoke River-Speaker at the flood and at the drought, at the crossing of the fords, and at the reading of the water, and the chronicler of the plains records has set down that the figure is older than the first ferry and the first fisher's weir, and that the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the river kindreds have honoured the figure since before the first formal confederation of the kindreds beneath the open sky.

The figure of River-Speaker is patient and observant, and the rites that attend the figure are marked by the same steady quality that marks the flow of the long rivers across the grasslands. The figure is invoked at the morning of the first crossing of the season, at the evening of the last fishing of the year, at the rising of the water in the spring, and at the falling of the water in the autumn. The pastoralists do not ask the figure for blessings in the abstract; they ask the figure for clear water, for a steady flow, and for the wisdom to read the signs of the river. The chronicler records that the figure is honoured by every act of stewardship of the long rivers, and that the enduring productivity of the fisheries and the fords of the grasslands is the clearest evidence of the figure's favour.

## The Figure and Her Domain

River-Speaker is conceived as the living presence of the moving water, and the figure's domain extends from the small brooks that gather in the foothills of the western mountains to the broad rivers that the grasslands send eastward to the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean). She is the spring snowmelt that swells the upper tributaries, the brown water of the season of the rains, the deep green of the summer pools, and the long slow current of the autumn when the water has returned to its ordinary depth. The chronicler of the plains records has set down that the figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, and that the figure is invoked by the reading of the water and the management of the fords as much as by any formal rite.

The principal dwelling of River-Speaker is the long rivers themselves, the broad ribbons of water that cross the grasslands from one horizon to the other. The figure is invoked at the customary fords of the rivers, the marked places where the pastoralists of the open country cross from one grazing ground to another. The fords are not built upon in the manner of the bridges of the settled country; they are natural shallow places, and the tending of the fords is a responsibility shared among the kindreds of both banks. River-Speaker is present in the fords as the spirit of the water that the crossings acknowledge, and her presence is renewed each year by the offerings of the first fish and the first wool of the season.

The figure is also invoked in the songs of the ferrymen, who work the small boats that carry the pastoralists and their goods across the deeper reaches of the rivers. The ferrymen are the principal labourers of the river kindreds, and the chronicler records that the rowing of the ferry is a communal labour that the families of the river perform together at the customary season. River-Speaker is present in the songs of the ferrymen as the spirit of the river that the songs acknowledge, and the singers are held to be the human instruments of the figure's care for the long waters. The chronicler notes that the songs are passed from parent to child in a manner similar to the transmission of the faith itself, and the songs preserve the names of the spirits of the pools and the reaches.

## Festival and Observances

The festival of River-Speaker falls at the height of the spring runoff, when the rivers are at their fullest and the fish are running upstream to spawn. The festival is the principal observance of the figure among the river kindreds, and the rites are conducted at the principal ford of each river by the elder of the kindred. The elder stands at the ford and speaks the traditional words, asking River-Speaker to carry the flood gently and to grant a fair catch to the fishers of the community. The chronicler records that the spring festival is observed with great solemnity in every river kindred, and the occasion is one of the most important of the ceremonial events of the pastoral year. River-Speaker's day at the height of the spring runoff is the most universal of the observances of the long rivers.

The rite of the first crossing is conducted at the opening of the grazing season, when the pastoralists lead their herds across the fords to the spring pasture. The elder of the kindred speaks the traditional words, asking River-Speaker to watch over the crossing and to grant that the water remain low enough for the herds to pass in safety. The rite of the last crossing follows at the close of the autumn, when the pastoralists bring their herds back across the fords to the winter shelter. The chronicler notes that the two rites of the ford - the first crossing and the last - form the pivot of the pastoral year along the rivers, and the observance of the rites with strict regularity is held to be the foundation of the continuity of the kindred through the seasons.

A distinctive observance of River-Speaker is the reading of the water, conducted by the watchkeepers of the kindred at the gathering of the spring floods. The watchkeepers are charged with observing the conditions of the rivers and the weather, and the reading of the water is a form of devotion that is practised by every household of the river kindreds. The chronicler records that the watchkeepers of the rivers have developed a refined understanding of the currents and the floods, and that their predictions are relied upon by every pastoralist and every fisher who works upon the long waters. The reading of the water is held to be a form of communion with River-Speaker, and the watchkeepers who have mastered the reading are regarded with a respect that is matched by no other accomplishment of the river life.

## The Order of the Greater Spirits

River-Speaker is the fourth of the greater spirits of the [Old Pantheon of the Plains](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-pantheon-of-the-plains), and the figure's position in the order of the greater spirits is the supplementing of the open country. [Sky-Walker](/content/belief/deity/sky-walker) is the first, the spirit of the open heavens and the weather, the [Herd-Mother](/content/belief/deity/herd-mother) is the second, the spirit of the grazing beasts and the fertility of the herds, and the [Stone-Watcher](/content/belief/deity/stone-watcher) is the third, the spirit of the standing stones and the memory of the ancestors. River-Speaker is the fourth, the spirit of the rivers that cross the grasslands. The four are acknowledged collectively at the seasonal gatherings of the kindreds, but each also has her own day of particular observance. The chronicler records that the order of the four is fixed by the pastoral cycle, and the figure of River-Speaker is honoured last because the rivers are the last of the four gifts that the spirits bestow upon the pastoralists of the open country.

The four greater spirits are understood to be a single order of divine care for the grasslands, and the figures are invoked together at the customary standing stones at the turning of the seasons. Sky-Walker is the open heavens that send the rain, the Herd-Mother is the fertility of the beasts that graze the grass, the Stone-Watcher is the memory of the ancestors whose standing stones mark the boundaries of the land, and River-Speaker is the waters that thread the grasslands from north to south. The chronicler notes that the four figures are honoured in a fixed sequence at the seasonal gatherings, and the sequence is observed with great strictness in every kindred of the open country. The figure of River-Speaker is present in the sequence as the spirit of the rivers that the gathering acknowledges, and her presence is the foundation of the unity of the observance.

Beneath the four greater spirits are counted a multitude of lesser powers, many of them local to a single reach, pool, or ford. The pastoralists hold that to know the name of the spirit of a place is to hold a claim upon its favour, and the elders of a kindred are charged with the remembering of these names and the teaching of them to the young. The chronicler records that the local spirits of the rivers are particularly numerous in the upper reaches, where each tributary and each pool has its own name and its own customary offerings. River-Speaker is present in the lesser powers as the spirit of the water that watches over every reach, and her presence is renewed each year by the offerings of the pastoralists at the customary fords and the customary pools.

## The Centaur Kindreds of the Rivers

The [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) are the principal adherents of River-Speaker, and their observance is adapted to the life of the river kindreds rather than the life of the open herder. The centaur kindreds of the rivers move along the banks in a seasonal cycle that mirrors the rise and the fall of the water, and the centaurs camp at the customary fords and the customary pools where the fishing is best in each season. The chronicler records that the centaurs honour River-Speaker at the customary fords, and that the centaur observance of the figure is conducted with a directness that the chronicler has described as the most attentive piety of the river life.

The centaur songs of the river are among the most refined of the pastoral literature, and the songs preserve the names of the reaches, the patterns of the currents, and the turning of the fishing seasons in a form that the wandering singers carry between the kindreds. The chronicler records that the centaurs are the first of the river peoples to read the flood and the last to leave the bank when the water rises, and that the figure of River-Speaker is honoured in the centaur practice of the river as the spirit of the water that the centaurs serve. The relationship between the centaurs and the human pastoralists of the river is one of quiet cooperation, and the centaur songs are learned by the human fishers and adapted to the local grounds of each kindred.

The figure is also honoured in the practice of the fishing weirs, which the river kindreds maintain at the customary reaches where the fish gather in the season of the run. The weirs are constructed of stakes and woven withes, and the fish are taken in baskets set at the openings of the weir. The chronicler records that the weir-fishing is regulated by the customary law of the river kindreds, and the law sets the seasons of the fishing, the methods that the fishers must use, and the proportion of the catch that must be returned to the water. River-Speaker is present in the weir as the spirit of the river whose bounty the weir gathers, and the fishers are honoured by their communities as the human instruments of the figure's care for the long waters.

## The Fords and the Crossings

The fords of the long rivers are the principal points of exchange between the kindreds of the two banks, and the crossings are sacred to the observance of River-Speaker. The fords are the natural shallow places where the river may be crossed without the aid of a structure, and the custom of the kindreds requires that the ford be maintained by the common labour of the communities on both banks. The chronicler records that the fords have been the foundation of the customary law of the river, and that the maintenance of the crossings is held to be a religious obligation that takes precedence over every private claim. River-Speaker is present in the fords as the spirit of the water that the crossings acknowledge, and the neglect of a ford is regarded as a breach of duty to both the figure and the community.

The chronicler notes that the fords of the grasslands are particularly important during the seasonal movements of the herds, when the pastoralists of the open country must cross from one grazing ground to another. The crossings are conducted at the customary places, and the timing of the crossing is determined by the reading of the water that the watchkeepers provide. The figure of River-Speaker is honoured in the crossings as the spirit of the river whose care the crossings require, and the pastoralists speak the customary words of acknowledgement as they enter the water. The chronicler records that the crossings are occasions of quiet ceremony, and the figure is present in the crossings in a manner that no other observance of the river kindreds quite matches.

The figure is also honoured in the observation of the long rivers, which the elders of the kindreds use to read the rise and the fall of the water and the approaching of the seasons. The rivers change with the season, from the clear low water of the late summer to the brown flood of the spring runoff, and the elders have developed a body of customary knowledge that governs the reading of the rivers and the timing of the crossings. The chronicler notes that the observation of the rivers is a form of devotion that is practised by every elder of the river kindreds, and River-Speaker is honoured in the observation as the spirit of the water that the elders observe. The figure is present in the long rivers in a manner that no other figure of the pastoral year quite matches, and the chronicler notes that the observation of the rivers is among the most attentive of the devotions of the open country.

## Significance

River-Speaker is the fourth of the greater spirits of the [Old Pantheon of the Plains](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-pantheon-of-the-plains), and the figure's observance is the foundation of the river life of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The figure endures because the figure asks nothing of her adherents that contradicts their daily survival, and the figure offers nothing that undermines it. The reverence for the long rivers that thread the grasslands, the formalisation of the stewardship of the fords and the fisheries through religious obligation, and the integration of the household observance into the everyday practice of the pastoral year have all contributed to the enduring productivity of the river kindreds. The chronicler finds in River-Speaker one of the most patient and least displaced of the figures of the known world, honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the ferrymen and the watchkeepers who depend upon the rivers for their livelihood and whose livelihood's continuation depends upon the rivers remaining patient. The figure endures, in the ford and at the reading of the water, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the long waters of the grasslands.
