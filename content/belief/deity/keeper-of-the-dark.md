## Overview

The Dark Keeper is the second of the three greater powers in the [Stone Reading](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/stone-reading), honoured in the unlit workings where no lamp will burn and no torch can find the air to feed it. He is the deep stone, the place beneath the mountain, the gallery that no surface light has ever reached, and the long memory of the rock that endures when the lamps have failed and the sound of the picks has died away. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the holds of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) hold him to dwell within the deep places themselves, and the older songs of the stoneworkers say that the sound of a step in a gallery that no one has entered in living memory is the sound of the figure's slow turning toward the visitor. The Dark Keeper has been honoured since the first workings were driven below the level at which the air will feed a flame, and his cult is the most particular of the three greater powers, for the deep places are not entered by every workman and the rites of the figure are observed only by those who have been taught to read the dark.

## The Figure and the Deep

The Dark Keeper is conceived as the living presence of the deep stone and the gallery no light has reached. His domain extends from the lowest workings of the deepest holds to the sealed chambers not entered at all. He is the cool air that rises from a newly opened gallery, the absolute stillness of a chamber undisturbed for generations, the dripping of water from rock the heat of the workings will never warm, and the long echo when a hammer is struck in a passage whose end is not in sight. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, honoured by the careful reading of the dark as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of the Dark Keeper is the unlit working itself, the gallery in which lamps burn poorly or not at all and the workman must move solely by memory and sound. The unlit working is not set apart from the rest of the mine. The workman knows it by the failure of the lamp and enters only when the work requires it. He speaks customary words in a low voice, a private acknowledgement that the figure is present.

The sealed chamber is the second dwelling, honoured with particular respect. A sealed chamber is a gallery closed at one end by a fall of stone or deliberate walling-up, and it is not entered. The workman does not break the seal or speak aloud in its presence. He passes in silence and leaves a small token as an acknowledgement of the figure's presence within.

## The Unlit Workings

The unlit workings are galleries in which the air will not feed a lamp, entered only by the workman taught to read the dark. The teaching of the dark is among the most demanding of mountain disciplines. The apprentice entrusted with unlit work has spent years in lit workings and has been observed to possess the steadiness and memory required. The master does not teach the reading of the dark as rules but as a way of attending to the place, and the apprentice learns by long practice in the master's company. An apprentice who enters the dark before ready is a danger to himself and the work.

The workman entering the unlit working carries no lamp, only the tools of the trade and the customary token of acknowledgement. He moves by the feel of the wall against the hand, the sound of his own breath, the memory of the gallery, and the small sounds the figure is held to make when the workman has wandered too far. These sounds are not a living voice but the sounds the gallery itself makes: the dripping of a distant spring, the settling of a loosened stone, the rustle of air through an undiscovered crack. They are read as the figure's guidance.

A workman found to have read the dark aright is held in particular respect, and his name is recorded in the hall book as a sign of the figure's favour. One who reads the dark amiss is returned to the lit workings until the master is satisfied the figure has forgiven the failure. The teaching holds that the figure does not easily forgive the workman who has presumed upon the dark.

## The Lost and the Returned

The chronicles of every hold record occasions upon which a workman lost in the deep galleries was returned to the light by the figure's intervention. These accounts are preserved in the hall books of the principal holds. They agree in general form, differing only in particulars, and this agreement is held to be the strongest evidence that the figure is real.

The typical account describes a workman who has wandered beyond the customary bounds of a familiar gallery and found himself in an unrecognised passage. He calls out, and the call is not answered, for the lit workings are too far. He waits, and the figure makes itself known by a small sound he can follow. He follows the sound to a passage he recognises and returns to the lit workings. The workman who is returned speaks of the experience with sobriety, not claiming to have seen or heard the figure but only to have been guided.

The figure is not invoked as a god who can be called upon to act at the request of the petitioner. The figure is honoured as a presence that may be acknowledged and may, in its own time, make itself known. Its guidance is not to be commanded, given only to those who have entered the dark with proper preparation and acknowledgement. The workman who is returned leaves a small piece of worked metal at the place where the figure made itself known.

## The Long Memory

The Dark Keeper is also the power of the long memory, the foundation of the mountain tradition of careful record. The figure is held to remember every gallery opened in the rock and every chamber sealed, and his memory is the model for the memory of the workman. The hall books of the principal holds are kept with care described as the most meticulous of any archives in the known world. A workman entering a gallery not entered for generations enters the figure's memory and leaves a record of the entry in the hall book.

The practice of the long memory is observed most strictly in the deepest holds, where galleries have been driven through stone the surface communities have not seen in living memory. Hall books of the deepest holds contain entries dating to the founding of the holds. The practice distinguishes the mountain stoneworkers from the miners of the lowlands.

## Relation to the Other Powers

The Dark Keeper is the second of the three greater powers, fixed by the order of the work. The [Smith God](/content/belief/deity/smith-god) is honoured at the forge and anvil, and the miner-god is honoured at the first strike. The Dark Keeper is honoured in the galleries the light does not reach, where the work of the miner is finished and the silence of the deep stone begins. The three are honoured in the order of the place, fixed by the labour of the hold.

The Dark Keeper is sometimes invoked together with the miner-god at the first opening of a gallery suspected to be unlit. The miner-god is thanked for the ore, and the Dark Keeper is asked to receive the workman. The Smith God is invoked at the forge and the smelting of the ore the dark has yielded.

## Observance Among the Holds

The observance of the Dark Keeper is most particular in the deepest holds and least particular in shallower workings. The principal holds of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) maintain the full discipline of the cult with the same care. The two realms have exchanged traditions of the figure's honour, contributing to the unity of the cult.

The hall books of the principal holds contain records of unlit workings, accounts of the lost and returned, and tokens left at seals. They are kept by elder masons who are custodians of the figure's honour. They teach apprentices the reading of the dark in the unlit workings themselves.

Human shepherds of the upper slopes have adopted a fragment of the observance, particularly leaving a token at the entrance of a cave they do not enter, a borrowing from dwarven custom acknowledged by both peoples. Elven foresters of the upper forest line have adopted a similar practice, leaving a token at the mouth of a hollow tree, held to be a kindred observance.

## Significance

The Dark Keeper is the second of the three greater powers of the [Stone Reading](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/stone-reading), and his observance is the foundation of the deep workings of the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the mountain spine. The reverence for the sealed chamber, the formalisation of the reading of the dark as a religious discipline, and the integration of the figure's long memory into the hall books have contributed to the unity of the deep workers. The Dark Keeper is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady attention of the workmen who labour in the deep stone, whose livelihood's continuation depends upon the figure's continued presence in the galleries where no surface light has ever reached.
