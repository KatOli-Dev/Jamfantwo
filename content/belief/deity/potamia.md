## Overview

Potamia is the principal deity of the [River Mouths](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/river-mouths), the living presence of the moving water in whom the great rivers of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and their tributaries are embodied. She is the flow from the high country to the sea, the silt that renews the fields, the fish that ascend the rivers to spawn, and the floods that the spring snowmelt sends down the valleys. The river communities of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) lowlands invoke her at the flood and at the drought, and her festival falls at the height of the spring runoff when the rivers are at their fullest and the fish are running upstream to spawn. The figure is older than the first dam and the first ford, and the human communities of the central river valleys inherited her observance from older water-dwelling peoples who had honoured her since before the first human settlement was founded upon the banks.

Potamia is generous and practical, and her observance is among the most useful of the agricultural and fishing year. She is invoked at every crossing, every net, every irrigation channel, and every boat-launching. River communities ask her for gentle floods, for clean water, and for wisdom to manage the rivers that sustain them. She is honoured by every act of river husbandry, and the prosperity of the great river valleys is the clearest evidence of her favour.

## The Figure and Her Domain

Potamia is conceived as the living presence of the water in motion. Her domain extends from the springs and snowfields of the high country to the estuaries where the rivers meet the sea. She is the snowmelt that feeds the upper tributaries, the rain that swells the rivers through the wet season, the silt that the current carries from the highlands to the floodplain, and the fish that ascend the rivers to spawn in the headwaters. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, invoked by reading the water and managing the current as much as by formal rite.

The great rivers of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are each a particular expression of Potamia, and the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael) is the most celebrated of her manifestations. The river communities of the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale) regard the Vael as the principal dwelling of Potamia, and the dykes, channels, and bridges of the valley are maintained as observances of the figure. The water law of the valley, by which the maintenance of dykes and channels takes precedence over every private claim, is understood as the human expression of Potamia's own care for the land.

The flood is the most visible expression of Potamia's power. The annual spring flood spills over the low banks and spreads across the fields of the floodplain, depositing fine silt that renews the fertility of the soil. The flood is not regarded as a danger but as a gift. The careful husbandry of the floodwater is the foundation of the prosperity of the river valleys.

## Festival and Observances

The festival of Potamia falls at the height of the spring runoff, when the rivers are at their fullest and the fish are running upstream to spawn. The rites are conducted at the principal ford of each valley by the elder of the community, who speaks the traditional words asking Potamia to carry the flood gently and to water the fields without destroying them. The words have changed little over generations, and the form of the festival is the same from the upper tributaries to the estuaries.

The rite of the spawning is conducted when the principal fish species begin their run upriver. The fisherfolk attend the rite before the net is set for the season. The elder asks Potamia to grant a fair catch and preserve the breeding stock for the year to come. The harvest of the river is the principal festival, celebrated at the drawing of the nets in late summer, when the community gathers at the riverbank to offer the first fish caught to Potamia, acknowledging that the fish are not the property of the fisher but the gift of the goddess.

A distinctive observance is the rite of the closed waters, a seasonal closure during which fishing is prohibited to protect the breeding stock. The closure is enforced by customary law as a religious obligation. River communities that have adhered to the closure have maintained richer fish stocks over generations. The closure is not absolute; elders may grant permits for taking certain species if the community faces hunger, but such permits are rare, and communities that grant them too freely often find fish stocks depleted.

## The Peoples of the River

The [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga) of the warm southern rivers maintain the oldest continuous form of the observance, and their form of the faith is the model upon which human communities of the central rivers have patterned their practices. The naga are river-dwellers inhabiting the water rather than the banks, and their understanding of the movements of fish and the quality of the water is regarded by human communities with respect and dependence. They observe Potamia at the customary seasons and maintain the closed waters with a strictness human communities have sometimes struggled to match.

The [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa) of the interior waters have adapted the observance to the still waters of the lake and pond, their form of the faith centred on the [Limna](/content/belief/deity/limna) rather than Potamia. Kappa communities are dwellers of the still water, with deep understanding of lake ecosystems. They observe the rite of the breaking of the ice at the first thaw and maintain closed waters to protect lake fish during spawning season.

The [lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians) of the warm wetlands honour Potamia in their own form, their observance centred on the spirit of the marsh at the edge of the river. Lacertian communities are dwellers of the swamp and slow-moving water, and their understanding of wetland ecology is respected by river communities. Their observance is among the oldest of any warm-water peoples, and river communities of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) lowlands have borrowed some customs from lacertian practice.

## The Boatbuilders and the Netmakers

The boatbuilders and netmakers of the river communities are central to the observance of Potamia, for their crafts are the means by which the river's bounty is drawn to shore. Boats take many forms: dugout for the shallows, longboat for open water, barge for cargo, and small ferry craft for crossing. Each boat is blessed by the elder at launching, a formal acknowledgement that the boat is a gift of Potamia and the builder is her instrument. Boatbuilding traditions are closely guarded secrets passed from parent to child.

The nets are blessed at the time of their weaving. The netmaker observes customary words as each knot is tied, believing the care of the maker is passed to the net in the water. The repair of a damaged net or re-caulking of a leaking boat is accompanied by a small rite of renewal, acknowledging that the craft, like the river, is always in motion and always requiring attention.

Crossings of the river are the principal points of exchange between communities on the two banks. Fords and bridges are sacred to the observance. Fords are natural shallow places; bridges are the principal works of the river communities. Both are blessed at their opening, a formal acknowledgement that the crossing is the inheritance of the community and the gift of Potamia.

## The Estuary and the Meeting of the Waters

The estuary is the principal dwelling of Potamia's counterpart, [Estuary Walker](/content/belief/deity/estuary-walker), the god of the brackish water where the river meets the sea. The [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) estuary, the principal mouth of the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael), is the most celebrated. The meeting of fresh and salt water is the most visible sign of Potamia's reach. The estuary is not built upon; it is the natural dwelling of the divine, and rites are conducted upon the shore and in the shallow water.

The estuary is also the meeting-place of river peoples and seafaring peoples, where the harvest of the river is exchanged for the harvest of the sea. The joint management of estuary fishing grounds requires river and sea communities to share knowledge about fish habits, tidal patterns, and seasonal movements. Potamia is honoured in the cooperation of the two communities.

## Significance

Potamia is the principal power of the moving water, and her observance is the foundation of the [River Mouths](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/river-mouths). The reverence for the breeding cycles of the fish, the formalisation of sustainable practice through religious obligation, and the integration of [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga), [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa), and [lacertian](/content/species/sapient/lacertians) knowledge into the everyday practice of the river communities have contributed to the prosperity of the river regions. Potamia is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the fisherfolk and farmers who depend upon the river for their livelihood, whose continuation depends upon the river remaining healthy.
