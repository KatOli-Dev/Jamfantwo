## Overview

Ice-Breaker is the third of the greater powers of the [Old Gods of the Inland Lakes](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-gods-of-the-inland-lakes), the divine of the ice that forms upon the still water of the interior lakes of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) in the cold months and of the breaking of that ice in the spring that releases the lake for the fishing season. Ice-Breaker is the slow accumulation of the frost upon the still surface, the thickening of the clear pane across the shallows, the dull sound of the working of the great plate under the sun, and the first cracks that run from the shore to the centre as the warmth of the spring reaches the water. The lake-shore communities invoke the figure at the closing of the lake in the late autumn and at the breaking of the ice in the early spring, and the chronicler of the lake records has set down that the figure is older than the first ice-house and the first winter fishery, and that the [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa) of the still waters have honoured the figure since before the first human settlement was founded upon the lake-shore.

The figure of Ice-Breaker is patient and exact, and the rites that attend the figure are marked by the same deliberate quality that marks the formation of the ice and the release of the water. The figure is invoked at the first hard frost of the autumn, at the thickening of the ice through the deep winter, at the working of the plate as the sun returns, and at the breaking of the ice that opens the lake to the fishing boats. The lake communities do not ask the figure for blessings in the abstract; they ask the figure for thick ice that will bear the weight of the fishers who cut the channels through the winter, and for a clean break in the spring that releases the lake without damaging the boats that have been laid up upon the shore. The chronicler records that the figure is honoured by every act of lake husbandry through the cold months, and that the prosperity of the inland lake communities is the clearest evidence of the figure's favour.

## The Figure and Her Domain

Ice-Breaker is conceived as the living presence of the ice upon the still water, and the figure's domain extends from the first skim of frost that forms in the sheltered bays of the lake to the great plate of clear ice that covers the entire surface at the depth of the winter. She is the slow accumulation of the cold that thickens the surface by night and by day, the colour that the ice takes as it thickens from the clear black of the new frost to the deep blue of the midwinter plate, the long groaning of the working pane as the temperature changes, and the bright cracks that run from the shore when the sun has done its work. The chronicler of the lake records has set down that the figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, and that the figure is invoked by the observation of the ice and the management of the channels as much as by any formal rite.

The principal dwelling of Ice-Breaker is the lake itself in its winter form, the great plate of ice that covers the still water from the shore to the centre. The figure is invoked at the customary shrines of the lake, the marked places at the principal shore and the principal islands where the lake communities have long gathered to conduct the seasonal rites. The shrines are not built upon in the manner of the temples of the land, for the shrines are understood as natural dwellings of the divine, and the rites are conducted at the shore and upon the ice with the customary observances. Ice-Breaker is present in the shrines as the spirit of the ice that the shrines acknowledge, and her presence is renewed each year by the first breaking of the ice and the reopening of the lake.

The figure is also invoked in the work of the ice-cutters, who maintain the channels that allow the fish to survive the cold months and the villagers to draw water from the lake. The ice-cutters are the principal labourers of the winter lake communities, and the chronicler records that the cutting of the channels is a communal labour that the families of the lake perform together at the customary season. Ice-Breaker is present in the work of the ice-cutters as the spirit of the ice that the cutting serves, and the cutters are held to be the human instruments of the figure's care for the winter lake. The chronicler notes that the work of the ice-cutters is held to be a form of devotion, and the cutters speak the customary words of acknowledgement as each channel is opened.

## Festival and Observances

The festival of Ice-Breaker falls at the breaking of the ice in the spring, when the ice that has covered the lake through the cold months gives way to the warmth of the returning sun and the lake is released for the fishing season. The festival is the principal observance of the figure among the lake communities, and the rites are conducted at the principal shore of each lake by the elder of the community. The elder stands at the shore and speaks the traditional words, asking Ice-Breaker to grant a clean break and to release the lake without harm to the boats that have been laid up. The chronicler records that the festival of the breaking is observed with great solemnity in every lake community, and the occasion is one of the most important of the ceremonial events of the lake year. Ice-Breaker's day at the breaking of the ice is the most universal of the observances of the winter lake.

The rite of the closing is conducted at the first hard frost of the autumn, when the elder of the community stands at the principal shore and speaks the customary words of acknowledgement, asking Ice-Breaker to grant a thick and even ice that will bear the weight of the fishers and the cutters through the cold months. The rite of the first channel is a distinctive observance, performed at the first opening of a channel through the new ice. The elder speaks the traditional words, asking Ice-Breaker to grant that the channel remain open and that the fish beneath the ice survive the cold. The chronicler records that the first channel is a small but significant labour, and the opening of the channel is the formal acknowledgement that the lake has entered its winter form.

A distinctive observance of Ice-Breaker is the reading of the ice, conducted by the watchkeepers of the lake at the changing of the seasons and through the cold months. The watchkeepers are charged with observing the conditions of the ice and the weather, and the reading of the ice is a form of devotion that is practised by every elder of the lake community. The chronicler records that the watchkeepers of the lakes have developed a refined understanding of the formation and the breaking of the ice, and that their predictions are relied upon by every fisher and every cutter who works upon the winter lake. The reading of the ice is held to be a form of communion with Ice-Breaker, and the watchkeepers who have mastered the reading are regarded with a respect that is matched by no other accomplishment of the winter lake life.

## The Order of the Greater Powers

Ice-Breaker is the third of the greater powers of the [Old Gods of the Inland Lakes](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-gods-of-the-inland-lakes), and the figure's position in the order of the greater powers is the closing of the lake cycle. The [Lake-Mother](/content/belief/deity/lake-mother) is the first of the greater powers, governing the still water, the reflection, and the depth, and Marsh-Walker is the second, governing the wetland that borders the lake and the creatures that inhabit the reed and the mud. Ice-Breaker is the third, governing the ice that forms upon the lake in the cold months and the breaking of which in the spring releases the lake for the fishing season. The three are acknowledged collectively at the seasonal gatherings of the lake communities, but each also has her own day of particular observance. The chronicler records that the order of the three is fixed by the lake cycle, and the figure of Ice-Breaker is honoured last because the breaking of the ice is the last of the three gifts that the gods bestow upon the lake communities before the spring.

The three greater powers are understood to be a single order of divine care for the still water, and the figures are invoked together at the customary shrines of the lake and the islands. The Lake-Mother is the still water that mirrors the sky, Marsh-Walker is the wetland that borders the lake and the creatures that inhabit the reed, and Ice-Breaker is the ice that forms upon the lake in the winter and the breaking of which releases the lake for the fishing season. The chronicler notes that the three figures are honoured in a fixed sequence at the seasonal gatherings, and the sequence is observed with great strictness in every lake community. The figure of Ice-Breaker is present in the sequence as the spirit of the ice that the gathering acknowledges, and her presence is the foundation of the unity of the observance.

Beneath the three greater powers are counted the gods of the particular lakes, islands, inlets, and species of fish. The community of each lake maintains the names of its local powers in small books kept by the elder, and the names are updated only when a power has been known across several generations. The chronicler records that the catalogue of local gods is long, with distinct entries for the lake of the eastern valley, the lake of the northern basin, and the smaller ponds and back-channels that feed into the larger waters. Ice-Breaker is present in the local gods as the spirit of the ice that forms upon the local lakes, and her presence is renewed each year by the first breaking of the ice and the reopening of the water.

## The Watch and the Ice-Breaker's Labour

The watch is the central duty of the inland lake communities through the cold months, and the watchkeepers are responsible for monitoring the conditions of the ice and the weather, for maintaining the channels that the ice-cutters have opened, and for warning the communities of the approach of storms or the weakening of the ice. The watch is conducted from the principal shore and the island shrines, and the watchkeepers are often the same elders who conduct the seasonal rites. Ice-Breaker is present in the watch as the spirit of the ice that the watchkeepers observe, and the neglect of the watch is regarded as a breach of duty to both the figure and the community. The chronicler records that the communities that have maintained the watch faithfully over the generations have been more resilient through bad winters, and the figure is present in the watch as the spirit of the ice that the watchkeepers serve.

The labour of the ice-breaker is performed by a dedicated team of workers from the community, and the work is considered a form of service to the [Lake-Mother](/content/belief/deity/lake-mother), for the ice-breaker ensures that the lake remains alive and accessible even in the depths of winter. The ice-breaker is conducted with picks and with saws, and the channels that the workers open are kept clear of new ice through the cold months. The chronicler records that the work is among the most demanding of the communal duties of the lake year, and the workers who perform it are honoured by their communities as the human instruments of the divine care for the winter lake. Ice-Breaker is present in the work as the spirit of the ice that the workers shape, and the figure is honoured in the cutting of each channel as the foundation of the prosperity of the inland lake communities.

The chronicler notes that the watch and the ice-breaker are among the most practical applications of the faith, and the communities that have maintained them faithfully over the generations have been more resilient through bad seasons. The watch is also a source of valuable information for the surrounding farming communities, as the lake communities are often the first to observe the weather patterns that precede the spring rains and the autumn storms. Ice-Breaker is present in the exchange of information as the spirit of the ice that the lake communities observe, and the exchange is the foundation of the cooperation between the lake and the farming communities. The chronicler notes that the cooperation has done much to maintain the unity of the observance across the wide extent of the interior waters.

## The Northern Lakes and the Long Winter

The northern lakes of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim) and the cold interior of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) experience the longest and the most severe of the lake winters, and the observance of Ice-Breaker in the northern communities is conducted with a strictness that the chronicler has described as the most austere of the inland year. The figure is invoked at the customary shrines of the northern lakes, the marked places at the principal shore where the cold-climate communities have long gathered to conduct the seasonal rites. The chronicler records that the northern communities maintain the watch and the ice-breaker through the long winter with a vigilance that has been remarked upon by every chronicler who has visited the northern lakes, and the labour of the ice-cutters is held to be among the most demanding of any of the seasonal labours of the cold interior.

The northern observance of Ice-Breaker is adapted to the long cold, and the communities maintain the channels through the ice with a care that the warmer lake communities do not need to match. The chronicler records that the channels are kept open through the entire winter, and the work of the ice-cutters is renewed each morning as the new ice forms across the cuts of the previous day. Ice-Breaker is present in the work as the spirit of the ice that the cutters shape, and the figure is honoured in the cutting of each morning's channels as the foundation of the survival of the fish and the village through the long cold. The chronicler notes that the northern communities that have maintained this practice faithfully have been more resilient through the bad winters than those that have allowed the channels to freeze over, and the prosperity of the northern lake fisheries is the clearest evidence of the figure's favour.

The figure is also honoured in the observation of the light upon the ice, which the elders of the northern communities use to read the thickening of the plate and the approaching of the breaking. The light upon the ice changes with the season, from the pale gleam of the new frost to the deep blue of the thick midwinter plate to the bright cracks that run from the shore when the sun has done its work. The chronicler records that the observation of the light is a form of devotion that is practised by every elder of the northern lakes, and Ice-Breaker is honoured in the observation as the spirit of the ice that the light reveals. The figure is present in the long winter light in a manner that no other figure of the lake year quite matches, and the chronicler notes that the observation of the light is among the most attentive of the devotions of the cold interior.

## Significance

Ice-Breaker is the third of the greater powers of the [Old Gods of the Inland Lakes](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-gods-of-the-inland-lakes), and the figure's observance is the foundation of the winter life of the inland lake communities of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the cold lakes of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim). The figure endures because the figure asks nothing of her adherents that contradicts their daily survival, and the figure offers nothing that undermines it. The reverence for the patience that the long winter requires, the formalisation of the watch and the ice-breaker through religious obligation, and the integration of the household observance into the everyday practice of the cold months have all contributed to the prosperity of the inland lake regions of the known world. The chronicler finds in Ice-Breaker one of the most patient and least displaced of the figures of the known world, honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the ice-cutters and the watchkeepers who depend upon the ice for their survival and whose survival's continuation depends upon the ice remaining patient. The figure endures, in the long cold and at the breaking of the season, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the still water and the ice that covers it.
