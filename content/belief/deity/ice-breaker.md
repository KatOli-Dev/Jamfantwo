## Overview

Ice Breaker is the third of the greater powers of the [Lake Watchers' Path](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/lake-watchers-path), the divine of the ice that forms upon the still water of the interior lakes of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) in the cold months and of the breaking of that ice in the spring that releases the lake for the fishing season. Ice Breaker is the slow accumulation of frost upon the still surface, the thickening of the clear pane across the shallows, the dull sound of the working of the great plate under the sun, and the first cracks that run from the shore to the centre as spring warmth reaches the water. The lake-shore communities invoke the figure at the closing of the lake in late autumn and at the breaking of the ice in early spring. The figure is older than the first ice-house and the first winter fishery, and the [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa) of the still waters have honoured the figure since before the first human settlement was founded upon the lake-shore.

Ice Breaker is patient and exact, and the rites attending the figure are marked by the same deliberate quality as the formation of the ice and the release of the water. The figure is invoked at the first hard frost of autumn, at the thickening of the ice through deep winter, at the working of the plate as the sun returns, and at the breaking of the ice that opens the lake to fishing boats. Lake communities ask for thick ice that will bear the weight of fishers cutting channels through the winter, and for a clean break in the spring that releases the lake without damaging the boats laid up upon the shore. The figure is honoured by every act of lake husbandry through the cold months.

## The Figure and Her Domain

Ice Breaker is conceived as the living presence of the ice upon the still water. Her domain extends from the first skim of frost in sheltered bays to the great plate of clear ice covering the entire surface at the depth of winter. She is the slow accumulation of cold that thickens the surface night and day, the colour the ice takes as it thickens from clear black of new frost to deep blue of the midwinter plate, the long groaning of the working pane as temperature changes, and the bright cracks running from the shore when the sun has done its work. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, invoked by observation of the ice and management of the channels as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of Ice Breaker is the lake in its winter form, the great plate of ice covering the still water. She is invoked at customary shrines of the lake, marked places at the principal shore and islands where lake communities gather for seasonal rites. The shrines are not built upon; they are natural dwellings of the divine, and rites are conducted at the shore and upon the ice. Her presence is renewed each year by the first breaking of the ice.

The figure is also invoked in the work of the ice-cutters, who maintain channels allowing fish to survive the cold months and villagers to draw water. Cutting the channels is a communal labour performed together at the customary season. Ice-cutters speak customary words of acknowledgement as each channel is opened.

## Festival and Observances

The festival of Ice Breaker falls at the breaking of the ice in spring, when the ice that has covered the lake through the cold months gives way to the warmth of the returning sun and the lake is released for the fishing season. The rites are conducted at the principal shore by the elder, who speaks the traditional words asking for a clean break that releases the lake without harming the boats. The festival is observed with great solemnity in every lake community.

The rite of the closing is conducted at the first hard frost of autumn, when the elder asks for thick and even ice that will bear the weight of fishers and cutters through the cold months. The rite of the first channel is a distinctive observance performed at the first opening of a channel through the new ice. The elder speaks the traditional words, asking Ice Breaker to grant that the channel remain open and that the fish beneath the ice survive the cold.

A distinctive observance is the reading of the ice, conducted by watchkeepers at the changing of the seasons and through the cold months. Watchkeepers observe the conditions of the ice and weather, a form of devotion practised by every elder of the lake community. They have developed a refined understanding of the formation and breaking of the ice, and their predictions are relied upon by every fisher and cutter who works upon the winter lake.

## The Order of the Greater Powers

Ice Breaker is the third of the greater powers of the [Lake Watchers' Path](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/lake-watchers-path), her position being the closing of the lake cycle. [Limna](/content/belief/deity/limna) is the first, governing the still water, the reflection, and the depth. [Quagmire](/content/belief/deity/quagmire) is the second, governing the wetland bordering the lake. Ice Breaker is the third, governing the ice that forms and breaks in the spring. The three are acknowledged collectively at seasonal gatherings, but each has her own day of particular observance. The order is fixed by the lake cycle, and Ice Breaker is honoured last because the breaking of the ice is the last of the three gifts the gods bestow before spring.

Beneath the three greater powers are the gods of particular lakes, islands, inlets, and species of fish. Each lake community maintains the names of its local powers in small books kept by the elder, updated only when a power has been known across several generations.

## The Watch and the Ice Breaker's Labour

The watch is the central duty of inland lake communities through the cold months. Watchkeepers monitor the conditions of the ice and weather, maintain the channels the ice-cutters have opened, and warn communities of approaching storms or weakening ice. The watch is conducted from the principal shore and island shrines. Watchkeepers are often the same elders who conduct the seasonal rites. The neglect of the watch is regarded as a breach of duty to both the figure and the community.

The labour of the ice-breaker is performed by a dedicated team of workers from the community, considered a form of service to the [Limna](/content/belief/deity/limna). The ice-breaker ensures the lake remains alive and accessible even in the depths of winter, conducted with picks and saws. The channels are kept clear of new ice through the cold months. The work is among the most demanding of the communal duties of the lake year.

The watch and the ice-breaker are among the most practical applications of the faith. Communities that have maintained them faithfully have been more resilient through bad seasons. The watch is also a source of valuable information for surrounding farming communities, as lake communities are often the first to observe weather patterns preceding spring rains and autumn storms.

## The Northern Lakes and the Long Winter

The northern lakes of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim) and the cold interior of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) experience the longest and most severe lake winters, and the observance of Ice Breaker in northern communities is conducted with the strictness of the inland year. Northern communities maintain the watch and the ice-breaker through the long winter with vigilance. The labour of the ice-cutters is among the most demanding of the seasonal labours of the cold interior.

The northern observance is adapted to the long cold. Channels are kept open through the entire winter, and the work of the ice-cutters is renewed each morning as new ice forms across the cuts of the previous day. Northern communities that have maintained this practice faithfully have been more resilient through bad winters.

The figure is also honoured in the observation of the light upon the ice, which elders of northern communities use to read the thickening of the plate and the approaching of the breaking. The light upon the ice changes with the season, from the pale gleam of new frost to the deep blue of thick midwinter plate to bright cracks running from the shore when the sun has done its work.

## Significance

Ice Breaker is the third of the greater powers of the [Lake Watchers' Path](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/lake-watchers-path), and the figure's observance is the foundation of the winter life of the inland lake communities of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the cold lakes of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim). The reverence for the patience that the long winter requires, the formalisation of the watch and the ice-breaker through religious obligation, and the integration of the household observance into the everyday practice of the cold months have contributed to the prosperity of the inland lake regions. Ice Breaker is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the ice-cutters and watchkeepers who depend upon the ice for their survival, whose continuation depends upon the ice remaining patient.
