## Overview

The River-Mother is the principal deity of the [Faith of the River Mouths](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/faith-of-the-river-mouths), the living presence of the moving water in whom the great rivers of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and their tributaries are embodied. She is the flow from the high country to the sea, the silt that renews the fields, the fish that ascend the rivers to spawn, and the floods that the spring snowmelt sends down the valleys. The river communities of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) lowlands invoke her at the flood and at the drought, and her festival falls at the height of the spring runoff when the rivers are at their fullest and the fish are running upstream to spawn. The chronicler of the river records has set down that the figure is older than the first dam and the first ford, and that the human communities of the central river valleys inherited her observance from the older water-dwelling peoples who had honoured her since before the first human settlement was founded upon the banks.

The figure of the River-Mother is generous and practical, and her observance is among the most useful of the agricultural and the fishing year. The figure is invoked at every crossing, at every net, at every irrigation channel, and at every boat-launching, and the rites that are conducted in her honour are marked by a directness that the chronicler of the river customs has described as the most practical piety he has witnessed. The river communities do not ask her for blessings in the abstract; they ask her for gentle floods, for clean water, and for the wisdom to manage the rivers that sustain them. The chronicler records that the figure is honoured by every act of river husbandry, and that the prosperity of the great river valleys is the clearest evidence of the figure's favour.

## The Figure and Her Domain

The River-Mother is conceived as the living presence of the water in motion, and her domain extends from the springs and the snowfields of the high country to the estuaries where the rivers meet the sea. She is the snowmelt that feeds the upper tributaries, the rain that swells the rivers through the wet season, the silt that the current carries from the highlands to the floodplain, and the fish that ascend the rivers to spawn in the headwaters. The chronicler of the river records has set down that the figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, and that the figure is invoked by the reading of the water and the management of the current as much as by any formal rite.

The great rivers of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are each understood to be a particular expression of the River-Mother, and the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael) - the greatest waterway of the continent - is the most celebrated of her manifestations. The river communities of the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale) regard the Vael as the principal dwelling of the River-Mother, and the chronicler records that the dykes, the channels, and the bridges of the valley are maintained as observances of the figure. The custom of the water law of the valley, by which the maintenance of the dykes and the channels takes precedence over every private claim, is understood to be the human expression of the River-Mother's own care for the land.

The flood is the most visible expression of the River-Mother's power, and the annual flood of the spring is the event that the river communities regard as the figure's gift to the lowland. The floodwater spills over the low banks of the river and spreads across the fields of the floodplain, depositing a layer of fine silt that renews the fertility of the soil. The chronicler records that the flood is not regarded by the valley communities as a danger but as a gift, and the customary law of the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale) gives the maintenance of the dykes, the channels, and the bridges precedence over every private claim. The chronicler notes that the figure is honoured in the management of the flood as much as in any formal rite, and that the careful husbandry of the floodwater is the foundation of the prosperity of the river valleys.

## Festival and Observances

The festival of the River-Mother falls at the height of the spring runoff, when the rivers are at their fullest and the fish are running upstream to spawn. The festival is the principal observance of the river communities, and the rites are conducted at the principal ford of each valley by the elder of the community. The elder stands at the ford and speaks the traditional words, asking the River-Mother to carry the flood gently and to water the fields without destroying them. The chronicler records that the words have changed little over the generations, and that the form of the festival is the same in every river community from the upper tributaries to the estuaries of the coast.

The rite of the spawning is conducted when the principal fish species begin their run upriver, and it is the duty of the fisherfolk of the community to attend the rite before the net is set for the season. The elder speaks the traditional words of acknowledgement, asking the River-Mother to grant a fair catch and to preserve the breeding stock for the year to come. The harvest of the river is the principal festival, celebrated at the drawing of the nets in the late summer, when the community gathers at the riverbank to offer the first fish caught at the season to the River-Mother. The offering is a formal acknowledgement that the fish are not the property of the fisher but the gift of the River-Mother, lent for the keeping of the community.

A distinctive observance is the rite of the closed waters, a seasonal closure period during which fishing is prohibited to protect the breeding stock of the principal species. The closure is enforced by the customary law of the river communities as a religious obligation, and the chronicler records that the river communities that have adhered to the closure have maintained richer fish stocks over generations than those that have abandoned it. The closure is not absolute, for the elders may grant permits for the taking of fish from certain species during the closure period if the community faces hunger, but the granting of such permits is rare, and the chronicler notes that communities who grant permits too freely often find the fish stocks depleted in subsequent years.

## The Peoples of the River

The [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga) of the warm southern rivers maintain the oldest continuous form of the observance, and their form of the faith is the model upon which the human communities of the central rivers have patterned their own practices. The naga are themselves river-dwellers, inhabiting the water rather than the banks, and their understanding of the movements of the fish and the quality of the water is regarded by the human communities with a mixture of respect and dependence. The chronicler records that the naga observe the River-Mother at the customary seasons of the warm river, and that they maintain the closed waters with a strictness that the human communities of the north have sometimes struggled to match.

The [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa) of the interior waters have adapted the observance to the still waters of the lake and the pond, and their form of the faith is centred on the [Lake-Mother](/content/belief/deity/lake-mother) rather than the River-Mother. The kappa communities are dwellers of the still water, and their understanding of the lake ecosystems is deep, though the chronicler notes that their knowledge is often inaccessible to the human communities who live on the shores and do not share the kappa's ability to move freely beneath the surface. The kappa observe the rite of the breaking of the ice at the first thaw of the spring, and they maintain the closed waters to protect the fish of the lake during the spawning season. The chronicler records that the kappa observance of the River-Mother is the same in principle as the human observance, though adapted to a different medium, and the two communities have maintained a quiet cooperation through the generations.

The [lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians) of the warm wetlands honour the River-Mother in their own form, and their observance is centred on the spirit of the marsh at the edge of the river. The lacertian communities are dwellers of the swamp and the slow-moving water, and their understanding of the wetland ecology is regarded by the river communities with a respect that the chronicler has recorded as the foundation of the cooperation between the two peoples. The lacertians observe the River-Mother at the customary seasons of the warm wetland, and they maintain the closed waters to protect the breeding grounds of the species that share the marsh and the river. The chronicler notes that the lacertian observance is among the oldest of any of the warm-water peoples, and the river communities of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) lowlands have borrowed some of their customs from the lacertian practice.

## The Boatbuilders and the Netmakers

The boatbuilders and the netmakers of the river communities are central to the observance of the River-Mother, for their crafts are the means by which the river's bounty is drawn to shore. The boats of the river communities take many forms: the dugout for the shallows, the longboat for the open water, the barge for carrying cargo, and the small ferry craft for crossing. Each boat is blessed by the elder of the community at the launching, and the blessing is a formal acknowledgement that the boat is a gift of the River-Mother and that the builder is her instrument. The chronicler records that the boatbuilding traditions are closely guarded secrets of the families who practise them, and the knowledge is passed from parent to child in a manner similar to the transmission of the observance itself.

The nets are blessed at the time of their weaving as well, and the netmaker is required to observe the customary words as each knot is tied, believing that the care of the maker is passed to the net in the water. The boats and nets are maintained through the seasons, and the repair of a damaged net or the re-caulking of a leaking boat is accompanied by a small rite of renewal, acknowledging that the craft, like the river, is always in motion and always requiring attention. The chronicler notes that the boatbuilders and the netmakers are honoured by their communities as the human instruments of the River-Mother, and their crafts are regarded as a form of devotion.

The crossings of the river are the principal points of exchange between the communities on the two banks, and the fords and the bridges are sacred to the observance. The fords are the natural shallow places where the river may be crossed without the aid of a structure, and the bridges are the principal works of the river communities, built to carry traffic across the wider reaches of the main channel. Both the ford and the bridge are blessed at their opening by the elder of the community, and the blessing is the formal acknowledgement that the crossing is the inheritance of the community and the gift of the River-Mother. The chronicler records that the maintenance of the fords and the bridges has long been the common labour of the communities on both banks, and the chronicler notes that this common labour is the foundation of the customary law of the river.

## The Estuary and the Meeting of the Waters

The estuary is the principal dwelling of the River-Mother's counterpart, the [Estuary-Walker](/content/belief/deity/estuary-walker), the god of the brackish water where the river meets the sea, and the estuaries of the great rivers are the gathering points of the river communities at the turning of the season. The [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) estuary, the principal mouth of the [River Vael](/content/location/natural/feature/river-vael), is the most celebrated of the estuaries, and the chronicler records that the meeting of the fresh and the salt water is the most visible sign of the River-Mother's reach. The estuary is not built upon in the manner of the temples of the land, for it is the natural dwelling of the divine, and the rites are conducted upon the shore and in the shallow water of the estuary with the customary observances.

The estuary is also the meeting-place of the river peoples and the seafaring peoples, and the exchange that takes place there is the foundation of the wider trade of the region. The fisherfolk of the river meet the fisherfolk of the sea at the estuary, and the meeting is the occasion of the exchange of the harvest of the river for the harvest of the sea. The chronicler observes that the joint management of the estuary fishing grounds is one of the most practical applications of the observance, for it requires the river communities and the sea communities to share knowledge about the habits of the fish, the patterns of the tides, and the seasonal movements of the species that inhabit both the fresh and the salt water. The River-Mother is honoured in the cooperation of the two communities, and the prosperity of the estuary fisheries is the clearest evidence of the figure's favour.

## Significance

The River-Mother is the principal power of the moving water, and her observance is the foundation of the [Faith of the River Mouths](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/faith-of-the-river-mouths). The figure endures because the figure asks nothing of her adherents that contradicts their daily survival, and the figure offers nothing that undermines it. The reverence for the breeding cycles of the fish, the formalisation of sustainable practice through religious obligation, and the integration of the [naga](/content/species/sapient/naga), the [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa), and the [lacertians](/content/species/sapient/lacertians) knowledge into the everyday practice of the river communities have all contributed to the prosperity of the river regions of the known world. The chronicler finds in the River-Mother one of the most practical and least displaced of the figures of the known world, honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the fisherfolk and the farmer who depend upon the river for their livelihood and whose livelihood's continuation depends upon the river remaining healthy. The figure endures, in the river and at the estuary, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the moving water.
