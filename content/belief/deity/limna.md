## Overview

The Limna is the principal deity of the [Lake Watchers' Path](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/lake-watchers-path), the goddess in whom the still water, the reflection, and the depth of the interior lakes of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are embodied. She is the calm surface that mirrors the sky, the dark depth that holds the secret of the lake, the still water that reflects the evening light, and the cold water that the winter ice seals and the spring thaw releases. The lake-shore communities of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) interior invoke her at the calm of the evening when the lake is at its stillest, and her festival falls at the breaking of the ice in the spring, when the lake becomes accessible to the fisherfolk. The figure is older than the first net and the first shore-dwelling, and the [anurans](/content/species/sapient/anurans) of the marshes and the [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa) of the still waters have honoured her since before the first human settlement was founded upon the lake-shore.

The Limna is patient and reflective, and her observance is among the most intimate of the inland year. She is invoked at every cast of the net, every launch of the boat, every quiet evening upon the shore, and every breaking of the ice. Lake communities ask her for clear water, for the preservation of the fish stocks, and for wisdom to manage the lakes that sustain them. She is honoured by every act of lake husbandry, and the prosperity of the inland lake communities is the clearest evidence of her favour.

## The Figure and Her Domain

The Limna is conceived as the living presence of the still water. Her domain extends from the smallest pond of the marshes to the greatest of the inland lakes of the interior. She is the calm surface that mirrors the sky, the dark depth that holds the secret of the lake, and the cold water that the winter ice seals and the spring thaw releases. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, invoked by the observation of the water and the management of the fishery as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of the Limna is the lake itself, and the islands of the great inland lakes are the sacred centres of her observance. The islands are not built upon; they are natural dwellings of the divine, and rites are conducted upon the shore and the interior of the islands. The lake islands have long been centres of learning and cultural exchange among the lake communities, and the gathering of communities on the islands has preserved the unity of the observance across the interior waters.

The figure is also honoured in the sacred coves along the lake-shore, sheltered places where rites requiring quiet water are conducted. The coves are held in common by the lake communities, and their maintenance is the responsibility of all the lake peoples regardless of species or origin.

## Festival and Observances

The festival of the Limna falls at the breaking of the ice in spring, when the lake becomes accessible to the fisherfolk and communities resume their full seasonal activities. The elder stands at the shore and speaks the traditional words, asking the Limna to receive the community back into her care after the long winter. The festival is observed with great solemnity in every lake community.

The rite of the first catch is a distinctive observance performed at the first fishing of each season. A young fisher is presented to the lake by the elder, who asks the Limna to receive the candidate as a child of the water and to grant safe passage. The young fisher touches the surface and receives a small token -- a piece of polished lake stone, the scale of the first fish caught, or a waterfowl feather -- kept for life. This practice strengthens the emotional bond between the young and the lake, and communities that observe it report lower rates of accidental drownings among the young.

The rite of the spawning follows the return of the fish to the inlet streams, conducted before the nets are set for the season. The elder asks the Limna to grant a fair catch and preserve the breeding stock. The harvest of the lake is celebrated in late summer when fish stocks are at their peak. The first fish of the catch is offered at the customary shrine, a formal acknowledgement that the fish are not the property of the fisher but the gift of the Limna.

## The Peoples of the Lake

The [anurans](/content/species/sapient/anurans) of the marshes are the principal non-human adherents of the Limna, their observance adapted to the wetland environment bordering the lake. Anuran communities dwell in the marsh and still water, and the marsh is held to be their inheritance. They honour the figure at the customary seasons and offer the first fish of the catch at the customary shrine. The marsh is understood as a shared space in which each people occupies its own niche.

The [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa) of the still waters are also adherents of the Limna, their observance adapted to the life of the deep water. Kappa communities dwell in the lake and pond, and their understanding of the lake ecosystem is deep, particularly knowledge of fish species and winter conditions often hidden from shore-dwellers. They observe the breaking of the ice at the first thaw and maintain customary quotas governing fishing during the spawning season. Kappa knowledge of the lake is often inaccessible to human communities, and they are sometimes reluctant to share it given the history of exploitation by shore-dwelling peoples.

The human lake-shore communities of the [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) interior are the third of the principal adherents. Their observance is more formal than the anuran or kappa observance, with a body of customary law governing the management of the fishery, maintenance of shrines, and conduct of seasonal rites.

## The Watch and the Ice Breaker

The watch is the central duty of the inland lake communities. Watchkeepers monitor the conditions of the water and relay warnings about storms, dangerous ice, and the movement of waterfowl. The watch is conducted from the principal shore and island shrines, often by the same elders who conduct the seasonal rites. The neglect of the watch is regarded as a breach of duty to both the figure and the community.

The ice-breaker is the duty of keeping channels open through the ice during winter, allowing fish to survive the cold months and villagers to draw water. Performed by a dedicated team, the work is considered a form of service to the Limna, ensuring the lake remains alive and accessible even in the depths of winter. It is among the most demanding of the communal duties of the lake year.

The watch and the ice-breaker are among the most practical applications of the observance. Communities that have maintained them faithfully have been more resilient through bad seasons. The watch is also a source of valuable information for surrounding farming communities, as lake communities are often the first to observe weather patterns preceding spring rains and autumn storms.

## The Sacred Coves and the Lake Islands

The lake islands of the great inland lakes of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) are the principal sacred centres of the observance, serving as gathering points for lake communities at the turning of the year. The islands are not built upon but understood as natural dwellings of the divine. They have long been centres of learning and cultural exchange, and the gathering of communities on the islands has preserved the unity of the observance.

The sacred coves are sheltered places along the lake-shore where rites requiring quiet water are conducted. Held in common by the lake communities, the coves are places where the young are presented to the lake in the rite of the first catch and where elders speak the customary words of blessing for the new season. They are among the most peaceful places in the interior of the continent.

The island shrines are maintained by the communities in common, considered the responsibility of all the lake peoples regardless of species or origin. The teaching holds that the island is the dwelling of the divine in the still water, and lake communities are required to honour it with customary observances.

## Significance

The Limna is the principal power of the still water, and her observance is the foundation of the [Lake Watchers' Path](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/lake-watchers-path). The reverence for the breeding cycles of the fish, the formalisation of sustainable practice through religious obligation, and the integration of the [anurans](/content/species/sapient/anurans) and the [kappa](/content/species/sapient/kappa) knowledge into the everyday practice of the lake communities have contributed to the prosperity of the inland lake regions of the known world. The Limna is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the lake-fishers and farmers who depend upon the lake for their livelihood, whose continuation depends upon the lake remaining healthy.
