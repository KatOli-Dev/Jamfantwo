## God of the High Winds

Aero is the second of the greater powers in the [Snow Country](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/snow-country), the god of the high winds and great storms that descend from the peaks of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind). He is conceived as the spirit of the gale that rises from the snows of the highest crests, the long storm that scourges the upper slopes, and the sudden squall that catches travellers on the high passes. The dwarven high clans and human communities of the snow-bound valleys invoke him at the height of the storm season, and his festival falls during the longest gales, when watchkeepers sound alarms and communities secure shelter. The figure is older than the first refuge hall and the first watch fire, and highland communities have honoured him since before the first great road was cut through the passes.

Aero is honoured with a mixture of reverence and caution. The storms are both necessary to the fertility of the high country and dangerous to the communities within their reach. The winds that descend from the peaks carry the snow that protects winter stores and the melt that fills rivers in the spring, but the same winds can flatten flocks and seal passes for weeks.

## The Figure and His Domain

Aero is conceived as the living presence of the wind across the cold interior. His domain extends from the highest crests to the sheltered valleys where highland communities maintain settlements. He is the gale that rises in the afternoon and dies with the night, the long storm that rages for days followed by a stillness regarded with caution, and the sudden squall that descends from a clear sky when conditions change. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, invoked by reading the wind and managing the high country as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of Aero is the high pass, the opening in the range where wind gathers force and communities are most exposed. Watchkeepers maintain signal fires and refuge halls that protect travellers crossing the passes during storm season. The figure's presence is renewed each year by the autumn and spring storms.

## The Greater Powers of the Cold Interior

Aero is the second of the greater powers of the [Snow Country](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/snow-country). [Nivea](/content/belief/deity/nivea) is the first, goddess of the snow that blankets the peaks and the ice that bridges chasms. [Long Night](/content/belief/deity/long-night) is the third, power of the polar darkness that holds the interior in unbroken night during deep winter. Beneath these three are the gods of individual peaks, glaciers, and valleys. The dwarven clans maintain lists of the gods of the principal summits of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) alongside the names of the passes and water sources beneath each peak.

The order of the greater powers is fixed by the conditions of the high country. Aero is honoured second because the wind is the second force the highland communities must master after the snow. Nivea provides the snow that the wind moves, Aero provides the wind that the snow obeys, and Long Night provides the darkness that the storm inhabits.

## The Rites of the Storm Season

The storm season occupies autumn and spring, and Aero's rites are bound up with the practical labour of the watch. Highland communities maintain watch fires that signal the state of the passes. Stormwatchers have developed a refined understanding of meteorological phenomena, and their predictions are often relied upon by lowland communities who have no comparable means of foreseeing gales from the interior.

The principal observance of Aero falls at the height of the storm season, when watchkeepers gather at the principal hall of the high clan to renew the customary words of acknowledgement. The elder speaks the traditional words, asking Aero to spare the season and flocks and to grant wisdom to read the signs of approaching gales. The words have changed little over generations, and the form of the observance is the same in every high clan from the western marches to the eastern ranges.

## The Watch and the Storm Signs

The watch is the central duty of the high country. Watchkeepers are charged with observing signs in the sky and wind that warn of approaching storms. The watch is conducted from high points of the escarpment, and signal fires are the chief means of communication across the broken country. The [Frostwall Fire Signs](/content/language/pseudo/frostwall-fire-signs) are observed with great strictness; failure to maintain signals at customary hours is regarded as a breach of religious obligation.

Watchkeepers are drawn from the warrior class of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans), but their role extends beyond the martial. They are tasked with reading the weather, and their knowledge of sky and storm patterns is considered sacred. Stormwatchers are trained from youth in reading the wind, and the most experienced are held in particular esteem for the accuracy of their predictions.

## The Long Storms and the Refuge Halls

Long storms can last for days. Highland communities maintain refuge halls at principal points of the high country where travellers and herders can shelter. The halls are stocked with dried meat, peat, and spare timber, maintained in accordance with customary law established at the founding of the watch. The refuge halls are among the most useful works of the high country, having saved the lives of many who would otherwise have been lost to the gales.

Aero is honoured in the refuge halls as the spirit of the wind that the halls protect against. The halls are not built as temples of the lowland faiths; they are practical structures whose purpose is saving life, but they are regarded with the same reverence that lowland temples inspire.

## The North and the Wind of the Tundra

Aero is also honoured in the cold countries of the far north. The continent of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim) lies beyond the principal seas of the known world, and the winds that sweep across its tundra are as exacting as those of the Morvind interior. Human communities of Nordheim have long observed customs resembling those of the high country, though Aero is not invoked by the same name in every community. The practice of the watch, the reading of the wind before the spring thaw, and the maintenance of shelters are customs that the two regions refined in parallel.

The cold interior and the far north share a respect for the long storms and a recognition of the wind's necessity to the renewal of the land. Aero is honoured in both regions as the spirit of the wind that scours snow from the slopes and carries spring warmth to the valleys.

## The Lupines and the Hunt

The [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines) of the cold steppes are the principal non-dwarven adherents of the faith. Their observance is adapted to the life of the hunt rather than the high pasture. Lupine elders observe the rite of the first kill each season, offering the first portion of the prey to the shrine of the peak that governs their territory. The faith holds that the land provides the game, and a hunter who takes more than needed or wastes flesh offends Aero.

The relationship between lupine hunters and dwarven high clans has been generally peaceful. Lupines have warned clans of approaching herds or enemy parties. This mutual assistance is formalised through customary agreements governing hunting grounds and protection of passes during the hunt season. Lupines do not observe the full rites of the northern peaks; they take only those portions relevant to the hunter's life, and the dwarven clans accept this without objection.

## The Dwarven High Roads

The dwarven high clans are the principal builders of passes and bridges of the cold interior. The road networks connecting remote valleys to the principal halls of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) are largely the product of dwarven engineering. High roads are designed to withstand gales of the upper slopes. Retaining walls are set deep into rock, and cuttings through ridges are angled to deflect prevailing winds away from the road surface.

The high roads are maintained by the clans and by tolls levied upon caravans that use them. The tolls are considered a form of payment to the gods of the peaks for safe passage.

## The Greeting of the Gale

A distinctive observance of Aero is the greeting of the gale, conducted when the first great storm of autumn descends upon the high country. The elder stands at the customary point of the valley and speaks the traditional words, a formal recognition that Aero has returned for the season and that the community places itself under his protection. The greeting of the gale is observed with great solemnity in every highland community.

The greeting is not confined to the highland communities. Lowland towns at the foot of the ranges maintain their own observances, and magistrates mark the arrival of the first great storm with a brief acknowledgement at the customary shrine. The lowland observance is less elaborate, but the communities acknowledge that the gales descending from the peaks are the same that the highland communities endure.

## Significance

Aero is the second of the greater powers of the cold interior, and his observance is the foundation of the stormwatching tradition of the [Snow Country](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/snow-country). The reverence for the long storms, the formalisation of the watch and the high roads through religious obligation, and the integration of dwarven engineering and lupine hunting customs have contributed to the continuity of the cold interior through the long centuries. Aero is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the watchkeepers and herders who depend upon the high country for their livelihood, a livelihood whose continuation depends upon the wind returning each year in its season.
