## Overview

The Earthshaper is the third of the three greater powers in the [Stone Reading](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/stone-reading), honoured at the first strike into virgin rock and at the opening of a new way through solid stone. He is the ore in the vein, the metal sleeping in the dark, the seam that the workman follows into the body of the mountain, and the spirit of the rock that yields its treasure to those who approach it with proper respect. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the holds of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) hold him to dwell within the ore itself, and the older songs of the stoneworkers say that the colour of a freshly broken face of ore is the colour of the figure's regard. The Earthshaper has been honoured since the first vein was followed into the heart of the mountain, and his cult is the most demanding of the three greater powers, for the working of ore requires the closest attention to the will of the figure.

## The Figure and the Vein

The Earthshaper is conceived as the living presence of the ore in the vein and the rock that holds it. His domain extends from the small prospector's cut upon a hillside to the great galleries of the deepest holds. He is the colour of the freshly broken face, the weight of the bar in the workman's hand, the grain of the seam the eye can follow into the dark, and the long patience of the rock that has held the metal since the mountain was raised. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, honoured by the careful reading of the vein as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of the Earthshaper is the seam itself, where the ore lies in a body that the workman can follow. The seam is not a temple set apart from the mine. The workman approaches as a guest approaches the dwelling of one whose favour he seeks, speaking customary words before the first strike. The teaching holds that the mountain gives according to its own schedule, and the workman who presumes upon the seam without proper respect will find the seam closed to him.

The strike is the second dwelling of the figure, honoured with a short invocation. The workman raises the pick and speaks the customary words, asking the Earthshaper to receive the work and open the way. The workman observes the rock for the colour and the grain. A clean and ringing sound with a face showing the colour of metal within is held to be a sign of favour; a dull and resistant sound with barren rock indicates the workman must seek another place. Observation of the strike is the foundation of the workman's skill.

## The First Strike

The first strike into virgin rock is the principal occasion of the Earthshaper's honour, conducted with ceremony. The first strike of a new gallery is made by the master miner of the hold in the presence of apprentices and journeymen, at a place the master has chosen after long observation of the rock. The master speaks the customary words of invocation, asking the Earthshaper to open the vein and give ore in the measure the hold requires. The pick falls, and the master observes the face of the rock. The observation is recorded in the hall book of the gallery as the entry of the figure's favour.

The festival of the first ore is held at the first substantial strike of the working year, gathering the miners of the hold. The first ore of the year is carried in procession to the shrine of the Earthshaper at the mouth of the gallery and offered before any metal is smelted. A small portion is set aside and left at the shrine until the next first ore is brought. The festival is observed with the same care in every hold.

The first strike of a new seam is a particular occasion conducted with a different ceremony. The strike is made by the master miner where the seam has been followed to its narrowest exposure, and the master speaks a different set of customary words, asking the Earthshaper to widen the seam. The words are older than those of the first strike of a gallery, reserved for occasions when the seam itself is being opened.

## The Closed Vein

The custom of the closed vein is the most distinctive of the customs surrounding the Earthshaper and the foundation of the teaching of restraint distinguishing mountain miners from lowland miners. A closed vein is a seam that has been exhausted or become too poor for safe working. The vein is not abandoned immediately; the miner leaves a set portion of the vein in place, and the spirit of the vein is thanked and released from further claim. The offering is an admission that the mountain gives according to its own schedule and that the miner is a temporary custodian.

The offering is observed with great care in every hold and recorded in the hall books as one of the chief rites of the working year. The portion left is set by the master miner in consultation with the elders of the hold, large enough to be a genuine restraint rather than a token. Mines where the custom is observed have historically maintained steadier output across decades than those where it was neglected.

The closed vein is often paired with the festival of the closing of the heights, held at the first snowfall of autumn. Miners and surface dwellers come together to give thanks for the year's working. A miner who has closed a vein reports the closing, which is entered in the hall book. The festival is also the occasion for arbitration of disputes over mining rights.

## The Karhold Tradition

The miners of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) are the most renowned of the mountain miners, and the Karhold tradition is the standard against which other holds measure their work. Karhold miners have developed a body of technique passed from master to apprentice. The master does not teach technique as a set of rules but as a way of attending to the vein, and the apprentice learns by long observation and correction of first attempts. The Karhold tradition has been maintained for many centuries.

The miners of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) maintain a parallel tradition, renowned for the iron from [Ironhollow](/content/location/settlement/town/ironhollow). The two traditions have influenced one another over the centuries, evident in shared techniques and forms of offerings at the first strike.

The hall books of the principal holds of the Karhold Realm are the principal archives of the cult, containing records of first strikes, closed veins, and offerings. Hall books are kept by elder miners who are the custodians of the figure's honour. They teach apprentices the reading of the vein at the working face itself.

## Relation to the Other Powers

The Earthshaper is the third of the three greater powers, fixed by the order of the working. The [Smith God](/content/belief/deity/smith-god) is honoured at the forge and the anvil, and the [Dark Keeper](/content/belief/deity/keeper-of-the-dark) is honoured in the unlit workings and sealed chambers. The Earthshaper is honoured at the first strike and at the closed vein. The three figures are not in competition and not ranked by importance. They are honoured in the order of the work, fixed by the labour of the hold.

The Earthshaper is sometimes invoked together with the Smith God at the first smelting of newly brought ore. The miner-god is thanked for the ore and the Smith God for the fire. The Dark Keeper is invoked in the galleries where the lamps burn poorly.

## Observance and the Day of the Strike

The daily observance of the Earthshaper is observed by every miner. The miner approaches the working face at the customary hour and speaks a brief invocation before the first pick is raised. The miner observes the rock for colour and grain, then speaks a brief word of thanks when ore is revealed, spoken quietly so other miners may not hear. This is the miner's private acknowledgement of the figure's presence.

The day of the strike is the annual festival, held at the first strike of the working year. The festival is observed in every hold, gathering miners for the offering of the first ore at the shrine. A meal is shared after the offering. The festival is a renewal of the bond between the miners and the figure, and the occasion upon which the guild sets production for the year. The festival has been observed in the same form in every hold for as long as records preserve.

Miners of lowland towns of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) have adopted some customs, particularly speaking a word before the first pick is raised, but they do not share the full discipline of the mountain cult. Mountain miners do not seek converts beyond the holds. Human miners of villages at the foot of the mountain passes have adopted a fragment of the observance, particularly the rite of the first strike.

## Significance

The Earthshaper is the third of the three greater powers of the [Stone Reading](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/stone-reading), and his observance is the foundation of the mining tradition of the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the mountain spine. The reverence for the closed vein, the formalisation of the first strike as a religious obligation, and the integration of the figure's long memory into the hall books have contributed to the unity of the mountain miners. The Earthshaper is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the miners who depend upon the vein for their livelihood, whose continuation depends upon the figure's continued favour.
