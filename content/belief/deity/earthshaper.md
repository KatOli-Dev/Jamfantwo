## Overview

The Earthshaper is the third of the three greater powers in the [Stone Reading](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/stone-reading), the power who is honoured at the first strike into virgin rock and at the opening of a new way through the solid stone. He is the ore in the vein, the metal sleeping in the dark, the seam that the workman follows into the body of the mountain, and the spirit of the rock that yields its treasure to those who approach it with the proper respect. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the holds of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) hold him to dwell within the ore itself, and the older songs of the stoneworkers say that the colour of a freshly broken face of ore is the colour of the figure's regard. The chronicler of the mountain rites records that the Earthshaper has been honoured since the first vein was followed into the heart of the mountain, and that his cult is the most demanding of the three greater powers, for the working of ore is a discipline that requires the closest attention to the will of the figure.

## The Figure and the Vein

The Earthshaper is conceived as the living presence of the ore in the vein and the rock that holds it, and his domain extends from the small prospector's cut upon a hillside to the great galleries of the deepest holds. He is the colour of the freshly broken face, the weight of the bar in the workman's hand, the grain of the seam that the eye can follow into the dark, and the long patience of the rock that has held the metal since the mountain was raised. The chronicler of the stoneworkers has set down that the figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, and that the figure is honoured by the careful reading of the vein as much as by any formal rite.

The principal dwelling of the Earthshaper is the seam itself, the place in the rock where the ore lies in a body that the workman can follow. The seam is not a temple in the formal sense, and the seam is not set apart from the rest of the mine. The workman approaches the seam as a guest approaches the dwelling of one whose favour he seeks, and the workman does not enter the seam without the customary acknowledgement. The chronicler records that the figure is honoured by the workman who speaks the customary words before the first strike, and the words are the workman's acknowledgement that the figure is the master of the place and the workman is the visitor. The teaching holds that the mountain gives according to its own schedule, and the workman who presumes upon the seam without the proper respect will find the seam closed to him.

The strike is the second dwelling of the figure, and the strike is honoured with a short invocation that the chronicler of the mountain rites has recorded as the most distinctive of the mountain customs. The workman raises the pick and speaks the customary words, asking the Earthshaper to receive the work and to open the way. The pick falls, and the workman observes the rock for the colour and the grain that the figure is held to reveal. A clean and ringing sound and a face that shows the colour of the metal within is held to be a sign of the figure's favour; a dull and resistant sound and a face that shows only the barren rock is held to be a sign that the workman must seek another place. The chronicler notes that the observation of the strike is the foundation of the workman's skill, and the workman who neglects the observation is held to be careless, and the workman's work is watched with greater attention by the master of the hold.

## The First Strike

The first strike into virgin rock is the principal occasion of the Earthshaper's honour, and the strike is conducted with a ceremony that the chronicler has described at length. The first strike of a new gallery is struck by the master miner of the hold in the presence of the apprentices and the journeymen, and the strike is made at the place that the master has chosen after long observation of the rock. The master speaks the customary words of invocation, asking the Earthshaper to open the vein and to give the ore in the measure that the hold requires. The pick falls, and the master observes the face of the rock for the colour and the grain. The chronicler records that the master's observation is the formal beginning of the working, and the observation is recorded in the hall book of the gallery as the entry of the figure's favour.

The festival of the first ore is held at the first substantial strike of the working year, and the festival is the occasion for the gathering of the miners of the hold. The first ore of the year is carried in procession to the shrine of the Earthshaper at the mouth of the gallery, and the ore is offered to the figure before any of the metal is smelted. The offering is a small portion of the ore, set aside before the miner begins the day's work, and the offering is left at the shrine until the next first ore is brought. The chronicler notes that the festival is observed with the same care in every hold, and the festival is one of the most uniform of the mountain rites.

The first strike of a new seam is a particular occasion, and the strike is conducted with a ceremony that differs from the ceremony of the first strike of a new gallery. The strike is made by the master miner at the point where the seam has been followed to its narrowest exposure, and the master speaks a different set of customary words, asking the Earthshaper to widen the seam and to give the ore in abundance. The chronicler records that the words are older than the words of the first strike of a gallery, and the words are reserved for the occasions when the seam itself is being opened. The practice is uniform across the holds, and the practice is one of the most distinctive of the customs that mark the mountain miners as a people apart from the miners of the lowlands.

## The Closed Vein

The custom of the closed vein is the most distinctive of the customs that surround the Earthshaper, and the custom is the foundation of the teaching of restraint that distinguishes the mountain miners from the miners of the lowlands. A closed vein is a seam that has been exhausted or has become too poor to sustain safe working, and the vein is not abandoned immediately. The miner leaves a set portion of the vein in place, and the spirit of the vein is thanked and released from further claim. The offering is not a gift intended to buy future wealth; the offering is an admission that the mountain gives according to its own schedule and that the miner is a temporary custodian of whatever runs.

The chronicler of the mountain rites records that the offering is observed with great care in every hold, and the offering is recorded in the hall books of the community as one of the chief rites of the working year. The portion that is left is set by the master miner in consultation with the elders of the hold, and the portion is large enough to be a genuine restraint and not a token. The chronicler notes that the mines where the custom is observed have historically maintained steadier output across decades than those where the custom was neglected, and the steadiness is held by the mountain workers to be the figure's favour. The chronicler cautions that the correlation is not the same as the causation, but the mountain workers hold that the figure's favour is real and that the custom is its sign.

The closed vein is often paired with the festival of the closing of the heights, held at the first snowfall of autumn, and the festival is the occasion upon which the miners and the surface dwellers come together to give thanks for the year's working. The miner who has closed a vein in the course of the year reports the closing at the festival, and the report is entered in the hall book of the hold. The chronicler records that the report is made without ceremony, and the report is the formal acknowledgement that the vein has been released. The festival is also the occasion for the arbitration of disputes over mining rights, and the disputes are settled by the council of elders with reference to the custom of the closed vein and to the other customs of the working year.

## The Karhold Tradition

The miners of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) are the most renowned of the mountain miners, and the tradition of the Karhold mines is the standard against which the other holds measure their work. The Karhold miners have developed a body of technique that is the foundation of the reputation of the realm, and the techniques are passed from master to apprentice in the customary manner. The master does not teach the technique as a set of rules but as a way of attending to the vein, and the apprentice learns by long observation and by the master's correction of the apprentice's first attempts. The chronicler records that the Karhold tradition has been maintained for many centuries, and the tradition is the foundation of the unity of the cult across the mountain ranges of the continent.

The miners of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) maintain a parallel tradition, and the Stoneheim miners are renowned for the iron that they produce. The iron of [Ironhollow](/content/location/settlement/town/ironhollow) is the foundation of the metalwork of the other holds, and the Stoneheim miners are held in particular respect by the miners of the mountain spine. The chronicler records that the two traditions have influenced one another over the centuries, and the influence is to be seen in the shared techniques and the shared forms of the offerings that the miners make at the first strike. The Earthshaper is honoured in the exchange of technique, and the figure is the foundation of the unity of the cult across the two great dwarven realms.

The hall books of the principal holds of the [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) are the principal archives of the cult, and the hall books contain the records of the first strikes, the closed veins, and the offerings that have been made at the shrines. The hall books are kept by the elder miners of the holds, and the elder miners are the custodians of the figure's honour. The chronicler notes that the elder miners are the foundation of the continuity of the cult, and the elder miners teach the apprentices the reading of the vein in the customary manner. The teaching is conducted at the working face itself, and the apprentice learns by long practice in the company of the elder miner.

## Relation to the Other Powers

The Earthshaper is the third of the three greater powers, and his position is fixed by the order of the working, for the vein is the third of the mountain places after the hearth and the deep. The [Smith God](/content/belief/deity/smith-god) is honoured at the forge and the anvil, and the [Dark Keeper](/content/belief/deity/keeper-of-the-dark) is honoured in the unlit workings and the sealed chambers. The Earthshaper is honoured at the first strike and at the closed vein, and the vein is the place where the work of the miner begins and ends. The three figures are not in competition, and the three are not ranked by their importance. The three are honoured in the order of the work, and the order is fixed by the labour of the hold.

The chronicler records that the Earthshaper is sometimes invoked together with the [Smith God](/content/belief/deity/smith-god), and the invocation is the customary form at the first smelting of ore that has been newly brought from the deep. The miner-god is thanked for the ore and the Smith God for the fire, and the dual invocation is the formal acknowledgement of the two powers that have contributed to the work. The [Dark Keeper](/content/belief/deity/keeper-of-the-dark) is invoked in a different context, and the invocation belongs to the galleries where the lamps burn poorly. The chronicler notes that the three figures are honoured in their proper places, and the figure of the Earthshaper is the dwelling of the vein and the ore.

## Observance and the Day of the Strike

The daily observance of the Earthshaper is observed by every miner in every hold, and the observance is the most regular expression of the figure's honour. The miner approaches the working face at the customary hour, and the miner speaks a brief invocation before the first pick is raised. The pick falls, and the miner observes the rock for the colour and the grain. The miner speaks a brief word of thanks when the ore is revealed, and the word is spoken in a low voice so that the other miners in the gallery may not hear. The word is the miner's private acknowledgement of the figure, and the word is spoken as a sign that the miner knows the figure to be present.

The day of the strike is the annual festival of the Earthshaper, and the festival is held at the first strike of the working year. The festival is observed in every hold, and the festival is the occasion for the gathering of the miners of the hold and for the offering of the first ore at the shrine. The festival is not a feast in the usual sense, though a meal is shared after the offering is complete. The festival is a renewal of the bond between the miners and the figure, and the festival is the occasion upon which the guild sets the production of the year. The chronicler records that the festival has been observed in the same form in every hold for as long as the records preserve, and the festival is one of the most uniform of the mountain rites.

The miners of the lowland towns of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) have adopted some of the customs of the mountain miners, particularly the custom of speaking a word before the first pick is raised, but the lowland miners do not share the full discipline of the mountain cult. The chronicler records that the mountain miners do not seek converts beyond the holds, and the mountain miners do not travel to the lowlands to teach their craft. The craft is taught to the apprentices of the holds, and the apprentices are the foundation of the continuity of the tradition. The human miners of the villages that lie at the foot of the mountain passes have adopted a fragment of the observance, particularly the rite of the first strike, and the rite is conducted with a short word of invocation that the chronicler has recorded as a borrowing from the dwarven custom.

## Significance

The Earthshaper is the third of the three greater powers of the [Stone Reading](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/stone-reading), and his observance is the foundation of the mining tradition that has distinguished the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the mountain spine for centuries beyond reckoning. The figure endures because the figure asks nothing of his adherents that contradicts their daily labour at the working face, and the figure offers nothing that undermines the work of the vein. The reverence for the closed vein, the formalisation of the first strike as a religious obligation, and the integration of the figure's long memory into the records of the hall books have all contributed to the unity of the mountain miners across the wide range of their settlements. The chronicler finds in the Earthshaper one of the most patient and least displaced of the figures of the known world, honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the miners who depend upon the vein for their livelihood and whose livelihood's continuation depends upon the figure's continued favour. The figure endures, at the working face and at the closed vein, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the ore in the rock.
