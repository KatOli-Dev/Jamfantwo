## Overview

Sky Walker is the first of the greater spirits of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), the divine of the open heavens above the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). Sky Walker is the high pale blue of the summer noon, the low gold of the autumn evening, the long clear night of the steppe in which the stars wheel from horizon to horizon, and the storm that gathers in the afternoon and breaks across the open country with brief and violent rain. The pastoral peoples of the southern grasslands invoke Sky Walker at the first greening of the grass, at the turning of the seasons, and at the reading of the weather. The figure is older than the first standing stone and the first herder's camp, and the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the open country have honoured the figure since before the first formal confederation of the kindreds beneath the open sky.

Sky Walker is generous and changeable, and the rites attending the figure are marked by the same variety as the open sky itself. The figure is invoked at the morning of the first turning out of the herd, at the evening of the last bringing in, at the lightning that splits the summer sky, and at the slow wheeling of the constellations. Pastoralists ask for the rain that greens the grass, for the clear days that dry the hay, and for the wind that sweeps the long grass flat and reveals the tracks of the herds. Sky Walker is honoured by every act of stewardship of the open country, and the enduring fertility of the grasslands is the clearest evidence of the figure's favour.

## The Figure and Her Domain

Sky Walker is conceived as the living presence of the open heavens. Her domain extends from the highest cloud of the summer thunderhead to the deepest blue of the winter night. She is the morning light that strikes the eastern grass, the high sun of the summer noon, the long afternoon that matures the seed, and the low gold of the harvest evening. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, invoked by reading the weather and timing the labour of the herder as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of Sky Walker is the open sky itself, the unbroken dome arching above the grasslands from horizon to horizon. She is invoked at customary standing stones of the plains, marked places at the edge of old grazing grounds where the kindreds gather for seasonal rites. The standing stones are not formal buildings but markers, often ancient and weathered, tended by the households of the kindred.

The figure is also invoked in the songs of the wandering singers who travel between the kindreds, carrying the names of the spirits and stories of the seasons. The wandering singers are the living memory of the pantheon, and a kindred that has lost its singer is held to be in danger of forgetting the spirits of its own ground. Sky Walker is named first in the customary order of invocation, for the open sky is the first of the gifts the spirits bestow.

## Festival and Observances

The festival of Sky Walker falls at the turning of spring, when the first rains return to the dry land and the grass begins to green. The rites are conducted at the customary standing stone of each kindred by the elder, who speaks the customary words asking Sky Walker to grant the rain the grass requires and to send the winds that drive the clouds across the open country. The spring festival is observed with great solemnity by every kindred of the grasslands.

The rite of the first turning out is conducted at the opening of the grazing season, when the herds are led from winter shelter to the first spring pasture. The elder asks Sky Walker to watch over the herd and grant the rain the grass requires. The rite of the bringing in follows at the close of autumn, when the herds are returned to winter shelter. These two rites form the pivot of the pastoral year.

A distinctive observance is the reading of the storm, conducted by watchkeepers at the gathering of the summer clouds. Watchkeepers observe the conditions of the sky and weather, a form of devotion practised by every household of the open country. They have developed a refined understanding of the weather, and their predictions are relied upon by every herder who grazes the open country.

## The Order of the Greater Spirits

Sky Walker is the first of the greater spirits of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), her position being the opening of the pastoral cycle. [Pasara](/content/belief/deity/pasara) is the second, governing the fertility of the grazing beasts. [Stone Watcher](/content/belief/deity/stone-watcher) is the third, governing the standing stones and the memory of the ancestors. [River Speaker](/content/belief/deity/river-speaker) is the fourth in some traditions, the spirit of the waters. The four are acknowledged collectively at seasonal gatherings, but each has her or his own day of particular observance. Sky Walker is honoured first because the open sky is the first gift the spirits bestow.

Beneath the four greater spirits are a multitude of lesser powers, many local to a single hill, spring, or stand of trees. The [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) of southern [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) is said by the desert peoples to be thronged with such local [Kharesh Spirits](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/kharesh-spirits).

## The Heavens above the Plains

The observation of the heavens is the most enduring of the pastoral sciences. [Spherical Cartography](/content/belief/science/theoretical/spherical-cartography) attempts to set down in measured form what the herders and watchkeepers have long observed by eye. The pastoralists of the open country are among the most attentive observers of the heavens in the known world, and the songs of the wandering singers preserve the names of the constellations.

The long clear night of the steppe is the most visible sign of Sky Walker's presence. Watchkeepers use the wheeling of the constellations to mark the passing of hours and seasons, having developed a body of customary knowledge governing the reading of the stars. The afternoon storm is also a particular occasion of honour. Pastoralists mark the gathering of summer clouds with a customary prayer at the edge of the camp, a brief acknowledgement of the figure's gift of rain.

## The Centaur Kindreds and the Open Sky

The [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) are the principal adherents of Sky Walker, their observance adapted to the life of the open country. Centaur kindreds move across the grasslands in a seasonal cycle mirroring the movements of the herds, building no permanent settlements. They honour Sky Walker at the customary standing stones with a directness described as the most attentive piety of the pastoral life.

Centaur songs of the sky are among the most refined of the pastoral literature, preserving the names of constellations, patterns of weather, and the turning of seasons. Centaurs are the first to read the storm and the last to seek shelter. The relationship between centaurs and human pastoralists is one of quiet cooperation, and centaur songs are learned by human herders and adapted to local grounds.

The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) is the political entity through which centaur kindreds and human pastoral communities coordinate their use of the land, governing grazing rights, water access, and hunting territories. Sky Walker is present in these agreements as the spirit of the sky watching over the open country, renewed at seasonal gatherings at the customary standing stones.

## Significance

Sky Walker is the first of the greater spirits of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and the figure's observance is the foundation of the pastoral life of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The reverence for the rain that greens the grass, the formalisation of the stewardship of the grazing lands through religious obligation, and the integration of the household observance into the everyday practice of the pastoral year have contributed to the enduring fertility of the grasslands. Sky Walker is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the herders and watchkeepers who depend upon the open sky for their livelihood, whose continuation depends upon the sky remaining open.
