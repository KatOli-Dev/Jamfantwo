## Overview

Sky Walker is the first of the greater spirits of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), the divine of the open heavens above the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). Sky Walker is the high pale blue of the summer noon, the low gold of the autumn evening, the long clear night of the steppe in which the stars wheel from horizon to horizon, and the storm that gathers in the afternoon and breaks across the open country with a brief and violent rain. The pastoral peoples of the southern grasslands invoke Sky Walker at the first greening of the grass, at the turning of the seasons, and at the reading of the weather, and the chronicler of the plains records has set down that the figure is older than the first standing stone and the first herder's camp, and that the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the open country have honoured the figure since before the first formal confederation of the kindreds beneath the open sky.

The figure of Sky Walker is generous and changeable, and the rites that attend the figure are marked by the same variety that marks the open sky itself. The figure is invoked at the morning of the first turning out of the herd, at the evening of the last bringing in, at the lightning that splits the summer sky, and at the slow wheeling of the constellations that the watchkeepers read for the turning of the year. The pastoralists do not ask the figure for blessings in the abstract; they ask the figure for the rain that greens the grass, for the clear days that dry the hay, and for the wind that sweeps the long grass flat and reveals the tracks of the herds. The chronicler records that the figure is honoured by every act of stewardship of the open country, and that the enduring fertility of the grasslands is the clearest evidence of the figure's favour.

## The Figure and Her Domain

Sky Walker is conceived as the living presence of the open heavens, and the figure's domain extends from the highest cloud of the summer thunderhead to the deepest blue of the winter night. She is the morning light that strikes the eastern grass, the high sun of the summer noon, the long afternoon of the late summer that matures the seed, and the low gold of the harvest evening that closes the day. The chronicler of the plains records has set down that the figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, and that the figure is invoked by the reading of the weather and the timing of the labour of the herder as much as by any formal rite.

The principal dwelling of Sky Walker is the open sky itself, the unbroken dome that arches above the grasslands from horizon to horizon. The figure is invoked at the customary standing stones of the plains, the marked places at the edge of the old grazing grounds where the kindreds have long gathered to conduct the seasonal rites. The standing stones are not buildings in the formal sense; they are markers, often ancient and weathered, and the tending of the standing stones is a responsibility shared among the households of the kindred. Sky Walker is present in the standing stones as the spirit of the sky that the markers acknowledge, and her presence is renewed each year by the offerings of the first milk and the first wool of the season.

The figure is also invoked in the songs of the wandering singers who travel between the kindreds, carrying the names of the spirits and the stories of the seasons. The wandering singers are the living memory of the pantheon, and a kindred that has lost its singer is held to be in danger of forgetting the spirits of its own ground. The chronicler records that the songs of the wandering singers name Sky Walker first in the customary order of invocation, for the open sky is the first of the gifts that the spirits bestow upon the pastoralists. The figure is present in the songs as the spirit of the heavens that the singers honour, and the singers are held to be the human instruments of the figure's care for the open country.

## Festival and Observances

The festival of Sky Walker falls at the turning of the spring, when the first rains return to the dry land and the grass begins to green beneath the open sky. The festival is the principal observance of the figure among the pastoralists, and the rites are conducted at the customary standing stone of each kindred by the elder of the household. The elder speaks the customary words of invocation, asking Sky Walker to grant the rain that the grass requires and to send the winds that drive the clouds across the open country. The chronicler records that the spring festival is observed with great solemnity by every kindred of the grasslands, and the occasion is one of the most important of the ceremonial events of the pastoral year. Sky Walker's day at the turning of the spring is the most universal of the observances of the open country.

The rite of the first turning out is conducted at the opening of the grazing season, when the herds are led from the winter shelter to the first spring pasture. The elder of the kindred speaks the traditional words, asking Sky Walker to watch over the herd through the season of growth and to grant the rain that the grass requires. The rite of the bringing in follows at the close of the autumn, when the herds are returned to the winter shelter and the pasture is given over to the cold. The chronicler notes that the two rites of the herd - the first turning out and the bringing in - form the pivot of the pastoral year, and the observance of the rites with strict regularity is held to be the foundation of the continuity of the kindred through the seasons.

A distinctive observance of Sky Walker is the reading of the storm, conducted by the watchkeepers of the kindred at the gathering of the summer clouds. The watchkeepers are charged with observing the conditions of the sky and the weather, and the reading of the storm is a form of devotion that is practised by every household of the open country. The chronicler records that the watchkeepers of the grasslands have developed a refined understanding of the weather, and that their predictions are relied upon by every herder who grazes the open country. The reading of the storm is held to be a form of communion with Sky Walker, and the watchkeepers who have mastered the reading are regarded with a respect that is matched by no other accomplishment of the pastoral life.

## The Order of the Greater Spirits

Sky Walker is the first of the greater spirits of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and the figure's position in the order of the greater spirits is the opening of the pastoral cycle. The [Pasara](/content/belief/deity/pasara) is the second, governing the fertility of the grazing beasts and the prosperity of the herds, and the [Stone Watcher](/content/belief/deity/stone-watcher) is the third, governing the standing stones and the memory of the ancestors. The [River Speaker](/content/belief/deity/river-speaker) is the fourth in some traditions, the spirit of the waters that cut the plains. The four are acknowledged collectively at the seasonal gatherings of the kindreds, but each also has her or his own day of particular observance. The chronicler records that the order of the four is fixed by the pastoral cycle, and the figure of Sky Walker is honoured first because the open sky is the first of the gifts that the spirits bestow upon the pastoralists of the grasslands.

The four greater spirits are understood to be a single order of divine care for the open country, and the figures are invoked together at the customary standing stones at the turning of the seasons. Sky Walker is the open heavens that send the rain, the Pasara is the fertility of the beasts that graze the grass, the Stone Watcher is the memory of the ancestors whose standing stones mark the boundaries of the land, and the River Speaker is the waters that thread the grasslands from north to south. The chronicler notes that the four figures are honoured in a fixed sequence at the seasonal gatherings, and the sequence is observed with great strictness in every kindred of the open country. The figure of Sky Walker is present in the sequence as the spirit of the sky that the gathering acknowledges, and her presence is the foundation of the unity of the observance.

Beneath the four greater spirits are counted a multitude of lesser powers, many of them local to a single hill, spring, or stand of trees. The pastoralists hold that to know the name of the spirit of a place is to hold a claim upon its favour, and the elders of a kindred are charged with the remembering of these names and the teaching of them to the young. The [Kharesh](/content/location/natural/feature/kharesh) of southern [Sahrana](/content/location/natural/continent/sahrana) is said by the desert peoples to be thronged with such local [Kharesh Spirits](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/kharesh-spirits), and the caravan songs of that region name them by the hundred, a testament to the patience of those who first walked the sand. Sky Walker is present in the lesser powers as the spirit of the sky that watches over every place, and her presence is renewed each year by the offerings of the pastoralists at the customary standing stones.

## The Heavens above the Plains

The observation of the heavens is the most enduring of the pastoral sciences, and the [Spherical Cartography](/content/belief/science/theoretical/spherical-cartography) is the theoretical discipline that attempts to set down in measured form what the herders and the watchkeepers have long observed by eye. The cartographers of the spheres hold that the world is a sphere enveloped by the heavens, and that the fixed stars that the watchkeepers read upon the open country are the surest evidence of the roundness of the world. The chronicler records that the pastoralists of the open country are among the most attentive observers of the heavens in the known world, and that the songs of the wandering singers preserve the names of the constellations that the watchkeepers read for the turning of the seasons.

The long clear night of the steppe is the most visible sign of Sky Walker's presence, and the watchkeepers of the grasslands use the wheeling of the constellations to mark the passing of the hours and the seasons. The chronicler records that the watchkeepers have developed a body of customary knowledge that governs the reading of the stars, and that the reading is a form of devotion that is practised by every elder of the open country. Sky Walker is honoured in the observation of the stars as the spirit of the heavens that the watchkeepers observe, and the figure is present in the long clear night in a manner that no other figure of the pastoral calendar quite matches.

The afternoon storm is also a particular occasion of Sky Walker's honour, and the pastoralists mark the gathering of the summer clouds with a customary prayer at the edge of the camp. The prayer is a brief acknowledgement of the figure's gift of the rain, and the words have changed little over the generations. The chronicler records that the prayer is observed with great regularity in every kindred of the open country, and the figure is present in the afternoon storm as the spirit of the rain that the grass requires. Sky Walker is honoured in the observation of the storm as much as in the observation of the clear night, and the chronicler notes that the two observations are understood to be aspects of a single divine care for the open country.

## The Centaur Kindreds and the Open Sky

The [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) are the principal adherents of Sky Walker, and their observance is adapted to the life of the open country rather than the life of the standing settlement. The centaur kindreds move across the grasslands in a seasonal cycle that mirrors the movements of the herds, and the centaurs do not build permanent settlements upon the plains. The chronicler records that the centaurs honour Sky Walker at the customary standing stones of the open country, and that the centaur observance of the figure is conducted with a directness that the chronicler has described as the most attentive piety of the pastoral life.

The centaur songs of the sky are among the most refined of the pastoral literature, and the songs preserve the names of the constellations, the patterns of the weather, and the turning of the seasons in a form that the wandering singers carry between the kindreds. The chronicler records that the centaurs are the first of the pastoral peoples to read the storm and the last to seek shelter from it, and that the figure of Sky Walker is honoured in the centaur practice of the open country as the spirit of the sky that the centaurs serve. The relationship between the centaurs and the human pastoralists of the open country is one of quiet cooperation, and the centaur songs are learned by the human herders and adapted to the local grounds of each kindred.

The [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) is the political entity through which the centaur kindreds and the human pastoral communities of the southern plains coordinate their use of the land, and the agreements that govern grazing rights, water access, and hunting territories are among the oldest continuous political arrangements in the known world. Sky Walker is present in the agreements as the spirit of the sky that watches over the open country, and the chronicler records that the agreements are renewed at the seasonal gatherings of the kindreds at the customary standing stones. The figure is honoured in the renewal of the agreements as the spirit of the sky that the kindreds share, and the cooperation of the centaurs and the human pastoralists is held to be the human expression of the figure's care for the open country.

## Significance

Sky Walker is the first of the greater spirits of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and the figure's observance is the foundation of the pastoral life of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) of central [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The figure endures because the figure asks nothing of her adherents that contradicts their daily survival, and the figure offers nothing that undermines it. The reverence for the rain that greens the grass, the formalisation of the stewardship of the grazing lands through religious obligation, and the integration of the household observance into the everyday practice of the pastoral year have all contributed to the enduring fertility of the grasslands. The chronicler finds in Sky Walker one of the most generous and least displaced of the figures of the known world, honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the herders and the watchkeepers who depend upon the open sky for their livelihood and whose livelihood's continuation depends upon the sky remaining open. The figure endures, in the standing stone and at the reading of the storm, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the heavens above the grasslands.
