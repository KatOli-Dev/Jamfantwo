## Overview

Long Night is the third of the greater powers in the [Snow Country](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/snow-country), the divine of the polar darkness that descends upon the cold interior of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim) for the weeks of deep winter when the sun does not rise above the horizon. Long Night is the patience of the dark, the long wait that the year imposes upon the people of the interior, and the figure is honoured at the heart of the winter rather than at its edges. The figure is older than the first watchtower and the first snow-bound valley, and the dwarven high clans and the human communities of the snow valleys have honoured the figure since before the first formal gathering of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) at the principal hall of the high country.

Long Night is austere, and the rites attending her are austere in proportion. The figure is invoked in the long evenings of the high country, in the deep watches of the night, and in the brief hours of midday twilight that the winter allows. The communities of the cold interior ask her for endurance, for the patience to bear the weeks of the polar dark, and for the wisdom to husband the stores of the household through to the return of the light. She is honoured by every act of watchkeeping and by every long evening passed in the company of the household.

## The Figure and Her Domain

Long Night is conceived as the living presence of the polar darkness. Her domain extends from the brief midday twilight of deep winter to the long evenings of autumn and spring, when the sun sets early and the cold interior is given over to the dark. She is the slow accumulation of night across the weeks of high winter, the stillness of unbroken dark, the cold that settles in the deep of the night, and the long waiting that the year imposes upon the peoples of the interior. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, invoked by the observation of the dark and the husbanding of stores as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of Long Night is the high country itself, the snow-bound valleys, glaciers, and high meadows above the tree line. She is invoked at the long-night gathering of the clans, held each year at the principal hall of the high country at midwinter. The gathering is a multi-day event during which stories of the past year are told, deeds of the watch are recounted, and the young are taught the old songs that name the spirits of the peaks in order.

The figure is also invoked at customary shrines of the high country, marked places upon the high passes and principal summits. The shrines are not formal buildings but marked places, usually a cairn of stones, a standing stone, or a sheltered hollow in the lee of a rock. Their tending is a responsibility shared among the households of the clans.

## Festival and Observances

The festival of Long Night falls at midwinter, the darkest time of the year, when the people of the high interior most need the reassurance that the polar dark will not last forever. The festival is observed in every clan and every snow-bound valley of the cold interior. The [Long Night Festival](/content/culture/long-night-festival) that the temperate lowlands observe at the same season is a separate observance descended from older hunting rituals, sharing the timing but not the form.

The rite of the long-night gathering is the principal observance, conducted at the principal hall of the high clan over several days. The elder speaks the customary words asking Long Night to receive the community into her care for the remainder of the winter and to grant the endurance the long dark requires. The words have changed little over generations, and the form of the rite is the same in every clan from the high passes of the eastern mountains to the western reaches of the snow-bound valleys.

A distinctive observance is the rite of the watch fire, conducted at the changing of the watch upon the high points of the escarpment. Watchkeepers maintain the signal fires, and the [Frostwall Fire Signs](/content/language/pseudo/frostwall-fire-signs) are the code by which they convey information. The lighting of the watch fire at the changing of the watch is accompanied by a brief invocation of Long Night, a formal acknowledgement that the watch is the service of the community to the gods of the peaks.

## The Order of the Greater Powers

Long Night is the third of the greater powers of the [Snow Country](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/snow-country), her position being the closing of the winter cycle. [Nivea](/content/belief/deity/nivea) is the first, governing the snow that blankets the peaks and the ice that bridges the deep chasms. [Aero](/content/belief/deity/aero) is the second, governing the high winds and great storms. Long Night is the third, governing the polar darkness. The three are acknowledged collectively at the long-night gathering, but each has her own day of particular observance. The order is fixed by the annual cycle, and Long Night is honoured last because the dark is the last gift the gods bestow before spring.

Beneath the three greater powers are the gods of individual peaks, glaciers, and valleys. The dwarven clans maintain lists of the gods of the principal summits of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) alongside the names of the passes and water sources beneath each peak. A new name is added only when a summit has been known across several generations.

## The Dark and the Endurance of the People

Long Night is honoured not only in the gift of darkness but also in the recognition of the endurance the dark requires. The dark of high winter is the most severe test of the year, and communities of the cold interior mark the passage of the weeks of polar dark with observances described as among the most austere of the high country. The observance recognises that the figure has the power to grant or withhold the endurance of the people.

The weeks of polar dark are the time when watchkeepers are most active. The watch is the central duty of the clans during the long winter. Watchkeepers monitor conditions of snow and weather, maintain signal fires, and warn communities of approaching storms or enemy parties. Watchkeepers who have served through the long winter are honoured as the human instruments of Long Night's care for the high country.

The figure is also honoured in the observation of the stars, visible in the long evenings and used by watchkeepers and elders to read the passing of the weeks. The stars are the only light of the long winter beyond the watch fires and household lamps. Elders of the clans have developed a body of customary knowledge governing the reading of the stars and the timing of the watch.

## The Long Night and the Return of the Light

The return of the light is the most visible sign of Long Night's withdrawal, and communities of the cold interior mark the moment with a quiet observance. The first appearance of the sun above the horizon at the end of the polar dark is marked by a brief ceremony at the principal shrine, the formal acknowledgement that the dark has ended and the year has begun anew.

The long evening of autumn is also an occasion of Long Night's honour. Communities mark the closing of the grazing season with a customary rite at the principal shrine, acknowledging that the long dark is approaching and the community must prepare for the weeks ahead.

## Relation to the Stone Reading

The [Stone Reading](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/stone-reading) is the dwarven reverence for the stone, and the two faiths are complementary rather than contradictory. The gods of the ice are the powers of the surface of the cold interior; the gods of the stone are the powers of the rock beneath the ice. Long Night is honoured in both traditions, and the dual observance is considered the mark of the complete dwarf. The gods of the ice and the gods of the stone are held to be kin, for both are powers of the mountain, one above ground and one below.

The dwarven high clans are the principal builders of the passes and bridges of the cold interior. The road networks connecting remote valleys to the principal halls of the [Frostwall Clans](/content/government/national/frostwall-clans) are largely the product of dwarven engineering, maintained jointly by the clans and tolls levied upon caravans.

## The Lupines and the Long Night

The [lupines](/content/species/sapient/lupines) of the cold steppes are the principal non-dwarven adherents of Long Night, their observance adapted to the life of the hunt. Lupine elders observe the rite of the first kill each season, offering the first portion of the prey to the shrine of the peak governing their territory. Lupine observance takes a distinctive form: they do not gather at the principal hall of the high country but at customary camps of their own territories, speaking the customary words at the changing of the watch in the cold camps.

The relationship between lupine hunters and dwarven high clans has been generally peaceful. Lupines have warned clans of approaching herds or enemy parties.

## Significance

Long Night is the third of the greater powers of the [Snow Country](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/snow-country), and her observance is the foundation of the winter life of the peoples of the cold interior of [Nordheim](/content/location/natural/continent/nordheim). The reverence for the patience the long dark requires, the formalisation of the watch through religious obligation, and the integration of the household observance into the everyday practice of the winter have contributed to the prosperity of the high country. Long Night is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the watchkeepers and householders who depend upon the endurance of the dark for their survival, whose continuation depends upon the dark remaining patient.
