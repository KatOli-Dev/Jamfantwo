## Elder of the Sea

Cetus is the second of the greater powers in the [Grey Sea Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/grey-sea-way), the god of the great whales that migrate through the cold waters of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) and the temperate latitudes of the western coast of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). He is conceived as the elder of the sea, a creature of great age and wisdom who moves through the cold water in advance of the great pods. His passage is honoured by whalers and fishers of the western coast as the signal of the season of the great hunt. Whalers of the [Grey Coast](/content/location/settlement/region/grey-coast) invoke him at the season of the hunt and at the offering of the first blubber. His festival falls at the time of the northward migration, when the great whales return to the feeding grounds of the polar latitudes. The figure is older than the first whaling boat and the first cliff town, and whalers have honoured him since before the first net was woven from hemp of the inland valleys.

Cetus is not worshipped as a deity to be placated but regarded as the elder of the sea, a creature to be honoured and respected before the hunt, a being whose bounty is earned rather than given. This attitude is unusual among whaling peoples of the known world, who typically treat the whale as either an enemy to be hunted or a sacred creature not to be taken at all. Cetus occupies a position between these extremes, a being whose presence in the cold water is a sign of the bounty of the sea and whose killing is a solemn undertaking approached with customary acknowledgements.

## The Figure and His Domain

Cetus is conceived as the living presence of the great whales themselves. His domain extends from the temperate waters of the western coast to the polar latitudes where the whales feed through the summer months. He is the great shape rising from the grey water, the spout that whalers identify from a distance, the long dive that carries the whale beneath the surface, and the song that males sing on the breeding grounds in the late autumn. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, invoked by reading the migration and conducting the hunt as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of Cetus is the cold water of the Greyring, and he is present in the great whales that move through the water in their seasonal procession. Whalers maintain headland shrines at which offerings of first blubber are made, the gathering points of whaling communities at the turning of the season. The offerings are the formal acknowledgement that the whale is not the property of the hunter but the gift of the elder of the sea.

## The Greater Powers of the Western Deep

Cetus is the second of the greater powers of the [Grey Sea Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/grey-sea-way), and his position is the honouring of the great whales of the cold water. [Cana](/content/belief/deity/cana) is the first, the principal power of the grey sea, the fog, and the long swell. [Fury](/content/belief/deity/fury) is the third, the power of the long gales that sweep in from the open ocean. Beneath these three are the spirits of the particular rocks and headlands of the western coast. Each cliff town maintains the names of the spirits of nearby headlands and rocks in small books kept by the elder, updated only when a spirit has been known across several generations.

The order of the greater powers is fixed by the conditions of the western sea. Cetus is honoured second because the whale is the second of the gifts the sea bestows upon the coast. Cana provides the water and the weather, Cetus provides the great creature that the whalers pursue, and Fury provides the gales that the watchkeepers must observe.

## The Migration and the Hunt

The migration of the great whales through the cold water is a predictable event, and the hunt is organised and conducted by whaling communities in a manner that has changed little over centuries. The faith teaches that the whale is the elder of the sea, honoured before the hunt and taken only according to customary quotas. The first blubber of the kill is offered at the headland shrine by the elder of the whaling community, a formal acknowledgement that the whale is not the property of the hunter but the gift of the elder of the sea.

Whalers of the cold coast are a hardy folk who have pursued the great whales for centuries. The fishery is conducted from small boats launched from larger vessels, pursuing the whale with hand harpoon and long lance. The crews are among the most skilled mariners of the cold coast. The fishery is regulated by customs of the coastal communities: the taking of whales is forbidden during the breeding season, and the smallest whales must be released.

## The Greyring Whale and the Honouring

The [Greyring whale](/content/species/beasts/greyring-whale) is the largest of the cetaceans of the cold water, a great baleen whale of dark grey-blue whose range extends across the cold waters of the western ocean from the polar south to the temperate latitudes of the western coast. The whale takes its name from the colour of its skin and the waters it inhabits, for the cold currents of the Greyring give the ocean a distinctive grey cast. The Greyring whale is the principal creature of Cetus's domain and the object of the customary offerings at the headland shrines.

The Greyring whale is migratory, moving northward in the spring to feed in the rich waters of the polar latitudes and southward in the autumn to breed in warmer waters. Whalers of the western coast closely follow the migrations, and the timing of the northward and southward passages is one of the principal determinants of the rhythm of the fishing year.

## The Quotas and the Sustained Yield

The quotas that govern the whaling hunt are among the strictest in the known world. Whaling communities that have adhered to the quotas have maintained stable whale populations over generations, while those that expanded catches during years of abundance often found populations depleted in subsequent seasons. The faith teaches that the sea is a common inheritance of present and future generations, and that taking the last breeding stock denies grandchildren the bounty of the sea. This principle of restraint represents one of the earliest known formal management systems for marine megafauna.

Cetus is honoured not by the largest catch but by the most careful catch. He is present in the customary law that governs the taking of the whale as the spirit of the elder whose protection is earned by the observance of customary limits.

## The First Blubber and the Offering

The first blubber of the kill is the principal offering of the whaling season, made at the headland shrine by the elder of the community. The offering is a formal acknowledgement that the whale is not the property of the hunter but the gift of the elder of the sea. The offering is one of the most solemn observances of the cold coast, attended by whalers and fishers with a quietness described as the deepest piety of the western year.

The offering is accompanied by customary words that have changed little over generations. The words acknowledge Cetus as the elder of the sea and request the continuation of the bounty. The words are the same in every cliff town from the northern headlands to the southern estuaries, a measure of the unity of the observance across the cold coast.

## The Merfolk and the Cetus

The [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) of the cold waters honour Cetus as the elder of the sea, and their observance is adapted to the life of the deep water rather than the cliff town. Merfolk are dwellers of the cold water itself, with deep understanding of the movements of whales and currents of the Greyring. They observe Cetus at the customary seasons and maintain the traditional quotas governing the whaling hunt. In recent centuries, merfolk have acted as primary intermediaries between whaling communities and whale pods, warning whalers of approaching pods and advising on hunt conditions.

The relationship between merfolk and human whalers is one of practical reciprocity. Humans provide boats, harpoons, and technology of the hunt, while merfolk provide knowledge of whales and deep water. The exchange is formalised through customary agreements governing fishing grounds and the conduct of the hunt.

## The Songs of the Breeding Grounds

Whales are highly vocal during the breeding season. The songs that males sing on the breeding grounds are among the most remarkable natural sounds of the sea, a series of low and resonant calls that may continue for hours upon the still water. The songs are held by cliff communities to be the voice of Cetus, and they are listened for with care during the breeding season. Fishers report the songs to the elders, who record the timing and duration in small books.

The songs are answered in the traditions of the storm-cantors, who perform the old songs at the height of the winter gales. The songs of the storm-cantors are in turn answered by the songs of the whales in the deep water, and the exchange is held to be a form of communion between the cliff community and the elder of the sea.

## The Cliff Settlements and the Watch

Cliff towns of the [Grey Coast](/content/location/settlement/region/grey-coast) are the principal settlements of the faith, each maintaining its own headland shrine upon the highest point overlooking the sea. The watch is the central duty of the cliff settlements. Watchkeepers maintain lighthouses, signal towers, and storm warnings that protect the fishing fleet and merchant traffic. Watchkeepers of the cold coast maintain a level of vigilance that is exceptional, and the lighthouses and signal towers of the western coast are among the most densely maintained in the known world.

Cetus is honoured in the watch as the spirit of the elder whose passage the watchkeepers must observe. Watchkeepers are required to report the passage of great whales during the migration seasons. They are drawn from the fisherfolk, and their role is an extension of their daily labour. The neglect of a lighthouse is considered a betrayal of the elder of the sea and the fishing community alike.

## Significance

Cetus is the second of the greater powers of the cold western sea, and his observance is the foundation of the whaling tradition of the [Grey Sea Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/grey-sea-way). The reverence for the migration of the great whales, the formalisation of the hunt through religious obligation, and the integration of merfolk knowledge into the everyday practice of whalers have contributed to the stability of the whale populations of the cold coast. Cetus is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the whalers who depend upon the great whales for their livelihood, whose continuation depends upon the whales returning each year in their season.
