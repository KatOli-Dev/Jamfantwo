## Overview

Among the three greater powers of the [Stone Reading](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/stone-reading), the Smith God is reckoned the first, invoked before the others at the kindling of a new hearth and at the casting of the first metal of the working year. He is the power of the forge, the fire that will not let the bellows go out, the heat that wakes the ore, and the patient anvil upon which the metal is brought to its proper shape. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the holds of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) hold him to dwell within the fire itself, and the older songs of the stoneworkers say that the sound of a hammer falling true is the sound of his attention turning toward the work. The Smith God has been honoured since the founding of the first hold, and his cult is the most uniform of the three greater powers, for the working of metal is a single discipline recognising no boundary between the clans.

## The Figure and the Forge

The Smith God is conceived as the living presence of the forge-fire and the worked metal. His domain extends from the small hearth of a village smith to the great forges of the mountain cities. He is the heat that gathers in the belly of the furnace, the bright orange of the metal at the moment of drawing, the dull red of the cooling bar, and the grey ash of the spent fire. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, honoured by every act of working metal as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of the Smith God is the forge itself, the enclosed space in which the fire is fed and the metal is brought to its proper heat. The forge is not a temple set apart from the workshop. The fire is the figure, honoured by careful tending and by customary words spoken when the first bellows is pumped. A new forge becomes a dwelling only after the first casting has been completed and the customary offering made.

The anvil is the second dwelling, honoured with a short invocation before any metal is laid upon it. The smith places the bar upon the anvil and speaks the customary words asking the Smith God to receive the work and give it proper shape. The words have changed little across generations and are the same in every hold from the deep interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar).

## The First Hearth and the First Casting

The kindling of a new hearth is one of the principal occasions of the Smith God's honour, conducted according to a ritual observed in every hold. A new hearth is built of firebrick and stone in customary dimensions. The fuel is laid with care, and the first fire is kindled by the master smith in the presence of apprentices and journeymen. The master speaks the customary words of invocation, asking the Smith God to enter the hearth and abide there as long as the workshop stands. The words are brief and unadorned, repeated by the apprentices as a sign of acceptance.

The first casting of the season is the second principal occasion. The first substantial bar of the working year is poured at the first convenient moment after the spring thaw and offered to the Smith God before any metal is shaped. A small portion is set aside and left upon the anvil until the next fire is kindled. The offering is not a sacrifice or a gift to purchase favour but an acknowledgement that the figure is present and the metal is his to give.

The festival of the opening of the forge is held at the first casting, gathering the smiths of the hold. The master smiths of principal workshops attend with apprentices and journeymen. The festival is not a feast, though a meal is shared after the casting. It is a renewal of the bond between smiths and the figure, and the occasion upon which the guild sets production for the year.

## The Rites of the Forge

The daily rites of the forge are observed by every smith. The fire is kindled at the customary hour, and the smith speaks a brief invocation as the first bellows is pumped. The metal is laid upon the hearth, and the smith watches for the colour indicating proper heat. The smith draws the metal from the fire and lays it upon the anvil, speaking a brief word as the first hammer falls. The words are few and spoken in a low voice, the smith's private acknowledgement of the figure's presence.

The quench is honoured with a short word as the metal is plunged into the cooling trough. The colour of the steam rising from the quench is observed with care: a clean, straight column indicates favour; a ragged, curling column indicates the work requires correction. A smith who neglects observation of the steam is held to be careless.

The closing of the forge is conducted with a short word of thanks as the fire is allowed to die down for the night. The smith cleans the anvil and tools and covers the bellows with the customary cloth. The fire is not quenched, for the fire is the figure and must be allowed to rest in the manner of a living thing.

## The Mountain Smiths and the Stoneheim Tradition

The smiths of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) are the most renowned of the mountain smiths, and the Stoneheim tradition is the standard against which other holds measure their work. [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) smiths have developed a body of technique passed from master to apprentice. The master teaches not as rules but as a way of attending to the work, and the apprentice learns by long observation and correction of first attempts. The tradition has been maintained for many centuries.

Smiths of [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar) are renowned for decorative metalwork, their objects sought by rulers and the wealthy of every continent. Smiths of [Ironhollow](/content/location/settlement/town/ironhollow) are renowned for iron, the foundation of the metalwork of other holds. Each principal hold has its own speciality, not in competition but in complement.

The [Karhold Realm](/content/government/national/karhold-realm) maintains a parallel tradition older than Stoneheim by several generations, renowned for durability. The two traditions have influenced one another over centuries in shared techniques and forms.

## The Work and the Closed Hearth

The teaching of the closed hearth is one of the most distinctive customs surrounding the Smith God, a counterpoint to the closed vein belonging to the miner-god. A closed hearth is one fallen into disuse, treated with the same care as a closed vein. The master does not dismantle the hearth or remove the anvil. The hearth is left as it is, the fire allowed to die and not rekindled. The teaching holds that the Smith God dwelt in the hearth as long as it was in use and withdraws when the work is done, not returning to a hearth that has been closed.

The opening of a new hearth in a workshop that previously contained a closed hearth is conducted with particular ceremony. The new fire is held to be a renewal and not a continuation. The smith addresses the figure as one who is invited, a fresh request for the figure's presence.

## Relation to the Other Powers

The Smith God is the first of the three greater powers, fixed by the working of metal, which is the first of the mountain crafts. The Dark Keeper is honoured in the deep places where no light reaches, and the miner-god is honoured at the first strike into virgin rock. The Smith God is honoured at the hearth, where the metal is brought to its proper shape. The three are honoured in the order of the work, fixed by the labour of the hold.

The Smith God is sometimes invoked together with the miner-god at the first smelting of newly brought ore. The metalworker thanks the miner-god for the ore and the Smith God for the fire. The Dark Keeper is invoked in the galleries where the lamps burn poorly.

## Observance Among Other Peoples

The Smith God is honoured chiefly by the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the mountain ranges, with few adherents among other peoples. Smiths of lowland towns of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) have adopted some customs, particularly speaking a word as the first hammer falls, but they do not share the full discipline of the mountain cult. Mountain smiths do not seek converts beyond the holds.

Human smiths of villages at the foot of the mountain passes have adopted a fragment of the observance, particularly the rite of the first casting, a borrowing from dwarven custom acknowledged by both peoples.

## Significance

The Smith God is the first of the three greater powers of the [Stone Reading](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/stone-reading), and his observance is the foundation of the metalworking tradition of the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the mountain spine. The reverence for the first hearth, the formalisation of the first casting as a religious obligation, and the integration of the figure into the daily round of the forge have contributed to the unity of the mountain smiths across their settlements. The Smith God is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the smiths who depend upon the fire for their livelihood, whose continuation depends upon the fire's blessing.
