## Overview

Among the three greater powers of the [Old Gods of the Mountains](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-gods-of-the-mountains), the Smith-God is reckoned the first, the one who is invoked before the others at the kindling of a new hearth and at the casting of the first metal of the working year. He is the power of the forge, the fire that will not let the bellows go out, the heat that wakes the ore, and the patient anvil upon which the metal is brought to its proper shape. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the holds of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and of the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) hold him to dwell within the fire itself, and the older songs of the stoneworkers say that the sound of a hammer falling true is the sound of his attention turning toward the work. The chronicler of the mountain rites records that the Smith-God has been honoured since the founding of the first hold, and that his cult is the most uniform of the three greater powers, for the working of metal is a single discipline that recognises no boundary between the clans.

## The Figure and the Forge

The Smith-God is conceived as the living presence of the forge-fire and the worked metal, and his domain extends from the small hearth of a village smith to the great forges of the mountain cities. He is the heat that gathers in the belly of the furnace, the bright orange of the metal at the moment of the drawing, the dull red of the cooling bar, and the grey ash of the spent fire. The chronicler of the stoneworkers has set down that the figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, and that the figure is honoured by every act of working metal as much as by any formal rite.

The principal dwelling of the Smith-God is the forge itself, the enclosed space in which the fire is fed and the metal is brought to its proper heat. The forge is not a temple in the formal sense, and the forge is not set apart from the workshop in which the smiths labour. The fire is the figure, and the figure is honoured by the careful tending of the fire and by the customary words that are spoken when the first bellows is pumped. The chronicler records that the forge is consecrated by use, and that a new forge becomes a dwelling of the figure only after the first casting has been completed and the customary offering has been made.

The anvil is the second dwelling of the figure, and the anvil is honoured with a short invocation before any metal is laid upon it. The smith places the bar upon the anvil and speaks the customary words, asking the Smith-God to receive the work and to give it the proper shape. The chronicler notes that the words have changed little across the generations, and the words are the same in every hold from the deep interior of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) to the mountain spine of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The custom is observed with the same care by the smiths of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) and by those of the [Deep Realm of Karhold](/content/government/national/deep-realm-of-karhold), and the common observance is the foundation of the unity of the cult.

## The First Hearth and the First Casting

The kindling of a new hearth is one of the principal occasions of the Smith-God's honour, and the kindling is conducted according to a ritual that is observed in every hold. A new hearth is built in a workshop that has not previously contained one, and the hearth is constructed of firebrick and stone in the customary dimensions that the guild of smiths has established. The fuel is laid with care, and the first fire is kindled by the master smith of the workshop in the presence of the apprentices and the journeymen. The master speaks the customary words of invocation, asking the Smith-God to enter the hearth and to abide there as long as the workshop stands. The chronicler records that the words are brief and unadorned, and the words are repeated by the apprentices in turn as a sign of their acceptance of the master and of the hearth.

The first casting of the season is the second principal occasion of the figure's honour, and the casting is conducted with a ceremony that the chronicler of the stoneworkers has described at length. The first substantial bar of the working year is poured at the first convenient moment after the spring thaw, and the bar is offered to the Smith-God before any of the metal is shaped. The offering is a small portion of the bar, set aside before the smith begins the day's work, and the offering is left upon the anvil until the next fire is kindled. The chronicler notes that the offering is not a sacrifice in the sense that the term is ordinarily used, and the offering is not a gift intended to purchase the figure's favour. The offering is an acknowledgement that the figure is present and that the metal is the figure's to give.

The festival of the opening of the forge is held at the first casting, and the festival is the occasion for the gathering of the smiths of the hold. The master smiths of the principal workshops attend, and the apprentices and the journeymen attend with them. The festival is not a feast in the usual sense, though a meal is shared after the casting is complete. The festival is a renewal of the bond between the smiths and the figure, and the festival is the occasion upon which the guild sets the production of the year. The chronicler records that the festival has been observed in the same form in every hold for as long as the records preserve, and the festival is one of the most uniform of the mountain rites.

## The Rites of the Forge

The daily rites of the forge are observed by every smith in every hold, and the rites are the most regular expression of the figure's honour. The fire is kindled at the customary hour, and the smith speaks a brief invocation as the first bellows is pumped. The metal is laid upon the hearth, and the smith watches for the colour that indicates the proper heat. The smith draws the metal from the fire and lays it upon the anvil, and the smith speaks a brief word as the first hammer falls. The chronicler records that the words are few and are spoken in a low voice, and the words are not intended for the ears of the apprentices. The words are the smith's private acknowledgement of the figure, and the words are spoken as a sign that the smith knows the figure to be present.

The quench is honoured with a short word, and the word is spoken as the metal is plunged into the cooling trough. The chronicler notes that the colour of the steam that rises from the quench is observed with care, and the colour is taken as an indication of the figure's reception of the work. A clean and straight column of steam is held to be a sign of favour; a ragged and curling column is held to be a sign that the work requires correction. The smith returns the metal to the fire and works it again, and the figure is honoured in the correction as much as in the original shaping. The chronicler records that a smith who neglects the observation of the steam is held to be careless, and the smith's work is watched with greater attention by the master of the workshop.

The closing of the forge is conducted with a short word of thanks, and the word is spoken as the fire is allowed to die down for the night. The smith cleans the anvil and the tools, and the smith covers the bellows with the customary cloth. The smith does not quench the fire, for the fire is the figure, and the figure must be allowed to rest in the manner of a living thing. The chronicler notes that the practice is uniform across every hold, and the practice is one of the most distinctive of the mountain observances.

## The Mountain Smiths and the Stoneheim Tradition

The smiths of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) are the most renowned of the mountain smiths, and the tradition of the Stoneheim forges is the standard against which the other holds measure their work. The [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) smiths have developed a body of technique that is the foundation of the reputation of the realm, and the techniques are passed from master to apprentice in the customary manner. The master does not teach the technique as a set of rules but as a way of attending to the work, and the apprentice learns by long observation and by the master's correction of the apprentice's first attempts. The chronicler records that the Stoneheim tradition has been maintained for many centuries, and the tradition is the foundation of the unity of the cult across the mountain ranges of the continent.

The smiths of [Khazgar](/content/location/settlement/city/khazgar) are renowned for the decorative metalwork that the forges of the city produce, and the work of the Khazgar smiths is held to be the most refined expression of the figure's favour. The decorative objects that the Khazgar smiths produce are sought by the rulers and the wealthy of every continent, and the objects are among the most valued products of the dwarven craft tradition. The smiths of [Ironhollow](/content/location/settlement/town/ironhollow) are renowned for the iron that they produce, and the iron of Ironhollow is the foundation of the metalwork of the other holds. The chronicler records that each of the principal holds has its own speciality, and the specialities are not in competition but in complement, and the Smith-God is honoured in each as the giver of the work.

The [Deep Realm of Karhold](/content/government/national/deep-realm-of-karhold) maintains a parallel tradition, and the Karhold smiths are renowned for the durability of the metalwork that their forges produce. The Karhold tradition is older than that of Stoneheim by several generations, and the Karhold smiths are held in particular respect by the smiths of the other holds. The chronicler records that the two traditions have influenced one another over the centuries, and the influence is to be seen in the shared techniques and the shared forms of the objects that the smiths produce. The Smith-God is honoured in the exchange of technique, and the figure is the foundation of the unity of the cult across the two great dwarven realms.

## The Work and the Closed Hearth

The teaching of the closed hearth is one of the most distinctive of the customs that surround the Smith-God, and the teaching is a counterpoint to the teaching of the closed vein that belongs to the miner-god. A closed hearth is one that has fallen into disuse, and the hearth is treated with the same care that is given to a closed vein. The master of the workshop does not dismantle the hearth, and the master does not remove the anvil. The hearth is left as it is, and the anvil is left upon it, and the fire is allowed to die and is not rekindled. The chronicler records that the closed hearth is honoured with a short word, and the word is the smith's acknowledgement that the work of the hearth is complete. The teaching holds that the Smith-God dwelt in the hearth for as long as the hearth was in use, and the figure withdraws when the work is done, and the figure does not return to a hearth that has been closed.

The opening of a new hearth in a workshop that has previously contained a closed hearth is conducted with a particular ceremony, for the new fire is held to be a renewal and not a continuation. The smith speaks the customary words of invocation, and the smith does not address the figure as one who has returned. The smith addresses the figure as one who is invited, and the invitation is a fresh request for the figure's presence. The chronicler notes that the practice is uniform across the holds, and the practice is one of the most distinctive of the customs that mark the mountain smiths as a people apart from the smiths of the lowlands.

## Relation to the Other Powers

The Smith-God is the first of the three greater powers, and his position is fixed by the working of metal, which is the first of the mountain crafts. The keeper of the dark is honoured in the deep places where no light reaches, and the miner-god is honoured at the first strike into virgin rock. The Smith-God is honoured at the hearth, and the hearth is the place where the metal is brought to its proper shape. The three figures are not in competition, and the three are not ranked by their importance. The three are honoured in the order of the work, and the order is fixed by the labour of the hold.

The chronicler records that the Smith-God is sometimes invoked together with the miner-god, and the invocation is the customary form at the first smelting of ore that has been newly brought from the deep. The metalworker thanks the miner-god for the ore and the Smith-God for the fire, and the dual invocation is the formal acknowledgement of the two powers that have contributed to the work. The keeper of the dark is invoked in a different context, and the invocation belongs to the galleries where the lamps burn poorly. The chronicler notes that the three figures are honoured in their proper places, and the figure of the Smith-God is the dwelling of the forge and the fire.

## Observance Among Other Peoples

The Smith-God is honoured chiefly by the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of the mountain ranges, and the figure has few adherents among the other peoples of the known world. The smiths of the lowland towns of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) have adopted some of the customs of the mountain smiths, particularly the custom of speaking a word as the first hammer falls, but the lowland smiths do not share the full discipline of the mountain cult. The chronicler records that the mountain smiths do not seek converts beyond the holds, and the mountain smiths do not travel to the lowlands to teach their craft. The craft is taught to the apprentices of the holds, and the apprentices are the foundation of the continuity of the tradition.

The human smiths of the villages that lie at the foot of the mountain passes have adopted a fragment of the observance, particularly the rite of the first casting, and the rite is conducted with a short word of invocation that the chronicler of the mountain rites has recorded as a borrowing from the dwarven custom. The borrowing is acknowledged by both peoples, and the borrowing is not regarded as a conversion. The chronicler notes that the figure is honoured in the borrowing as the power of the forge, and the figure is not honoured as a god of the dwarven holds. The distinction is observed with care, and the distinction is one of the foundations of the peaceful coexistence of the two peoples upon the mountain.

## Significance

The Smith-God is the first of the three greater powers of the [Old Gods of the Mountains](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/old-gods-of-the-mountains), and his observance is the foundation of the metalworking tradition that has distinguished the [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves) of [Morvind](/content/location/natural/continent/morvind) and the mountain spine for centuries beyond reckoning. The figure endures because the figure asks nothing of his adherents that contradicts their daily labour, and the figure offers nothing that undermines the work of the hold. The reverence for the first hearth, the formalisation of the first casting as a religious obligation, and the integration of the figure into the daily round of the forge have all contributed to the unity of the mountain smiths across the wide range of their settlements. The chronicler finds in the Smith-God one of the most patient and least displaced of the figures of the known world, honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the smiths who depend upon the fire for their livelihood and whose livelihood's continuation depends upon the fire's blessing. The figure endures, in the forge and at the anvil, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the worked metal.
