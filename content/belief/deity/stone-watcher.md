## Overview

The Stone Watcher is the fourth of the four greater spirits of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), the power who is honoured at the standing stones, the burial mounds, and the long line of the ancestors whose memory the plains have not surrendered. He is the patient stone that has stood since the first of the kindreds was laid to rest in its shadow, the barrow that marks the place of one whose name is still spoken by the elders, and the long memory of the land itself, the memory that outlasts the grass and the herd and the people who tend them. The pastoral peoples of the southern grasslands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds who range the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) hold him to dwell within the standing stones and the burial mounds themselves, and the older songs of the plains peoples say that the wind that hums through the stones at evening is the figure's breath, and the breath carries the names of those who have gone before. The chronicler of the plains rites records that the Stone Watcher has been honoured since the first of the kindreds set a stone above the bones of their dead, and that his cult is the most enduring of the four greater spirits, for the memory of the ancestors is the foundation of every claim upon the land.

## The Figure and the Standing Stone

The Stone Watcher is conceived as the living presence of the standing stone and the burial mound, and his domain extends from the single menhir that a kindred has set upon a hill to the great rings of stone that mark the gathering places of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation). He is the rough granite that has been raised by the hands of the forebears, the weathered limestone that has stood since the first of the kindreds was laid to rest, the moss that creeps up the northern face of every ancient stone, and the long shadow that the standing stone casts at the hour of the evening. The chronicler of the plains records has set down that the figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, and that the figure is honoured by the careful tending of the stones as much as by any formal rite.

The principal dwelling of the Stone Watcher is the standing stone itself, the single monolith that the kindred has set upon a rise of ground that commands a view of the surrounding grass. The stone is not a temple in the formal sense, and the stone is not enclosed or set apart from the surrounding land. The stone stands where the elders have chosen to set it, and the place of the stone is the place of the kindred's memory. The chronicler records that the figure is honoured by the pastoralist who passes the stone and who leaves a small token of wool or of milk at its base, and the token is the pastoralist's acknowledgement that the figure is present and that the memory of the ancestors is unbroken. The token is taken by the elders and is offered at the next gathering, and the offering is the formal act of the kindred's devotion.

The ring of stones is the second dwelling of the figure, and the ring is honoured with a ceremony that the chronicler has described at length. The ring is a circle of standing stones that the kindreds of the plains have set around a sacred space, and the ring is the gathering place of the kindreds at the turning of the year. The ring is not entered save at the appointed times, and the pastoralists do not pasture their herds within the ring. The ring is held to be the place where the figure's memory is most concentrated, and the ring is the dwelling of the figure in the formal sense. The chronicler notes that the rings are of different ages, and the older rings are held in greater respect than the newer, for the older rings have stood through more generations and have absorbed more of the figure's long memory.

## The Burial Mound and the Ancestors

The burial mound is the third dwelling of the Stone Watcher, and the mound is the foundation of the cult's concern for the dead. The pastoralists of the plains bury their dead in mounds that are raised upon the open ground, and the mounds are marked by a single stone at the head of the grave. The mound is the dwelling of the figure in the sense that the figure is the keeper of the mound, and the figure holds the memory of the one who has been laid to rest. The chronicler records that the figure is honoured by the relatives of the dead who visit the mound and who leave a token of milk and of wool at the headstone, and the token is the relatives' acknowledgement that the dead are held in the figure's memory. The token is taken by the relatives at the next visit, and the taking is the formal sign that the dead have been released from the claim of the living.

The figure is also honoured in the rites of the passing, and the rites are conducted by the elders of the kindred at the burial mound. The elder speaks the customary words of commendation, asking the Stone Watcher to receive the dead into the long memory of the land. The elder names the dead by the customary name, and the naming is the formal act of the kindred's recognition. The chronicler records that the words are brief and are spoken in a low voice, and the words are not intended for the ears of the young. The words are the elder's private acknowledgement of the figure, and the words are spoken as a sign that the elder knows the figure to be present and that the dead are about to be commended to his keeping.

The chronicler notes that the burial mounds are scattered across the plains in a pattern that the pastoralists have long observed, and the pattern is held by the elders to be the figure's own arrangement. The pastoralists do not set a new mound in a place that has not already been marked by a stone, and the pastoralists do not move the stones that the forebears have set. The teaching holds that the figure has chosen the places of the stones, and the figure's choice is not to be gainsaid. The chronicler records that the practice is conservative by design, and the practice is one of the foundations of the unity of the cult across the wide range of the plains settlements.

## The Long Memory

The Stone Watcher is the power of the long memory, and the long memory is the foundation of the plains tradition of careful record. The chronicler records that the figure is held to remember every stone that has been set upon the plains and every name that has been spoken at the burial mound, and the figure's memory is the model for the memory of the elders. The wandering singers who carry the songs of the kindreds from one camp to another are the keepers of the long memory, and the singers are held in particular respect by the pastoralists and the [centaurs](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) alike. The chronicler records that the singers are the living memory of the figure, and the singers are the foundation of the continuity of the cult.

The chronicler notes that the long memory is observed most strictly in the matter of the boundaries, for the boundaries of the kindreds' grazing grounds are marked by the standing stones that the forebears have set, and the boundaries are not to be altered. The pastoralists do not move the boundary stones, and the pastoralists do not pasture their herds beyond the boundary stone without the permission of the neighbouring kindred. The teaching holds that the figure has marked the boundaries, and the figure's marking is not to be gainsaid. The chronicler records that the practice has been the foundation of the peaceful coexistence of the kindreds across the wide range of the plains, and the practice is one of the most distinctive of the customs that mark the plains peoples as a people apart.

The elders of the kindreds maintain small books in which the names of the standing stones and the burial mounds are recorded, and the books are the formal archives of the long memory. The books are passed from elder to elder, and the books are added to as new stones are set and as new mounds are raised. The chronicler records that the books are consulted whenever a dispute arises over a boundary or over the claim of a kindred to a particular piece of ground, and the books are the evidence by which the dispute is settled. The practice of the long memory is one of the foundations of the plains discipline, and the practice distinguishes the pastoral peoples of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) from the settled peoples of the lowland towns, whose records are kept in different forms and whose memory of the land is less immediate.

## The Boundaries

The Stone Watcher is the keeper of the boundaries, and the boundaries are the formal expression of the kindreds' claim upon the grazing grounds. The boundaries are marked by the standing stones that the forebears have set, and the boundaries are the figure's own arrangement. The pastoralists do not cross a boundary stone without the permission of the neighbouring kindred, and the pastoralists do not pasture their herds upon ground that has not been marked by a stone of the figure's choosing. The chronicler records that the practice has been the foundation of the peaceful coexistence of the kindreds, and the practice is one of the most distinctive of the customs that mark the plains peoples as a people of order and of memory.

The figure is honoured in the rite of the boundary stone, and the rite is conducted at the turning of the year when the kindreds gather at the ring of stones to renew their bonds. The elder of each kindred visits the boundary stones of the kindred's ground, and the elder leaves a token of wool at each stone and speaks the customary words of acknowledgement. The chronicler records that the words are brief and are spoken in a low voice, and the words are the elder's acknowledgement that the figure is the keeper of the boundary and that the kindred is the keeper of the ground. The practice is uniform across the kindreds of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation), and the practice is one of the most distinctive of the customs that mark the plains peoples as a people apart.

## Relation to the Other Spirits

The Stone Watcher is the fourth of the four greater spirits, and his position is fixed by the order of the pastoral life. The [Sky Walker](/content/belief/deity/sky-walker) is the spirit of the open heavens and the weather, and the Sky Walker is honoured first at the customary gatherings because the sky is the first of the gifts that the spirits bestow. The [Pasara](/content/belief/deity/pasara) is the spirit of the grazing beasts and of fertility, and the Pasara is honoured at the birthing and at the founding of a new herd. The [River Speaker](/content/belief/deity/river-speaker) is the spirit of the waters that cut the plains, and the River Speaker is honoured by those kindreds whose grounds lie along the great rivers. The Stone Watcher is the spirit of the standing stones and the buried dead, and the Stone Watcher is honoured last at the gathering because the memory of the ancestors is the foundation of every other claim.

The four spirits are not in competition, and the four are not ranked by their importance. The four are honoured in the order of the pastoral life, and the order is fixed by the labour of the kindred. The chronicler records that the Stone Watcher is sometimes invoked together with the Pasara, and the invocation is the customary form at the founding of a new herd upon a piece of ground that has been newly opened. The Pasara is asked to give the herd, and the Stone Watcher is asked to hold the ground in his memory. The Sky Walker and the River Speaker are invoked in different contexts, and the invocations belong to the rains and to the waters.

## Observance Among the Centaurs and the Pastoralists

The observance of the Stone Watcher is most particular among the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains), and the observance is the foundation of the centaur tradition of careful record. The centaurs are the oldest of the plains peoples, and the centaurs have maintained the figure's cult in a form that the human pastoralists have adopted in many of its particulars. The chronicler records that the centaur kindreds are the keepers of the long memory in its most rigorous form, and the centaur singers are the most respected of the wandering singers of the plains. The centaurs do not set a new stone without the counsel of the elders of three neighbouring kindreds, and the counsel is the formal sign that the figure's arrangement is observed.

The human pastoralists of the southern grasslands honour the figure with the same care, and the human observance is in most particulars identical to the centaur observance. The chronicler notes that the two peoples are united in the cult of the standing stone, and the unity is the foundation of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) and of the political arrangements that the confederation has maintained for centuries. The human pastoralists do not claim to have originated the cult, and the human pastoralists acknowledge the centaur kindreds as the elders of the figure's honour. The chronicler records that the acknowledgement is observed with care, and the acknowledgement is one of the foundations of the peaceful coexistence of the two peoples upon the plains.

The elven foresters of the northern forest edge have adopted a fragment of the observance, particularly the custom of leaving a token at a standing stone that lies at the margin of the wood, and the custom is held by the elves to be a kindred practice. The chronicler notes that the figure is honoured in the borrowing as the spirit of the stone, and the figure is not honoured as a god of the plains peoples. The distinction is observed with care, and the distinction is one of the foundations of the peaceful coexistence of the elves and the pastoralists along the forest edge.

## Significance

The Stone Watcher is the fourth of the four greater spirits of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and his observance is the foundation of the pastoralist tradition of careful record that has distinguished the peoples of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) for centuries beyond reckoning. The figure endures because the figure asks nothing of his adherents that contradicts their daily labour upon the grazing grounds, and the figure offers nothing that undermines the work of the kindred. The reverence for the standing stone, the formalisation of the burial rite as a religious obligation, and the integration of the figure's long memory into the records of the elders have all contributed to the unity of the plains peoples across the wide range of their settlements. The chronicler finds in the Stone Watcher one of the most patient and least displaced of the figures of the known world, honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the pastoralists who depend upon the land for their livelihood and whose livelihood's continuation depends upon the figure's continued memory. The figure endures, at the standing stone and at the burial mound, as the living inheritance of those whose work and whose life are bound up with the long memory of the plains.
