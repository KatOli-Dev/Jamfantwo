## Overview

The Stone Watcher is the fourth of the four greater spirits of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), honoured at the standing stones, burial mounds, and the long line of the ancestors whose memory the plains have not surrendered. He is the patient stone that has stood since the first of the kindreds was laid to rest in its shadow, the barrow that marks the place of one whose name is still spoken by the elders, and the long memory of the land itself, the memory that outlasts the grass and the herd and the people who tend them. The pastoral peoples of the southern grasslands of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar) and the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds who range the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains) hold him to dwell within the standing stones and burial mounds themselves. The older songs of the plains peoples say that the wind humming through the stones at evening is the figure's breath, carrying the names of those who have gone before. The Stone Watcher has been honoured since the first of the kindreds set a stone above the bones of their dead, and his cult is the most enduring of the four greater spirits, for the memory of the ancestors is the foundation of every claim upon the land.

## The Figure and the Standing Stone

The Stone Watcher is conceived as the living presence of the standing stone and the burial mound. His domain extends from the single menhir that a kindred has set upon a hill to the great rings of stone marking the gathering places of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation). He is the rough granite raised by the hands of the forebears, the weathered limestone that has stood since the first of the kindreds was laid to rest, the moss creeping up the northern face of every ancient stone, and the long shadow the standing stone casts at evening. The figure is not a person in the human sense but a presence, honoured by the careful tending of the stones as much as by formal rite.

The principal dwelling of the Stone Watcher is the standing stone itself, the single monolith set upon a rise of ground commanding a view of the surrounding grass. The stone is not enclosed or set apart from the surrounding land. It stands where the elders have chosen to set it, and its place is the place of the kindred's memory. A pastoralist passing the stone leaves a small token of wool or milk at its base, an acknowledgement that the figure is present and the memory of the ancestors is unbroken.

The ring of stones is the second dwelling, honoured with ceremony. A circle of standing stones set around a sacred space serves as the gathering place of the kindreds at the turning of the year. The ring is not entered except at appointed times, and herds are not pastured within it. The older rings are held in greater respect than newer ones, having stood through more generations and absorbed more of the figure's long memory.

## The Burial Mound and the Ancestors

The burial mound is the third dwelling of the Stone Watcher, the foundation of the cult's concern for the dead. Pastoralists bury their dead in mounds raised upon the open ground, marked by a single stone at the head of the grave. The figure is the keeper of the mound, holding the memory of the one laid to rest. Relatives visiting the mound leave a token of milk and wool at the headstone, acknowledging that the dead are held in the figure's memory.

The figure is also honoured in the rites of the passing, conducted by the elders at the burial mound. The elder speaks the customary words of commendation, asking the Stone Watcher to receive the dead into the long memory of the land. The elder names the dead by the customary name, the formal act of the kindred's recognition.

Burial mounds are scattered across the plains in a pattern held to be the figure's own arrangement. Pastoralists do not set a new mound in a place not already marked by a stone, and they do not move the stones the forebears set.

## The Long Memory

The Stone Watcher is the power of the long memory, the foundation of the plains tradition of careful record. The figure is held to remember every stone set upon the plains and every name spoken at the burial mound. The wandering singers who carry the songs of the kindreds from one camp to another are the keepers of the long memory, held in particular respect by pastoralists and centaurs alike.

The long memory is observed most strictly in the matter of boundaries. The boundaries of the kindreds' grazing grounds are marked by standing stones the forebears set, and the boundaries are not to be altered. Pastoralists do not move the boundary stones or pasture their herds beyond them without permission of the neighbouring kindred. The teaching holds that the figure has marked the boundaries, and his marking is not to be gainsaid. This practice has been the foundation of peaceful coexistence of the kindreds.

Elders of the kindreds maintain small books in which the names of standing stones and burial mounds are recorded, passed from elder to elder and added to as new stones are set and new mounds raised. The books are consulted whenever a dispute arises over a boundary or claim to a piece of ground.

## The Boundaries

The Stone Watcher is the keeper of the boundaries, the formal expression of the kindreds' claim upon the grazing grounds. Pastoralists do not cross a boundary stone without permission of the neighbouring kindred. The figure is honoured in the rite of the boundary stone at the turning of the year, when the kindreds gather at the ring of stones to renew their bonds. The elder of each kindred visits the boundary stones, leaves a token of wool at each, and speaks the customary words of acknowledgement.

## Relation to the Other Spirits

The Stone Watcher is the fourth of the four greater spirits, his position fixed by the order of the pastoral life. [Sky Walker](/content/belief/deity/sky-walker) is the spirit of the open heavens, honoured first. [Pasara](/content/belief/deity/pasara) is the spirit of the grazing beasts and fertility, honoured at birthing and founding of a new herd. [River Speaker](/content/belief/deity/river-speaker) is the spirit of the waters. The Stone Watcher is honoured last because the memory of the ancestors is the foundation of every other claim.

The four spirits are not in competition or ranked by importance. They are honoured in the order of the pastoral life, fixed by the labour of the kindred. The Stone Watcher is sometimes invoked together with Pasara at the founding of a new herd upon newly opened ground: Pasara is asked to give the herd, and the Stone Watcher to hold the ground in his memory.

## Observance Among the Centaurs and the Pastoralists

The observance of the Stone Watcher is most particular among the [centaur](/content/species/sapient/centaurs) kindreds of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains), the foundation of the centaur tradition of careful record. Centaurs are the oldest of the plains peoples and have maintained the figure's cult in a form human pastoralists have adopted in many particulars. Centaur kindreds are the keepers of the long memory in its most rigorous form, and centaur singers are the most respected of the wandering singers. Centaurs do not set a new stone without the counsel of elders of three neighbouring kindreds.

Human pastoralists honour the figure with the same care, identical in most particulars to the centaur observance. The two peoples are united in the cult of the standing stone, the foundation of the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation). Human pastoralists acknowledge the centaur kindreds as the elders of the figure's honour.

Elven foresters of the northern forest edge have adopted a fragment of the observance, particularly leaving a token at a standing stone at the margin of the wood, held to be a kindred practice.

## Significance

The Stone Watcher is the fourth of the four greater spirits of the [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way), and his observance is the foundation of the pastoralist tradition of careful record that has distinguished the peoples of the [Great Grass Plains](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/great-grass-plains). The reverence for the standing stone, the formalisation of the burial rite as a religious obligation, and the integration of the figure's long memory into the records of the elders have contributed to the unity of the plains peoples. The Stone Watcher is honoured not by a ruling class or a priesthood but by the steady labour of the pastoralists who depend upon the land for their livelihood, whose continuation depends upon the figure's continued memory.
