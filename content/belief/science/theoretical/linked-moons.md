## Overview

Linked Moons is the theoretical doctrine that the two moons of the world are bound to one another by an unseen influence, and that the changes of the one are reflected, after a measured interval, in the changes of the other. The school takes its name from this supposed link, and it holds that the lesser moon, known to navigators as the Wanderer, governs certain phenomena upon the earth that the greater moon, the Lady, does not govern, and that the two together produce effects that neither could produce alone. The hypothesis is reckoned among the theoretical sciences, for its conclusions are drawn from reasoning upon long records of celestial observation rather than from any direct demonstration of the link itself.

## The Two Moons

The peoples of the known world have observed from the earliest ages that the night sky is illuminated by two moons, of unequal size and unequal period. The greater moon, called the Lady, completes her circuit of the heavens in twenty-eight days and is held to govern the tides of the seas and the slower cycles of women's bodies. The lesser moon, called the Wanderer, completes his circuit in nineteen days, and his influence is reputed to be upon shorter matters, the swelling of sap, the ripening of certain fruits, and the mood of those who suffer in their spirits. The calendars of every nation of the world take account of both moons, and a date that names only one is reckoned incomplete.

The hypothesis arose among the astronomers of the libraries of [Vaela](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth) and [Nordhaven](/content/location/settlement/city/nordhaven), who, in comparing their long records of the two moons' positions, observed that certain changes in the lesser moon were followed, after an interval of several days, by corresponding changes in the greater. The correspondence was not perfect, and the astronomers were at first disposed to attribute it to the imperfection of their observations, but when the records of several generations were brought together and examined with care, the correspondence could no longer be dismissed.

## The Postulated Link

The astronomers of the school postulate a link between the two moons of a nature not yet understood, by which the motions of the one are communicated to the other across the intervening space. They are careful to disavow any explanation that would require the two bodies to be joined by a visible cord or by any material bond, and they propose instead an influence of the kind that the natural philosophers are only beginning to admit, a pull or sympathy that operates at a distance without intervening matter.

The hypothesis is set out in the principal treatise of the school, composed by a scholar of [Nordhaven](/content/location/settlement/city/nordhaven) and preserved in the libraries of the [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven). The treatise proposes that the link operates with a delay, so that a change in the Wanderer is not immediately reflected in the Lady but only after a measured interval of days. The interval is reported to vary with the position of the two moons in the heavens, and the astronomers of the school have constructed tables of the interval, by which, they claim, future positions of one moon may be predicted from observed positions of the other.

## Supporting Observations

The adherents of the hypothesis have assembled several kinds of observation in support of their doctrine, though none of the evidence is held to be conclusive by those outside the school. The first is drawn from the records of the tides, which the [Tide-Reading](/content/belief/science/physical/tide-reading) has long been accustomed to predict by the phases of the Lady alone. The astronomers of the linked moons observe that the predicted tides do not always arrive at the expected hour, and that the deviations are themselves periodic, and that the period of the deviations corresponds to the period of the Wanderer. The hypothesis holds that the deviation is explained by the unseen influence of the lesser moon, transmitted to the seas through the link with the greater.

A second piece of evidence is drawn from the observation of certain beasts, and in particular the [greyring whale](/content/species/beasts/greyring-whale), which the whalers of the western coast report to surface more frequently at the full of the Lady than at any other time, and to surface also, but less frequently, at a particular phase of the Wanderer. The hypothesis holds that the two surfacings are connected by the link, and that the behaviour of the whales is the surest evidence of the influence of the lesser moon.

## Difficulties and Objections

The hypothesis is not without its serious difficulties, and the more cautious of its adherents acknowledge them. The nature of the proposed link is unknown, and no instrument has yet been devised by which the supposed influence might be measured directly. The cartographers of the [Spherical Cartography](/content/belief/science/theoretical/spherical-cartography), who take a particular interest in celestial questions, observe that the influence would have to operate across an immense distance, and they ask how any influence could cross such a distance without the intervention of some medium, of which the school offers no account.

The astronomers of the school reply that the objection assumes the nonexistence of influences that act at a distance, and that this assumption is itself unproved. The natural philosophers of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), who have given the hypothesis a sympathetic hearing, observe that the falling of a stone to the ground is itself an influence that acts at a distance, and that no philosopher has yet explained how the stone finds the ground. The reply is not conclusive, but it serves to show that the difficulty is not peculiar to the hypothesis of the linked moons.

## Reception Among the Learned

The hypothesis has been received with caution by the astronomers of the great cities and with outright rejection by the practitioners of the physical sciences, who hold that an unobserved link is no link at all. The [Vael](/content/government/national/vael), where the doctrine of the celestial spheres is a long-established tradition, has given the hypothesis a hearing in the academies of [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth), but has not adopted it as a teaching of the kingdom's own schools. The mariners of the strait towns, who must trust their lives to the predictions of the astronomers, are divided in their allegiance, and many continue to use the older tables that take account only of the Lady.

The dispute is therefore a long and unresolved one, and the chronicler records it here without taking a side. The hypothesis may in time be confirmed by further observation, or it may pass from the memory of the learned as one of the more elegant of the speculations that the theoretical sciences are apt to produce when observation is uncertain.

## Significance

Linked Moons is, in the judgment of the chronicler, among the more interesting of the theoretical doctrines that have been advanced in recent generations, for it has directed the attention of scholars to the correspondence between celestial phenomena that had previously been studied separately. Whether or not the link is real, the hypothesis has enlarged the careful keeping of celestial records, and the astronomers of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds) and the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles) have begun, in the schools that favour the doctrine, to keep their logs with a precision that the older astronomers did not require. The doctrine may yet prove to be a true account of the relation between the two moons, or it may serve only as a monument to the inquiring spirit of an age that would rather hazard an explanation than confess ignorance.
