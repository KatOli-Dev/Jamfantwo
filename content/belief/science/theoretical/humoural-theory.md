## Overview

Humoural Theory is the theoretical science of health and disease that ascribes the functioning of the living body to the balance of four internal substances: blood, phlegm, yellow bile, and black bile, which the practitioners term the humours. Health is the proper balance and free motion of these four substances within the body; disease is their imbalance, corruption, or stagnation. The doctrine is reckoned among the theoretical sciences, proceeding by reasoning upon observed signs of health and disease and upon the effects of remedies, rather than by any direct inspection of the humours themselves.

## The Four Humours

The doctrine distinguishes four humours, each with its own character and its own seat within the body. Blood is held to be warm and moist, produced in the liver from nourishment taken in by mouth, and flowing through the veins to every part of the body, giving heat and strength. Phlegm is held to be cold and moist, residing chiefly in the head and the lungs, where it cools the blood and lubricates the joints. Yellow bile is held to be warm and dry, secreted in the spleen and the stomach, where it sharpens the appetite and kindles digestion. Black bile is held to be cold and dry, secreted in the kidneys and the bowels, where it thickens the blood and gives stability to the frame.

The four humours are further associated with the four qualities of matter, the four seasons, and the four ages of human life. Blood predominates in spring and in the young, phlegm in winter and in the old, yellow bile in summer and in the mature, and black bile in autumn and in those of middle years. The physician who knows the humour that predominates in a patient may better understand the patient's disposition and the disorders to which he is most prone.

## The Causes of Disease

Disease arises when the balance of the humours is disturbed, either by an excess of one humour, a deficiency of another, the corruption of one of them, or its stagnation in some part of the body where it ought to flow. The disturbance may be brought about by several causes. The seasons are held to be powerful: a long winter may engender an excess of phlegm, and a dry summer may burn up the blood. The quality of food and drink is of great importance, and the physicians are particular in their prescriptions of diet. The emotions are also held to affect the humours: chronic anger inflames the yellow bile, while settled grief thickens the black bile.

Each disease has its seat in some particular humour, and the physician must first determine the humour affected before he may hope to cure. A fever with heat and thirst is held to be a disease of the blood, and a heavy cough with copious expectoration a disease of the phlegm. A wasting sickness with loss of appetite and sallow complexion is held to be a disease of the yellow bile, and a lingering melancholy with heaviness and lack of joy a disease of the black bile. The diagnosis of the humour affected is the first and most important step in the cure.

## The Art of Treatment

The physician treats disease by correcting the humour that is at fault. If the humour is in excess, it must be drawn off or reduced; if deficient, replenished; if corrupted, purified; if stagnant, moved. The means of correction are of several kinds. Diet is the first and gentlest: the physician prescribes foods of the quality opposite to the humour in excess, so that an excess of warm and moist blood is treated with cool and dry foods, and an excess of cold and moist phlegm with warm and dry foods. The second means is purging and bleeding, by which the offending humour is drawn out of the body. The third means is the use of simples and compounded remedies drawn from the herbs of the field and the bark of the forest, prepared according to the ancient formulae of the doctrine.

The physicians of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra) and [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) are reputed among the most skilled, and the great hospitals of those cities are renowned for their careful keeping of case books, in which the humours diagnosed and the remedies prescribed are recorded against the course of the disease. The [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact), whose people have a particular regard for the art of healing, supports a college of physicians at [Vashari](/content/location/settlement/city/vashari) where the doctrine is taught to apprentices under the direction of elder masters.

## Difficulties and Objections

The four humours have never been directly observed, and no anatomist has yet succeeded in isolating them within the body. The natural philosophers of the theoretical sciences observe that the assumed correspondence between the humours and the seasons is not confirmed by the more accurate observations of the astronomers, who report that the seasons vary in their onset from year to year. The physicians reply that the correspondence with the seasons is approximate and not precise, and that the experienced physician takes account of the season as one sign among several.

The natural philosophers further object that the remedies prescribed sometimes fail, and that the failures are not accounted for by the doctrine itself but explained away by the unskillfulness of the physician or the resistance of the patient. The physicians reply that no art is infallible, and that the failures of particular physicians are not arguments against the doctrine itself.

## Reception and Influence

The doctrine is the prevailing theory of health and disease among the physicians of every nation of the known world, and the physicians of the [Aldreth](/content/government/national/aldreth) and the [Plains Confederation](/content/government/national/plains-confederation) are united in their adherence to it, even where they differ upon the details of its application. The [Still Flame](/content/belief/religion/monotheist/still-flame), whose temples maintain houses of healing in many cities, has given the doctrine a cautious approval, and the physicians of the temples are trained in the doctrine as well as in the scriptures of the faith. The [Sky Walkers' Way](/content/belief/religion/polytheist/sky-walkers-way) is more reserved, holding that the humours are subject to the disposition of the gods and that no remedy may avail without their favour.

Certain scholars maintain that the living body is governed by principles not yet understood, and that the doctrine, though useful in practice, is in theory an oversimplification. The critics are few, and their voices are little heeded by practising physicians, who find the doctrine sufficient for the daily work of healing.

## Significance

Humoural Theory is among the most influential of the theoretical sciences, governing the practice of every physician of the known world and touching every household in which the sick are tended. Whether or not the humours are real in the sense that the doctrine supposes, the doctrine has produced an art of diagnosis and treatment that has relieved more suffering than any other single body of learning, and its long history is a record of careful observation and painstaking record. The doctrine may in time be confirmed or give place to a better account of the body and its disorders. In either case it will be remembered as the principal medicine of its age.
