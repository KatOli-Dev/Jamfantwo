## Overview

The Philosophy of the Underlying Fire is the theoretical school that ascribes the shaping of the world's surface, the rising of its mountains, and the trembling of its land to the action of an inward fire seated beneath the solid crust. The school holds that the visible earth rests upon a deeper mass of molten and ever-moving matter, and that the movements of this matter are the true cause of the great geological events that the chroniclers have recorded since the earliest ages. The philosophy is reckoned among the theoretical sciences, for its conclusions are drawn from reasoning upon observed phenomena rather than from direct inspection of the supposed fire, which no one has seen and no instrument has yet reached.

## The Original Postulate

The doctrine arose among the stoneworkers of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim), whose delving into the deep rock brought them early acquaintance with the heat that increases as the miner descends. The deeper shafts of the Stoneheim holds, which pierce several hundred fathoms into the flank of the mountains, are reported by the delvers to grow steadily warmer with depth, until at the deepest reaches the air is scarcely bearable without constant ventilation, and the rock itself, where newly broken, will scorch the bare hand. The thanes of Stoneheim, whose authority extends over the workings, have long preserved the testimony of the delvers in the ledgers of the holds.

From this observation the scholars of the school reasoned that the heat cannot be local to the Stoneheim mountains alone, for similar reports reach them from the deep mines of the [Deep Realm of Karhold](/content/government/national/deep-realm-of-karhold) in the distant north, and from the workings of the dwarves of the [Holds of Stoneheim](/content/government/national/holds-of-stoneheim) along the western shore of [Aethelar](/content/location/natural/continent/aethelar). The school therefore postulates an interior fire that underlies the whole of the world's surface, more intense in some regions than in others, but everywhere present at sufficient depth.

## The Behaviour of the Fire

The philosophers of the school describe the underlying fire as a mass in slow but perpetual motion, rising in some places and sinking in others, and conveying heat from the deeper parts of the world to the cooler surface. They observe that the [Kharesh Glass Spider](/content/species/beasts/kharesh-glass-spider), which is found only in the volcanic margins of the southern desert, requires heat that no surface sun could supply, and that the creatures are most numerous in the years following an eruption of the deep vents. The fire, in their account, is the parent of such heat and of the molten rock that issues from the craters of [Emberfall](/content/location/settlement/city/emberfall) and the other volcanic peaks of the known world.

The school further holds that the inward fire is the cause of the trembling of the earth, which is recorded in the chronicles of many lands. The great quake that damaged the city of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) in the reign of [Queen Sera of the Amber Vale](/content/people/historical/queen-sera-of-the-amber-vale) is set down by the philosophers as the consequence of a sudden shifting of the underlying fire beneath the [Amber Vale](/content/location/settlement/region/amber-vale), and the smaller tremours that are felt from time to time in the [Grey Coast](/content/location/settlement/region/grey-coast) are explained in the same way. The school argues that no other cause so well accounts for the observed facts, and that the alternative explanations offered by other sciences are wanting in coherence.

## The Question of Volcanoes

The handling of volcanoes is the principal stronghold of the philosophy, for the eruption of a mountain is a phenomenon so striking that no theory of the earth can afford to pass it by. The philosophers of the underlying fire hold that the volcanoes of the [Stormfall Coast](/content/location/natural/ecosystem/stormfall-coast) and the southern reaches of the [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean) are the openings by which the inward fire makes its way to the surface, and that the lava and ash that issue from them are the very matter of the deep. The people of [Emberfall](/content/location/settlement/city/emberfall), whose city stands in the shadow of an active peak, are well acquainted with the behaviour of the fire, and their records of eruptions are among the most detailed kept by any community of the known world.

The practitioners of the [Study of Waters and Currents](/content/belief/science/physical/study-of-waters-and-currents) object that the volcanoes are more usefully explained as the consequence of certain kinds of stone taking fire when exposed to air and water, a theory that does not require an inward mass of molten matter. The two explanations are not necessarily exclusive, the philosophers reply, for the burning of stone may itself be set alight by the deeper fire, and the debate between the schools upon this question is a long and vigorous one.

## Difficulties and Objections

The philosophy is not without its serious difficulties, and the more careful of its adherents acknowledge them. The proposed inward fire has never been directly observed, and the depth at which it is supposed to reside is so great that no mine or well has reached it. The cartographers of the [Cartography of the Spheres](/content/belief/science/theoretical/cartography-of-the-spheres), who take a particular interest in the question, observe that if the underlying fire were as widespread as the school supposes, the seas would long since have been boiled away, and they ask why the world is not yet consumed.

The philosophers reply that the surface of the world is preserved by the great mass of water upon it and by the rigid crust that separates the fire from the air, and that the consumption of the world is held back by a balance that the school cannot at present explain in full. The reply is not entirely satisfactory, and the philosophers know it, but they maintain that no other theory so well accounts for the observed warmth of the deep mines, the eruption of volcanoes, and the trembling of the earth.

## Centres of the Doctrine

The philosophy is most firmly held in the mining communities of [Stoneheim](/content/location/settlement/city/stoneheim) and [Karhold](/content/location/settlement/city/karhold), where the delvers' reports supply its foundation in observation. The scholars of the libraries of [Ashford](/content/location/settlement/city/ashford) and [Vaelmouth](/content/location/settlement/city/vaelmouth), where the geology of the western coast has long been a subject of inquiry, have given the doctrine a sympathetic hearing, and the [Thessaran League](/content/government/national/thessaran-league) maintains a small academy devoted to the study of the earth's interior. The [dwarves](/content/species/sapient/dwarves), whose kinship with the deep rock is proverbial, are counted among the principal adherents of the school, and individual [gnomes](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) of the deep workshops have contributed treatises upon the heat of mines.

## Significance

The Philosophy of the Underlying Fire is, in the judgment of the chronicler, among the more coherent of the theoretical sciences, for it brings under a single principle a great variety of observed phenomena that would otherwise require separate explanations. If its central postulate of a universal interior fire should in time be confirmed, the philosophy will be remembered as one of the great constructions of the human mind. If it should be refuted, it will at least have served to direct inquiry toward the deep places of the world, and to remind scholars that the surface upon which they walk rests upon a depth whose nature is not yet understood.
