## Overview

Ebb and Flow is the theoretical doctrine that accounts for the regular rising and falling of the seas, the swelling of certain waters at certain seasons, and the long-period variations of level observed upon the coasts of the known world. The theory holds that the waters of the world are in perpetual motion, drawn now toward one shore and now toward another by the action of celestial bodies, by the warming and cooling of the air, and by the slow movement of the world itself. The doctrine is reckoned among the theoretical sciences, proceeding by reasoning upon the observed behaviour of the waters rather than by direct measurement of the forces supposed to move them.

## The Original Doctrine

The theory arose among the natural philosophers of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), whose scholars in the generation after the principal treatises of the [Tide-Reading](/content/belief/science/physical/tide-reading) began to compare records of the tide kept by port towns of several coasts. The comparison revealed that the tides of different coasts did not all rise and fall at the same hour, and that the times of high water shifted in a regular manner from one coast to another. The scholars proposed that the tides were not local phenomena set up by the particular configuration of each coast, but the visible signs of a single great motion of the waters propagated across the seas from one region to another.

The principal treatise of the school holds that the waters of the world are drawn by the attraction of the two moons, and that they are also affected by the warming of the air above them and by the slow turning of the world beneath the heavens. The tides of the coasts are the local signs of these great motions, and careful observation at any coast may reveal what is happening in the open seas beyond.

## The Behaviour of the Tides

The theory offers an explanation for several patterns of the tides that mariners have long observed. The twice-daily rise and fall of the waters upon most coasts is explained as the consequence of the daily turning of the world beneath the two moons, and the greater height of the tides at the full of the Lady is explained as the consequence of the alignment of the two moons with the sun, by which their attraction is combined. The seasonal variation, in which tides are higher in some months than in others, is explained by the changing distance of the moons from the earth in the course of their circuits, and the long-period variation observed by the most careful port keepers is explained by a slow precession of the heavens above.

The school further holds that the behaviour of the tides differs in different seas, and that the peculiarities of each coast are to be explained by the local configuration of the shore and the depth of the water beyond. The tides of the [Velden Strait](/content/location/natural/feature/strait-of-velden), which rush through the narrows with great violence, are explained as the consequence of the narrowness of the passage and the great body of water that must force its way through. The tides of the [Auric Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/auric-ocean), which are small and uncertain, are explained as the consequence of the open reach of the water and the absence of any land to contain it.

## The Question of the Long Swell

The handling of the long swell, observed upon the coasts of the [Stormfall Sea](/content/location/natural/feature/stormfall-sea) and the open [Greyring Ocean](/content/location/natural/feature/greyring-ocean), is the principal stronghold of the theory. The long swell is a slow rise and fall of the water distinct from the ordinary tides, occurring at intervals of several days and held by the practitioners of the study of waters to be the consequence of distant storms. The philosophers of the theory propose that the long swell is in truth a tide of a different period, set up by the slow motion of the waters beneath the heavens, and that the storms of the open ocean are themselves the consequence of this slow motion.

The two explanations are not necessarily exclusive, for a distant storm may set the water in motion, and the motion so set up may persist after the storm has passed. The practitioners of the study of waters accept the explanation in part, but they hold that the long swell is more usefully explained as the direct consequence of the wind upon the open water.

## Difficulties and Objections

The forces that are supposed to move the waters have never been directly measured, and the philosophers admit that they are inferred from the regularities of the observed tides. The cartographers of the [Spherical Cartography](/content/belief/science/theoretical/spherical-cartography) observe that the regularities may be explained as well by the local configuration of each coast as by a single great motion of the waters.

The philosophers reply that the regularities are too numerous and widely distributed to be the consequence of local configuration alone, and that the hypothesis of a single great motion is the simplest account of what is observed. The reply is not conclusive, but they maintain that a theory which brings a great variety of phenomena under a single principle is to be preferred to one which leaves them separate.

The mariners of port towns, who must trust their lives to the tide tables, are divided in their allegiance, and many continue to use the older tables of the study of waters, which take account only of the observed times of high and low water at each port. The philosophers observe that the older tables do not explain why the times shift from one port to another.

## Centres of the Theory

The theory is pursued chiefly in the libraries of [Amurra](/content/location/settlement/city/amurra), where records of tide and current are longest kept, and in the academies of [Vael](/content/government/national/vael) and [Nordhaven](/content/government/national/nordhaven), where the motions of the moons are most carefully studied. The scholars of the [Auric Isles](/content/government/national/auric-isles), whose wide experience of the warm ocean gives them particular authority upon the behaviour of its waters, have contributed treatises upon the long swell and the seasonal variations of level. The [merfolk](/content/species/sapient/merfolk) of the southern coasts are said to keep their own records of the tides, and [Sael Pactguard](/content/people/notable/sael-pactguard) of the [Vashari Pact](/content/government/national/vashari-pact) is said to have composed a treatise upon the tides of the strait.

## Significance

Ebb and Flow is among the more elegant of the theoretical sciences, bringing under a single principle a great variety of phenomena previously studied separately. Whether or not the great motion of the waters is real in the precise form that the school supposes, the theory has directed the attention of scholars to the regularities of the tides and to the relation between the waters and the heavens above them. The theory may in time be confirmed or give place to a better account. In either case it will be remembered as one of the great monuments of the inquiring spirit.
