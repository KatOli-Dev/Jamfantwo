## Overview

Lodestone Lore is the practical discipline by which smiths, navigators, scholars, and workshop curiosi observe and record the peculiar attractions of the lodestone and the alignment of iron in the field of the world. The discipline originates in the observation that a piece of the dark stone of certain northern quarries turns upon a pivot until one face points toward the cold of the north, and that a needle of iron touched to the stone holds the same alignment. This observation is among the most remarkable of the practical disciplines, and the subject has been studied carefully by scholars of every academy.

The discipline is shared across the smithies of the [Stoneheim Holds](/content/government/national/stoneheim-holds), the workshops of the [gnome](/content/species/sapient/gnomes) communities, the academies of the coastal cities, and the navigators of the great ports. The lodestone is both a useful tool and a puzzling thing, and proper understanding of its behaviour is among the most important of the practical and theoretical inquiries of the age.

## The Behaviour of the Lodestone

The basic observation of the discipline is the behaviour of the lodestone, a piece of the dark stone from quarries of the [Frostwall Marches](/content/location/settlement/region/frostwall-marches) that turns upon a pivot until one face points north and the other south. The lodestone is mounted in a small wooden frame and floated in a bowl of water until it settles. The face pointing north is marked with a small notch, which becomes the reference for every subsequent use of the compass.

The lodestone does not point exactly north in every place, and the discipline maintains tables of the variation. Variation is the angle between the line of the lodestone and true north, differing from place to place and year to year. Navigators consult the tables of variation before setting out and correct their bearings accordingly. The tables are compiled by scholars of the academies and revised as new observations are made. The variation is one of the most puzzling features of the lodestone, and the tables are maintained as carefully as any other production of the discipline.

## The Needle and the Compass

The lodestone is too heavy and cumbersome for use at sea, so navigators use a needle of iron that has been touched to the lodestone. The needle is balanced upon a fine point in a small wooden box and allowed to settle. The face of the box is marked with bearings, and the bearing is read against the needle. The compass is small enough to be held in the hand and can be consulted at any moment by the pilot.

The compass must be made with care. The needle must be of good iron, drawn out fine and straightened carefully. The pivot must be set in the centre of the box, and the box must be level. The needle must be touched to the lodestone several times before mounting, using the marked face of the lodestone. The compass must be tested against the lodestone before use, and corrected if the test shows deviation. A carelessly made compass can mean the difference between a safe voyage and a wreck, so the discipline maintains these rules as one of its most important responsibilities.

## The Use of the Compass at Sea

The compass is the most useful of the navigator's tools. The pilot reads the bearing of the sun at noon and the stars at night, enters the bearings in the log, then consults the variation tables and corrects the bearings. The corrected bearings give the position of the vessel, which the pilot compares with the chart to determine the course to the desired port.

The compass must be consulted at every watch, and bearings entered in the log at the time of observation. A pilot who neglects the bearings is a danger to the vessel. The compass has made navigation of the open sea far safer than it was before the lodestone was known.

## The Behaviour of Iron in the Field

A separate set of observations concerns the behaviour of iron in the field of the world, forming the foundation of theoretical inquiries. A scholar places a needle of iron upon a pivot in a quiet room and observes its alignment, noting it against the time of day. The alignment is steady through most of the day but shifts during a storm. It is also altered when a piece of iron is brought near the needle. All such alterations are entered in the record.

The behaviour of iron in the field is one of the most promising inquiries of the present age, and every observation is recorded carefully. The records are consulted by scholars of the academies and form the foundation of theoretical branches concerned with the fields that surround the world. The practical observations of the lodestone and the needle have produced more theoretical inquiry than any other set of observations in the present age.

## Reception Among the Learned

The discipline is respected by every branch of learning that touches the navigation of the sea or the inquiry into the fields of the world. The scholars of the [Linked Moons](/content/belief/science/theoretical/linked-moons) consult navigators for the bearings of the stars, and the scholars of [Spherical Cartography](/content/belief/science/theoretical/spherical-cartography) consult navigators for the positions of vessels that have carried the charts. Navigators in turn consult scholars for the tables of variation and corrections that must be applied to the compass, and the consultation has produced several useful agreements.

The discipline contends with the theoretical branches upon the question of the nature of the field, for the field is not directly observed and explanations differ from one theoretical branch to another. The question is beyond the present observation of the practical disciplines, and the discipline must await the accumulation of more observations before it can be settled. The discipline is patient in the face of theoretical disputes, maintaining the practical observations as carefully as it has always done.

## Significance

Lodestone Lore is among the most useful of the practical disciplines, having produced the compass that made navigation of the open sea possible. The discipline has no famous disputes and no contested doctrines, because its conclusions are tested by the practical outcome of the voyage. A bearing that does not produce the expected position is rejected; a bearing that produces the expected position is entered in the log. The discipline has produced some of the most remarkable observations of the present age, and these observations are the foundation of theoretical inquiries that may one day explain the nature of the field that surrounds the world. The compasses of the great ports and the needles of the scholars are the most widely consulted productions of the discipline, and no responsible authority of any land neglects to maintain a careful set of the instruments.
