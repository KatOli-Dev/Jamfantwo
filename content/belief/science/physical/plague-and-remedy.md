## Overview

Plague and Remedy is the practical discipline by which healers, apothecaries, and public health watchers observe the patterns of disease, the conditions under which it spreads, and the remedies that have proven useful against it. The discipline is younger than the other practical disciplines, for the first systematic observations of plague were made only in the last several centuries, and it still has the character of an inquiry gathering its material rather than a settled body of knowledge. The discipline is shared across monasteries, academies, and public hospitals of the larger cities. Plague is the most dangerous enemy of the people, and the proper management of disease is among the most important responsibilities of any authority.

The discipline is empirical, and its conclusions are tested by the gradual agreement of independent observers in different places. A healer who reports a remedy that cannot be confirmed by others loses standing; a watcher whose records of the spread of a disease withstand checking is consulted by magistrates for decades. The discipline therefore maintains a strict code of record, with the place, time, conditions of the patient, and remedy used all noted alongside the outcome. The records of a careful watcher are the foundation of the discipline, consulted by every healer and magistrate.

## The Observation of the Patient

The basic work of the discipline is observation of the patient at the bedside. The healer notes the colour of the skin, the pulse, breathing, temperature, state of the bowels, and state of the urine. The healer notes the food the patient has taken, the water drunk, and the air breathed. The healer notes the season, weather, conditions of the dwelling, and whether other members of the household have fallen sick. The observation is recorded carefully, forming the foundation of diagnosis and remedy.

Observations must be made at fixed hours and compared from hour to hour. A patient whose pulse is steady and skin cool is recovering; a patient whose pulse is rapid and skin hot is worsening. The discipline maintains tables of the signs that indicate the progress of a disease, consulted by every healer of every land. The tables are revised as new observations are made. Careful observation can be the difference between recovery and death, and the discipline maintains it as one of its most important rules.

## The Catalogue of Remedies

The discipline maintains a catalogue of remedies, listing the medicines and treatments that have been used against the diseases healers have encountered. The catalogue includes the name of the remedy, the disease for which it was used, the conditions of the patient, the dose given, and the outcome. It is compiled by scholars of the monasteries and academies and revised as new remedies are introduced or new observations made. The catalogue of [Khalisar](/content/location/settlement/city/khalisar) is the most comprehensive of the warm coast, and the catalogue of [Vassholm](/content/location/settlement/city/vassholm) is the most comprehensive of the cold north.

The catalogue is consulted by every healer and is the foundation of the practice of healing. A healer who uses a remedy not in the catalogue is expected to test it carefully and record the test. No remedy is accepted without the test of repeated observation, and the records of every test are maintained in the archives. The careful work of an earlier healer often outlasts the institution that employed him, and the records of the healers of the monasteries are the foundation of the modern practice of healing.

## The Spread of Disease

A separate set of records concerns the spread of disease, forming the foundation of public health in every region. Public health watchers are appointed by magistrates of cities and regions to observe the appearance of disease in the populations under their care. The watcher notes the place, time, and conditions of the patient, and whether other members of the household or neighbourhood have fallen sick. The observation is entered in the public register, consulted by magistrates and healers of the region.

The spread of disease can be limited by separation of the sick from the well, by purification of the air and water, and by careful disposal of the dead. The discipline maintains a set of rules for the management of a plague, agreed upon by magistrates and healers of every land. A magistrate who neglects the rules is judged harshly, for neglect can be the difference between a contained outbreak and a devastation. The careful management of a plague has saved many lives, and the rules are maintained as carefully as the catalogues of remedies.

## The Use of the Apothecary

The apothecary prepares the medicines and is consulted by every healer. The apothecary maintains a shop in which the plants and minerals of the catalogue are prepared according to the prescribed methods. The apothecary tests the materials brought in by gatherers and merchants, rejecting those that do not meet the standard, and prepares medicines in small batches, labelling each batch with the date, ingredients, and method of preparation.

The apothecary is the custodian of the catalogue of remedies, and the discipline maintains a strict code of practice. An apothecary who sells a medicine that has not been tested is judged harshly, and one who sells a medicine past its date is judged even more harshly. The discipline maintains a register of every apothecary of every city, consulted by magistrates and healers. The careful work of an apothecary is the foundation of the practice of healing, and the standards of the apothecary are among the discipline's most important responsibilities.

## Reception Among the Learned

The discipline is respected by every branch of learning that touches the health of the people, though its practitioners rarely make theoretical claims. The scholars of [Humoural Theory](/content/belief/science/theoretical/humoural-theory) consult healers for the patterns of disease, and the scholars of [Plant-Lore](/content/belief/science/physical/plant-lore) consult apothecaries for the virtues of the plants used in healing. Healers in turn consult scholars for the principles that may guide the search for new remedies.

The discipline does not contend with the theoretical branches, because its conclusions are tested by the practical outcome of the patient. A remedy that does not produce the expected effect is rejected; a remedy that produces the expected effect is entered in the catalogue. The discipline has no famous disputes and no contested doctrines, because the test of the remedy is the test of the patient, and the test cannot be argued with. The practical disciplines have a particular authority that arises from this immediate confrontation with the world.

## Significance

Plague and Remedy is among the most useful and least controversial of the practical disciplines. Its conclusions enter the daily life of every healer, apothecary, magistrate, and army commander, and its records are the foundation upon which the health of the people of every land is maintained. The discipline has no famous discoveries and no spectacular disputes with other branches of learning, because its work is judged by the standard of practical agreement with the recovery of the patient. The catalogues of the monasteries and academies are the most widely consulted of its productions, and no responsible authority of any land neglects to maintain a catalogue of the remedies of its own region.
