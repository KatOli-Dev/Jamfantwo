## Origins

The origins of humanity are the subject of considerable debate among scholars. Unlike [elves](/content/elves) or [dwarves](/content/dwarves), whose early histories are preserved in long and continuous oral and written traditions, the earliest human records are fragmentary and difficult to reconcile. What is clear is that humans were present on Aethelar from a very early period, and that they spread to Morvind and Sahrana in antiquity, whether by migration, exploration, or settlement from coastal voyages.

Several human cultures preserve origin myths that place their ancestors in specific regions of Aethelar, usually in fertile river valleys or along the coasts. These myths rarely agree on particulars, and chroniclers have long since stopped trying to reconcile them into a single narrative. What they share is a common thread: humans emerged from the land they inhabited, shaped by it and in turn shaping it. Whether this reflects a historical memory or a shared cultural impulse toward belonging remains a matter of interpretation.

## Characteristics

Humans are a versatile and short-lived species, with a typical lifespan of seventy to ninety years under favourable conditions. They are of moderate height and build compared to other peoples, and they display considerable physical variation across regions and climates. This adaptability extends to temperament and culture. Humans are quick to adopt new ideas, new techniques, and new ways of organising themselves, and their societies change more rapidly than those of longer-lived peoples.

This brevity of life shapes much of the human outlook. Where elves measure events in centuries and dwarves in generations of craft, humans work within decades, and this lends their civilisations a particular energy and urgency. They build, trade, and govern with an awareness that their time is limited, and this has produced both remarkable achievements and periodic upheaval. Other peoples sometimes find human society restless or impatient, but the same quality has made humans the most widespread species in the known world.

## Distribution and Settlement

Humans are found on every continent and in nearly every climate the world offers. In Aethelar, they are the dominant population, with kingdoms and city-states scattered from the northern forests to the southern plains. The temperate river valleys of the continent's interior support the densest human populations, and the great cities of Aethelar are overwhelmingly human in character, though they typically host communities of other peoples as well.

In Morvind, humans settle chiefly along the coastal lowlands, where they fish, trade, and maintain close ties with the dwarven communities of the interior. The Morvindian humans have developed a distinctive culture shaped by the cold climate and the proximity of the sea. In Sahrana, humans are concentrated along the fertile northern coast, where their city-states share the region with elven and gnomish communities. The desert interior is home to small numbers of human nomads who travel alongside other peoples of the region.

## Society and Governance

Human political structures are diverse. Hereditary monarchies are common, particularly in Aethelar, where dynasties have ruled over kingdoms for centuries. Other regions have developed mercantile republics, council-based governments, and looser confederations of towns and villages. There is no single model of human governance, and the same kingdom may pass through several forms of rule within a few generations.

This political diversity is both a strength and a source of difficulty. Human societies are capable of rapid reform and innovation, but they are also prone to internal conflict when competing visions of order come into collision. The histories of Aethelar's kingdoms are records of rise, division, and renewal, as old dynasties give way to new ones and old institutions are reshaped to meet new circumstances. Yet from this flux has come much of the world's recorded progress, in scholarship, craft, and the arts of governance.

## Relations with Other Peoples

Humans live in closer and more frequent contact with other species than perhaps any other people in the known world. Their settlements often stand at the crossroads of trade routes and along the coasts where maritime peoples come to exchange goods, and this has made human cities the primary meeting places of the world's peoples. In the larger cities of Aethelar and Sahrana, humans, elves, dwarves, and others live side by side, sharing markets, districts, and sometimes institutions.

These relations are generally peaceable but not without friction. Differences in lifespan and custom can lead to misunderstandings, and humans are sometimes regarded by longer-lived peoples as hasty or short-sighted. Conversely, humans can find elven caution excessive or dwarven tradition inflexible. Such tensions are usually managed through trade, diplomacy, and the simple necessity of shared habitation. The history of human relations with other peoples is one of accommodation and mutual influence, and the cultures of the known world are richer for it.
