## Origins

The origins of the kappa are bound up with the rivers and lakes of the interior, for the kappa body bears the unmistakable mark of a people shaped by fresh water and the damp margins of the land. Kappa are an amphibious people, found in the rivers, lakes, and wetlands of the interior of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), and their bodies are adapted to a life that moves freely between water and land. Some naturalists have proposed a kinship between kappa and [merfolk](/content/merfolk), citing the aquatic adaptation of both peoples, but the differences are considerable, for the kappa are creatures of fresh water rather than salt, and their bodies retain a far greater capacity for terrestrial life. The kappa themselves preserve oral traditions that speak of their having emerged from the rivers of the interior at the dawn of the world, and they regard the fresh water as their first and truest home.

What is certain is that kappa have inhabited the rivers and lakes of the Aethelaran interior for as long as any people can account for, and their presence in these waters is attested in the oldest human and elven records. The kappa and the [humans](/content/humans) of the river valleys have coexisted for centuries, and the relationship between the two peoples is among the oldest and most interdependent of any in the known world.

## Anatomy and Physiology

Kappa are a small to medium people, standing shorter than [humans](/content/humans) but taller than [gnomes](/content/gnomes), with a compact and sturdy frame that is well suited to both swimming and walking. Their skin is smooth and moist, ranging from greenish to olive and brown, and it is thinner and more permeable than that of land-dwelling peoples, requiring regular immersion in water to remain healthy. Their limbs are proportioned for a life that alternates between water and land, with webbed fingers and toes that aid in swimming and strong, short legs that are capable of sustained walking.

The most distinctive feature of the kappa is the shell, a tough, domed carapace that covers the back and shoulders, providing protection and structural support. The shell is a natural part of the kappa body, grown from the same tissues as the scales and skin, and it ranges in colour from dark green to brown and grey. The shell is strong enough to turn aside a blow that would injure an unarmoured person, and it contributes to the kappa reputation for resilience. The head is broad and flat, with a dish-like depression on the crown that holds a pool of water, and the kappa must keep this pool filled, for if it empties, the kappa grows weak and disoriented. This singular feature is the most remarkable aspect of kappa physiology, and its function is not fully understood by naturalists.

Kappa senses are well adapted to both aquatic and terrestrial environments. Their vision is keen both above and below the water, and their eyes are large and set forward, granting good depth perception. Their hearing is competent in air and excellent in water, where sound travels more efficiently, and they are able to communicate with one another over considerable distances through submerged vocalisations. Their sense of smell is acute, particularly in water, and kappa can detect the chemical signatures of different water sources, a gift that serves them in navigation and the assessment of water quality.

## Life Cycle

Kappa are moderately long-lived, with a typical lifespan of sixty to ninety years under favourable conditions. They mature at a pace comparable to humans, reaching full stature and physical adulthood by their late teens. Kappa women carry their young for approximately seven months before laying a clutch of three to six eggs in a shallow nest dug into the muddy bank of a river or lake. The eggs are incubated for a further two months before hatching, and the hatchlings are small and aquatic, spending their first years entirely in the water before developing the legs and lungs that allow them to venture onto land.

Kappa populations grow steadily when the rivers and lakes are clean and the water is plentiful, and the young are raised communally, with the adults of the community sharing in their care and protection. The education of a kappa is long and practical, encompassing swimming, fishing, the knowledge of the rivers and their currents, and the traditions of the people. A kappa is not considered fully adult until they have mastered all of these, and the rites of passage that mark the transition include a journey from the headwaters to the mouth of their river, a test of endurance and knowledge that every kappa must complete.

## Habitat and Distribution

Kappa are found chiefly in the rivers and lakes of the interior of [Aethelar](/content/aethelar), where the water is fresh and the land is well watered. They favour the slower reaches of the great rivers, the still waters of the lakes, and the marshes and wetlands where the water spreads wide and shallow, and their settlements are typically built on the banks, combining structures above and below the waterline. The [geography](/content/geography) of each river system shapes the character of the kappa communities, and the kappa of the mountain streams differ in habit from those of the lowland marshes.

Smaller populations of kappa are found in the rivers and lakes of the Morvind coast, where the water is cold but clean, and in the river systems of the [Sahrana](/content/sahrana) interior, where the seasonal rivers provide a temporary habitat that the kappa share with the nomadic peoples of the desert. Kappa are absent from the sea, for their bodies are adapted to fresh water and they find salt water unpleasant and harmful, and they are not found in the arid regions where permanent water is scarce.

## Diet

The kappa diet is a mixture of aquatic and terrestrial foods, reflecting the amphibious nature of their way of life. Fish, crustaceans, and aquatic plants form the staple, gathered from the rivers and lakes by a variety of techniques including nets, traps, and the patient art of hand-fishing, for which the kappa are noted. Cucumbers and other watery vegetables are a particular favourite, cultivated in gardens along the riverbanks, and the kappa reputation for fine cucumber cultivation is recognised throughout the regions where they are known.

Meat is eaten in moderation, chiefly fish and waterfowl, and the kappa approach to food is communal and practical, for meals are prepared and shared by the community. The kappa are skilled in the preservation of food through drying and salting, and they store provisions for the winter months when the rivers are frozen and the fishing is poor. The kappa do not brew strong drink, for their constitution is sensitive to alcohol, but they are known for a light, fragrant tea made from water herbs, which is consumed in quantity and offered to guests as a gesture of hospitality.

## Ecology

Kappa occupy the niche of an amphibious forager and predator in the freshwater ecosystems they inhabit. Their impact on their surroundings is considerable, for the kappa management of the rivers and their banks sustains both the aquatic and the terrestrial life of the region. The kappa are careful stewards of the water, maintaining the quality and flow of the rivers through the management of vegetation, the clearing of obstructions, and the prevention of pollution, and their presence in a river system is generally associated with clean water and abundant life.

Kappa share their habitats with [humans](/content/humans) across much of their range, and the two peoples coexist closely, for their needs are complementary. The humans tend the land and the towns, while the kappa tend the water and the rivers, and each benefits from the produce and the skills of the other. [Merfolk](/content/merfolk) and kappa occasionally encounter one another where the rivers meet the sea, and the two peoples maintain a cautious but respectful distance, each recognising the other as a people of the water.
