# Skill (`content-page`)

Write information-dense prose in the manner of Wikipedia: paragraphs under `##` and `###` headings, no bullet lists for body prose. Link the first mention of any existing page as `[Name](/content/category/subcategory/page)` — full path with leading slash. Never use bold, italic, or the `#` top-level heading (handled by layout). No purple prose — no atmospheric scene-setting, no emotional adjectives, no metaphors, no simile, no poetic personification, no rhetorical flourishes.

## Frontmatter

Every content page begins with:

```yaml
---
layout: default
title: "Page Title"
description: "1–2 sentences, 120–200 characters, in-world voice, unique per page."
---
```

## Hard Constraints

- At least 1,000 words of body prose per page.
- No dead links — if a page does not yet exist, mention it in plain text until it does.
- Present multiple perspectives on contentious events.
- Avoid mechanical repetition across pages.
- Index pages are auto-generated by `_plugins/generate_category_indices.rb`; never maintain them by hand.
- Never address readers or reference the real world.
- Medieval fantasy tone — not grim or bleak; honest about hardship without exaggeration.
- No slang, modern idioms, or anachronisms.
- Prejudices are background reality, not narrative centrepieces; consolidate them on [content/culture/prejudices.md](/content/culture/prejudices).
- The content tree is extensible — new subdirectories may be added under any category.
- Be paranoid about inconsistent lore: check existing pages before introducing or repeating names, dates, places, relationships, terminology, or historical claims, and resolve contradictions in favour of established facts rather than inventing a compromise.

## Purple prose — what to avoid

Information-dense prose reports facts. Purple prose renders experience. The validator cannot catch every instance, so the writer must police themselves. These patterns are forbidden:

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Emotional adjectives in place of facts | "the most beautiful of all the desert cities", "among the most magnificent works" |
| Superlatives without measurable basis | "unlike any other in the known world", "the greatest dwarven city" |
| Simile and metaphor | "like curtains of light in the wind", "the spirit of the high peaks made flesh" |
| Atmospheric scene-setting | "a stone that glowed with a warm golden light at dawn and at dusk" |
| Poetic personification | "the desert remembers them still", "a great stillness that fell upon the desert" |
| Rhetorical closings | "a symbol of the endurance and achievement of the dwarven civilisation" |
| Sensory invocation | "the fragrance of the moonflower is sweet and heavy, the scent being carried on the air" |

### Rewriting purple into information-dense prose

Purple — superlatives and scene-setting:

> Ubar was built in a valley that was said to be the garden of the desert, a place of springs and groves and flowing water that the inhabitants had channelled through the city in marble aqueducts. The city was built of white stone that gleamed in the sun.

Information-dense — what is known, how, and why it matters:

> Ubar was built in a valley fed by the Sahrani karst aquifer, whose springs supported groves of date palm and poplar. Canals and cisterns of cut marble distributed water through the city, and the surviving aqueduct fragments indicate a system capable of servicing several thousand inhabitants. The city's limestone constructions have bleached under centuries of exposure.

Purple — atmospheric scene-setting and poetic personification:

> The lights that appear above the Stormfall Sea during the winter months are one of the most beautiful of the sea's phenomena. The Stormfall Lights are bands of colour that dance across the sky, green and purple and red, shifting and flowing like curtains of light in the wind.

Information-dense:

> The Stormfall Lights appear above the Stormfall Sea during the winter months, visible from the northern Aethelaran coast. They are bands of green, purple, and red that shift in position over the course of an evening. Mariners use their brightness and motion as a rough indicator of the approaching weather.

### Error of excess article use

In-world voice uses `the` before concrete nouns more often than modern English (`the collection of the kermes`, `the knowledge of the kermes collection`). This is deliberate and is not purple prose.

## Examples

### Prose style

Write in long, dense paragraphs that convey information without flourish. Sentences run together with commas where natural; the prose is expository, not persuasive. No bullet lists for narrative content.

```markdown
## The Market for Arms

Mercenary companies have been a feature of warfare in the known world since before the [Founding Era](/content/history/founding-era) and have grown in importance as the wealth of the established realms has increased. A well-trained company of mercenaries can provide a ruler with experienced soldiers who are ready to fight at a moment's notice, without the need to train them or to maintain them in peacetime. The cost is high — mercenaries expect regular pay, good provisions, and a share of the plunder — but for a realm that faces a sudden threat or that needs specialised troops it lacks, the expense is often justified.
```

### Linking

Link the first occurrence of any term that has its own page. Use the full path from `/content/`. If a page does not yet exist, mention the term in plain text — do not create a dead link.

```markdown
The collection of the kermes is a tradition that has been practiced for the centuries in the Sahrani interior — the [Sahrani](/content/language/spoken/sahrani) women who collect the kermes are the specialists who know the groves and the trees, and the knowledge of the kermes collection is passed from the mother to the daughter in the families that have collected the insects for the generations.
```

Link text should match the target page title as closely as possible. Do not write "click here" or "see this page" as link text.

Correct:

> The treaty was signed under the [Greywash Compact](/content/history/greywash-compact).

Incorrect — link text does not match page title:

> The treaty was signed by [the agreement](/content/history/greywash-compact).

### What to avoid

These patterns will cause the validator to fail. Write around them.

| Avoid | Reason | Alternative |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| `**bold text**` or `*italic text*` | Emphasis not allowed | Use structure and word choice for emphasis |
| `# Top-level heading` | Rendered by layout | Start with `##` |
| `you` / `your` / `yourself` | Addresses the reader | Write in third person |
| `on earth` / `the real world` / `in our world` | References the real world | Use in-world geography |
| `computer` / `internet` / `electricity` / `video` | Anachronistic terms | Use period-appropriate terms |
| Unresolvable internal links | Dead link | Mention in plain text until the page exists |
